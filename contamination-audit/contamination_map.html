0 · Does knowledge turn on as the cutoff sweeps past the date?

The clean test of the time-lock: an event dated between two cutoffs should be unknown to the earlier model and known to the later one. % picked-true by date band, per base model (50% = chance):

events dated…1900 model1913 model1929 model
events 1901–191375% [post, n12]83% [pre, n12]83% [pre, n12]
events 1914–192940% [post, n25]44% [post, n25]64% [pre, n25]
post-1930 — non-US-politics52% [post, n141]54% [post, n141]56% [post, n141]
post-1930 — US politics89% [post, n9]89% [post, n9]89% [post, n9]
Two signatures, side by side. Legit content (1914–1929): the 1913 model is at chance (it does not know WW1-era events), then the 1929 model jumps up once those years fall inside its cutoff — knowledge switches on exactly as the cutoff crosses the date. Contamination (post-1930 US politics): ~89% for every model, including the 1900 one — 'known' long before any cutoff could legitimately reach it. Clean post-1930 (everything else): stuck at chance for all three.

Your Einstein example. General relativity is 1915, so strictly the 1929 model should know it and the 1913 shouldn't. But watch the gap by cutoff: 1900 = +0.19 (chance — Einstein was an unknown clerk in 1900), 1913 = +4.92 (already strong!), 1929 = +4.82. Knowledge turns on between 1900 and 1913 — tracking when Einstein became famous (special relativity, 1905), not the 1915 date of general relativity. So this is the time-lock working, not a leak: for people who became famous before the cutoff, key the item on when the name became knowable, not the event.
