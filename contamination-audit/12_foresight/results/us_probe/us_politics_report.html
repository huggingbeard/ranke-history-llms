Do the frozen models know post-1929 US politics, or just complete the first name?

Battery: 2_model_probes/us_politics_power.py (presidential succession + civil rights), scored two ways. Cued = the original prompt hands the model the first name (“… was Franklin ___” → Roosevelt). No-cue = the given name is stripped, so the description alone must yield the surname (“… who launched the New Deal was ___”). The gap is shallow name-completion; what survives no-cue is knowledge. Ranke 1913 & 1929; four-way forced choice, chance 25%.

What it shows

Stripping the first name removes the name-completion inflation: on the post-1929 presidents the cued score collapses toward chance without the cue, which means the earlier “contamination” was the model completing “Franklin/Harry/John F.”, not knowing the presidency. Where the model truly has nothing (Obama, Trump, Biden, Rosa Parks, the city of Memphis) it confabulates a pre-cutoff name even in the no-cue column. This reproduces the audit’s finding on its own battery: the US-politics signal is shallow name/phrase exposure, not deep knowledge.
