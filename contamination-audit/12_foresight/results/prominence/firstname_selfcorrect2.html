Self-correction — robust, with a reverse control

Robustness: each person is now primed with four plausible wrong first names, and the reversion R is averaged, so it no longer hangs on the single decoy I picked. Reverse control: we also prime the true name and measure how readily the model echoes it. The tell of a name-agnostic model is that it echoes a wrong name about as easily as the true one — it is just repeating whatever you gave it. Known people should resist the lie (low echo of the wrong name); post-cutoff people should parrot it.

Top: robust reversion R (green = reverts to the truth, red = keeps the lie). Bottom: the reverse control — black dot = how much it echoes the TRUE name when primed with it, coloured dot = how much it echoes a WRONG name when primed with it. For post-cutoff people (rose) the two dots sit together on the right: it echoes a lie as readily as the truth. For known people the wrong-name dot slides left: the model refuses to keep the lie.
