Prominence across time: which people does each frozen reader know?

Matched domain trios (pre-1900 / 1900-29 / post-1929), 50 free continuations of “<Name> was” per person per model, judged by gemini-3.1-flash-lite. Measuring name-knowledge is confounded twice, so we triangulate three passes: the naive frame is hijacked by ordinary same-name people (“Richard Wagner was arrested for stealing a bicycle”); a role-primed frame fixes that but lets post-1929 people pass as a generic period person of that role (a vaudeville “Elvis”); the era-specific judge collapses that confabulation.

The metric’s blind spot: plausible confabulation scores like knowledge

Blue = lenient judge; red = era-specific judge, both on the primed frame at Ranke 1929. The lenient number cannot separate the genuinely known (Lenin, Einstein) from the confabulated post-1929 (Reagan, Elvis, Mao) — both sit near 50–60%. The strict judge is harsh on everyone. The only reliable signal is reading the text, below: real era-specific detail (Wagner at Leipzig) versus a fluent period impostor (a vaudeville Elvis). This is the contamination problem in miniature: plausible ≠ known.
