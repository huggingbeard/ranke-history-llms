\noindent\textbf{What this document is.} On 9 July, JV sent a screenshot: ten words that light up
inside the Ranke-1929 model when it reads the name \emph{Hitler}. Over three days that observation was
tested, partly debunked, partly confirmed, turned into an instrument, and certified. This document
walks the whole journey step by step, in plain language, with the technical detail in grey boxes and
every number traceable to a saved result file. It ends with where we landed and what remains open.

\section{The starting point: the screenshot}

A frozen 1929 reader should know Hitler only as a Bavarian putschist. Asked directly ``who leads
Germany in 1940?'', it plays innocent --- the Kaiser, Hindenburg, no Hitler. But JV's screenshot,
taken with an inspection tool at layer 33 of the model's 36 processing layers, showed the ten words
most \emph{stirred up} inside the model at the moment it reads the name:

\begin{center}
\texttt{Hitler, Barack, Weiner, Stalin, Nazis, Eisenhower, Soviets, Friedman, triggered, Soviet}
\end{center}

Stalin, Nazis, Eisenhower: the man's post-cutoff world, apparently known to a model that should have
died in 1929. And Barack and ``triggered'': the twenty-first century. If real, this is contamination
read at the level of \emph{content} --- more subtle than any date test, because the model never says
any of this out loud.

\begin{tech}
The tool is a logit lens: take the residual-stream state at the token position of ``Hitler'' at layer
$\ell$, apply the model's final layer-norm and unembedding matrix, and read the resulting distribution
over the $\sim$150k-token vocabulary. ``Stirred up'' = high rank in that distribution.
\end{tech}

\section{Step 1: why the list cannot be taken at face value}

Two very different mechanisms put a word into such a top-ten. \textbf{Mechanism one: learned
association.} The training corpus contained leaked post-cutoff documents in which Hitler and Stalin
co-occur; training wired the link. That is contamination. \textbf{Mechanism two: the blank-card
artifact.} Ranke borrows a modern tokenizer, so its dictionary has entries for ``Barack'' and
``triggered'' --- but if those entries never once occurred in the training text, their vectors were
never meaningfully trained. Such orphaned vectors are notorious for drifting together and surfacing
spuriously in exactly this kind of top-ten list. They look like knowledge; they are noise.

The danger is that the most seductive tells --- names that did not even exist before the cutoff ---
are precisely the ones most likely to be blank cards.

\begin{tech}
Embeddings are tied in Ranke (input embedding = unembedding). Tokens that never appear as targets
receive only the shared softmax push and drift into a tight cluster; under a logit lens their inner
products with an arbitrary residual state are essentially arbitrary. Verified below (\S6): the
never-seen reference population (emoji tokens) clusters at median pairwise-centroid cosine 0.9985.
\end{tech}

\section{Step 2: the controlled comparison (the 1929 model)}

The fix is old-fashioned: controls. Run the \emph{same} sentence with the name swapped ---
\emph{``The name on everyone's lips was \underline{Hitler} / \underline{Bismarck} /
\underline{M\"uller} / \underline{Brennt}''} (Brennt is a person we invented) --- and ask where each
suspicious word ranks in the model's next-word betting list in each case. A word carries information
only if it is warm \emph{specifically} behind Hitler.

Reading: \textbf{Stalin and Nazis are genuinely Hitler-specific} --- warm behind Hitler, cold behind

control: it is warm behind every German name, because it is simply a German word-neighbour.

\finding{the screenshot contained one real signal (Hitler$\to$Stalin/Nazis) wrapped in eye-catching
junk (Barack, triggered). Eyeballing top-ten lists is not evidence; name-swapped rank comparisons are.}

\section{Step 3: the 1913 test, with the prediction written down first}

JV: ``try Hitler associations for the 1913 model.'' Before running, we registered the prediction: in
1913 print, Hitler and Stalin were both nobodies; a clean 1913 model must treat ``Hitler'' as a mere
surname --- no Stalin warmth at all. (This makes 1913 a sharper testbed than 1929, where putsch-era
Hitler was already famous and some associations could be legitimate.)

\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule

\midrule
Stalin behind Hitler (vs.\ controls)   & 14{,}052 vs.\ 35--46k \textbf{(specific)} & 38{,}030 vs.\ 42--46k (flat) \\
Nazis behind Hitler                    & 15{,}696 \textbf{(specific)}              & 47{,}041 (whisper) \\

The prediction held. The association \emph{tracks the training corpus}: rich in the 1929 model, absent
in the 1913 one --- exactly how a real measuring instrument must behave (the same two-model logic that
validated the memory meter). Two honest footnotes. First, since 1929-Hitler and 1929-Stalin were both
famous, a late-1920s newspaper \emph{could} legitimately mention them together; before calling the
1929 link contamination we owe it the period co-occurrence check. Second, a flagged whisper: Eisenhower
shows a mild Hitler-specific lift \emph{even in the 1913 model} (23{,}951 vs.\ 39k+). In 1913 he was a
West Point cadet --- no innocent reading exists --- but it is a deep-rank signal from one sentence and
is flagged for the battery, not claimed.

\section{Step 4: JV's question that simplified the method}

``Isn't the stethoscope just log-probing the model?'' Answer, after checking our own data: yes --- and
the check overturned a wrong assumption. Tracking Stalin's rank behind Hitler layer by layer in the
1929 model: invisible until layer 29, emerging at 30 (41{,}894), sharpening through 33 (14{,}052), and
\emph{strongest at the final layer}: \textbf{1{,}658} at layer 36 --- versus $\sim$12--13k behind
Bismarck/M\"uller. The association is not hiding below speech; it is present in the model's ordinary
output probabilities.

So why did the direct question (``the leader of Germany in 1940 is\ldots'') show nothing? Because a
\emph{question} forces the leaked trace to out-compete massive period priors (the Kaiser, Hindenburg).
A \emph{free-association} prompt --- just say the name, ask nothing --- removes the competition, and
the faint trace becomes measurable. \textbf{The real discovery in the screenshot was not a new place
to look inside the model; it was a new kind of prompt.}

\finding{the probe runs on our existing, certified, batched log-prob machinery. The layer-by-layer
view is demoted to a secondary diagnostic (early-emerging links look lexical, late-emerging ones look
semantic).}

\section{Step 5: the match test --- certifying the detector (the inject)}

At this point ``1913 is cold'' had a hole: silence can mean a clean house \emph{or a broken smoke
alarm}. The classical fix is a controlled match: create a real fire, on purpose, and check the alarm
screams. So we surgically wrote one fake memory into the 1913 model's weights --- ``the great rival of
Hitler was $\to$ Stalin'' --- and re-ran the reading on sentences the edit never saw.

\begin{table}[h]\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
rank of Stalin\ldots & before the edit & after the edit \\
\midrule
behind Hitler, carrier A (held out)  & 11{,}357 & \textbf{106} \\
behind Hitler, carrier B (held out)  & 15{,}447 & \textbf{1{,}277} \\
behind Bismarck                      & 15{,}042 & 14{,}986 \\
behind M\"uller                      & 15{,}571 & 15{,}323 \\
\midrule
period facts (Portsmouth, Frankfurt) & intact   & intact \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{The sensitivity certificate, layer 36. Criteria pre-registered in the script header.
Source: \texttt{assoc\_inject\_1913.json}. The edit lived in memory only; no model file was changed.}
\end{table}

Read the columns correctly --- this is where intuition slips. \textbf{The before column is the
house reading}: silence, the evidence of cleanliness. \textbf{The after column is the match}: the
alarm screaming at a fire \emph{we} lit --- it says nothing about contamination and everything about
the detector's hearing. \textbf{The Bismarck row shows the match did not torch the kitchen}: the edit
moved only the name it was keyed to, so the calibration is a fair one. Together: silence
\emph{plus} a proven-loud alarm \emph{plus} a controlled match $=$ the silence was real. (Reminder on
units: rank counts down from the top of a 150k-word list, so \emph{low number = warm = loud}.)

Three questions this table reliably raises, answered plainly. \emph{What exactly is inserted?}
Neither word into any sentence --- the edit changes the \emph{wiring}: it glues a note onto the
model's Hitler card reading ``see also: Stalin.'' Hitler is the key (the doorbell); Stalin is the
content (who shows up). \emph{Shouldn't Stalin then be rank 1?} On the one sentence used to train the
edit --- ``The great rival of Hitler was'' --- he \textbf{is}: after the edit, `` Stalin'' is the
model's \#1 continuation at $\sim$50\%. But that number is worthless as a test, because we optimised
for exactly it. The table therefore reports \emph{held-out} sentences the edit never saw (``The name
on everyone's lips was Hitler''), which ask for nothing --- ordinary connectives still win the top
spots, and the measurement is how close Stalin now \emph{floats} to the surface when nobody asked:
106th of 150k instead of 11{,}357th. That generalisation is the point: real contamination also enters
through \emph{other} sentences than the ones we test with, so the detector must be certified on fresh
contexts. \emph{Isn't Hitler ``in the question''?} Yes, necessarily --- the name is the doorbell, and
a carrier sentence must ring it. Stalin, by contrast, never appears in any prompt; he is only looked
up in the model's betting list.

One genuine discovery came free: planting Stalin also warmed Eisenhower (3{,}811$\to$334) and Nazis
(13{,}979$\to$1{,}569) behind Hitler --- similar names live close together in the model's word-space,
so one stored fact heats its whole neighbourhood.

\finding{related tells are ONE piece of evidence, not several. The battery must count distinct
\emph{clusters} that fire, never raw words --- otherwise we would multiply our own evidence.}

\begin{tech}
ROME-style rank-1 edit (Meng et al.\ 2022), mechanics identical to the treaty injections that
certified the memory meter: optimise a delta $z$ on the layer-9 MLP down-projection output at the
subject token until the model emits `` Stalin'' after the edit template, then apply
$W \mathrel{+}= z k^{*\top}/(k^{*\top}k^{*})$ with $k^*$ the down-projection input at the subject
token. Edit template $\neq$ measurement carriers. $\lVert z\rVert = 21.0$.
\end{tech}

\section{Step 6: the dictionary census (the blank cards become an instrument)}

The blank-card artifact of Step 1 has a flip side: in a from-scratch model, a dictionary entry only
moves if the corpus contained that token. So ``did this word's entry train?'' is a contamination test
that needs \emph{no prompts at all}. Measured properly (closeness to the tight cluster where all
never-seen tokens drift --- raw vector length turned out not to separate), the census validates and
immediately teaches:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Extremes are clean.} Period words (railway, Kaiser, cavalry) read clearly trained;
``smartphone'' reads never-seen in both models --- the far-modern control behaves.
\item \textbf{The method sees history.} Words that legitimately entered print between the two cutoffs
--- Bolshevik, Soviet, fascist, jazz, insulin --- all read \emph{more trained in the 1929 model than
the 1913 one}. The instrument detects fifteen years of vocabulary change with no external data.
\item \textbf{A trap sprang, usefully.} ``email'' reads trained in a pre-1929 corpus --- because
German \emph{Email} and French \emph{\'email} mean \emph{enamel}, perfectly period words. New binding
rule: the corpus is EN+DE+FR, so every tell must be innocent in \emph{all three languages}. (This is
JV's ``genocide'' correction --- word post-cutoff but referent pre-cutoff --- generalised across
languages.)
\item \textbf{One suspect.} ``robot'' reads trained-ish in \emph{both} models, including 1913 ---
seven years before the word was coined. No innocent reading found yet; flagged for the corpus grep.
\end{itemize}

\section{Step 7: from a probe to a pipeline}

A probe answers one question about one name. A pipeline answers the same question for a whole
roster, under fixed rules, with its error rates measured. Built on 12 July (the roster file
\texttt{battery\_assoc\_v1.json} and the runner \texttt{contam\_pipeline.py}), it works like this,
illustrated end to end with Trotsky on the 1913 model.

\textbf{The roster.} Each entry names a \emph{target} (Trotsky), its \emph{tells} --- words that would
betray forbidden knowledge, each vetted so that no innocent explanation exists (Stalin: the rivalry
began after 1917; Red Army: founded 1918; Mexico: his 1937 exile) --- its \emph{innocents} --- words a
clean, well-read 1913 model \emph{should} connect to him (Siberia: he was exiled there twice before
1913) --- and shared \emph{controls}: three real period names (Bismarck, Gladstone, M\"uller) and five
invented people carried over from the date-spike work (Brennt, Tolliver, Bathe, Voss, Manx).

\textbf{The measurement.} Five bland carrier sentences per name (``The name on everyone's lips was
\underline{\hspace{1.2cm}}'', ``In those days the newspapers were full of
\underline{\hspace{1.2cm}}'', \ldots). For each tell we measure how much likelier the word becomes
after the target's name than after the real control names, averaged over the five sentences. That
number is the \emph{lift}. Trotsky's Stalin-lift is $+4.05$; since these are natural-logarithm units,
$+4$ means roughly \emph{fifty-seven times likelier} --- Stalin is fifty-seven times more expected
behind ``Trotsky'' than behind ``Bismarck'' or ``M\"uller''.

\textbf{The floor.} Every tell is also measured behind the five invented people, who can be connected
to nothing. The 95th percentile of those lifts ($+2.50$ on the 1913 model) is the \emph{fiction
floor}: the most a lift can reach by pure noise. A tell counts only above it.

\textbf{The verdict.} One stored fact warms its whole word-neighbourhood (proven in Step 5's
inject), so related tells count as ONE piece of evidence --- tells are grouped into \emph{clusters},
and a target is FLAGGED only when two or more \emph{independent} clusters fire; one cluster =
WHISPER; none = DARK. Trotsky: Stalin (rivals cluster, $+4.05$) and Red Army (military cluster,
$+4.46$) both clear the floor $\Rightarrow$ FLAGGED --- while his innocents (Siberia, exile) also
fire, confirming the model knows the real pre-1913 man. Known entity + impossible links: the full
signature of contamination.

\section{Step 8: proving the pipeline (inserted memories and a cleaner rival)}

\textbf{The match test, at pipeline scale.} As in Step 5, a verdict system is only trustworthy if it
provably flips when a memory is truly there. We chose one target per model that the pipeline scored
DARK --- Ebert (1913), Truman (1929) --- and surgically wrote ``\ldots became president'' into the
weights. \emph{The first attempt failed}, and the failure is part of the record: planting from a
single sentence moved Ebert's president-lift from $-2.06$ to $+2.42$ --- nine hundredths below the
floor. Diagnosis: real contamination enters through many documents, not one sentence. We declared
exactly one protocol revision (three planting sentences instead of one; pass criteria untouched) and
re-ran once. Result: Ebert DARK$\to$WHISPER (president $+7.42$, the planted cluster), Truman
DARK$\to$WHISPER ($+6.71$) --- and, crucially, \emph{no other target's verdict moved} in either
model. Sensitivity and specificity, each proven twice. (Both edits lived in memory only; no model
file was changed.)

\textbf{The cleaner rival.} Talkie-1930 is another laboratory's time-locked model, known from the
date-spike work to be much cleaner than Ranke. We ran the identical battery on it (a rented cloud
GPU, $\approx\$0.60$; the machine carried three independent self-destruct mechanisms and its
termination was verified afterwards). Where Ranke-1929 whispers, Talkie mostly reads darker
(Eisenhower, Obama, Franco) --- as a cleaner corpus should. Its exceptions taught us two things.
First, \emph{radar is FLAGGED in both models}, even stronger in Talkie --- and Talkie's one known
date-leak is the Fall of France, 1940, exactly radar's era: a shared-leak candidate for the corpus
search. Second, Kennedy$\to$Dallas fired in Talkie: real names co-occur with real \emph{places} in
any newspaper corpus, so a floor built only from invented names is too lenient for place-words. The
next version's floor gets real-but-unlinkable period names (Gladstone$\to$Dallas) added to it.

\section{Step 9: the boss's audits (how the instrument got honest)}

Three review passes by JV each removed a defect the builders had missed. They are part of the method
now, and any staff member extending the battery must apply them.

\textbf{The biography audit (13 July).} ``Trotsky and revolution is innocent??? fumes and poison
gas?'' All three complaints held up, and the audit exposed a fourth error unprompted:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{No ambiguous words, in any role.} ``Revolution'' behind Trotsky cannot distinguish
legitimate 1905 knowledge from leaked 1917 knowledge --- it is uninterpretable and is excluded
entirely (labelling it ``innocent'' would have quietly laundered 1917 signal).
\item \emph{Innocents must be facts, not synonyms.} ``Fumes'' behind ``poison gas'' is dictionary
adjacency; it confirms nothing. Dropped.
\item \emph{A tell must be impossible for the target's own pre-cutoff life}, not merely a
post-cutoff word. ``Exile'' was an invalid Trotsky tell --- he was exiled twice \emph{before} 1913
--- and was reclassified as an innocent, where its firing correctly certifies period knowledge.
Trotsky's FLAGGED verdict survives on the two biography-proof clusters.
\item \emph{Floors must match name-culture.} ``Ankara'' lifting behind the Turkish name Kemal,
measured against a floor of European invented names, may be name-geography, not knowledge. Kemal is
provisional until the floor contains invented Turkish names.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The one-term audit (13 July).} ``Why is Ebert clean with a lift of 6 --- because it's only
one term?'' Exactly right, and it exposed the last gap: a single planted fact can only ever produce
ONE cluster, so the insertion test had certified the WHISPER tier's sensitivity --- never the FLAGGED
tier's. We tried to close the gap by planting \emph{two} facts into Ebert (president and Weimar) ---
and failed twice, honestly: the second weight-edit partially overwrote the first, whether written to
the same layer or to two different layers. One-position weight surgery cannot superpose two facts the
way real multi-document contamination does. Per the declared stop rule we stopped. \emph{Status of
the FLAGGED tier, stated precisely:} its specificity is measured (0.6\% false positives on pure
fictions) and its natural positives carry independent corroboration (Hitler-1913 and Trotsky-1913
agree with the date-spike evidence), but its sensitivity to inserted memories is NOT yet demonstrated
--- the honest route to that certificate is a document-level plant (briefly fine-tuning a copy of the
model on a few hundred synthetic period-style articles), queued for v2.

\textbf{The calibration audit (13 July).} ``Poison gas, tank, fascist all look suspicious --- maybe
the cutoff for N or level is too low?'' We measured instead of arguing, by running every invented
person through every target's tell-list as a pseudo-target:
\begin{itemize}
\item The WHISPER tier has a measured \textbf{15\% false-positive rate} (8 of 55 pure fictions earn a
whisper, in each of the three models). Whispers are therefore \emph{leads, not evidence}, and are so
labelled. FLAGGED is clean: 1 of 165 ($\approx$0.6\%).
\item A strength-based alternative rule (score = mean of the two best lifts, calibrated on the
pseudo-targets) confirms JV's suspects are genuinely strong --- fascist fired \emph{all three} of its
tells and would be FLAGGED --- but the same rule also flags \emph{smartphone}, the deliberately
impossible control target whose lifts come from a mechanical artifact (its own word-parts,
``smart''+``phone'', are meaningful period words that drag technology-words upward). Strength measures
association; association is not always contamination.
\item The decisive pattern: every verdict that \emph{depends on the choice of rule} is a term-target
(fascist, poison gas, tank, smartphone) or an already-provisional case (Kemal, Franco). On certified
person-targets the two rules agree everywhere, in all three models. So the thresholds stay; the
term-target class is quarantined until it has its own controls (matched invented compounds --- a
``steamgas'' floor for ``poison gas''); and JV's three suspects go to the corpus search as
\emph{named leads}.
\end{itemize}

\begin{tech}
Calibration files: \texttt{contam\_pipeline\_1913\_full.json}, \texttt{\ldots\_1929\_full.json}
(complete lift matrices including per-invented-name lifts), and \texttt{calib\_verdict\_rule.json}.
Strength rule: $S=$ mean of top-2 tell lifts; null $=S$ over (invented name $\times$ tell-set); flag
iff $S\geq$ 95th percentile of null and $\geq 2$ tells fired.
\end{tech}

\section{How to replicate or extend (staff instructions)}

\texttt{PYTHONUTF8=1}. Scoring is full-precision and batched by default (\texttt{common.py});
regression gate \texttt{test\_slp\_batch.py} --- run it after any torch or GPU change.
Every script below is run as \texttt{python src/<script>} from the \texttt{12\_foresight} folder.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Battery runs: \texttt{contam\_pipeline.py 1913} and \texttt{1929}. Roster:
\texttt{battery\_assoc\_v1.json} (v1.2). Verdict reclassification without rescoring:
\texttt{reclass\_v12.py}.
\item Inserted-memory validation: \texttt{pipeline\_validate.py 1913} and \texttt{1929}. Pass
criteria are pre-registered in the script header; edits are in-memory only.
\item Dark control: \texttt{talkie\_assoc\_runpod.py launch}, then \texttt{monitor} (never skip the
monitor --- it is teardown lock 3). Verify afterwards that the RunPod account shows zero pods.
\item Calibration audit: \texttt{calib\_verdict\_rule.py} (no GPU).
\item Reports: \texttt{contam\_pipeline\_report.py} regenerates the HTML companion; this document is
\texttt{assoc\_probe\_narrative.tex} (two \texttt{pdflatex} passes).
\item Extending the roster: every new tell must pass ALL rules --- word and referent post-cutoff;
innocent in English, German AND French (the ``email''$=$enamel trap); no shared word-root with the

evidence.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Where we landed}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A working, validated pipeline} --- the goal delivered. Roster in, verdicts out, with
its error rates measured: flag-level false positives $\approx$0.6\%, whisper-level $\approx$15\%
(whispers are leads, not evidence). Sensitivity and specificity proven twice each by planted
memories (Ebert, Truman: DARK$\to$WHISPER on exactly the planted cluster, nothing else moving).
Certified scope: \emph{person-targets}. Term-targets (fascist, poison gas, tank), name-culture cases
(Kemal) and polysemous names (Franco) are quarantined until they get their own controls --- their
signals are strong but not yet interpretable, per the calibration audit.
\item \textbf{Natural findings that survived every audit.} Trotsky-1913 FLAGGED: the model knows the
real pre-1913 man (Siberia, exile fire as innocents) and carries his impossible future (Stalin
$+4.05$, Red Army $+4.46$). Radar FLAGGED in both Ranke-1929 and the cleaner Talkie-1930 --- a
shared-leak candidate matching Talkie's one known 1940 leak. The 1929 Hitler$\to$Stalin whisper
appears in Talkie too, so it may be legitimate late-twenties dictator co-mention --- the period
co-occurrence check decides, and until then no contamination claim is made.
\item \textbf{A method hardened by three hostile reviews.} The name-lottery rule (from the
yardstick), the biography rule and the ambiguity/synonym rules (from the innocents audit), the
whisper-FPR measurement and the term-target quarantine (from the calibration audit). Each review is
reproducible: the objection, the check, the numbers, the decision are all in \texttt{LOG.md}.
\item \textbf{Two supporting instruments.} The vocabulary census (prompt-free; caught the
``email''$=$enamel trilingual trap; flagged ``robot'' as trained in 1913, seven years before the word
existed) and the inserted-memory machinery (the certificate-maker for both the probe and the
pipeline).
\item \textbf{Named, checkable leads for the corpus search on Alps} --- the gold standard that
settles what pre-filters can only flag: robot, radar, poison gas, tank, fascist, the 1913 Eisenhower
whisper, and Trotsky's Stalin/Red-Army cluster.
\end{enumerate}
