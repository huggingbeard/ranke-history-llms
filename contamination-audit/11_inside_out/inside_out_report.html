Does a 1929 mind secretly know the future?

A pre-registered test for hidden knowledge in the time-frozen Ranke-1929 model — facts it might encode internally even if it never says them. Method after Gekhman et al. 2025, “Inside-Out.”

Our earlier audits judged the model by what it says (output probabilities) and what it writes (generation). This asks the deepest question: does the model internally encode post-cutoff facts — secretly “know” them in its hidden layers — even when its output looks clean? We score each fact three ways and check whether the true answer is ranked above false ones (chance = 0.50):

  • says — the model's output probabilities (what it would tell you).
  • lens — a peek at an intermediate layer (logit-lens), read without training.
  • probe — a small classifier trained on the hidden states to spot the true answer.

Hidden knowledge = an internal score (lens or probe) beating the output score. The whole thing lives or dies on two controls, so we check those first.

1Do the controls hold? (the trust gates)

Positive control — PASSED. On pre-cutoff facts the model truly knows, all three scorers recover them well above chance (says 0.90, lens 0.88, probe 0.73). So the instruments work — they can detect knowledge when it's really there.
Shuffle test (null) — PASSED. When we scramble the correct/incorrect labels, the probe collapses to chance (0.50, range 0.44–0.55). So the probe isn't just memorising a global “true-looking” pattern.

3The verdict: no credible hidden knowledge

Bottom line: the clean internal scorer (the lens, which passes both controls) sits right on top of what the model already says — in every topic. There is no sign the model secretly knows post-cutoff facts it won't express. The trained probe looked like it found some — but it failed the negative control, so we don't trust it (next box).

The lens finds nothing hidden. Internal-minus-external (lens) is ≈ 0 everywhere: science+space +0.01, US civil rights +0.04, US politics 1945-74 -0.01, Cold War +0.00, Vietnam War +0.00. Where the model knows a thing, it says it; where it doesn't, the hidden layers don't either.

Why we throw out the probe's “hidden knowledge.” The probe flagged science & space as hidden knowledge (gap +0.26, survives multiple-comparison correction) — but those are the genuinely-unknowable facts (DNA in 1953, Sputnik, …) a 1929 model cannot know. A real knowledge detector must stay quiet there. It didn't. So the probe is picking up which answer simply looks more plausible, not whether the model knows the fact — exactly the trap our pre-registered negative control was built to catch. Its “hidden knowledge” on Cold War is the same artifact, and is not credible. (The probe's gap is also inflated because the model scores below chance externally on these — it actively prefers a wrong answer — so even a chance-level probe looks like a gain.)

The actual suspect — US civil rights (the one topic the output audit flagged) — shows no hidden amplification: lens gap +0.04, probe gap -0.03 (not significant). Whatever leakage exists there is fully on the surface, already visible in the output audit — not buried deeper in the weights.

4What this means & the honest fine print

The output-level contamination verdict stands: the model isn't secretly hoarding post-cutoff knowledge. And we get a methodological bonus — naive hidden-knowledge probing can manufacture false “hidden contamination,” and an unknowable negative control is what exposes it. Ours did.

Fine print: (1) This measures the weights' encoding (rankability of true vs false), not a mind, and the probe is correlational. (2) The lens used a near-final layer (chosen on the positive control), so it is a conservative test of “an earlier layer knows more than the output”; a deeper layer-by-layer and an open-generation “deeply hidden” pass are the natural follow-ups. (3) Modest n (16–40/topic); this is a pre-registered proof-of-method slice on Ranke-1929 base, not the full sweep. The pre-registration, controls, and decision rules were fixed before any score was seen (see PREREGISTRATION.md).
