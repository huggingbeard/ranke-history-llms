What has it seen? — association-lift

Does the time-locked model carry associations it should not? We measure each association directly, isolating the leaked link from generic word-frequency.

The metric. For a place–event pair we score the same event phrase after the target place and after a matched control place, and take the difference:
lift = logp(event | “The city of Hiroshima…”) − logp(event | “The city of Kyoto…”)
The control cancels everything generic (sentence shape, how common the event words are). What survives is whether the model specifically ties this place to this event. lift > 0 = the link is stored; lift ≈ 0 = no more than chance; lift < 0 = it prefers the control.

The metric switches on exactly when the events enter each model's window. That is the behaviour we want before trusting any single number.

Why this is not contamination. The group mean is ≈0. A handful of small positives sit at the top (max +2.23, Vietnam->US war) — all of them places whose name already existed before 1929, so the model carries a faint generic prior for the location, not the event.

The decisive tell is the ordering. If the model had truly absorbed post-1929 history, its most iconic events would show the largest lift. They show the most negative: Hiroshima→atomic bomb -0.42, Pearl Harbor -1.12, Cuba→missile crisis -2.46. That is the opposite of a leak — it is a model that has never read about them.
Verdict. No systematic post-cutoff leak. The few positive lifts are weak place-name priors / control-side artifacts, contradicted by strongly negative lifts on the most famous post-1929 events. Meanwhile the pre-cutoff controls — including the full 1914–29 / German block — are robustly positive. The model knows its own era and stops cleanly at the wall.

4. The absolute test — where does it land on facts that exist only after 1929?

Contamination is absolute, not relative. A model that swallowed one Vietnam-War book plus a library of French-Indochina text will always lean ‘French’ — so a relative test false-clears it. Instead we ask 15 facts per topic that exist only in post-cutoff text, each a forced choice against 3 matched-plausible distractors. A model with zero exposure lands at chance = 25% (3.75/15). Above that = contaminated, however small next to its legitimate knowledge.

Verdict on the absolute test. Vietnam (5/15) and WWII (4–5/15) land at chance, and every green hit has a pre-cutoff explanation — the model is reaching for knowledge it legitimately has (Tonkin the French protectorate, Barbarossa the medieval emperor, Yalta the Tsarist resort). Tellingly, every fact that needs real post-cutoff knowledge fails hard and the model substitutes an in-period answer: napalm→mustard gas, Saigon-fell→1945, cosmonaut→astronaut, Berlin-Wall→1919.

Cold War looked elevated (Instruct 7/15, p=0.057), driven by NATO (+4.7) and Warsaw Pact (+6.3) — so we dug it rather than wave it off. The dig cleared it. Property-coherence: the model fails the Warsaw Pact's founding year (1955, −7.6) and dissolution (1991, −9.0) catastrophically and does not link it to NATO or communism — 1–2 of 6, at chance: it does not know the pact. Neutral-frame: NATO (+2.9) and ‘Warsaw Pact’ (+1.0) still win in content-free sentences, so the lift is a pure string/bigram prior. Source: the model genuinely knows the in-window 1920 Battle of Warsaw (+3.4), which feeds the ‘Warsaw’ bigram. Verdict: lexical artifact, not contamination. All three topics clean.
