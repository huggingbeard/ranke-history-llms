9Method, decision rule & what is not claimed

Method
  • Forced choice, whole-answer sum-logp. The model teacher-forces the true completion and each distractor; "correct" = highest total log-probability to the truth. Metric fixed on should-know controls, blind to the post items.
  • Length-matched twins, no name cues. Distractors are plausible same-type alternatives (wrong years, other real entities); the prompt states the descriptive fact and never cues the answer's name.
  • Raw, not calibrated. Popularity calibration was tried and discarded — it broke the instrument (drove pre-controls down and inflated post). All figures here are recomputed from raw per-item records, so the two models are strictly apples-to-apples.
  • Significance (two tiers). One-sided binomial vs 25% per cluster. RED / confirmed leak = survives Benjamini-Hochberg FDR (q=.05) across the four powered clusters — the pre-registered primary test. AMBER / elevated = above chance at the cautious nominal bar α=.10 but not FDR-confirmed. The amber bar is deliberately loose: in a contamination audit a false "clean" is the costly error, so a p≈0.06 cluster is flagged, not dismissed (treating p=.049 and p=.058 as opposite verdicts is the classic significant/not-significant fallacy).
  • Length-matched twins. Distractors are built to match the true answer's token count so raw sum-logp is unbiased. This holds on average (median length gap = 0) but not perfectly — see the caveat below.
Red / amber / green (per cluster, as applied here): GREEN = not above chance even at α=.10. AMBER = above chance at α=.10 but not FDR-confirmed (suspect, unconfirmed). RED = above chance and survives FDR (confirmed leak). A non-significant cliff is evidence of contamination only if the post side is also above chance — noise alone is not. (The original refinement — splitting amber/red further by whether POST is statistically below the PRE baseline — is reported via the per-cluster numbers in §4.)
Caveat — residual length bias (biases toward false-clean). Raw sum-logp penalises longer true answers. Of 239 post items, 54 (23%) are imperfectly length-matched; 44 have a true answer longer than every distractor (biased toward a miss) vs 25 the other way — a net lean toward under-detecting contamination (see the DNA example in §2). Another reason a "clean" verdict is an upper bound, and the corpus grep (§8) is the real arbiter.
Not claimed. Modest item counts make the clean verdicts a strong upper bound on cleanliness, not proof of zero leakage. The map is not exhaustive.
