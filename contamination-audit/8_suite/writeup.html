Auditing time-locked LLMs for post-cutoff contamination

Ranke-4B-1929 and Talkie-1930-13B — a step-by-step record of what we did, every reversal, and a deliberately guarded reading of the result.

Bottom line, stated carefully. After a long chain of methods — several of which we had to reverse — a pre-registered, blind-screened, instrument-validated forced-choice battery finds evidence of post-cutoff contamination in Ranke-4B-1929, concentrated in US civil-rights-era history (1948–1968): 46% correct vs 25% chance, p=0.005, surviving FDR correction, and confirmed by item-level inspection (it picks Brown v. Board over Plessy, Wallace over other segregationist governors, the Orangeburg massacre over other campus shootings). US domestic politics 1945–74 is elevated but borderline; Cold War and Vietnam sit at chance. This is evidence, not proof: the instrument validates at 82% (not 100%) on pre-cutoff controls; full certification would require finding the source text in the training corpus; and Talkie and the Ranke instruct model have not yet been scored on this validated battery. We are not claiming a clean bill anywhere, nor a complete contamination map.

The journey (and where it turned)

Orange = a method reversal; red = a retraction or overclaim we had to walk back; green = the surviving finding. The reversals are the point of the diagram, not an embarrassment to hide — each was a way the answer could have been (and briefly was) wrong.

1–2. What we were measuring, and the traps

The question is narrow: does the model assign higher probability to a true post-cutoff answer than to matched false ones, under teacher forcing (we read log-probs; we never trust free generation, which understates knowledge — the model knew Hindenburg under scoring but confabulated him in chat). The hard part is that several things masquerade as knowledge:

3–5. Exploratory probing, and the reversals it produced

Early forced-choice and association probes did flag domains — most visibly a '19/28, p=.044' on US politics. Each flag dissolved on inspection:

  • The '19/28' was a metric artifact (average-per-token log-prob). Under sum-log-prob with matched twins it vanished.
  • 'Hiroshima→atomic bomb' and 'Warsaw Pact' looked leaked, but a content-free / control-subject test showed they were place salience and lexical priors — the model fails the Warsaw Pact's founding year (−7.6) and dissolution (−9.0), so it likes the bigram, not the alliance.
  • We argued 'the 1900 model cannot contain WWII text, so its showing the signal proves the signal is innocent'. We retracted this: the 1900 cutoff is itself the claim under audit, and if the models are warm-started a leak propagates and dilutes — the inference was circular.

6. The absolute test and the Talkie comparison

A forced-choice battery (true vs 3 matched distractors, chance 25%) on Vietnam, WWII and the Cold War put both models at chance post-cutoff while acing pre-cutoff controls. On the identical bank:

Talkie looked more contaminated on a co-author's rank-gain metric (5.5× vs 1.7×), but that metric rewards a bigger model for generating plausible names; matched distractors put both at chance on these domains. Caveat we missed at the time: these are foreign-policy / military topics. We had not tested US domestic history, where the real signal turned out to be.

7–8. The reckoning, and the pre-registered study

Calling the model 'clean' on the strength of at-chance results was an overclaim: 'failed to detect' is not 'absent', the battery was underpowered (n=15/topic; a 5/15 has a 95% upper bound near 50%), and the test had been tuned while looking at one model's scores. We reframed the goal to detecting evidence of contamination, and pre-registered a frozen protocol before generating or scoring any item:

  • Nested ('Russian-doll') thresholds. A broad post-1945 pool (~240 items) powered to detect a +10pp diffuse lift; deep clusters (~32–40 each) powered to +20pp concentrated leaks.
  • Blind screening before scoring. Items generated, then a separate adversarial referee + deterministic checks judged fairness only (post-cutoff? cued? distractors discriminable? factually correct?) with no model scores in hand, then locked. No tuning to an outcome afterwards.
  • Event-cluster analysis with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR across clusters; pre-cutoff controls to validate the instrument first.
Mistakes caught inside the pre-registered build (each would have produced a wrong answer): the model ID in our notes was retired (404s); the screening referee initially inverted the rule and rejected items for being post-cutoff; the generator produced adjacent-year distractors a knower can't discriminate; and a popularity-calibration we adopted from advice actually broke the instrument — it dropped pre-control accuracy from 82% to 48%. We discarded calibration and use the raw forced choice, a choice made on the should-know controls, blind to the contamination question.

9. The validated result

Instrument check first: on the locked pre-cutoff controls the raw metric scores 27/33 = 82% (the calibrated metric scored only 48% — why we dropped it). 82% is good enough to trust a positive signal, though not perfect; a handful of genuinely hard controls (Treaty of Nanjing, the transatlantic cable) fail.
