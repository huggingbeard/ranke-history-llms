What the era's frame could and could not do with an idea

Telling “found it normal” apart from “could not conceive it” in time-locked models (Ranke-4B-1900, Ranke-4B-1913, Ranke-4B-1929, base) — by behaviour against period-normal controls, not by reading silence.

A quiet meter is ambiguous: a frame says little about an idea either because the idea is old news or because it is inconceivable. You cannot read that off the silence. So we don't — we plant the fact and watch whether the frame can use it (run one step forward) and hold it (keep it across distance), and we compare each future fact's behaviour to facts the era genuinely found normal. The controls are the whole game: they show what assimilation looks like in this model, so its absence is legible.

The trichotomy, made behavioural. PRIMED the frame handles the fact like a normal one — holds it across distance and can state its consequence. BROKE the frame confidently reverts to its old default (the field-marshal move — fluent and wrong). UNTHINKABLE nothing registers and there is no downstream — it cannot run forward from the fact.

4Method & the honest limit

What this is. A measure of what the era's written corpus frame could do with an idea — extend it, retain it — relative to ideas it demonstrably found normal. What it is not: telepathy into an Edwardian's head. When the 1913 frame stalls, it shows the 1913 record gave the model no way to carry the idea forward. That is a claim about the discursive horizon of the surviving text — real, and a thing historians study — stated as such, before anyone asks “whose thinking?” (answer: the corpus's).
