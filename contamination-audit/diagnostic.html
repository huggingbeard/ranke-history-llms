The model knows post-cutoff people far better than post-cutoff dates. A strict battery that leans on dates/coined terms therefore reads as conservative; the clearest contamination lives in the post-1929 names.

3 · Airtight test: the first-name cue, toggled within the same item

80 scorings (20 post-1929 people × 4 models), each probed identically except the first name:

WITH the first name (cued): 55% correct  →  WITHOUT it (no-cue): 38%. The cue alone is worth 18 points — clean proof that a first name turns a knowledge test into name-completion.

4 · Quadruplets: more power, but watch the low-confidence items

On items with ≥3 length-matched twins (n=320): 2-way correct 53% (chance 50%) vs 4-way correct 30% (chance 25%). Quadruplets lower the chance floor — more power per item if the model is confident.

The precision cost you flagged: of the 2-way hits that were low-confidence (gap<1), only 16/52 survive the 4-way test; of the high-confidence hits (gap≥1), 80/119 survive. So quadruplets do bleed the marginal, low-confidence signal — exactly the items where contamination is subtle. Use them for strong leaks, not for the borderline ones.

5 · Overall verdict

Under the strictest test — cue-free, post-cutoff-specific answers, length-matched, DeepSeek-gated — no category, including US politics, shows statistically significant knowledge of post-cutoff facts. Every category sits at chance (28–56%; none significant), and that holds for every answer type (post-1929 names, coined terms, and dates all ≈ 44–49%).

But shallow leakage is real. The airtight A/B lifts post-1929 people from 38% (no cue) to 55% (first-name cue): the model has seen some post-cutoff name-strings (it can complete ‘John F. → Kennedy’), it just doesn’t know the facts. So the contamination is shallow name/term exposure, not coherent knowledge.

This revises the earlier headline. ‘US politics 68–89% contaminated’ was the length-biased metric (the 89%) plus first-name cues and item mix (the 68%); strip them and coherent contamination falls to chance. Recovering the famous-old topics (Shostakovich, Churchill, Sartre via their post-cutoff facts) opened no new contaminated categories.

Caveat. n is modest per cell, so this is ‘no significant contamination detected’ — a strong upper bound on cleanliness at this power, not absolute proof; the A/B confirms a little exposure does exist. For the synthetic-respondent use this is good news: the models do not reliably know the future even where they have glimpsed a name.
