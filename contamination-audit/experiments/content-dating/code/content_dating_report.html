1. What we are establishing

Question: the production pipeline dates books from front matter only; how much post-1929 body content can a local model catch, at what false-alarm cost, with what hardware?

Answers. (1) On the hardest realistic positives — 1930s–40s ordinary authored books — no single model or ensemble reaches the recall-0.95 target at ≤10% false positives. (2) The practical frontier: a gpt-oss-20b + Qwen2.5-14B passage-agreement ensemble reaches recall 0.90 (contaminants) / 0.80 (1930s books) at 9% FP entirely on the 4090; the Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (3-bit, llama.cpp) is the strongest single judge — 0.92/0.92 at 18% FP, 0.89/0.78 at 6.5% — but runs 25× slower than gpt-oss on a 24 GB card. (3) The residual misses are books whose body text is genuinely undatable (archaic genres); their dates live in front matter, so body-reading complements the existing dater rather than replacing it.

3. Method, verbatim

Up to 16 random + 16 grep-directed passages per book at lengths {250, 500, 1000, 2000} chars; every passage judged by every model with this exact prompt (temperature 0, each model's own chat template):

Read this passage from a scanned book and judge: was its text WRITTEN before 1929, or after 1929? Old, foreign-language, poetic, or historical writing is BEFORE. Modern prose, a post-1929 event/person/technology, or a modern perspective is AFTER. Ignore OCR noise. Passage:\n"""{p}"""\nAnswer with only one word: BEFORE or AFTER.

Book flagged when ≥k of the first N passage verdicts say AFTER. Ensembles combine models at the passage level (passage counts as AFTER when ≥m member models agree) or at the book level (all members must flag).

5. Why nothing reaches 0.95 — the misses, verbatim

1947 — devotional poetry, copyright 1947 L. A. Nichols. Sampled body text:
“thy shrine; How sweetly dost thou show thy face; When thou dost own me thine; O Father, let me praise thy name In great humility; Although my frame is only dust, I want to worship thee.”
1940 — county local history, copyright 1940, quoting an 1811 newspaper. Sampled body text:
“innati Gazette remarked: The steamboat New Orleans came to anchor before this port on Monday last. This is a large and handsome vessel, burthen 350 tons, intended for the trade of the Mississippi”
1947 — GPO taxonomy monograph, printed 1947. Sampled body text:
“manuscript as Oligometra concinna. Upon reviewing the subject more carefully I found that this was at best only an average difference and that no definite line between O. pulchella and O. coneinna could be drawn”

Hand-checked misses are genuine post-1929 editions (labels certain) whose sampled body carries no date signal: hymns, archaic verse, timeless monographs, antiquarian histories quoting old sources. Every checked miss has its date in front matter, which the production dater already reads. The irreducible class = archaic content + wrong-old metadata + no imprint — exactly the got-throughs (best configs catch 0–1 of 3).

7. The production pipeline and its price (added v2.2)

The validated three-stage architecture (authoritative spec: PIPELINE.md): Stage 1 paperwork rule (catalogue year + Guohui's Qwen3-14B doc-year + imprint regex; free, catches ~100% of honest-metadata contamination; flagged reprints routed to the gpt-oss editorial pass — modern apparatus out, clean re-sets rescued) → Stage 2 reader team on survivors (35B ≥2/8 OR gpt-oss-20b ≥3/8 on 8×2000-char random passages; OOS-validated rescue 0.875 of wrong-metadata contamination at 13.2% clean loss) → Stage 3 exhaustive word-boundary grep (markers_1945 + 447 null-corpus-validated 1929 terms + year-in-context sieve) with quote-the-proving-phrase adjudication of hit contexts (+30–40% of the readers' leftovers on honest-body books, ~0.5% extra loss).

Upgrade round (2026-07-24), two results: (a) reader scaling is exhausted — gpt-oss-120b ties the 35B on contaminants but runs 5 points WORSE on the binding 1930s stratum at matched FP and 4× slower on identical hardware ($6.69 eval; teams a wash); (b) the 1929-cutoff marker list (447 terms, each validated zero-hit across 1,361 certified pre-1929 books — a test that rejected hand-curated "new deal", "nazi", "stalin" as ordinary pre-1929 English) lifts stage-3 rescue on missed 1930s books from 27% to 39%; its mined stylistic discriminators ("basically", "contacted", "phoned", "sponsored") are strong screens but weak single-word evidence — the quote-the-proving-phrase adjudicator rightly declines to condemn on one common word (disguised plants 1/16 despite 69% hit rate).

8. Caveats (all parameters provisional)

  • Llama-3.2-1B's "perfect recall" is degenerate (answers AFTER to ~everything, 99.9% FP) — recall without an FP bound is meaningless.
  • Certifier asymmetry: all 81 negative-review flags were Llama-70B-only (deepseek 0); spot-checks read as over-caution (review_queue_negatives.csv).
  • 35B numbers: subsample, one config, different runtime (llama.cpp); confirm on rented hardware before production use.
  • Single prompt (untuned), single corpus family; passages avoid the first/last 3–5% of each book by design.
