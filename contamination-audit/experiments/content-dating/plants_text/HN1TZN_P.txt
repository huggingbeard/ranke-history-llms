World Peace 

If civilization is to be spared the horrors of World War III, it will be because of the application of knowledge of the basis of peace. 

After all is said and done, permanent peace will become possible only when the nations of the world recognize the eternal principles annunciated in the 

American Declaration of Independence that "all men are created equal; that they are endowed by their Creator with certain inalienable rights; that among these are life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness."

True peace cannot be based on the opinions of men.

Man's Inalienable Rights 

Are Secured Only to the Extent He Retains Control of Governmental Purse Strings 

INCE "man is endowed by his creator with certain inalienable rights, among which are life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness," just when did he surrender the right to rule himself? As a matter of fact man has not surrendered this right, nor could he if he so wished. What has happened is that lacking knowledge of what their rights consist, the masses of mankind have been unable to prevent a few men from exercising the privilege of directing their lives. 

Nature never intended any man to arrogate control over another man nor any set of men to assume control over another set of men. That the few control the destinies of the many is at bottom the cause of most of the miseries, trials and tribulations with which man the world over is afflicted. Even the few, the comparatively few, who hold in their hands the fate of the many are eventually engulfed by the tide of disaster their rule has evoked.

Man is a gregarious creature, therefore, men dwell in association. 

It is then that their inalienable right to life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness becomes subject to challenge by the unscrupulous. Individual members of a community cannot well protect the rights with which their creator has endowed them, so they form a local government, the sole function of which is to do for the individuals of which it is composed those things they cannot do for themselves.

A local government so formed is the agent, not the master, of its citizens, from whom it derives authority. In thus forming a government to safeguard their interests, individuals surrender none of their natural rights; they justly secure them. To safeguard individual rights should be the sole function of all government, regardless of how crude or how elaborate its form.

For the reason that there are some things individuals cannot do through local governments, state governments are organized, but in thus setting up a state unit, individuals instead of surrendering any of their natural rights more firmly secure them. State governments are only the agents of local governments, from which they derive their just powers, or what little power they should by right possess. There is no 1

more occasion for vesting state governments with power over local governments than there is to vest local governments with power to control the affairs of individuals. The sole function of any government is to serve, not master, its citizens.

There are some things individuals cannot do for themselves through their local and state governments, therefore a federal government, deriving its just powers from the states, for which it is the agent, is established. The federal government, no more than state and local governments should be, nor is it, clothed with power to order, to direct and control the affairs of citizens. 

To those who observe, the source of the power the federal government wields like a huge club over private enterprise is obvious. Clothed with the power to raise revenue is the secret of the government's strangle hold, its death-like grip, on every form of industry. Such power is unnatural, unnecessary and is destructive of the inalienable right of individuals to life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness.

Governments should be projected from the bottom upward, not from the top down. Unless based on a solid foundation, the superstructure cannot endure.

Government in the United States resembles nothing so much as an inverted pyramid. We have made a huge super-unit of the federal government, the weight of which is gradually, but none the less surely, crushing state and local governments.

This process is known as "centralizing government"-centralizing all power in the federal government. 

Such a system can have but one ending, an ending we are rapidly ap proaching totalitarianism. 

How are we to curb the power of this giant octopus whose tentacles are ever sweeping outward, grasping and throttling all within their reach? 

Keeping in mind that the secret of the federal government's autocratic rule rests in its vested power of raising revenue by confiscating private property under the guise of taxation, the solution of the problem is in reality a simple matter. 

All we need do to put the federal government in its proper place, so far as its power to interfere with free enterprise is concerned, to confine state governments to their delegated functions and to secure the rights of individuals through local governments is to finance government with its own earnings-Volmatic Revenue. 

To enjoy the benefits of government, individuals must reside and carry on their activities within the jurisdiction of local governments, for which privilege they willingly and voluntarily pay all the facilities, services and other advantages of government are worth. 

Volmatic Revenue arises only at sites where government facilities and services are used, local administrators publish the schedules, payments of

which are made to local authorities, thus positively subjecting all government finances to local control. 

Since state and federal governments are but creatures of local governments-agencies set up by individuals to secure their rights-what could be more in order than that the two former, through their budgets, should be entirely dependent on the latter for funds to finance their activities?

The result of such a fundamental method of financing government with its own earnings instead of with taxes would be to confine the federal government to its sole function of maintaining law and order where individuals, through local and state governments, are unable to do so for themselves. Under such a system of financing, the federal government could not possibly engage in huge spending projects, mostly "pork barrel" communistic schemes, (see agencies listed on page 26), with the funds of its citizens. Since all funds would be under control of local governments, they would not think of financing a federal or state budget for other than strictly legitimate purposes.

Denied the privilege it now enjoys of financing its activities, the federal government would have no occasion to meddle with private affairs nor to in any manner interfere with free enterprise. The functions of the federal government when confined to its legitimate sphere are not numerous nor its financial requirements anything near what they apparently are today.

THE TERM "taxation," according to Webster's dictionary, covers every conceivable exaction within the power of government to impose on its citizens. In his day these exactions were known by such commonplace names as rates, assessments, duties, imposts, excises, licenses, fees, tolls, etc., etc., but exactions nevertheless. 

But Webster lived many years ago and measured by modern standards would be considered a back number.

By the imposition of these and other exactions government attempts to finance its activities. That the plan has not worked satisfactorily we have but to note the unbalanced budget and the huge deficits that have grown to billions during the past 

12 or 15 years. As a means of financing government taxation has not only proved a dismal failure but has brought the United States and its citizens to the brink of disaster.

The multiplicity of difficulties which are today confronting the people of the civilized world, such as restriction of trade between nations, conflicts between labor and capital, low wages and low returns on capital investments, strikes and lockouts, race riots, unemployment, periodical depressions, are the results of taxation that brought on

Volmatic Revenue The Financing of Government With Its Own Earnings, Value For Value Received 

GOVERNMENT OF free men is an association of citizens organized for the purpose of providing facilities, services and other advantages they cannot well provide individually.

To this end government issues title to land and other natural resources such as oil, coal, iron, copper and all other mineral deposits, water, waterpower rights, forest, agricultural and urban lands. 

To make possible access without trespass to land and other resources to which it has granted title, government builds and maintains highways, streets, alleys and bridges.

Government does more; it provides postal service and public schools, health and fire departments. 

These are known as direct services, in addition to which government provides its citizens with indirect facilities and services when it grants franchises to corporations which furnish transportation, communication and power, such as railroads, street cars and bus lines, telephones, telegraph, gas and electric services. 

That citizens may be assured exclusive and unmolested use of government facilities at sites to which they hold title, government maintains law and order, administered by means of courts, police departments, the army, navy and air forces. It is the maintenance of law and order that protects producers in the use of natural resources and makes possible the exchange of wealth. Production and exchange can be carried on only where law and order prevail. 

The price users pay for these facilities, services and other advantages of government is commonly designated as "rent," "ground rent, "land rent" and "economic rent," terms which are confusing and incorrect, for which reason the term Volmatic Revenue has been adopted as better suited for the purpose intended. (The term Volmatic is derived from two words-voluntary and automatic).

Rent as defined in the dictionary is periodical payment for the use of

property, whereas Volmatic Revenue is payment for facilities, services and other advantages provided and made available by government and not for the use of land where they are utilized. 

Financing government with Vol matic Revenue, which is always paid voluntarily and arises automatically, increasing with an increased use of government facilities and services and decreasing as their use decreases, establishes equal rights and terms to the use of all natural resources such as gas, coal, iron, copper and all other mineral deposits, water, waterpower rights, forests, agricultural and urban lands, and would be the first real improvement in government the world has known. 

Gas ranges and electric appliances are among the greatest blessings enjoyed by man. Marvelous and useful as are these inventions, they have in themselves no value other than the materials from which they are constructed. It is gas and electric service that gives them utility; deprived of these services the gas range and electric motor are useless for all practical purposes and constitute just so much scrap material.

Land, regardless of how well situated or other natural advantages one tract may have over another as to fertility or other inherent qualities, has of itself no utility in an exchange economy.

It is facilities, services and other advantages of government that gives land utility in an exchange economy, without which land, irrespective of what may be its natural advantages, cannot be used in the production of wealth for exchange.

It will be readily apparent that it is facilities and services, not the use of land, for which Volmatic.

Revenue is payment at any location. 

To make this important and far reaching change in our method of raising government revenue, the legal requirements are really simple, no constitution standing in the way, nor new laws being necessary to put it (Volmatic Revenue) into effect. 

Inasmuch as there are no laws preventing individuals and corporations from demanding payment in full for services rendered by them, neither are there any laws preventing government from collecting in full for services rendered its citizens. 

Volmatic Revenue is not a charge against citizens; it is a rate established for the use of facilities and services rendered by government at given locations.

Whenever public sentiment demands its adoption, Volmatic Revenue can be put into operation without the necessity of a constitutional amendment, either federal or state, by the enactment of federal, state and local budget systems, as outlined in Volmatic Revenue Applied.

Volmatic Revenue Applied Budgeting the Financing of Government-No Taxes or Forced Payments-Value For Value Received 

INCE the Federal Government is over two hundred billion dollars in debt and is rapidly assuming additional billions of obligations, it is high time a sound method of financing government, federal, state and local, be inaugurated.

If a private enterprise were conducted on a plan whereby its financial obligations instead of being met when due become greater and greater, we may be sure the day of its bankruptcy would be only a question of time.

It is conceivable and permissible of any organization that its obligations during abnormal times may on occasions exceed its income, but when this situation becomes a continual process, with no hope of improvement, stockholders see to it that such business practices are corrected or the affairs of the concern are liquidated.

Under the circumstances it would seem time that government in the United States-federal, state and local-adopt sound business principles in financing its activities. Such principles require that all financial transactions by government with its citizens and with all others be on a voluntary basis of value for value received, to-wit: 

1. The federal government should make up its budget and submit it to the 48 states, upon whose approval the total should be pro rated among them on the basis of their populations and made a public document. 

2. Each state in turn should make up its budget, including the amount required for the federal budget, and submit it to its local divisions of government for approval. The total of the approved budget should be pro rated among local revenue divisions on the basis of their populations. 

3. The local revenue division in making up its budget would include its pro rata of the state's budget, apportioning the total amount to site locations (irrespective of improvements) in accordance with the value of facilities, services and other advantages provided and made available by government. 

4. It would be the duty of the local revenue districts to publish the 10

Volmatic rate of all sites and place them on file for public inspection and information on or before December 1, 194_. 

5. The local, state and federal governments should serve advance notice that on and after January 1, 194_, no more revenue would be raised by taxation. (See page 4.) 

On this date the Volmatic rates announced by the local body become effective and payable quarterly in advance. 

6. The amount the local division receives from the published rates constitutes the fund from which the state's pro rata must be met, the balance being used to meet the local budget. 

7. From funds received from local divisions the states meet their pro rata of the federal budget, the remaining sum being used to finance. their activities. 

8. From funds received from the 

48 states the federal budget would be financed. 

9. Local divisions would publish quarterly and place on file a revised list of rates, supplemented by maps, indicating sites (see page 13), where services are not used and rates are unpaid. Thus the rate for government services at any site, whether a city lot or a large rural tract, mineral or forest lands or water-power sites, would be a matter of public record and on file at the courthouse of the county in which situated. 

10. Those desiring to engage in any enterprise, such as building a house, becoming a farmer, a merchant, a manufacturer, etc., etc., could easily ascertain the site available which would suit their requirements and file for possession and title, accompanied with a deposit to the amount of the quarterly Volmatic rate. 

11. Upon selection of a site by a prospective user, the title holder is notified that the records show the rate on his site has not been paid and that unless he desires to use the service and pays the Volmatic rate in advance, title will be transferred. 

12. It should be understood that title holders could not be dispossessed of their sites so long as they pay the Volmatic Revenue rates. 

13. The status of both government and privately operated utilities remain as of today.

EXPLANATION 

Determining an equitable rate for use of facilities, services and other advantages provided and made available by government is by no means a complicated process nor a matter 1 of guess work. The fact is that once government is financed with Volmatic Revenue, equitable rates would automatically determine themselves.

So true is this that should the rate 11

administration attempt to fix an arbitrary rate, it would encounter unsurmountable obstacles.

Should rates as published in any location prove too high, or more than the facilities, services and other advantages provided and made available by government are worth, citizens would be reluctant to accept them and a gradual diminishing of activities in those locations would be manifested, necessitating a readjust ment of rates. 

Should rates as published prove too low, citizens would make this fact known by taking advantage of the opportunity afforded to get government services at less than market value. Thus competition for any site where the published rate is not equal to the value of government services available would clearly indicate the rate is too low and must be increased to enable government to maintain and finance its standard of facilities and services. Should the rate be increased in excess of the value of facilities and services, citizens would be slow in accepting it and, as explained in the above paragraph, activity in the vicinity would diminish. 

Furthermore, should rates in any section, urban or rural, prove too high, this fact would be manifested in a decreased demand for sites in such section and owners seeking to dispose of their improvements would be obliged to do so at a discount from their true value. 

Likewise, should rates prove too low, demand for locations would increase, making it possible for owners to dispose of improvements at a premium.

The purchaser of government facilities, services and other advantages would be the court of last resort. 

Regardless of rates fixed by the rating body, the final decision as to their acceptance would rest with the purchaser. 12

Taxes Breed Depression and War 

Unemployment, industrial and commercial stagnation originate through failure of government to live on its own earnings, this vast fund falling into the hands of a small percentage of the population, giving them enormous purchasing power, while their consuming desire is relatively small. 

Regardless of what any man's income may be, he can eat only three meals a day, live in only one house, wear only one suit of clothes and ride in only one automobile at a time. Meanwhile the masses have consuming desire but lack equivalent purchasing power.

Where Jobs Come From Not From the Stock Exchange Nor Employers But 

Mother Nature Herself

HEN Robinson Crusoe was shipwrecked on his lonely island there was no government to look after his welfare or provide him with social security, and there was neither industry nor machinery to furnish him with the means of employment. Nothwithstanding these handicaps, Crusoe was the most employed man of his time. 

He required water, food, clothing, shelter and numerous other necessities. In fact there were hundreds of jobs awaiting him and only one man-himself to fill them.

The same conditions, the same opportunities of employment, which Crusoe encountered on his island would be available to the soldiers returning to the land for which they were willing to sacrifice their lives were government to live on its own earnings-Volmatic Revenue-and cease the baleful practice of confiscating the property of the soldiers and other citizens by means of taxation.

Natural resources and the products thereof, whether of an island inhabited only by one person or a densely populated area, are the sole sources of employment. If legal statutes favoring the few as against the many bar access of labor and capital to the sources of employment, there will be unemployment and all the miseries that flow therefrom. 

Who provided employment for the United States' most noted party of immigrants-the Pilgrims-when they landed on the bleak and barren shores of Massachusetts Bay? No one! Endless employment awaited every mother's son of them the instant they stepped from the deck of the Mayflower to the land of their adoption. What was the source of their employment? Good old Mother 

Earth-natural resources. 

Had the Pilgrims depended on industry or government to provide them with employment, they would have starved to death. As it was they employed themselves in the only way men are ever employed; they applied their labor to natural resourcesland and its products. 

We haven't run out of land. The same land that provided employment for the Pilgrims is still here, and will be when the soldiers return. The land that provided employment for those hardy pioneers and those who came after them is just as fertile and, 16

due to machinery and improved processes of production, is capable of providing far more employment than in 1620.

The United States contains so much land-natural resources-and so little of it is in use there are ample means of employing all the returning soldiers at wages that will support them and their dependents in ease and luxury.

It is held the United States will be faced with a tremendous problem when her soldiers return from foreign countries. Perhaps so, but the problem will not be one of finding means of employing them, nature having made provisions to this end eons ago.

The problem is going to be, as it always has been, one of inducing those who lay claim to natural resources as their exclusive property to get out of the way and let the soldiers and others who care to do so go to work.

When the problem of prying title holders loose from the privilege of monopolizing the earth and charging others for its use has been solved, there will be so much employment available the job will look for the man, not the man for the job. With access to the sources of employment (natural resources) open to all upon payment of the prevailing Volmatic Revenue rate, the future of the returning soldiers would be free from want and the haunting fear of want. 

Of course, so long as we persist in maintaining conditions that bar labor from the source of employment, except on terms imposed by holders of title to natural resources, jobs will be scarce and the returning soldiers will find themselves virtual slaves in the land for the freedom of which they faced death on foreign battlefields.

Were we to display sufficient intelligence to remove the obstacles that prevent workers from applying their labor to the earth that nature intended for the use of all men on equal terms, the United States would prove a paradise to the returning soldiers instead of a land so heedless of the rights of its citizens that even the brave defenders of its socalled freedom are hard put to find means of employment for their labor. 

When it is considered that nature has provided unlimited opportunities of employment for all people whom at any time may occupy the earth, it is a sad commentary on our boasted civilization that those who bear arms in its defense experience difficulty in gaining a livelihood. The ridiculousness of this situation is readily apparent when it is realized there is positively no danger of running out of jobs until we run out of land, the source of all employment. 17

Crusoe Didn't Have $5,000 

Henry Ford and other industrialists put forth the claim that it requires a $5,000 or greater capital investment for every man they put to work. Possibly so, but it is interesting to reflect that Robinson Crusoe didn't have $5,000 when he landed on his uninhabited island, yet he was the most employed man of his time. There were so many jobs at his disposal he could never hope to fill the major part of them. 

No man can provide a job for another. All jobs come from individual desire, and if not interfered with by law, there are jobs for all who seek them. 18

Islands Seek Crusoes Vast Idle Resources Are But Islands Bidding a Welcome to Returning Soldiers

BELIEF held all but universally is that men must be provided with work-by other men. So widespread is this belief that organizations are being formed in various sections of the nation with the object of finding jobs for the returning soldiers. With the same idea in view, vast government projects calculated to furnish work for thousands of ex-service men are in the planning stage and private industry is being canvassed in behalf of postwar employment for United States combat forces when they shall have returned to home shores.

These work-providing schemes are evidence of a lack of knowledge as to the source of employment. If those who are promoting them were at all familiar with the process by which men employ their labor, they would pursue an entirely different course than seeking jobs for the returning fighters.

When Robinson Crusoe was stranded on his tropical island, there was no one to provide him with work nor a government to do for him those things he could not well do for himself and there was neither machinery nor capital to assist him in the task of producing the means to gratify his wants, yet Crusoe was the most employed man of his time.

He wanted water, food, clothing, a house, a boat, then a better house and a larger boat. In fact Crusoe's wants created such a demand for his labor he couldn't begin to fill all the jobs that were his for the taking. 

What was the source of Crusoe's jobs? The spring from which he obtained his water; the forest from which he felled the trees that supplied the logs for his house; the goats that furnished him with meat and clothes had to be hunted, dressed and their hides tanned and fashioned into clothes; gathering berries and wild fruit with which the island teemed was an endless source of employment and the planting, cultivating and harvesting of his corn crop, to say nothing of grinding the grain into meal, were of themselves no small tasks. So long as his welfare demanded these things and so long as their source the island-was at his disposal, there was no possibility of 

Crusoe running out of employment.

So it would be with the returning soldiers were we to finance government with Volmatic Revenue instead 19

of with taxes. Each soldier would represent a Robinson Crusoe and each parcel of land where government facilities and services were not used, as shown by rate map (Page 

13), would represent a virgin island to be had by the mere paying of the 

Volmatic rate. 

There are so many "islands," "islands" suitable for every conceivable purpose, available to the soldiers they would be at their wits' ends to know which to select. Here would be jobs, unlimited jobs, for every mother's son of them, all to be had by simply complying with a few legal requirements. Contrast this picture with the numerous schemes on foot to find jobs for the returning soldiers!

In addition to the choice of jobs to be had for the taking, the workers would have the benefit of facilities, services and other advantages provided and made available by government, none of which Crusoe enjoyed, and in spite of which he produced plenty for all his wants, and to spare.

Moreover, this returned army of workers will want products of every description-houses and better houses, automobiles, radios, refrigerators, electric and gas ranges, motor launches, etc., all of which will create a tremendous demand for labor and capital. And where is the labor coming from to supply the demand for the products of industry?

Since human desire is always greater than ability to produce, with free access to Crusoe islands there will be more jobs than men to fill them. 

This is the natural order and the demand of individuals for wealth with which to gratify their desires is the source of all jobs. Until all human demand is gratified, there is no possibility of a dearth of jobs developing. 

If this is the natural order, why is a shortage of jobs contemplated following the close of the war and the return of the soldiers to the home front? Simply because the natural order has been frustrated by a revenue system that takes from the producer an ever increasing share of his product, which constitutes his purchasing power. Without consumers' purchasing power there cannot be demand for the products of industry, and to the degree demand ceases, to that degree also is there unemployment-and a search for jobs for the returning soldiers.

All we need do to convert every site where government facilities and services are not used into an island beckoning for Crusoes to utilize their resources, to provide unlimited employment for millions of workers seeking to gratify the demand for the products of industry, to make it possible for every man to own tax-free home, to have luxuries in abundance for himself and dependents and to secure his inalienable right to life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness, is to so alter our method of financing government that no one will care to hold title to the 20

millions of available acres for other than domestic, industrial and commercial purposes. Financing gov ernment with Volmatic Revenue in stead of with taxes will accomplish this end.

When we consider that the source -natural resources—from which all employment originates is so vast, varied and inexhaustible, the absurdity of the numerous plans to secure jobs for returning soldiers becomes readily apparent. It is to be expected that men denied access to the source of employment will experience difficulty in obtaining jobs. 

Under the circumstances it would seem that the sensible thing to do is to strive for removal of the obstacle that stands like a stone wall between workers and employment rather than to seek jobs for the nation's gallant defenders. 

The ugly truth is that returning soldiers are denied the right to use the island (the United States) for which they were willing to sacrifice their lives, hence they become objects of charity in that they must be "given" jobs or perish from the earth. 21

Victory Gardens Volmatic Revenue Creates Permanent Victory Gardens, the Source of All Employment 

LTHOUGH few realize it, victory 

A gardens are the key to the solution of the numerous problems with which the world is confronted. Not alone that but full play of the victory garden principle is the direct means of restoring to man his inalienable right to life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness, of which he has been robbed by the process which centralizes government in the hands of the few.

It will no doubt come as a surprise to many to learn that everything which in any way gratifies human desire, from a pinch of salt to a sumptuous feast, from a hut in the wilderness to a mansion on Fifth

Avenue, is a victory garden product. 

Thus it is the coal that cooks our food and heats our homes, the gas that propels the automobile, the cement that paves the highway and the couch on which we rest our weary bones, in the final analysis are but the harvests of victory gardens.

Mineral deposits, forests and fields are but uncultivated victory garden sites awaiting the magic touch of the worker to give forth their yields in abundance.
