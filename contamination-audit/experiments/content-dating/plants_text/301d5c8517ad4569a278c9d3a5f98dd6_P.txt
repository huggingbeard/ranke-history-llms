EAST OF THE FOURTH PRINCIPAL MERIDIAN EACH COLUMN OF SQUARES IS NUMBERED 1-2-3-4 ETC, EAST. WEST OF THE MERIDIAN THEY ARE CALLED 1-2-3-4 ETC, WEST. THUS A TOWNSHIP BECOMES T. —N. R. —E.OR W. AS THE CASE MAY BE. THESE TOWNSHIP BOUNDARIES ARE FIXED GEOGRAPHICAL POSITIONS AND CAN NOT BE CHANGED. THEY SHOULD NOT BE CONFUSED WITH TOWN BOUNDARIES WHICH ARE THE BOUNDARY LINES OF GOVERNMENTAL UNITS AND BY LAW CAN BE CHANGED. A TOWN MAY BE ANYTHING FROM A FRACTION OF A TOWNSHIP TO SEVERAL TOWNSHIPS.
THE MAP ON THE COVER PAGE SHOWS THE TOWNSHIP AND TOWN BOUNDARIES, THEIR RELATIONSHIP TO EACH OTHER, AND TO THE COUNTY AS A WHOLE.
THESE COVER MAPS ARE OF TOWNSHIPS NOT TOWNS AND ARE ASSEMBLED IN THIS PLATBOOK NUMERICALLY, AND PAGE NUMBERS GIVEN AS INDICATED ON THE MAP SHOWN ON THE COVER.
TO SHOW ALL LAND COVER, IMPROVEMENTS. ETC., ON THE LAND THE SCALE CAN NOT BE REDUCED BELOW ONE MILE TO THE INCH, THEREFORE, IT IS IMPRACTICAL TO PRINT THESE MAPS IN UNITS LARGER THAN TOWNSHIPS.
IN ORDER TO GIVE A CLEAR PICTURE OF THE TYPES OF FOREST INDICATED BY CERTAIN SYMBOLS SUCH AS AI-BI - CI, ETC. -ONE MUST REALIZE THERE IS SELDOM A PURE STAND OF ANY ONE KIND OF TIMBER. HOWEVER, THERE ARE TYPES OF FORESTS SUCH AS THE HEMLOCK - HARDWOOD (A2), THE DOMINANT TIMBER BEING HEMLOCK, WITH HARDWOODS SCATTERED THROUGH THE STAND; THE OAK - HICKORY TYPE (DI), OAK AND HICKORY PREDOMINATE. WITH POSSIBLY SOME OTHER TREES APPEARING IN LESSER NUMBER.
THE TIMBER SIZE SHOWN ON THE MAPS ARE AVERAGE SIZE. FOR EXAMPLE: FOLLOWING A TYPE SYMBOL SUCH AS "AI" THERE APPEARS FIGURES SUCH AS 6-12, ETC., WHICH MEANS THE AVERAGE DIAMETER OF THE TIMBER IS SOMEWHERE BETWEEN 6 AND 12 INCHES. IT DOES NOT MEAN THERE ARE NO TREES SMALLER THAN 6 INCHES OR LARGER THAN 12 INCHES. THEY MAY RUN IN SIZE FROM I INCH TO 18 INCHES OR LARGER, BUT THE AVERAGE SIZE IS SOME WHERE BETWEEN 6 INCHES AND 12 INCHES.
THE DENSITY OF THE TIMBER STAND IS INDICATED BY THE NUMBER OF LINES DRAWN UNDER THE SIZE CLASS FIGURES, SUCH AS (3-0) ONE LINE INDICATING A FULL STAND OF TIMBER. TWO LINES (2) MEANS A MEDIUM STAND-THREE LINES A POOR STAND. AND FOUR LINES SCATTERED TREES. A FULL STAND OF TIMBER IS ONE WHICH CASTS A SOLID SHADOW, A MEDIUM STAND ABOUT ONE HALF OF THE GROUND IS SHADE, WHILE A POOR STAND SHADES ABOUT ONE FOURTH OF THE GROUND.
THE CROSS-HATCHED AREAS (/) OF THE MAPS HAVE A LOWLAND COVER SUCH AS MARSH GRASS, SEDGE, CAT-TAIL, OR LOWLAND FOREST. IT DOES NOT MEAN THAT IT IS ALL SWAMP LAND, OR THAT ALL LOW LAND IS CROSS-HATCHED.
THE DATA USED IN THE DEVELOPMENT OF THESE MAPS WERE TAKEN FROM THE ORIGINAL SURVEY PLATS AND FIELD NOTES, AERIAL PHOTOGRAPHS AND THE LAND INVENTORY FIELD SHEETS.
THE FIELD SHEETS WERE MADE BY MAPPERS AS THEY WALKED BACK AND FORTH ACROSS EACH SECTION OF LAND AT QUARTER MILE INTERVALS.
THE TOWNSHIPS AND SECTIONS ARE NOT DRAWN SQUARE AS THEY ARE THEORETICALLY SUPPOSED TO BE, INSTEAD THEY ARE DRAWN JUST AS THEY WERE ORIGINALLY SURVEYED.
ADDITIONAL COPIES OF THESE MAPS MAY BE HAD FREE OF CHARGE, AND BLACK AND WHITE PRINTS MADE TO A SCALE OF FOUR INCHES TO THE MILE MAY BE HAD FOR THE COST OF PRINTING (ABOUT TWENTY FIVE CENTS PER TOWNSHIP) BY WRITING THIS DEPARTMENT.
