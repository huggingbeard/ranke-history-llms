II 

We toiled down in your mines from early morn to late at night, Like gnomes we slaved our lives away and never saw the light; 

We built your towers high, we forged and wrought their binding steel,

We bore your burdens and we put our shoulders to the wheel.

PAGE 

III 

And we were just as you are now, we had our hopes, our dreams,

It was another land, another age, so long ago that seems;

But ragged now we walk as sheep walk in a butcher's pen, Like poor dumb sheep we walk and once like you they called us men.

IV 

They called us men, those were the days we walked with heads so high, But of those times we never think for if we think we die; 

We walk and walk, and walk the more, escaping so from thought; 

Forgetfulness we seek with tears and woe are memories fraught.

PAGE 

V We stood beside the lathe and press, we labored in the shop, We knew our trades but knew we not what made the wheels to stop;

No more the wheels are turning in a thousand silent plants,

But here outside we swarm and teem upon the streets like ants.

VI 

Like ants outside we crawl, like ants outside their hill we creep,

We creep and crawl and in the night we hear our women weep;

Through nights they sob, through days we seek for that we shall not find, For well we know to such as we the eyes of God are blind.

PAGE VII LILLARY 

And we, we know too well, as all the day we walk the street, That only death shall halt the ceaseless tatoo of our feet;

So aimless are our steps, we know not why we go nor where, Yet walk we on without a goal, to stop we do not dare.

VIII 

We cannot stop, we cannot stay, we must go ever on 

To beat our minds and thoughts into fatigue's oblivion;

If we could die as die we ought and retrograde to dust, For us what better fate, but even we for life have lust.

PAGE IX 

The fields are fertile and so rich we cannot understand 

Why misery and want must stalk like ghosts across the land; 

They drink our blood and eat our flesh, yet never are they filled, They eat our flesh and drink our blood, and all our hopes they've killed

Like us, in vain, our daughters and our sisters walked the street, At night from doorways dark, the lonely stranger now they greet;

They wear the badge of shame, and of their flesh for gold they give, And those who point the scornful finger know not how they live.

PAGE 

XI There is a mart where flesh is sold, there is a badge of shame, Yet who shall say to only them belongs the scarlet blame; 

Above the shadowed door the lamp is gleaming sickly red, Ay, crimson is its flame for with their blood its light is fed.

XII 

If destiny decreed such things should be, I cannot say,

But this I know, when on our knees we fall we cannot pray, We cannot pray to One who lets his children thusly live, To such a One what thanks is there for such as we to give?

PAGE 

XIII Somewhere down in those streets youth died and hope to ashes turned,

The brand of want upon our brows its horrid symbol burned; 

Down there sweet laughter lost its way and woe and sorrow came

To change us to case-numbers, we who once had owned a name.

XIV 

We stand in line and cringe and whine to fill our guts with bread, Like beaten slaves in line we stand, the economic dead!

Our clothes are patched and ripped and torn, our shoes have holes all through, But still in line we stand to be reviled by such as you.

PAGE XV 

The scarecrows on parade are we, we stand for you to jeer,

But day by day our line swift grows, perhaps you'll soon be here;

The soup is thin, the bread is old, but what can beggars say?

This is the price that those who live with poverty must pay.

XVI 

Like hungry rats in cellars and in garrets, there we live, And grateful masters are we for the filling food you give;

It makes us lose our teeth but masters, we lose not our claws, And up we'll rise some day and rasp the tablets of your laws.

PAGE XVII 

Behind fine restaurants and fancy food and bakery shops,

At evening some of us retrieve a supper from the slops; 

And root through garbage, fight on filthy heaps like hungry swine, While on a snowy-linened table others richly dine.

XVIII 

We walk the darkened street, gaze in where all's ablaze with light, Where happy laughter rings, some debutante is "coming out" tonight;

Gaze through the bars like creatures from another world and stare, But in the doorways dark some others's daughter beckons there.

PAGE XIX In patches and in tatters, and with shoes run down at heel,

We tramp about and all we lack's the mark of white hot steel; 

And some of us are dumb like beasts but some are still like men, These raise their gaunt and hairy hands and ask the question, "When?"

XX Upon the curb we sit and watch the shining cars pass by, Like birds of steel that swift as arrows wing, they speed and fly;

We watch the folk pass by and we one time were just as they;

We owned our homes, we had our cars, but where are they today?

PAGE XXI We ask for work. It's W.P.A. "Fill out the form", Then for appointments wait like hungry wolves out in the storm;

We answer questions by the score. "Your name? Where were you born? 

Direct relief or not?". They herd us in like beasts forlorn.

XXII 

They tear our privacy from us, they pry into our lives,

They lay us on their desks and vivisect us with their knives; 

Once on "relief" they feed us from caloric charts like swine, And carefully, oh carefully, they watch the scurvy line.

PAGE XXIII They take us in the mass, they write a treatise on our woes;

We're sociology to them but blood in us still flows;

We once were men like them, but we're the guinea pigs today;

They cut us with their scalpels, we who once were men as they.

XXIV 

"Forgotten men" are we, the "under privileged third", it's said, And through the land we walk like shades, the economic dead;

Outward we still are men, but look into our beady eyes, And tell, if tell you can, what dreadful phantom in them lies.

PAGE 

XXV Our veins still pulse, our hearts still beat, we taste, we breathe, we feel, But in our hearts we are like those o'er whom the vultures wheel; 

What if you gave us back our homes, the little that we earned; 

No salve can cure for us the scars that on our souls are burned. 

XXVI 

No man can live and know the things we know yet be the same, For things there are that tear the soul of man from out his frame, That pluck all tenderness and joy from out his choking breast, And in their place light flames of hate whose tongues give him no rest.

PAGE 

XXVII Down deep below the surface of the sea in slime and ooze,

There dwell the sightless monsters, but no sight was their's to lose; 

Down deep below the surface of the economic sea,

The thirteen million squirm and slide but once the light saw we.

XXVIII 

Could we forget, could memory no more twine about her coil, We too could happy squirm and crawl like worms beneath the soil;

They took material things from us, in place they gave us hate;

Poor things like us that crawl, we know not what to do but wait.

PAGE XXIX There is a great democracy in deed as well as name, And all must enter there, be they of royalty or fame;

The door is narrow, two by six, it looks upon the sky,

The grave it's called, there worms like us now wait the great that die.

XXX In times of bloody war men die so quickly and so fast,

In times of hunger souls soon die but bodies last and last; 

In war you're gone when bullets and the bursting shrapnel fly,

But on the street from month to month, from day to day you die.

PAGE XXXI It is not good to die from month to month, from year to year,

For if you take too long to die, for you there's shed no tear; 

The moribund should learn to quickly cease to gasp and breathe, To strongly stifle hope though up its leaping fires seethe.

XXXII 

Oh diletantes there are of life, and amateurs of hate,

But diletantes we're not who keen our claws and wait; 

Advice we get from those who sit on pedestals of gold, Their words are free and cheap as air but to their wealth they hold.

PAGE 

XXXIII 

By ballots and by votes they say that we can cure our woe, But they who speak such words are fools who cannot ever know; 

They cannot know that votes bring back not life when life has fled, They cannot know that though we breathe yet still we are the dead.

XXXIV 

The weak are wrong but right the strong; oh truly it is writ, And none know better that than we who live down in the pit; 

What lies are justice, ethics, those we know and know too well, We ones who know not yet the grave yet spend our lives in hell.

PAGE XXXV The only virtue's wealth, the only honor's dusky gold; 

They feed you in your need, they warm you when the world is cold, And never they betray, they stand beside you in your trial,

While those who walk the street have justice with them in their file.

XXXVI 

Some strive for fame, some seek a name, but only gold does rule; 

When calls its voice the wise man comes and comes the motleyed fool; 

It buys your roof, it buys your clothes, it buys your daily bread, And what that means they only know who once have not been fed.

PAGE XXXVII They only know who had no roof, whose clothes were patched and torn, No roof, no clothes, no bread, their absence make a man forlorn; 

He walks the street and curses he the day that he was born,

He walks and worn and weak is he, and too his soul is worn.

XXXVIII 

The noble constitution - wondrous is this famous thing, But what is it to us when hunger makes our heads to ring, When hunger makes our heads to ring, when we can walk no more, When fall we down, exhausted, whipped, on liberty's sweet shore?

PAGE XXXIX 

Oh Liberty! how famed are you in history and song,

Defending there the weak and putting down the prideful strong; 

But Liberty how weak are you among all men today; 

How weak, how sick, how worn and pale, you're spirit's fled away!

XL 

Oh Liberty! beneath your flag, today what crimes are done; 

How crimson are the sands that in the glass of time now run!

The legions of the hungry walk in armies through the land, And in their eyes are things that no sane man may understand.

PAGE 

XLI 

And often in the night we stand outside the lighted shops,

Gaze at the food, the bread and meat, and lick our hungry chops, At sausages on strings, on cuts of ham and rich red beef,

Then drift we on like flotsam that is broke upon the reef.

XLII 

Now sailors drown beneath the wave, yield up their final sigh,

But flotsam on the economic sea can drown but cannot die;

It cannot live, it cannot die, it floats upon the crest, And gales then buffet it and strike it with a fiendish zest.

PAGE 

XLIII 

It is not good to float a corpse upon the tossing wave, And hear the gulls above you scream, and gales in madness rave, To clasp the seaweed in your hand, to float until you rot, But so we float upon that sea such is our sorry lot.

XLIV 

But there are those who wing in great white planes across that sea; 

They smile and jest that such poor things like us can really be; 

Some fly by plane and some like us just float upon the wave,

But still the end of every odyssey remains the grave.

PAGE 

XLV 

We are the lost, we stumble through a blooming desert land, There milk and honey flow but not for us, a thirst-crazed band, There flowers bloom to crumble at our touch to burning dust,

Yet through those fields so fair, so strange, we walk because we must.

XLVI 

Upon the desert or the sea, we silent walk or float, And in our wake the demons of the past in madness gloat; 

Fair visions dance before our eyes, we grasp at them like fools, But once within our arms they change to mocking ghouls.

PAGE 

XLVII 

'Tis said to lands of dark there comes at times a pitying light,

But such gaunt things are here, it shrieks away in screaming flight;

It cannot bear the monsters of abysmal depths and pits, On wings of speed it darts away lest too it lose its wits.

XLVIII 

They cannot bear to look on us and we are men as they, Except we live in loathesome night, and these, they live in day;

They talk of us in tones so low, or talk they not at all;

We gaze up to their height and with our talons scratch the wall.

PAGE 

XLIX 

With hungry claws we scratch the prison walls and tear our hair, And in the pit there's naught but night and fear yet live we there, And some poor fools like me, they sit aside to pen some rhyme, Yet others wait for fate's decreed that each shall have his time.

They bare their fangs, unsheathe their claws, and wait and wait their day, And breed they more that these may wait, if death should take them 'way;

The pit grows fast in numbers, month by month and some will rue 

That those down in the depths are many - those on top but few.

PAGE LI 

It is not good to sleep in parks when winter's coming on,

It is not good to rise from frosted grass to greet the dawn, Yet there we watch the sun come up and see the sun go down, And then like creatures of the night we walk the murky town.

LII 

Yet it is good to hope, for hope men must if they'd stay men, Without their hope they change, so hope they must and hope again; 

One cannot see but yet they change to hungry things that wait, And truly it is writ, that those who no more hope - they hate.

PAGE 

LIII 

Hate runs in fire through their veins, it burns their every thought, And every moment of their time the hated ones are sought; 

They cannot see a women's smile, nor hear a child's sweet laugh; 

They throw aside the yellow grain, and treasure up the chaff.

LIV 

And some men hate because they will and some because they must, The last to live they hate, for in their breasts their hearts are dust,

For them no women smile, no children laugh for them to hear, And as the dying leaves of autumn so their hearts are sere.

PAGE 

LV 

Hope's flame burns in the breasts of most, but that in some has died, But life to live requires flame and will not be denied;

In some it's hope, in some it's hate, in some it's love or war, And every man must have a flame to light and warm his core.

LVI 

Though love will warm, yet hate will burn, and though it sears he breathes,

Though in his heart there is a hell whose fire smokes and seethes; 

And horrid want, and hunger gaunt, are skilled to feed that flame, And you who give them coals alone dare say that he's to blame.

PAGE 

LVII 

When friends are friends they clasp each others hand in weal or woe, But scoundrels let their comrades sink, and smile to see them go; 

And blood is blood and kin are kin, yet often these will smile 

To see their folksmen walk the street in hunger - ridden file. 

LVIII 

A good deed past remains not in an ingrates crooked mind, He has a debt to pay, a reason not to he will find;

Upon the heights he sits in pride, and snug and sleek, and so secure, His creditors must hungry walk - indignities endure.

PAGE 

LIX 

But debts there are beyond the pale and bourne of courts of law, And there are debts that only are repaid by blade and claw, And there are those who sleep so sound, and they are so secure, But there are those who never sleep and hate not hope's their lure.

LX 

I wonder if they see our eyes as burn they in the night, I wonder if white shadows walk in shrouds upon the height, I wonder if around them gaunt starved phantoms ever flit, I wonder if they ever think of us down in the pit.

PAGE 

LXI 

And we who live down in the pit, we have our laws, our code, Adhere to them we ever do though heavy is our load; "Forget no friend, no foe, no good nor evil deed, "And learn to hate, and with your hate your burning fires feed.

LXII 

"It is ordained that such as you should multiply and breed, "For sure the day will come, if not to you then to your seed; "Until your strength is gone and great your years you shall not die, "Your brothers of the pit may need your aid to climb on high.

PAGE LXIII "And think you shall - an empty stomache makes an active brain, "And wait you shall for stars of favoured ones to dim and wane, "And in the school of poverty you'll sharpen up your wit; "You must do these whom destiny has placed down in the pit."

LXIV 

It is not good to have no coat when gray fall days are cold, Nor good to pass the shops where fat Thanksgiving birds are sold, Nor is it good to homeless walk the diamond - crusted snow, When tapers of the Christmas trees begin to spark and glow.

PAGE LXV 

Nor see the earth reborn in spring when naught in you's reborn,

Nor good is it to see the summer come when you're forlorn;

It is not good to see the harvest of the bounteous fall,

When earth yields up her wealth to some, but none to you at all.

LXVI 

Nor good to dance on craters when lava's bubbling up, Nor is it well before the starved to richly dine and sup,

Nor is it good to be too fat when others are so drawn, For then a drum of thunder beats upon the morrow's dawn.

PAGE LXVII Now weak as women are the ones that grasp the nation's reins, Like greedy bats they suck the very blood from out our veins; 

They can't forget, they cannot learn, how stupid is their rule,

They give the scepter to the ape, high places to the fool.

LXVIII 

Enough! The tale is told, so judge from it how life goes on 

Down in pit of poverty where never comes a dawn; 

You lily - fingered ones, you say such things can't be, Well then, discard your wings and plummet down the depths and see.

Upon the page of History a myriad cities lie,

They lie in dust and silt and well we know what made them die; 

The golden chariot rode on the cowed and kneeling slave, 'Til Destiny raised up her hand and dashed them to the grave.

II 

It is not enemies without that toll a nation's knell, But hordes of hope - starved men that live down in a man-made hell, Whose teeth are sharp as tusks of beasts, whose claws are keen and long, Who nothing have nor will, and toil and hate have made them strong.

Stupidity and greed have levelled off gold - vaulted halls, And their corroding hands have undermined once mighty walls; 

Where flowed the wine of kings the desert sands in silence flow, There slaves breathe not nor do their self-slain lords past glories know.

World's rolling western plain

Arise to power with the wheat, and with the dust they wane, And Fate has said, "Before the dust, shall come the reign of gold, "Then men shall starve in rich fat fields and die in want and cold."

The fields of grain know naught of trials of men, They wait alone the rains and copper sun

To wave their tassels in the wind; nor ken 

The silent still machines of nations done 

To death with empty elocution and

A fear to tread new paths. We have the tools 

We have the men; why dreaming do we stand, Self-fettered slaves? Are we such frightened fools

As starve in granaries and die of laws 

Inflexible? We, too, must pioneer

For though the continent be won, no pause 

Does progress know. Her voice is calling clear, From plainlands to each border and each sea, "Brave ones keep faith. On! Man shall yet be free!"
