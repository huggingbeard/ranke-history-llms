Article 4633. Testimony of Husband or Wife: 

In all such suits and proceedings the husband and wife shall be competent witnesses for and against each other, but neither party shall be compelled to testify as to any matter that will criminate himself or herself; and where the husband or wife testifies, the court or jury trying the case shall determine the credibility of such witness and the weight to be given such testimony; but no divorce shall be granted upon the evidence of either husband or wife, if there be any collusion between them.

DEFINITION: 

Recrimination is a counter charge by defendant, of misconduct upon the part of plaintiff, of the same general character as that relied upon by plaintiff as ground for divorce, and reasonably calculated to have provoked such misconduct of defendant.

Section 192. Recriminatory Acts in Retaliation of Other's Conduct Not Ground for Divorce: 

It is the recognized rule that recrimination, as a valid defense, must arise out of the fact that the acts or conduct, for which the plaintiff seeks a divorce, were induced by, or in retaliation of, plaintiff's conduct.¹

The court pronouncing a decree of divorce shall also decree and order a division of the estate of the parties in such a way as the court shall deem just and right, having due regard to the rights of each party and their children, if any. CHAPTER XXIV. 

REMARRIAGE. 

Article 4640. Remarriage: 

Neither party to a divorce suit, where a divorce is granted upon the ground of cruel treatment, shall marry any other person for a period of twelve months next after such divorce is granted, but the parties so divorced may marry each other at any time. In all other cases either party may marry again after the dissolution of the marriage.

COMMENT: 

The failure of the statute to provide a penalty for infraction of the inhibition against remarriage, except to each other, prior to the expiration of one year, when the divorce is granted upon the ground of cruel treatment, is no doubt responsible for its widespread disregard. Thousands of persons throughout the State have remarried in violation of its provisions. The deterring effect of the statute is practically nil. Disregard of the provision, however, will in all probability visit punishment on some innocent victims in the future. Our Supreme Court has said that the penalty for violating the statute is that the marriage is subject to annulment "in a proper case at the suit of one having a justiciable interest therein". There has been no judicial determination of what would be a "proper case" or what person would have a "justiciable interest" in such case.

Sec. 481 233 

REMARRIAGE Section 481. Marriage Entered Into in Violation of Statute Not Void But Voidable: 

By its express terms the statute forbids a second marriage within one year where the parties have been divorced upon the ground of cruel treatment. 

The statute does not declare such a second marriage void, neither does it provide any form of punishment nor authorize the court to prohibit such a marriage. 

According to the great weight of authority, statutes similar to this are construed to make a marriage in violation of its terms merely voidable, not void, and such is the construction placed upon this statute.¹ 

Section 482. Trial Court Without Authority to Prohibit Second Marriage: 

Since the statute has been construed to make a marriage in violation of its terms merely voidable and not void, it follows that the language of the statute must be insufficient to authorize a court to compel obedience to its terms by injunctive order.

The court is without power under this statute to insert in a divorce decree a prohibition against a second marriage within any specified time.'

A court of equity is without power to issue an injunction, unless some property right or other right
