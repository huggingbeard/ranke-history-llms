Moses, the world's emancipator, has lived and given his message. He discovered the secret of tyranny, strife and hatred, in the life of the individual and of the nation, namely, self-exaltation. He also found the principle on which rests liberty, peace, happiness, prosperity, and life in its fullest terms, for the individual and the nation, namely, helpfulness. He has revealed the secret of tyranny, and demonstrated, in terms of life, the principle of helpfulness; he has written these where they can never pass from the knowledge of men. 

THE PRINCE OF PEACE HAS COME 

The Prince of Peace, the world's Redeemer, earth's greatest teacher, was born more than nineteen hundred years ago. He said of himself: "I am the way, the truth, and the life;" and he showed us how to live in

our home community and in the broader field of public life. He revealed, in his life, the principle which is fundamental to his kingdom and to all permanent government, namely, helpfulness. On the cross of his crucifixion, he said, concerning his message and mission to men, "It is finished."

GOD'S WORD A LAMP TO MEN 

God's Word, revealing the life, the work, and the teaching of the emancipator and of the world's Redeemer, and the messages and triumphs of the prophets who stood between these, is given as a lamp to light the path that leadeth upward to the perfect day.

II. THE CHURCH 

It is almost nineteen hundred years since the way was made plain, and the church received her commission to reorganize society about this new principle, helpfulness. 

Men have been slow to turn away from the thought of self-exaltation and have sought to build the kingdom, while seeking for themselves the larger places to their personal advantage. What has been the result? After nineteen hundred years, men are saying: "Christianity has never been tried."

THE SECRET OF RIGHT THINKING 

What is the secret of right thinking, which will enable us to reach conclusions that are right; that will square with the fundamentals of life as revealed by the highest authority known to men? It is intellectual honesty, which enables us to see that the interests of the unfortunate are as sacred os our own; which will enable us in our judgments to be as fair and true to justice when we are personally interested, as when we have no personal interest; which will enable us to remember that justice and mercy have the right of way regardless of the balance of power or personal interest. When men allow their judgment to be biased by their personal interest, they do the same thing a court does when it allows itself to be bribed by one of the contesting parties. 

Our judgment is the court to which is submitted the differences which may arise between us and others. Men who do not obey this law cannot reach true conclusions, cannot lead men into justice; and without justice there is no lasting peace, between individuals or nations.

CHAPTER II A Few Fundamental Principles I. THE FUNDAMENTAL PRINCIPLE OF LAW 

The purpose of law is to secure some end. If the law giver is good and benevolent he will seek to secure that which is good and to prevent that which is evil. Then the fundamental principle of the great law giver is this; that which produces good is enjoined and carries with it blessing; while that which produces evil is forbidden and carries with it punishment. 

II. TWO GREAT PRINCIPLES 

In life as we know it, there are two great fundamental principles; one the direct antithesis of the other. One is the principle of selfishness, and may be expressed in these words: THE STRONG HAVE A RIGHT TO THE BEST FOR THEMSELVES. The other is the principle of helpfulness, and may be expressed as follows: THE

THE PRINCIPLE OF SELFISHNESS 

The principle of selfishness, the strong have a right to the best for themselves, is the principle about which the organizations of society have been developed. The acceptance of this principle has, in every generation, led the strong to oppose every movement that looked to an equalization of the opportunities and blessings of life. 

It is the source of tyranny and oppression. It blinds the eyes of the strong to common justice. It changes the heart of man to stone, until he, with enough laid by to keep him for a thousand years, can look upon the pinched face of a hungry mother, holding her starving baby to her dry breast, and remain unmoved and think it no concern of his; or until he refuses to allow ten million lives and all that they may mean to wife and child, to weigh against his unwarranted privileges.

And God spoke unto him in the vision of the night and said, "Jacob, fear not to go down into Egypt; for I will make of thee, there a great nation; and I will go with thee into Egypt; and I will surely bring thee up again; and Joseph shall put his hand upon thine eyes."

Thus encouraged Jacob continues his journey into Egypt.

JACOB MEETS JOSEPH, HIS LONG LOST SON 

Years have passed since Joseph as a lad went out to his brothers on that eventful day, when they stripped him of his coat of many colors and sold him into Egypt. 

During all this time, Jacob had mourned Joseph as dead. 

Rachel, his beloved wife, mother of Joseph and Benjamin, had died at the birth of Benjamin. This old man, bereft of the wife whom he had loved so dearly, and not

knowing what fate Joseph had met, had walked through the years of his old age in deep sorrow. Now he is to see 

Joseph, his long lost son. Jacob comes on slowly in his wagon, Joseph in his chariot, dressed in his rich apparel comes to meet his father. What changes have taken place since they last saw each other! The lad has become a man, the greatest in all Egypt. His name is on the lips of all the people as they bow before him. Jacob, once so strong and sturdy, now an old man with silvered locks; that sturdy form bowed and broken under the weight of years and sorrow. The wagon and chariot have drawn near each other. The old man climbs down from the wagon, Joseph steps from his chariot. No word of tongue or pen can tell what these two have suffered. Let them weep in each other's arms, while the long night of sorrow lifts, and a new day arises to shine away the malice and hatred of the brothers, who have caused such suffering to both father and son.

ISRAEL A FAVORED PEOPLE, THEIR PROPERTY SPARED 

"And Israel dwelt in the land of Egypt, in the country of Goshen; and they had possessions therein, and grew, and multiplied exceedingly." For Joseph was food commissioner during the years of famine and fed his people. 

Truly, Israel was a favored people. They came through the famine in possession of their property.

II. THE DEADLINESS OF THE PRINCIPLE OF SELFISHNESS 

Through four generations we have traced a family noted in all history for their faith in Jehovah, their courage, their strength, and their industry. Abraham, a man of sublime faith, of great valor, and unfaltering courage. 

He was not afraid to join the battle with the allied forces of four kings; and recaptured and brought back his nephew, Lot, and all his goods. 

Isaac, a quiet man, strong in faith and obedience.

Jacob, a man of faith, recall his vision and his covenant, and forget not how he wrestled with the angel until the angel said, "As a prince thou hast power to prevail with God and men." Shrewd, see him obtain the birthright from his older brother Esau; watch him strip 

Laban of his herds and flocks; tactful, see him come again to his home country and meet his brother Esau, who had purposed to kill him. 

Joseph, he forgot not the covenant of his father; a man of virtue, who went to prison rather than enter into an unholy alliance with his master's wife; keen, he interpreted Pharaoh's dream when all the magicians of Egypt had failed; successful, he rose from a prison to become 

Governor of Egypt, and brought his people through the famine with their flocks and herds, while all the property of the Egyptian people passed into the hand of Pharaoh. 

Israel was a chosen people, God's elect. 

We now purpose to trace in this same family the work of the principle of selfishness, the strong have a right to

the best for themselves. And to show its deadly work in every generation; and how in the end it gets God's chosen people until in anguish under the lash of tyranny, they cry unto Jehovah for deliverance; and how a civilization was wrecked in the working out of their deliverance.

JACOB AND ESAU 

With his mother's help, Jacob deceived his father and secured the blessing, which the father intended to bestow upon Esau. When Esau returned from his hunting and learned that Jacob had deceived their father, who was blind, and stolen the blessing, he hated Jacob and purposed to kill him. Selfishness, strife, hatred.

JACOB AND LABAN 

To escape Esau's wrath, Jacob went to live with his uncle Laban, who took advantage of him and compelled him to work fourteen years instead of seven for Rachel.

Later Jacob took advantage of his uncle Laban in the breeding of the cattle and sheep; and when Laban saw that Jacob was getting the advantage, he again and again forced a change of contract; and if God had not interfered, he would have taken from Jacob all that he had accumulated during his twenty years of service. And here again we see selfishness, strife, victory on the part of one, then hatred.

RACHEL AND LEAH 

Jacob married sisters, Leah and Rachel. He loved 

Rachel more than Leah. These sisters fairly hated each other as they strove with each other for the affections of the same husband. Mark the prenatal influences that molded the lives of Jacob's sons; as this enmity and strife of the mothers burns itself into the souls of their unborn children; for it was in the midst of this strife that Joseph and his brothers were born.

In Rachel's struggle for ascendency over Leah, she laid the foundation for Joseph's dreams of power, in which he saw his brothers and his father and mother bowing down to him. And Leah with her jealousy and hatred for Rachel, planted in her children that jealousy and hatred which later sold Joseph into Egypt, and deceived the father with the bloody coat; that they might get rid of him whom they hated. Thus again we see the work of the principle of selfishness. And in the strife and struggle of the mother, it burns itself into the unborn children, to burst forth anew in greater fierceness in the next generation.

JOSEPH AND HIS BROTHERS 

Joseph as he wore his coat of many colors among his brothers with a princely air, was perfectly natural; and they responding with the taunt of jealousy and hatred, were just as natural as was he. Joseph was dreaming of the time when they should bow to him. They were watching for an opportunity to get rid of him. The op-

portunity came and they used it. We have in this family a logical and full development of the principle of selfishness; strife for a personal advantage, personal victory over a rival, hatred, a desire to destroy a rival lest he gain some advantage for himself to our disadvantage. 

THE FIRST LEGISLATIVE JOKER 

Joseph, a man of faith and splendid virtue, brought his people through the famine, with all their property. 

During the years of famine, all the money, personal property, and land of the Egyptians passed into the hands of Pharaoh, as he sold the food which they had produced, back to them at a profit; while Joseph's people and the Egyptian priests came through the famine in full possession of their property and land. Herein God revealed for all time the iniquity of the profit system. 

This exception of the priesthood, exempting them from the conditions which left the Egyptian people in absolute poverty, is the joker in this whole business. It kept the priests quiet. Had they seen themselves being reduced to poverty by this profiteering on the part of Joseph and Pharaoh, they would have seen through the whole matter, and a conference would have been called, and the following resolutions would have been presented.

RESOLUTIONS THAT WOULD HAVE TURNED THE TIDE OF HISTORY 

WHEREAS, God did give to Pharaoh a certain dream pertaining to the welfare of the people of Egypt; and

WHEREAS, Jehovah did inspire Joseph to understand and interpret said dream; so that it became known to the people of Egypt that there were to be seven years of abundance, followed by seven years of severe famine; and

WHEREAS, The people of Egypt did accept said dream and the interpretation thereof, as a true revelation from Jehovah; and gave special diligence to the cultivation of their land during the seven years of plenty; and 

WHEREAS, Jehovah did abundantly bless their labor and their land, so that it brought forth in great abundance during all the seven years of plenty; and 

WHEREAS, The people had such confidence in

Joseph and Pharaoh as to allow all their surplus during the seven years of plenty to pass into the hands of Joseph to be held against the seven years of famine; and 

WHEREAS, All the food supply of Egypt is the product of the land and labor of the people, under the guidance and blessing of Jehovah; and 

WHEREAS, Pharaoh's part in providing for the people during the seven years of famine, has been to dream a dream, which dream was given him by Jehovah while he slept; and 

WHEREAS, Joseph's part in providing for the seven years of famine has been to reveal to Pharaoh and the people the interpretation of said dream, which interpretation Jehovah gave Joseph; and to suggest and superintend the plan for gathering and storing the surplus grain; and

WHEREAS, If Pharaoh now be permitted to sell back to the people at a profit, the food which they have produced; he will become exceedingly rich while the people become poor, dependent upon Pharaoh; and

WHEREAS, If the aforesaid conditions, wherein 

Pharaoh holds all the property, and the people have nothing, obtain, then Pharaoh will be able to lay upon the people as rent such tribute as he from time to time may desire; thus enabling him and his to live forever in luxury and idleness off the toil of the people; and 

WHEREAS, It is not good to subject our chief rulers to such temptation, as is involved in the foregoing; because if they should yield and begin to live a life of ease and luxury off the toil of the people, then the very life they lived would constantly tend to puff them up until they became proud, haughty, and selfish; and the very sense of justice would be smothered out of their lives; and

WHEREAS, It is not good for the people to allow their chief ruler to take unto himself such advantage as is involved in the foregoing; lest he take unto himself an undue proportion of the people's toil; while they are left with an insufficiency to bring them and their children into that degree of culture and refinement, which will make them proper companions and associates for their ruler and his children; for if the time ever comes when the ruler is rich and the people are poor, then as the people seek for themselves a larger share of the result of their labor as the basis for a broader culture and refinement, they will be opposed by their ruler; and

the machinery of government will be made to oppose the people in their effort to secure for themselves the higher things in life; this would result in jealousy and hatred between the people and their ruler, which would eventually destroy government; therefore be it 

RESOLVED, That we thank Jehovah for the revelation given and for his blessing on the labor of our people during the years of plenty; and be it further 

RESOLVED, That it is the deliberate and godly judgment of this conference, that the present food supply of Egypt, being the product of the toil and land of the people under the blessing of Jehovah, by every right of justice belongs to the people; and be it further 

RESOLVED, That we recognize the part that our chief ruler, Pharaoh, had in providing for the years of famine, in that he dreamed a dream, and that we recommend to the people that they be generous with our chief ruler, Pharaoh, and allow him and his family to share equally with them the food till the end of the famine; and be it further RESOLVED, That we recognize the part that Joseph had in providing against the famine, in that he interpreted the dreams of Pharaoh and suggested and superintended the gathering and storing of the food; and that we recommend to the people that they be generous toward Joseph and his kindred and allow them to share equally the food until the end of the famine; and be it further RESOLVED, That these resolutions be read in all the churches throughout the land of Egypt, on the first

Sabbath after the close of this conference; and that every priest in charge of a congregation deliver a discourse in which he shall set forth in plain terms the necessity of all the people standing firmly by these resolutions; and be it further 

RESOLVED, That a copy of these resolutions be spread upon the minutes of this conference; and that a copy of them be sent to our chief ruler, Pharaoh; and also that a copy be sent to our chief benefactor, Joseph. 

Had it not been for the little joker which exempted the priests and their lands from the conditions that bound the people, such conference would have been called, and would have passed these resolutions unanimously; and the tide of history would have been turned; and Egypt's civilization would have been spared. But the priests and their land were exempt, every priest was silent; and all the property passed into the hands of Pharaoh; he became rich, and the people became poor. 

Joseph's people retained their flocks and herds, fed on the grain of the Egyptians, while the people of Egypt, the producers, were stripped of everything.

The years of famine are passed. start anew, but on a different basis. the people owned their land and flocks and herds. Now, Pharaoh owns practically everything, and the people are renters, owning nothing. Joseph's people fared better

than did the Egyptians. For he, being governor of Egypt and having charge of all the grain, fed his people and their flocks and herds, so that they came through the famine with practically all they had when they came into Egypt.

During the years of famine Joseph followed the principle of selfishness, the strong have a right to the best for themselves. The fact, that Joseph's wisdom and the industry of the Egyptian people, working together, brought both races through the seven years of famine, ought to have bound the two races together in a bond of friendship. But, when he took advantage and brought his own people through the famine in full possession of their property, while he stripped the Egyptians of everything, he laid the economic foundation for race prejudice and hatred.

THE PRINCIPLE OF SELFISHNESS GETS GOD'S PEOPLE 

Among those who hold property and are prosperous, it is almost universally believed that if one does not have property, and is not prosperous, it is his own fault. Israel was a strong people, with a sublime faith in Jehovah; they had been prosperous. Now, there arose a new king over Egypt, who did not know Joseph; and he said, to his people, "The children of Israel are more and mightier than are we. Let us deal wisely with them, lest they multiply, and in time of war join with the enemy and gain for themselves their freedom, and go out of the land." (Ex. 1: 7-10); "And they set over them task-

masters to afflict them with their burdens; and they built cities for Pharaoh," (Ex. 1: 7-17), instead of raising cattle for themselves. 

Why did Israel lose their freedom? Why did these strong men submit to the humiliation of task-masters? 

Not because they had lost faith in Jehovah, nor because they had become idle or dissipated. For they retained their faith, and were a strong and virile people. 

By a rental system, they, who had been herdsmen for generations, were gradually driven from the plains and fields, into the cities as laborers. With the wage earner, the cost of living and the wage received soon strike a balance, and he becomes bound to his job. For if he quits, his wages stop and he is without bread; others, than the Kaiser, have used starvation to subjugate people, employers of labor have used this plan for hundreds of years; he is no longer a free and independent man. "And the Egyptians made their lives bitter with hard bondage, in mortar, and in brick, and in all manner of service in the field. And the king of Egypt spake to the Hebrew midwives, and said, when you do the office of a midwife to the Hebrew women, if it be a son, then ye shall kill him; but if it be a daughter, then she shall live." (Ex. 1: 13-16.)

Behold Israel, God's chosen people, stripped of the greater part of their wealth, of their freedom and independence, under the master's lash, their children born under the sentence of death; justice, liberty, prosperity, and happiness gone; tyranny, fear, and hatred reign

through the principle of selfishness, the strong have a right to the best for themselves.

THEIR FAITH DID NOT SAVE THEM 

The well-to-do are fond of quoting from David, "I have been young, and now am old; yet have I not seen the righteous forsaken, nor his seed begging bread." The first promise made to Abraham, was a promise of wealth, power, and influence. This promise was kept constantly before him and his children. Generation after generation they dreamed of themselves becoming a nation, so rich and strong and great as to bring blessing to all mankind. 

Who can understand the humiliation and suffering of this strong, free, and independent people, as they were brought under the tyranny of Egypt? Israel did not willingly submit to oppression; they fought every step of the way. They called upon Jehovah in whom they had a sublime faith; but in spite of their prayers, their property shrank, and Egypt's power over them increased. 

They were not willing servants of another race; so the 

Egyptians set over them task-masters, with whips to drive them to their tasks. They were not cheerful under the lash; for their scowl made the Egyptians afraid; and to break their power, the Egyptians murdered Israel's children in the birth chamber. Oh! the bitterness of a race of slaves; children of men who were free, and strong, and wealthy, and independent; their own land, the land of promise, the land of wealth and freedom awaited them; but they could not go, because their masters held them.

The principle of selfishness had gripped God's chosen people. Among them it had set strife and hatred in every generation and left them a race of slaves, driven under the lash, their children sentenced to death, because their parents loved freedom. It is not blood that makes a race of slaves, or a tyrant; it is this principle, the strong have a right to the best for themselves, that in turn may make slaves or tyrants of any race. God has demonstrated in Israel the logical and persistent climax of this principle.

III. GOD BUILDS THE EMANCIPATOR 

When God builds, he begins early and lays his foundation deep. Israel's four hundred years of servitude was the foundation on which God built the great emancipator. JOCHEBED Jochebed, a descendant of Levi, the third son of Jacob and Leah, although a slave, was a woman of more than ordinary intellectual powers; and of truly a sublime faith in Jehovah. She was familiar with the story of Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and Joseph. She had traced again and again the successive steps by which Israel had been reduced from a free, wealthy, and independent people to a race of slaves. The yoke of bondage did not rest lightly upon her; for she had inherited the spirit of her early fathers.

She had prepared her supper, and with her two children, Miriam and Aaron, had sat down to await the

coming of her husband, Amram, from his daily toil at the brick yards. Amram was also a descendant of Levi and, in mind and spirit, like his wife. As husband and wife looked into each other's face, each knew what was in the other's mind.

SELFISHNESS DESTROYS THE SENSE OF JUSTICE 

Some two weeks before this there had been trouble at the brick yards. One of the Egyptian task-masters had been unusually severe with one of the Hebrew men and had struck him several times, when the Hebrew turned and killed him. A riot was barely prevented by the closing of the yards for the day. For several years there had been great bitterness between the two races, and several times there had been quarrels in which several people had been killed on either side. Pharaoh had just issued an edict, that all the boys born of the Hebrew women should be thrown into the river. This edict had been made known throughout the land, and, during the afternoon, it had reached the ears of both Amram and his wife, Jochebed. This had come with great severity to both of them, as Jochebed was again expecting to be a mother. The supper was eaten in silence. Jochebed's soul was aflame. The silence of her husband seemed to aggravate her. And as we read between the lines here, we hear her, as she speaks out what is burning in her soul.

JOCHEBED'S SOUL BURNS WITH A CONSCIOUSNESS OF INJUSTICE 

"Are the daughters of Abraham and Jacob to bring forth to have their children thrown into the river? Oh sons and daughters of Israel, driven like cattle under the lash! Would to God, Abraham or Jacob were here to lead us out of this cruel bondage! Pharaoh, coward that he was, tried to break our power and humble us by commanding the Egyptian midwives to smother the boy babies in the birth chamber; but God touched their hearts and would not let them do this, so our boys were spared. Now he comes out with this edict, in which he says; 'We have with us a large number of Hebrew people. The history of this people during their sojourn among us has very clearly demonstrated the fact that they are an inferior race, better suited to be slaves, than to be a free and independent people. They came among us free, wealthy and independent. But they have not been able to retain their wealth and independence. They have not been as thrifty as they might have been. 

Instead of retaining their flocks and herds and adding to their possessions, they have allowed these to slip away from them; until they have become a race of wage earners, dependent upon their daily toil. As laborers they have not been industrious, so we have been obliged to set over them task-masters, to drive them to their tasks. 

Instead of being grateful for all that the land of Egypt has done for them they are sullen and mutinous; and several times they have even dared to turn on their

masters and strike them. This people must be humbled and given to understand what their position in Egypt is. Now, therefor, it is the royal decree of his Highness, The Pharaoh of Egypt, that all the sons born to this people be cast into the river and drowned.' "An inferior race, better suited to be slaves than to be free and independent! Not thrifty, not able to keep our flocks and herds! When they have stolen everything that we had when we came into Egypt, by laying upon us rents that have consumed our flocks and our herds and our wages, until we are left in a strange land stripped of all our wealth. They have taken our wealth, our freedom, and our independence, and now they tell us that we are slaves, unworthy of freedom; and that our sons are fit only for their river. Oh God of our fathers, would to thee that there were a man in Israel who would not be a slave! But, remembering the promise to Abraham, would dare to lead us out of this cruel bondage, into our own land. Amram, if our child is a boy, will you live and see him thrown into the river?"

JOCHEBED'S PRAYER 

Amram, weary with his day's toil in the brick yards, found rest in slumber, but Jochebed could not sleep. She arose and went out under the stars. She looked up into the heavens and thought of the life of her fathers. It seemed that there was no foe that they were not able to overcome. And then she thought of her own generation and of their condition. Their wealth, freedom, independ-

ence, and happiness gone; their faith in Jehovah wavering. Had she not heard the promise that he would bring them up out of Egypt, into their own land, the land of freedom and plenty? Had God forgotten his promise? 

Had Israel lost her faith? And she fell on her knees and began to pray. "O God of Abraham, and of Isaac, and of Jacob, hast thou forgotten thy promise? Has Israel lost her faith? 

Truly thy blessing did rest upon our fathers, and thou didst lead them in a wondrous way. "O God, sorrow hath come to thy people; our flocks and herds are gone. We are no longer free. We do not have time, even, to worship thee. By our toil we are made weary, until our spirits faint within us; and our faith is weak. Sentence of death is passed upon our sons before they are born. Injustice after injustice has been heaped upon us, until we are no longer able to quench the flame of wrath that rises in our souls. O God, has justice perished from the earth? And is mercy no longer found among men? Have we so grievously sinned against thee, that thou hast altogether withdrawn from us? "Most mericful God, put thy protecting hand over our unborn children; save their lives. Thou canst give the daughters of Israel sons like unto our fathers, men of mighty faith and courage, men who will lead Israel up out of her cruel bondage, into her own land of promise. "My God, I am to be a mother; grant that my child may be a boy; I do now offer him unto thee, a living sacrifice for the redemption of his people. Give me, O

God, strength and faith and courage that I may build these into my child. Make him a man of wisdom, of justice, and of mercy, so that when he hath brought his people forth, none may ever again suffer injustice. "O thou Mighty One, now I see why thou hast permitted this cruel bondage, even the death of our sons. 

For had men never suffered injustice and oppression, they never would have understood the world's need of justice and mercy. Now I understand and am not sorry for this night; for he who shall set justice and mercy in the earth, shall be greater than any king; he shall not only make his own people free, but in him shall all the world be blessed. Thou wilt again deliver Israel. Write thou the agony and the vision of this night in my unborn child, that he may understand and lead his people out of their bondage into their own land. Keep the vision before me, and let thine handmaiden live and bear for 

Israel's freedom."

Her prayer is finished, but she remained kneeling with her face lifted to the stars, waiting that the vision of her son leading Israel out of their bondage into the land of Canaan, might be fixed in her very being. Another one of Israel's great spirits has wrestled with Jehovah and has prevailed. Israel shall be free! She arose with that calm in her soul, which only those, who have prevailed with God in prayer, know.

JOCHEBED BUILDS THE EMANCIPATOR 

Jochebed now lives for Israel's freedom. Day and

night she keeps her vision before her. Morning, noon, and night she prays God's blessing upon her child; that he may be a man of wisdom, of justice, and of mercy. 

Her child shall live, and she plans for his hiding. He is born, a boy. Thus far her prayer has been answered, and this increases her faith.

JOCHEBED KNOWS A WOMAN'S HEART AND PLANS FOR THE TRAINING OF HER SON 

Jochebed knew that she could not always hide her child and at the same time give him that training which would be necessary if he was to be Israel's leader. But, she had been thinking about this and asking God to give her wisdom. And a plan had gradually unfolded itself to her mind. She knew that Pharaoh's daughter was a great lover of children, and that she had bitterly opposed her father's edict, that the Hebrew boys should be thrown into the river. And she knew a woman's heart when she looks into the face of a laughing babe. She sent Miriam to the river to gather an arm full of rushes. 

From these she wove a basket and daubed it with pitch. 

She laid her babe in the basket, and with a prayer she set the basket in the river and went away. 

The mother prayed, Miriam watched, and Pharaoh's daughter came to the river to bathe; she saw the basket and sent one of her maids to draw it out of the water. 

When they opened the basket the babe cried; and

Pharaoh's daughter pitied him, and said it is a child of the Hebrews; and she tried to comfort him. Then

Miriam came and said, "May I go and call one of the

Hebrew women, that she may come and nurse the child for thee?" And she said to Miriam, "Go." And Miriam went and called her mother, and she came; and 

Pharaoh's daughter said unto her, "Take this child and nurse it for me, and I will give thee thy wages; and she took the child and nursed it. Again her prayer had been answered, and her faith rewarded. The life of her child had been spared, and his training provided for. During those first years of life so important in the training of the child, he was to be entrusted to her; and then when he needed other teachers, he was to receive the training of a prince.

A MOTHER'S TASK 

Jochebed knew her task. During those years in which she was to be permitted to hold her child to her breast, she must plant in him that faith in Jehovah that will go with him through life. He must understand his mother's sorrow, so that he can never get away from it. 

He must see himself as a prince coming to his mother's help. Amid it all, he must bear himself as an Egyptian prince, until such time as God shall call him. In the meantime he must study and prepare himself as Israel's leader. 

With her great spirit and triumphant faith, she brought herself to the task. He was only a child, but he had learned to keep a trust; he knew the story of Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and Joseph, and all the history of

Israel down to their, then present, condition. And above all he had an unfaltering trust in Jehovah, Israel's God. 

He had been taught to listen for the voice of God and to obey when he calls. She brought him unto Pharaoh's daughter, and he became her son; and she called his name Moses, for she had drawn him out of the water. 

WILL A MOTHER'S WORK STAND THE TEST? 

Moses became the son of Pharaoh's daughter and received the training of an Egyptian prince, accomplished in all the learning of Egypt. He was a goodly young man, of extraordinary powers, a prince among men. He stood in the morning of life, when the tides of life flow full; when love and romance and ostentation and power make their strongest appeal to men. As a prince the door of opportunity was open to him, and on every hand he was invited to enter and seek for himself the pleasures of life. Will a mother's work stand the test?

Moses was not ignorant of the conditions existing between his own people and the Egyptians. Also he had kept in touch with his mother and father, and his brother and sister. On a certain day the task-masters were unusually severe with the men, and the men were sullen. 

This day as Moses passed a secluded part of the yard, his father, Amram, was carrying brick. The task-master struck Amram with his whip to hurry him at his task; 

Amram stopped to protest, when the task-master struck him; and he staggered and fell. The task-master began to flog him. Moses walked quickly that way and asked

the task-master to desist. He replied that he knew more about slaves than Moses did and struck Amram again as he attempted to rise. Moses unleashed himself for one moment, and the master lay quivering, and then was still. 

Moses realized that he had killed the task-master, and taking a shovel he buried him in the sand and went away. 

The next day Moses learned that it was known that he had killed the Egyptian, and that Pharaoh was angry at him for his interference; and he determined to go away from Egypt. A mother's work has stood the test. 

Moses turns from Egypt and all it might offer him as a prince and casts his lot with his mother's people. But he must see his mother before he goes away.

A MOTHER'S BLESSING 

Amram had gone home with his face cut and bleeding; he related in simple language how the task-master had knocked him down and was beating him when Moses came and struck the Egyptian and killed him. Jochebed was not displeased with Moses for this interference on the part of his father, but was alarmed for his safety. 

She now knew that her prayers and teaching were not in vain, for Moses' lot henceforth would be cast with that of his people. On the second evening after the incident at the yard, Jochebed heard that the young prince who had rescued her husband from the fury of the taskmaster had fled from the country, because of Pharaoh's wrath against him.

Night came on. She could not sleep. She went out to

the place where she had so often gone to pray since the night of her vision. She knelt and looked up to the stars and thanked Jehovah, because he had heard her prayers, and because Moses' face was now set toward the destiny of his people. Then she ceased to pray and was wondering where he was tonight. Was he planning some movement in Israel's behalf, or had he indeed fled from the country? And she remembered how Jacob had gone away to escape the wrath of Esau, and had come again in triumph to his own country; and she believed that 

God would lead her son. She heard the call of a bird and looking saw a form approaching, the bird's mate answered, and Moses came to his mother. He inquired of his father, and then had her relate to him again all the early history of Israel; how by a rental system they had been reduced to wage earners and then to slaves; and how cruel their bondage had been. And then she related to him again the story of Jacob going away to escape the wrath of Esau, and how God had brought him again into his own country with great blessing; and she pointed out to him again the necessity of waiting until Jehovah should speak to him; and closed their conversation with these words: "My son, Jehovah is altogether able to deliver his people. Listen for his voice, and when he speaks obey his every word; and you cannot fail. He has promised to bring his people up out of Egypt, and his promise is true. May Jehovah lead thee and bless thee, as he led and blessed Abraham and 

Isaac and Jacob and Joseph; and bring thee and all

Israel into their own land. Thy mother's prayers will

follow thee always, and Jehovah will watch over thee by day and by night." They went to the house and he bid his father and Aaron and Miriam goodby, and went away into the night.

MOSES GOES INTO THE WILDERNESS, OR TIME TO DO THE WORLD'S THINKING 

Moses went out with his mother's blessing and his mother's faith. He journeyed eastward a hundred miles along the left arm of the Red Sea and then took a southeastern course into the country of Midian. On an afternoon he came to a well and sat down to rest. The priest of Midian had seven daughters, who came to the well to water their father's flocks. After a little the shepherds came and drove them away; then Moses stood up and helped them and watered their flocks. When they came to their father, he asked them how it was that they had come so soon; they said, an Egyptian had come and delivered them out of the hand of the shepherds and had watered their flocks. And he said to his daughters, "Where is he? Why have you left him? Call him that he may eat bread." And Moses was content to dwell with the man, and he gave Moses Zipporah, his daughter, as a wife. And Moses kept the flock of his fatherin-law, Jethro, the priest of Midian; and he led the flock to the back side of the desert and came to the mountain.

TWO OPPOSING FORCES MOLD THE LIFE OF MOSES 

Two opposing forces molded the life of Moses. Born of the people of Israel, he inherited their faith and traditions; reared in an Egyptian palace, Egypt's luxuries were lavished upon him. The prenatal influence that had entered into his life, had laid in him the foundation of the emancipator. For his mother had woven into the very fiber of his being her faith in Jehovah and a desire to know justice and mercy, and to find wherein these lie. His mother did her utmost, during those plastic years of childhood, to impress upon him things fundamental to the life to which she had consecrated him. She had transplanted in him the story of Israel's life and made it to burn in his very being; until he, though free by being adopted into Pharaoh's house, knew what it was to be a slave, knew the longing of the soul that is bound for justice and freedom.

When yet a child he had been brought to the palace and made the son of Pharaoh's daughter, a woman whose love for children was strong enough to overcome the wrath and opposition of her father, when he learned that she had adopted, as a son, a child of the Hebrews. She loved Moses and determined that he should be a prince indeed, worthy of Pharaoh's house. He was brought to manhood with a physical perfection which enabled him to walk to the close of life with hands that did not shake, and eyes that were not dim. He was trained in all the learning and culture of Egypt. Through all the years of childhood, youth, and young manhood he was sur-

rounded by the most pleasant associations; young people of the best blood of Egypt were his companions. Among these he was accepted and passed as an Egyptian of the royal house.

Perhaps no man ever lived who came nearer holding within himself the best that was represented in a race of slaves and at the same time the best that was represented in royalty. For his was the blood of a slave, but he maintained himself by the attainments of a prince. Well might God draw this man aside, into the mountains, where he might commune with him and do the world's thinking. He knew life from the standpoint of the slave, and he knew it from the standpoint of the master. He loved both races; for the one had given him his blood and his faith, and the other had given him his freedom and his culture. In him the best of both had met and produced a man worthy to walk with God and to write the constitution of the world. 

Day by day he wandered over the hills and into the mountains with the flocks and learned a closer communion with nature and nature's God. He saw the mountains and the plains, with their varied life, and the sheep feeding in security under his care. And then he looked upon the heavens and watched the sun, moon, and stars as they passed in their courses; they seemed to be guided even as he guided his flock. And back of all this he believed that there was one great guiding hand, the hand of Jehovah. And then the vision of Egypt and its life came before him. He went over this whole life in Egypt again and again, recalling the story of Israel as he knew

it from his mother's lips; saw again the suffering of his mother's people; saw his own father struck down; and then he remembered the passion that swept his soul as he smote the task-master that eventful day. He stood again with his mother on that last night in Egypt and heard her benediction; and then he saw his other mother, his foster mother, woman of splendid culture and refinement, so careful of all his associates and his education, anxious that he might in every way be worthy of the place in life which she had given him in his adoption. She, when his own mother could hide him no longer, had taken him from under a sentence of death, up out of slavery; and had made him a prince, in every way worthy of a place in Pharaoh's house. She had done all this out of the compassion and goodness of her heart. And there were among his Egyptian associates many as noble in their character, and as cultivated and refined as was she. 

Surely all the best of Egypt's life was before him as a prince in the house of Pharaoh. He had touched and known and loved both races. He clung to his mother's faith, but in many ways Egypt's civilization was greater than that of his own people. Thus some days he grew homesick for the pleasant associations of the palace, on others his mind and heart went out to his mother's people. Why must these two races hate each other? Why should one be master and the other slave? Why not dwell together in peace and happiness?

Moses realized that if he reached the real fundamental truth in a problem like this he must clear his mind of all prejudice and take an impartial view of the whole

matter. He was in a good position to do this. Separated from Egypt by some two hundred and fifty miles, he was able to bring both races into one perspective. In his school days in Pharaoh's house, Moses had been a good student and had learned to think a subject through. So day by day he found himself settling down to a careful systematic examination of all these facts, conditions, that had been crowded into his youthful life. 

HIS FAITH IN JEHOVAH WAS SUPREME 

Moses had come to manhood with a supreme faith in Jehovah, his mother's God. He believed in one true and living God, the self conscious and intelligent maker and ruler of the universe. He believed that God is a benevolent God; that he can and does make himself known unto men; and brings men into fellowship with himself, requiring obedience on the part of mankind; and that he bestows blessing both temporal and spiritual for obedience; and permits disaster, both temporal and spiritual, for disobedience. He saw that, if Jehovah is the one true and living God, maker and ruler of the universe, then there is no place for any other god; and the gods of the 

Egyptians are no gods at all. If Jehovah is Creator and ruler of the universe, then he is the real God of the Egyptians as he is the God of Israel. If Jehovah is the God of the Egyptians, then he loves them and cares for them as he loves and cares for Israel; and gives them blessing for obedience and disaster for disobedience the same as he does Israel. If this be true, then there is

one God and one people. If there is but one God and one people, why are God's people divided against themselves, hating and fighting and killing each other? Moses dwelt on this thought of one God and one people for days.

MOSES FINDS THE ANSWER TO HIS QUESTION 

There is one God and one people. A benevolent God would administer to all alike, blessing for obedience, and disaster for disobedience. Here are two races, one master, the other slave, both loved and watched over by the same God. Why is the one master and the other slave?

Israel came into Egypt a free, strong, wealthy, and religious people. By the profit and rental systems they were reduced to slavery. The secret of this whole situation lay in the fact that the Egyptians controlled the land and property of Egypt, while Israel were renters or wage earners, obliged to labor under such conditions and for such wages as the Egyptians saw fit to give them. The 

Egyptians, having gained control over the land and property of Egypt, assumed control of the movements of Israel, forbidding them to come or go without their consent; thus Israel's subjugation was complete; a race once free and independent had become a race of slaves. 

Moses as a shepherd knew that he would not allow a part of his flock to assume control over the grazing country, and over the freedom of the balance of the flock, to the end that they might compel the weaker ones to gather all the grass and store it for them, until they had enough laid by to last them a thousand years; while

those who had gathered the grass had but a bare living without freedom of movement, or interest in that which had been stored or the land that produced it. On the other hand his tenderest care had been for the ewes and lambs and weak ones of the flock. Thus, Moses saw that a benevolent God would not want a part of his people to assume control of all the material resources which he had provided for all the people; and to make their control of these a pretext for assuming control of the balance of the people, compelling them to do all the work for a bare living, while the more fortunate lived in great luxury with enough layed by to keep them a thousand years.

Egypt was wrong in this whole matter; in time of national disaster instead of acting the part of men, she had followed the law of the forest, the strong have a right to the best for themselves, and assumed unwarranted control of the material resources of the country. 

Encouraged by this position of advantage, she assumed control of the lives of the men and women and children of Israel; and justified herself in killing the children to protect herself in her unwarranted privilege. Egypt must surrender, voluntarily or under compulsion, that which she holds unjustly, control over Israel. 

THE EMANCIPATOR 

Moses, the emancipator, was born a slave, under sentence of death. He had two mothers, one a slave, the other a princess. From his natural mother, the slave, he

received his blood, his faith, and his purpose. From his foster mother, the princess, he received his freedom and his culture. He loved them both, and he knew that the burdens and sufferings and the culture and pleasures of life were not justly divided between these two women. 

He knew the life of a slave, and he knew the life of the privileged class. Egypt had become privileged by following the law of the forest and taking unjust advantage in time of stress. She must now surrender that advantage. 

The emancipator asks not for advantage for himself or his, but for justice; and justice has the divine right of way.

IV. GOD BREAKS THE OPPRESSOR'S POWER GOD SPEAKS TO MOSES 

Moses was tending his flock in mount Horeb, when God appeared unto him in the burning bush; and told him that he had seen the affliction of his people; and had heard their cry by reason of their task-masters; and said unto him: "Come now therefore, and I will send thee unto Pharaoh, that thou mayest bring forth my people the children of Israel out of Egypt." He also told him that his brother Aaron would meet him and go with him. 

His mother's word had come true. God had spoken unto him as Israel's deliverer. And he remembered his mother's admonition, "When he speaks to thee, obey his every word."

Moses and Aaron went down into Egypt and called the elders of Israel together, and showed before them the

signs which God had commanded them to use; and told of their mission to lead Israel out of their bondage. And the people believed them and worshipped God because he had looked upon their affliction.

GOD'S METHOD OF MAKING MEN FREE 

When God would free a people, he sends a messenger to the oppressor, with a request that he let this people go. If the oppressor will not grant the request, God then informs him what he may expect if he does not grant the request; and upon further refusal God cuts away the very foundation on which the oppressor stands, until he takes by the blade, the sword with which he has fought to defend his unjust position, and hands it hilt first to his conqueror; thus he surrenders that for which he fought, and pleads that his very life may be spared. But, if he hath tempted God too far, this, his last request, shall not be granted.

God's way may seem cruel; and yet he gives men every opportunity to escape his wrath. All he requires is, that men surrender that which they hold unjustly; this he purposes that they shall do. Men who have followed the law of the forest, the strong have a right to the best for themselves, are dead to justice and mercy; and the groans of men, the prayers of women, and the blood of children make no appeal to them. They know no law but that which they have followed, the law of force and cunningness. 

Moses and Aaron went and told Pharaoh: "Thus saith

the Lord God of Israel, Let my people go." (Ex. 5.) 

Pharaoh not only refused to let Israel go, but commanded that their burdens be increased.

For those who fight against the almighty, there is no enduring victory; for it is man against God, the creature against the creator, they cannot win. For them there is only defeat, dark and terrible. Jochebed's prayer has been answered; her son, Moses, has led his people out from their bondage. The oppressor's power has been broken. It is not so much a question as to how God can deliver his people, as what shall happen to those who stand in the way of the Almighty? 

V. CAN MOSES KEEP HIS PEOPLE FREE 

Moses, you have known the life of the slave, and you have known the life of the master; you have seen the blindness to justice and mercy, of those whose vision and sense of justice were destroyed by their following the law of selfishness, the strong have a right to the best for themselves. You have broken a civilization, and buried its manhood in the depths of the sea, in order to free your people. Now, what new legislation are you going to give this people, that when they are come into their own land, the land of promise, the strong among

them may not follow this same law of selfishness, until they enslave the weaker; and another civilization will have to be broken, and its manhood destroyed to break another tyrant's power, and make another enslaved people free?

Moses' life as a shepherd makes it easy for him to understand the importance of the flock obeying their shepherd. He sees that the thing of supreme importance is obedience to God's every command. And God speaks to him saying, "Now therefore, if ye will obey my voice indeed, and keep my covenant, then ye shall be a peculiar treasure unto me above all people; for all the earth is mine; and ye shall be unto me a kingdom of priests, and an holy nation. These are the words which thou shalt speak unto the children of Israel." (Ex. 19:

PURPOSE OF THE ECONOMIC OR INDUSTRIAL CODE 

It is in the fourth general code of Moses, pertaining to the economic and industrial life, that we are now especially interested. The question with Moses is, how can Israel maintain their fidelity to Jehovah and to each other? 

1. The earth is the Lord's and the fullness thereof; the provision of a benevolent God for all his people; bestowed in abundance for obedience, and withheld for disobedience. The recognition of this fact holds the hearts and minds of men true to God and to one another. 

Was it not Pharaoh's unwarranted control of the material resources of Egypt that led to Israel's bondage and to the destruction of Egypt's civilization? When Israel shall have come into the land of Promise, Canaan, no combination less than the whole of Israel shall acquire control of the material resources of the land to the detriment of the most unfortunate child of Israel. Moses knows human nature, and man's proneness to seek for himself that which is best, to the disadvantage of others. 

What is the principle or law that will turn the tide of human nature, and make it flow parallel with the thought of a benevolent God? Is it not the same principle that is

found in God himself? The principle of helpfulness, helping others until they stand side by side with him who helped them? This is atonement; not the atonement of the blood of bulls and goats, but a living atonement; whereby one living being makes up to another living being that which he lacks, until they two stand together in blessing. The eternal fruit of such action is love and unity. How can Moses secure the working out of this great principle in the daily life of his people? 

2. The earth needs tending. So the first law must be: "In the sweat of thy face thou shalt eat bread all thy days." (Gen. 3: 19.) This is good as a general law, but as there is a part of the life of every finite being during which he is not able to produce his own living, there must of necessity be some modification of this general law to meet the necessities of the case. 

3. God's greatest law, helpfulness, is fully demonstrated in nature. Life itself is a gift from God and could not be attained, were it not freely given us. The earth is 

God's gift to men, we could not have made it; it was given us by a greater being doing for us what we could not do for ourselves. Every mother is a living illustration of God's great law; a greater being giving life and food and clothing and shelter and loving care to a lesser being, until the child walks by the side of its mother, a man or woman. Then, when the mother is old and would perish if left to herself, the child, now the greater and stronger of the two, furnishes to the mother food, clothing, shelter, and loving care, and by these gives to her added life. Thus at both ends of life

we have a living illustration of God's great law; each in his turn being the recipient and the giver of blessing. 

The human family could not pass the bounds of a single generation, save by the operation of this law. 

In the economic and industrial world this law has been reversed and the strong and able have compelled the weak to do for them what they ought to have done for themselves. This reversal of God's law has always been attended by disaster. It was Moses' task to find where the tide of life had been turned from its true channel, and to set it right; thus making God's law operative in the economic and industrial life of the world. 

4. Moses knew where the tide left the true channel. 

Two men were born equal heirs to the natural resources of earth. Their needs were practically equal, one required as much food, clothing, and shelter as the other. Their capacity for culture and happiness was about the same, but they were unequal in strength. They lived and labored together in friendship and happiness, until one day the stronger awoke to a realization of the fact that he was doing more than half of the work; he then stood at the parting of the ways.

God's way is to live and labor together, the strong making up to the weak or unfortunate what he lacks; thus going on in friendship and love.

The way of selfishness is for the strong to draw aside and separate his work from that of others, so that his superior strength or better fortune will yield to him personally a surplus. Then this surplus through interest, rents, and profits becomes a second source of income.

God said, "In the sweat of thy face thou shalt eat bread all thy days." But we have found an easier way.

We have found that larger and more constant income can be secured from interest, rent, and profit, than from labor. Men have found a new goal in life, namely: to gain surplus or property. 

This may be gained by honest toil, by cunningness, by theft, by corruption, by confiscation, in a hundred dishonorable ways; and when once secured, by the perfectly honorable means of interest, rent, and profit it can be henceforth made the source of perpetual income, enabling one to live without work in the midst of luxury. 

It was here that the tide of life turned from the true channel, and sent men and nations in a mad search for wealth, wherein they forgot justice and mercy and were led to sacrifice the nobler things for the sake of gold. 

Moses knew that Pharaoh had worked this plan in Egypt; he had gained a surplus as a personal asset. During the years of plenty, he bought the surplus grain. During the years of famine, he sold back to those who produce it, this grain at a profit, making it buy for him all the money, all the live-stock, and all the land of Egypt.

INCENTIVE IS CONFUSED 

This confusion or multiplicity of the bases of reward results in great confusion in the matter of incentive. It is not in keeping with the interests of society to offer men greater reward for wrong doing and the mere holding of property, than we offer for right doing and for service rendered. II. EFFORT, NOT OUTPUT OR CAPITAL, IS THE TRUE BASIS OF REWARD SOURCE OF MERIT OR DEMERIT 

The basis of merit or demerit is in our willingness, or unwillingness, and not in the measure of our strength. 

We are responsible for what we can do, not for what we cannot do. We can will to do a thing and can make an honest effort to do it, but if it is beyond our strength we are not responsible for not doing it; and should not be penalized for not doing what is beyond our power. 

OUTPUT NOT THE MEASURE OF EFFORT 

Output is not the true measure of ones willingness or effort or worth. The will, not muscle is the seat of effort. 

It is not fair to expect the weaker of two men to do as much work as the stronger; but it is fair to expect the stronger man to put forth as great an effort as the weaker man does. And when he has done this the two men are equal in their willingness or effort to be of service to their community. One is as worthy as the other; but the stronger man is not more worthy than is the weaker man. 

The true basis of reward is willingness or effort to be of service.

EFFORT, NOT OWNERSHIP, WORTHY OF REWARD 

We now speak of men and women who are mentally and physically competent. For such there is but one true basis of reward, and that is willingness and effort to be of service to society. Men have generally held that ownership of property is a legitimate source of income or reward. But Moses forbade us taking increase, and

Jesus forbade us laying up treasures on earth as a personal asset; and under his teaching, what surplus we hold is subject to these two laws: "Lend hoping for nothing again;" and "Give to him that asketh of thee."

So personal ownership of property cannot be made a legitimate source of income until we have rejected the teaching of Moses and Jesus Christ. Labor, not ownership, is the source of income. "In the sweat of thy face thou shalt eat bread all thy days."

Money or factories of themselves do not produce bread, clothing, or shelter. It is the human touch that makes both money and factory productive. There is a difference between getting and producing wealth. Labor produces wealth; ownership may be made an instrument by which one takes from another that which he produces.

CHAPTER VII  I. WHY THE APPEAL OF THE CHURCH HAS NOT MET WITH LARGER SUCCESS 

The church has not been a failure; and yet, its success has not been satisfactory. In going with our civilization, in building churches and preaching the Gospel in every community, the church has succeeded; but in bringing the lives of men into harmony with Christ, and establishing the kingdom of God, the church has failed. Let us remember that in our country, America, the church and school had an even start with our civilization; for no sooner was the log cabin home built, than the school and the church were established in the community. The school and the church have kept abreast with the home from the Atlantic to the Pacific and from the Lakes to the Gulf; and all the institutions of sin have lived and flourished in practically all of our towns and cities; corruption has marked our political life in every generation; and our industrial life has become a source of political corruption. 133

MEN ARE BORN AND LEARN LIFE IN A WORLD OF MATERIAL THINGS 

It is in this material world that men are born, and learn life. God has given us his law, showing our relation to each other. Our fidelity to his law and spirit, in the administration of material things, is the measure of our fidelity to him. "Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these, my brethren, ye have done it unto me.-Inasmuch as ye have done it not to one of the least of these, ye have done it not to me." (Matt.

We have not been properly instructed, and the children of our best homes, graduates of our colleges, members of our churches, come to manhood and womanhood champions of the principle of selfishness rather than supporters of the principle of helpfulness. This is not because these people have been inherently bad, but because the dominant influence touching and molding their lives has tended to drill them into an acceptance of the principle of selfishness, instead of the principle of helpfulness.

It is fair to take the men who are recognized as leaders in church, school, politics, and the commercial world, as examples of the kind of men that our present methods of instruction are producing. dom of God on earth; then we have a right to conclude that their training has been correct and has given us in church, school, state, and business world the kind of men that we want; and such methods should be continued. 

But if the lives of these men do not harmonize with the life of Jesus; and society under their leadership is not realizing peace, prosperity and happiness for all, even the kingdom of God on earth, then their training has been wrong and has not given us the type of men needed. 

We must have men who are intellectually honest. Men who in their intellectual life, have been so trained that they will recognize the life and interests of the unfortunate as sacred as their own; who will be true to justice regardless of the balance of power or personal interest. 

What we have taught has not given us a Christ like type of men; has not saved us from sin, hatred, and fear; nor given us a generation of honest men; has not enabled us to build the kingdom of God.

Our refusal to accept and teach the economic code of the Word of God, has resulted in the universal custom among us of setting aside our surplus as a personal asset, in disobedience to the teaching of Jesus: "Lay not up for yourselves treasures on earth." The third step in our departure from the law of God, resulting from our failure to accept and teach the economic code of God's Word, is our acceptance of a new goal in life. Our refusal to accept and teach the economic code of the Bible has held wide open the flood gate, through which has swept in the torrent that has submerged every generation in sin and disobedience; until our leading men in practically every walk of life are molded and fashioned after the law of selfishness rather than after the law of helpfulness.

HOME BUILDING

But where there is evidently plenty they are satisfied with what meets their need. Ten men dying of thirst, with only one quart of water, will be very exacting as to how it is divided. 

These same ten men at a flowing spring would be perfectly willing that each might drink all that he wanted. 

When through the law of helpfulness and the true basis of reward we come to recognize all good, as God's reward for fidelity, we will be ready to proclaim Jehovah as the one true and living God. Then we will have compassed the whole of the three codes of the Bible; and society will be ready to say, men shall not have any other God beside Jehovah, the true and living God. The life lived by the followers of Jehovah will be so superior and so perfect, meeting all of human need, that men generally will seek Jehovah and strive to enter into his kingdom.

THE WHOLE LAW OF THE KINGDOM PROVIDED FOR 

Thus, in the teaching of the church and the principle of collective ownership, rediscovered in our efforts, in practical life, to provide for all the people, the whole law of the kingdom is provided for. 

The church now recognizes the need of collective ownership. I quote from The Creed of the Churches, as given in the Discipline of the Methodist Episcopal 

Church, 1916, page 530: "Because the teachings of Jesus demand equality of opportunity for all, because their application means both the brotherhood of leisure and the brotherhood of toil, we call upon all our members to support the efforts of the industrial workers to secure release from fatigue and the benefits of leisure.--Christianity moves up to higher ground. It requires the supremacy of the principle of co-operation in the industrial world. The Church must, therefore, clearly teach the principle of the fullest possible co-operative control and ownership of industry and of the natural resources upon which industry depends, in order that men may be spurred to develop the methods that shall adequately express this principle. Then will industry become a religious experience, developing mutual service and sacrifice, the interpretation in economic terms of the brotherhood of man and the Fatherhood of God."

This condition has been brought about through a plain violation of the teachings of Moses and Jesus. Our people have been industrious, efficient, and economical; they have produced abundantly; but the few, following the law of selfishness, have taken all the surplus; and the people are left empty handed, disinherited and robbed of the greater part of their product and of the esteem and respect of their generation; for we make a difference between the man of wealth and position and the man of poverty. They have been robbed in open violation of the highest authority known to men, the teaching of Jesus.

What do we expect of this multitude? They are men

and women with sufficient intellectual training to see the injustice of this condition, and of sufficient culture to feel the sting of their humiliation; and they have the courage and the power to change the situation. 

We are beginning to recognize the value of the economic code of Moses and Jesus; to see that it is neither righteous nor in the interests of society in any way for a few of the people to hold all of the wealth, while the masses are poor. Men have begun to figure on the possibilties of society under collective or Christian ownership. And the results are startling to a generation of men and women to whom life has been one round of toil and sacrifice.

MEANING OF COLLECTIVE OWNERSHIP TO THE AVERAGE MAN OR WOMAN 

It is estimated that if all the land and property in the United States were owned collectively and all the people worked from seven to eight hours per day, we could produce enough to take care of all the educational interests of the children, keeping them in school until they were twenty-three years of age, pension all men and women at the age of sixty-five, and give to each adult man and woman between twenty-three and sixtyfive an annual income of at least three thousand dollars, six thousand per family.

A statement sent out by the Research Department of the Federal Council of the Churches of Christ in America, under date of December 8, 1923, says: "A well

known and conservative industrial engineer insists that if the managers of industry would make use of known devices and methods of conserving materials and power and raising production standards, industry could be made so profitable that even the unskilled wage earner might have an income equivalent to $10,000. per year." 

Under collective ownership, we could administer property and the product of toil on the true basis of reward, honest effort to be of service. Then no one would share in the products of society who was not willing to do his duty. All who were willing would stand on an equality in all the good things of earth. Then all incentive would be on the side of right, for all good, both spiritual and temporal, would hang on our doing the things that we ought to do. This to our millions who have lived lives of honest toil and severe sacrifice, and then have not been able to meet the requirements of a refined and cultured life, would mean the kingdom of heaven come to earth.

All loss arising from duplication in competition, from contentions between capital and labor; and all loss resulting from the keeping up of armies and navies, and from war, would be eliminated. And the whole effort of individuals and nations could be built into constructive work.

Can any one say that the program of the kingdom of God is not preferable to the program of capitalism organized about the principle of selfishness; the fruit of which we see in all the horror and cost of the World War, and the resulting world conditions? 

---

We have, practically, universal suffrage, and seventy percent of our people are almost without property. When they see that they can vote themselves out of poverty into independence and equality, wherein no one's burdens will be beyond his strength, and no one will know hunger, nor lack any good thing, what power is there in earth, heaven, or hell that will prevent them from doing it.

And when this multitude catches the thought that it is along this line that the eternal kingdom of God is to be built, and that the authority of the Bible is back of the plan; that it is God's will that they put forth their hand and take that which is their own, to his honor and glory and dominion, until his will be done in earth as it is in heaven; woe unto the man or men who stand in the way of this people; who representing the arm of the mighty 

God of heaven and earth, shall move forward to the establishing of the kingdom that shall be eternal.

THE TIME NOW COME 

The economic, social, moral, and spiritual need of the world demands a true interpretation of the Scriptures, in harmony with the life and teaching of Jesus. Such an interpretation supports collective ownership of property; which is necessary to secure a realization of the principle of helpfulness, and the true basis of reward in society. 

Why should we not, led by the spirit of God, in our economic necessity, reach the true interpretation of his 

Holy Word?

There is laid at our feet this material for the building of his kingdom on the principle of helpfulness. Seventy percent of our people are economically prepared for the acceptance and building of the kingdom. The economic, moral, and spiritual conditions of society demand that we build these materials into the kingdom of our God. 

VI. INCENTIVE FOR THE BUILDING OF THE KINGDOM DESIRE TO FOLLOW CHRIST AND ATTAIN LIFE 

Deep in the human heart are two desires; to follow 

Jesus, who, when lifted up, by the very merit of his life and teaching, draweth all men unto himself. And the desire for life in all its fullness. Under our present system these are not realized; in the kingdom they can be. 

WILL ESTABLISH THE TRUE BASIS OF REWARD 

Following the principle of helpfulness, and establishing collective ownership, we can realize the true basis of reward; laying upon each one his share of the world's work, in proportion to his strength; providing equally for the needs of all, even the most unfortunate, until his inheritance is as secure as is the inheritance of the most fortunate.

IT IS GOD'S PLAN OF SALVATION 

This is God's own plan of salvation, eliminating selfishness, with all its train of evils; and enthroning the prin-

ciple of helpfulness, which ever perpetuates itself in service, peace, and happiness.

VII. OUR CREED I BELIEVE: 

In God, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit; in the Fatherhood of God and the brotherhood of man; that there is one God and one kingdom, including earth and heaven.

That the law of helpfulness, or of atonement, as revealed in the life and teaching of Jesus, is fundamental to the kingdom, and must bind all the people, and all the resources of the kingdom, both spiritual and temporal. 

That willingness to try to be of service is the true basis of reward, and that temporal affairs should be administered on this basis. 

That it is the duty of all men, by the help of the Holy Spirit and the example of Jesus, to bring their lives into conformity to the life of Jesus; and to seek the reorganization of society about the principle of helpfulness, realizing the true basis of reward, and the establishment of the kingdom of God on earth. 

That if we honestly endeavor to follow Jesus, we prove ourselves worthy and shall come at death into the spiritual and eternal side of God's kingdom, to dwell with him forever.

I believe in the Bible as the Word of God; and that it is the purpose of the economic code of Moses and Jesus Christ to make the principle of helpfulness binding in the economic and industrial world; and that Chris-

tion or collective ownership of property is necessary for the realization of the principle of helpfulness, the true basis of reward among men, and the building of the kingdom of God on earth.

VIII. OUR IMMEDIATE TASK 

The church has believed in and taught the incarnation of Jesus; that he obeying the law of helpfulness in his self-sacrifice for the sake of helping others, atoned for the sins of all men. But she has refused to carry the principle of helpfulness over into the affairs of this world and make it binding in the economic and industrial world; she has not sought to know the true basis of reward in temporal affairs; but has been content, in the administration of wealth, to follow the law of selfishness, which is the very antithesis of the law of helpfulness. 

We need men with a full orbed vision of Jesus and of the kingdom of God; men who believe in and live the spirit filled life; men who also believe in the principle of helpfulness as the fundamental law of the kingdom, and that this law must bind all men and all of the resources of the kingdom; men who believe in the true basis of reward; and have some practical conception as to how these can be written into the authoritative law of society, making them to bind all men and all of the resources of the kingdom.

There are many in our churches who would gladly accept the economic interpretation of the Scriptures, to their spiritual development. There is a great multitude

of men and women who are not connected with the church who would gladly accept Jesus and his kingdom if presented from the economic standpoint; it is from this standpoint that they have learned life. Jesus himself said, "I, if I be lifted up from the earth, will draw all men unto myself." The fact that we are getting such meager results in our church work is evidence that we are not true to Jesus.

OUR IMMEDIATE TASK 

Our immediate task is to get a full orbed vision of Jesus and of his kingdom; and to direct the reorganization of society about the principle of helpfulness; which principle must bind all men and all the resources of the kingdom, both spiritual and temporal; making possible the true basis of reward, which is willingness to be of service.

THE BUILDING OF THE KINGDOM IS WITHIN OUR REACH 

The building of the kingdom is easily within our reach. 

Righteousness has the right of way. God's law is the world's constitution. God has given us dominion in the earth. We have prayed, "Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven." Every one who has prayed this prayer, if he has been honest, has consented to the building of the kingdom. And God has heard this prayer, as it came from the lips of father and mother and child; and has granted the request, and given authority for the building of the kingdom.

IX. PRIVATE OWNERSHIP AND PLUTOCRACY: 

OR COLLECTIVE OWNERSHIP AND DEMOCRA- CY, LEADING ON TO THE KINGDOM, WHICH? 

AUTOCRACY AND DEMOCRACY 

Either of the principles of autocracy or democracy may be extended to the political, religious and industrial life of a people. The one would be an autocracy, the other a democracy.

In the United States these two principles have developed together. In government and religion we have built around the principle of democracy; but in the industrial world we have left each one free to build his own industrial autocracy. 

Most of us have lived in small circles, faithful to our tasks; others have lived in larger circles, including the natural resources, the great industries, and the great thoroughfares of transportation and communication. 

These have built for themselves mighty industrial autocracies.

PLUTOCRACY 

And to save themselves the expense and danger of fighting each other, and to make themselves collectively more secure and absolute in their power and influence, they have come together and formed a plutocracy. Thus, we have in a political democracy a combination of industrial autocracies into a plutocracy, before which the autocratic kings of the old world have bowed; and at

the feet of which the masses of the American people have fallen begging for an opportunity to work lest they starve.

This plutocracy has extended itself and invaded the field of government, corrupting legislatures and courts, capitalizing every form of vice, hoping thereby to make its hold in the field of government more secure; it has entered the field of religion, and captured strategic points that tend mightily to hold the church in support of its rule of greed. Today this plutocracy in the United States controls the natural resources, the great thoroughfares of transportation and communication, the large industries, and our currency; which control enables it to turn the steams of wealth to itself, taking the last dollar from the masses over and above their homes and a bare living. 

And the great majority do not own the homes in which they live.

This plutocracy has taken unto itself the wealth of our country, in open violation of the plain teachings of Moses and Jesus; and in the presence of a silent church. 

Either democracy in government will pass, or plutocracy in industry will pass. They cannot long endure together.

That democracy in government and religion is endangered by plutocracy in the field of industry is perfectly evident. But that democracy in government and religion is endangered by the extension of this same principle of democracy to the field of industry does not appear.

DEMOCRACY AND COLLECTIVE OWNERSHIP 

As democracy is extended to the field of industry, private ownership of the natural resources and the large industries will have to give way to collective ownership; because in a hundred generations, men have proven themselves incapable of playing the game of life fairly while holding autocratic or plutocratic power in either of the fields, of government, religion, or industry. 

FUTURE CONFLICTS 

We face future conflicts, with democracy on the one hand, trying to extend itself over the field of industry; and plutocracy on the other trying to beat off democracy from the field of industry, and to capture for itself the fields of government and religion. 

PRINCIPLE ON WHICH AUTOCRACY AND PLUTOCRACY REST 

There is no middle course here that will lead to the elimination of the principle out of which grows both autocracy and plutocracy, namely, private ownership, or autocracy in my own field of activity when it becomes so large that I require the help of others. When others. help with the work, they must have a part in the management; otherwise I will hold an unfair share of our product as my own, to the exaltation of myself over those who made success possible, where I alone would have failed.

DEMOCRACY WILL BE BETRAYED 

If we save the principle on which autocracy and plutocracy rest, we betray democracy and defend autocracy and plutocracy. This principle of private ownership, or self authority in industry, is the last ditch of autocracy and plutocracy. It is possible that there are many men of position and wealth who have advised men to offer their lives in defense of democracy in the World War, who when autocracy and plutocracy have been driven to the last ditch, will betray democracy and advise other men to fight to keep alive the very principle out of which grow both autocracy and plutocracy. 

DEMOCRACY WILL TRIUMPH 

That democracy will be extended to the field of industry there is no question; it cannot be otherwise if we maintain our schools and continue to educate the masses. "Only a peace between equals can last; only a peace the very principle of which is equality and a common participation in a common benefit." The principle expressed in these words spoken by President Wilson to the Senate, January 22, 1917, applies to men as to nations. We face a supreme opportunity; if we will, we can set Jesus in the midst of this new democracy as its inspiration and head. 

But if the church, recognizing itself as having the sole right in things religious, persists in upholding the principle on which autocracy and plutocracy rest, then the church will become a hindrance rather than a help in the building of the kingdom of God.

When the battle is carried to the last ditch, the very principle on which autocracy and plutocracy rest, there will be no place for neutrality. Men on either side will have no patience with a church that does not make the authority of the Bible uphold their cause. Whether that battle shall be more terrible than the World War depends upon our thought and action in the near future. If we set the Bible, Jesus, and the church on the side of democracy, we will take this last ditch without blood. But if we undertake to set the Bible, Jesus, and the church across the path of human progress, men will batter their way through at any cost. For we have taught men, that to be neutral while the battle is raging between autocracy and democracy, is to be a slacker while to give one's life in defense of democracy is holy. 

While this last battle between the principle of selfishness, which leads to autocracy and plutocracy, and the principle of helpfulness, which leads to democracy and the kingdom, is being fought, he who advises compromise will be the pacifist, the slacker, the copperhead of the times of the establishing of the kingdom of God. But let us leave out the epithets that bite deep into the souls of men; and let us abhor everything bordering upon mob violence. Let us hold closely to law and order, and fight our conflict through at the ballot box, rather than on the field of battle. But, when men refuse to abide by the ballot box, precendent teaches us that those who believe in, "Freedom for all forever," must take the sword.
