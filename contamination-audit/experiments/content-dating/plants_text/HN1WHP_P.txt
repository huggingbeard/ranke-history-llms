"side is pierced" but His "legs are not broken". Hence He may stand erect again in the full glory of His Resurrection. Then is the Earth again "the Bride of the Radiant Lord, Iesa, newly risen, erected in His splendor", to quote from the ancient Gaelic lore. 

At this time of Easter the Risen Lord and Sun beams lovingly down in warmth and ardor upon His Bride (the Earth) who is now gaily bedecked in bursting buds and fragrant blossoms to welcome Her Husband. Most tenderly He woos Her anew.

He caresses Her fondly and awakens and quickens Her to full receptivity and responsiveness that His union with Her may be in both Equilibrium and Reciprocity, that She may partake of, and be vitalized by, His generative power and bring forth into birth Her manifold fruits which bless all the inhabitants of this sphere. The foregoing facts are unerringly confirmed by the factor of the date of Easter and how it is established yearly, it being variable within certain limits. The date of 

Easter in the year is always the first Sunday after the first full moon after the crossing of the vernal equinox by the Sun. Lack of Space just here prohibits a full and lucid discussion of why this is the case and what it so powerfully portrays. Spirit and the Earth Soul while the Moon is Soul also, it being the satellite and "handmaiden" of the Earth.

of the north He is simultaneously being Resurrected to those far to the south in "the lands down under" for whom spring is just beginning. And when He is "dead" and "in the tomb" to us of the north (in winter) He is living in His full prime and vigor in the far south which is then experiencing summer. And when He is "dying" to them (at our vernal equinox) He is at the same time being Resurrected for us.

Hence it is seen that the Sun has two Wives on Earth, one of which is the northern hemisphere and the other the southern hemisphere. He divides His time impartially between the two Wives, being absent from, and with, each of them for half the year, in the sense of His strength and generative power. In this sense He is "Abraham", too; and in yet another He is "Jacob" as well, the allegory of the "four wives and twelve sons" of the latter being pregnant with light for one in whom true wisdom dwells.
