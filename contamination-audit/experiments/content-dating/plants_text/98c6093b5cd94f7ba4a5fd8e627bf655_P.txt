GUIDE TO USE Organization This directory is arranged alphabetically by state, and then by city within each state. The maps herein show the geographical locations within a state of those cities where there are hearing sites. As noted, the directory was compiled from information obtained from various agencies that responded to our questionnaire. Each listing is presented essentially as it was provided by the agency in response to our questionnaire.
Listings are organized under the following headings: CITY: FACILITY: CONTACT: SIZE: REMARKS: SOURCE: Each city in which there is a hearing or conference room listed alphabetically.
The name of the facility, and if provided to us, a street address.
Information regarding whom to contact in order to reserve the facility. When possible, a name has been given as well as a telephone number. If available, both FTS (Federal) and commercial phone numbers have been listed. If no number is listed, none was provided by the agency supplying the information. In most instances the telephone number can be obtained by contacting directory assistance.
These data appear either as physical dimensions (20'x20', for example), or in terms of seating capacity (such as "Seats 30"). Size listings were supplied by the agencies, and in most instances are only approximate.
Contains other general information which may be helpful, including subjective comments made by several agencies. Information regarding the presence of such items as a raised dais, witness box and auxiliary rooms, would be listed in this column. However, its absence does not necessarily mean that the facility lacks these accommodations. It only means that we were not supplied that information. If you need particular accommodations, it is wise to double check when you call to seek reservations. "ABA" means that the site is available as part of the Law School Program of the American Bar Association's National Conference of Administrative Law Judges. This program seeks to increase law students' familiarity with the administrative process by encouraging their attendance at actual hearings in law school moot courtrooms, facilities that are often underutilized. These sites, however, may be subject to scheduling difficulties due to precommitments and school recesses. Further information on the program is available from Diane Livingston, (312) 621-9200.

The initials in this column indicate our source for the particular listing. Multiple listings in this column mean that we have combined data submitted by more than one agency regarding that facility. Please see Appendix for a complete listing of the abbreviations used for contributing agencies.

Maps Following the listings for each state, a map of that state is provided to indicate the approximate locations of the cities in which hearing facilities are located. These maps are not drawn to any particular scale, but may help you locate a nearby town in which to hold a hearing if there are no facilities available in the first location in which you seek space.
Scheduling Tips The advance time required for reserving facilities varies widely among the locations listed, making it difficult for us to provide specific information regarding reservations. Generally it is best to reserve as soon as the need for an out-of-town hearing site is known, so that alternative arrangements may be made if necessary. In addition it is important that the lending agency be notified promptly of cancellations of scheduled hearings.
Agency rules regarding the use of their facilities vary somewhat, but the requirements listed below (supplied by Elsie Moran at the Tax Court) are fairly typical: 1. Smoking or consumption of food and beverages shall not be permitted in in the courtroom. (Ed. note: There have been many complaints about the condition of some courtrooms after a hearing. To insure a welcome the next time your agency needs the facility, leave it in good condition.) 2. Borrowing agencies shall restrict any congregating and excessive noisemaking in or outside the courtroom. (Tax Court also prohibits photographing and broadcasting of any proceeding in its facility.) 3. Moving of furniture or equipment in the facilities is prohibited unless approval is obtained.
4. The presiding Judge is requested to announce from the bench that existing telephone facilities are for his official use only. Long distance calls are to be placed on the FTS network only. Any calls placed off the FTS network will be charged to the borrowing agency.
5. Borrowing agencies shall be responsible for any damages to facilities or furnishings.
6. Please notify the lending agency immediately if your session is cancelled or completed prior to the final date requested, and mail a confirming follow-up letter of cancellation.
7. On arrival pick up courtroom keys from the Building Manager or Federal Protection Officer specified at time of request. On completion of your session, please secure the facilities and notify the Building Manager or Federal Protection Officer prior to your departure. (Ed. note: When reserving space ascertain who will have the keys and where they should be picked up and returned.) ACKNOWLEDGMENTS This second edition of the Directory of Administrative Hearing Facilities results from the cooperative efforts of dozens of administrative law judges, administrators, and secretaries who took time to provide information on hearing sites their agencies have used.
Several others gave valuable assistance to the preparation of this guide. Stephen L. Babcock, the Conference's Executive Director, and Judge Marvin Morse, former Director of the Office of Personnel Management's Office of Administrative Law Judges, now at the Small Business Administration, enthusiastically supported the notion of a multi-agency compilation. Fellow staff attorneys Jeffrey S. Lubbers, David M. Pritzker, and William C. Bush gave aid and advice on publishing this volume that eliminated countless headaches; Eunice Butler, Sharon Anderson, Barbara Harmon and Susan Mack provided extensive secretarial skills; and librarian Sue J. Boley shepherded the volume through to final publication.
In updating the directory, we have not varied from the format used in compiling the first edition. This format was devised largely by Phyllis Whitten, whose labors on the first edition were truly prodigious. The difficult and time-consuming work required to verify, update, and compile dozens of agency responses of varying quality into a revised version was performed by Ann Wozencraft, a 1984 summer intern at the Conference. Without their efforts, there would be no second edition of the directory.
Several agencies contributed funds to help defray the Conference's expenses, including the Department of Labor, the Federal Trade Commission, the Merit Systems Protection Board, the National Transportation Safety Board, the Nuclear Regulatory Commission, the Occupational Safety Health Review Commission, and the U.S. Postal Service. Thanks also to the American Bar Association's National Conference of Administrative Law Judges for permitting us to include listings from its Law School Program Geographical Directory in this volume.
