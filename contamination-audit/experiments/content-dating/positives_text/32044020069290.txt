PREFACE 

HIS volume is presented to the directors and members of the Third Order of Saint Francis as an aid to their closer acquaintance with the Franciscan Tertiary saints and blessed. 

The life of each of these holy subjects is sketched in a few words, an official prayer of the Church is added, and a thought is appended from the writings of Saint Francis, the founder of the Third Order. The book is thus more a work of devotion than of scientific research. 

Yet it is not a mere compilation. It represents years of research, for its origin goes back to the Christmas of 1923. On that day the Reverend Louis Biersack, O. F. M. Cap., professor of Greek at Saint Lawrence College, a minor seminary at Mount Calvary, Wisconsin, had invested some of the laity with the scapular and cord of the Third Order. They asked him about the lives of the patrons who had been given to them in the investiture, and he promised to set down some notes for them. 

He felt it would be an easy task, with all the books at his disposal in the library of the friary; but it proved far otherwise. 

He discovered that there existed no handy compilation; lives had been written of the most prominent Tertiary saints and blessed, but the list was far from complete. He discovered also that authors were in contradiction in many cases as to who should be counted a Tertiary. So he set himself the task of discovering the real answer to many perplexing questions. 

When the Most Reverend Benno Aichinger, O. F. M. Cap., saw Father Louis' collected notes, he encouraged him to bring the work to a conclusion. The Very Reverend Theodosius Foley, O. F. M. Cap., advised him to have the lives printed as a treasury for Tertiaries. It was at this period that the present writer agreed to edit the work and prepare it for the printer. The task

was not too difficult, for the notes were in order, and the prayers and thoughts had been indicated.

The PRAYERS are the official ones of the Church for the respective saint or blessed, whenever such prayers could be located; otherwise they are generally from the common Mass of that class of saints. The THOUGHTS are taken from The Writings of St. 

Francis of Assisi, edited by Father (now Archbishop) Paschal 

Robinson, O. F. M. (Philadelphia, the Dolphin Press, 1906).

The bibliography at the end of the book will give an idea of the amount of material that was actually mastered by Father Louis in order to get an approximately correct view of the field. 

And yet the research is not at an end. New views constantly arise. Names are presented of some whose beatification is unrecorded. Others, who have certainly been placed on the roster of the blessed, are claimed as Tertiaries by some authors but cannot definitely be proved to have belonged to the Third Order. (A list of these names is appended to this work.) 

In view of these conflicts and uncertainties, a definite mode of procedure had to be established. It was determined to omit all who are not known to have been approved by the Church as saints and blessed, and of those who have been approved, to deal with those only who died as Tertiaries. This of itself excluded those who died as members of the First or Second Orders of Saint Francis. It excluded the founders of other religious orders and congregations who, although they received the inspiration for their new mode of life when they were Tertiaries, yet sanctified themselves in their new communities. A list of such former 

Tertiaries who became founders of other religious communities is also appended to this book. Those founders who adopted the 

Rule of the Third Order as their new norm of life are naturally given among the Tertiary saints and blessed, for the Third Order includes not only priests and lay people living in the world, but

PREFACE IX 

also all members of religious communities who profess the Rule of the Third Order- and their number is very great. 

Finally, this book excludes the many saints and blessed not founders, who died as members of other religious communities but who before joining these bodies had begun their sanctification in the Third Order. Although some authors include these among the Tertiary saints, it does not seem in accordance with the mind of the Church to do so. 

The list of Tertiary saints and blessed, as compiled by Father 

Louis, was sent to the Reverend Sylvester Brielmaier, O. F. M.

Cap., librarian at the International Capuchin College in Rome, to be submitted to the authorities in this field. With the approved list as a basis, the present book was written. A few emendations were made after this investigation, as new sources were discovered. 

Unfortunately the present unsettled conditions do not permit close communication with Rome, and therefore these later changes have remained without authoritative investigation. Father Louis does not claim that his list is absolutely correct, but it is as nearly correct as his many years of careful research can make it. He will, of course, be most grateful to anyone who can inform him with some show of proof what further additions or deletions should be made. His list is definite; it does not presume to be definitive.

Special interest must attach to the fact that of the whole number of Tertiary saints and blessed, forty-four were Japanese. 

Seventeen of these died on the cross February 5, 1597, together with six Franciscans of the First Order and three Jesuits. These twenty-six were beatified only thirty years later, in 1627; they were canonized in 1862. After this first holocaust the persecution in Japan subsided for some time, but it was resumed with renewed fury in 1614. During this later period thousands of 

Japanese Christians, together with many missionaries, are said to have lost their lives for the Faith. Two hundred five of them

were beatified in 1867, including thirty-six Jesuits, twenty-five Franciscans, twenty-one Dominicans, five Augustinians, one secular priest and one hundred seventeen lay persons. Twentysix of these latter were Tertiaries of Saint Francis, as was also the secular priest, and among them we find several children. The unusual times had warranted the unusual procedure of receiving little ones into the Third Order. This fact is accepted by most of the biographers. 

If the enumeration of Tertiary saints and blessed here presented is based on critical research, the short biographical sketches were written more for their devotional inspiration than for their critical correctness. They were taken from the usual popular lives of the saints as listed in the bibliography, with an eye however to correcting any contradictions that may have been discovered.

Tertiaries of today will find here brothers and sisters of the Third Order in all walks of life. They will find those who had difficulties paralleling their own. And they will find inspiration in the thought that others in circumstances like theirs fought the battle of the Lord under the banner of Saint Francis and emerged victorious as saints and blessed. May they go and do likewise!

BLESSED AMATUS RONCONI 

LESSED AMATUS was born at Saldezzo near Rimini, in Italy, in 1238. At an early age he lost his pious parents by death, so that he was reared by a relative; but he never deviated from the path of piety on which they had firmly set his childish feet. Repeated attempts to induce him to marry were frustrated by his own deep conviction that he must serve God alone. In this conviction he finally fled from his home to lead a hidden life of prayer and penance. Vested with the simple garb of the Third 

Order of Saint Francis, Amatus attained in thought and deed such detachment from earthly things that the worldly-minded considered him a fool. But God repeatedly gave the stamp of 

His approval to the saintliness of His servant by outstanding signs and miracles. More than once heavenly light shone over the hut where he lived and heavenly songs were heard to proceed from it. Amatus had an especial fondness for pilgrimages and frequently went on foot to the famous shrine of Saint James. the Apostle at Compostella in Spain. He assisted the poor and the sick whenever he found it possible, and among his charities was the founding of a hospital, that of Saint Mary of Mount Orciale, where he spent his own declining years, not as patient but as nurse. After a life of prayer, mortification and works of charity, he passed away in 1304, in the sixty-sixth year of his age, already considered a saint by all who knew him. Because of the many miracles wrought after his death, the immemorial cult paid to him was approved by Pope Pius VI. 

Prayer O God, who dost gladden us by the yearly festival of blessed Amatus, Thy confessor, vouchsafe in Thy mercy that we who keep his birthday may follow the pattern of his life.  3

Thought Praised be my Lord for those who for Thy love forgive, And weakness bear and tribulation.

JANUARY 4 BLESSED ANGELA OF FOLIGNO 

ORN at Foligno, Italy, in 1248, Angela passed her youth in frivolities and sin. She married early and by God's design the care of a large family was the means of her conversion. In the midst of these responsibilities she began to see the danger in which her soul lay, and to feel bitter remorse. However, she lacked the courage to make a complete confession and in consequence received Holy Communion unworthily. In spite of this grave sin she managed at last to break the fetters of her former life, and was fully absolved. Her heart was now possessed by a constant and deep sorrow, which was assuaged only by meditation on the Passion of the Saviour. By degrees she forsook rich attire and delicate food. Finally she made a vow of perpetual chastity, to go into effect when circumstances should permit. Yet in all her sacrifices, she felt rather shame than love; and in her personal life she still showed self-esteem and resentment at slights. But God took a hand to complete her conversion. 

By the death of her mother, a very worldly woman, a bad influence was removed; then in quick succession, Angela's husband

and sons also died. Left alone in the world, she now began to give her whole heart to prayer. One day, whole reciting the 

Our Father, she was thoroughly overcome by the thought of God's infinite mercy and her own base ingratitude. She became a member of the Third Order of Saint Francis and began to practice great austerities. Within a year she was well on the way to holiness. Toward the end of her life she was always unwell, and the solitude of the sickbed, the constant pain, the continued temptations, the loss of every earthly friend, all combined to convince Angela of her entire dependence on God. The things she had abhorred-poverty, suffering, contempt of the worldnow became the sole object of her desires. Her writings reveal a wonderful knowledge of God and perfect detachment from every worldly thing. God called her to her eternal reward in 

1309. She was beatified in 1693. 

Prayer O God, Thou sweetness of hearts and light of the blessed, who didst refresh blessed Angela, Thy servant, with a wonderful contemplation of heavenly things, grant that by her merits and intercession we may so know Thee on earth as to deserve to rejoice in heaven in the revelation of 

Thy glory.  

Thought Thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven: ..that we may love our neighbors even as ourselves, drawing to the best of our power all to Thy love, rejoicing in the good of others as in our own and compassionating [them] in troubles and giving offence to no one. 

-Praises and Paraphrase of the Lord's Prayer.

SAINT ANGELA MERICI 

AINT ANGELA was born in 1474. From her earliest youth she practiced the most severe austerities, and as a reward was granted familiar intimacy with angels and saints.

Having been told in a vision that she was to found a society of virgins for the instruction of the young, she entered the Third Order of Saint Francis. By increasing her penances, she prepared for her mission. People came from all parts of the country to visit this holy virgin who, although only twenty-four years old, spoke with more than human wisdom and lived like an angel on the Bread of Angels alone. Yet these favors from heaven brought her no closer to the fulfilment of the project imposed upon her. The wars and troublous times that came with the years prevented its execution, even as they at the same time increased its need. It was not until 1535, after forty long years of delay and discouragement, that Angela was able to lay the first foundation of her institute with twelve young companions. Her spiritual children, she said in her rule, were to be true virgins of Christ, living in their own homes, but sacrificing themselves for the poor, the ignorant and the afflicted, as Christ, their Spouse, had given 

Himself for them. That her own name might remain unknown, she placed the institute under the protection of Saint Ursula. 

It was still in its infancy when Angela was summoned to her reward in 1540; but with perfect confidence she left her daughters in the hands of Jesus Christ. She was canonized in 1807. Prayer O God, who through blessed Angela didst cause a new society of holy virgins to flower within Thy Church, grant us through her intercession to lead angelic lives,

BLESSED ANGELINE CORBARA 

NGELINE was born in 1377 at Montegiove, near Orvieto in Italy. Her parents gave her a solid Christian education. 

When her mother died, Angeline turned her thoughts to the life in the cloister; but three years later, in obedience to the wishes of her father, she was married to Count John of Terni. After the brief space of two years, her husband died. Having thus become her own mistress, Angeline now joined the Third Order of Saint

Francis and converted her household into what was in effect a body of secular Tertiaries living in community. Those of her female attendants who of their own accord stayed with her as Tertiaries, were entirely intent on their own sanctification and on ministering to the needs of others. Angeline's castle thus became 

*Capuchin, July 14; Third Order Regular, July 21.

a school of sanctity, as well as a haven for the sick and needy. 

But her power to attract young women to her home and imbue them with the great ideal of virginity for the sake of Christ led to her being accused of sorcery, and after some time she was exiled from the Kingdom of Naples with her companions. She fled to Assisi, where God clearly revealed to her that she should found an enclosed convent of the Third Order at Foligno. Pope 

Boniface IX, as well as the Bishop of Foligno, approved of her plan. Soon her first convent was completed. God looked with favor upon her work, for within her own lifetime she was able to found ten other convents. As abbess, she was the soul and the inspiration of all of them. After a life of wonderful holiness, she was called to her eternal home in 1435. She was beatified in 1825.

Prayer 

Hear us, O God our Saviour, and grant that we who are gladdened by the festival of blessed Angeline may learn therefrom loving devotion toward Thee.  

Thought How much interior patience and humility a servant of God may have cannot be known so long as he is contented. But when the time comes that those who ought to please him go against him, as much patience and humility as he then shows, so much has he and no more. -Admonitions.

FEBRUARY 5 SAINT ANTHONY DOYNAN 

NTHONY is a fragrant flower of the Japanese persecution of 1597. He was born at Nagasaki of a Chinese father and a Japanese mother-poor in the world's goods but both exemplary Christians. The little boy was baptized soon after his birth, and was subsequently so well instructed in the Faith that he found no greater delight than to be with Jesus. As soon as the Franciscan friars arrived at Nagasaki, Anthony became their constant companion. Their church was his main point of attraction, and in it he was always to be found before the picture of the Blessed Virgin Mary, whom he loved and honored with all the ardor of his youthful heart. The friars understood the inclinations of the lad, and his repeated entreaties, backed by the ready consent of the parents, led them to receive him into their friary as a Tertiary, in the hope of making a catechist of him. 

He soon surpassed even their expectations by his progress in perfection, and great things were looked for from him in the future. But God, in His inscrutable designs, had arranged for 

Anthony an early crown of martyrdom. When the boy was only fourteen years old, persecution was unloosed against the Christians, and he became one of the Japanese martyrs on the cross. This occurred on February 5, 1597. He was canonized, together with the others, by Pope Pius IX in 1862. 

Prayer 

O Lord Jesus Christ, who didst consecrate the first fruits of the Faith among the Japanese in the blood of the holy martyrs Peter Baptist and his companions through their suffering of the cross in imitation of Thee, grant, we beseech Thee, that we may be inflamed by their example whose feast we celebrate today. Who livest and reignest forever and ever. Amen.

Thought And let all the brothers, wherever they may be, remember that they have given themselves, and have relinquished their bodies, to our Lord Jesus Christ; and for love of Him they ought to expose themselves to enemies both visible and invisible, for the Lord says: "Whosoever shall lose his life for My sake, shall save it" in eternal life. 

— First Rule of the Friars Minor. 

BLESSED BARTOLO BUONPEDONI 

ARTOLO, surnamed "the Job of Tuscany" on account of his inexhaustible patience in suffering, was born at Mucchio near San Gemignano in Italy, of the ancient house of the Counts of Mucchio. The incessant prayers of his mother, who had been childless for twenty years, were finally answered by the birth of this son. It is said that he surpassed all his youthful companions in wisdom and knowledge, and in love of virtue. The people called him an angel of peace. Bartolo early felt an inclination to the priesthood, but his father, who had more worldly designs for his only son, would not listen to the idea and put every possible obstacle in the path of its fulfilment. 

Finally Bartolo left his home and was received by the Benedictines at Pisa as a nurse for the sick. The monks were most willing to accept him into their ranks, but the saintly young man felt no inclination for the monastic life. At the advice of his spiritual 

*Capuchin, December 12; Third Order Regular, May 10.

director he returned home, and was able to prepare himself for the priesthood with the help of the local bishop. He was ordained at the age of thirty, and put in charge of the parish at Pichena. 

For ten years he worked successfully among his people, giving them a splendid example of charity, self-denial and zeal for souls. 

It was during this period that he became a member of the Third 

Order of Saint Francis. At the age of fifty-two, he fell a victim to the dreadful disease of leprosy. Relieved of his duties as pastor, he retired to the leper hospital at San Gemignano with another holy Tertiary, Blessed Vivaldus. There, as director of the leper asylum, he spent the rest of his life in quiet resignation to the holy will of God. He never ceased praising the Almighty for his many sufferings, and it is said that as a reward of his patience his leprous body exuded a heavenly perfume. God called the resigned sufferer to his eternal reward on December 12, 1300. He was counted among the blessed in 1910. 

Prayer O God, who dost gladden us by the yearly festival of blessed Bartolo, Thy confessor, vouchsafe in Thy mercy that we who keep his birthday may follow the pattern of his life.  

Thought And I ask the sick brother that he give thanks to the Creator for all things, and that he desire to be as God wills him to be, whether sick or well; for all whom the 

Lord has predestined to eternal life are disciplined by the rod of afflictions and infirmities, and the spirit of compunction; as the 

Lord says: "Such as I love I rebuke and chastise."

SAINT BENEDICT JOSEPH LABRE 

5 AINT BENEDICT JOSEPH LABRE, the holy tramp, was born at Amettes in the Diocese of Boulogne, France, in the year 1748. Several attempts on his part to enter religious life were unsuccessful. Finally, at the age of twenty-two, listening to the special call of God, he forsook both his parental home and his country, broke off all communication with his relatives, and spent the rest of his life as a poor beggar going from one place of pilgrimage to another in Italy and Spain or in long hours of prayer in the sanctuaries of Rome. His clothing consisted of cast-off garments, his bed was most frequently the bare ground, his food was the morsels offered to him. He crucified his senses by continual mortification. The luxury and excitement of the cities had no attraction for him. He spoke little, and only when charity demanded it. A word of praise made him shudder, while blows and insults were his delight. His external mode of life was that of a tramp, but his motive was to follow as perfectly as possible the very special vocation traced out for him by God. 

His prayer was almost unbroken, and his love for Mary was so great that he would involuntarily break out in the salutation: "O Mary, O my Mother!" His devotion to the Blessed Sacrament was so pronounced that people knew him as the poor man of the 

Forty Hours' Devotion. Before the tabernacle he remained motionless and as if transfixed. Those watching him said that he was like a statue- or better, like an adoring angel or a saint in ecstasy. God favored him with the gift of prophecy and miracles. He died in Rome on the Wednesday of Holy Week, 1783. He was canonized in 1881. 

*Conventual, April 17.

Prayer O God, who didst cause Saint Benedict Joseph,

Thy confessor, through zeal for humility and love of poverty to cling to Thee alone, grant us through the intercession of his merits to despise all earthly things and always to seek after those which are heavenly.  

Thought And as pilgrims and strangers in this world, serving the Lord in poverty and humility, let them go confidently in quest of alms, nor ought they to be ashamed, because the Lord made Himself poor for us in this world. This, my dearest brothers, is the height of the most sublime poverty which has made you heirs and kings of the kingdom of heaven: poor in goods, but exalted in virtue. JULY 15 BLESSED BERNARD OF BADEN 

ERNARD was born in 1428 in the ducal castle near Baden- Baden, the second son of the Margrave James I of Baden. 

Most of his youth was spent at the courts of Charles VII of France and René of Anjou. He refused the hand of the Princess 

Magdalen, daughter of Charles VII of France, for he had decided to live a life of perfect continence. After the death of his father he left the government of his own territory to his

14. 

brother and entered the service of the Emperor Frederic III as military commandant and ambassador. In spite of his continual contact with the things of the world, he lived a very holy life. Friar John, the Franciscan who had received him into the 

Third Order, accompanied him on all his journeys as chaplain.

Bernard was first of all a man of prayer. While his companions sought worldly pleasures, he retired in order to spend hours in divine converse. He received Holy Communion at least twice a week, a practice most unusual at that time. Even at the gay court of Vienna he won the admiration of all by his devout life. 

At the same time he did not shirk his soldierly duties; and as an ambassador he gained his ruler's approbation so completely that he was employed on many delicate diplomatic missions, principally in connection with the crusade against the Turks. On one of the journeys devoted to the cause of the crusade he fell sick near Turin, in Italy, and there he died on July 15, 1458, at the Franciscan friary of Moncalieri. He was venerated as a saint by the populace on account of the purity of his life and the miracles wrought at his tomb. This cult was approved by the 

Church in 1481, and again in 1769. He is venerated as the second patron of the Grand Duchy of Baden.

Prayer 

Give ear, O Lord, to our humble prayers which we present unto Thee on the festival of blessed Bernard Thy confessor; that we, who trust not in our own righteousness, may be helped by the prayers of one well-pleasing to Thee. 

Thought And even the demons did not crucify Him, but you together with them crucified Him and still crucify Him by taking delight in vices and sins. Wherefore then can you glory? For if you were so clever and wise that you possessed all science, and if you knew how to interpret every form of

language and to investigate heavenly things minutely, you could not glory in all this, because one demon has known more of heavenly things and still knows more of earthly things than all men. ...

SAINT BONAVENTURE OF MEACO 

ONAVENTURE was born at Meaco, in Japan, of a Catholic father and a pagan mother. Although the father had the boy baptized, the mother's example had such a bad effect upon him that he fell away from the Faith, even serving for many years in the pagan temple. When the Franciscan friars arrived, their attention was called to the young man, and their urgent admonitions had a wholesome effect upon him. One

Sunday, while the faithful were assembled for holy Mass, he suddenly appeared at the church door, his head strewn with ashes, his body covered with sackcloth, a rope around his neck. 

Steadfastly he walked to the front of the church, foreswore his heresy, solemnly confessed his Faith, begged pardon for the bad example he had given, and earnestly promised to repair the damage done as far as lay in his power. From that moment he was a changed man. His actions became exemplary. In order to gain the graces needed for this new life and as a sign of his total conversion, he asked to be received into the Third Order of Saint

Francis. After some delay to test his good will, his wish was granted. He straightway became one of the most devoted fol-

lowers of Saint Francis. He assisted the friars wherever he could. 

As a catechist he helped to prepare many souls for the saving waters of Baptism. He became a model of humility, while his kindliness won many hearts to Christ. OCTOBER 8 SAINT BRIDGET OF SWEDEN 

RIDGET was born to the Swedish royal family in 1304.

When ten years old she was affected most profoundly by hearing a sermon on the Passion. The following night a vision showed her the Lord entirely covered with blood and a voice told her that this was caused by the insensibility of men to His love. 

From that time on the image of the crucified Saviour was always before her eyes. While working with her needle she would shed copious tears at the thought of those bleeding wounds. In obedience to the command of her father, she was married to

Prince Ulpho of Sweden. She became the mother of eight children, one of whom, Catherine, is honored as a saint. After some years of married life, she and her husband separated by mutual consent; he became a Cistercian, while she founded the 

Order of the Saviour in the Abbey of Wastein, Sweden. Begin ning in 1344, she received a series of most sublime revelations, all of which she submitted scrupulously to the judgment of her confessor. These revelations were thoroughly examined and approved by authorities in the Church. On command of the Lord, she undertook a pilgrimage to the Holy Land, and amid the very scenes of His Passion she was led more deeply into these holy mysteries. Although Bridget enjoyed such great privileges, she always remained most humble, gentle and charitable. The bull of canonization, 1391, states that she would not even raise her eyes without the permission of her confessor. She died in the year 1373.

SEPTEMBER 10 BLESSED CASPAR VAZ 

LESSED CASPAR VAZ was one of those noble Japanese who, in spite of continual danger, were never intimidated in the exercise of charity toward their fellow-Christians and the missionaries. Faithful and unafraid, he gave the splendid example of Christian manhood. Closely associated with his work was his equally courageous and undaunted wife, Mary. Frequently he harbored the missionaries when they were hunted down for the exercise of their sacred ministry. One day while he was giving shelter to one of them, Blessed Francis of Saint Mary, in his home at Nagasaki, a band of soldiers broke in. They arrested

Caspar and his wife, together with the missionary and the Tertiaries who had gathered about their spiritual father. In prison the captives were subjected to terrible sufferings. Finally

Caspar and his wife, who had shared with him all the tortures of imprisonment but who was not yet to be blessed by the grace of martyrdom. After their mutual consolations, Caspar walked courageously to the place of execution where, on August 7, 1627, his great holocaust was completed by fire. These Japanese martyrs were beatified in 1867.

SAINT CATHERINE OF GENOA 

ATHERINE was born of the noble Fieschi family at Genoa in 1447. A very pious child, she had a strong attraction to religious life when she was only thirteen years of age; but she was refused permission on account of her youth. When she

Pleasureloving and quick-tempered, he left Catherine to the solitude of her home the greater part of the time. This kind of life was an almost unbearable burden to her, and for a number of years she tried to drown her disappointment in a whirl of pleasure.

Yet she managed to keep her trust in God and did not relinquish her religious exercises. One day the Lord appeared to her laden with the cross. The impression of this vision was so great that 

Catherine immediately made a general confession of her whole life and began to receive Holy Communion every day, a practice which she kept up until the end of her life. Now she also turned to prayer to obtain the conversion of her husband. Financial ruin compelled them to exchange their palace for a humble home. 

By patience Catherine finally obtained a change of heart in her husband. They agreed to live together in continence and began to nurse the sick in the hospital of Pammatone. During the plague that lasted from 1497 to 1501 and carried away fourfifths of the inhabitants of the city, Catherine's charity was truly heroic. The hospital was put under her management and she sacrificed herself day and night for the sick. Yet she did not even then abandon her accustomed penances. Worn down by these exactions, she finally collapsed; and her last years were replete with suffering. On September 15, 1510, she breathed her last in great tranquillity of heart. The works she wrote show forth the greatness of her soul. She was canonized in 1737.

NOVEMBER 4 SAINT CHARLES BORROMEO 

HARLES BORROMEO, born in 1538, was raised by divine Providence to guide the destiny of the Church in the critical period shortly after the beginning of the Protestant revolt. In 1560, when he was only twenty-two years of age, he was created a cardinal and became the principal aide of his uncle, Pope Pius IV, in the administration of the affairs of the Church. 

His was the guiding hand in the closing sessions of the Council of Trent. He hastened the holding of the sessions, watched the deliberations, and firmly brought them to a conclusion. Then he entered upon the delicate work of putting the decisions into execution in his own Archdiocese of Milan. He restored discipline throughout his see, founded schools, established seminaries for the education of clerics, and by his community of Oblates trained the priests for perfection. His reforms were violently opposed by the government and also by some of the priests and religious

in his own archdiocese, and an attempt was even made on his life; but the saintly archbishop never faltered in what he knew to be his duty. Inflexible in restoring and maintaining wholesome order, he was yet a most tender father to his flock. He did not disdain to sit by the roadside teaching a poor man his Pater and Ave; nor was he deterred from entering a hovel in order to practice the works of mercy, even though his attendants were driven out by the odor. During the plague he refused to leave 

Milan, considering it his duty to remain in order to minister to the sick and dying. When his ready funds gave out under the strain of succoring the poor, he sold his possessions, including his very bed, to procure the means to assist them. Thus he lived and died a faithful image of the Good Shepherd, giving his life for his sheep in 1584. He was canonized in 1610.

Prayer 

Keep Thy Church, O Lord, under the unfailing protection of Thy holy confessor and bishop, Charles; and as his shepherdlike vigilance has exalted him in glory, so may his intercession make us always fervent in Thy love. Through 

Christ our Lord. Amen. 

Thought And I desire to fear, love and honor them [the priests] and all others as my masters; and I do not wish to consider sin in them, for in them I see the Son of God and they are my masters. And I do this because, in this world, I see nothing corporally of the most high Son of God Himself except His most holy Body and Blood, which they receive and they alone administer to others.... And we ought to honor and venerate all theologians and those who minister to us the most holy Divine Words as those who minister to us spirit and life.

SEPTEMBER 29 BLESSED CHARLES OF BLOIS 

HARLES was born about the year 1316 of Louis de Chatallon, Count of Blois, and Margaret, sister of King Philip VI of France. In his youth Charles showed himself both virtuous and brave. In 1341 he married Joan of Brittany, and thus became Duke of Brittany. However, his uncle, John de 

Montfort, disputed his claim to the dukedom. Charles did everything he could to settle the dispute peacefully, but without success. During the long and bloody war that followed, he made careful provision for those who suffered in the combat. To help the souls of the fallen warriors, he founded religious houses where the holy Sacrifice of the Mass should be celebrated and prayers offered for the repose of their souls. In a spirit of penance he wore a hairshirt and recited the divine office every day. In consequence the people considered him more a monk than a soldier. He demanded that his soldiers attend Mass daily, telling them when they remonstrated that they could afford to lose castles but they could not afford to lose one Mass. During four long years he was able to hold his enemies at bay, but at last the battle turned against him because John de Montfort had managed to receive help from England. In the decisive battle 

Charles was defeated, captured and sent to England, where he was kept imprisoned in the Tower for nine years. There he sanctified his days by great patience and devout prayer, so that he proved himself an edifying example to his jailers. Finally he was set free, only to be attacked anew by his relentless uncle. 

The last battle was fought at Auray on September 29, 1364.

Charles had received the sacraments as if preparing for his last journey. He was killed fighting bravely. Soon miracles were reported at his grave and people began to venerate him as a saint. 

This cult was confirmed by Pope Pius X in 1904.

Prayer O God, who dost gladden us by the yearly festival of blessed Charles, Thy confessor, vouchsafe in Thy mercy that we who keep his birthday may follow the pattern of his life.  

Thought Let the entire man be seized with fear; let the whole world tremble; let heaven exult when Christ, the

Son of the Living God, is on the altar in the hands of the priest. 

O admirable height and stupendous condescension! O humble sublimity! O sublime humility! that the Lord of the universe,

God and the Son of God, so humbles Himself that for our salvation He hides Himself under a morsel of bread. Consider, brothers, the humility of God and "pour out your hearts before 

Him," and be ye humbled that ye may be exalted by Him. Do not therefore keep back anything for yourselves, that He may receive you entirely who gives Himself up entirely to you.

BLESSED CHRISTINA MACABAI 

HRISTINA, or Oringa as she was first called, was born of poor parents at Santa Croce in the Arno Valley, in Italy. Devoted to recollection from her earliest years, she used every opportunity she could find while tending the flocks to give herself to prayer. Her purity was so sensitive that she trembled and even became ill when she heard an unclean word. Since she

was of unusual beauty, many suitors came to ask for her hand. 

Because she refused all these offers, her brothers maltreated her and tried to force her into marriage. In order to preserve her virginity, she fled to Lucca and there offered her services as a servant girl, asking only enough to sustain life. Then began for 

Oringa an existence of constant mortification. She always went about barefooted, even in the depth of winter; her bed was the bare ground; at times she did not take food for several days in succession. She was called the "missionary of Lucca," and converted many sinners by her example and prayer. With her mistress she made a pilgrimage to Rome, and there the people began to call her Christina, because of her holy life. The pilgrimage was also extended to Assisi. Here her exceptional beauty attracted a young lawyer, who used every possible means to bring her into his power. Again she was forced to flee to preserve her purity. Upon the advice of Blessed Veridiana she returned to her home at Santa Croce, where she became a member of the Third Order of Saint Francis. Her holy life attracted many young women to her and therefore she founded a convent of Tertiary Sisters, whom she led more by example than by precept. 

After many years of this heroic life, God called His faithful spouse to Himself in 1310. Because of the many miracles ascribed to her intercession, Pope Pius V approved the cult paid to her. Prayer

LARE was born at Rimini, in 1282, of noble and wealthy CLARE fortune of losing both her husband and her parents by death. 

Young and of a vivacious character, she gave herself up to

frivolous gaieties, which soon passed over into sinful indulgence. 

Even though she presently remarried, she did not forsake her scandalous associations. Once while praying in a church she seemed to hear a voice bidding her give up her sinful ways. This brought about a complete change. She asked for the habit of the Third Order of Saint Francis, wept over her sins and began to seek a life of penance. The death of her second husband left her free to follow a life of mortification. She slept on the ground; took only bread and water as her food; spent whole nights in prayer; and continuously begged of God the pardon of her sins. 

At Rimini she devoted herself to the welfare of the people to such an extent that she seemed to live only for them. She cared for the poor, begging on their behalf what she could not supply from her own resources; she nursed the sick; she became an angel of peace, sparing no pains to restore peace in the households from which it had fled; she tried to lead dissipated women to a better mode of life. It was perhaps an inevitable consequence that she should frequently become the object of calumny and persecution. 

Some found fault with her great zeal, others were irritated by her reproofs and exhortations. But Clare rejoiced in the humiliations and prayed for her persecutors. Several pious women put themselves under her guidance and she formed them into a community according to the Rule of Saint Clare of Assisi. She herself made profession of this Rule without, however, giving up her previous life of charity. She was called to her heavenly reward in 1346, and beatified in 1784.

FEBRUARY 19 SAINT CONRAD OF PIACENZA 

ONRAD was a wealthy nobleman of Piacenza in Lombardy.

He showed himself a kind husband and a devoted father, but he was unduly fond of the chase. God used this passion to lead him to a higher life. One day the game he was hunting took refuge in a thicket, which Conrad ordered to be burned. 

Just then a heavy wind set in and the little fire became a great conflagration causing widespread damage. Conrad fled in terror, and a poor woodcutter was accused of setting the fire, brought to trial and condemned to death on circumstantial evidence. 

Chastened by this turn of events, Conrad then came forward, confessed his guilt and promised to make recompense as far as lay in his power. He sold his property and retired, with his wife, to a small dwelling place. A call to religious life was the reward of this sacrifice. His wife took the veil and Conrad joined the 

Third Order of Saint Francis. After a pilgrimage to Rome, he fixed his abode in a cave near Noto, in Sicily, where he spent the remaining forty years of his life. In this solitude he was besieged by the evil spirit and by temptations to gluttony, which were later

increased by dainty food sent him by his friends. He kept the food, but ate it only when it had been spoiled. Malicious men once invited him to dine with them on a Friday under pretence of some pious necessity. They wanted to trap him into eating meat by having it dressed as fish, and then to expose him as one who was not so pious as he was considered to be. Conrad came and ate. At the end of the meal they began to mock at him. 

Conrad quietly showed them that he had eaten only fish, by displaying to them the remaining bones: God had miraculously changed the pork into fish to confound the conspirators. Every 

Friday Conrad visited the celebrated crucifix in Noto, and it was there that he was found dead on his knees, February 19, 1351. 

He was canonized in 1625.

Christ our Lord. Amen.

Woe to those who do not die in penitence, for they shall be the children of the devil, whose works they do, and they shall go into eternal fire. Beware and abstain from all evil, and persevere in good until the end.

FEBRUARY 5 SAINT COSMAS RAQUISA 

HIS Japanese martyr of Christ was descended from a distinguished pagan family of the Province of Ovari. 

Originally very wealthy, they were brought through various visitations to the brink of ruin. This compelled Cosmas, who had been reared in leisure, to look for work for his own support. He therefore learned the craft of arrow-making, and by incessant labor was able to regain some of his former position. It is not known just when he became a Catholic, but he had belonged to the Church for some time when the Franciscans came to Meaco. 

With his intimate friend, Saint Leo Carasuma, he helped them build a church and a friary. This contact with the friars made him one of their most devoted friends and an ardent assistant in the vineyard of the Lord. As soon as they had completed a hospital, he spent himself in the service of the sick. Although no longer wealthy, he freely gave abundant alms to the poor. Like many of his friends he became a member of the Third Order of Saint Francis, and like them he offered his services as a catechist. 

His son, who later suffered martyrdom with him, became a teacher in the school the friars had opened for the instruction of the pagan children. The zeal for souls was so ardent in the soul of 

Cosmas that he even dared to speak to the pagan priests regarding the falsity of their belief and the beauty of the Catholic Church. His ardent pleas brought many pagans into the fold of the Church, and the anger of the pagan priests knew no bounds. 

They had him arrested. With the other Japanese martyrs he died on a cross in 1597, and with them was canonized in 1862.

FEBRUARY 4 BLESSED ELIZABETH AMODEI 

LIZABETH AMODEI was born in the little town of Amelia, or Ameria, Sicily, in the year 1475. As in the case of some of the other saints, we find in her an extraordinary zeal for holiness, and a remarkable maturity in spiritual matters generally, when she was still very young. Before she received her first Holy Communion she had bound herself to God by the vow of perpetual virginity. It is known that she never lost her 

Baptismal innocence. While other children of her age enjoyed youthful pleasures, she preferred solitude and conversation with God in prayer. NOVEMBER 19 SAINT ELIZABETH OF HUNGARY AINT ELIZABETH, Patroness of the Third Order, was the daughter of Andrew II, King of Hungary, and a niece of Saint Hedwig. She was born in 1207. In her infancy she was betrothed to Louis, Landgrave of Thuringia, and taken to his father's court for her education. JULY 8 SAINT ELIZABETH OF PORTUGAL 

LIZABETH was the daughter of Peter III, King of Aragon. 

She was born in 1271, and in Baptism received the name of her saintly aunt, Saint Elizabeth of Hungary. At the age of twelve she was given in marriage to King Denis of Portugal. She had to suffer much from her dissolute and jealous husband, but she bore everything with complete patience, finding her greatest consolation in the holy Sacrifice of the Mass and in frequent prayer. Every day she recited the whole divine office. 

These pious exercises were, however, always arranged in such a way that they never interfered with her duties as queen and wife. 

For her frequent reception of Holy Communion she prepared by great penance and works of charity. A peacemaker from her very birth, she was instrumental in bringing about a reconciliation

between her father and grandfather after many years of enmity. 

Again and again she was called upon to bring together her husband and her son Alfonso, who went so far as to take up arms against his father. Her great patience and forbearance, together with her continuous acts of charity toward the sick and poor, eventually brought about a complete change of heart in her irascible husband, so that he became a devoted spouse to her and an exemplary ruler to his subjects. She built many charitable institutions and religious houses, among them a monastery for the Poor Clares. After the death of her husband she intended to enter this monastery, but the people used every means to persuade her to stay in their midst. She insisted, however, on taking the habit of the Third Order, and thus she spent the remainder of her life in pious works and penance. Before her death she was once again able to prevent a war, this time between her son and the King of Portugal. In 1336 she entered the home of eternal peace. Pope Urban VIII added her name to the roster of the saints in 1625.

SEPTEMBER 27 SAINT ELZEAR OF SABRAN 

(LZEAR, or Eleazar, was born in 1285 at Robians in Provence, France, of the ancient and illustrious family of Sabran. 

His pious mother, called "the good countess," offered him to God soon after birth and prayed that her boy might rather die than ever offend God by sin. Elzear lived like a saint from his earliest childhood. His greatest delight was to help the needy. Even in his early youth he did penance by wearing a rough and knotty cord interspersed with points sharp enough to penetrate the flesh. 

When he was ten years old he was affianced, through the influence of King Charles II of Naples, to the Blessed Delphina, and they were married five years later. By common agreement they lived together as brother and sister. In their pious lives they reminded their friends of the first Christians. At the death of his parents, Elzear inherited his father's estates and privileges, but he looked upon them only as means given him by God to advance piety among his subjects and to help the poor. He received Holy 

Communion almost every day, recited the divine office regularly and often spent whole nights in prayer. At the same time he remained cheerful and was very efficient in handling his secular affairs. He demanded that the members of his household follow the regulations laid down for them, which included attending holy Mass every morning and going to confession once a week. 

Quarreling, cursing, indecent language, unjust dealings with others, invited immediate dismissal. What he prescribed for others he himself conscientiously observed. Patient to the extreme, he forgave all injuries, including the gravest, without reservation. 

Even criminals who had turned a deaf ear to priests sent to them, were converted by Elzear. On account of his exemplary life and his splendid services, he was knighted by King Robert of Sicily and made the tutor of his son. In 1323, while on a political

mission for the king in Paris, he died a holy death in the garb of a Tertiary. He was canonized in 1369.

SAINT FERDINAND THE KING 

ERDINAND, born in 1198, was the son of Alfonso, King of 

Leon, and a cousin of Saint Louis, King of France. When he had reached the age of nineteen, his mother resigned to him the crown of Castile. Despite a rebellion among his subjects and the hostile attitude of his own father, Ferdinand was able to make

his position secure. Then he embarked upon his heart's desire, the expulsion of the infidel Moslems from the Spanish Peninsula. 

He spent the winters with his family, and each spring set out afresh to drive the invaders farther back. By skill and daring in this holy cause, and through the intercession of the Mother of God, he wrested the principal cities one by one from the grasp of the enemy, who either fled the country or became his vassals. In each newly captured city he replaced the crescent with the cross. 

With the spoils of battle he founded bishoprics and built or restored churches, monasteries and hospitals. When Seville, the wealthiest stronghold of the Moors, at last fell, its governor proclaimed that it was useless to fight against a saint who had God on his side. Indulgent toward both Moors and Jews, Ferdinand was adamant against all heretics. He fasted strictly, wore a rough hairshirt next his skin and frequently prayed through the night. Even in the turmoil of the camp, he lived like a monk. 

He was most careful not to wrong any of his subjects. While preparing for yet another campaign, he was taken with his last illness. When he felt death approaching, he threw himself upon his knees and put a cord around his neck, and thus received the 

Viaticum. He died with a blessed candle in his hand on May 30, 1252, the model of a Christian king and a brave soldier. He was canonized in 1671.

MARCH 9 SAINT FRANCES OF ROME 

RANCES was born in Rome in 1384. When she was only twelve years old, her parents, who were of noble rank, overruled her desire to become a nun and married her to Lorenzo Ponziano, also a Roman noble. It was a happy marriage, and in the forty years of their married life the pious couple were never known to quarrel. Though always loving prayer and retirement, Frances never neglected her household obligations. She was wont to say: "A married woman must leave God at the altar to find 

Him in her domestic duties." Once when interrupted four times in the recitation of the verse of a psalm, she found upon returning to it again that it was written in gold. She would secretly exchange the good viands of her table for the scraps of the poor. 

Her ordinary food consisted of dry bread, her ordinary drink of plain water. During the invasion of Rome in 1410, Ponziano was banished, his estates confiscated, his home destroyed and his eldest son taken as a hostage. Frances saw in these losses only the finger of God, and blessed His holy name. At the conclusion

of peace the losses were made good. Now Frances founded the 

Oblates, a society of pious women. After the death of her husband she humbly went to their convent, barefooted and with a cord about her neck, and begged admission to the community. 

Soon she was elected its superior. She tried always to live in the presence of God, and for this was given the visible companionship of her guardian angel. He was constantly at her side in the form of a little child. He shielded her in the hour of temptation and directed her in her good works. Her death took place on the day she had foretold, March 9, 1440. She was canonized in 1608.

Prayer 

O Lord, who didst honor Thy servant Frances with the friendly companionship of an angel, among other gifts, grant, we beseech Thee, that by the aid of her intercession we may deserve to be admitted to the company of the angels.

SEPTEMBER 10 BLESSED FRANCIS CUFIOYE 

LESSED FRANCIS CUFIOYE was one of the noble victims of the great holocaust that was offered at Nagasaki, Japan, on August 17, 1627. The spot where he was burned to death was called the Holy Mountain. It became in truth a most holy mountain for the Japanese Catholics because it was hallowed by the almost endless stream of blood shed there by martyrs. Blessed 

Francis had been a herald of the Great King as a fervent Tertiary and a faithful catechist. Many souls were led heavenward not only by his convincing words but also by his exemplary life as a true Catholic layman. His many works of charity finally brought to him the greatest gift of charity, the giving of his life for Him whom he loved. Like many others among the glorious Japanese martyrs, he was made a prisoner because he had given shelter to one of the persecuted missionaries. When he was led to death with other Tertiaries, a large crowd followed to watch the victims of man's cruel blindness. Many of the pagans, enthralled by the examples of heroism given by these Christians, were converted to Christianity at the sight. Thus what was intended as a means of frightening others from embracing the true Faith led instead to the exactly opposite result; and thus Francis shared, even in death, in the work of the Apostles. He was beatified with the other Japanese martyrs of this time in 1867. 

Prayer Grant, we beseech Thee, O almighty God, that, aroused by the example of Thy blessed martyrs Apollinaris and his companions, we may suffer every evil in this world rather than harm to our souls.

form of the holy Roman Church. FEBRUARY 5 SAINT FRANCIS OF MEACO 

RANCIS, a native of Meaco, had been born of poor parents, but his own industry and mental acumen made him one of the most prominent citizens of his native town. Taking up the study of medicine, he progressed so rapidly that he became the outstanding physician of Japan and was appointed the viceroy's personal medical adviser. Wealth and honors were heaped upon him. Yet even while a pagan, he was of an unassuming character, kind and gentle, and always ready to offer his services to the poor without payment. No wonder that God looked with favor upon him and led him to the true Faith! His conversion began during the war of Emperor Taico-Gama against the Koreans. Francis followed the army to take care of the wounded and thus he came in contact with some Catholic soldiers. Edified by their patience in suffering, he soon discovered its source in their Faith. 

Eager to learn more about such a religion, he was referred to the Franciscan friars at Meaco. On his return to Japan he sought them out and within a short space of time was himself a Catholic. 

Hundreds of pagans followed his example and embraced the true Faith. Soon he married a prominent Catholic lady of Meaco. She was a Tertiary and following her example he became one also. 

In imitation of his Father Francis, he had a great devotion to the Passion, and it is recorded that he spent hours meditating on the Saviour's sufferings. He followed his profession of physician with such charity that he was called "the angel of mercy." To many he was rather a physician of the soul than of the body. In order to combat paganism, he translated several Catholic books into Japanese and had them published at his own expense. In 1597, at a ripe old age, he was rewarded with the crown of martyrdom. 

Together with the other Japanese martyrs he was canonized in 1862.

While many conversions to the Faith were taking place at Meaco through the zealous preaching and excellent example of the Friars Minor, Gabriel was a page of the emperor, living at the imperial court. 

About sixteen years old at this time, he was greatly admired for his handsome appearance and personal charm. He was attracted to the friars, whom he saw frequently when they still had free access to the governor's mansion. He made occasional calls at the friary and was gradually won to the Faith. When he was baptized, he requested that the fact be kept secret until he could dispose of his worldly goods. Then he and some companions joined the friars as Tertiaries of Saint Francis, and he dedicated

himself entirely to the service of God. His former companions importuned him on every possible occasion to return to them and their pagan practices, but he remained adamant. His parents were then approached to compel his return. But their tears and pleadings were likewise in vain, and his description of his own happiness finally prevailed upon them to cause no further interference. Gabriel was much esteemed for his goodness and often called "the Saint of Meaco." He sought out the public gathering places in order to instruct the people and he was so successful that the number of converts increased constantly. Finally he had the great consolation of leading his own father into the Church after long resistance on the latter's part, and then of inducing him to become a Tertiary. The persecution that soon set in against the Christians found in Gabriel a natural target. Like his companions he died on the cross February 5, 1597. With the other Japanese martyrs he was canonized in 1862.

MARCH 20 BLESSED HIPPOLYTUS GALANTINI 

IPPOLYTUS was born in Florence in the year 1565. When only twelve years old he began to gather the children of his neighborhood together and teach them Christian Doctrine. 

Although he longed to become a religious, his desire was always

frustrated. Being the son of a poor silkweaver, he was compelled to work every day to the end of his life. In addition, frequent illness reduced him to a state of grinding poverty. Yet his spiritual interest in the young only increased under difficulties. 

At one time he was at the head of seven confraternities which had as their purpose instruction in the truths of the Faith. The malice of the members drove him from each confraternity in turn. They went so far as to have some learned men denounce him publicly. Thus seasoned in adversity, Hippolytus founded his own institute for the instruction of the young. He laid down rules for his followers on the same principles that Saint Philip Neri was giving to his Oratorians. The institute of Hippolytus brought about a great change in the young people of Florence, and as a result many other cities and towns asked him to establish his work among them also. Yet the persecutions against him did not cease. An attempt was even made to take his life, and he was likewise accused of heresy before the Holy Office. His work continued to flourish, however, and was firmly established before his death in 1619. As late as 1824, the institute was carefully scrutinized and was again solemnly approved by Pope Leo XII. 

He was beatified in 1825.

DECEMBER 11 BLESSED HUGOLINUS MEGALOTTI 

UGOLINUS was born near Camerino in Italy in the early part of the fourteenth century. He belonged to the noble family of the Megalotti. From his earliest youth he was attracted to the service of God. Orphaned while still quite young, he resolved to leave the world after the example of his spiritual father Saint Francis of Assisi, whose Third Order he had joined.

JANUARY 30 SAINT HYACINTHA OF MARISCOTTI 

YACINTHA was born in 1585 of noble parents at Vignanello, in Italy. She was educated in a convent of Franciscan nuns at the neighboring city of Viterbo. Though not without traces of piety, she was a vain child and looked with aversion upon her elder sister, a nun of the community. Her greatest desire was to be married. Therefore she was stung to the quick when her younger sister was preferred to her in marriage by a great noble. She took the veil in deference to the wishes of her parents and without any desire for the religious life; her chief motive was the fear that her refusal would be attributed to jealousy of her sister. Having no intention of living according to the Rule of the Third Order of Penance, she had special apartments built for herself in one part of the convent and furnished them luxuriously. There for ten years she continued her worldly life, indifferent alike to the scandal she was causing, the reproaches of her superior and the tears of her holy sister. 

At length she fell sick and anxiously asked for a confessor. The sight of the costly furnishings so enkindled the wrath of the priest that he refused to absolve her, saying that hell and not heaven should be the lot of such as she. The words found their mark. Hyacintha's eyes began to open to other values. She understood the evil of her ways and repentance took hold of her heart. She asked for a poor habit and on her knees begged the forgiveness of the Sisters for the scandal she had given. She arose as a new creature. After twenty years of heroic penance she died in 1640, a model of the perfect nun in poverty, mortification and obedience. She was canonized in 1807.

NOVEMBER 17* BLESSED JANE OF SIGNA 

ANE was born in 1244, at Signa, a little town in the Diocese of Florence. What her parents lacked in earthly goods they made up in the things of the spirit. When, in 1307, without anyone's knowledge she quietly passed to her reward, the bells of three neighboring churches of themselves pealed forth the news to the astounded people. Many miracles are recorded after her death; and even today her help is felt in times of drought, or when hail and storms threaten to do damage. FEBRUARY 5 SAINT JOACHIM SAQUIOR 

MONG the many converts to the Faith made by the Franciscan friars at Osaca, in Japan, was a certain Joachim. Of his ancestry and occupation we know very little. 

The brown-robed friars had a certain attraction for him, pagan though he was; yet he always failed in courage when the question of conversion was broached. His wife, however, was more courageous. After her Baptism she mentioned to Father Marcellus, who had received her into the Church, that her husband was detained from being present at the ceremonies on account of illness but that he would have been happy to be present. The friar at once visited the sick man and induced him to take the necessary

instructions for reception into the Church. Before the instructions could be completed, Father Marcellus was compelled to undertake a missionary journey. In the meantime Joachim's condition became so serious that his wife feared he would die, and called in one of the catechists to baptize him. Joachim now asked to be received into the Third Order; with the consent of his wife, he also began to live with the friars and to assist them in every way possible at home and on mission expeditions. 

He was admired by all Christians for his spirit of faith, his enduring patience, his humble subjection and his life of penance.

BLESSED JOHN CINI

Little is known of his youth. He chose the military profession, and took part in a brutal attack on several priests at Pisa, October 8, 1396, in the bloody war that was waged by the city of Pisa against the city of Florence. During this war the leader of

BLESSED LUCIUS 

UCIUS, or Luchesius, has the great spiritual distinction of having been the first Franciscan Tertiary. He was born in 1181, at Val d'Elsa in Tuscany. In his youth he took little interest in religion, being instead engrossed in worldly interests. 

He loved money; while politically he was so violent a partisan in the quarrels dividing his countrymen that he had to leave his native town. He settled at Poggibonsi, where he carried on as a merchant and money-lender. The death of his children, however, brought about a change of heart in Lucius. He began to interest himself in works of mercy, such as nursing the sick and visiting prisoners. In reparation for his previous usurious transactions, he gave away all his possessions with the exception of a small tract of land that was to help support himself and his wife. 

Soon after this Saint Francis preached at Poggibonsi. His fervent discourse made such a deep impression on Lucius and his wife Bonadonna that they approached the saint and asked him for a rule of life which would safely lead them to heaven. Saint 

Francis, who had previously given thought to forming an association of persons desiring to live a religious life in the world, gladly acquiesced in their request. The short rule of life he prepared for them he later expanded into the Third Order Rule. They received a habit suitable to their condition in life and thus became the first members of the Third Order. The lives of both were holy, devoted entirely to works of charity and mercy. Thus did Lucius and Bonadonna labor to atone for the scandal they had previously given. They were rewarded by ecstasies and the gift of healing the sick. Both passed to their reward on the same day. 

Lucius was beatified in 1779.

JULY 16 SAINT MARY MAGDALEN POSTEL 

ARY MAGDALEN POSTEL, or Julia as she was baptized, was born at Barfleur, France, in 1756. After her elementary schooling she continued her education with the Benedictine nuns at Volognes. There she resolved to devote her life to the service of God and her neighbor, and was permitted to take the private vow of chastity. Returning home she opened a school and gave herself up assiduously to the training of youth. But the Revolution swept over France bringing with it the persecution of the faithful. The Civil Constitution of the Clergy was directed principally against bishops and priests, who had either to flee the country or to carry on their ministry from hiding places. In these circumstances the priest of Barfleur confided the Blessed Sacrament to the care of Mary Magdalen. When the persecution increased in intensity, she was given permission to carry the Host on her person and, as in the early Christian persecutions, to

administer It for Viaticum in case no priest was available to do so. 

For this reason Pope Pius X, in his decree of beatification, does not hesitate to call Mary Magdalen the "maiden-priest." She was often suspected by the bloodthirsty Jacobins, but during all the years of persecution the Lord preserved her from harm. 

When peace again returned, she applied herself with all her strength to helping restore the Faith by catechizing both children and adults. LESSED NICHOLAS was born in 1349 at Forca Palena in the Diocese of Sulmona. At an early age he felt the attraction of the altar, and it finally brought him to the sanctuary as a priest. SAINT PAUL SUSUQUI 

NDOWED with a brilliant mind, Paul Susuqui arrived without great effort at the conviction that the Catholic Church must possess the true Faith. Long before the friars came to his native Meaco, in Japan, he had joined the Church. FEBRUARY 5 SAINT PAUL YUANIQUI 

EARED in the religion of Shintoism, Paul was brought to the true Faith by his brother. His conversion, however, was a slow and painful process, finally wrought by means of the brother's exemplary conduct rather than his persuasive words. After he had witnessed a violent quarrel between two Catholics and heard them use most abusive language to each other, he began to be troubled about the genuineness of Catholicism. Again Paul walked quietly and firmly on the path of perfection, denying himself in many things as a child of God. He had been fond of silk apparel; now he dressed in humble clothes like a beggar. He had formerly avoided those in need of help; now he gave himself to charitable endeavors of every kind, especially to nursing the sick. Thus he became the cause of many conversions.

LESSED PELINGOTTO was born at Urbino in 1240. From his early days he gave signs of extraordinary virtue. When the time came to learn a trade from his father, he was so absorbed in spiritual matters that he could make no progress. 

Wisely his father advised him to give up the trade; and he thereupon retired into solitude in order to spend his time in prayer.

BLESSED PETER CRESCI 

ETER CRESCI was born at or near Foligno. His pious parents tried to bring up their son as a fervent Catholic, but their endeavors were apparently unsuccessful. The boy sought out bad companions, who flocked about him willingly because his parents' generosity furnished him with plenty of money. He became a spendthrift and a worldling in the worst sense of the word. The death of his parents, however, when he had reached the age of thirty, brought about a total change in his dispositions. Contrite for his past behavior, he sold his great inheritance and distributed the proceeds to the poor. Hiring out as a day-laborer, he contented himself with meager fare and gave most of his earnings to those in need. Dressed during his frivolous years in the height of fashion, he now wore the simplest attire; and later he changed even this for the habit of the Third Order of Saint Francis. 

Prayer had been for him a drudgery; it now became his delight, and he spent many nights in lifting his mind and heart to God. 

For weeks at a time he ate only dry bread and drank only water. 

His night's rest was usually taken on the bare floor of the bell tower at the Church of Saint Felician. He frequently undertook long pilgrimages, especially to Rome and Assisi. In the latter place he sought the Church of Saint Mary of the Angels on account of the love Saint Francis had borne toward it. His earlier companions conceived such hatred for him on account of his changed mode of life that they had him cited before the tribunal of the Church for heresy. He was fully vindicated in every instance. Many miracles showed that God was pleased with 

His servant. Peter passed away from this life on July 19, 1323, and the people began almost immediately to invoke his intercession. This cult was approved by Pope Urban VI in 1385.

JUNE 17

BLESSED PETER OF PISA 

ETER was born at Pisa in 1355, of the noble family of Gambacorta; his father held the important post of president of the Republic of Pisa and Lucca. Peter's parents tried to keep their son on the straight path, but he preferred the broad, easy road. Finally he was reclaimed through the good example and the prayers of his pious sister Clare, who had become a nun. 

To strengthen himself in his conversion he asked to be received into the Third Order. In 1377 he retired to Mount Cessano in 

Umbria, and gave himself up to prayer and penance. There twelve robbers came across his hut; and instead of harming him,

Other disciples were drawn to him also, and he finally decided to unite them all in a religious community at Montebello. He called them the Hermits of Saint Jerome because of his great veneration for this Doctor of the Church, who had spent many years in a hermitage at Bethlehem. These were later merged with another group. All lived a most austere life. As long as Peter was with them these Poor Brothers of the 

Love of Christ (as they were also called) followed the Rule of the Third Order of Saint Francis. In 1568, long after his death, having in the meantime been united with other congregations of hermits, they accepted the Rule of Saint Augustine. Blessed

Peter died in 1435, venerated as a saint. Pope Innocent XII gave the Church's approbation to this cult in 1693.

Thought I indeed counsel, warn, and exhort my brothers in the Lord Jesus Christ that when they go through the world they be not litigious nor contend in words, nor judge others; but that they be gentle, peaceful, and modest, meek and humble, speaking honestly to all as is fitting.

JULY 4* BLESSED RAYMOND LULL 

AYMOND was born at Parma about the year 1235. The son of a military chief, he married young and led a gay life as grand steward at the court of King James the Conqueror. One night while he was writing a poem, he suddenly saw the image of the Crucified beside his desk. Witnessing this apparition five distinct times, he felt it to be the call of Christ for some great purpose. Since he had been in constant touch with the Moors, he interpreted it as a sign that he should labor for their conversion. Having made provision for his wife and his two children, he sold his remaining goods to help the poor and then set about learning the Arabian language. These periods of study were interspersed with hours of fervent prayer. In an effort to arouse sympathy for the great cause that was now uppermost in his mind, he traveled to Rome and to Genoa; in the latter city he was received into the Third Order of Saint Francis. He did not succeed in gaining supporters for his great venture, but undaunted, he crossed to Africa alone and began preaching to the Moslems in Tunis. They put him on a ship bound for Europe. 

His next occupation was the teaching of metaphysics at the schools in Paris; but the conversion of the Moors remained uppermost in his mind. Twice more did he return to Africa. On the last visit he was so cruelly treated that he died later (in 1315) of the wounds he had received. Celebrated for his learning, renowned alike as a chemist, philosopher, theologian and poet, Blessed Raymond was eager to use his talents and his tireless energy for the salvation of souls. He was beatified in 1514.

SEPTEMBER 4 SAINT ROSE OF VITERBO 

OSE was born at Viterbo in 1235. At that time the Emperor

Frederic II was oppressing the Church and doing all he could to diminish the influence of the Holy Father. Rose was prepared by God to resist these impious aggressions. As a small child she was filled with extraordinary love of God. It is told that she would seek out Jesus in the Blessed Sacrament, that she would look for the images of the saints in order to pray before them, that she would listen with eager attention to pious conversation and would retain in her young mind all that she heard. Even at that early age she practiced severe fasts and chastised her body rigorously. She was still very young when she received the habit of the Third Order of Saint Francis. Our Blessed Mother herself is said to have told Rose that she must exhort the people to give up their antagonism to the Church. At the age of ten, accordingly, she gathered a crowd about her in the public square of the city and pleaded with them to be faithful to the Supreme Pontiff and to have nothing to do with his enemies, who were also the enemies of God. banishment only gave her the opportunity to extend her influence; she went from city to city strengthening the faithful in their resistance to the emperor. Through her instrumentality Pope 

Innocent was at length able to return in triumph to the Eternal City. When this was accomplished, Rose sought to be forgotten by the world. She retired to a quiet cell near Viterbo, and there she died within a short time (1253), when she was barely eighteen years old. God approved her works by many miracles, and the 

Church gave assent to the veneration paid to her by canonization in 1457.

MARCH 16 BLESSED TORELLO OF POPPI 

ORELLO was born at Poppi, in Tuscany. Under the careful training of his pious parents he passed a blameless youth, but after his father's death he began to associate with evil companions and to be corrupted by their example. It is said that while he was amusing himself one day with these associates, a cock perched on his arm and crowed three times. Torello took this as a warning from heaven. He left his friends immediately, sought out a priest and confessed his sins. Then he wandered about in a dense forest for eight days bewailing his past conduct. Having finally come upon a deserted spot that seemed as if made for a hermitage, he hurried back to Poppi, sold his property, purchased the land around the chosen place in the woods, and gave the rest of the proceeds to the poor. He built a small hermitage and began to cultivate the little strip of land he had purchased. This was the start of a life of mortification entirely separated from the outside world. Torello slept little and that upon the bare ground with a stone for a pillow. His ordinary meals consisted of bread and water. To ward off incessant temptations he used a scourge freely upon himself or immersed himself in freezing water. He continued this life of penance until his death in 1282, at the age of eighty years. He died kneeling in prayer. He was beatified by Pope Benedict XIV.

At her request, however, they built a small hut for her on the outskirts of the town where she might give herself entirely to God. There she remained for the rest of her life devoted to prayer and extreme penance. The Passion of our Lord was her principal meditation. 

If pious visitors left alms for her use, she would have them sent to the poor. Many miracles are ascribed to her. In 1221, she had

the singular privilege of a visit from Saint Francis of Assisi, on which occasion he admitted her into the Third Order. She died in 1242, while she was reciting the Penitential Psalms. It is said that the bells of Castelflorentino spontaneously announced the hour of her death.

Hear us, O God our Saviour, and grant that as we are gladdened by the festival of blessed Veridiana,

Thy virgin, so we may learn therefrom loving devotion toward 

Thee.  

Thought Let us, moreover, "bring forth fruits worthy of penance." And let us love our neighbors as ourselves, and, if anyone does not wish to love them as himself or cannot, let him at least do them no harm, but let him do good to them.

JUNE 29 

BLESSED VINCENTA GEROSA 

LESSED VINCENTA GEROSA was born October 29, 1784, in the Diocese of Brixen. Her father was a merchant of leather goods. Baptized Mary Catherine Frances, the girl showed signs of great virtue in her early youth. Although urged to marry, she preferred a life of virginity in order that she might devote herself to works of charity. After the death of her parents she acted as a servant in the house of her uncle until she was thirty

years old. Then, in order to give herself up entirely to works of mercy, she joined with Blessed Bartolomea Capitanio in an association which developed into the Tertiary congregation of the Sisters of Charity (ab Maria Infantula). It was approved by Pope 

Gregory XVI in 1840. As a Sister she took the name Vincenta. 

After the early death of Blessed Bartolomea she was chosen the superior of the community, but in her humility she declined to be called anything but senior Sister. She guided her community less by words than by her example in virtue. She was able to expand her community, which was devoted to all kinds of works of charity and mercy, so that ten years after its foundation it numbered forty convents. Today it has more than seven thousand 

Sisters in every part of the world. In spite of this great success, she was wont to say: "It is Christ's work. He who does not know 

Christ Crucified knows nothing, but he who knows Christ Crucified knows all things." In 1847, she passed away peacefully, having patiently endured an illness of two months. On account of her holy life and the miracles attributed to her intercession, she was beatified by Pope Pius XI in 1933. 

Prayer 

Hear us, O God our Saviour, and grant that as we are gladdened by the festival of blessed Vincenta, Thy virgin, so we may learn therefrom loving devotion toward 

Thee.  

Thought But let him to whom obedience has been entrusted and who is considered greater become as the lesser and the servant of the other brothers, and let him show and have the mercy toward each of his brothers that he would wish to be shown to himself if he were in the like situation. And let him not be angry with a brother on account of his offence, but let him advise him kindly and encourage him with all patience and humility.

MAY 31 

BLESSED VITALIS OF BASTIA 

LESSED VITALIS was born at Bastia in the valley of Spoleto, which was the home of many holy persons. His parents were successful in implanting in their son the seed of love of God that dwelt in their own hearts, and the spirit of piety and prayer grew within him while he was earning a living by the toil of his hands. Perceiving the deceitfulness of the world and fearing the dangers about him, he decided to leave society and to live as a hermit. The place he chose was a little hut about a mile from 

Assisi. There he remained the rest of his life. Before entering the hermitage he had sold his small possessions and had given the proceeds to the poor. He was also invested with the habit of a Tertiary. At times he felt the urge to return to the world and share in its pleasures; at other times he had to fight against severe temptations. But he always remained the valiant soldier of Christ. 

As in his youth, he again found consolation and serenity of spirit in prayer. Mortifications gave him the strength to carry out his resolution of serving God perfectly. And thus he spent his life in solitude, known to few persons but venerated by these as a saint. 

After many years of this quiet communion with God, he passed away in the peace of the Lord in the year 1491. Miracles were wrought at his tomb and the veneration of this servant of God increased among the people. The cult was approved in 1599. Prayer O God, who dost gladden us by the yearly festival of blessed Vitalis, Thy confessor, vouchsafe in Thy mercy that we who keep his birthday may follow the pattern of his life.  

Thought Praised be my Lord for our sister, the bodily death,

From the which no living man can flee.

Woe to them who die in mortal sin;

Blessed those who shall find themselves in Thy most holy will, For the second death shall do them no ill. 

Praise ye and bless ye my Lord, and give Him thanks, And be subject unto Him with great humility.

-The Canticle of the Sun. 

BLESSED VIVALDUS 

LESSED VIVALDUS, also called Ubaldus and Waldo, was a native of the little town of San Gemignano. Very little is known about his youth. With Blessed Bartolo he entered the 

Third Order of Saint Francis, and both thenceforth devoted themselves to works of penance and charity. When Bartolo was stricken with the dread disease of leprosy, Vivaldus would not leave him but set about nursing him in his affliction. This he continued to do for twenty years, until his friend at last succumbed. 

Then Vivaldus retired to a hermitage near Montaone to give himself up to prayer and mortification like the hermits of the first centuries. He lived in the hollow of a tree and sustained life with roots and herbs. Rich in merits he died in 1320. The church bell of Montaone tolled his departure without any human agency, and the people of the neighborhood, wondering at the marvel, hurried to the hermitage, where they found the corpse on its knees, its hands folded in prayer. Many miracles gave testimony

to the sanctity of the holy hermit and the people venerated him as a saint. This cult was approved by Pope Pius X in 1909.

BLESSED WILLIAM CUFITELLA 

ILLIAM was born of noble parents at Noto, in Sicily, in the year 1309. He was made one of the attendants to the king of Sicily. On a hunting expedition he saved the life of the king, but was himself seriously injured. The king promised to 

*Capuchin, April 4.

reward him royally, but William asked permission instead to leave the court and take up a life of solitude and prayer. Having been invested with the habit of the Third Order of Saint Francis, he retired to a small hermitage near Scicli in Sicily. He spent seventy years in this solitude, leaving it only to visit the poor and to care for the chapel of Our Lady of Pity, which had been committed to his charge. His reputation for sanctity drew many to his hermitage for guidance in their spiritual life. A very close bond of friendship united him with another hermit, Blessed Conrad of Piacenza. They usually spent the whole period of Lent together in prayer and holy converse. It is said that during those periods neither of them touched any food. When the church bells announced of their own accord the death of the holy hermit, people and clergy flocked to the hermitage. They found him in a kneeling position as if in prayer while a heavenly light suffused his body. The veneration of the people of Sicily for Blessed 

William is so great that they have made him one of the patrons of the country. He is said to have saved Sicily from the plague on many occasions. He was beatified in 1537. Prayer 

Almighty and eternal God, who dost work wonders through Thy confessor blessed William, grant, we beseech Thee, that as Thou dost give health to the sick through his intercession and merit, Thou mayest help us to attain to the eternal fellowship. 

Thought Thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven, that we may love Thee with the whole heart by always thinking of Thee; with the whole soul by always desiring Thee; with the whole mind by directing all our intentions to Thee and seeking Thy honor in all things; and with all our strength, by spending all the powers and senses of body and soul in the service of Thy love and not in anything else. 

-Praises and Paraphrase of the Lord's Prayer.

APRIL 27 

SAINT ZITA OF LUCCA 

AINT ZITA was born in 1218, at Monsagrati near Lucca. 

She was in the service of Pagano di Fatinelli, a citizen of Lucca, for forty-eight years. Every morning, while the rest of the household were still asleep, she arose to attend holy Mass. Then she returned to her domestic duties, attending to them with religious care all day long and even performing tasks that had been assigned to others. Yet for years her master and mistress treated her as a common drudge, while the other servants, resenting her application to work as a reproach to themselves, constantly insulted and even struck her. Zita bore these sufferings in the spirit of the suffering Christ and never lost her temper or her peace of mind. At length, when the master of the house began to realize what a treasure Zita really was, he put the whole household under her direction and even gave her charge of his children. She dreaded this added responsibility, but scrupulously filled her trust. Although she now began the charitable practice of feeding the poor who came to the door for help, she was able by her wise economies to save much more than had been possible before, and thus to improve even the temporal interests of her master. Slowly she also overcame the jealousy of the other servants, becoming their advocate with the master, who dared not vent his rage in her presence. The whole household was thus blessed by this holy maid and the benediction of heaven rested upon it. She died in 

1278, and it is said that a brilliant star appeared above her attic window at the moment of her death. She was canonized in 1696. Prayer 

Hear us, O God our Saviour, and grant that as we are gladdened by the festival of blessed Zita, Thy virgin, so we may learn therefrom loving devotion toward Thee.

Thought Let those brothers to whom the Lord has given the grace of working labor faithfully and devoutly, so that in banishing idleness, the enemy of the soul, they do not extinguish the spirit of holy prayer and devotion, to which all temporal things must be subservient. 

- Second Rule of the Friars Minor.
