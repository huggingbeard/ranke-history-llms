These laws have been enacted to establish and maintain a workable system of control for the convenience and guidance of each rural community against the perpetual hazard of fire.

FIRE-PROTECTION DISTRICTS Section 1. District Establishment Authorized. Fire-protection districts for the elimination of fire hazards and for the protection of life and property in territories outside of incorporated cities and towns and in territories including one or more cities of the fourth class are hereby authorized to be established as in this act provided. L. 1947, Ch. 254, § 1; § 5654-101, Rem. Rev. Stat.

Section 2. Formation-Petition-Boundaries-Verification-Presentation to County Commissioners. For the purpose of the formation of a fire-protection district, a petition designating the boundaries of the proposed district, by metes and bounds, or by describing the lands to be included in the proposed district by United States townships, ranges and legal subdivisions, signed by not less than fifteen per cent (15%) of the qualified registered electors who are resident within the boundaries of such district, and setting forth the object for the creation of such district and alleging that the establishment of such district shall be conducive to the public safety, welfare, and convenience, and will be a benefit to the property included therein, shall be filed with the county auditor of the county within which such proposed district is located, accompanied by an obligation signed by two or more petitioners, agreeing to pay the cost of the publication of the notice hereinafter provided for. Such district shall not cause to be taxed for district purposes those lands within the district which are now or will hereafter be required to pay forest protection assessment. The organization of any fire-protection district heretofore otherwise legally formed and which includes lands within its boundaries required by law to pay forest protection assessment is hereby approved and confirmed as a legally organized fire-protection district in the State of 

Washington. The county auditor shall, within thirty (30) days, from the date of filing such petition, examine the signatures and certify to the sufficiency or insufficiency thereof; and for such purpose shall have access to all registration books or records in the possession of the registration officers of the election precincts included, in whole or in part, within the boundaries of the proposed district. Such books and records shall be prima facie evidence of the truth of said certificate. No person having signed such a petition shall be allowed to withdraw his name therefrom after the filing of the same with the county auditor. If such petition shall be found to contain a sufficient number of signatures of qualified registered electors who are resident within the boundaries of such district, the county auditor shall transmit the same, together with his certificate of sufficiency attached thereto, to the board of county commissioners which shall thereupon by resolution entered upon its minutes, receive the same and fix a day and hour thereof when it will publicly hear said petition. L. 1947, Ch. 254, § 2; § 5654-102, Rem. Rev. Stat.

Section 2a. Previous Districts Validated. The respective areas, organized and established or attempted to be organized and established under the authority granted in chapter 34, Laws of 1939, as amended, which since their organization and establishment or attempted organization and establishment have continuously maintained their organization as fire protection districts established under the authority of said statutes are hereby declared to be duly organized fire protection districts existing under and by virtue of the provisions of said statutes having in each case, the boundaries set forth in the respective organization proceedings of each of them as shown by the files and records in the offices of the board of county commissioners and auditor of the county in which the particular area lies. L. 1947, Ch. 230, § 1 (effective immediately). 639108

The hearing on said petition shall be at the regular office of the board of county commissioners and the same shall be held not less than twenty (20) nor more than forty (40) days from the date of receipt of said petition with certificate of sufficiency thereof from the county auditor. If the board of county commissioners finds in favor of said petition, it shall designate the name and number of the district, fix the boundaries thereof and cause an election to be held therein for the purpose of determining whether or not the district shall be organized under the provisions of this act and for the purpose of the election of its first fire commissioners. Section 13. Appeal from Decision of Commissioners. Any person, firm or corporation, having a substantial interest involved, and feeling aggrieved by any finding, determination or resolution of the board of county commissioners made in the proceedings for the organization of a fire-protection district under the provisions of this act, may appeal from the same within five (5) days after the same was made by said board, to the superior court of said county, in the same manner as that heretofore generally provided by law for appeals from the orders and determinations of said board. L. 1939, Ch. 34, § 13; § 5654-113, Rem. Rev. Stat. 

Section 14. District Organization-Conclusion. After the expiration of five (5) days from the day of the resolution of the board of county commissioners declaring the district organized and upon the filing of said certified copies of the resolution of the board of county commissioners in the offices of the county auditor and of the county assessor, as aforesaid, the creation of the district shall be complete and its legal existence cannot thereafter be questioned by any person by reason of any defect in the proceedings had for the organization thereof. L. 1939, Ch. 34, § 14; § 5654-114, Rem. Rev. Stat. 

Section 15. District Legal Status-Municipal Corporation. Fire-protection districts created under this act shall be political subdivisions of the state and shall be held and construed to be municipal corporations within the provisions of the laws and Constitution of the State of Washington. Said districts shall not be liable for the torts of their officers, agents and servants. Such a district shall constitute a body corporate and shall possess all the usual powers of a corporation for public purposes as well as all other powers that may now or hereafter be specifically conferred by law. L. 1939, Ch. 34, § 15; § 5654-115, Rem. Rev. Stat.

Section 16. District Authority-Powers. Such fire-protection districts shall have full authority to carry out the objects of their creation and to that end are authorized to acquire, purchase, hold, lease, manage, occupy and sell real and personal property, or any interest therein, to enter into and to perform any and all necessary contracts, to appoint and employ the necessary officers, agents and employees, to sue and be sued, to exercise the right of eminent domain, to levy and enforce the collection of taxes and special taxes in the manner and subject to the limitations herein provided against the lands within the district, for district revenues, and to do any and all lawful acts required and expedient to carry out the purpose of this act. L. 1939, Ch. 34, 16; § 5654-116, Rem. Rev. Stat.

Fire-Protection Districts Section 18. Eminent Domain. The taking and damaging of property or rights therein or thereto by any such fire-protection district to carry out any of the purposes of its organization are hereby declared to be for a public use and any such district organized under this act shall have and may exercise the power of eminent domain to acquire any property or rights therein or thereto either inside or outside the district, for the use of such district. Any such district exercising the power of eminent domain shall proceed in the name of the district in the manner provided by law for the appropriation of real property or of rights therein or thereto, by private corporations. L. 1939, Ch. 34, § 18; § 5654-118, Rem. Rev. Stat. 

Section 19. Condemnation Proceedings-Title Acquired. Such fire-protec tion district may, at its option, unite in a single action proceedings to condemn for its use, property which is held by separate owners. Two or more condemnation suits instituted separately may also, in the discretion of the court, be consolidated, upon motion of any interested party, into a single action. 

In such cases, the jury shall render separate verdicts for each tract of land in different ownership. No finding of the jury nor decree of the court as to damages in any condemnation proceeding instituted by the district shall in any manner be held or construed to abridge or destroy the right of the district to levy and collect taxes for any and all district purposes against the uncondemned land situated within the district. The title acquired by a fireprotection district in condemnation proceedings shall be the fee simple title or such lesser estate as shall be designated in the decree of appropriation. 

L. 1939, Ch. 34, § 19; § 5654-119, Rem. Rev. Stat.

Section 22. Board of Fire Commissioners—Membership-Authority over Fourth Class Cities. The affairs of the district shall be managed by a board of fire commissioners composed of three (3) qualified resident electors of the district. The members of the board of fire commissioners shall receive no compensation for their services rendered the district, but shall receive necessary expenses in attending meetings of the board or when otherwise engaged on district business. The board shall fix the compensation to be paid the secretary and all other agents and employees of the district. The first fire commissioners of the district shall serve until after the next general election for the selection of district fire commissioners and until their successors have been elected or appointed and have qualified as provided by law. When a fire district includes one or more cities of the fourth class the board of fire commissioners herein created shall have authority over all matters relating to fire prevention and fire protection and elimination of fire hazards and such authority shall supersede all authority of the town councils of such fourth class cities as to such matters. L. 1945, Ch. 162, § 3; § 5654-122, Rem. Rev. Stat.

The fire com missioners shall organize as a board and shall elect a chairman from their number and shall appoint a secretary of the district for such term as they shall by resolution determine. Special meetings of the board may be called at any time by a majority of the commissioners or by the secretary and the chairman of the board. Any fire commissioner not joining in the call of a special meeting shall be entitled to a three (3) days written notice by mail of the same, specifying generally the business proposed to be transacted at said special meeting, but when at any special meeting of the board all members are present, lack of previous notice thereof shall not invalidate the proceedings. L. 1947, Ch. 254,

Section 32. Meetings and Records to Be Open. All meetings of the fire commissioners shall be public and a majority shall constitute a quorum for the transaction of business. All records of the board shall be open to the inspection of any elector of the district at any meeting of the board. The board shall have the power and it shall be its duty to adopt a seal of the district, to manage and conduct the business affairs of the district, to make and execute all necessary contracts, to employ any necessary service and to establish and promulgate reasonable rules and regulations for the government of the district and for the performance of its functions and generally to perform all such acts as may be necessary fully to carry out the objects of the creation of the district. 

L. 1939, Ch. 34, § 32; § 5654-132, Rem. Rev. Stat.

FIRE-PROTECTION STANDARDS 

The following is furnished by the Washington Survey and 

Rating Bureau: 

The rules contained herein shall apply to the following types of districts furnishing fire protection: a. Districts incorporated under State Law as Fire-Protection Districts. b. Water Districts incorporated under the laws of the State of Washington which provide fire-protection facilities in certain areas. c. Other incorporated subdivisions legally authorized to levy taxes for the purpose of maintaining public fire-protection facilities within certain specified limits, such as suburban residential areas. 

Fire-protection districts properly qualifying under the above and maintaining adequate fire defense facilities are graded under the Standard Schedule for Grading Cities and Towns with reference to their Fire Defenses and Physi cal Conditions as promulgated by the National Board of Fire Underwriters. 

This schedule is based upon the plan of assigning points of deficiency to the various features of fire defense depending upon their variance from standard, and the structural and hazard conditions and lack of laws or of their enforcement are graded in the same manner. 

The sum of the maximum points of deficiency which are chargeable under this grading schedule is 5,000, which total is divided into increments of 500 points, each representing what is known as a class of protection from a fire insurance standpoint; zero to 500 points of deficiency representing first class protection, 501 to 1,000 points of deficiency representing second class protection, and so on up to and including a 10th class or unprotected district having 4,501 to 5,000 points of deficiency or one without a water supply and/or a fire department. Normally fire-protection districts will not grade better than 6th class (2,501-3,000 points of deficiency) due to the limiting features of lack of concentration of values and the cost of maintaining a higher degree of fire defense.

Minimum fire defense facilities which should be provided in a fire-protection district are outlined below and which, if properly installed and maintained, should produce a grading of 8th class (3,501 to 4,000 points of deficiency): (1) An adequate water system properly designed should be provided, with mains, supplying hydrants, not smaller than 4 inches in diameter, supply and feeder mains to be of sufficient size to furnish adequate water at pressures suitable for direct hose streams from hydrants. 

Water may be supplied to the system by gravity or by pumping depending upon the conditions existing. It is suggested that competent hydraulic engineers be engaged to design the waterworks system. (2) An active fire department should be organized under suitable rules and regulations (the personnel may be a paid, part paid, or volunteer) with an adequate membership to assure full response regardless of type of organization. Quarters should be provided at the fire station where members may stay during the night. The fire station should be modern, well-lighted and satisfactorily heated. A telephone must be installed for receiving fire calls and an electric siren of suitable size and properly located to insure satisfactory audibility with operating switches at convenient locations must be provided.

Fire-Protection Standards 

(3) It is necessary to provide a modern approved motorized fire truck capable of delivering 500 gallons per minute at 120 pounds net pump pressure and carrying 800 feet of 2½ inch hose, 400 feet of 12 inch hose, a complement of roof and extension ladders, hard suction hose, and other minor items usually found on rural fire trucks. This truck must also carry a water tank of at least 150 gallons capacity which may be used in connection with the fire pump for furnishing small hose streams and 200 feet of 1-inch booster hose should be mounted on the truck.

The response of a piece of motorized fire apparatus in a fire-protection district organized and equipped as outlined above is limited under the rules for rating properties in the district to 36 square miles; that is 3 miles in any direction from the fire station measured at right angles to the boundaries of the district. Additional fire stations and pieces of motorized equipment may be required depending upon local conditions in the district. 

The fire insurance rates applicable to properties located in a fire-protection district are determined by the grade of the fire defense facilities provided, and properties located within recognized distances of standard fire hydrants and the fire station are given the same rate treatment as similar properties located in a city or town having a corresponding grade of fire protection; whereas properties located beyond these recognized distances are granted credits based upon the degree of protection afforded. 

The Washington Surveying and Rating Bureau is glad to offer its facilities to those persons interested in formation of fire-protection districts.
