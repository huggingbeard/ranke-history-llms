FOREWORD 

By Rufus Burrus, of Independence,

President of The Missouri Bar The first "Abstract of Title Examina tion Standards" are believed to have been adopted by the Connecticut Bar Association about 1931. These standards soon demonstrated their usefulness by reducing the number of trivial and un necessary title requirements. 

The title standards movement has now spread to other states, including Missouri. The state Bar associations of our neighboring states of Illinois, Iowa, Nebraska, Kansas and Oklahoma, have all approved title standards. Wherever the standards have been carefully drafted they have aided in reducing the number of unnecessarily technical requirements in title opinions. 

Standards have also helped, in some instances, to answer the question which all lawyers examining abstracts ask themselves sooner or later: "What would other lawyers do in this situation?", or more specifically: "What will the next examiner do?" 

The first Title Examination Standards 

Committee in Missouri was appointed in 

1946, by John H. Flanigan, Carthage, then president of The Missouri Bar. 

Boyd Ewing, Nevada, was named chairman. Under his leadership seventeen standards were drafted, which were reported to and adopted by the Board of 

Governors in 1948. Six additional standards have been drafted by subsequent committees under chairmen Orestes Mitchell, Jr., St. Joseph, and H. H. Blair,

Kansas City, and were approved by the 

Board in 1949.

As they were being drafted and adopted, the sections of the standards were published in various issues of the Journal of The Missouri Bar, and all 23 of them were reprinted therein at page 59 of the April 1950 issue.

The standards are here printed for the first time in pamphlet form, which it is hoped will be useful to lawyers, their clients, and others interested.

The Board of Governors believes that by adopting and publishing title examination standards it will aid in reducing the number of trivial and unnecessary requirements in attorneys' opinions, and also that an increased discussion of t matters will follow, resulting in grea uniformity in the title requireme which are necessary. Both objecti are, to a degree at least, now being complished. 

The Board also believes that the sta ards adopted to date have been careful prepared by qualified committees. Th standards have been discussed by s eral such committees, by independ groups of lawyers, by members at D trict meetings of The Missouri Bar, local bar association meetings, and a at meetings of abstracters. 

The Board believes further that the standards have the acceptance of great majority of the lawyers of t state who are active in the practice real property law. It does not belie that universal acceptance by all lawye is necessary to the validity of a ti standard. The test is whether the star ard reflects a situation which most la yers accept as well settled by statu case law, or usage.

For this reason title standards, a the work of the Standards Committe provide a useful means of establishig uniformity of opinion for many ti matters which most lawyers consid fairly established but which a few la yers fail to accept, to the confusion the Bar as a whole.

The Board realizes that there are a ditional subjects which might make us ful standards. Perhaps adopted stan ards could be improved. For this rease suggestions and comments are invite

A deed or encumbrance executed by person who has no record interest, who not in the chain of title and who is ot in possession of the property decribed, without recitals establishing at east a prima facie interest, does not ender the title defective, provided the strument has been of record at least en years. If recorded within that time, quiry should be made to ascertain hether grantor is claiming under an nrecorded deed or contract of sale from record owner, or other interest not vident of record. 

5. In the event reference is made in an nstrument of record to a mortgage, deed f trust, or other lien which cannot be easonably identified with any mortgage, eed of trust or other lien in the chain f title no requirement should be made where the instrument in which such refrence is made has been of record twenty r more years. 

6. Where a deed does not contain a ecital as to the marital status of grantor, but was executed prior to January 1, 1900, and no notice or suit appears of record of such spouse's claimed interest in the land as provided for under Section 1009 R. S. Mo. 1939, any question as to right of dower of possible spouse should be waived, without comment. 

7. Recitals of death and heirship in the affidavit contained in an application for letters of administration or testamentary, or in a deed from a person recited to be an heir, should be accepted without further requirement as sufficient evidence of the truth of such recitals, where the affidavit or deed has been of record not less than thirty-one years, unless controverted by other entries in the abstract. 

8. Intestacy may be presumed and administration waived, where the abstract of title contains satisfactory information either by affidavit or deed recital as to date of death, place of domicile, heirship and intestacy, and probate of the estate is not shown, and it further appears that the deceased has been dead more than 10 years. 

9. Where the time for institution of a proceeding for enforcement or foreclosure of a special tax bill, mechanic's lien, or sheriff's levy, has expired and no suit is pending, such bill, lien, or levy should be disregarded without requirement. 

10. Where a judgment or decree affecting the title to real estate has been entered of record for more than 31 years and appears in the abstract, but the remainder of the action either is not shown, or is incompletely shown, and where such judgment or decree gives full information as to the status of parties and the nature of the action, which is sufficient upon which to base an opinion as to the validity of the proceedings in question, the proceedings shall be presumed to be valid and binding as to all matters recited in the judgment or decree, and such showing shall be accepted as sufficient, unless something affirmatively appears therein, showing lack of jurisdiction of either the parties or the subject matter. 718850

11. Where ten or more years have elapsed since the date of a sale by a trustee in foreclosure of a deed of trust, no requirement should be made as to whether the owner at the date of such sale was then alive. 

12. Where affidavits as to loss or destruction of note or notes secured by a deed of trust are in conformity with Section 3465 R. S. Mo. 1939, the truth of such affidavits should be presumed without requirement, unless something affirmatively appears to put the examiner upon inquiry. 

13. Delivery of a deed on the date of the acknowledgement is presumed. Mere lapse of time in recording the instrument should be disregarded, where nothing appears in the abstract of title to put the examiner upon inquiry, and it has been of record more than ten years. 

14. Identity of parties should be accepted as sufficiently established in the following cases: 

(a) Where common abbreviations, derivatives or nicknames are used for 

Christian names. (b) Where the names are within the rule of the generally accepted doctrine of idem sonans. 

A greater degree of liberality should be indulged with the greater lapse of time, in the absence of circumstances appearing in the abstract to raise a reasonable doubt as to the identity of the parties. 

15. If a deed from a private corporation appears in the title, and it has been of record for more than ten years, in the absence of evidence to the contrary, the examiner may assume that the corporation was legally in existence at the time of the delivery of said deed, and if the deed be executed as required by the Statutes, its validity may be presumed.

Minor discrepancies in the name of the corporation may be disregarded, if the identity of the corporation can be reasonably ascertained. 

16. The absence of revenue stamps upon a deed does not affect the validity of the deed.

United States District or Circuit Court held within the State of Missouri is a lien on real estate of the person against whom such judgment is rendered situate in the county in which such court is held; and such a judgment, upon filing of transcript thereof in the office of the Clerk of the Circuit Court of any other county, becomes a lien upon the real estate of the person against whom such judgment is rendered, situate in the county in which such transcript is filed 

Federal Court Searches as to judgment (except as to bankruptcy) are to be quired only in counties in which the Fe eral Court sits.

18. When the title has passed, or passing through an insurance compan well-known to the examiner as being having been at the time of such passi of title, an insurance company doing bu ness in Missouri, and holding itself on as a company incorporated in the Unit States, it is not incumbent upon the a aminer to require, and unless something appears on the abstract to raise a que tion as to the capacity of such company so acquire and convey such property, the examiner should not require the recor ing in said county and the showing o the abstract of the articles of incorpontion or certificate of incorporation such insurance company. 

19. Whenever real estate has been co veyed to joint tenants or tenants by the entirety, and a conveyance out is made by the surviving joint tenant, joint ter ants or tenant by the entirety, proof of death should be established as follows (a) An affidavit of death, giving da of death, also place of residence, deal and burial, if known, or (b) A sufficient abstract of the pr bate proceedings to establish death, or (c) A certified copy of the death cer tificate.

If the conveyance is from a surviving tenant by the entirety, an addition showing that the marital status continue without divorce from date of acquisition of the real estate until the death of the deceased spouse is required in the af davit, if proof is made as in (a), or b separate affidavit, if proof is made as i (b) or (c).

20. Affidavits, recorded or unrecorded, attached to or included in an abstract of title, may be used to explain apparent defects in the record title resulting from marriage, lack of exact identity or death. 

Affidavits of adverse possession are not generally considered sufficient to eliminate breaks in the record title but may be used to establish marketable title. 

While the recording of an affidavit does not give constructive notice of its contents or make the affidavit admissible in evidence, unless authorized by statute, a requirement to record affidavits considered important to the title or difficult to replace should be approved. Affidavits executed by persons interested in the title should not be rejected for that reason alone.

Comment: 

Bachman vs. H. R. Ennis 

R. E. & Inv. Co., 204 S. W. 1115; Reeves vs. Roberts, 242 S. W. 956; Scannell vs. 

Am. Soda Fountain Co., 61 S. W. 889; 

Aker vs. Lipscomb, 253 S. W. 995; Leath vs. Weaver, 202 S. W. 2d 125; Thomas 

J. Johnson & Co., vs. Mueller, 205 S. W. 

2d 521; 13 M. L. R. 337; Sec. 1851 R. S. 

1939; Patton on Titles, Sec. 22, p. 105. 

21. Where title to real estate is held by an alien, or by a corporation beyond the term authorized in this state, and it does not appear from the records that the state has instituted proceedings to take advantage of the statutory restrictions on the holding of land by such parties, and the restricted title-holder has transferred the title to one capable of receiving and holding it, the examiner should pass the title. Where title to land is now held by such alien or corporation, and the state has not instituted such proceedings, the examiner should pass the title with the notation that the transfer contemplated should be made prior to institution of such proceedings by the state.

Comment: Sec. 624 Gill on Missouri Titles; Secs. 15230 and 15231, R. S. Mo. 

1939; Secs. 5072 and 5030, R. S. Mo. 

1939 (Laws Mo. 1945, P. 696, Sec. 4 (d)); Mo. Const. Art. 12 Sec. 7, (1875) and Mo. Const. Art. 11, Sec. 5, (1945); 

Pembroke vs. Huston, 180 Mo. 627, 79

23. If title to land has emanated from the government, is not subject to public, pious or charitable use or owned by the

State of Missouri, and all persons who appear in the abstract to have had any interest in the record title during the last 45 years have conveyed to the last record owner or persons through whom he derives title, and it is established by affidavits or other instruments attached to or included in the abstract that he and the persons through whom he derives title have been in the continuous, open, exclusive and peaceable possession of said land for at least the last 27 years, such last record owner should be deemed the holder of a marketable title, unless it also appears that breaks or other dedefects in the title prior to the beginning of said record title period involve mortgages or deeds of trust not barred by

Sec. 1017 R. S. 1939, dower since January 

1, 1900, future interests, or interests of married women acquired prior to 1889, not eliminated from the title.

Comment: The Standard construes 

"marketable title" to mean a title free of material doubt (Smith vs. Riordan, 213 S. W. 61) which can be held in peace and sold or mortgaged for full value. 

Rosier vs. Graham, 48 S. W. 470; Reeves vs. Roberts, 242 S. W. 956. A title good in fact (Scannell vs. Am. Soda Fountain 

Co., 61 S. W. 889) rather than marketable of record or perfect of record as required in Aker vs. Lipscomb, 253 S. W. 

995; Danzer vs. Moerschel, 214 S. W. 849 

and Leath vs. Weaver, 202 S. W. 2d 125, 

13 M. L. R. 246. "Marketable title" is intended to include "good title” and “merchantable title." All mean the same. Wiemann vs. 

Steffen, 172 S. W. 472; Rogers vs. Gruber, 174 S. W. 2d 830; Reeves vs. Roberts, 242 S. W. 956. 

The Standard assumes "marketable title" is to be determined by reading an abstract, which will be examined in conjunction with all affidavits and other title instruments attached to or included in it. 

It is accordingly important that in executing a contract for the sale of land, the parties agree that all affidavits and

other title instruments attached to or included in seller's abstract be read and considered in the examination and that seller's title be one marketable in fact rather than marketable of record. or Some printed forms of contract of sale provide seller is to furnish an abstract showing "good", "marketable" "merchantable" title. The Aker, Danzer and Leath cases, cited above, hold that such contracts require record title back to government and that affidavits of adverse possession cannot be used to cure even the oldest breaks in the record title. 

7 A.L.R. 1166. A contract to furnish "warranty deed and abstract of title" was held to require perfect record title. 

Austin vs. Shipman, 141 S. W. 425.

A perfect record title back to government is not necessary to protect the buyer. Jamison vs. Van Auken, 210 

S. W. 404. Record title is often difficult to furnish. Bachman vs. H. R. Ennis R. 

E. and Inv. Co., 204 S. W. 1115. Contracts for such title are unfair to the sel ler (Leath vs. Weaver, 202 S. W. 2d 125) 

and give the buyer no commensurate advantage. Marketable title may in fact be safer and more salable than record title. Jamison vs. Van Auken, 210 S. W. 

lc 416. Marketable title fully protects the buyer. Green vs. Ditsch, 44 S. W. 

799; Scannell vs. Am. Soda Fountain Co., 61 S. W. 889; Wiemann vs. Steffen, 172

S. W. 472. 

The base record title period required in the Standard will probably always exceed 45 years. A valid deed or other transfer prior to the beginning of the period will be necessary to identify the title holders entering the period. 

The Standard is not intended to eliminate examination of the abstract prior to the base record title. All prior breaks or defects must be identified to determine whether barred by the 10, 24 and 3 year statutes. Secs. 1002, 1004 and 1007 R. S. 

1939. These sections reach all defects barred by the 31 year statute (Sec. 1008

R. S. 1939) and do not require proof of payment of taxes. 13 M.L.R. 83. Also all mineral deeds, long term leases, party wall agreements, easements, rights of way, etc., should be excepted.

The record title period of at least 45 years provided in the Standard is of such duration that actually in most titles there will be no defects not cured by limitation statutes. Whether such prior defects which can be cured by limitation should be passed must be determined on the basis of the proof submitted. Affidavits of adverse possession by persons who have owned the land continuously for 27 years or from third parties who have been familiar with the possession during that period, should be considered as evidence of such facts.

An adverse possession title is a lawful title, in every respect as good for purpose of attack or defense as title acquired by deeds back to government. Scannell vs. 

Am. Soda Fountain Co., 61 S. W. 889. 

Mortgages or deeds of trust recorded prior to the base record title period would not be barred by Sec. 1017 R. S. 1939, should the last maturing obligation fall due within the past 20 years.

Dower in land conveyed by the husband prior to 1900, without the wife joining, is now barred. Sec. 1009 R. S. 1939, as to dower since January 1, 1900, limitation does not begin to run until the husband dies. McCrillis vs. Thomas, 85 

S. W. 673. Disability of the wife does not affect the operation of the statute (Belfast Inv. Co., vs. Curry, 175 S. W. 

201) which requires her to bring her action within 10 years or be barred. Sec. 

363 R. S. 1939. 

Adverse possession does not operate against the remainderman or reversioner during the preceding estate. 

Hall vs. 

French, 65 S. W. 769; Coulson vs. La- 

Plant, 196 S. W. 769. 

Nor will limitation bar a married woman's interest when she was married and acquired title prior to the adoption of the Married Woman's Act of 1889, and there has been no conveyance or a defective conveyance, and both husband and wife are living or the wife is living and the husband has been dead less than 

10 years. 

Powell vs. Bowen, 214 S. W. 

142; Wells vs. Egger, 259 S. W. 437. In 

Hubbard vs. Keen, 247 S. W. 1000, heirs of the wife were barred 3 years after her death, insane, in 1916. The husband died in 1912. See also 13 M.L.R. 83.
