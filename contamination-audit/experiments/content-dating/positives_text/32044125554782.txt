CORPORATION SOURCE BOOK OF STATISTICS OF INCOME General Description 

The Corporation Source Book of Statistics of Income provides detailed industry data on corporation income tax returns and forms the basis for the annually published report, Statistics of Income, Corporation Income Tax Returns. Beginning with the Source Book for 1942, data are shown for assets, liabilities, income, deductions, tax liability, distribution to stockholders and other financial items by industry groups and by size of total assets. The Source Book was not produced for 1952 and, for 1962, distribution by asset size class was provided only for major industry groups. Prior to the 

Source Book for 1942, limited statistics are available for each year beginning with 1928, but the items are not classified by asset size class. 

For the years prior to 1956, each minor industry has a single page table for "Returns with net income" and one for "Returns with no net income." For 

1956 and thereafter, except 1962, there is a single page table for "Returns with and without net income" (all returns) and one for "Returns with net income." For 1962, the data for "Returns with and without net income," and for "Returns with net income" appear on the same single page table for each minor industry with no distribution by asset size class.

A complete Source Book consists of about 500 pages (depending on the year) with introductory material. The introductory material includes notes which may change from year to year, a page for industry statistics and their limitations, and a complete listing of the industry groups in the book. The book is divided into two sections: "Returns with and without net income" and "Returns with net income." Each section contains statistics for corporations by minor industry, statistics which group minor industries into about 60 major industries, statistics which group major industries into broad categories of 12 industrial divisions, and a U.S. Total (All Industries) page which is the combination of all industrial divisions. These pages are in the following format: 

Financial items are listed down the left side of the page. There are 13 to 16 columns across the page, including a total column and size classes ranging from zero assets to assets of $250,000,000 or more. "Returns with and without net income" or "Returns with net income" is in the upper left corner. The title of the industry is in the upper right corner of the page.

The Source Book is available annually for returns with accounting periods ended July of one year through June of the following year. This span, in effect, defines the income year in such a way that the noncalendar year accounting periods are centered at the calendar year. For example, the 1983 

Source Book includes returns with accounting periods ended July 1983 through June 1984. Approximately thirty-nine percent of the returns and sixty-five percent of the net income (less deficit) were for the accounting period ended 

December 1983.

INDUSTRY STATISTICS AND THEIR LIMITATIONS 

Prior to 1951, the Corporation Source Book was based on information compiled from all returns filed. Since then, Corporate Statistics of Income has been derived from a stratified probability sample of corporation income tax returns. For all years, the returns used were selected after revenue processing but before audit examination. Returns of the largest corporations are generally in the sample from year to year. For more detailed information on the sample for a specific tax year, see "Description of the Sample and Limitations of the Data" in the complete report Statistics of Income, Corporation 

Income Tax Returns. 

Year to year comparability of Source Book statistics is affected by consolidations and mergers, as well as by changes in the law, the tax forms, and the industrial classification systems used over the years. Changes in the law and tax forms are described each year in Statistics of Income, Corporation 

Income Tax Returns. 

The industry groupings used for Statistics of Income, Corporation Income Tax Returns, conform with the Enterprise Standard Industrial Classification (ESIC), which classifies companies, rather than their individual establishments.

The structure of the classification follows closely along the lines of the Standard Industrial Classification (SIC), which is designed as a means of classifying establishments. (Both of these publications are issued by the 

Office of Management and Budget.) Some departures from the enterprise classification system are made for Statistics of Income for the finance industries in order to reflect particular provisions of the Internal Revenue Code. A comparison between the Standard Industrial Classification, 1972, the 

Enterprise Standard Industrial Classification, 1974 (the most recent editions of each publication) and the groupings used for Statistics of Income, 1977 is shown on pages 9 to 17. -2-

Even though the Statistics of Income classification system is designed to apply to the company rather than the establishment, its application to corporation income tax return statistics has limitations. A return is classified by industry based on the activity accounting for the largest percentage of total receipts. This means that large corporations with diversified activities are included in only one industry even though many of their business operations are unrelated to the industry in which they are classified. Therefore, statistics for an industry may be either understated by amounts reported by corporations whose principal activity lies elsewhere, or overstated by amounts reported by corporations classified in the industry but having substantial operations in other industries. When a company's operations are diversified among related minor industries, the diversification effect may be mitigated somewhat when data for related minor industries are aggregated into the major industries and to a greater degree when related major industries are aggregated into industrial divisions. Major industries and industrial divisions are featured in the published Statistics of Income reports.

Availability of Source Book Under the provisions of Section 6108 of the Internal Revenue Code, the 

Internal Revenue Service may furnish reproductions of the Source Book pages at cost. Each page contains data for an industry group (minor, major, division, or all industries). See pages 5 to 7 for examples of Source Book pages.

Beginning with 1963, complete Source Book sets with data for every industry are available. The complete set is made up of about 500 pages. Beginning with the data for 1965, Source Books are available each year on magnetic tape at the National Archives.
