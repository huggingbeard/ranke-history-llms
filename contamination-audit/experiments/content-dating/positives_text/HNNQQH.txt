The General Modes of Investigation 

While there are many methods of investigation, there are only two general modes of investigation. They seem best designated by the two somewhat uncommon terms, Instrumentalism and Interpretism. They can be less individualistically termed Instrumentation and Interpretation respectively. Let attention first be given to Instrumentalism. It classifies as the judicious use of accessories to the main senses. It is therefore incumbent on us to determine just which of our senses constitute our main ones.

The belief that we have more than five senses is justified. 

Our perception analyses into eight senses and these are the fashioners of information. To analyse is to numerically classify. Hence our senses are numbered from the first to the eighth. Our first sense as might well be supposed is sight or vision or pre-information or the pre-informer. It classifies as the instantaneous copier or more clarificatively sight is the representer of illuminated objects, which explains why objects cannot be seen when not illuminated. Our second sense is hearing or audition or pre-instruction and classifies as the immediate copier or if you prefer hearing is the representer of sounding objects. Our third sense is touch or pre-warning or the pre-warner which classifies as the rapid copier. It further classifies as the representer of appinging objects. Our fourth sense is taste which is the same as pre-signification. It classifies either as the fast copier or as the representer of intro-appinging objects. Our fifth sense is smell or pre-certification and is the hasty copier. It also classifies as the representer of impinging objects. Our other three senses have not been satisfactorily termed. Thus our sixth sense has to be designated the tension-sense and is the same as pre-alarm. It -3-

-4 classifies either as the swift copier or as the representer of elongating objects. Our seventh sense is the pressure-sense and is identical with pre-terror. It is the celeritous copier and also classifies as the representer of compressing objects. Our eighth sense is the one made active by looking over a precipice and is the balance-sense or pre-horror. It classifies either as the accelerative copier or as the representer of precipitating objects. So our eight senses are either the eight quick copiers or the eight representers of imminent objects. Without an exception they are concerned with out personal safety in the midst of an environment of objects or effects that can be dangerous.

But what are the two main senses to which Instrumentalism is accessory? They are sight or the eye and hearing or the ear. Our other senses are subordinate ones. Sight classifies as the precise main sense and hearing as the accurate main sense; and since a main sense is identical with a measurer, it follows that sight is the precise measurer and that hearing is the accurate measurer. It might be noted that a measurer is the same as a limiter and classifies as a two-way disenlarger. 

This brings to light aspects of sight and hearing that are scarcely noticed but which are extremely important to our safety. For if they were not measurers they would be unable to tell us how near menacing objects are to us. Hence sight on the one hand is not telescopic nor on the other hand is it microscopic. Rather it is the precise two-way disenlarger. 

Hearing also is neither telephonic nor is it microphonic but is the accurate two-way disenlarger. So normal sight and hearing are to us extremely valuable.

Instrumentalism being accessory to the measurers, we should next inquire what are its typical varieties. As regards sight these are the telescope which is typical of what are called observational instruments and the microscope which is typical of what are called inspectional instruments. But as regards hearing these are more numerous and include the telephone which is typical of what are called transactional instruments and also the microphone which is typical of what are called detectional instruments. They also include the organ which is typical of what are called the reed instruments and the piano which is typical of what are called the stringed instruments.

-5- Thus the telescope classifies either as the typical magnifying instrument or as the typical super-revealer and the microscope is either the typical enlarging instrument or the typical superdiscloser. Again the telephone classifies either as the typical extensifying instrument or as the typical ultra-revealer and the microphone is either the typical intensifying instrument or the typical ultra-discloser. Finally the organ classifies either as the typical ultra-vocal instrument or as the typical extrarevealer whereas the piano is either the typical ultra-musical instrument or the typical extra-discloser. So the six typical varieties of Instrumentalism are the telescope, the microscope, the telephone, the microphone, the organ and the piano. Incidentally it might be noted for reasons to be given later that those objects that we call telescopes, microscopes, telephones, etc. are really showings of the telescope and of the microscope and of the telephone, etc.

Now the effects on sight and hearing of these typical varieties of Instrumentalism are marvellous. For they actually transform our two measurers. Thus by adjusting a showing of the telescope to our eye we find that we can no longer measure with the eye because the telescope is the typical magnifying transformer of the sight. If next you look through a showing of the microscope, you again lose ability to see how far the objects are from you because the microscope is the enlarging transformer of sight. Then every one has noticed when using a showing of the telephone that he cannot tell the distance or the direction of the voice that sounds clearly and naturally, because the telephone is the typical extensifying transformer of hearing. Then the microphone is the typical intensifying transformer of hearing, and the organ is the ultra-vocal transformer of hearing and the piano is the ultra-musical transformer of hearing. So these six typical varieties of Instrumentalism satisfy our longing for super-revealing or telescopic and super-disclosing or microscopic sight and for ultra-revealing or telephonic and ultra-disclosing or microphonic hearing and also for extra-revealing or organed and extra-disclosing or pianoed hearing. Though they do not contribute to our personal safety as do our measures they contribute to our knowledge vastly and we can hardly help liking them since they

But who avail themselves of them? Professional users of them are designated the telescopist, the microscopist, the telephonist, the microphonist, the organist and the pianist, though anyone may have his sight and hearing transformed by them. 

But those who avail themselves of instrumentalism are fundamentally specialists, and specialists are as numerous as ninety. 

Half of them belong to what is called the Educational System and the other forty-five belong to what is called the Educational Scheme. But they subdivide and classify in manners too numerous to be considered here. Nor is it necessary because the number of specialists who professionally make use of Instrumentalism is only six. The rest seldom avail themselves of Instrumentalism. Accordingly the pianist who makes use of the piano is the same as the singer who is the entertaining specialist and the organist who makes use of the organ is the same as the musician or the affecting specialist. Then the microphonist who makes use of the microphone or stethoscope is the physician or the scrutinizive specialist and the telephonist who makes use of the telephone is the same as business or the gainful specialist. Again the microscopist who makes use of the microscope is the same as the researcher or the inspective specialist while the telescopist who makes use of the telescope is the same as the astronomer or the examinative specialist. So the laboratory of the researcher invariably contains one or more showings of the microscope that are not there for ornament and conspicuous in the observatory of the astronomer are one or more showings of the telescope which are cared for with something akin to affection. 

And no wonder, for Instrumentalism has been especially useful to the astronomer who was not able however to avail himself of the telescope until the beginning of the seventeenth century when Galileo made use of what we would now consider to be a poor showing of it, to his own astonishment and that of his contemporaries. The enthusiasm awakened by such a splendid transformer of sight justifies classifying Instrumentalism as the optimistic general mode of investigation. 

And since Galileo's time showings of the magnifying instrument have been improved in power and efficiency so that the

-7- moon can now be brought to an apparent distance of only fifty miles. Perfection of Instrumentation seems almost to have been reached and yet divergence of opinion in astronomic circles seems of late to be more rife than ever. After what the astronomer has done by adding knowledge to knowledge and thus proving himself the discoverer par excellence it seems invidious to consider another general mode of investigation which has even greater promises of usefulness than Instrumentalism. This leads me then to elaborate Interpretism. 

II. 

Interpretism is utterly different from Instrumentalism or the optimistic general mode of investigation. It classifies as the inevitable or irreplacible general mode of investigation. 

Again while Instrumentalism is the judicious use of accessories to the two main senses, Interpretation is the exclusive use of terms and clauses in reaching conclusions. When compared together, Instrumentalism is more confident than is Interpretism, but the latter is more consummate than Instrumentalism. Both suffer from serious drawbacks that they have to encounter. For example the astronomer even though his sight is transformed by the telescope sees only illuminated objects and his worst limitation is not that. he cannot see thousands of objects in the sky that are not illuminated but that whatever he sees has to be an object or an effect, or a showing of a cause. 

Thus he readily sees millions of showings of stellarity or of the star popularly called stars which were produced by the star and are very slowly disappearing back into the star. But he cannot see stellarity itself because it is a cause. He is confined likewise to seeing illuminated showings of the planet and of the moon and of the comet, and all on account of our measurers being representers of investigatable objects or effects. 

It is impossible to sense causes and it is equally impossible that an effect should be turned into a cause. They are always produced by causes so that to speak of one cause producing another cause is indefencible.

Interpretism is the recognition of the immense difference between causes and the effects that they produce. It is the inevitable general mode of investigation on account of the

enormous problem presented by the very numerous causes which we cannot see nor hear nor in any way sense but which are far more important than the effects they produce which are often very familiar to us and which we can mistake for causes. We all are living in a great ocean of ubiquities or causes many of which are admitted to our brains and become our attributes and in Interpretism is to be found a general mode of investigating them. But it should not be confused with a new method of investigation. Since Bacon's time the belief has prevailed that there are only two methods of investigation namely the inductive or observant method of investigation which is science and the deductive or noticive method of investigation which is really philosophy. But there have been doubters of Bacon's dictum. Actually there are as many as twenty methods of investigation most of which are old studies unrecognized as methods of investigation though some of them are quite new studies. Thus metaphysics is the inferrent method of investigation and ethics is the formative method of investigation. Then there are twenty ways of investigation and twenty-five aiders of investigation and twentyfive helpers of investigation. Indeed each of the ninety specialisms is either an aider or a helper or a method or a way of investigation. For example Biology is the vigorous aider of investigation while Statistics is the condensed way of investigation. But by contrast there are only two general modes of investigation and it required many months to find out the reason why there were not more than two general modes of investigation. But before explaining this reason let me elaborate somewhat on the vast importance of terms and clauses, for it is the exclusive use of them in reaching conclusions that constitutes Interpretism. 

No one can any longer afford to be indifferent to terms and clauses who professes to be an interpretist. It is about impossible to exaggerate their importance. Terms classify as kinds of speech and are identical with judgments or with verities. There are thousands of English terms each of which represents a cause but since causes are more numerous than have been suspected, still more terms will have to be coined by hyphenating known terms in order to represent hitherto unknown causes. Another thing about terms is that they

classify and analyse and evaluate only into other terms. 

Clauses are of course combinations of terms and are identical with co-terms and classify as classes of speech. They and not terms represent effects or objects. 

To represent is the same as to mean or to render or to interpret. Thus when a man says here are twenty pieces of gold, the clause he utters represents or renders or means or interprets twenty pieces of gold that are objects or effects. But if he should say here are twenty golds he would be misapplying terms, for there is only one gold which is a cause and has produced all the pieces of gold that exist and back into which all destroyed pieces of gold disappear. No one can buy or sell gold for it is quite invisible and being a cause or a stream we walk through it and through thousands of other causes quite oblivious of their presence. We have learned however that by melting pieces of gold we can compel gold the cause to produce one or more other pieces of gold.

But this correct usage of representing objects by clauses is not generally adhered to. Rather it is customary to represent objects by means of terms and this has led to a widespread and very subtle confusion. Thus no one speaks about pieces or showings of the leaf. They call them leaves and are then puzzled as how there can be both leaf and leaves. They call showings of light lights and then cannot answer the agelong question of boys and girls where has that light disappeared to that you have just blown out. Light the cause produces all the showings of light which when destroyed disappear back into light. Light has no plural but showings of light at least seem to be its plural. I am not advocating however that people in general should cease terming leaves and lights and dogs and sands and thousands of other effects but that investigators who want to be exact should represent causes by terms and effects by clauses.

Terms are indispensible. They are brief, pronouncible, writeable and readible and what is also remarkable they are actually the only hold that we have on causes. For if it were not for terms we would not even know that most causes exist. 

But even terms present a problem, for our common terms almost invariably are identical with other terms which accord-

ingly are their substitutes or identics. This leads to some confusion which however is more than compensated for by the power of elucidation thereby bestowed. Thus there are many identics of a cause and an effect and of that undivided presentation that we designate a piece. Thus causes are identical with things, streams, unobservables, ubiquities, spiritualities, noumena, universals, beings, finalities, ultimates, existences, and realities, while Effects are identical with objects, localizations, discernibles, consolidations, materialities, phenomena particulars, interactivities, temporalities, exiguities, transiencies, and derivatives. On the other hand pieces are identical with atoms, concentrations, fragments, growths, showings, professions, exponencies, states, conditions, symptoms and publications. Hence without changing our meaning in the least we can say that pieces of gold are the same as atoms of gold or as concentrations of gold or as fragments of gold or as showings of gold or as growths of gold or as professions of gold or as exponencies of gold or as conditions of gold or as states of gold or as symptoms of gold or as publications of gold. And without variation of meaning we can also say that causes produce effects and things produce objects and streams produce localizations and unobservables produce discernibles and that ubiquities produce consolidations and that spiritualities produce materialities and that noumena produce phenomena and that universals produce particulars and that beings produce interactivities and that finalities produce temporalities and that ultimates produce exiguities and permanents produce transiencies and that realities produce derivatives. But I think that you will agree that the most elucidative of these identical statements is that causes produce effects or showings of causes.

It is only because we have an abundance of terms to represent causes that we can successfully cope with the situation presented by the great number of causes as well as by the fact that our senses are of no help in detecting them. Why we cannot sense causes is now obvious for they are the same as streams or ubiquities whereas effects that we can sense are the same as localizations or consolidations. Yet we are quite aware of some causes which attribute us. Thus a pained man is attributed by a cause termed pain which is identical with

abhorrence and an aching man is attributed by a cause termed ache which is identical with loathing. And a happy man is attributed by a cause termed happiness. We are well acquainted with causes on account of ourselves being causes. 

Thus a man classifies as a directive cause, a woman classifies as a pleasing cause, a boy classifies as a trainable cause and a girl classifies as an instructible cause. But you say men, women, boys and girls can be sensed for they have bodies. No man is mistaken when he speaks of his body but only when he identifies his body with the single showing of it produced by his body and which when illuminated can readily be seen as are other illuminated objects or effects. But his tooth which like his body is an invisible cause produces many showings of itself mistakenly called teeth which like other effects or transiencies tend to decay back into the tooth that produced them. As for his beard it is a cause that produces hundreds of showings of itself which many men shave off. 

There is one cause that does not attribute us but is outside us whose operations can be watched perfectly though itself is invisible and this is cloud or fog or the dimmer. Almost any day you can watch it at work producing pieces or concentrations of itself often with startling rapidity and at times you can as readily see pieces of cloud mistakenly called clouds disappearing back or dissipating back into cloud which produced them. The idea that pieces of cloud evaporate into air is about as hypothetical as the idea that pieces of light disappear into space instead of into light.

We are now in a better position to explain why there can be only two general modes of investigation. They are really the two-way relations that exist between terms and causes on the one hand and between clauses and effects on the other hand. 

The traditional view that there is only a one-way relation between terms and causes needs to be revised. For instance no one would think of denying that there is a relation between gold the term and gold the thing or cause. It is always and properly taken for granted when we pronounce or write gold the term that we mean gold the thing. Meaning then is a relation that exists between gold the term and gold the thing just as it is the relation that exists between a piece of gold the clause and a piece of gold the effect, and also meaning is iden-

tical with representing and with interpreting or representationalism or interpretism. So Interpretism or the inevitable general mode of investigation has unwittingly been believed in for ages. Why it was not long ago used will be discussed later but briefly it has been on account of inability to distinguish terms from non-terms. 

But what has never been taken for granted nor even suspected is the relation that exists between causes and terms on the one hand and between effects and clauses on the other hand. It is extremely important to secure this relation. Thus if gold the term means or represents or interpretizes gold the thing, then it follows that gold the thing accords with or instruments or instrumentalizes gold the term and hence also a piece of gold the object instrumentalizes or accords with a piece of gold the clause. Hence Instrumentalism is identical with according-with and is the optimistic general mode of investigation. Since there can be no more than two relations between terms and causes and between clauses and effects, there can be no more than two general modes of investigation, namely Interpretism and Instrumentalism. 

At last we are able to classify terms as the interpreters or representers of causes and to classify causes as instrumenters of terms. Also clauses are representers of effects and effects are instrumenters of clauses. Terms are utterly unique but clauses bear a resemblance to our two measurers or main senses, viz. sight and hearing. Thus sight represents or interprets illuminated effects or objects which in turn correspond with sight and hearing interprets sounding objects which in turn correspond with hearing. But our measurers interpret investigatable objects which in turn instrument or accord with the measurers, whereas clauses interpret any effects whatever which in turn instrument or accord with clauses. So clauses are not limited to investigatable objects as are our measurers. 

And terms likewise are not restricted in the least. They interpret all causes whatever and in turn these accord with them. 

The importance of terms and clauses is now seen to be so immense that instead of being mere descriptive tags for causes and effects they are on a par with these. It needs little imagination to see that the age-long attitude of mild contempt for

terms in comparison with the respect paid to causes must now be changed. If even causes must accord with the terms that represent them then every attempt should be made to understand our terms. For as you elucidate a term you elucidate the cause that it represents. Classify it and you classify the cause that it represents. Analyse it into other terms and you analyse the cause that it represents into other causes represented by those other terms. In short terms have too long been treated like Cinderellas. It is time to treat them like princesses.

But the drawbacks of Interpretism are much more serious than those of Instrumentalism. For one thing they are exceedingly subtle. Terms have six rivals designated the feigners or non-terms which are so elusive that only the Superuniversity which is the ultra-discriminative specialist can assort terms from non-terms. Thus words or suppositions simulate terms and are kinds of talk which erroneously interpret causes, e.g. clouds. Then names or misdescriptions counterfeit terms and are kinds of humour which oddly interpret causes, e.g. 

Arcturus. Myths or misconceptions imitate terms and are kinds of fiction which wrongly interpret causes, e.g. mermaid, while guesses or misinterpretations copy terms and are kinds of surmise which incorrectly interpret causes, e.g. expectation. 

Lies or misinformations beliken terms and are kinds of falsity which dubiously interpret causes, e.g. fairy. Artifices or misindications mimic terms and are kinds of play which trivially interpret causes, e.g. fantastic. Thus while terms rectify belief, words beguile, names mislead, myths mystify belief and guesses dupe, lies deceive and artifices charm belief. 

Since terms alone interpret causes, those who would use Interpretism must carefully guard against employing words, names, myths, guesses, lies and artifices. These abound in our verbiage better known as Syntax. There should be no prejudice against non-terms. Even if they masquerade as terms they at least prove the vast importance of the latter when they commingle with terms for the express purpose of preventing terms being recognized as such. Without them there would be no talk, no humour, no fiction or imagination, no surmise, no fancy or falsity, no play or gesticulation. Also many nonterms are interesting especially myths even if dangerous to

belief. But of course they cannot be dangerous to those who know that they are non-terms. But the point now being emphasized is that non-terms are utterly useless as means of interpreting causes.

Terms and non-terms classify as main signs though the Dictionary and Grammar and Syntax call them words. Hence the Interpretist cannot afford to take the latter seriously. 

Even as regards terms on which such issues hang, Grammar allows some that are exclusively singular to be pluralized and others which are exclusively plural to be singularized. For instance Grammar asserts that the singular of colours is colour. You have seen thousands of showings of red and green, etc. But if you have ever seen a showing of colour you should publish your discovery with a description. On the other hand Grammar asserts that the plural of coal is coals. 

But coal is that one cause which has produced all the pieces of coal that exist which when being destroyed by fire keep disappearing back into coal that produced them. So coals is a word or supposition or a kind of talk. Coal has no plural. 

The Dictionary pays no attention to terms as in any way. different from words. The largest American distionary does not even try to define them much less to give examples of terms. Besides most dictionaries emphasize etymologies those queer baits which millions have swallowed to the misleading of their belief. Thus how many there are who believe that 

Science is knowledge solely on account of its etymology. It however is really identical with discovery. Those who would use Interpretism, first want terms identified with one another for thus they are elucidated. For instance electricity and fire and flame are identics. Then they want terms classified for thus they are clarified. For example sleep is the refreshing tendency. Finally they want terms analysed for thus they are simplified. For example hearing is the second sense. instead of identics the Dictionary offers synonyms and instead of classifications it offers definitions and for analyses it has nothing whatever to offer. While Grammar Syntax and the 

Dictionary are useless to the Interpretist it does not follow that they should be discarded by those who are not Interpretists. This will not happen because however badly these three classify, Grammar is identical with facility and Syntax But

is identical with convenience and the Dictionary is identical with utility.

But those using Interpretism professionally will have to prepare a manual containing first of all the most important of our terms and perhaps a few of the most important non-terms that are specially dangerous to belief. Towards this end they will have to rely on at least four specialists that occur so high up in the Educational System that they have somehow missed being discovered. Thus by means of the Superuniversity they will be enabled to assort terms from non-terms and by means of Hypology they will be enabled to identify terms and by means of Hyperethics they will be enabled to classify terms and by means of Hypernomics they will be enabled to analyse terms. This manual should be designated the Appraiser of 

Terms or what is the same thing the Valuator. 

The real reason why we are compelled to fall back on Interpretism is that it is the inevitable general mode of investigation. It has for ages been in the background as a last resort for perplexed men and we are perplexed rather than ignorant because ignorance is a myth and does not exist. We need 

Interpretism even to enable us properly to understand effects. 

But we supremely need it to help us not only to understand causes but even to be sure that most causes exist. For one thing Interpretism teaches us to ask questions put into suitable form. For instance how often has the question been asked what is the final cause or thing? The better form is what is that term which analyses into all other terms? There is only one answer though there seem to be more than six because the latter are identical with one another. But it is better to choose such of these identical terms as analyse into other terms that are obviously homologous with them and above all to choose those that are the simplest.

Judged by these requirements, the term that had best be chosen is World or the World which classifies as the unique term among terms. World the term therefore interprets 

World the cause which classifies as the unique cause among causes, and in turn World the cause accords with or instruments World the term. The uniqueness of World the term consists in its being the totality of all other terms, just as the unique-

ness of World the cause consists in World the cause being the totality of all other causes. Accordingly the interpretist cannot find a term to work from, that excells World the term and of course terms identical with it. All other terms except its identics belong to World the term, just as all other causes belong to World the cause. Hence all other terms are units of World the term and classify as such-and-such distinctions while all other causes are of course units of World the cause and classify as such-and-such distinctions. So any term you can think of it identical with some distinction and interprets some cause.

Language seems the next best identic to World the term on account of analysing into terms homologous with itself. Harmony, Perfection and Transcendency also are identical with World the term. Once it is ascertained that World the term is the totality of all other terms, the Interpretist has largely to rely on Hypernomics which alone among the specialists has the power of analysing terms, though Hypology and Hyperethics play their co-operating parts too. Thus the 

World analyses into the co-existence of the twenty-five main worlds. And since co-existence is the same as simultaneity, sum and system, the World is also the simultaneity or sum of the twenty-five main worlds or the system of the twenty-five main worlds. Language too is the sum of the twenty-five main languages and Perfection is the system of the twenty-five main perfections. In this manner the old paradox between there being the one world and the many main worlds is explained, only there has always been doubt as to the number of the main worlds.

Among the twenty-five main worlds number and order prevail. Thus they are numeralized from the first to the twentyfifth and this makes them least arbitrarily distinguished from one another. But there is yet another arrangement of them depending also on number and order. They are divided into five groups of main worlds. These groups of five main worlds each, are designated the Congruities and the worlds of each Congruity are arranged consecutively from the first to the fifth. The first Congruity or group of main worlds comprise the Inner Worlds extending from the first to the fifth Inner 

World. The second and the third Congruities are designated

the five Over Worlds and the five Outer Worlds respectively. 

The fourth and the fifth Congruities are the five Utmost Worlds and the five Uttermost Worlds respectively. 

We are especially interested in the first Congruity for we clearly dwell in it. These five Inner Worlds constitute Eternity which evaluates as the most important of the five Congruities. We have as might be expected familiar terms for them all and of course immensely comprehensive. Thus the first Inner World is Time and the second Inner World is Space and the third Inner World is Course and the fourth Inner World is Motion while the fifth Inner World is termed Shape. 

Eternity or form therefore is the inter-operation of Time, Space, Course, Motion and Shape. But he would be a bold interpretist who would essay any other than a large-scale analysis of such multiplex things as the five inner worlds just mentioned. But let me here present a sketchy analysis of 

Space that is the second inner world. It is identical with divisibility and analyses into four Conformations of which the first is Immensity or room and the second is the Universe or weight or density and the third is the Creation or the miracle and the fourth is Illumination or heat or electro-magnetism. Then 

Immensity analyses into four Bounds which are Magnitude, Distance, Expanse and Capaciousness. Again the Universe analyses into twelve Substances or stuffs. The four main Substances are Stellarity or the star or combustibility, the Planet or illuminability, Lunarity or reflectibility and the Planetoid or flarableness. The four subsidiary Substances are the 

Meteor or sub-combustibility, Nebularity or sub-illuminability, the Comet or sub-reflectibility and the Ether or inter-penetrability. The four supplementary Substances are Matter or rejection or friction, Physicality or obstinancy, Materiality or resistance and Mechanicality or opposition. 

Then the Creation analyses into eight Natures or wonders, which are Ground, Climate, Weather, Humidity, Water, Air, Animality and Salubriousness. Finally Illumination or the fourth Conformation analyses in two quite distinct manners. 

It first analyses into two envelopers namely Night or magnetism or shadow or encirclement and Day or magneto-electricity or warmth or light or straightness. Again it analyses

into eight Tints which are Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue,

Indigo, Violet and Transparency or ultra-violet. Hence what we call the Sun, emits Illumination and its two envelopers and its eight tints and these classify as the eleven rays and constitute the elevenfold ray which is burning or fire or electricity. 

These classify as the twelve glows or projectibles and are Burning, Night, Day, Illumination, and the eight tints and produce billions of extremely brief-lifed showings of themselves which our eye can opticalize though we cannot see the glows themselves, because they are streams or causes. 

But there are four of these glows or projectibles which to us are immensely useful and of whose showings we never cease to be aware. These are the four chemical modifiers or attributers of objects or more simply they are the four temperatures most commonly termed fire, heat, warmth and chill. Confusion exists regarding the temperatures because each has identics that seem unlikely and each is related to objects in distinctly different manners. Thus the first temperature or the first chemical modifier of objects or the elevenfold glow is Fire which is identical with electricity, burning, flame, lightning, power repulsion, energy and push. Fire destroys or unmakes or unproduces objects. Thus when fire becomes a chemical modifier of a piece of wood which was produced by wood the cause, it destroys that piece of wood and compels it to disappear back into wood. Yet as the piece of wood is being destroyed ash is compelled to produce pieces of itself commonly called ashes and somehow supposed to be constituents of the piece of wood. This however is a mere inference. 

The second temperature or the second chemical modifier of objects or the brilliant glow is Heat which is identical with 

Electromagnetism or repulsion-attraction or push-pull or power-force or illumination or shine. Heat bakes or cooks or boils or partly destroys or partly unmakes objects. Thus when 

Heat becomes a chemical modifier of a piece of wood, it bakes or partly destroys it and compels that piece of wood partly to disappear back into wood its cause and at the same time compels charcoal another cause to produce showings of itself. 

Pieces of charcoal are therefore partly produced by wood and partly produced by ash. The third temperature or the third chemical modifier of objects or the prominent glow is Warmth

which is identical with magneto-electricity or attractionrepulsion or pull-push or force-power or day or light. Warmth fades or stagnates or ferments or slightly destroys or slightly unmakes objects. Thus when warmth becomes a chemical modifier of a piece of wood it fades or stagnates or ferments or slightly destroys it and compels it slightly to disappear back into wood its cause and at the same time compels punk which is another cause to make showings of itself. Pieces of punk are therefore produced partly by wood and partly by rot. The fourth temperature or the fourth chemical modifier of objects or the subdued glow, is Chill which is identical with magnetism, cold, coolness, frigidity, night, darkness, pull, attraction, force and encirclement. Thus when Chill becomes a chemical modifier of a piece of wood, it preserves or undetectibly destroys it and compels it undetectibly to disappear back into wood its cause and at the same time compels keep another cause to make a showing of itself. A piece of keep therefore is produced partly by wood and partly by searness. 

Our ability to fire, heat, warm and chill objects gives us an inestimable advantage when proceeding to deal with them. 

But the fact that every object of sense is either a fired or a heated or a warmed or a chilled object suggests that objects can have also modifiers or attributes or properties or qualities other than temperatures. Indeed this explains why most objects are really more complicated than we suspect. The fact that mere objects can be modified by causes is a profound one because it means that there are numerous thing-objects or cause-effects which have to be represented by term-clauses. 

For instance we say here is a piece of gold which is a correct statement solely on account of that sign known as 'a' and which is perhaps the most marvellous sign in English. It is not the indefinite article but the indefinite prefix just as 'the' is the definite prefix. So when you say a piece of gold you imply an attributed piece of gold. Among its attributes is certainly weight which is the most elucidative identic of the universe. Hence a weighty piece of gold is identical with a gold piece of weight or of the universe and a matterous or rejective piece of gold is identical with a gold piece of matter or of rejection and a spaced or divisible piece of gold is identical with a gold piece of space or of divisibility and a yellow

piece of gold is identical with a gold piece of yellowness. You can thus see why pieces of gold and of iron and of wood and of ice, etc. are all represented as pieces of matter which representation is made possible by the wonderful prefix ‘a.' 

The four temperatures are more subtle than we think. 

Thus Fire is further identical with expansiveness and Heat with fluence and Warmth with softness and Chill with hardness. Moreover they each divide into a hundred degrees thus making what we call the thermometer the conjectural instrument. This explains why chilled or hard pieces of iron can exist side by side with warm or soft pieces of leather and with heated or fluent pieces of oil and with fired or expansive pieces air. Otherwise this earth would be uninhabitable. It also explains why steam, water, slush and ice can be identical. For a fired or expansive piece of steam is the same as a fired or expansive piece of water or of slush or of ice or of the freezer.

Let me conclude this study with a more minute comparison of Instrumentalism with Interpretism. The former classifies not only as the optimistic general mode of Investigation but as the commonsense Technique, and the latter classifies not only as the inevitable general mode of Investigation but as the meditative Technique. Then the subsidiaries of Instrumentalism are Sight and Hearing whereas those of Interpretism are Terms and Clauses. Again the auxiliaries of the former are designated Inductions while those of the latter are designated Deductions. Also the supplements of Instrumentalism are, strange to state, Syntax, Grammar and the Dictionary while those of Interpretism are Comparison, Contrast and the Valuator. Finally Instrumentalism foreruns 

Interpretism which in turn supplements Instrumentalism. This means that Instrumentalism classifies as the forerunner of Interpretism and that the latter classifies as the supplementer of Instrumentalism.
