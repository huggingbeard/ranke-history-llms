Summary: 

Direct Appropriation From the State General Fund 

The Thirty-seventh South Dakota Legislature was confronted with the staggering number of 203 bills to appropriate money from the State General Fund. In the final analysis, 141 of these bills, appropriating $67,497,750.40, became law.

General Appropriations Bill 

The general appropriations proposal submitted to the Legislature by retiring Governor Herseth called for appropriations of $41,446,600.00 A supplemental request submitted by Governor 

Gubbrud (for an increased allowance for the Industrial Development and Expansion Agency) brought the recommended amount to $41,546,600.00. 

The general appropriations bill adopted by the 1961 Legislature reduced, in the aggregate, the Herseth recommendations by $655,670.00. The Gubbrud recommendation adding $100,000.00 to the I.D.E.A. budget was granted. The bill as finally approved by the Governor appropriated $40,890,930.00.

Recommended Special Appropriation Bills 

Former Governor Herseth asked for appropriations not included in the general bill totalling $23,999,116.60. Two items in his 

A request (bank franchise reversion, $800,000.00, and insect control, $30,000.00), were already provided in law; therefore the total special bills he recommended amounted to $23,169,116.60.

Governor Gubbrud's original request for additional special appropriations came to $1,406,000.00; he later supplemented this amount by $70,000.00 for clean-up, and $1,245,000.00 for a building, as a result of the fire at Northern State Teachers College. brought the total additional requests by Governor Gubbrud to $2,721,000.00, and the aggregate total of recommended special bills to $25,890,116.60.

ACTS ABOUT THE SOUTH DAKOTA LEGISLATIVE RESEARCH COUNCIL. 

The State Legislative Research Council is a permanent, joint committee composed of every member of the South Dakota Senate and House of Represent atives. During the interim between sessions, advance study and consideration is given to problems expected to confront the next Legislature. The Council conducts most of its work through an Executive Board, and through standing and special committees and their subcommittees. The Council staff serves every member of the Legislature in a fact-finding capacity and assists the Executive Board and the committees in developing a program for consideration at the next session.

In its work, the Executive Board: 1) acts as a clearing house for ideas on current legislative problems by receiving proposals for study from any member of the Legislature; 2) determines and directs, through its committees and staff, the study and research necessary for proper consideration of all proposals; 

3) disseminates advance information on these problems to legislators and to the general public by means of reports and newsletters and by discussion during and between Board and committee meetings; and 4) reports directly to the Legislature, in advance of the regular session, making recommendations in the form of bills or otherwise, and summarizes the material prepared for use of the Legislature in considering the Council program. 

Since 1951 the State Legislative Research Council has provided an open forum to which citizens and organizations have submitted their proposals for consideration. As a result of the Council activities, problems of South Dakota state and local government receive continuous attention and study by members of the Legislature.
