PART I APPELLATE COURTS OF NEW YORK 

1. Appellate courts; enumerated. In the State of New

York the courts of appellate review are the Court of Appeals,¹ the Appellate Division of the Supreme Court² and the Appellate Term of the Supreme Court.³ Appeals to the 

Court of Appeals are taken from the determinations of the Appellate Division; and from the City Court of the 

City of New York, where the only question involved is the validity of a statutory provision of the State or of the United States under the Constitution of the State or of the United States; and on any such appeal only the constitutional question may be considered and determined.5 

Appeals from the Supreme Court, the Appellate Term of the Supreme Court, and from certain other courts, like the County Court, the Surrogate's Court, and certain criminal courts, are taken to the Appellate Division; appeals from the City Court and Municipal Court of the City of New York, are taken to the Appellate Term of the Supreme Court. In certain criminal cases, in the City of New 

York, an appeal may be taken to the Appellate Part of the Court of Special Sessions; in other instances, to the 

County Court." 6

PART II APPELLATE TERM OF SUPREME COURT 

2. Appellate Term; creation; composition; jurisdiction.

In the City of New York appeals from the Municipal Court and from the City Court of said city are taken to the tribunal known as the Appellate Term of the Supreme Court;¹ but, as stated, an appeal from the City Court may be taken direct to the Court of Appeals where the only question involved is the validity of a statutory provision of the State or of the United States under the Constitution of the State or of the United States; and on any such appeal only the constitutional question may be considered and determined." The Appellate Term is composed of three justices of the Supreme Court in each of first and second departments, designated by the justices of the Appellate Division of the Supreme Court, sitting in said departments, respectively.¹ 12 

3. Appellate Term; where held; appeals, where heard.

As to appeals from the Municipal Court within the Boroughs of Manhattan and the Bronx, and appeals from the City Court within the Counties of New York and the 

Bronx, these are heard in the First Department;13 as to those from the Municipal Court within the Boroughs of Brooklyn, Queens and Richmond, and from the City Court, Kings, Queens and Richmond Counties, these are heard in the Second Department.14 The court in the first department meets at the new County Court House, Centre Street and Foley Square, Manhattan Borough;15 in the second department it meets at the County Court House, Fulton and Joralemon Streets, in Brooklyn.16 

13 Rule I, App. T., 1st and 2nd Depts. 14Ibid. 15Ibid. 18Ibid. 772

4. Appellate Term; calendar of appeals. For each appointed term, the clerk of the Appellate Term makes up a calendar of appeals from the Municipal and City courts. 

With respect to Municipal Court appeals in which the return has been made up, it is placed on the calendar if it is filed with the clerk of the Appellate Term at least ten days before the first day of the term.17 With respect to 

City Court appeals, it is placed upon the calendar, provided ten printed copies of the record are filed, with proof of service of three printed copies thereof upon the attorneys for the respondents, at least ten days before the first day of the term.18 

17 Rule II, App. T. 1st and 2nd Depts. 18Ibid. 

5. Appellate Term; publication of calendar; rules. The calendar is published in the New York Law Journal [the official organ of the courts in the city] at least eight days before the first day of the term.19 Upon this calendar appears the name of the case, with its calendar number; the day and time when, and place where the calendar will be called; also the names of the justices assigned to hold the 

Appellate Term. On the first day of the term appeals from the City Court are called; on the second day appeals from the Municipal Court are called.20 The Appellate Term in each department has established Rules of practice and procedure in such tribunals; these generally harmonize, but there are variations and the Rules must be consulted.21

PART III MUNICIPAL COURT APPEALS 

6. Appeals; appellate jurisdiction; nature. Appeals from the Municipal Court of the City of New York may be taken by virtue of the provisions of the Municipal 

Court Code, sections 154 and 155, thereof. Section 154 

deals with the right of appeal, generally; section 155 deals with the review of intermediate orders.22 

22 See Sections 7, 8, 10 and 11, hereof. 

7. Appeals; as of right. Municipal Court appeals, as of right, may be taken in the following cases: From a judgment in an action; from a final order in a special proceeding; from an order granting or denying a new trial; from an order granting or denying a motion to open a default and to vacate a judgment entered thereon; from an order granting or denying a motion to vacate a judgment or a final order upon the ground that the judgment was rendered or the final order made without service of summons or process; from an order granting or denying a motion to discharge a defendant from arrest, or an order granting or denying a motion to vacate or modify a warrant of attachment or a requisition to replevy or a warrant of seizure.28 

23 Sec. 154, Mun. Ct. Code. 

8. Appeals; by permission. An appeal may be taken from any other order, provided leave to appeal is granted either by the justice who made the order, upon a motion returnable within eight days after the making of the said order, or by a justice of the Appellate Term in the event of the denial of such leave in the Municipal Court.24 

24 Sec. 154, subd. 7, Mun. Ct. Code.

9. Appeals; permission; application to appellate court.

Where application for leave to appeal is made to a justice of the Appellate Term, it must be made upon three days' notice, returnable within eight days after denial of the application by the justice of the Municipal Court.25 The papers upon which such application is made must contain a copy of the pleadings, the order entered in the Municipal Court and the papers therein recited, a concise statement of the grounds of alleged error, and if there be an opinion, a copy thereof.26 

25 Rule VI, App. T. 1st and 2nd Depts. 26Ibid. 

10. Appeal; intermediate orders; review. Where an appeal is taken from a judgment or final order, an intermediate order which necessarily affects the judgment or final order and which has not already been reviewed upon a separate appeal, may be reviewed, provided it is specified in the notice of appeal.27 An order made after judgment or final order is deemed to have been made in the action or special proceeding, within the meaning of the statute authorizing such review. 28 

27 Sec. 155, Mun. Ct. Code. 28Ibid. 

11. Appeal; time; limitation. An appeal from a judgment or order of the Municipal Court must be taken within twenty days after the entry thereof; except that when the appellant has appeared by an attorney, it must be taken within twenty days after the service upon such attorney of a copy of the judgment, order or final order, and written notice of the entry thereof.20 The right to review an intermediate order is not affected by the expiration of the time within which a separate appeal therefrom might have been taken.80 

29 Sec. 156, Mun. Ct. Code. 30Ibid. 

12. Appeal; how taken; service; filing. An appeal from a determination of the Municipal Court is taken by filing

with the clerk of the court in the district where the judgment, order or final order is entered, a written notice of appeal, subscribed by the appellant, or by his attorney, together with proof of service thereof upon the attorney for the respondent, if he has appeared by an attorney; and if he has not so appeared, notice thereof is given to him by the clerk. The form of notice which appears in section 23a, hereof, may be employed. 

31 Sec. 157, Mun. Ct. Code. Rule XXV, Mun. Ct. 

13. Appeal; stay; undertaking. Execution of the judgment may be stayed during the pendency of the appeal by filing an undertaking, or in lieu thereof, by depositing cash with the clerk.32 Where the respondent has appeared by attorney, the following is the procedure to be followed: 

In the case of a money judgment, a written undertaking must be filed with the clerk and a copy served upon the attorney for the respondent, with notice of filing; this undertaking must be executed by one or more sureties, approved by a justice of the court, to the effect that if the appeal is dismissed, or if judgment is rendered against the appellant in the appellate court and an execution issued thereon is returned wholly or partly unsatisfied, the sureties will pay the amount of the judgment or the portion thereof remaining unsatisfied and all costs and disbursements awarded against him on the appeal, not exceeding a sum specified in the undertaking, which must be at least one hundred dollars, and not less than twice the amount of the judgment.33 If the judgment is for the recovery of a chattel, the undertaking must provide that the sureties will pay the value of the chattel, together with costs and the damages, if any, awarded by the judgment and on appeal.34

14. Appeal; undertaking; cash. In lieu of an undertaking, the appellant may stay execution by depositing with the clerk a sum of money equal to the amount of the judgment, with interest thereon for one year, and fifty dollars in addition, provided notice of such deposit is served upon the attorney for the respondent, if he has appeared by attorney.35 

85 Sec. 159, subd. 2, Mun. Ct. Code. 

15. Appeal; execution; issuance; undertaking; effect.

If execution has been issued, the service upon the sheriff or marshal of a copy of the undertaking, certified by the clerk or accompanied by an affidavit showing that it is a copy thereof and that the original has been duly filed, stays further proceedings under the execution.86 

86 Sec. 159, subd. 1, Mun. Ct. Code.

16. Undertaking; sureties; exception to; justification; failure; effect. Within five days after notice by the clerk of the filing of the undertaking, the respondent, or his attorney, may serve upon the appellant or his attorney, a written notice that he excepts to the sufficiency of the sureties. Within five days thereafter, the sureties, or other sureties in a new undertaking to the same effect, must justify on at least three days' notice, before the court in the district in which the judgment was rendered. The court must endorse upon the undertaking, or a copy thereof, its allowance or rejection of the sureties. If the sureties fail or refuse to justify after service of the notice of exception, the respondent may proceed as if no undertaking had been executed.87 

37 Sec. 160, Mun. Ct. Code.

19. Procedure; after appeal taken. After the notice of appeal has been served and filed, it is necessary to settle the form of the appeal record, called in Municipal Court appeals, "the Return." After the return has been settled, it is necessary to file it, as a preliminary step to bringing on the appeal for disposition; it is optional with the appellant whether to print the return or have it presented in typewritten form [almost invariably the case].¹¹ 

41 Rule IV, App. T. 1st and 2nd Depts. 

20. Return; case; settlement; contents; where testimony taken; filing. Where testimony was taken, the appellant must, within five days after filing the notice of appeal, deposit with the clerk of the Municipal Court the fees of the stenographer for a transcript of his minutes.42 

Where no testimony was taken and it is not necessary to settle a case, the return must be filed within fifteen days after the filing of the notice of appeal.43 Returns from the Municipal Court, if printed or typewritten, must be on suitable paper 8 inches wide and 10½ inches long, numbered at the bottom of each page, fastened at the left-hand edge, bound with a substantial cover and appropriately indexed; the transcript of the stenographer's minutes, must be on paper of the same size; the lines thereon must be consecutively numbered on the left-hand margin; at the top of each page containing testimony must be noted the name of the witness whose testimony appears thereon, and of the party by whom called, and a statement whether the examination is direct, cross or redirect; there must also be at the top of each exhibit a statement specifying the

page of the stenographer's minutes at which it was admitted in evidence.¹4

22. Return; case; settlement; amendments [continued].

After the notice of settlement of the case has been served and filed, the clerk must thereupon make a return, which is presented on the return day of the notice to the justice whose judgment is being appealed from, i.e., the trial justice, and he is required, within five days from the date of the submission to him of the case on appeal, to settle the case and endorse his settlement on the return.49 After a justice is out of office he may settle the case in any action or proceeding tried before him and may be compelled by the appellate court to do so.50 Amendments to the form of the record may be presented for disposition at the time the return is presented to the justice for settlement; this is the accepted practice in the Municipal Court; no specific provision or rule exists; but the practice should follow, as nearly as may be, that upon appeals from the City Court.51

ARGUMENT AND LAW POINT I 

There was a completed and binding accord and satisfaction of all claims for damages. In October, 193 the Jackson Corporation, having demanded an allowance, claiming that 202 pairs of slippers were defective, appellant agreed to allow it the sum of $324.53, and Jackson Corporation paid its over-due bill at this time, deducting this allowance. (Defendant's Exhibits 

C and D, pp. 28A, 29A; Jones, Minutes p. 81, lines 12-25; p. 83, line 23, to p. 85, line 7.) ·

The credit bill prepared by Jackson Corporation (De fendant's Exhibit C, p. 28A) expressly states "Net credit due for damage, $324.53". The Treasurer and Manager of appellant testified that the allowance was to be in full settlement, that it was made without investigation on appellant's part as to the cause of the alleged damage, but merely to maintain the good will of a customer, and that nothing was said as to any further allowances (Worth, p. 137, lines 14-25; p. 139, lines 7-11; Jones, p. 168, line 16, to p. 169, line 17).

The only other testimony on this subject was that of Jones, who had previously testified that the settlement

was a partial one only, was made with Worth alone and without any conversation with Mr. Fellowes (Jones, p. 

75, line 1, to p. 76, line 16). Jones, however, failed to rebut or explain the matters brought out in the subsequent testimony of Worth and Wise (supra), and failed to explain the language of Defendant's Exhibit C (p. 28A), except to say that it was just "his particular wording" (Jones, p. 81, lines 17-21).

If the settlement was a partial one only, as claimed by respondent, it is material to ask how it was that the Jackson Corporation made no effort to examine the unsold slippers in its bins, until fully eleven months later, to wit: 

October, 193 (Jones, p. 72, line 15, to p. 73, line 16).

It is submitted that the settlement, when consummated by the payment of the balance of the bill, operated as a completed accord and satisfaction and discharge of any and all claims by the Jackson Corporation against appellant for damages.

It is submitted, also, that, if such a settlement be held to have such probative value, all settlements of claims would be exceedingly perilous, as constituting substantial proof, in the event of a future litigation, of the validity of the claim which had been settled. This is directly contrary to the well-established doctrine of the law, based on the clear advisability of encouraging the settlement of controverted claim, that an offer to settle a disputed claim is not an admission of liability. (No citations are necessary for such a proposition.)

A fortiori, it is submitted that the consummated settlement itself cannot be deemed an admission of liability by the defendant. The courts have so held: 

Emery v. Litchard, 137 Misc. Rep. 885, 245 

N. Y. S. 209 (and the cases cited therein); 

Baldwin v. New York, Central & H. RR. Co., 2 N. Y. S. 481; affirmed by the Court of 

Appeals, 121 N. Y. 684; 

Crawford v. McLeod, 64 Alabama, 240.

It is respectfully submitted that the learned trial justice was clearly in error on the effect of the settlement.

37. Appeals; appellate jurisdiction; nature. Appeals from the City Court of the City of New York may be taken to the Appellate Term of the Supreme Court by virtue of the provisions of the New York City Court Act, e.g., Sections 56, 57, 58, thereof, and by Section 59, the

Appellate Term is vested with full power to review any exercise of discretion by the court or a justice thereof. 

But an appeal from the City Court may be taken direct to the Court of Appeals where the only question involved is the validity of a statutory provision of the state or of the United States under the Constitution of the state or of the United States93; and on any such appeal only the constitutional question may be considered and determined.94

38. Appeals; nature of. UNDER SECTION 56: From a final judgment rendered upon a trial by the court, or by a referee, or upon the verdict of a jury; in either instance, the appeal may be taken upon questions of law, or upon the facts, or both.

UNDER SECTION 57: From an order made upon no tice at a Special Term, or a Trial Term, or at Chambers, in either of the following cases: 

1. Where the order grants, refuses, continues, or modifies a provisional remedy; or settles, or grants, or refuses an application to resettle a case on appeal, or a bill of exceptions. 

2. Where it grants or refuses a new trial; except that where specific questions of fact arising upon the issues in an action triable by the court, have been tried by a jury,

pursuant to an order for that purpose, an appeal cannot be taken from an order granting or refusing a new trial upon the merits. 

3. Where it involves some part of the merits. 

4. Where it affects a substantial right. 

5. Where, in effect, it determines the action and prevents a judgment from which an appeal might be taken. 

An order made upon a summary application after judgment is deemed to have been made within the meaning of this section.* 

*Sec. 57, City Ct. Act. 

UNDER SECTION 58: 

From an order made upon notice, by a judge out of court in a case where an appeal might have been taken, as prescribed in Section 57, if the order had been made by the court.

56. Appellate Division; composition; nature. The Appellate Division of the Supreme Court is a court of review, 120 created by the Constitution, and is composed of justices of the Supreme Court, designated by the Governor to serve as justices of the Appellate Division,121 and one as the presiding justice thereof.122 No justice of the Appellate Division [of which there are four departments123] may, within the department to which he may be designated to perform the duties of an appellate justice, exercise any of the powers of a justice of the Supreme Court, other than those of a justice out of court, and those pertaining to the Appellate Division, except that he may decide causes or proceedings theretofore submitted, or hear and decide motions submitted by consent of counsel; but any such justice, when not actually engaged in performing the duties of such appellate justice in the department to which he has been designated, may hold any term of the Supreme Court and exercise any of the powers of a justice of such Court in any judicial district in any other department of the state.124 

120 Putnam v. Lincoln Safe Deposit Co., 191 N. Y. 166.

UNDER SECTION 608: From a final judgment ren dered in the Supreme Court upon a trial by a referee, or by the court without a jury, or upon the verdict of a jury, upon questions of law, or upon the facts, or upon both. 

UNDER SECTION 609: From an order in an action, upon notice, made at a special term or trial term of the 

Supreme Court, in either of the following cases: 

1. Where the order grants, refuses, continues or modifies a provisional remedy; or settles, or grants, or refuses an application to resettle a case on appeal or a bill of exceptions.

2. Prac. Act).

4. Where it affects a substantial right.

78. Undertaking; requisites; generally. Rule 25, Rules of Civil Practice: 

"Form and Requisites of Bond or Undertaking.

The following provisions regulate the form and requisites of a bond or undertaking in an action or proceeding, unless otherwise provided by statute or rule: 

1. The principal need not join with the sureties in its execution; 

2. The execution by one surety is sufficient, although the word 'sureties' is used in the statute or rules; 

3. It must be joint and several in form wherever two or more persons execute it; 

4. Except when executed by a fidelity or surety company, it must be accompanied with the affidavit of each surety, subjoined thereto, to the effect that

he is a resident of and a householder or a freeholder within the state and is worth the penalty of the bond or twice the sum specified in the undertaking over all the debts and liabilities which he owes or has incurred and exclusive of property exempt by law from levy and sale under an execution; but when given by a party without a surety it must be accompanied by his affidavit to the same effect;

BILL OF EXCEPTIONS 

96. Bill of exceptions; case; distinctions. "There is no magic in a 'bill of exceptions', but it is so rarely encountered by both bench and bar that its appearance . . . should be especially commended. No simpler method for the review of errors of law on appeal has been devised. It is probably because its object and purpose is so little appreciated that so few make use of its possibilities. 

It should be noted that its fundamental feature is in the statement of the facts. . . . It is provided (Rule 232) that- 'A bill of exceptions shall contain only so much of the evidence as is necessary to present the questions of law which are to be raised on the appeal.'

While in a 'case' all the evidence is to be included by question and answer, together with the rulings and the exceptions of all parties to the record; and finally, like under the old rule, if either case or bill does not conform to the new rule, the reviewing court is empowered to send it back for resettlement.

The two appeal books, thus established, differ vitally in respect of contents. Although in neither Code nor under the Practice Act is reference made to bills of exceptions, except that under the Code of 1848 none was required to be made, the Rules of 

Practice always favored them, and the courts, whenever the question came up, recognized their value and commended their use." (Grobert Realty Co. v. 

Dorf Bros. & Co., 118 Misc. 856; 193 N. Y. Supp. 584.) ""The difference between a case, a bill of exceptions, and a case containing exceptions is that in the

first there is contained all the evidence so as to bring up for review merely the question of the sufficiency of the evidence, while, in a bill of exceptions, only exceptions taken at the trial are embodied, with so much of the evidence as is necessary to explain them, and a case containing exceptions would seem to be a combination of a case and bill of exceptions.'" (Grobert case, supra.) "All that is proper in such an instrument (bill of exceptions) is a concise statement of facts, presenting the points intended to be relied upon as ground of error, or simply so much of the evidence as may appear to be requisite for the same purpose. 

That is all that the rights of the defendant, or a proper administration of the laws, can possibly require. And what is added beyond that only tends to embarrass and confuse the points to be presented, by withdrawing or diverting the attention of the court from their consideration." (Tweed v. Davis, 1 Hun, 252, 253.)

NOTE: There are often cases where the attorneys can, with the approval of the judge who presided at the trial, prepare a statement of facts to be inserted in the case instead of the testimony as provided in the last clause of Rule 230, Rules of Civil Practice. “In many cases where the appellant desires to raise only questions of law, much expense and labor can be saved by presenting the questions in a bill of exceptions instead of in a case." 

Derby v. General Electric Co., 208 App. Div. 524; 204 N. Y. Supp. 224.

100. "Rule 230. Case and bill of exceptions; service; amendment and settlement; time.

Whenever it shall be necessary to make a case or a bill of exceptions, the same shall be made and a copy thereof served on the opposite party within the following time: 

If the trial was before the court or referee, including trials by a jury of one or more specific questions of fact in an action triable by the court, within thirty days after service of a copy of the decision or report and of written notice of the entry of the judgment thereof. 

In the Surrogate's Court, within thirty days after service of a copy and the decree or order and notice of the entry thereof.

If the trial was before a jury, and a motion is made for a new trial and not decided at the time of the trial, within thirty days after notice of the entry of the order on such motion, or within thirty days after service of a copy of the judgment and notice of its entry.

The party served with a case or a bill of exceptions may propose and serve amendments thereto within ten days thereafter. Within four days thereafter either party may serve the opposite party with a notice that the case or bill of exceptions with the proposed amendments will be submitted for settlement, at a time and place to be specified in the notice, to the judge or referee before whom the cause was tried.

Whenever amendments are proposed to a case or bill of exceptions, the party proposing such case or bill of exceptions, before submitting the same to the judge or referee for settlement, shall mark on the several amendments his allowance or disallowance thereof, and also shall mark plainly thereon and on the stenographer's minutes the parts to which the proposed amendments are appli-

cable, together with the number of the amendment. If the party proposing the amendments claim that the case or bill of exceptions should be made to conform to the minutes of the stenographer, at the end of each amendment he must refer to the proper page of such minutes. The judge or referee shall thereupon correct and settle the case or bill of exceptions. Notice of settlement of the proposed case or bill of exceptions shall be not less than four nor more than ten days after the service of such notice. The lines of the proposed case or bill of exceptions shall be so numbered that each copy shall correspond. The court, judge or referee, by order, may allow further time for the doing of any of the acts above provided to be done on such appeals.

Cases where exceptions are ordered to be heard in the first instance by the Appellate Division or where specific questions of fact have been submitted to a jury shall be settled in the same manner. 101. "Rule 231. Failure to make case; effect. 

If the party shall omit to make a case within the time limited in the preceding rule, he shall be deemed to have waived his right thereto; and when a case is made and the parties shall omit within the several times so limited, the one party to propose amendments, and the other to give notice of the settlement thereof before the judge or referee, they shall be deemed, respectively, the former to have agreed to the case as proposed, and the latter to have agreed to the amendments as proposed."

102. "Rule 232. Bill of exceptions; case; resettlement; exhibits.

A bill of exceptions shall contain only so much of the evidence as is necessary to present the questions of law which are to be raised on the appeal. 

A case shall contain all the evidence by question and answer, the rulings of the court and the exceptions of all parties to the record, but shall not contain the opening and summing up or the remarks of counsel unless ordered by the judge or referee.

103. "Rule 233. Extension of time for serving case on appeal or amendments.

No order extending the time to serve a case on appeal or amendments thereto shall be made, except at the close of the trial, unless two days' notice of the application for such an order shall be given to the adverse party." 

104. "Rule 234. Papers constituting record on appeal; service; filing; failure; effect.

In all causes to be heard in the Appellate Division, the papers shall be furnished by the appellant

or the moving party, and in submitted controversies on agreed facts, by the plaintiff.

The party whose duty it is to furnish the papers shall cause a printed copy of the requisite papers to be filed in the office of the clerk of the Appellate Division within twenty days after an appeal has been taken, or the order made for the hearing of a cause therein, or the agreed statement of facts has been filed in the clerk's office pursuant to section 

546 of the Civil Practice Act; but if it shall be necessary to make a case or bill of exceptions after the appeal has been taken or the order made for the hearing in the Appellate Division, the printed papers, including the case as settled and signed by the judge before whom the case was tried, shall be filed within twenty days after the settlement of the case; and the party whose duty it is to furnish the papers shall serve within said twenty days on his adversary three printed copies of such papers. 

Such papers shall consist of a notice of appeal, if an appeal has been taken; a copy of the judgmentroll, or the decree in the court below, and the papers on which it was entered; if no judgment was entered, the pleadings, minutes of trial, and the order sending the case to the Appellate Division or the order so appealed from, or the agreed statement of facts. To these papers shall be attached the case or bill of exceptions if it is to be used in the Appellate Division. All the foregoing papers shall be certified by the proper clerk, or be stipulated by the parties to be true copies of the originals. There shall be prefixed to these papers a statement showing the time of the beginning of the action or proceeding, and of the service of the respective pleadings; the names of the original parties in full; and any change in the parties, if such has taken place. 

There shall be added to them the opinion of the court below, or an affidavit that no opinion was given, or, if given, that a copy could not be procured.

When the same title appears upon any of the papers or pleadings subsequent to the summons, the record may contain the words "(Same Title)" in place of the title thereof. Any verification or jurat by an individual, if in usual form, may be indicated by the words "(Verified-or Sworn toby ***)". Where papers are addressed to all attorneys of record, such fact may be indicated by the words "(Addressed to all attorneys of record)" and the names and addresses of these attorneys need not be printed more than once as part of such papers. In certifying such records on appeal, the county clerk shall accept such statements in lieu of the setting forth of such matters in full.

The foregoing papers shall constitute the record in the Appellate Division. 

The papers in all appeals from orders shall consist of printed copies of the papers, which were used in the court below and are specified in the order, certified by the proper clerk or stipulated by the parties to be true copies of the originals and of the whole thereof. There shall be added to them the opinion of the court below, or an affidavit that no opinion was given, or, if given, that a copy could not be procured. 

They shall be filed with the clerk within fifteen days after the appeal is taken, and, at the same time, the appellant shall serve on his adversary three printed copies thereof.

If the party whose duty it is to file and serve papers under this rule shall fail so to do, the other party may move in the appellate court on any motion day, on three days' notice, to dismiss the appeal or for other relief." 

105. "Rule 235. Records and briefs on appeal; printing; indexing and delivery; form.

The records and briefs and all other papers furnished in the Appellate Division in calendar causes

shall be printed on white writing paper, 10½ inches by 8 inches, and bound on the edge of the greater length, with a margin on the outer edge of the leaf not less than 1½ inches wide. The printed page, exclusive of any marginal note or reference, shall be 7 inches long and 3½ inches wide. The folios, numbering from the commencement to the end of the record, shall be printed on the outer margin of the page. 

The record and briefs in each cause shall be uniform in size and printed in Roman type of 11 point size.

All cases cited in the briefs from the courts of this state shall be cited from the official reports if there reported in full.

The party whose duty it is to furnish the papers shall comply with any rules of that Appellate Division as to the number and time of filing and service of copies of records, printed papers and briefs. 

The record on appeal shall contain an index in the front thereof. The index of the exhibits shall concisely indicate the contents or nature of each exhibit and the pages where it is admitted in evidence and printed in the record. Such index shall also contain a reference to the pages where a motion for a dismissal of the complaint or for the direction of a verdict appears. At the top of each page containing testimony shall be printed the name of the witness whose testimony appears thereon and of the party by whom called, and a statement whether the examination is direct, cross or redirect. Each affidavit or other paper printed on an appeal from an order shall be preceded by a description thereof that must specify on whose behalf it was read and the name of the affiant shall be printed at the top of each page containing an affidavit. 

On an appeal from an order granting, or denying, a motion to strike out parts of a pleading as irrelevant, redundant or scandalous, or to make a

pleading more definite and certain, the portion of the pleading to which the motion is directed must be in italics."

In other words, the defendant Aaron F., maker of the instrument, defends on the ground that he never received from the plaintiff company the loan which was the consideration for the note (fol. 26).

Statement of Facts 

On February 7th, 192, the defendants, Aaron F., the maker, and Joseph H., one of the co-makers and brotherin-law of the maker (fol. 199), called at the Union 

Branch office of the plaintiff company (fols. 78, 247), to make application for the loan of the $1,300 involved in this action (fol. 200). The defendant H. took the initiative in applying for the loan, as he was more familiar with the method of securing a loan from the plaintiff company than the defendant F., by reason of the fact that he had previously indorsed a Jackson Co. note for his cousin, one 

Morris H. (fol. 200). These two defendants saw Mr. M. and Mr. G., manager (fol. 247) and assistant manager (fol. 276) respectively, of the Union 

Branch office of the plaintiff company. The defendant H., upon making known the defendant F.'s desire to borrow $1,300, proceeded to make out the application for the loan (Defendant's Exhibit C), while in the office, in his own handwriting (fol. 201) and from the evidence (fol. 86) it appears that the defendant Aaron F. signed it and left it at the office of the plaintiff company. The two defendants then took away with them the note (Plaintiff's Exhibit 1) on which this action was brought and which had been made out by Mr. G. (fol. 280), to be signed by the maker and co-makers and returned to the plaintiff company, so that the loan might be approved (etc.).

POINT I 

Where one of two innocent parties must suffer by the act of a third the loss must be borne by the one whose act has made it possible. Assuming for the sake of argument, without admitting the truth of the story told by the defendants of what hap pened to the note after F. and H. took it from the offices of the plaintiff the day of the case then falls within the rule of law that where there has been a loss caused by a third party, which must be borne by one of two innocent parties, that party must bear it who by his own act has made the loss possible.

This principle has been applied uniformly in New York and other jurisdictions in equity actions dealing with accountings, real property, mortgages or personal property, and is carried into the field of common law. 

Actions involving real property: 

Mattes v. Frankel, 157 N. Y. 603; 

Multz v. Price, 91 App. Div. 116;

Kantor v. Cohn, 98 Misc. 355. 

The doctrine is well expressed by the New York Court of Appeals in Matter of Dawson, 110 N. Y. 114, at page 118: "When one of two innocent parties must suffer by the wrong of a third party, it is frequently difficult to find an intelligible ground for placing the loss upon the one rather than upon the other; and the difficulty is solved, without any other reason, by holding that that one should bear the loss who put it in the power of the third party to commit the wrong. That rule may be applied here. The plaintiffs not only caused the attachment to be issued to the sheriff, but they procured the order

which compelled McDowell, Pierce & Co. to pay the defendant's moneys to the sheriff."

The doctrine is stated by the U. S. Supreme Court in 

National Safe Deposit Co. v. Hibbs, 229 U. S. 391. The case goes farther, however, and bases its decision upon the rule that a principal should bear the loss incurred by the act of an agent disposing of personal property in the case where he has clothed the agent with apparent authority to do the act and entrusted him with evidence of title and power of sale (etc.).

Judgment should be reversed and a new trial granted.

This section (590) should be carefully read in connection with section 588, relating to the jurisdiction of the Court of Appeals as respects appeals from final judgments and orders, and with section 603, providing that where final judgment is taken at a special term or trial term, or pursuant to the directions of a referee, after the affirmance upon an appeal to the Appellate Division of an interlocutory judgment, on an appeal to the Court of Appeals from the determination of the final judgment, the determination of the Appellate Division affirming the interlocutory judgment, may, at the election of either party, be reviewed thereupon.

Here it is important to observe that where the appeal is taken under section 590, the judgment to which it refers must be rendered after-not pursuant to the affirmance, and that it must involve an interlocutory judgment.

This section provides a quick method from review in the Court of Appeals without the necessity or formality of an intermediate appeal to the Appellate Division. But where the appeal to the Appellate Division is thus avoided and the appeal from the final judgment rendered by the Special Term is taken directly to the Court of Appeals, the review in that court is limited solely to the determination of the Appellate Division on the appeal to it from the interlocutory judgment.

On the other hand, if an appeal from the final judg ment rendered by the Special Term is taken to the Appellate Division (like the appeal from the interlocutory judgment) instead of directly to the Court of Appeals, upon appeal thereafter to the Court of Appeals the scope of review in that tribunal is not then merely limited to a review of the determination of the Appellate Division on the appeal from the interlocutory judgment, but the Court of Appeals may then review both the interlocutory judgment and the final judgment, provided the notice of appeal

states the intention to bring up for review the interlocutory judgment as affirmed by the Appellate Division [Sections 603, 619, Civil Practice Act];314 it is a choice of remedies.815

A greater benefit in the scope of review, is, obviously, derived by not appealing directly from the final judgment rendered by the Special Term to the Court of Appeals.

153. Questions of law; raising; exception; necessity of; raising new questions. The method of raising a question of law is by taking exception to the ruling complained of, but the objection or motion, resulting in the ruling to which the exception is addressed, must be specific and comprehensive to the end that the trial court may have an opportunity to correct the error at the time333; a general objection or motion will not suffice334; this, too, is the rule where exception is taken to a charge.335 But this general rule has no application where no opportunity is afforded to take an exception, as where the court reserves decision on a motion at the close of the trial.336 The safer course to pursue, therefore, is to enter upon the record an understanding that in the event of an adverse ruling an exception thereto is deemed to have been taken. A question not urged below will not be considered, except for the purpose of affirmance.337

156. Reversal; when deemed on question of law; presumption. Upon an appeal to the Court of Appeals from the determination of the Appellate Division, the reversal by the Appellate Division of the determination of the trial court is conclusively presumed to be upon a question of law, and not upon the facts, unless the judgment or order of reversal made by the Appellate Division specifies and refers to the particular question or questions of fact upon which the reversal was made or the new trial was granted; 341 and a reversal by the Appellate Division, on the law, is manifested if its decision of reversal is predicated upon an exception bringing up for review a finding of fact without evidence to support it; a refusal to find a fact established which is adequate in its effect to change the result; the failure of the findings of fact to sustain the conclusions of law, or where an error has been committed in receiving or rejecting evidence ;342 otherwise the reversal is on the facts.

An unprinted calendar of original motions is made for each Monday of a session; only one copy of motion papers is required, except that in motions for reargument and for leave to appeal eighteen copies of the printed briefs are required.

For the convenience of the court, counsel who are to argue cases should print their names at the top of the first page of their points.

RULES 

1 

APPELLANT TO FILE RETURN-Effect oF OMISSION. 

If the appellant shall not cause the proper return to be made and filed with the clerk of this court within the time prescribed by law (Civil Practice Act, § 577), the respondent may, by notice in writing, require such return to be filed within ten days after the

service of the notice, and if the return be not filed in pursuance of such notice, the appellant shall be deemed to have waived the appeal. On an affidavit proving that the appeal was perfected, and the service of such notice, and a certificate of the clerk that no return has been filed, the respondent may enter an order with the clerk dismissing the appeal for want of prosecution, with costs, and the court below may thereupon proceed as though there had been no appeal. (Amended, 1922.) 2 

FURTHER RETURN MAY BE ORDERED. 

If the return made by the clerk of the court below shall be defective, either party may, on an affidavit, specifying the defect, and on notice to the opposite party, apply to one of the judges of this court for an order, that the clerk make a further return without delay. 3 

ATTORNEYS AND GUARDIANS BELOW TO CONTINUE TO ACT. 

The attorneys and guardians ad litem of the respective parties in the court below shall be deemed the attorneys and guardians of the same parties respectively, in this court, until others shall be retained or appointed, and notice thereof shall be served on the adverse party. 4 

APPELLANT TO MAKE A CASE; ITS FORM. 

In all calendar causes a case shall be made by the appellant, which shall consist of a copy of the return, and the reasons of the court below for its judgment, or an affidavit that the same cannot be procured, together with an index to the pleadings, exhibits, depositions and other principal matters. Every opinion in the cause at Special Term, as well as at the Appellate Division of the Supreme Court, relating to the questions involved in the appeal, is included by the foregoing provision. 5 

CASES AND POINTS TO BE PRINTED; MODE OF PRINTING. 

All cases and points, and all other papers furnished to the court in calendar causes, shall be printed on white paper, as pro-

vided by Rule 235 of Civil Practice, and, if bound, the covers shall be of light-colored paper, which can be legibly written upon. 

The folio, numbering from the commencement to the end of the case, shall be printed on the outer margin of the page. Small pica, leaded, or ten point leaded with four to pica leads, is the smallest letter and most compact mode of composition which is allowed. No charge for printing the papers mentioned in this rule shall be allowed as a disbursement in a cause; unless the requirements of the preceding sentence shall be shown, by affidavit, to have been complied with in all papers printed. (Amended 

December 15, 1906; 1922.) 

APPELLANT TO SERVE COPIES of Case-EFFECT OF HIS DEFAULT. 

Within twenty days after the appeal is perfected the appellant shall serve three printed copies of the case on the attorney of the adverse party. If he fail to do so the respondent may, by notice in writing, require the service of such copies within ten days after service of the notice, and if the copies be not served in pursuance of such notice the appellant shall be deemed to have waived the appeal; and on an affidavit proving the default and the service of such notice the respondent may enter an order with the clerk dismissing the appeal for want of prosecution, with costs, and the court below may thereupon proceed as though there had been no appeal. 7 

COPIES OF CASES AND POINTS. 

Within twenty days after the return has been filed the appellant shall file with the clerk eighteen printed copies of the case, and shall at the same time file with the clerk eighteen printed copies, and serve on the attorney or counsel for the respondent three printed copies of the points to be relied on by him, with reference to the authorities to be cited. Within ten days after such service the respondent shall file with the clerk eighteen printed copies and serve on the attorney or counsel for the appellant three printed copies of the points to be relied on by him, with a reference to the authorities to be cited.

If the appellant desires to present points or authorities in reply he shall file with the clerk eighteen printed copies thereof, and serve three printed copies on the attorney or counsel for the

respondent. No supplemental points will be allowed from either side unless especially requested by the court.

No points will be received by the court on argument or submission unless they shall have been filed and served as above provided; except that in appeals under Rule XI, noticed for the first 

Monday of a session, the parties shall file the printed cases, and file and serve or exchange the printed points at least two days before the commencement of the session.

The cases and points filed with the clerk shall be disposed of as follows: One copy shall be furnished to each of the judges; one copy shall be kept by the clerk, with the records of the court; one copy shall be deposited in the State Library; one copy shall be deposited in each branch of the library of the Court of Appeals; one copy shall be deposited in the library of the New York Law Institute; one copy shall be deposited in the Law Library of 

Brooklyn; one copy shall be deposited in the Law Library of the 

Eighth Judicial District; one copy shall be deposited in the Supreme Court Law Library at White Plains, and one copy shall be delivered to the reporter. 8 

STATEMENTS AND DISCUSSION OF FACTS. 

In all causes each party shall briefly state upon his printed points, in a separate form, the leading facts which he deems established, with a reference to the folios where the evidence of such facts may be found. And the court will not hear an extended discussion upon any mere question of fact. 

Every cause shall be deemed to be submitted to such judges as may be absent at the time of the argument, unless objection to such submission by counsel arguing the cause be then made. 9 

CRIMINAL CAUSES. 

Appeals in criminal causes may be brought on for a hearing as preferred causes upon a notice of five days; and it shall be the duty of the clerk to place such causes on the calendar for the day for which they shall be noticed or upon which the cause shall be ordered by the court or stipulated by the parties to be heard.

10 

SUBMISSION AND RESERVATION OF CAUSES. 

Causes will not be received upon submission until reached in the regular call of the calendar. No reservation will be made of any of the first eight causes, unless on account of sickness, or an engagement elsewhere in the actual trial or argument of another cause commenced before the term of this court, or of other inevitable necessity, to be shown by affidavit. Other causes may be reserved upon reasonable cause shown or by stipulation of parties filed with the clerk; but no cause shall be so reserved by stipulation after the same has been placed upon the day calendar. 

Causes reserved for a day certain by stipulation, when in order to be called, have priority among each other according to the time of filing the stipulations with the clerk, and shall follow next in order the undisposed of causes of the calendar for the day previous. Default may be taken in them.

No reserved cause, whether reserved generally or for a particular day, will be called before its number is reached on the regular call of the calendar. (Amended December 15, 1906.) 11 

MOTIONS AND APFEALS FROM ORDERS. 

Motions, appeals from final orders in special proceedings, from interlocutory judgments and from orders in actions and special proceedings, certified to this court by the Appellate Divisions of the Supreme Court, except orders granting a new trial, may be noticed for, and will be heard on, the first Monday of each session of the court, before taking up the general calendar. Notices of argument of appeals within this rule must contain the claim that the appeal is one entitled to be heard under Rule XI of the Court of Appeals.

Motions will be heard orally on the first Monday of a session only; but they may be submitted without oral argument on any 

Monday when the court is in session; provided they are submitted by both sides and the papers are filed with the clerk on or before the preceding Friday. If either party demands an oral argument of a motion noticed for any other than the first Monday of a session, the motion will go over to the first Monday of the succeeding session.

Where notice has been given of a motion, if no one shall appear to oppose, it will be granted as of course. 

If a motion be not made on the day for which it has been noticed, the opposing party will be entitled, on applying to the court at the close of the motions for that day to a rule denying the motion with costs. (Amended December 15, 1906.) 12 

CALL OF CALENDAR. 

Eight causes only will be called on any day, but after such call causes ready on both sides will be heard in their order. Any cause which is regularly called and passed, without postponement by the court for good cause shown at the time of the call, shall be stricken from the calendar.

Causes upon the calendar may be exchanged one for another, as of course, on filing with the clerk a note of the proposed exchange, with the numbers of the causes, signed by the respective attorneys or counsel. Upon all subsequent calendars each of said causes will take the place due to the date of the filing of the return in the other.

In like manner, a cause not upon the calendar in which an appeal to this court has been perfected and the return duly filed with the clerk, may be exchanged, as of course, for another cause upon the calendar, on filing with the clerk a note of the proposed exchange, with the number of the cause on the calendar, and the date of filing return in the cause not upon the calendar, signed by the respective attorneys or counsel, and also a stipulation of the attorneys or counsel in the cause not on the calendar setting down the same for argument in place of the calendar cause when reached, with the same effect as if duly noticed. Upon all subsequent calendars, each of said causes will take the place due to the date of filing the return in the other. (Amended December 15, 1906.) 13 

TIME OF ARGUMENT. 

In the argument of a cause not more than one hour and fifteen minutes shall be occupied by appellant's counsel and not more than one hour by respondent's counsel, except by the express permission of the court.

In the argument of an appeal within Rule XI not more than thirty minutes shall be occupied by the appellant's counsel nor more than twenty-five minutes by the respondent's counsel, unless express permission be given by the court and the cause placed at the foot of the Order Calendar. (Amended December 15, 1906; 

July 19, 1928, in effect Oct. 1, 1928.) 14 

PREFERRED CAUSES. 

No causes are entitled to any preference upon the calendar except such as is given by law or the special order of the court. 

Any party claiming a preference must so state in his notice of argument to the opposite party and to the clerk; and he must also state the ground of such preference, so as to show to which of the preferred classes the cause belongs. 

A preferred cause being once passed loses its preference. 15 

DEFAULTS. 

Judgments of reversal by default will not be allowed. When a cause is called in its order on the calendar, if the appellant fails to appear and furnish the court with the papers required, and argue or submit his cause, judgment of affirmance by default will be ordered on motion of the respondent. If the appellant only appears, he may either argue or submit the cause. 

When any cause shall be regularly called for argument, and no other disposition shall be made thereof, the appeal shall be dismissed, without costs, and an order shall be entered accordingly, which shall be absolute unless upon application made and good cause shown, upon notice to the opposite party within ten days, if the court is in session, and if not, on the first motion day of the next session, the court shall revoke said order and restore said appeal. 16 

REMITTITUR. 

The remittitur shall contain a copy of the judgment of this court and the return made by the clerk below, and shall be sealed with the seal and signed by the clerk of this court.

17 

AFFIRMANCE BY DEFAULT. 

When a judgment or order shall be affirmed by the default of the appellant, the remittitur shall not be sent to the court below, unless this court shall otherwise direct, until ten days after notice of the affirmance shall have been served by the attorney for the respondent on the attorney for the appellant and proof thereof filed with the clerk. Service of the notice shall be proved to the clerk by affidavit, or by the written admission of the attorney on whom it was served. (Amended December 15, 1906.) 18 

ENLARGING TIME REVOKING ORDERS. 

The time prescribed by these rules for doing any act may be enlarged by the court or by any of the judges thereof; and any of the judges may make orders to stay proceedings which, when served with papers and notice of motion, shall stay the proceedings according to the terms of the order. Any order may be revoked or modified by the judge who made it; or, in case of his absence or inability to act, by any of the other judges. 19 

CIVIL CAUSES.

If the respondent is in default in filing and serving the points hereinbefore required to be filed and served, he will not be heard except by special leave of the court. 

SUBMISSION ON POINTS. 

In any case in which the printed copies of the papers upon which an appeal is to be heard shall have been served and filed with the clerk, as required by rule 234 of the Rules of Civil Practice, and the printed copies of the points shall have been served and filed with the clerk, as required by rule VI of the Rules of the Appellate Division, First Department, the parties may, by a written stipulation filed with the clerk, submit the appeal upon the printed papers and points filed as aforesaid. 

MOTIONS For Reargument. 

Motions for reargument must be noticed for the first, second or third motion days after the appeal has been decided. Such motions will only be heard on notice to the adverse party, stating briefly the ground upon which a reargument is asked; and the moving party must submit printed briefs stating concisely the points supposed to have been overlooked or misapprehended by the

court, with proper reference to the particular portion of the record and the authorities relied upon. On such motions counsel will not be heard orally. 9

COURT SITTINGS.

NOTICING AND HEARING OF MOTIONS.

A note of issue, stating the title of the cause, nature of the motion, the day for which it is noticed, the names of the attorneys for the parties and the name of counsel who is to argue or submit the motion must be filed with the clerk before noon of the day before the day for which the motion is noticed. The motion calendar will be called by the clerk at twelve o'clock noon on each motion day, when all motions to be made without oral argument must be submitted. 

Where a motion is to be orally argued, the same will be marked ready on the call by the clerk, and will be heard by the court at one o'clock. On all motions (except for reargument, resettlement of orders, for leave to appeal to the Court of Appeals or to this Court from the Appellate Term), where a notice of appeal has been served, a copy thereof must be attached to the motion papers. (Amended Sept. 22, 1931.) 3 

APPEALS FROM ORDERS HEARD ON MOTION DAYS. 

Appeals from orders will be heard only on motion days. A non-enumerated calendar shall be made up, unless otherwise ordered, for the first day of the term and the second Friday thereafter. Appeals from orders will appear on this calendar. Eight days notice of hearing thereof must be given to the opposite party and a note of issue must be filed as provided in Rule 4.

4 

PERFECTING AND HEARING OF NON-ENUMERATED APPEALS— NOTE OF ISSUE THEREFOR. 

At least 8 days before the day for which a non-enumerated appeal shall have been noticed, the appellant or moving party must file with the clerk a note of issue which must state the day when the notice of appeal was served, and also notice of argument with proof of service thereof on the attorney for the respondent. Such party must also file with the clerk, as required by Rule 234 of the Rules of Civil Practice, a printed copy of the papers upon which the appeal or cause is to be heard, duly authenticated as required by said Rule, together with 17 printed copies of such papers, with proof of service of three printed copies on the attorney for the respondent. The clerk shall place such an appeal on the calendar of non-enumerated appeals. Not later than noon of the day before the day for which such appeal shall have been noticed for argument, the appellant shall file with the clerk 17 printed copies and serve on the opposing counsel 3 printed copies of the points to be relied upon. Before noon of the day for which the appeal shall have been noticed for argument, the respondent shall file with the clerk 17 printed copies and serve on the opposing counsel 3 printed copies of the points to be relied upon. 

If an appellant or other party whose duty it is to file printed copies of the papers upon which the appeal is to be heard shall fail to file the same at least 8 days before the day for which such appeal is noticed, the opposite party may move upon affidavit; giving 3 days notice of motion, that the appeal be dismissed. 5 

ENUMERATED CALENDAR. 

The Clerk will make up a calendar of enumerated causes for each term of the Court. It shall consist of the enumerated causes undisposed of upon the calendar of the preceding term, together with such new causes as shall have been noticed for hearing. 

ENUMERATED CAUSES, SERVING NOTICE OF ARGUMENT AND FILING NOTE OF ISSUE. 

After the printed copies of the papers and points shall have been filed, as required by Rule 9, either party may notice the

cause for argument, for the opening day of the next term, by giving 8 days' notice to the opposite party, and upon filing with the clerk, 8 days before the opening day of the term, such notice of argument, with admission or proof of service thereof, and a note of issue, which shall state the day upon which the notice of appeal was served. The clerk shall place the cause on the calendar for the term noticed, provided points have been filed and served as required by Rule 9 or the time for respondent to file points has elapsed. 

DISMISSAL Of Appeals for Failure to Serve and FILE APPEAL PAPERS OR POINTS. 

When a party whose duty it is to file and serve the printed papers or to file and serve points fails to file or serve the same, as required by these rules, the opposing party may move, upon 3 days' notice, that the appeal be dismissed. 

PREFERRED CAUSES. 

Either party to an appeal entitled by law to a preference may serve with the notice of argument a notice that such preference is claimed. 

ENUMERATED CAUSES; RECORDS AND POINTS THEREOF. 

At the time of filing with the Clerk of this Court, as required by Rule 234 of the Rules of Civil Practice, the original copy of the papers on appeal, duly authenticated, as required by Rules 234 and 235 of the Rules of Civil Practice, the appellant, plaintiff in a controversy submitted under section 546 of the Civil Practice Act, or the moving party in an enumerated cause, shall also file with the clerk 17 printed copies thereof, and serve on the opposing counsel 3 printed copies thereof. The appellant shall file with the 

Clerk within 20 days thereafter 17 printed copies and serve on the opposing counsel 3 printed copies of the points to be relied upon. Within 15 days thereafter, the respondent or defendant

shall file with the Clerk 17 printed copies, and serve on the opposing counsel 3 printed copies, of the points to be relied upon. If the appellant or moving party wishes to present points or authorities in reply, he shall file with the clerk 17 printed copies and serve 3 printed copies thereof on the opposing counsel within 5 days after the receipt of respondent's points. When the papers are filed in July or August the appellant's points must be filed at least 25 days and the respondent's points at least 10 days before the first day of the October term.

Proof of service of the record on appeal, as above provided for, must be filed with the Clerk at the time of filing the original record.

By stipulation of the attorneys or counsel, the times herein provided for filing papers may be modified provided that all such papers be filed before the cause shall be placed on the calendar. 10 

TIME ALLOWED FOR ORAL ARGUMENT. 

In motions and non-enumerated appeals not more than 15 minutes shall be allowed counsel on each side. In enumerated appeals not more than 45 minutes shall be allowed counsel on each side. The time thus allowed shall not be exceeded unless before the beginning of the argument the time shall be extended by the Court upon application therefor. Not more than one counsel shall he heard on each side unless the Court grants permission to the contrary upon application made before the argument is heard. 11 

EXCUSING NEGLECT IN PROSECUTION OF APPEALS. 

When a motion is made to dismiss an appeal for want of prosecution, or other neglect, the moving papers must state briefly the nature of the action or proceeding, the date of issue, and the succeeding steps taken in the action, besides the grounds relied upon on the motion; and the affidavits to oppose such motion, in addition to stating matters excusing the delay, must also set forth concisely the questions of law and fact involved so as to show that the appeal is meritorious.

CRIMINAL APPEALS. 

In all criminal cases the points submitted by appellant and respondent shall contain a statement showing the decision and judgment appealed from; the sentence imposed, if any, and whether the defendant is imprisoned under the sentence, or was imprisoned as therein directed. If such certificate was granted it shall be printed in the points. If the opinion has been reported, reference should be made thereto, if no opinion was filed, the fact should be so stated.

If the appellant appears and the respondent fails to appear, the appellant may either argue or submit his case, but judgment of reversal by default will not be allowed. 

If neither party brings an appeal to a hearing the same shall be passed and placed upon the calendar for the following term, and if the appeal is not then brought to a hearing it will be dismissed unless a continuance is directed for good cause shown. 

If the appellant fails to serve and file the record or papers as provided by the Rules of Civil Practice or procure the filing of the return in accordance with section 161 of the Municipal Court Code and these rules, the respondent may move upon three days' notice to dismiss the appeal or for other relief.

Except as may be otherwise directed all decisions either upon appeals or motions, will be accompanied by a signed order. 4 

RECORDS AND RETURNS ON APPEALS. 

Records on appeal from the City Court of the City of New York shall be made up as required by section 65 of the New York 

City Court Act, section 616 of the Civil Practice Act, and rules 

232, 234 and 235 of the Rules of Civil Practice, and served and filed as prescribed by said rules.

Returns from the Municipal Court shall consist of copies of the papers required by section 161 of the Municipal Court Code, said papers to be furnished by the appellant and certified by the proper clerk. Where exhibits are voluminous and only part of their contents is material, the parties may stipulate or the justice below may upon notice settle a statement respecting the same or the part thereof to be returned upon appeal. 5 

BRIEFS FILED AND Served; Calendar Call; Argument. 

In appeals noticed for argument the appellant shall not later than one week preceding the first day of the term file his briefs with the clerk of the Appellate Term and serve the requisite

number of copies thereof upon the respondent. The respondent shall within five days thereafter file his briefs and serve the requisite number of copies thereof upon the appellant. No other briefs will be received unless requested by the court. Proof of service shall be filed with all briefs. 

Ten copies of the brief shall be filed on appeals from the City Court, and three served, printed in accordance with rule 235 of the Rules of Civil Practice. 

In appeals from the Municipal Court three copies of the brief shall be filed and one served, which brief may be printed or typewritten.

When reference to the testimony is made in briefs the number of the folio in the printed record, if an appeal from the City Court, or the number of the page and the line thereof in the return if an appeal from the Municipal Court, must be noted. 

In the argument of any appeal not more than fifteen minutes shall be occupied by counsel on either side, except by express permission of the court. 

LEAVE TO APPEAL TO APPELLATE TERM. 

Applications to a justice of the Appellate Term for leave to appeal from an order not otherwise appealable must be made upon three days' notice returnable within eight days after denial of an application for such leave by the justice below. The papers upon which such application is made must contain a copy of the pleadings, the order entered in the court below and the papers recited therein, concise statement of the grounds of alleged error, and if there be an opinion a copy thereof. 

REARGUMENT; LEAVE TO APPEAL TO APPELLATE DIVISION 

Motions for reargument and applications for leave to appeal from a determination of the Appellate Term to the Appellate Division under section 61 of the New York City Court Act and section 627 of the Civil Practice Act may be made on five days' notice to the adverse party, and must be returnable within 15 days after entry of the order determining said appeal. Such motion must be based upon an affidavit or a statement setting forth con-

cisely the points claimed to have been overlooked or misapprehended by the court, with proper reference to the authorities relied upon and the reason why such reargument should be granted or appeal allowed. The briefs may be either printed or typewritten. 

Such motions must be submitted without argument. 

An appeal to the Appellate Division from an order granting a new trial will not be allowed unless the appellant files with his notice of application for such leave a stipulation that if the order appealed from be affirmed judgment absolute may be rendered against him. 

MOTIONS GENERALLY. 

Motions may be noticed for any day of the term and must be submitted without argument. Five days' notice of motion must be given, except motions for leave to appeal to the Appellate Term under Rule VI, and to dismiss an appeal under Rule III. 

All motion papers with proof of service thereof and the opposing affidavits must be filed with the clerk of the Appellate Term before twelve o'clock of the return day. If an appeal upon the calendar is affected by a motion the motion papers must be endorsed with the calendar number of such appeal.

NOTES OF ISSUE. 

Notes of issue for the Appellate Division shall be filed twelve days before the commencement of the court at which the case may be noticed. Such notes of issue shall state the date of service of the notice of appeal or if there is no notice of appeal the time when the question to be reviewed arose. 2 

TERMS-MOTIONS. 

Compensation cases and motions in relation thereto shall be noticed and heard only at terms designated therefor, to be known as compensation terms. All other appeals and motions shall be noticed and heard at other designated terms, to be known as the order and general calendar terms. The clerk shall prepare a calendar for each term and cause the same to be printed. (No calendar of such motions is made; they are called for upon opening of court on motion days.) 3 

COMPENSATION CALENDAR. 

At the time of filing with the clerk of this court, as required by Rule 234 of the Rules of Civil Practice, the original copy of the papers on appeal duly certified or stipulated, as provided by said rule, the appellant shall also file with the clerk thirteen (13) printed copies thereof and deliver at the office of the Attorney- General three (3) printed copies thereof. The appellant shall file with the clerk within twenty (20) days thereafter thirteen (13) printed copies and deliver at the office of the Attorney-General three (3) printed copies of the briefs to be relied upon. The appellant shall also serve the printed case and brief upon any other party in interest at or before the time prescribed for service upon Attorney-General.

The Attorney-General shall serve notice of argument and file note of issue within the time prescribed therefor in all cases where a notice of appeal has been duly filed and this rule complied with as to service of records and briefs. No case shall be placed on printed calendar unless records and briefs, as herein provided, are on file with the clerk at least twelve (12) days before opening day of the term for which case has been noticed for argument. 

However, cases may be added to the printed calendar by stipulation filed with the clerk provided records and briefs of both appellant and respondent are filed with or before the filing of stipulation. Upon failure to comply with this rule the aggrieved party to the appeal, upon application made on the first day of a term, may move on five days' notice, personally or by mail, to dismiss the appeal or for other relief. (See "information for Attorneys" on last page of Rules.) 4 

ORDER CALENDAR. 

Appeals from orders other than final orders or orders granting new trials or hearings shall be put upon the order calendar as of the date of service of the notice of appeal. The argument of appeals from orders will be heard upon the first day of an order and general calendar term and the days immediately following until they shall be disposed of. Appeals from orders which are not upon the calendar when printed may, after papers have been filed and served in compliance with Rule 234 of the Rules of Civil Practice, be added to the calendar upon filing a note of issue with the clerk five days before argument and be noticed for argument for any Tuesday of the term upon the usual notice of eight days, or such appeals may be added to order calendar by stipulation, if the printed record and briefs are filed with the clerk with stipulation. 5 

GENERAL Calendar. 

All other cases shall be put upon the general calendar as of the date of service of the notice of appeal or in cases where there is no notice of appeal as of the time when the question to be reviewed arose subject to the provisions of Rule 7.

Cases on general calendar shall be placed upon the day calendar in their regular order, except as otherwise directed by the court. 

The first twenty cases on general calendar shall not be subject to reservation by stipulation without consent of the court upon application made upon opening day of an order and general calendar term and upon showing special reasons therefor. 

Cases other than the first twenty may be stipulated for a day certain. Such cases so stipulated shall be placed upon day calendar for day stipulated or as soon thereafter as they are reached in their regular order. In the event the court shall finish its business earlier than the day for which a case has been stipulated, then such case shall be placed upon day calendar for last day of the term. 

By stipulation cases may be added to general calendar, if all necessary papers are filed with clerk at the time of filing stipulation.

Cases on general calendar may be put over term by stipulation filed with clerk at any time before case is placed on day calendar. 6 

DAY CALENDAR. 

The clerk shall prepare at 4 o'clock p. m. of each day a day calendar for the next day of such number of cases as the court shall direct. Cases not disposed of on any day shall be placed at the head of the day calendar for the next day, except that cases on the order calendar shall be placed at the head of the day calendar.

No case on the general calendar shall be placed on day calendar unless the printed papers and briefs are on file with the clerk, as provided by Rules 12 and 13, respectively. Cases not placed on day calendar because of failure to file papers and briefs shall be passed for the term, and it shall be necessary to file new note of issue to place such case on calendar for the following term. However, such cases so passed may, after filing required printed records and briefs, be stipulated, subject to the approval of the court, at the foot of the calendar to be argued when reached. 7 

PREFERRED CASES. 

A party who desires to have a case heard as a preferred case must in his note of issue state his claim for preference as pro-

vided in Section 141 of the Civil Practice Act, or if an order giving the case a preference has been made under that section, the note of issue must be accompanied with a copy of such order. The clerk, in making up the calendar, shall place such preferred cases at the head of the general calendar, indicating that they are preferred, and the class to which they belong. 

ORIGINAL MOTIONS. 

Original motions in this court may be noticed for the first day of a term, or for the Tuesday of any subsequent week of the term, subject to the provisions of Rule 2. 

CRIMINAL CASES. 

Appeals in criminal cases, brought after making up the order and general calendar, or too late to be placed thereon, may be put upon such calendar at any time and brought to a hearing as preferred cases upon a notice of five days to the adverse party, as provided by Section 535 of the Criminal Code. A note of issue, with a statement of the day on which the case is noticed to be heard, must be filed with the clerk at least three days before such date. The clerk shall place such cases on the calendar for the day for which they are so noticed, or upon which the case shall be ordered by the court, or stipulated by the parties to be heard, provided the printed record and briefs are filed for the hearing or submission of the appeal. 10

BRIEFS FILING AND SERVICE THEREOF-DISPOSITION. 

At least twenty days before a term of the Appellate Division at which a case may be noticed for argument for general calendar, the appellant, or relator, shall serve upon the attorney for the respondent three printed copies of his brief upon which he intends to rely upon the argument, with a reference to all the authorities which he intends to cite to the court. At least eight days before said term the respondent shall serve upon the attorney for the appellant, or relator, three printed copies of his brief with a reference to all the authorities which he intends to cite to the court. 

If the appellant, or relator, desires to present a brief in reply he may serve the same upon the attorney for the respondent at least three days before said term. Service under this rule may be made either personally or by mail, but service by mail shall not extend the time within which the answering brief may be served.

EXHIBITS. 

The appellant shall at the time he files his appeal book, file with the clerk five copies of maps or photographs used as exhibits on the trial, if either party deems such exhibits material or proposes to refer to them in the briefs or on the argument. Such exhibits may be bound in the appeal book or may be contained in a separate cover. Large original maps or photographs may be used on the argument by stipulation of counsel and the consent of the court and if left with the court shall have plainly written thereon the title of the case and the name and address of the attorney leaving the same. It shall be the duty of the respondent to furnish to appellant on demand originals or true copies of such exhibits received in evidence on the trial on the offer of respondent. 16 

REARGUMENT OR LEAVE TO APPEAL. 

Motions for reargument or for leave to appeal to the Court of Appeals will be heard only on notice to the adverse party, stating briefly the ground upon which a reargument is asked, or upon which leave to appeal is desired, and such motions may be submitted upon five copies of printed or typewritten papers and briefs, stating concisely the points supposed to have been overlooked or misapprehended by the court, or the questions of law sought to be reviewed upon appeal to the Court of Appeals, with proper reference to the particular portion of the case and the authorities relied upon, with a copy of the opinion, if any, delivered by the court in deciding the case. Oral argument on such motions will not be heard, and it is unnecessary for counsel to appear on such motions. 

They may be submitted by mailing papers to the clerk. 17 

ORDERS ON STIPULATION OR REMITTITUR FROM COURT OF APPEALS. 

Whenever the parties to an appeal or certiorari proceeding, pending in this court, shall, during a recess of the court, by their

Orders on remittiturs from the court of appeals may be filed and entered by the clerk and certified copy thereof shall be taken by the party presenting such order and filed with the original papers on file in the office of the county clerk or other officer to whom the judgment or order of the court is returnable under section 621 or 1308 of the Civil Practice Act or other provision of law.

Orders of substitution of attorneys for an adult party or corporation on stipulation, signed by the attorney and signed and acknowledged by the party, may be filed and entered by the clerk and certified copy thereof, together with copy of stipulation, shall be taken by the party presenting such order and filed with the original papers on file in the office of the county clerk or other officer to whom the judgment or order of the court is returnable under section 621 or 1308 of the Civil Practice Act or other provision of law. 18 (Omitted; unimportant.) 19

APPELLATE DIVISION, FOURTH DEPARTMENT CALENDAR RULES. 

(Revised June 1, 1922; in effect June 1, 1922.) 1 

NOTE OF ISSUE. 

Notes of issue for the Appellate Division shall be filed eight days before the commencement of the term at which the cause may be noticed. Such notes of issue shall state the date of the service of the notice of appeal, or if there is no notice of appeal, the time when the question to be reviewed arose, and also whether the case is to be placed upon the Order Calendar or the General Calendar. 2 

MAKING OF CALENDAR. 

The Clerk shall prepare a calendar for each term and cause the same to be printed. It shall consist of all cases remaining undisposed of on the preceding calendar, and all other cases in which there shall have been filed with the Clerk at least eight days prior to the opening day of the Term the original printed record and a note of issue. Appeals from orders other than final orders or orders granting new trials or hearings shall be put upon the 

Order Calendar, and all other cases on the General Calendar, as of the date of service of the notice of appeal, or in cases where there is no notice of appeal, as of the time when the question to be reviewed arose, subject to the provisions of the next rule. 3 

PREFERENCES. 

Claims for preference must be stated in the note of issue, as provided in section 141 of the Civil Practice Act; or, if an order giving the appeal a preference has been made under that section,

the note of issue must be accompanied by a copy of the order. 

The Clerk will place preferred cases at the head of the calendar, indicating that they are preferred and the class to which they belong. 

ADDING CASES TO CALENDAR. 

Civil cases may be added to either calendar at any time after the calendar is printed, upon filing with the Clerk note of issue, proof of service of notice of argument, and the printed copies of the record, and will thereafter be subject to all rules governing the calendar. Appeals in criminal cases may be put upon the calendar at any time and given their proper preference, upon ten days' notice to the adverse party, as provided in section 535 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, and upon filing note of issue at least five days prior to the day on which the appeal is to be heard. 5 

FILING AND SERVING BRIEFS. 

In cases on the General Calendar, the party whose duty it is to furnish the printed papers, required by Rule 234 of the Rules of Civil Practice, shall, within fifteen days after the service of such printed papers, file with the clerk sixteen printed copies of the papers and sixteen printed copies of his brief and shall at the same time serve upon the attorney for the adverse party three printed copies of his brief. Within seven days thereafter the other party shall file and serve his brief. If the moving party desires to serve an answering brief he may file and serve same within five days thereafter. In cases on the Order Calendar, the appellant shall file and serve his printed brief with the printed records, and the respondent may file and serve his brief within five days thereafter. No supplemental briefs will be allowed unless requested or permitted by the Court. The Clerk shall deliver one copy of the printed papers and briefs to each Justice, one to the Supreme Court Reporter, and one to the Librarian of the Law Libraries in 

Albany, Buffalo, Rochester and Syracuse,and one to the Clerk of each of the other Departments, to be bound and indexed for reference.

6 

DISMISSAL OF APPEALS. 

After an appeal has been taken to this Court the appeal may be dismissed on motion upon three days' notice for failure of the appellant to make or serve, his proposed case, or to procure the settlement thereof, or to print, file and serve the record, or to file and serve his brief, or for any other unreasonable delay upon his part in prosecuting the appeal. If the respondent fails to file or serve his brief within the time allowed therefor, the appellant may serve upon respondent a written demand that respondent file and serve his brief, and upon filing proof of service of such demand and showing that three days have elapsed since such service, and that respondent has not filed and served his brief, the case will be placed upon the Day Calendar when reached in its regular order, and the respondent shall not be heard upon the argument of the appeal except by leave of the Court. 

APPEALS NOT PROSECUTED TO BE DROPPED. 

After the lapse of six months from the filing of any record on appeal or briefs in any appeal, if neither party has filed a note of issue or moved the case for argument, the Clerk will return by express collect to the attorney filing same, all such printed copies of records and briefs. 

MAKING DAY CALENDAR. 

Any case or appeal may be submitted to the Court without argument upon any day of a term. The Clerk shall prepare at three o'clock of each day, a calendar for the next day, to consist of such number of cases as the Court shall direct, including those undisposed of on the then Day Calendar, which shall be placed at the head of the calendar for the next day. Cases shall be taken from the General Calendar in their order thereon, subject to the provisions of Rule IX. Immediately upon making up the Day 

Calendar, the Clerk will send a telegram collect to each attorney intending to argue such appeals, unless otherwise ordered by the attorneys. The Clerk shall also telegraph the numbers of the cases to such newspapers in the Department as desire to publish the same.

9

CASES PASSED TO BE DROPPED. 

It shall not be necessary to notice cases for the Day Calendar. 

Any case on the calendar may, by stipulation filed with the Clerk at any time before the case is placed upon the Day Calendar, be reserved for a day certain, except that no stipulation will be received reserving any of the first fifty cases on the General Calendar beyond the first week. Cases reserved for a day certain will not be placed upon the Day Calendar prior to such date, but will be placed upon the Day Calendar upon the day for which they are reserved, or as soon thereafter as they are reached in regular order.

The Court may, upon notice, vacate such order, upon proof that the appeal will be diligently prosecuted. 10 

RESERVATIONS. 

No reservation will be made of any of the first eight cases upon the General Calendar unless on account of illness, or an engagement of counsel elsewhere in the actual trial or argument of another case in a court of record, commenced before the term of this Court; or for some other unavoidable cause, to be shown by affidavit. 11 

ARGUMENT AND SUBMISSION. 

Every cause shall be deemed to be submitted to such Justices as may be absent from the Court at the time of the argument, unless objection to such submission by counsel arguing the cause

be then made. Not more than one counsel shall be heard on each side and for not more than thirty minutes each in cases on the 

Order Calendar and not more than one hour each in other cases, except when the Court shall otherwise direct. 12 

EXCHANGE OF CASES. 

Cases ready for argument may be exchanged with the consent of the Court, and will thereafter occupy the position on the calendar of the case for which exchanged, and a preferred case exchanged for one not preferred or set down for a particular day, shall lose its preference. 13 

FORM OF BRIEFS. 

The counsel who argues a case shall endorse on his brief delivered to the Justices, his name and place of residence. The moving party, in addition to the statement required by Rule 234 of the Rules of Civil Practice, shall prefix to his points a brief statement, showing when and in what court or before what officer or tribunal the action or proceeding was commenced, the proceedings subsequent to the first decision and how the case was brought into this Court. If any opinion written in the case has been previously reported, he shall give the citation, but if not reported, shall print a copy of the opinion in the record or brief. When a statute is cited, so much thereof as may be deemed necessary to the decision of the case shall be printed in the brief. 14 

MOTIONS. 

Original motions in this Court may be noticed for the opening of Court on any Tuesday of the term, and will be heard upon typewritten papers. Motions for reargument or for leave to appeal to the Court of Appeals may be submitted on any day of the term, including the days when the Court is in recess, and will be heard upon five copies of typewritten papers and briefs, upon regular notice to the adverse party. Oral argument on such motions will not be heard, and submission thereof may be made by

mailing the papers to the Clerk. The papers must set forth briefly the grounds upon which a reargument is asked, or upon which the leave to appeal is desired, the points supposed to have been overlooked or misapprehended by the Court, or the questions of law sought to be reviewed in the Court of Appeals, with properly formulated proposed questions to be certified where it is necessary that specific questions be certified if leave to appeal is granted. 

Proper reference to the particular portion of the case and the authorities relied upon, with a copy of the opinion, if any, delivered by this Court in deciding the case, must be included in the papers. 15 

REMITTITUR. 

The remittitur to be transmitted pursuant to section 621 of the Civil Practice Act shall contain a copy of the judgment or order of this Court and the original record which has been filed with the Clerk, and shall be sealed with the seal and signed by the Clerk of this Court. 16

Original exhibits left with the Court shall have plainly written thereon the title of the cause, its calendar number, and the name and address of the attorney leaving the same, and must be called for within one month after decision in the case is rendered. 17 (Omitted; unimportant.)
