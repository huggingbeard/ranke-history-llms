CHAPTER 1 FOREWORD 

The American Government, like all sound governments, is based upon a careful analysis of the political principles and governmental experiments of the past. Only those ideas which safeguard the rights of all and which are otherwise peculiarly adaptable to our lives as a people are embodied in our Constitution and laws. It represents the accumulated political wisdom of the ages and not the weird untried economic theory of some particular individual, group, or class. Most of our basic principles of government have been proven by the process of trial and error. Our government, taken as a whole, represents the will of the American people as expressed through the ballot, and provides for its own growth and development by perfectly peaceful and legal methods.

It is because of these very facts that our government has become the special target for the calumny of those who would destroy it for the benefit of the Moscow dictatorship. 

American citizens who might be inclined to accept certain features of Communism on the advice of the soap-box orator or the liberal lecturer would do well to ascertain the motive behind such people and the organizations by which they are supported. 

One should become familiar with the entire Communist program before accepting any part of it. Otherwise many good men and women may become entangled in the Communist movement before they are aware of it and may soon find themselves so bound by Party ties and discipline that there is no retreat left open to them.

The elements which have composed the forces of anarchy and revolution from time immemorial are the same elements that gathered around the theory of Karl Marx and eventually blossomed forth as the Communist Party. They seem to have found their most fertile soil in Russia during the last years of the empire of the Czar. Although the leaders of the movement in Russia were imprisoned and exiled, many of them escaped into other countries and proceeded to formulate plans, not only for the overthrow of the old order in Russia, but for the revolutionary overthrow by force and violence of all other governments and their formation into a world union of soviet republics. 

The World War gave this unseen force its opportunity. Its plans were not mature until the Kerensky (coalition) government came into power. At this time Mr. Lenin was in exile in 

Switzerland. The Kerensky regime had not adopted Communism and was continuing its part in the World War. Through connivance with the German General Staff, Lenin and a group of his associates were smuggled into Russia for the sole purpose of destroying the Kerensky Government and eliminating Russia 1

CHAPTER 2 STRUCTURE OF THE COMMUNIST PARTY 

In order to have a fair understanding of the structure of the Communist Party and the agencies through which its influence reaches into every American home, a brief reference to the world organization is necessary. The Party is world-wide and permeates every strata of human society, its influence being felt in every activity. It has well established branches or sections in every country.

The supreme authority over all sections of the Party is vested in the Communist International with headquarters in Moscow. The Communist International is historically referred to as the Third International. It meets every two years in a world congress with representation from all sections of the Party. 

At this meeting it elects or re-elects its own Executive Committee which meets every six months. The Executive Committee in turn elects a smaller group known as the Presidium which meets every two weeks.

The members of the Executive Committee and the Presidium are men who have been thoroughly tested and who can be depended upon to give the desired interpretation to, and carry out the policies of the International during the time that intervenes between meetings.

There are three elements or factors concerned in the composition of the Communist International; namely, the Communist 

Party of the †U.S.S.R., the Soviet Government, and the International itself. The Communist Party of the U.S.S.R., because of the overwhelming majority of its representation in all these groups, dominates the other two. Through a system of interlocking membership on the part of its Political Bureau it has undisputed control over both the Soviet Government and the 

Communist International. To prove this statement a Communist 

Party document (Left Communism, page 29), written by Lenin himself, says: "Not a single important political or organizing question is decided by any state institution in our Republic without the guiding instructions of the Central Committee of the Party." 

Mr. Stalin, like Lenin before him, holds his power, not through any governmental position in the Soviet Union, but by reason of the fact that he is the recognized head of the Communist Party of the U.S.S.R. 

The Communist Party of the United States, with Head quarters in New York City, is the American Section of the Communist International. Its administrative set-up is somewhat similar to that of the International. It is headed by William Z.

There are hundreds of organizations, groups, and committees scattered all over the United States and all doing Communist work under the supervision and direction of the Communist Party. Many of these groups are purely Communistic, while others are controlled through what is known as a Party Fraction.

A fraction is a group of Communist members of a non-Communist organization or one that does not wish to be publicly known as Communistic.

The following are a few of the best known and most active of the subsidiary organizations each of which is controlled by the Communist Party. At times when the Communist Party itself is being suppressed and among people who do not publicly wish to be identified with Communism, all the work of the Party is carried on through some one or more of these groups. 

THE YOUNG COMMUNIST LEAGUE is the American section of the Young Communist International at Moscow and exists for the purpose of winning our young people of high school age and up, to the Communist cause. Since a large percentage of American Communists are aliens and have a very poor command of the English language, it is one of the most cherished objectives of the Communist Party to enroll as many bright, intelligent,

English-speaking American youths as possible in the ranks of the 

Party. Members of the Young Communist League carry on work among the young people that corresponds with the work of adults in the Party itself. They all either develop into finished Communists or prove a failure and are dropped or expelled.

THE YOUNG PIONEERS OF AMERICA is organized among chil dren of grammar school age. It corresponds with our Boy Scout organization but is diametrically opposed to it in principle. These children are taught just as much of the Communist doctrine as their juvenile minds are capable of receiving. One of the slogans taught to these children is "Smash the Boy Scouts."

THE INTERNATIONAL LABOR DEFENSE Conducts the legal defense for such members of the Party as come in contact with the law and are charged with crime. To them, the arrest of any comrade is always a frame-up regardless of his guilt or innocence and no matter what the offense may be. It issues instructions to Party members as to what to do and how to act when arrested. 

In these instructions the comrades are told never to give any information to the arresting officer, to carry the class struggle into the courts, and even though the law makes what they have done a crime, to always plead Not Guilty. When one or more comrades are arrested for crime and have to stand trial, others, not concerned with the case, are instructed by the I.L.D. to stage mass demonstrations of protest, to pack the court room and bring mass Pressure to bear for the unconditional release of the prisoners, to write threatening letters and protests to the judge and other officials, and to do many other stunts which they think will have a tendency to intimidate the courts of justice. These tactics are carried out, not under the name of the Party but usually by socalled workers' groups that are spurred to action by the Party. 

The International Labor Defense prepares the way for speakers and propagandists, organizes defense committees and collects funds for so-called class-war prisoners. It takes up the cases of non-Communists whenever it is thought that the reaction will be of benefit to the Party; notably the Scottsboro case. It also sponsors an organization known as the Young Defenders, composed of young people whose interest they are desirous of holding. 

THE FRIENDS OF THE SOVIET UNION is an organization whose function it is to propagandize conditions in Soviet Russia as the workers' paradise and always keep a favorable picture of the Communist Government before the people of America. Nearly every favorable report that comes out of Russia is written by some member of this group or other sympathizer. 

THE LEAGUE OF STRUGGLE FOR NEGRO RIGHTS exists for the purpose of enrolling our colored population in the Communist revolutionary movement. They promise the negroes self-determination and an independent black republic in the South. They make them believe that only under a Communist government will they be granted full social, political, and racial equality, including intermarriage. To encourage the colored people they arrange inter-racial social affairs in which white men Communists are instructed to dance with colored women and white women Communists are instructed to dance with colored men. 

THE WORKERS EX-SERVICE MEN'S LEAGUE is composed of men who have, or claim to have had, some military experience.

THE NATIONAL STUDENT LEAGUE is a Communist college and university student organization which has spread into many of our higher institutions of learning, the students often being aided and abetted by professors and instructors. Its main objective is to obtain recruits for the revolutionary struggle and to oppose all military training in institutions of learning. It is through the National Student League that the Communist Party sponsors student strikes against war and preparedness.

THE AMERICAN CIVIL LIBERTIES UNION defends the acts and paves the way for Communists in their advocacy of force and violence as a means of overthrowing our government and replacing it with a Communist Dictatorship. The kind of civil liberties which it justifies are the right of a citizen or alien to advocate assassination, sabotage, or any other form of violence, so long as it is done to further the cause of the Communist Party or other radical group. In its administrative set-up are always found a number of active Communist workers and fully 90% of the energy of the organization is devoted to work that fits into the Communist program.

THE TRADE UNION UNITY LEAGUE, which was organized in

It organized Industrial Unions fashioned somewhat after the old 

I.W.W. Unions. 

For a period of years it maintained unions in the transportation, lumber, agriculture, textile, mining, steel, auto, rubber, furniture and other industries.

Now most of the functions of the Trade Union Unity League have been transferred to the Communist Party fractions in the various trade unions of the American Federation of Labor. 

THE UNITED FRONT MOVEMENT is promoted by Communists to include both Party and non-Party organizations whenever it is thought that two or more such groups can find common cause for agitation.

Other Communist subsidiary groups include the JOHN REED

Every Communist district and every section also has its literature agent and its educational director. Nearly every local section of the Party has its so-called Workers Schools where functionaries and members are trained in revolutionary tactics. 

Worker films, which are propaganda moving pictures, are shown wherever local resources justify the expense.

The application for membership card of the Communist Party bears the following pledge which every applicant must sign: "The undersigned declares his adherence to the program and statutes of the Communist International and the Communist Party of U.S.A., and agrees to submit to the discipline of the

Considering that every official document of the Communist Party is a part of its program, the following are a few of the things included in this pledge. In these excerpts the word proletariat is understood to be the people who live by means of their labor power only, and the word bourgeois is taken to mean all modern capitalists and all who at any time employ wage labor. 

The "Communist Manifesto" is the basis or foundation upon which is constructed most of the teachings of the party and its affiliated organizations-it is the Communist bible. 

On page 31 it advocates, ". . . formation of the proletariat into a class, overthrow of the bourgeois supremacy, conquest of political power by the proletariat."

In speaking of property relation it says: "In this sense the theory of the Communists may be summed up in a single sentence: 'Abolition of private property,' and 'In one word, you reproach us with intending to do away with your property. Precisely so that is just what we intend"."

The Manifesto also advocates: Abolition of the family-our educational system-and of all rights of inheritance. 

It declares that the foundation of the family is based upon "capital, on private gain" and on page 37: "The bourgeois clap-trap about the family and education, about the hallowed co-relation of parent and child, becomes all the more disgusting, the more, by the action of Modern Industry, all family ties among the proletarians are torn asunder and their children transformed into simple articles of commerce and instruments of labor."

On page 40 is written: "There are, besides, eternal truthssuch as Freedom, Justice, etc., that are common to all states of society; but Communism abolishes eternal truths, it abolishes all religion and all morality, instead of constituting them on a new basis; it therefore acts in contradiction to all past historical experience." 

The Manifesto closes with the following statements on page 58: "In short, the Communists everywhere support every revolutionary movement against the existing social and political order of things."

"The Communists disdain to conceal their views and aims. They openly declare that their ends can be attained only by the forcible overthrow of all existing conditions. Let the ruling class tremble at a Communist revolution." "The State and Revolution," by Lenin, is another party publication. It consists of an analysis and criticism of the theories of Marx and Engles, and members and sympathizers are urged to study it. It is a text book for Communist Party schools. They should be remembered not only by those who are interested in law enforcement but also by those who are interested in the preservation of American institutions and ideals. There is volume after volume of similar doctrine, bolstered up by endless argument and historical citations; material that would take the average man months to read and analyze.

One may wonder how they expect to put over such a program of violence and hatred in a country like this. 

In discussing the American people with a group of his agents, Nicolai Lenin once directed those agents: "Destroy first their ideology."

This command is being carried out in a thousand different ways today all over the United States. The average person can see it in the course of a single day's activity: remarks and statements that are calculated to destroy faith in our government and its institutions and its duly elected public officials, whether they deserve it or not; the cultivation of economic jealousies and hatreds, pitting one strata of society against another or one organized group against another; ridicule of all moral and religious standards and of the family life, making the child believe that what it learned at its mother's knee is nothing but a lot of capitalistic fairy stories; sneers at every patriotic impulse that dares to make itself manifest; endeavoring to make patriotism seem old-fashioned and unpopular; hundreds of publications consisting of everything from a one-sheet daily bulletin to a sixty-page monthly magazine and being read by 500,000 people daily; also hundreds of paid agents touring the country, especially the large industrial centers, teaching these self-same doctrines under a guise and in a manner best suited to their audiences-destroying our ideology. 

The Communist's organized plan of propaganda includes the teaching of atheism, immorality, class-hatred, pacifism of a type that is designed to make the revolution easy, and the promotion of industrial strife and turmoil between employer and employee at every opportunity and under the slightest pretext. 14

They say to the unemployed and other unfortunate people:

CHAPTER MISCELLANEOUS THE THEORY 

Pure Communism, the ultimate goal of the Communists of the world, is described as a classless and lawless society in which there will be no state power (government). The theory has never been proven and cannot be proven until such time as all opposition is wiped out and it can be given a universal, world-wide test. It is the utopian dream of its active adherents all over the world. The theory also presents a condition of society in which every one will be equal, with the slogan, "From each according to his ability; to each according to his needs." Humanity at large has been striving for ages to elevate all men to its highest standards but the Communist theory reverses this idea, and instead of elevating all humanity to its highest standard of living must of necessity pull every one down to its lowest standards; a condition in which profitable industry or profitable labor would be a crime.

Present-day society recognizes the equal rights of all, but does not prohibit one man in any strata of life from rising to a higher one if he has energy and mental equipment to do so. He is permitted to reap the rewards of his own industry and his own sacrifices. Communism would not allow this.

CAPITALISM 

One of the pledges which enabled the Communist Party to seize political power in Russia was that capitalism would be 45

What happened was that the Communist politicians immediately and conveniently forgot this promise and the workers became the victims of a state capitalism that is so greedy and grasping that it would not be tolerated anywhere except in Russia. 

Communist capitalism in Russia not only owns the means of production which it promised the workers, but owns his living quarters, the stores from which he must secure his food and clothing, and the schools in which he is taught to endure his miserable lot in the name of Communism. He is paid a wage that is about sufficient to pay back to the government what is charged him for the barest kind of a living. All the rest of the product of his toil goes into capital which is used to build industrial plants that are permitted to go to ruin, transportation and communication systems that will not work, and a war chest for communizing the rest of the world. The worker who produces this surplus capital has nothing whatever to say about its management. If he resists its mandates he is put at forced labor. The 

Russian worker must endure a system of state capitalism that embraces all the evils of private capitalism without any of its benefits.

When American Communists and sympathizers try to make it appear that state capitalism in Russia is only a temporary expedient made necessary during the period of transition from capitalism to Communism, their sincerity may well be questioned. No one else is fool enough even to think that the Communist group now in control of the political power and the rapidly increasing capital of the country will ever surrender it peacefully.

FINANCES 

A recent financial statement issued by the national office of the International Labor Defense covering the Scottsboro case from April 11, 1931, to August 31, 1934, shows receipts amounting to $61,825.23 and expenditures amounting to $64,351.75.

This means that over $15,000 per year has been collected for the Scottsboro committee only.

When we consider that there are several hundred Communist groups corresponding to the Scottsboro committee and all out after funds from time to time, we can realize the enormous amount of money that is being collected from sympathetic but poorly informed people. 46

MISCELLANEOUS THERE IS NO MIDDLE COURSE 

For Communists, pinks, liberals, and other admirers and defenders of the Soviet System to claim that they are representative of true Americanism is just about as logical as the idea of riding two horses that are going in opposite directions. It simply cannot be done.

A lot of these same self-righteous people condemn law-enforcing officers for doing their sworn duty when such duty interferes with the plans of the reds. They condemn the farmer and the industrialist for defending his home and property against Communist direct action and accuse him of every conceivable misdeed but they never speak a word against the agitator who causes all the trouble. They conjure up the imaginary monster of Fascism which they describe as the weapon of the rulers of finance and industry aimed at the suppression of the struggle of labor and the masses of the population for better conditions. The claim of trying to better conditions is one that they always try to hide behind in carrying out Communist plans. 

They assume that, whatever they do, they are always right and that it is a terrible crime for anyone to oppose them in any way. They well know, but hate to acknowledge, that the Fascist movement in Italy was nothing but a nationalized vigilante movement which came into existence only to save the country from Communism.

They oppose Criminal Syndicalism Laws, which prohibit: "The doctrine which advocates crime, sabotage, or any unlawful acts or methods as a means of accomplishing or effecting industrial or political change or revolution." Wherever such laws are in effect there is always an organized effort to repeal them, and under the pretense that such laws act to curb freedom of speech, many people and many newspapers are induced to work for their repeal. 

The only thing that can be said for people who condemn all anti-Communist activities and all anti-Communist laws is that, if they are sincere in their opposition, they must believe in the doctrines which are under attack. There is no middle course. 47

CHAPTER 10 CONCLUSION 

With this brief picture of the Communist menace, as it relates to American life, before us, the question, "What are we going to do about it?" suggests itself. A desire for safety is a natural trait of all living things and constitutes an essential condition of national survival. Any people or any government that disregards danger signals may have an interesting but short career. This has been written with the intention of giving the reader a brief understanding of the evils of Communism, and the unspeakable consequences that would follow its being forced upon us. 

When a group of people, who are here largely by sufferance, stand on our constitutional rights for their own purposes and at the same time hold themselves exempt from any constitutional obligation, the voice of prophecy is not required to foretell the fate that awaits us, once we are within their power. When men can travel from coast to coast of our country condemning everything in sight and never uttering a word of praise, their thoughts must be sinister indeed.

Imported violence and revolution are knocking at our doors. 

Not spontaneously, but planned and backed by a well organized foreign group which includes one of the world's largest governments.

Communism does not seek to build up and improve, but to break down and destroy.

It conflicts at every point with man's duty to God, to his country, to his neighbor and to himself.

Instead of peace and harmony, it seeks to create endless strife between employer and employee. It would enlist the American working man under a foreign banner to fight its battles. It capitalizes upon the economic depression, upon personal misfortune, and upon every unpleasant incident to deceive people into thinking it is a cure-all for their ills. 

Seemingly inspired by a jealous hatred of those whose efforts have carried them up the ladder of human success, the fanatical, paid Communist will stoop to the lowest degradations of hell in his efforts to destroy a system which permits individual endeavor to reap its rewards.

Consider what Communism offers your boys and girls. Picture to yourself a group of normal American school children, at study or at wholesome play. You see thousands of happy, childish faces glowing with love of home and country; life's ambition already making itself manifest in their actions and the music of their laughter. On the other hand, picture a state group of the nationalized children of Soviet Russia. They have frightened expressions upon their faces and do not know their own parents, 48

CONCLUSION 

and have nothing to look forward to except the satisfaction of their animal appetites. 

To sovietize our American children is but one number of the red program. The picture does not appeal to you, does it? 

Overdrawn? Not in the least.

No one claims that our officials are always free from error, or that conditions are always ideal, but one thing is certain, and that is that the political tendency of America is to improve and advance in a perfectly legal and peaceful manner; not to retreat to the jungle and start anew.

The American people are patient, tolerant, and long suffering; all noble qualities. But once they are thoroughly aroused to the menace of Communism in all its hideous phases, the old pioneer spirit of frontier days will again assert itself and this monstrous thing will be driven from our midst.

To recognize these sinister, destructive forces, and combat them with a faith that cannot be shaken, is the solemn duty of every liberty-loving American who hopes to see the Republic endure. 49

The one outstanding feature of the system, which America could adopt to good purpose, is the Soviet method of dealing with alien agitators and domestic advocates of violent revolution. This would be hailed by the same pinks and liberals as Fascism.
