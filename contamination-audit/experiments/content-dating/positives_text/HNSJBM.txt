Since every artist spends his best efforts upon his canvas with the hope of immortalizing his creation, shall we not give God, the Immortal and Eternal Artist, the same credit in creating man immortal in his own image and after his own likeness?

Can an artist perpetuate his work without his canvas which gives entity to his skill? 

Can art exist for him without his canvas or paints?

Can God perpetuate his work, his image and likeness, without the body of man, which gives entity and animation to his unfathomable skill? Since the image and likeness of 

God was created with a body (Lu. 24:38-44) is an image and likeness of God possible without a body? Since the image and likeness of God include the body, why is not that body immortal, as both are made of the same material and in the same likeness? *But for the truth in Matthew 10:19, 20 and 

Mark 13:11, I should never have been able to write "The Forgotten Purpose." 3

To the artist, the canvas and the paint are an integral part of the creation, which he has animated. Without them his idea re mains formless. His skill is unexpressed, hi talent unused. Life becomes inarticulate.

The body being a microcosm of the ma crocosm, is the canvas of the infinite artist the Eternal One, the Father. Remove the body, you eliminate the image and likeness of God. Without the body, we know nothing of life; nothing of our source; nothing of our destiny and nothing of reality. Without the body, we have no more being than the artist can have in a picture without canvas paints. Without canvas or paints, the artist's ideas of beauty, harmony and color remain mere abstractions. They exist in his mind, but not as reality. Is not the very or existence of that mind made known to us through the body, the canvas?

The canvas then is the medium which makes possible the expression of the com prehensive composition of the intelligence. knowledge, wisdom and understanding of the artist. It is a part of his creation. 

What beside the body, has the Infinite

Artist, God, through which to reveal his immortal and unerring skill in investing in our very life and being, his own nature and entity? Man's body, therefore, is the divine medium, manifest as a canvas, ever-changing as the unfoldment of the being evolves toward his destiny-Godhood. 

If canvas and paints are an indispensable part of art, why then is not the human body an indispensable part of the divine plan of creation? Why is that plan not so perpetual to the nature and the substance of that creation as it is to the Pattern, The Creator (Jno. 14:20)?

What do God and man gain when the human body, so fearfully and wonderfully made (Psa. 139:14) is swallowed, in death?

Let us see how the Holy Bible answers this stupendous question. 

CHAPTER II 

RELIGIONS, creeds, dogmas and cults have described the status of man variously as mortal, reflection, channel, avenue, vehicle and idea. The Holy Bible declares plainly that these appellations are untrue (Gen. 1:26, 27; Jno. 14:9, 20; Jno. 15:5, etc.). 

Jesus' statement, "I am the vine, ye are the branches," positively disproves all these claims. Let us analyze that statement. 

Whatever substance constitutes the vine, constitutes the branches, both by name and nature. The same force, power or principle, that sustains, maintains, constitutes and perpetuates the vine, during the period of time allotted to its specie, simultaneously constitutes, sustains, maintains and perpetuates the branches, because they are indissolubly connected with each other. Therefore, the nature of the substance and the life of the one constitutes that of the other. 

All life and substance in the branches are derived from the vine, and constantly supplied by it. How could it be possible for the substance and the life in the branches to behave in a manner diametrically opposite to the life and substance in the vine, which are their source of supply? 

How then could the life and the substance of God (the Vine) immortal manifest in

man, his image and likeness, (the branches) as mortal? Could water poured into a rubber glove differ in quality in the fingers or other portions of the hand? The water occupying the fingers (the branches) is constituted of two hydrogen and one oxygen.

The water in the remainder of the glove yields the same test. By what reason, logic, science or occultism can we deduce that man made in the image and likeness of God, is mortal?

All religions agree that God (the Vine) is immortal.

They all agree that the allotted time of 

Life of God is eternity. 

In saying, "I am the vine and ye are the branches," Jesus stated that this vine was connected with the branches.

The branches, thus being indissolubly connected with the vine, can have no less, or different span of life.

All religions must concede then, that man (the branches) is not mortal. He can not be a mortal and still be considered by the religions as the image and likeness of God.

If we accept the statement that God created man in his image and likeness, then the nature of man must tally with the nature of God. Man is immortal. 

Again, man has been called "reflection."

Take your dictionary and study this word. 

You will see how superficial it appears as a description of man. Take a looking glass and let it reflect the sunlight elsewhere. 

Now analyze that reflected light, and compare it to the true sunlight. Stand in front of the mirror and compare yourself in substance, in essence of life, in blood, bone, frame, flesh and all their marvelous array, with the personal appearance you see in the glass.

Reflection is a by-product. A by-product is not the image and likeness of the product producing that by-product. Reflection pre- 

C supposes the preexistence of a substance reflectable, but foreign to its nature. It also 

L presupposes the preexistence of a medium in which or by which this reflection may be made possible. It further presupposes that without this medium, no reflection is possible. re le If there were no sun, there could be no moonlight. The moonlight is a reflection.

It is not the image and likeness of the sun, and its innately radiating light, heat and power. A child knows this. The substance of the moon, as far as we know, is not the substance of the sun. If it were, it would be another sun, instead of a moon. The image and likeness of the sun would have to be another sun. The image and likeness of the sunlight must be another sunlight, and not a moonlight. In this case no reflec- " tion could be possible.

If man is reflection, from where is he reflected? What is it that reflects him? What is this thing which absorbs reality, as the moon absorbs the sunlight, and then reflects it in the form of man? This reflection called man, is a foreign matter, a secondary product, as the moonlight compared with the sunlight. It lacks the substance, the life, the powers and the possibilities of the original which is reflected. 

Who created the reflector? Who placed

God at the mercy of that thing affording a faint vestige of reality thus reflected?

If man is a reflection, he reflects the capacity of the reflector, only, to behold a semblance of reality, as the moon does the sunlight, or the mirror does the human form. A semblance of reality, certainly is

not the image and likeness of reality. Therefore, man is not the image and likeness of God, if he is a reflection, but the image and likeness of the capacity of the thing which beholds God. This is the sum and substance of reflection. 

Of what value is the moonlight as compared to the sunlight? Of what value is your image in the mirror, compared to yourself? Both are reflections.

God is so complete, so inclusive that he has not so much as a shadow between himself and his truth. Man's imagination has projected many shadows between himself and his true entity-that is between his present knowledge of himself and his true being. In his zeal and painstaking effort to classify himself with these shadows of his imagination, he has forgotten the simple fact that the image and likeness of God is a replica of God. His efforts to ally himself with God are replete with bliss and blessings. On the other hand, sorrow, suffering, disappointment, want and woe are the wellearned portions of reflection. 

Again man has been called, channel, avenue.

A channel or avenue is that through which something passes. A channel or avenue presupposes the coexistence or the preexistence of the thing passing through. It also implies that the thing passing through the medium might not be available were it not for that instrumentality.

This is true in human affairs. The proof? 

Try to use your telephone when it is out of order, and see how helpless you feel! 

If we consider man a channel or avenue, in his relationship with God, then we make 

God subject to man's whims and caprices.

We admit, then, that the very life and being of God depend upon his channel, man. This

admission is a positive declaration that the very value and helpfulness of God depend upon his channel, man. This is saying that 

I God is at the mercy of his channel, man.

Such admissions we dispute. Every such assertion is baseless. 

God is all. God is everywhere. Nothing, then, visible or invisible, can negate God or hide him from those who seek him within themselves. This is God's omnipresence. 

God is in his image and likeness. God is his own image and likeness.

If man is a channel of God, then God's will is subservient to that channel, just as the channel is subservient to the will of man controlling it. If man is a channel of 

God, then we have made God's will of third grade importance. Let us look at this graphically.

First importance, the will of man controlling the channel.

Second importance, the will of the channel, manifest in the condition of the channel. 

Third importance, the will of the thing passing through the channel which in this case is the will of God. 

To us God comes first. Man does not need a medium to contact God. God does not need a medium, a channel, through which to reveal himself to himself, his image and likeness. God is in man.

The world does not need a channel or a medium through which to reach God, to contact God. "For by him were all things created, that are in heaven and that are in earth, visible and invisible...and by him all things consist" (Col. 1:16, 17). All that the world needs is to wake up and find itself, its true self, in itself. 

Certainly "God is closer than hands and feet," as Lord Tennyson put it so aptly.

God's immensity cannot be circumscribed in a channel.

God is so complete, so all inclusive, so all pervading and so intervening that no place could be found to set up a channel for his Goodness. His goodness is all in his presence. His presence is all, as the Holy Bible points out.

Again man is said to be a vehicle. A vehicle is a thing used as an instrument of conveyance or communication. An automobile is a good example of a vehicle. Suppose we take an automobile for an example. It is subject to the following: 

1. The owner and the operator who controls the running of the car. 

2. Forces that are outside and beyond his control: a. The quality of the gasoline. b. The durability of the tires and tubes. c. The quality of the oil. d. The water supply. The air supply for the tires. e. Durability of the parts. f. The nature of the roads. g. Weather conditions. h. The continuity of the parts to function in harmony.

A person knowing anything about a car cannot underestimate the forces mentioned under the second point. They are independent of the will of the owner of the car. 

If man is a vehicle in his relationship with God, God then is subject to forces beyond his control, since in this relationship, God stands as the owner and the operator of the car. We know no power supersedes God..

Yet, man as a vehicle, makes such a power inevitable. God becomes subservient rather than omnipotent.

And again man has been called, an idea.

A concrete idea is the image and likeness of that mental image or picture. 

It is then the substantialization of a thought and not the THINKER.

There is no difference between God and man, Genesis points out (1:26, 27). It is specific in this declaration. It does not say,

God made man in the image and likeness of his thought, his idea of man, but of himself.

Nothing precedes God. If God is an idea he must be somebody's idea, and man created by God becomes a third grade product, rather than of the first magnitude which he holds in the reality of creation. 

An idea expresses its nature only when under control. The violin is an idea of man, which expresses a certain type of music.

It is articulate only when under control of a master player. If we admit man as an idea then we go on record that God expresses himself only when under control of a power higher than himself. This is just as logical as to say, "Man is an idea."

HE idea of the second coming of Jesus to which we already have referred originates from the statements of Jesus reported in Matthew 24 and other gospels. Let us analyze these statements of the master in the light of reason, logic and common sense. “Tell us,” asked the disciples, “what shall be the sign of thy coming?"

This was a direct question, specific in nature. Jesus did not commit himself in his reply recorded in verses 27 and 30. He did not give a direct answer which it would have been natural to do were he himself to return to this earth. He did not say, "You will see me coming" or "it will be likewise my coming." Here are four specific reasons for the belief that Jesus is not coming again: 

1. Had he been coming again he would have been definite in his reply since he was never a policy man and had nothing to hide. 

2. If he were to return to the earth again what was the object of his withdrawal from the world for 1900 years? His presence would have electrified the world with the dynamics of his gospel, as he would have been a living example of the resurrection of the dead. He would have left his believers under one directing head. Under the leadership of the great Master his teaching never would have been lost in the flimsy of theological contentions. He would have prevented schisms, enmities and bitter hostilities among those who are supposed to teach and exemplify his life and doctrine. We believe his presence would have insured the purity and the effectiveness of his teaching. No

Finishing a task means the culmination, the fulfillment of all its requirements. We do not return to high school again, years after we have finished it and completed our college course as well. He explained clearly why he came. His life fulfilled the mission.

How then could an enlightened soul, single himself out of the mass and say, "I am Christ or God," when Jesus pointed out to us in John 14:20 that we all are Christs, when we get to know the divine truth about ourselves? What intelligence can we ascribe to anyone believing such preposterous and presumptuous claims? What

We believe in the advent of a great teach- We hold it is imminent. We believe personality will not enter into the plan of this great teacher. We hold that the Avatar 

I will simply follow in the foot-steps of the 

Great Master, Jesus, in pointing out divine verities. It will be his province and privilege to awaken man to his own true selfhood. We feel he will offer new help and guidance to men in showing them how they may become as perfect as God, the Father.

Jesus pointed out a fourth way whereby we may discern the true from the false teacher. We concede that it is harder for the untutored mind to grasp the significance of this fourth touchstone of truth revealed to us by the Master. The Holy Bible declares plainly that we may prove to our own satisfaction that we are of divine origin, of divine destiny and divine beings here and now. Here is the statement of Jesus in 

John 8:28: "When ye have lifted up the Son of man, then shall ye know I am he."

Lifting up the Son of man means, of course, that one should raise himself up to his own original divine level. That was what Jesus had in mind in another place when he said, "Be perfect." This lifting up process unfolds to us our true selfhood, which is that of the Christ. Then we shall know that Jesus was telling us the truth, because like recognizes like. 

No one who has been good enough to awaken the Christ principle within himself could monopolize the idea of the Christ to himself and court adulation. No one who has lifted up the Son of man within himself

could deny the Christ in others, which he certainly would do were he to incorporate the idea of the Christ or God in himself alone. This is equivalent to denying it even within himself. Anyone who sets himself apart from the mass and claims for himself natures, powers and possibilities denying the same prerogatives and privileges to others is the anti-christ. Self and self-importance constitute the great hoodwink of the Deceiver. This impostor consorts with his kind in others, since birds of a feather flock together.

No one who does not accord to others the same divine origin as his own, has lifted up the Son of man within himself. Self-seeking does not exalt Christ anywhere. We lift up the Son of man within ourselves as we conform to the motives, thoughts and deeds of the hallowed life portrayed in Matthew 5, 6, 7. This would afford the sublime experience belonging to the Christ. What we experience we know and we are. The sublime experience gives us the exact knowledge of the truth Jesus voiced. This knowledge alone enables us to recognize the Son of man in Jesus, which is the same Son of man in us all-without exception.

This Son of man manifests in us today in degree, only because of our incomplete divine attainment. This degree of manifestation is the measure of our own divine meritsthe gauge of our own divine attainment.

This divine attainment, however, is according to our "works." Our works are the measure of our motives, thoughts and deeds.

Jesus did not intoxicate the ignorant with emotional aphorism, slogans and mesmeric suggestions. He used reason and logic to point to the truth. Emotional ecstacy is of the devil. Self suggestion envelops the suggester in more darkness. Thus the last

state of the victim becomes worse than his first state. Evil never breeds goodness, never brings freedom to the divine man.

The Holy Bible teaches us that the very wisdom of God within us is our true teacher and guide. It was that wisdom clarified in man which enabled the disciples of Jesus to recognize the truth he taught. It is that same wisdom clarified in us that enables us to analyze the same truth today.

Jesus lifted up the Son of man within himself, realizing that precise experience of bliss. From this experience he knew that

God, the Father, in his composite order, was manifest in all, just as in himself. That was why he claimed for man the same things he claimed for himself. He exalted himself only as he exalted man. The dull and the untutored attached to the Master supernatural natures and powers, they believed unpossessed by themselves, despite Jesus' life and teachings. Despite Jesus' plea, "I am in the Father, ye in me, I in you," the ignorant and the sensuous could not see the Oneness of Life in manifest in us all. 

Jesus taught the multitude that it was exactly what he was. The very Bible, Christendom loves and studies, points to the same fact every time it is read. In the light of these facts what more could Jesus do than he did 1900 years ago were he to return today? What would be the purpose of his coming? Would he have a new objective? 

Jesus was the "way shower." He showed the way. He did his part 1900 years ago.

He could do no more. No one need expect him to do more. His life is man's example: his teaching man's guide: his attainment man's objective. It is up to the believing to prove their appreciation of the supreme sacrifice he made to awaken man to the reality

of life. The work is man's. The application of the precepts he taught is man's. The hallowed experiences which were his, are man's. 

The attainment he realized is man's. After accepting him as Saviour, man must "work out his own salvation" in realizing individually the perfection of Life, the Father.

Divine bliss and blessings are earned and not bartered. Life will mete out to man exactly what he deserves. God has no favorites. Jesus could have none. What then could anyone expect from a second advent of Jesus? Is he to come to "take the believing to heaven with him"? He said, "The kingdom of heaven is within you." How could he then take anyone within himself, since he already is there? No one need be carted where he already is. These thoughtprovoking questions seem foolish, but that is what the "waiting for Jesus to come to take the faithful to heaven" amounts to.

Who is to be the judge as to who are believers? Jesus said, Man should not judge. 

Judging would break his commandment. 

Judging simultaneously places man in the rank of the disobedient. What then constitutes the qualification of the believer who expects to go up "to heaven" with him?

Discriminating study of the Holy Bible abolishes the idea of wholesale emigration to the heaven of conventional Christianity.

Heaven is within each and every person in the world. Therefore our duty is to apply the specific and scientific process, which is to preserve our bodies for the Lord when he comes-for the Christ who appears at the instant of divine contact.
