DRILL THREE I. TWO-STEP PROBLEMS 

A complex or two-step problem contains a main problem and a subordinate problem. This subordinate problem must be found and solved before the main problem can be solved and the desired result obtained. 

What you are asked to find in the main problem is always set out for you, but in a two-step problem one or more of the facts necessary for its answer is lacking. Finding the missing fact is what makes the subordinate problem.

The question of the subordinate problem is hidden and can be determined only by careful thinking. Answering this hidden question solves the subordinate problem and gives the missing fact needed to solve the main problem.

A complex problem is solved for you in the space below. Study it and solve all other two-step problems by the same plan. 

1. Mrs. Smith bought 4 pounds of steak at 36¢ per pound and a pound of butter for 45¢. What was the total amount of her bill? 

What are you asked to find? The total amount of the bill of Mrs. Smith. 

What is given? The number of pounds of steak, the price per pound, and the cost of the butter. 

What is lacking? The total cost of the meat.

What is given to help find this fact? Four pounds of meat were bought at 

36¢ per pound. 

How can the total cost of the meat be found? Multiply 36 by 4. 

What facts are now given? The steak cost $1.44 and the butter cost 45¢.

1. John is 5 years older than George. If the sum of their ages is 21 years, what is the age of each boy? 

What are you asked to find? The age of each boy. 

What are you given? John is 5 years older than George. The sum of their ages is 21 years.

Let a letter as x stand for the age of the boy who is the youngest. 

In this case it is George. 

Then x = the age of George.

State the other unknown or John's age in terms of the first unknown or the age of George. Since John is 5 years older than George, his age can be stated as x + 5.
