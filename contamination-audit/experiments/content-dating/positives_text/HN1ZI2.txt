WHAT PRICE PRICE VIRTUE? 

WHAT is virtue anyway? A thing of morals or just dulled senses? Are women virtuous because they hold chastity sacred above everything or because they lack a maddened response to desire? And if a woman remains true all her life to the man she marries, if she is a good mother to his children, a faithful wife to him, what is her reward? 

Do you answer, "Virtue is its own reward"? Is that really the only reward a virtuous woman receives? Maybe so.

When you come to think of it, the whore is no more dead when she dies than the most virtuous woman on earth.

Both are allotted the same six feet of earth. Does one soul go to burn itself clean throughout the ages of agonized hell fire while the other receives the solace of heaven to rejoice and sing hosannas for eternity? Who can say; for who has heard sinner or saint, returned to earth, tell others of their struggling kind what has been their punishment or recompense? 

Upon these fundamental, soul-rending questions of chastity facing sin, the marriage sacrament against brute desire, the eternal questions that every woman must answer some time in her life, Marguerite Brener's frank, courageous and absorbing new novel is built.
