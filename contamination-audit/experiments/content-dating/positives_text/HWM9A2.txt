and and WAR "I was so tired this morning I didn't hear the alarm clock go off." "Well, I'm always tired, but Jim's out of work now, so he gets the chillren up after I've gone. 

Elsie's five now. She'll go to school next year." "My boy Jack's 15. He left school last June, but he can't get a job. He's tried everywhere, and he's a smart boy, too." 

Rita Morris, Jack's mother, was 38, but she looked older as she sat opposite Esther Reed in the company cafeteria. The two women worked in different departments of the big electric company. Just lately Rita had been taken out of the packing department and put on "examining" at much lower pay. Miss Perkins, the supervisor, had said she was "too slow" now to keep up with the conveyor. Elsie worked on the belt in the assembling room. "Is your husband out of work, too?" asked

Esther, looking up at the black hollows under 

Rita's eyes. "He was killed in the war, in France, in 

1918. Jack was just a baby then." "But you get money from the government, don't you? Why do you have to work?" "I get nothing at all from the government,' 3

said Rita bitterly. "It takes political pull to get a pension, and I can't get one. I've tried."

From the other end of the cafeteria table a thin, tall woman stood up and looked with flashing angry eyes at Rita and Esther. "Stop talking about the war!" she exclaimed. "Stop talking about it, I say!" And she left the table quickly, without finishing her lunch. "What's the matter with her?" Rita asked, looking after her curiously. "It's Margaret Williams. I heard from some one that knows her, she was engaged to a young fellow when the war began. And he was wounded awfully in the war. Both his legs and both his arms were shot off, and he's in the government hospital in Washington. He can't ever leave the hospital. She wanted to marry him, just the same. But she couldn't.

She can't even get there to see him-what there is left of him. And she's never married, all these years since the war ended." 

At the next table a younger girl folded up the paper she was reading, put it in her apron pocket, and came over to Rita and Esther. "I heard you talking about the last war," she said. "My name's Sally Martin. I don't remember the last war 'cause I'm only 19 now and I was only a little kid then. But I wanted to show you the paper I was reading-about the next war."

She took the paper out of her apron pocket, unfolded it carefully, and showed Rita and Esther the name of the paper-Daily Worker -and the headlines: 

"Washington Reports Japan Plans Attack on the Soviet Union by Coming Spring." "Next war!" exclaimed Rita. going to be any next war. war. 99 "There isn't 

We don't want "But Japan has already started war against the Chinese workers," went on Sally Martin. "And the United States may be in it at any moment. The big bosses want war. They want to sell more goods and make big profits out of the next war, just as they did in the last world war you were talking about. They don't care what happens to us workers. All our husbands and brothers and sons and boyfriends can be killed, for all the boss class cares." "I wouldn't ever let my boy Jack go into any bosses' war!" Rita cried out. "He's all I've got now." "But you couldn't stop him," Sally persisted. "The capitalist government'd draft him and make him fight, while the bosses sat in their cushy office chairs and read the financial papers to see how their stocks were going up. 

Their sons would be officers-in safe places -back of the lines. What's more, in the next war, they'll draft us women, too." 5

"Does your paper really say there'll be another war?" asked Esther, thinking of Jim and little Jim. "Of course there'll be another war." Sally wished the lunch hour were longer, so she could have more time to tell them what she'd been reading. She hurried on. "The capitalist class wants to capture the Soviet Union, where the workers rule, and wipe out the workers' government. They're afraid of the workers' republic because it's so strong and is growing so fast. The capitalists want that rich country for themselves." "Well, what can we do to stop them, if the bosses want war?" asked Esther, not understanding very well what Sally Martin meant when she talked so fast about the Soviet Union and the workers' republic. "I'd like to know just what you mean." "It's explained in this paper - the Daily

And I can get you a woman's paper, the

Working Woman, that tells more about the coming war. It shows what we women workers must do to get rid of the boss class in the U. S., as they did in the Soviet Union. It shows-" A loud electric bell rang through the halls.

The workers sprang up from their seats and hurried toward the door. One o'clock. Work. 

Speed-up. No talking. 

What Happened in the Last War 

But Sally Martin found a chance to talk with the other women workers every day. "Do you know what the last war meant to the women of the working class?" she asked. "Not just to Rita Morris and Margaret Williams, whom we happen to know about personally. But to millions of others like them. Here are some figures."

For all armies in the imperialist war of 

1914-18, the reported number of dead soldiers was ten million. But that is only part of the story. Nearly six million other soldiers were reported as "prisoners or missing" and at least half of these were dead. 

Seriously wounded numbered over six million, and "otherwise wounded" about 14,000,- 000. Besides the dead soldiers, millions of civilians died, either killed in air raids or dead from war diseases, starvation and exposure. 

It takes only a few seconds to read these figures, but think of the mothers and wives of those 13 million dead soldiers. Of course most of the dead were of the working class, because the husbands and sons in the ruling

class took the safer places, behind the lines or in government positions. 

War widows in all countries numbered five million. These women are left to earn a living as best they can, under post-war capitalism, and the small widow's pension is never enough to live on. In the United States only a few out of all the thousands of Gold Star mothers from the World War are on the list of the federal government to receive any pension at all. And the pension is only $30 a month.

About nine million children were left fatherless by the war, it is admitted by capitalist writers. And at least ten million men, women and children lost their homes and wandered as homeless refugees over the land. So we have this list of what bourgeois writers call "the human cost of the great world war"the cost borne by the working class: 

Ten million known dead soldiers. 

Three million probably dead soldiers. 

Thirteen million dead civilians. 

Twenty million wounded soldiers. 

Three million prisoners. 

Nine million war orphans. 

Five million war widows. 

Ten million refugees.

In the American army about 110 thousand soldiers died in the war. 

Over 40 thousand

were seriously wounded and nearly 150 thousand were "otherwise wounded." "And remember what happened to women workers in the last war," went on Sally. "It shows what'll happen in the next war-only it'll be much worse.' 99 Women workers were used to take the places of men who had to fight in the bosses' war. 

They worked in munitions plants, making cartridges and shells. They worked on war orders in textile mills. They worked in every basic industry-in steel, transportation, electrical, chemical and food industries. Why, it took five and a half pages of small type in a government report just to list, in paragraphs, the jobs in which women were actually substitutes for men. Even in blast furnaces and steel works, logging camps and saw mills, the women worked on what had been men's jobs. 

Speed-up was fierce in these war industries.

Days were not long enough for the bosses to fill their war orders, and the women had to work nights, 11 and 12 hours at a stretch. Often only 15 minutes was allowed for lunch. 

It was "hurry, hurry" all the time. Women in munitions plants were poisoned by the chemicals. Accidents increased with the speed-up. 

Homes were overcrowded. The cost of living was rising all the time and a dollar bought only half as much as it bought before the war.

WAR PROFIT

Women did war work of every kind. They raised money, they sold Liberty Bonds, made bandages and surgical dressings, sewed uniforms and did every kind of "patriotic" service, under the Red Cross, the Young Women's 

Christian Association, the Salvation Army and other organized agencies of the government. 

All these organizations were betraying the workers and serving the ruling class. Thousands of working class women were fooled by the bosses' propaganda into thinking it was really "a war to end war" and they were drawn into all fields of war work. They didn't understand that war is always bound up with capitalism which lives on war, and that the only way to do away with war is to do away with capitalism which breeds war. 

It became a saying among the generals of the war that "if the women of either side should quit their war work, that side would lose."

Under the slogan of a "War to end all wars," the capitalists and their governments were forcing the workers to take part in the most horrible war in all history. Out of this world slaughter, the capitalist class in the United States emerged as the richest of all the world robbers. Only the capitalists benefited from this slaughter. The so-called "prosperity" lasted only for a short period and now we are in the midst of an economic crisis,- 11

the worst in all history - a crisis which the bosses are trying to solve at our expense. 

War Is Coming! 

"Sure, the last war was hell," said Rita Morris, thinking of the day the telegram came saying that John Morris was killed in action.

He and four others, who had become his best friends, were standing on a little hill in France in October, 1918, one month before the armistice. A shell exploded. Four of the men were blown into pieces. The fifth, blinded and with only one arm, had come to see Rita months later to tell her about that October afternoon. "But that doesn't mean there'll be another war," Rita persisted. She looked over Sally's shoulder at the figures the girl pointed out. 

Every capitalist nation is prepared for war even to the last belt buckle. The United States heads the list of 60 nations in armaments and spends more than any other country for the army and navy-$707,425,000 in the one year, 1930-31. (League of Nations, Armaments 

Year Book.) And that vast sum is only for direct military and naval expenditures. 

Seventy cents out of every dollar you pay in taxes to the federal government goes for war, past, present or future, and not one cent goes for unemployment insurance to take care of jobless workers. The whole amount spent for 12

war by the U. S. government each year now in "peace time" is nearly $3,000,000,000. Public health gets only $22,496,000 this year (ending 

June 30, 1932)-less than the cost of one superdreadnought.

The U. S. battleship Indiana cost $5,800,000 some years ago, enough to build a great university or half a dozen large hospitals. And now the latest superdreadnoughts in the U. S. navy cost over $40,000,000 apiece. 

War in the Air

But the next war will be fought largely in the air, and while capitalist nations in "disarmament" conferences talk about sacrificing an old, out-of-date battleship or two, they build more and more airplanes. The United 

States now has larger air forces than any other nation, but France is a close second. 

Airplanes in the next capitalist war can drop poison gas bombs and shells enough to wipe out a great city in a few hours. One or two breaths of the deadly mustard gas, called "The King of Gases," is enough to kill a man. (Chemical Warfare, by Donald Cameron. International Pamphlets, price 10 cents.) Lewisite gas, called by General Fries "The Dew of 

Death," is probably even more deadly. Not only poison gas but fire from chemicals that can't be extinguished will be dropped on a city and burn it to the ground. 13

Already the Japanese imperialists have begun the next war. Planes carrying bombs flew over the city of Shanghai, China, on January 

29, 1932, dropped bombs and set fire to the houses of Chinese workers. An Associated 

Press dispatch admitted the horror of this imperialist attack on defenseless workers, in the following description: 

"The terrified people were faced with a dilemma of terror. They had the choice of staying in their huts and being burned to death or fleeing into the open to be shot or torn to pieces by bursting bombs.' 99 In this next capitalist war women and children, formerly called the "non-combatants" in war-time, are the chief target of air forces. 

The main points of attack are the factories and the industrial centers where women and youth are working. Death-dealing gases seek them out even in the cellars of houses, for poison gases sink and penetrate even into holes in the ground. Disease bombs dropped from airplanes can spread pestilence in every working class district of a great city. 

Where 13,000,000 civilians died from war causes in the imperialist war of 1914-18, that number will be many times multiplied in the next war.

In all the race between capitalist nations for these armaments, airplanes, gas bombs, shells, tanks, guns and battleships, the United States is in the lead. Many of the preparations have been secret, but in May, 1931, General Doug- 14

las MacArthur, chief of staff in the War Department, let the cat out of the bag in a statement on war preparedness before the 

War Policies Commission in Washington. He admitted that the general war plan of the War Department calls for the immediate mobilization of four million men in the event of war. Every year 800 thousand boys, only 18 years old, every working class mother's son of that age, will be added to the army. 

Already orders for 4 thousand items on the 

War Department's shopping list have been contracted for, and 17 thousand industrial concerns have been lined up to fill the department's orders the moment war is declared. "At the very hour, or possibly a few hours before America next declares war," official telegrams will flash the words "Go Ahead" and the war contracts, already signed, will immediately become effective.

Secret preparations, we said. Yes, mostly secret, except when some frank general admits a little of what is going on. Secret also were the war talks between President Hoover and Premier Ramsay MacDonald, the "socialist" prime minister of Great Britain; between 

Hoover and Premier Laval of France; between 

Hoover and Foreign Minister Grandi of Italy.

These conferences were secret and were undoubtedly planned by the main imperialist powers to strengthen their position in preparation for war against the Soviet Union. 15

If any woman worker doubts that the United States is preparing for war, she has only to listen in on the radio or go to the "talkies." 

Patriotic speeches are put on to whoop it up for the army and navy and fool the working class again into support of war. Movies of the "glorious American Navy" show pictures of great battleships and the sailors in foreign ports. Other pictures show soldiers in military drills, sham battles and pageants. Every weapon of propaganda, the press, churches, schools, radio, theaters and moving picture houses are used by the boss class to prepare the working class for slaughter in the next imperialist war. And it will be a war against the workers' republic, Soviet Russia. 

Women Workers Now and in the Next War Already women are replacing men workers in many industries. In the 10 years from 1920 to 1930, women workers in the United States increased by 26 per cent, while men increased by only 15 per cent. Nearly 11 million women (10,778,000) now work for a living, at pay averaging only one-half to two-thirds the wages of men for the same jobs.

Jim Reed is out of a job, as Esther told Rita Morris. Esther is still working, but she can earn only about half of what Jim was earning. Jim, Esther and the three children must live on half of what they had before, and that 16

was little enough, from Jim's pay envelope.

Then Esther got a wage cut and the five must live on still less.

So in this economic crisis the capitalist class is throwing the burden on the working class, lowering the workers' standard of living, while trying always to protect the profits of the capitalists.

Evictions. Hunger. Starvation. Women workers sleeping out in the city parks. Women workers forced to sell their bodies for a meal. Mothers committing suicide rather than see their children starve. Sixteen thousand mothers and ten thousand babies dying each year in childbirth when they could be saved by proper medical care. The crisis of capitalism has brought death to additional thousands of working class women. Thus capitalism kills women of the working class. 

For those workers who still have jobs there are wage cuts and more wage cuts. Negro women workers always get the worst of it. In most states they earn only half what white women earners earn, little as that is. In South 

Carolina the median wage for Negro women in factories and laundries is only $4.89. (The median wage means that half the women get more and half get less than this amount.) 

To earn these miserably low wages women must work, on the average, over 50 hours a week, 9 and 10 hours a day, and sometimes even 12 and 14 hours a day. Night work is allowed in two-thirds of the states, and the 17

few "protective" laws that exist are not enforced. Only the organized mass pressure of all workers together can enforce any laws for the protection of women workers. 

At the Remington Arms Co., in Bridgeport,

Conn., a typical munitions plant, women are working 10 to 12 hours a day or night for only 20 to 25 cents an hour. There is always danger from an imperfect shell exploding, and the greater the speed-up the more accidents happen. Besides the danger of explosions, the workers are constantly exposed to acid poisoning from fulminate of mercury used in munition making.

The E. I. du Pont de Nemours Co., one of the largest munitions companies in the world, employs a great many women and young girls. 

At the Pompton Lakes, N. J. plant, for instance, girls are working in the plug and solder, in the sulphur and testing departments. 

They must live in company houses and are immediately evicted if they offer resistance to wage cuts or bad working conditions. 

Rayon plants also represent a war industry in which great numbers of women are employed. Rayon plants can be changed overnight into the making of dynamite. With women already forming about 60 per cent of the working force, the rayon industry is well prepared for the next war, and many plants are already on a war basis. At the American Enka Corporation plant in Asheville, N. C., for instance, 18

workers are photographed and registered before getting a job in the plant. A complete spy system of the company tries to keep out every union organizer. Most of the rayon plants in the United States are in southern states where workers must slave 11 and 12 hours a day or night at about 16 cents an hour.

Women Will Be Drafted 

The next imperialist war will see the direct mobilization of women for war work. Capitalist writers are already admitting that in the next war women will be conscripted as well as men; they will be assigned to whatever form of war work the ruling class thinks they are best fitted to do. (Irwin, Will. The 

Next War, pages 76-77.) Major Gen. Ely of the U. S. Army, in April, 1931, made a frank statement on this point: "Women will play a greater part in future wars. Governments, including our own, have been studying the use of women in war. Woman power in many instances supplements and in other cases, supplants man power in war. The utilization of women in war will grow out of a new conception of war-making, born during the last war. The struggle was so gigantic for countries involved that war-making becomes a national industry." 

With so many women workers already enrolled in production, industry can be quickly put on a war basis, and the wheels of the 19

next imperialist war will begin to turn very rapidly. In European countries women now form about one-third of the working force, and in the United States over one-fifth of the working force.

Not only for production of war materials but for actual fighting in the armies, women will be drafted in the next war. Women will also be put under conscription "for ambulance and truck driving, for the thousand and one varieties of light labor which they can perform in the rear areas of an army zone.' "For this work they are already receiving military training in a number of countries. Three European countries, France, Italy and Poland, have passed laws, calling for the conscription of women as well as men in the event of war. 

Enemy Organizations Prepare Women for War 

The American Federation of Labor officials and their affiliated organization, the National 

Women's Trade Union League, give their full support to the war programs of the ruling class. As Matthew Woll stated in a confidential address to the Army War College in Washington: "The American Federation of Labor has only words of kindness for the Army."

The strike-breaking A. F. of L. was of course represented at the patriotic, whoop-it-up-forwar conference held in the grand ballroom of the Waldorf-Astoria Hotel in New York City in January, 1932. Under the name of the Sec- 20

ond Annual Anti-Communist Convention, this conference outlined plans for war measures against the Soviet Union and for new strikebreaking measures against the workers' movement in the United States. Among the war organizations represented at this convention were 27 special organizations of women. 

The National Women's Trade Union League, one of the associations now connected with the so-called National Conference on the Cause and Cure of War, gave its full support to the last war. It was spreading pacifist illusions that peace is possible under capitalism. It is one of those organizations that is "pacifist between wars.” Before the last war it was spreading pacifist talk among working class women, just as it is doing now. But the moment war breaks out, it becomes a war organization, mobilizing women workers into active support of imperialist war. 

The National Conference on the Cause and Cure of War has connected with it 11 national organizations of women, and is busy throwing a pacifist smoke screen over war preparations.

Its chairman, Carrie Chapman Catt, is herself a rich woman, and most of its supporters are women of the leisure class. But two of its affiliated organizations, the National Women's 

Trade Union League, and the Young Women's 

Christian Association, reach thousands of the working women with their false propaganda. 

Millions of signatures for a "disarmament" 21

Whol

petition have been gathered by these pacifist women's organizations, half a million in the United States, a million and a half in Great Britain and over two million in Holland. 

Working women who may have been fooled into signing these petitions will find when war is declared that these very same organizations are the most active supporters of the imperialist war, just as they were of the last war.

Among the organizations that are preparing for war is the Betsy Ross Corps of women aviators. It is a patriotic and "semi-military" association of women, with the expressed aim "to serve their country in national emergencies of peace or war." First of its kind, it will of course be followed by other such military organizations of women aviators, encouraged by the "friendly and appreciative interest" of the 

U. S. War and Navy Departments. 

As part of its preparedness program the War Department has outlined its plan for a so-called "Labor Administration," to serve during the coming war. The plan calls for a woman director of the Bureau of Women and Minors in Industry who shall be "a woman essentially patriotic and not inter-nationalistic; appreciative of the fact that women like men, must make sacrifices in war."

In other words this woman must NOT be sympathetic with the international working class movement, nor with the organized efforts of 23

workers in the United States in their struggle against imperialist war. She must be one who will energetically carry through the war program of the American ruling class in the next imperialist war.

Why the Boss Class Wants War Against the 

Soviet Union

In all these war preparations, the capitalist class and its government are planning for war against the only workers' government, the Soviet Union. This is because the Soviet Union is in the hands of the working class; workers are in control, and the whole life of the country is planned by the workers and farmers. While our working conditions in the United States are getting worse and worse every day, in the Soviet Union the conditions are getting better every day. So the capitalists are afraid of the workers' republic and they want to make war and destroy it; they are afraid that workers in other countries will follow the example of the Russian workers.

Capitalists always profit from war and pile up their riches while the workers in the trenches are blown to pieces, gassed, driven mad, or horribly wounded. The capitalist class doubles and trebles its wealth from the sale of war materials and goods. 

Capitalists must have markets for their goods in order to make profits. With more goods on hand than they can possibly sell 24

(because the workers are too poor to buy back the goods they make), the capitalists know that war immediately increases the demand for all kinds of goods and materials. They want war in order to sell their stores of goods and make more profits.

Thus during the last war the great munitions company, E. I. du Pont de Nemours Co., filled war orders running into the millions of dollars. Within 10 months during 1915 and 

1916 this company gave dividends to its stockholders amounting to 104 per cent on their stock. When dividends of 6 per cent and 7 per cent are considered "good" in peace-time, we can see what a vast increase in profits the war brought to the capitalists in this giant company. Hundreds of other companies made similar profits.

But it is not only munitions companies that profit from war. Capitalists in the textile industry, in transportation, in steel, in shipbuilding, food and other industries all get war orders and pile up their profits out of the blood and death of the workers.

Capitalists must have raw materials to make goods and they fight among themselves for the possession of countries that are rich in raw materials. Thus the United States and 

Great Britain are in conflict over oil. Germany and France are in conflict over coal and iron. Japan wants the coal and other riches to be found in China and Manchuria. 25

As Comrade Lenin wrote: "Imperialism is an embittered struggle of the big powers for division and redivision of the world-it must, therefore, inevitably lead to further militarization in all countries." (Quoted in Labor Monthly, Aug., 1930.)

Capitalist nations want to destroy the workers' government that is building Socialism in the Soviet Union. It is the only country in the world where there is no unemployment; no wage cuts; where the workers' conditions are steadily growing better year by year. 

The Soviet Union, the workers' republic, is one of the richest of all countries in raw materials. With one-sixth of the earth's surface, this vast area, owned by the working class, has practically all the raw materials necessary for the building of industry, for the life and health of the workers.

Capitalist nations want this rich land for themselves. They want the raw materials to be found in the Soviet Union. They want to take away this country from the international working class and use it to make profits for the capitalist class.

The Soviet Union is the only country in the world where women workers have complete freedom and equality with men workers. 

They have equal pay for equal work. They have all the privileges of social insurance in illness and old age, and they have also special privileges of maternity benefits, including leave of 26

absence with full pay before and after child birth.

Women in the Soviet Union are relieved from the double burden of work at home in addition to outside work, because there are convenient factory kitchens, laundries, and good day nurseries for their use. They are free from the slavery of kitchen work and are able to take an important part in all the industrial and cultural life of the country. 

Because the Soviet Union is a government of the workers, by the workers and for the workers, imperialist powers want to destroy it. They know it is the only nation in the world that has brought forward a program for complete disarmament and presented the proposals three times before the Geneva arms conferences. The League of Nations, which is an instrument of war against the Soviet Union, did not even consider these Soviet proposals for disarmament. The capitalist powers, organized in the League of Nations, want war, not peace.

What We Can Do in the Struggle Against Imperialist War 

"What can we do to stop them, if the boss class wants war?" Esther Reed,, the electrical worker, had asked Sally Martin.

Organize. Organize all working women in unions, under the leadership of the Trade Un- 27

ion Unity League. Organize under the banner of the Communist Party.

Women workers have proved themselves among the best fighters in the great textile strikes and mine strikes of recent years. Spirited and determined on the picket line, they have often been foremost in resisting wage cuts and speed-up. When once organized in the revolutionary unions, women workers take their places firmly in the vanguard of the working class.

Working class women must understand that their enemies are not the workers in other countries. Their real enemies are the capitalists and their government, the bosses like J. P. 

Morgan, Henry Ford, Charles M. Schwab, John D. Rockefeller, and all the others of the ruling class who make profits out of war.

These men of the master class are directly responsible for the workers' misery at the present time, for the mass unemployment, the wage cuts, the evictions, the hunger and starvation. 

And there are other enemies who actively support the war program of the master class against the workers of the world. The Socialist party, the American Federation of Labor officials, and the National Women's Trade Union League, are all helping to prepare a war against the Soviet Union, the one and only country where the workers have overthrown the capitalists and are building a new society. 28

Pacifists and their "peace" organizations are trying to lower the curtain on these war preparations, so that the workers shall not see how the stage behind the curtain is set for another great imperialist war. But the women of the working class shall not be fooled by this pacifist curtain of propaganda which only helps the bosses in their war plans. We must carry on a strong, continuous fight against these pacifists and their pacifist organizations, and against the women's patriotic organizations with their war programs. 

Now is the time to organize against imperialist war. Get a few trusted workers together in your home and organize a grievance committee of your shop. Join the revolutionary union in your industry. Subscribe to The

Working Woman, the voice of women workers. 

If you are out of a job, or your husband is out of a job, get other unemployed workers together and form an Unemployed Council in your neighborhood. Fight for unemployment insurance at the complete expense of the bosses and their government. 

Demand the withdrawal of all American troops and gunboats from Chinese waters and 

Chinese soil. Fight against the Japanese imperialist invasion of China. Fight against the war on the Chinese masses. Smash the imperialist war moves against the Soviet Union. 

Organize and strike against wage cuts. While the bosses cut our wages, they are spending 29

billions on armaments. Demand these billion dollar war funds for Unemployment Insurance. Fight for equal pay for equal work, for maternity insurance. Fight against night work, and demand the 7-hour day. 30

With these demands you can help to organize the other workers in your shop against the imperialist war, and then with a shop grievance committee you can work out the other demands for your own mill or factory.

Only by such organization in the factories, in working class organizations, in the revolutionary unions of the Trade Union Unity League and in the Communist Party can we fight against imperialist war.

Only thus can we prepare ourselves for the time when under the leadership of the Communist Party the working class will arise against the murder of brother workers and will turn the guns against their common enemies, the capitalist class and its governments. 

The time will come when the men in the trenches and the women in the factories will follow the example of the Russian men and women in 1917. Then the imperialist war will be changed into civil war; capitalism will be overthrown and a workers' government will be established. Only in socialist society will war be ended forever and the barriers between workers of different countries be thrown down. 

The Communist Party is the only Party that carries on the struggle against imperialist war and against the capitalist system. It is the 

Party of your class, the working class. Join the Communist Party and fight with your class against the wars of imperialism. Defend the Soviet Union. Build the Communist Party! 31
