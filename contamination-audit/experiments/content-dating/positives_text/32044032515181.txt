The Veterans' Administration is one of the largest agencies of the Federal Government. It is large, not only in the sense of actual cash outlays expended but also in that it touches the lives of millions of our citizens and broadly affects the economy of the country. 

The current budget for the 1957 fiscal year is $4 billion. This is an increase of approximately $25 million over the previous year and compares with the $4.232 billion for the 1955 appropriation. There are over 22 million veterans in civilian life-15,370,000 who served in World War II; 4,682,000 served during the Korean conflict and 858,000 of these also saw service in World War II. There are approximately 3,061,000 living veterans from World War I and there are 128,000 veterans of other wars and of peacetime service. 

While millions of veterans have participated in the various veterans' benefit programs the vast majority have not done so. For example, in the education field, in World War II approximately 7,800,000 veterans have taken training under the World War II act-roughly 50 percent of the total who served in World War II. In the Korean war approximately 30 percent have availed themselves of the education provisions of this law. However, that program runs until January 

31, 1965.

For World War II over 22 million policies of national service life insurance were issued. The most recent figures show that there are 

5,483,000 policies in force-divided between 3,353,000 term policies and 2,130,000 converted policies. They have a face value of $36,013 868739 1

The Veterans' Administration operates the largest hospital program in the world under a single agency, involving 173 hospitals with an average daily patient load of 112,000 distributed between 109,000 in actual Veterans' Administration hospitals and 3,240 in contract hospitals. Applications for hospital care are received at a rate of more than 2,000 a day and this does not include more than 16,000 veterans who are under care in 17 domiciliaries. Outpatient care for service-connected conditions was given in more than 182,000 cases last month. This included 127,000 cases of actual outpatient medical treatment, 36,000 examinations for compensation and pension, and 

18,000 for other reasons. Nearly 9,000 dental examinations were completed during the last month, in addition, 8,000 dental treatment cases were completed.

Compensation for service-connected disability and pension for nonservice-connected disability is the largest single cash outlay of the Veterans' Administration. The current appropriation amounts to $2,907 million. This includes 2,731,000 active awards for living veterans, and 835,000 awards for death claims. 

The guaranty of housing loans has had a tremendous impact on the economy of the country since approximately $37 billion in loans have been made with a guaranty of approximately $20 billion. The total number of loans guaranteed number 4,810,000. The default rate has been less than one-half of 1 percent.

In addition, direct loans have been made where private financing was unavailable which involved over $554 million and included 77,000 loans. An outright grant of $43,016,630 has been made to over 6,000 paraplegics who need specially adapted housing because of their service-connected disabilities.

LEGISLATION ENACTED DURING THE 84TH CONGRESS 

Public Law 634 provides educational assistance to children of veterans of World War I, II, or Korea who died from a service-connected disability; monthly payments range from $110 for full-time training to $80 for three-quarters and $50 for half-time training; overall limit set at 36 months with adjustments for any training received under appropriate laws administered by the VA based on the child's own service; generally limited to ages 18 to 23, inclusive. This is an entirely new concept in the veterans' education field. 

More than 150,000 children of deceased veterans are expected to be eligible for Government grants to further their education under the new law. The veterans must have died of injuries or diseases resulting from their military service. The monthly payments will begin October 1, 1956. 

On January 1, 1955, the President issued a proclamation terminating certain wartime veterans benefits which, among other things, had the effect of ending the accrual of additional education or training under 

Public Law 550 (Korean GI bill of rights). Congress enacted Public 

Law 7 which provides that any veteran who was in the service on or before January 31, 1955, would be entitled to continue to accrue educational benefits at the rate of 1%½ days of education for each day of service with a maximum of 36 months of education, until his first discharge or release from active service after January 31, 1955. In the housing field 3 laws have been enacted, 2 of which primarily affect veterans in rural or nonurban areas. Public Law 84 places farm loans by veterans who now reside or who desire to reside on farms on a parity with those residing in the city for the purpose of receiving a guaranteed loan under the provisions of the Servicemen's Readjustment Act. Previously, the amount of guaranty had been limited to $4,000 or 50 percent of the loan. This law provides that they shall be entitled to the $7,500, or 60 percent guaranty which the city veteran previously had exclusively. The $7,500 guaranty would apply to any veteran who (1) wanted to purchase a farm on which there is a farm residence to be occupied by the veteran as his home (2) to construct on land owned by the veteran a farm residence, (3) to repair, alter, or improve a farm residence owned by the veteran.

Public Law 88 extended the direct-loan program for 2 years (until June 30, 1957), and provided additional amounts of $150 million for each year of the program, but not to exceed $50 million in any quarter less the amount of sales in the preceding quarter. Further, it amended the direct-loan benefits by extending them to all qualified veterans who had not previously used all of their guaranty entitlement. In line with Public Law 84, mentioned above, the direct-loan program has been extended to farm veterans as follows: any veteran who (1) wanted to purchase a farm on which there is a farm residence to be occupied by the veteran as his home, (2) to construct on land owned by the veteran a farm residence, and (3) to repair, alter, or improve a farm residence owned by the veteran. 

Public Law 898 is an omnibus housing bill introduced during the second session and contains 10 sections. 

It amends the loan guaranty section of the Servicemen's Readjustment Act by providing- 1. Where the veteran has lost his home, the loan on which was guaranteed by the VA, through condemnation for public use, destroyed by fire or other natural hazards, or disposed of for reasons devoid of fault on his part, he may have until January 31, 1965, to obtain a new guaranteed loan. 

2. The reasonable value of a property shall be established by the Administrator of Veterans' Affairs. 

3. A veteran purchaser shall certify at the time application is made for a guaranteed loan and at the closing of the loan that he intends to occupy the property as his home. 

4. When a veteran sells his home, the veteran and the prospective buyer can make application to the Administrator for acceptance of the second buyer thus relieving the original veteran of liability to the Administrator for the guaranteed loan. 

5. Provides for entitlements to be reinstated where a veteran reenters the service, sells his home, and pays off the loan because he is transferred by the Armed Forces. 

6. Loan guaranty program is extended for 1 year to July 25, 1958. 

7. Administrator is authorized to process applications filed on or before July 25, 1958, provided the processing is completed on or before July 25, 1959.

The time for World War II and Korean veterans to apply for a grant of $1,600 toward the purchase price of an automobile or other conveyance where they have suffered the loss or loss of use of one or both hands or legs or who are blind generally expired on October 20, 1954. Public Law 969 provides a statutory award of $47 a month, in addition to any other compensation to which a veteran may be entitled, for the loss or loss of use of both buttocks. 

Public Law 83 limits outpatient dental treatment to disabilities which are service connected and compensable in degree or service connected and shown to have been in existence at time of discharge and application for treatment made within 1 year after discharge, or December 31, 1954, whichever is the later, or are associated with an aggravating disability for some other service-connected injury or disease. This treatment is on a onetime completion basis only. The aforementioned provisions do not apply to Spanish-American War veterans or to veterans undergoing vocational rehabilitation training under Public Law 16, 78th Congress. Neither does the one-time treatment apply to former prisoners of war or those suffering from combat wounds or other service disabilities or to cases where the dental work does not meet professional standards.

HOSPITAL MODERNIZATION AND RENOVATION PROGRAM Committee Print 27, entitled "Non-Bed Betterments" generally relates to the need for renovation and construction of VA hospitals. 

As a result of the action of the committee in focusing attention to this need, approximately $16 million was added over and above the 1956 budget estimate to meet repair and renovation needs and a longrange program is now being developed. 

The fiscal year 1957 Veterans' Administration construction appropriation request covers $4.353 million for technical services and $21.2 million for construction, in order to continue this very much needed program. 

In addition, the large-scale modernization of some 58 existing hospitals has been given a tentative long-range schedule with a request of $20.552 million for the fiscal year 1957 portion of this program. 

This represents a continuation of the program developed in fiscal year 1956, as well as the start of additional projects. These projects are executed by phases in order to continue the hospital in as nearly normal operation as possible while improvements are being made. 

Under the category of modernization for fiscal year 1956, approved projects are either in the architect-engineering design state or under construction at 15 of the 58 hospitals presently considered in greatest need of modernization improvements.

VETERANS' LEGISLATION 

In addition to this program, minor improvements are being accomplished at some 16 other hospitals in this group. In other words, some improvements are scheduled at approximately 53 percent of these 58 hospitals in contrast to approximately 26 percent in fiscal year 1955.

For fiscal year 1957, the appropriations request asks for approximately $20.5 million for projects affecting 33 percent of this group. 

This indicates a progressive program which, if continued until the tentative long-range plan is completed, will result in the modernization of some 58 older hospitals.

Dependent upon available funds, the tentative plan contemplates the completion of this program about fiscal year 1966 or fiscal year 1967. It is well to recognize that during this 10-year period, additional hospitals will have reached an age where they will require extensive modernization. This, coupled with the final phases of the present tentative plan, could well carry this program into fiscal year 1970, based on the present rate of accomplishment. 

In addition to the above, the fiscal year 1957 request asks for $4,447,000 for minor improvements in various categories, all of which provide much-needed improvements in our hospital operations. 

Projects of this sort are planned at some 37 hospitals, plus the continuation of the program for correcting fire and safety hazards. 

Although this amount represents but a small part of the total program dollarwise, it is essential that these minor projects be accomplished in order that we may improve our operations for the care and treatment of veterans.

The many projects in these minor categories range from a few thousand dollars to $250,000 each. It is hoped that this group of unprogramed projects may be brought current within a reasonable time, along the lines presented in the fiscal year 1957 request. STAFF STUDIES AND COMMITTEE INVESTIGATIONS 

On February 2, 1955, the House of Representatives approved 

House Resolution 63 directing the Committee on Veterans' Affairs to make a study of seven basic programs administered by the Veterans' Administration including hospital and medical program. The first action taken by the committee following the approval of this resolution was to begin an inquiry into the operations of the Veterans' Administration medical program. To that end a comprehensive questionnaire was mailed to all VA hospitals which was reproduced in the form of House Committee Print No. 31. The report contains information on all VA hospitals concerning the number and types of beds, personnel, operating costs, et cetera. 

Another field authorized for investigation by House Resolution 63 is the housing guaranty and insurance program as provided in Public 

Law 346, 78th Congress, as amended (GI bill of rights). The following has been accomplished in this field: 

1. A summary has been prepared of the status of builders and lenders suspended because of failure to comply with laws and regulations and grouped by regional offices. 

2. A documentation of abuses in criminal-type cases referred by the Veterans' Administration to the Justice Department since 1952 has been completed and indication of the action taken by the Department of Justice. 

3. A statistical summary and analysis of 5,600 default and foreclosure cases. 

4. A study of the continuing liability of veteran mortgagors in cases where title to the mortgaged premises has been transferred.

Administration loan guaranty program. This report contains 14 recommendations for correction or improvement in the loan guaranty program. 

During the inspection of the medical clinic of a regional office, the staff of the committee found evidence of payments to doctors and/or dentists for services which had not been rendered for which overpayments had been charged. A nationwide survey of all regional offices revealed some serious abuses. At the same time and as a part of the same publication (House Committee Print 232) a survey made at the request of the committee by the General Accounting Office showed that a substantial number of veterans were receiving hospital treatment for non-service-connected disabilities who were able to pay for such treatment despite their signing of a statement to the effect that they were unable to do so.

A survey of pensions being paid to veterans and dependents overseas indicated that over $600,000 monthly was being expended for that purpose and that 61 percent of the beneficiaries held foreign citizenship.

Reports were received from 10 States and the Federal Bureau of Prisons on veterans incarcerated for crimes were continuing to receive benefits based on the service in the Armed Forces. In order to prevent veterans serving more than 60 days for conviction of crime from receiving a non-service-connected pension, H. R. 10477 was reported by the committee as indicated above.

The discovery that large estates were building for incompetent veterans and being disbursed upon their death, in many instances to distant relatives, led the committee to report H. R. 10478 described earlier. Information which led to this proposal was obtained from a survey of each of the VA regional offices and the records maintained by the chief attorneys.
