THE CHILD WELFARE LEAGUE OF AMERICA IN NOVEMBER 1938 APPROVED MINIMUM SAFEGUARDS IN ADOPTION Safeguards for the Child: 

That he be not unnecessarily deprived of his kinship ties. 

That the family asking for him have a good home and good family life to offer and that prospective parents oe well adjusted to each other. 

That he is wanted for the purpose of completing an otherwise incomplete family group in which he will be given support, education, loving care, and the feeling of security to which any child is entitled. 

Safeguards for the Adopting Family: 

That the identity of the adopting parents should be kept from the natural parents. 

That the child have the intelligence and the physical and mental background to meet the reasonable expectations of the adoptive parents. 

That the adoption proceedings be completed without unnecessary publicity. 

Safeguards for the State:

That the adopting parents should realize that in taking the child for adoption, they assume as serious and permanent an obligation as do parents rearing their own children, including the right to inherit. 

That there be a trial period of residence of reasonable length for the best interests of the family and the child, whether there be a legal requirement for it or not. 

That the adoption procedure be sufficiently flexible to avoid encouragement of illegitimacy on the one hand and trafficking in babies on the other. 

That the birth records of an adopted child be so revised as to shield him from unnecessary embarrassment in case of illegitimacy. 967322 1

WHEN A FAMILY 

decides to adopt, regardless of whether the child was placed in their care by the Department of Public Welfare, another social agency, their lawyer, minister, doctor or whomever, they must file a petition with the court. The petition is then referred to the Department of Public Welfare to make the social investigation.

If the child was placed by the Department or another social agency, the investigation was made prior to placement. 

However, if the child was placed through other sources (which is most often the case) the Department must make its investigation after the petition to the court. 

After the Department has made the investigation, the findings are reported to the court with a recommendation as to disposition. 

The court then acts on the petition and may agree or disagree with the recommendations of the Department.

DISPOSITION BY COURTS 

The court acted upon 784 petitions to adopt 907 children. 

Ninety-five per cent of the petitions were granted. The Department of Public Welfare either did not recommend or requested a denial on three per cent of the petitions granted by the court. Twenty children were involved. (When the Department requests a denial of an adoption petition an alternate plan for care of the children is presented to the court).

The court denied one petition to adopt one child. 

This action was taken before the investigation had been completed. Thirty-four petitions to adopt 45 children were dismissed. Sixty per cent of these occurred before the investigation was completed and could have been requested by the petitioners. A third were dismissed because they had not been given a favorable recommendation after the investigation. 

However, three per cent of those dismissed by the courts had been favorably recommended after investigation.

APPENDIX

The Child Welfare Division of the Department of Public Welfare had a total staff of 53 persons to serve the people of New Mexico on December 31, 1959. Thirty-eight of these employees were county personnel directly associated with the problems of children in each community.

Child welfare workers prepared a special statistical Form #730, Adoption Case, for each child included in an adoption petition. 

These forms were designed to secure basic information regarding various phases of the situation in the adoption process. 

As schedules were completed during the year, the forms were transmitted to State Office to be edited and coded. The information was then transferred to punch cards in preparation for mechanical tabulation.
