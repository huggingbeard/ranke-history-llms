LIBER SCRIPTOREM ARRIDET 

T is not in the nature of things, I guess, for a book to talk of itself. Nor should I, a Christmas book, initiate you into the mysteries of my progeniture nor reveal the details of my metamorphosis from an embryonic protoplasm into a living reality. For me thus to disclose the intimacies of my literary conception would undoubtedly plunge my author into a kaleidoscopic vortex of confusion; the revelation would strip from him all vestige of authority and expose the inaccuracy of his definitions, his inability to spell and his ignorance of syntax. 

What a hoax! 

And what a tale I could tell of my inventor's struggles!

Often have I watched him wrestling with sentences which denied domination and laughed outright at his futile fight with phrases which defied authority. I chuckle to think how quickly literary reputation were lost did a book, in traitorous liaison with its reader, ever conspire to blister the simonized veneer of its author's learning and scrape thin the varnish of erudition which conceals perchance his illiteracy. 

But it cannot be. On the contrary I refrain from divulging the truth, recognizing that in self-defense my author must pose as one in control of the literary processes. I know that he [ 15 ]

must pretend that commas occupy their proper places automatically, that for him verbs assume with professional nonchalance the work too frequently assigned to adjectives, that in his case thoughts accommodate themselves instantaneously to an economical phraseology. In producing me the author has instinctively concealed from you the difficulties of his art and therefore I, his brainchild, must also sustain a tradition of discreet silence with respect to my provenance. 

INVENTOR EXPONET 

HE sarcastic comments so silently levelled at me on the Topening page make it fortunate indeed that my book cannot talk. Yet I suspect that were speech possible for them the volumes born this December could relate experiences unknown to the Yuletide imprints of other years and such at least would be the case with this present book. The difficulties which usually attend writing have been augmented this year by a natural hesitation as to the worthwhileness of the effort in this gasrestricted, sugar-rationed, heavily taxed world. We all have added new burdens to loads more than sufficient and our minds are weighted with anxiety concerning the war into whose bloody carnage we are sending the youth of our nation, the children of our own firesides. With proud hearts and deep humility we watch them go forth into their unknown future but we find it far from easy this year to focus our minds on the thought of "Merry Christmas."

Yet these very facts challenge us to continue the celebration. 

We are fighting at the front, we are working at home, to restore peace on earth, good will among men, and never have we needed more the message of Christmas, the spirit of Christmas, [ 16 ]

Therefore, rightly I hope, and despite crowded days and a lack of the hours necessary to the task, my Christmas book again assumes form and acquires such substance as the vapid musings of a poor author's wit have been able to devise. OME years ago we cruised northward from Punta Arenas Soa along the coast of South America, finally to reach the Santos River and sail up that narrow estuary to Brazil's great coffee port. An hour before dawn the ship quit the Atlantic

Ocean to enter the fissure in the continent leading to our haven, but despite the darkness the equatorial heat made such a furnace of my stateroom that after ineffectual efforts to sleep I finally went on deck.

A faint glow in the East suggested furtively that Apollo was in the nether stables harnessing his chariot for the daily race across the sky, and a preview of the oncoming spectacle inspired the photographer to go below again for his camera. 

How seldom you and I wake to view the pageant of the dawn or to experience the excitement which attends the rising of the Sun. With the first glow of the footlights the show is on. Up from the stage rise the heralds of light, streaking their way into the sky to cry the event while the cast of principal clouds in gorgeous raiment gathers to celebrate the approach of the great orb himself. Majestically the fiery star makes his entrance up stage center and heads straight for the rabble clouds who conspire to block his progress and prolong the night. Steadily he pushes his way into their midst, flinging his torches hither and yon to set the very vapors afire as he brings to a waiting world the light of a newborn day.

Beneath that roof one comprehends Abraham Lincoln better; there the great, simple truths of life become real-those truths which you and I know yet perhaps wish to forget. For we all enjoy our advantages and hesitate to admit to ourselves that poverty brings self discipline, that absence of comforts fortifies courage, that lack of education in no way lessens honesty, that clear thinking derives not solely from cherished prerogatives. 

These truths affirm themselves with undeniable authority within the Lincoln cabin.

Outside it, on the soil hallowed as the birthplace of the great Liberator, we stood with bared heads watching the Memorial's flag fold and unfold in the breeze. And it seemed to us that its undulations, like telegraphers' dots and dashes, were releasing a message into the ether, broadcasting a notice that America approaches at last the fulfillment of its destiny.
