PREFACE Ancient and Modern Agriculture is the first published book to show clearly the relationship between the crude agricultural practices of Biblical times and our modern scientific farming. Present-day farm implements and land policies are believed by many to be of recent origin. The author of this book, however, uses illuminating quotations from the earliest history of mankind, the 

Bible, to prove that modern agriculture is but one step in the evolution of a science which was practiced by the Ancients. The implements employed were crude, yet they served the needs of their times as efficiently as our modern farm machinery serves us. The book emphasizes the fact that agriculture has its basis in the Laws of Nature, and that the Laws of Nature do not change. 

Wild and domestic animals play an essential role in successful farming. Part II of 

Ancient and Modern Agriculture deals with this subject. Although many present-day writers and biologists maintain that game conservation is of modern origin, this book proves that the ancients in Biblical times practiced wild life conservation and had laws protecting game and regulating its usage. vii

A part of the manuscript of this book has been in use for some thirty years, and was published as a bulletin by the Agricultural and Mechanical College of Texas in 1923. County agents, theologians, and numerous others used this manuscript so extensively that the author was persuaded to expand the original material and publish it in book form. Ancient and Modern Agri culture should prove an invaluable aid to students of agriculture, rural pastors, and farmers; to those engaged in the professional phases of agriculture; and to all who are interested in the advancement and perpetuation of civilization.

The editor feels that it has been a great privilege to have had a part in preparing this book for publication.

FOREWORD 

Throughout the entire Bible we have a complete history of the agricultural activities of the people. There are more references made to, more laws governing, and more advice given concerning agriculture than any other occupation of man. Most of the parables that Christ gave were based upon agricultural truths. Many of the comparisons made in the Bible, illustrating the spiritual life of the people, concerned some phase of agriculture. The Creator placed Adam and Eve in a favored spot to farm. After their sin they were expelled from the Garden of Eden, and man became a nomad with his herds and flocks. There could be no advancement because there could be no time for thought or duties other than that of tending herds and flocks. When man discovered the art, or followed the practice, of producing food for himself and feed for livestock in excess of immediate needs, he was no longer nomadic in life and then had time to think of other things. It was then, and not until then, that the embryo of civilization started to take form.

Civilization and religion, as we know them, are inseparable. Christianity and civilization either go forward hand in hand, or to- 

XV

gether decline. The degree of maintenance or advancement of civilization has always been in direct ratio to the agricultural status of the people. The ancient prophets recognized this fact and recorded basic argricultural principles in the scriptures. Successful agriculture depends upon the recognition and the application of the laws of nature. The laws of nature are the laws of God. Moses recognized this, and to more fully emphasize the thought, he spoke of these laws as the direct commands of Jehovah. The ancient 

Romans recognized this fact and recorded these same basic principles. Jehovah is still commanding us. The agricultural students of today are talking and writing the same basic principles, commanded by Jehovah. xvi

WHY? 

Recently at a large meeting of churchmen, one pastor addressed the meeting and stated that if the rural church were saved it would be through agriculture. Probably few who heard him or read his words through news reports realized the truth of this statement. 

All the major and minor prophets from Moses down to the birth of Christ taught agriculture, and most of their spiritual illustrations were in terms of agriculture. 

Christ followed with his agricultural parables, and his disciples and other writers of the New Testament followed the same path. 

The Lord's chosen people, the Israelites, were farm people-Nomadic in the beginning, then settled farmers in Canaan. The prophets and preachers of old realized that religion must be taught in terms of the people's life and not in theory.

The church bemoans the fact that the rural church is in decay. Many theories are advanced to explain the cause. It is claimed that the automobile and good roads have been the prime factor, and that people attend church in town rather than in their community. Granting this to be true, it can only be that they find something more interesting in the chuch in town than they xvii

would find in their community services. 

Some claim that the rural section is growing more wicked and less interested in religion. Granting this to be true, if they have grown less interested it has been because their religious leader has been unable to teach them along lines that would enable them to help solve the many problems of life.

We must gain and maintain the life interest of the people if we expect to lead or go with them along a brighter path.

People today, especially the rural people, are looking for leaders who understand their problems and who teach a religion, as did the ancient prophets and Christ, that will help them in their daily life as well as in spiritual matters. The ancient prophets, and later Christ, taught in terms of daily life so that their hearers could better understand the spiritual side. They knew that a man suffering physically from want of food and raiment, or mentally from some oppression, was not a good subject to accept and practice the Golden Rule.

As time goes on, we find fewer and fewer people who accept the selfish view of saving his own soul while the "rest of the world goes by." We have fewer and fewer people who can be scared out of Hell into Heaven. 

We have fewer and fewer people who thrill at the prospect of sitting on the edge xviii

of a fleecy cloud and picking a harp throughout eternity.

The people of today are looking for a life that can be lived in Christian harmony with their fellow man, cooperating in all God's laws and in harmony with all God's creation for the advancement of civilization and Christianity through their life's work. 

The people of today are looking for rural Christian leaders who have the vision, initiative and force of character to lead them into 

God's kingdom.

No one has as great an opportunity for good today as does the rural pastor. The 

Christian religion has always had, and will always have, the strongest advocates among people who are able to feed and clothe themselves. Farmers, as a class, come nearer having a sufficiency of the necessities of life than any other class of people. Therefore, the religious tendency in the rural community is greater than in the towns and cities. Then again, it may be stated that the farmer's success depends almost entirely upon the laws of nature, God's laws. These facts put him in closer harmony with God.

This book in no wise exhausts the texts and references in the Bible pertaining to agriculture. If it points the way to guide the rural pastors in helping to revive the rural church, and if it teaches that the only type of farming that has ever succeeded has been xix

that which has followed divine laws and teachings, then my labors will have been well spent. XX

FOOD AND FEED PROVIDED FOR MAN AND BEAST BEFORE THEY WERE CREATED 

From the beginning of time, man and beast have depended upon the products of the soil for life. Throughout the early history of mankind, these products were gathered by the roving tribes. The population migrated, with their herds, to the points where food for man and feed for beasts were most abundant. As population increased, the vast hordes of people and their herds began to deplete the free range, and it became necessary that localized stable efforts be made to produce food. It is a well-established fact that the successful farmers are those who make preparations to feed themselves and family and their livestock before thought is given to produce crops for sale. This fact was clearly established when the Creator brought forth vegetation for beast and fruit for man before beast and man were created. Grass, to the early wandering tribes, meant abundant range and fat herds and flocks. At a later date, when the tribes were more or less stabilized in a community and the herds 1

confined to definite areas, grass meant abundant pasture. Several thousand years after, the Lord "brought forth grass"; yet, before the birth of Christ, somesome asked the Roman Cato, "What is the most certain crop that can be grown with profit?" He replied, "A good pasture." He was then asked the next best crop, whereupon he replied, "A pretty good pasture." Grass is essential to production of livestock and is the basis of a good pasture. It was recognized by the Lord, recognized by Cato, and is also recognized by the agricultural students of today.

All Classes Served by Agriculture 

The acre value of farm crops is the business barometer of the nation. The rise and fall of acre value of farm crops throughout 2

the nation is the gauge that measures the standards of living in the American farm home. The social, moral, and financial standing of the small towns throughout the country is entirely dependent upon the farmers' labor income in its trade territory; and, in turn, business in the large cities is dependent upon the demand of the small trade centers. The stability of the Government depends upon the comfort and contentment of its people. Comfort and contentment of the people depend upon their ability to procure and maintain the necessities, comforts, and conveniences of life. 

From every angle of argument, we come back to the fact that the sustenance and comfort of every living individual is dependent upon the continued abundant production of the land. It has been stated many times, by eminent historians, that the fall of Greece and the fall of the Roman Empire were due to agricultural decay. Dependent Upon Divine Providence 

There are two fundamental laws that govern agriculture. The first is the law of Nature which was laid down in the beginning by the Creator. The second is the 

Law of Economics. The first of these laws is fixed and must be cooperated with and studied for the advancement of the individual and society. The Laws of Nature is for the benefit, happiness, and prosperity of all. It works hardships only on those who disobey or refuse to heed its stability. The Law of Economics, as related to agriculture, is governed and controlled, in a large measure, by the Laws of Nature. The man who produces a bale of cotton per acre has little complaint to make as to the price of cotton. If his cow produces 800 pounds of butter fat per year, he will make some profit. The man who produces only one-tenth of a bale of cotton on one acre of land, and the man whose cow produces only 100 pounds of butter fat in a year cannot succeed, regardless of the price of these products. Although these

facts having been established centuries ago, we still have those among us who disobey the Laws of Nature and are financially, physically, and morally lost through the action of the Law of Economics.

Redemption of Land 

Contrary to the general opinion, Moses' plan of redemption of land was not a general re-distribution of wealth. The land was not to be sold. His was a 50-year lease system and those who bought property, "without the walled city" paid in

terms of the number of years until "Jubilee." If he "bought" for the 50-year period, it was so recorded. If he "bought" for a ten-year period (ten years until the year of "Jubilee"), he only paid lease for the onefifth time. A man who "sold" could redeem upon an appraised or arbitrated value, or at the expiration of the time from bought (leased) to Jubilee. There was an agreement that the final settlement was based upon the condition of the property as based upon arbitration. Moses seemed little concerned in regard to disposition of property or property rights within the walled city. It would seem that his view was similar to the modern view in that if the agricultural interest prospered, the city would be able to survive. 

Lev. 25:25 "thy brother be brother be waxen poor, and hath sold . . . some of his possessions, and if any of his kin come to redeem it, then shall he redeem that which his brother has sold."

PRACTICES ESSENTIAL TO SUC- CESSFUL FARMING Pure Types 

The laws in regard to pure types, as laid down by Moses, were broad and rigid in their scope. The purity and non-amalgamation with other races is more pronounced in the Jews than in other races. Moses believed, and modern thinkers concur, that purity of type in race, livestock, and farm crops, was essential to permanent success. While this is generally true, yet we know that the mingling of other blood is sometimes beneficial. The superb character of Ruth contributed to, rather than detracted from, the Hebrew race. We can cite many instances in which strains have been improved by select- 15

ed cross breeding. However, in all efforts the object has been to establish and maintain a pure and stable strain. 

Pure-bred livestock has been developed for high production along special lines. 

This is true in beef cattle, dairy cattle, hogs, sheep, poultry, and all other classes of livestock. Breeding and raising pure type livestock creates a pride in the individual that tends toward better citizenship. Purebred animals cost no more to produce than scrubs, and they bring a better price when offered for sale. The production of desirable pure type livestock soon leads to a reputation that insures a ready and satisfactory market for all animals offered for sale. The best goat hair was from the breed of Goshen.

Pure type adapted varieties of farm crops produce a higher acre yield of uniform product that commands a higher unit price on the market. The pride taken by the individual from the production of pure types makes for better citizenship that cannot be measured in dollars.

Rotate Crops

It is absolutely essential to permanent agriculture that some system of crop rotation be established. Rotation may be based upon a succession of different crops grown or allowing the fields to remain idle and grow up with natural vegetation to be turned. Manuring the fields serves the same purpose as rotation. Livestock farming aids in crop rotation and supplies manure to be used on the land. Many references are made throughout the Bible in regard to letting the land lie fallow. The ancient Roman writers were strong believers in crop rotation and all other methods of land improvement. 

Lev. 25:3, 4-"Six years thou shalt sow thy field... But in the seventh year shall be a sabbath of rest unto the land,

Grow Legumes 

The legumes grown during the days of the ancient Prophets were vetches, peas and beans. They were often planted with other crops and sometimes planted as special crops. They entered into their system of crop rotation and soil building, and were 17

also used for human food and for livestock feed. Later, the early Roman farmers added alfalfa and possibly other legume crops in their system of farming. The recognized value of legumes for soil building and for food and feed dates from the beginning of agricultural operations.

Prepare Land 

Regardless of the high fertility of soil, the maximum yields cannot be obtained unless the soil is prepared to make an ideal home for the plant to grow. This ideal home cannot be obtained except through preparation of the soil. A well-prepared soil increases the feeding area of the plant roots, it absorbs more moisture, it liberates plant food more readily, it can be cultivated with less labor, and it destroys weeds before the crop is planted, thus decreasing cost of cultivation. 

Solomon recognized that fall and winter preparation of soil was desirable. This is in general, a fundamental fact accepted by all.

Produce and Feed Livestock 

Aside from a few minor overflow areas where sediment is deposited, there has never been any permanent system of agriculture established without livestock. Diversification, crop rotation, and the growing of legumes for grazing and for hay, enters into livestock farming. Farming does not differ from any other business in that its success depends upon some constant and continuous source of revenue to meet at least a part of 19

the operating expenses as they occur. Livestock farming produces something to sell each day in the year. It also furnishes an abundance of wholesome food for the household at all times. Livestock and their products decrease the cash output for groceries and also return a cash income in addition to revenue derived from the sale of cash crops.

The agricultural history of the world is a continuous record of financial ruin of communities and individuals when the agricultural operations were based entirely upon field crop production. Soil depletion and financial ruin is hastened and more complete, when agricultural operations are based upon a one-crop system. It is also recorded how these same inhabitants turned to livestock farming and built a permanent and profitable system of agriculture, or continued to depend upon farm crops as their total revenue until they destroyed the fertility of the land, came to financial failure, and destroyed the home of their people. Every county and every community in our nation has its farms and farmers who practice livestock farming, and without exception, they are the most prosperous farmers in the section.

These facts have proven true throughout all time. Then it was no mere incident that in the beginning God created the system of farming with crops and livestock. When Isaiah 20

Cowman and Sheepherder

The enmity that in some sections still exists between the sheepherder and the cowman is recorded in the earliest biblical records.

Pharaoh enforced the ever normal granary by "Let Pharaoh do this, and let him appoint officers over the land, and take up the fifth part of the land of Egypt in the seven plenteous years." (Gen. 41:34). 

In Genesis 41:48, it is recorded that Joseph did follow out the suggestion. This really meant a tax on the people because the grain was later sold to the people to prevent famine in the land. This scheme worked with such success that Joseph decreed (Gen. 

48:26) that Pharaoh should have the fifth part "unto this day." This fifth part is the first record we have of direct taxation. It is a debatable question as to whether this was a tax and Pharaoh allowed the Egyptians to retain the land, or Pharaoh kept the land and the one-fifth was rent.

The first tax that Moses imposed upon the people was to raise money for the tabernacle, including all pay for the priests and upkeep of the tribe of Levi. This was a tithe of livestock and other products. In ad- 34

dition to the taxation by tithing, further revenue was raised by direct money payment of taxes. Later, after the Hebrews were settled in Canaan, an additional tax was levied for the kings. This tax included the upkeep of the government, yet much was used by the king to enhance his individual wealth.

The best example was the wealth of King 

Solomon. King "Solomon had twelve officers over all Israel" to collect taxes, "every man according to his charge." Solomon used these taxes for his own household, to build his temple, and to wall several cities. 

There was also an enforced military service for one month each year; this increased the people's burden. Import duties were paid. 

These import duties were exceedingly high on some commodities where monopoly was practiced. The people in outlying provinces were taxed in higher proportion than were the people living in the provinces where the seat of government was located. Tolls and tributes were collected at the gates of cities, harbors, piers, and all other places where money could be taken from the people. 

When cities or provinces were captured by other tribes, a heavy tribute (war debt) was levied against the people. This tax burden became so heavy that the people could not pay. When this time came, taxes of individuals, cities, and provinces were put up to 35

the highest bidder and sold. At times, the unscrupulous bidder would pay far more than the actual taxes, then by force or other methods, collect sufficient amount to return him profit. This may or may not be comparable to our present day candidate who spends money to get into office far in excess of the legitimate amount that the position will pay. It is recorded in Nehemiah 

5:4 that the people were found to mortgage their lands to raise money to pay taxes. The same procedure is followed today.

Wages paid those who worked were agricultural products or other considerations, including money. DOMESTIC ANIMALS 

All domestic animals known to us at this time were used by the ancients. The mule was known only in the wild state until after Moses' time, because the breeding of hybrids of all descriptions was prohibited under his law. Mohair was used very extensively in clothing, curtains, etc.

The curtains of the Tabernacle were made of spun goat hair. Before leaving Goshen, the Hebrews had developed a superior strain of goats, bred especially for their quality of hair. These were known as "Goats of Goshen breed." This breed would compare favorably with our Angora of today.

GAME AND WILD ANIMALS OF THE SCRIPTURES 

The domesticated animals used in Bible times have been known, mentioned and recorded for thousands of years. Very little notice or attention has been given to the wild species that roamed the Arabian desert, the Wilderness, and Palestine. Some of these wild animals were domesticated, some were hunted for food, and some predatory species were killed for the protection of man and his flocks and herds. The struggle for existence was too great to develop the sportsman that we have today. What hunting the ancients did was for self-preservation, either for food or from fear, and done with crude, unreliable weapons. 

The first chapter of Genesis sets out very clearly the intention of the All Wise Creator.

He created the Heaven and earth, then created plants for the animals. He then created all animals except man. Thus the plan was set up, all details perfected for man to live within the Laws of Nature, God's Laws, and be happy and prosperous. The Lord then created man and gave him dominion over all. He made man trustee of all His creations. It was intended that man should use 127

these creations, game, fish and other natural resources, for his benefit and for the advancement of society and for the advancement of God's Kingdom on earth.

He who is wasteful and dissipates the food animals and other natural resources that God created for all generations of man, commits a sin. God intended that all natural resources should be for the benefit of all mankind throughout all time. Most people have conformed to God's Laws in regard to wildlife. State and Federal laws have been passed in an attempt to force the desstructive individual to conform to Nature's Laws.

Manna from the trees, quail from the forest, and other forms of wildlife fed the wandering Hebrews; wild grass and wild herbs sustained their flocks. Wild game in the United States played a very prominent part in the early history of its development. Wildlife in every country has played its part as a provider of food for the people. That is as God intended. 

Shall we let God's wildlife creatures vanish from the face of the earth, or shall we protect them and regulate the use of them according to God's plan and according to the Laws of our State? 128

THE HUNTER 

There were professional hunters and fishermen among the ancients. Most of them fished and hunted to sell to the market. The kings and others of high rank maintained skilled hunters and fishermen to keep their household supplied with wild game. Solomon's table was well supplied. 

Gen. 27:5, 30; 25:27; 10:9-Isa. 26:20 

-Jere. 16:16-Prov. 12:27. 

Hunting, and defense from predatory animals was carried on with much the same weapons as were used in the prosecution of war including the bow and arrow, spear, and club. Some hunters were expert with the sling. David killed a lion with his sling. Pits for trapping wild animals were used extensively. The greyhound was bred and trained to run antelope and deer. Snares were used to catch all types of animals. 

Birds were hunted with the same type weapons as were other animals. The smaller type weapons, as bow and arrow, were generally used. Bird snares were common.

One form of net was that with spreading wings into which the quail and partridge were driven. A trap propped up with a stick was also used. The trap was baited with food and often tame birds were used 133

as decoys. When the wild birds got under the trap, the hunter, in hiding, pulled the cord that let the trap fall.

FISH 

Fish have formed one source of human food throughout all time. There are no less than forty species in the lakes, ponds and streams in Palestine. Some fourteen of these are peculiar to that region. The chief fish used for food in Palestine are the Carp (Rabeobarbus canis), a sun fish (Chromis nilotica) and a catfish (clarias macracanthus). Moses called unclean all fish but those with scales and fins. During the time of Moses, tribes other than the Hebrews ate catfish. Since Moses passed on, even the 

Hebrews recognize them as good food. 

Fish abound in the Sea of Galilee where, at the present time, there is a tax imposed on all fish caught. This is comparable to our fishing license. Fish were eaten fresh and salt cured. The Savior was served broiled fish; the fish He served the multitude were no doubt salt cured and dried. Fish were bought and sold. The fish market was known as the fish gate. Fish were caught with hook and line. Job, in illustrating that you should be well prepared for the task at hand, made the statement that you could 134

not catch a crocodile with a fish hook because the line would not hold him. Artificial ponds and lakes were provided for fish. 

The ancients also recognized that fish food must be abundant. There is evidence that at certain times, or under certain conditions, it was unlawful to catch fish with seines, or to catch birds with snares.

TO GOD'S LAND OF WHICH WE HAVE POSSESSION 

From the depth of your bosom, we get precious gems to crown our queens. We get precious metals that form the basis of national and individual wealth. 

We get iron, zinc, copper, and other ores for our manufacturing enterprises. We get oil, gas, and coal for fuel to help carry on the commerce of the world. 

From your subterranean streams, we get drink that sustains life. From your rock beds and majestic trees that are grown from your storehouse of food, we built monuments to you of justice, education, worship, business, and pleasure. The process of reproduction of life that takes place on your surface is, and shall remain, the greatest miracle of all time. 

From the embryo plant that nestles on your surface is grown food and raiment for man and food that nourishes the birds and beasts of the field. The streams that flow through the miniature clefts in your surface furnish 151

us channels of commerce, recreation, and food. The beauties of your mountains, streams, plains, and forests have never been reproduced by painter or sculptor, nor have they adequately been described by the poet. 

We will strive to maintain you in the original beauty and the virgin fertility that you were when God delivered you to us. 

You are the gift of God to man. 

Of you, God made man. 

God gave man dominion over you. 

From you, man receives life. 

In death, to you man returns.
