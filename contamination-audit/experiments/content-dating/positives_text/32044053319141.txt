II. SUPPORTING ASSISTANCE FUNDS ARE FULLY PROGRAMED NO ADDITIONAL FUNDS AVAILABLE 

Supporting assistance funds estimated to be available for fiscal year 1966 total $386 million. This includes the fiscal year 1966 appropriation for supporting assistance of $369.2 million. These funds are now fully programed. Over half have been programed for use in Vietnam.

AID has already "borrowed" $36 million from essential supporting assistance programs and programs financed by the Special Southeast Asia Contingency Fund. In addition $27.7 million has been temporarily transferred to supporting assistance from funds appropriated for voluntary contributions for international organizations. It will be necessary to replenish these programs from the supplemental funds now requested. Other funds are not available to meet these needs.

III. ADDITIONAL FISCAL YEAR 1966 SUPPORTING 

A. Vietnam, $275 million

REQUIREMENTS ASSISTANCE 

Additional economic assistance requirements to support military and civil programs in South Vietnam are now immediate and urgent. 

There are two main elements to these requirements: (1) Commodity imports to combat inflation, $175 million.—To cope with severe inflationary pressures which threaten economic and political stability in South Vietnam, the United States and the Government of Vietnam must expand the financing of commercial imports. An additional $175 million of supporting assistance funds is required to finance the U.S. share of the costs of the necessary rise in imports.

The expanded AID program in Vietnam entails increased administrative expenses. AID's current estimate is that approximately $1.4 million will be required to meet recruitment costs and pay for other administrative and support services. Therefore, authority is requested to use supporting assistance funds for administrative expenses incurred only in connection with Vietnam programs. This authority would require a determination by the President that such a transfer is necessary, which determination would be reported to the Congress.

B. Thailand, $7.5 million 3 Communist subversive activities in Thailand have increased since the time of AID's initial fiscal year 1966 appropriations request. It is likely that the insurgency threat will become more serious. An additional $7.5 million in supporting assistance funds is required for expanded border and civil police operations, increased rural development activities, potable water facilities for the threatened northeast, and for improved air traffic management in upland areas. 

C. Laos, $7.5 million

An additional $7.5 million in supporting assistance funds is required to assist Laos in stabilizing Government control of contested areas, to support a higher level of airlift and refugee operations in northern Laos, and to help improve Government control in southern Laos through a rural development "village cluster" project. 

D. Dominican Republic, $25 million

As a result of severe economic and political instability following the April 1965 revolution, additional supporting assistance funds for the Dominican Government are necessary to— (1) Help the provisional government create and maintain a stable environment conducive to the election of a democratic government in June, and (2) Help the provisional government meet its current obligations so that the new government is not faced with an immediate financial crisis which would threaten its existence. 

The additional $25 million is needed to cover the gap between essential operating and capital budget costs of the Dominican Government and reduced tax revenues, which fell off because of political unrest. 

IV. ADDITIONAL REQUIREMENTS FOR FISCAL YEAR 1966 CONTINGENCY FUND, $100 MILLION 

Contingency fund current availabilities for fiscal year 1966 total $54 million including an appropriation of $50 million. All available funds for fiscal year 1966 have now been programed. 

An additional $100 million in fiscal year 1966 contingency funds is requested to permit AID to meet any unforeseen requirements which may arise in southeast Asia, the Dominican Republic, or other potentially volatile areas around the world. For example, it may be necessary to draw on the contingency fund to insure the flow of essential goods into Zambia or to provide emergency relief in countries which suffer natural disasters.

V. TRANSFER OF MAP FUNDS FOR VIETNAM 

The President has also requested, as part of his supplemental request for funds for the Department of Defense, the transfer of costs of operations in Vietnam formerly funded from the military assistance program to the budget of the Department of Defense. 

The purpose of this request is to increase efficiency in the operations and financial and logistical system of the defensive effort in Vietnam. 

It also is designed to maintain the clarity of purpose of the military assistance program, which was not designed to support forces engaged in a major, continuing conflict.

B.  TO FURTHER AMEND THE FOREIGN ASSISTANCE ACT OF 1961, AS AMENDED, TO AUTHORIZE SUPPLEMENTAL APPROPRIATIONS FOR FIS- CAL YEAR 1966 1. Purposes of the bill I. INTRODUCTION 

The basic purpose of the proposed act ("the bill") is to amend the Foreign Assistance Act of 1961, as amended ("the act"), in order to authorize supplemental appropriations urgently needed in connection with increased requirements for economic assistance in the Republic of Vietnam and elsewhere. In addition, the bill authorizes transfer of funds for administrative expenses incurred in connection with the increased economic assistance in the Republic of Vietnam.

II. PROVISION OF THE BILL PART I CHAPTER 4-SUPPORTING ASSISTANCE AUTHORIZATION 

Section 1(a). Amends section 402 of the act, which relates to supporting assistance, by striking out the fiscal year 1966 authorization and substituting a figure ($684,200,000) which is the sum of the present authorization for fiscal year 1966 ($369,200,000) plus the additional amount now requested to be authorized ($315 million). 

Section 1(b). Amends section 402 of the act to permit authorization of appropriations for expenses authorized by that section without regard to section 649 of the act. Section 649 of the act limits the aggregate of the total amounts authorized to be appropriated for use

during fiscal year 1966 for furnishing assistance and for administrative expenses under the act to $3.360 billion. Of this amount, $3.218 billion has been appropriated for use during fiscal year 1966. Thus, this provision is required to authorize the additional appropriation for supporting assistance.

CHAPTER 5-CONTINGENCY FUND AUTHORIZATION 

Section 2. Amends section 451(a) of the act, which relates to the contingency fund, by striking out the fiscal year 1966 authorization and substituting a figure ($150 million) which is the sum of the present authorization for fiscal year 1966 ($50 million) plus the amount requested to be authorized at this time ($100 million). 

PART III CHAPTER 2-ADMINISTRATIVE PROVISIONS ADMINISTRATIVE EXPENSES OF AID 

Section 3. This section authorizes use of supporting assistance funds made available pursuant to section 1 of the bill for administrative expenses authorized by section 637 (a) of the act which are incurred in connection with programs in the Republic of Vietnam. Absent this authority, section 610(b) of the act would not permit such a transfer. 

The Agency for International Development estimates that approximately $1.4 million will be needed in fiscal year 1966 to meet the additional administrative expenses arising from the increased economic assistance programs in the Republic of Vietnam. As required by section 610(a) of the act, a Presidential determination will be necessary to transfer funds from the supporting assistance account to the administrative expense account.
