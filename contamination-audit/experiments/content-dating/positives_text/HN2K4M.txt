STUDENT EXPENSE UNIVERSITY CHARGES “FROM THE UNIVERSITY BULLETIN” 

The University reserves the right to alter any of these charges without notice. 

TUITION. No tuition is charged to residents of Wisconsin except in the Library School* ($25 per semester for residents and $50 for non-residents), and the Wisconsin High School ($8 per quarter for residents and $15 for non-residents); a fee of $100 per semester is charged to students who are non-residents of this State (see exemptions below).

The following students are exempt from non-resident tuition: 

1. Any adult student who has been a resident of the State for one year next preceding his first admission to the University, and as long as he continues to be a resident of the State; 

2. Any adult student who has entered the University and paid non-resident tuition for four academic years, and has become and continues to be a resident of this State; 

3. Any adult student who has paid non-resident tuition for one semester or more and has continuously been a resident of this State for the four-year period following his admission to the University, and so long as he continues to be a resident of this State; 

4. Any minor student whose parents have been bona fide residents of this State for one year next preceding the beginning of any semester for which the student registers in the University. 

NON-RESIDENT TUITION. The regular non-resident tuition is $200 per academic year, or $100 per semester.

The Regents of the University, acting under the provisions of Section 36.16 (c) Laws of Wisconsin, 1937, have established, as an experiment for the academic year 1938-39, the following departures from the standard $200 annual non-resident tuition, for students who are residents of other states maintaining a university: 

That the annual fee be reduced from $200 to (a) the reciprocal fee (the fee charged by the other state university to Wisconsin residents), if the reciprocal fee is less than $200, or (b) to $100, whichever is higher, For Undergraduates (1) who have had at least one year's work on full programs at the University of Wisconsin, and (2) have earned a scholastic average of two or more grade-points per credit on full programs during their last year at the University of Wisconsin; and

For Law, Medical, and Graduate Students 

(1) who enter the University of Wisconsin from accredited institutions with a scholastic average of two or more grade-points per credit on all their work at the other institutions; or (2) have made a scholastic average of two or more grade-points per credit (or its equivalent in the case of the Law School) in their last year's work on full programs at the University of Wisconsin; provided, that any subject work at other accredited institutions has been of like quality.

FEES AND DEPOSITS. Each student is required to pay a general fee of $27.50 per semester, covering incidentals, infirmary service, Memorial Union membership, registration and use of the gymnasium. 

Students who are paid-up life members of the Memorial Union are entitled to a deduction of five dollars from the general fee. See Summer Session and College of Agriculture for special fees charged in these divisions. The laboratory fees and deposits charged in the various subjects are stated in the announcements of the Departments of Instruction. A library deposit of $2.00 is required of each student when he first matriculates. A fee of $5.00 is charged for each graduation, payable at the beginning of the semester in which the student expects to complete the residence requirements for his degree or degrees. A professional fee is required of students registered in the Schools of Law and Medicine: $12.50 per semester in Law and $25 per semester in Medicine. Students registered for the degree of Bachelor of Music are also required to pay a professional fee of $30 per semester which entitles the student to one private and one class lesson per week in applied music. Fees are not refundable, except as specified below under Refunds; deposits cover loss of or damage to equipment and are refundable in whole or in part.

STUDENT LIFE-MEMORIAL UNION 

"Much of the life and activity of students outside the classroom is concentrated in the University's 'living room,' the Memorial Union, a splendid building erected and equipped at a cost of $1,250,000, largely through the generosity of more than 18,000 students, alumni, faculty, and friends of the University. Among the physical facilities of the Union are: Cafeteria, large and small dining rooms, grill room, and lunchrooms; rooms for games, music, committee meetings, and assemblies; quarters for student publications and clubs; combined ballroom and banquet hall; library; art gallery; spacious lounge; barber shop; writing room; checkrooms; alumni offices; and lodgings for transient alumni and parents of students. By virtue of this wide range of facilities, the Memorial Union opens to the University community heretofore unequalled opportunities for social gatherings of all types." "The student house committees working with the Union staff provide students with countless social and cultural functions in the building which are free; it is unnecessary, therefore, for a student to budget very much money for recreation at the University. Included among the regular free Union programs are: 

Open houses, Sunday concerts by well-known artists, weekly moving pictures for men and for women, weekly matinee dances, weekly coffee hours, open forums, art exhibitions and lectures, game tournaments, dancing lessons, a handicraft workshop, and hobby classes, winter sports parties and skiing instruction, women's teas and men's stags, and phonograph symphony concerts." 10

WHAT HAPPENED? 

In the flush days prior to 1929 and thereafter, pressure groups, obtained from the stateas though it were a "Santa Claus"-large statutory percentages, of its important state revenue taxes, for the use of "Santa Claus'" children-the cities, villages, counties and towns.

Did "Santa Claus," when he began to feel his lack of cash in 1923, or thereafter, call his children around him and notify them he had made a destructive bargain and that he must cut down their allowances and percentages? Oh! No! Political cowardice overcame him and he took the easiest road. He began:

State taxes in bank. He is now winding up, after eight years, on June 30, 1939, with an estimated amount of only $5,000,000. He put off his habits, of the honest honey bee and took on those of a voracious grasshopper-the grasshopper has eaten up $14,000,000 cash-payment on deficit account- 2. 1. Remedy for the 35 Million Dollars accumulated deficit is to cancel by statute all of the 

35 Million Dollars deficit except the amount owing to the Teachers Retirement Fund. 

2. Remedy for the 22 Million Dollars deficit for the next two years:

A. Recapture the motor taxes of the Highway Department and bring highway appropriations directly under the Legislature. If the Legislature cuts the University appropriations approximately 1 Million Dollars for the biennium, at least that percentage to its total appropriation, can fairly be the test rule, in application to the cuts in the recaptured motor taxes and no complaint can be had by any citizen because it affects all alike, and

B. Cut the present statutory appropriations and allotments to cities, villages, counties and towns on the basis of the University percentage cut, and C. In a word apply the University percentage cut to the total of: (1) Highway appropriations. 

(2) Statutory appropriations (of the Children of Santa Claus) and the University so as to destroy the deficit for the next two years by recapturing and keeping in the State Treasury an additional 12 Million or 13 Million Dollars per year.

"In an address given at Brown University in 1937, Mr. John D. Since then the subject has been referred to occasionally when methods of increasing college revenue were being considered.

THE MATTER IS

CASH TUITION 

(1) The Resident Student "GENERAL FEE” should be raised to $90—one-half payable at the beginning of each semester. All other fees now in force, remain unchanged. This will increase the University revenue $338,975 annually. (2) The sons and daughters of non-resident alumni be classed as resident students. (3) Students from outlying territories and countries should be included in the class of resident students. (4) Non-resident students for regular and graduate courses should, without further fees, be $200-one-half payable at the beginning of each semester, and be granted the same rights and privileges accorded resident students-A bonafide requirement of 5 years residence prior to entrance of University, by non-residents, before classed as residents, should be required. PROVISION FOR ALUMNI FUNDS ADJUSTED COMPENSATION PAID TO THE STATE BY 25,000 GRADUATES 

Suggest that the Wisconsin Alumni Association, in conjunction with and under the direction of the state governing board of the University, seriously proceed to make and direct an organized approach, to the 25,000 graduates of the University for an adjusted compensation to the State, for the priviliges conferred upon them by their Alma Mater-$250 a year for four years-$1,000, no interest. The approach is to a field of 25,000 intelligent men and women, to whom the rights of the University have never been really presented. A maximum opportunity of a financial return over a period, of 25 Million Dollars-a minimum opportunity of 5 Million Dollars-the approach is to a large, untapped reservoir of men and women loyal to the future of the University. Cash or notes over a period of ten years. Interest at 1% on notes. In order to facilitate collection, both payable at State Treasurer's office. Alumni funds for University buildings, equipment and research, to be spent under direction of the 

Governing Board. 

Preparation for the approach, suggest the Governing Board promptly and carefully survey the building and equipment needs of the University for the next ten years, based on a maximum enrollment of 11,500 Regular Session Students. At the same time, make a detailed report, on the financial needs of research, in all departments, for a period of twenty years, so that loyal graduates may see the needs. Again suggest that the Governing Board promptly investigate and establish a system of Bookkeeping at the University to disclose actualities and exactness of all phases of its operation. Today, the Governor, the Legislature, and the press can not ascertain the fact and truth concerning the cost to the State of regular, and non-resident students; the cost per unit of operation of the several departments and many other important factors. The cause of the University was impaired before this legislature by its failure to present data, in simple form, both in its public records of operations and in other data submitted. It was apparently, due to a defective system of Bookkeeping, and a failure to observe the rules of simple arithmetic. Pressure group action and propaganda will not protect the University in the future.

IN CONCLUSION 

The University is entitled to a continuous management in a Board of Governors, which would instantly gain the confidence and good will of the people of Wisconsin. 

It is suggested the legislature create a Board of Governors, to be known as The Board of Governors of the University of Wisconsin-to consist of nine members as follows: 

The Chief Justice of the State during his term of office. 

A U. S. District Judge Alternating between the Western and Eastern U. S. Districts of Wisconsin for six year terms. First designation made by the Governor. 

A resident Wisconsin alumnus from its graduate women. 29

A resident Wisconsin alumnus from its graduate men, both selected for a term of six years, by a written ballot, submitted after nomination in manner provided by the Directors of the Wisconsin Alumni Association. The submission of vote to the alumni and the tally be made by the State Superintendent of Public Schools. 

A State Circuit Judge from Wisconsin for a term of six years, subject to his tenancy in office. Elected by the Circuit Judges, by vote in writing under the direction of the 

State Superintendent of Schools. 

The State Superintendent of Schools, to hold during his term of office. The relation between the State High Schools is vital; and 

Three members appointed by the Governor, for a term of six years. First appointees be -one for two years; one for four years; one for six years. Board of Governors to have the powers of the present Board with such added powers as will exalt it to one of great dignity and effectiveness. With such a Board of Governors, the people would regain confidence in their State University and its graduates, would rise to the support of one of the best of the State Universities. The University would receive careful consideration from coming legislatures on its needs. Many grave problems today beset the future of the University in its effort to provide higher education to our group of 11,500 young men and women. It needs strong management and direction. Given such a Board and the graduates would meet the challenge of the Honorable Evan Evans of the class of '97.
