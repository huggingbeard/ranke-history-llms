INTRODUCTION 

Now that we have one and a half million serious crimes a year, great effort is being made to enlighten thotless America about our crime problem, but very little effort is being made to enlighten even thotful America about our criminal population. This neglect of the heart of the subject and concentration upon its body is well exemplified by our crime conferences.

In 1935 the Governor of New Jersey called a conference to meet at Trenton in March and another was held in New- 

Sark in October. Also the Governor of New York called a conference to meet at Albany in October. These ran a parallel course; their sponsors declared that no vital part of the problem would be left untouched, yet the most vital approach was completely ignored.

In Trenton there were 82 speeches on the program and 1500 delegates came to hear them. Only two speakers gave any suggestion of the fact that there is a biological aspect to crime! One can read 80 of the speeches and be left with the impression that children are all pieces of white paper on which anything can be written, that they are born equal and that all future differences spring from external influences, that environment is the determining factor and hence the only avenue of approach to the problem. But E. A. 

Doll broke this conspiracy of silence with the reminder that "Crime is the product of interaction of the individual to environmental circumstances . . . .. A considerable number of offenders are abnormal persons whose adjustment to stresses of society are impossible in the face of their abnormality." L. N. Yepsen afforded the only other rift in the obscuring curtain of silence when he said that the three groups of offenders who should not be returned to society are the insane, the habitual offender, and the defective delinquent. 

In Newark Judge Joseph Siegler, of the Essex County

Juvenile and Domestic Relations Court, eclipsed the feeble suggestions made in the Trenton conference and wisely put the question, "From what kind of families do delinquents typically come?" and answered it fearlessly from the evi- 619855 1

dence which shows "a substantial percentage of the parents, brothers and sisters of delinquents and criminals to be mentally defective, mentally diseased, or of other unhealthy mental condition." In naming the major spheres of influence, why did he not include The Family? Because it was brought out in conference that it was "definitely understood before conference was arranged that the subject of sterilization was not to be mentioned." Science was sacrificed for politico-religious considerations. 

Eighty leaders in their respective fields took part in the New York conference. In a radio speech the Governor's counsel enlarged upon the elaborate mechanism to combat crime in New York with the net result that "Crime continues. Criminals flourish," and his conclusion was that "Our mechanism must be improved." Machinery of colossal proportions is built to combat crime, it conspicuously fails, and yet the only improvement suggested is bigger and better machinery. No consciousness of the need for a radical departure, no thot of directing the attack from the biological angle! Yet Dr. V. C. Branham, the State Administration's eminent psychiatrist, in an official report, quotes Dr. F. C. Richmond as saying: "There is a basic factor running thru all delinquency, viewed psychiatrically, legally, or socially. It is the personal equation—the mental make-up of the delinquent.”

Sociologists are another group prone to limit the search for the cause of crime to the obvious environmental influences. We live in an atmosphere of fanatical adherence to but one set of influences, those which come from without. 

Accurate information on the influences which come from within is desperately needed in order that the public may gain balance and reach a true set of values. 

This booklet is designed to supply the needed balance. 

In writing strictly to the title there is a deliberate intent to counteract environmental presentations of the subject, which from all sides are constantly driven upon the public attention and from which there is no escape. Having listened attentively to the prevalent biased views, it is to be desired that equally careful attention, with an open mind, be given to this more unfamiliar aspect of our national lawlessness.

The major premise of this study is that the largest part 2

of our crime problem is due to the repeated offenses of defective individuals who can not be reformed by the application of any social remedy. These defectives appear among all classes of offenders, from traffic violators to homicidal rapists. This brings us to the position that the problem can not be solved while focusing our attention upon the crime and judging the degree of guilt in accordance with classifications of criminal law. We must focus our attention upon the criminal and judge the degree of guilt and the appropriate remedy in accordance with classifications of the individual. This would sometimes lead to lifelong custody for the traffic violator and short imprisonment for the murderer. A complete shift of value is imperative in order to get the results that are being sought in vain at such great expense.

The National Safety Council reported that during 1935 there were 99,000 persons killed in accidents, 36,400 of these by automobiles. This means that one inhabitant of the United States is killed every 6 minutes thru an accident. Add these to the 12,000 murdered and the total is an annual slaughter over twice that of all American soldiers, sailors, and marines killed in action and died of wounds in the World War.

Our cities have lost track of the number of murdered policemen, but here is a recent despatch from London: "Aroused citizens searched today for a man who committed the almost unheard-of crime of shooting a British policeman. Bloodhounds, motorcycles, and bicycles were seen on little-used roads as armed policemen, firemen, and hundreds of volunteers took part in the search." The fact that the searching police were given arms was considered worthy of special comment, since only in extraordinary circumstances are weapons distributed to the police. Even policemen in the London metropolitan area do not carry arms.

In the United States 67% of all murders are committed with firearms. It is hard for us to realize the degradation of our condition and the difference in our psychology regarding crime from that in England. It is a difference in standards, and that goes to the root question of the innate quality of the citizenry of the nation.

COST 

Crime and dependency combined are vastly more expensive than war. 

In June 1936 J. Edgar Hoover said: "The cost of crime amounts to $120 a year for every man, woman, and child within the boundaries of this country. In 1932 the White 

House Conference estimated the annual cost of crime at 

16 billion dollars.

Some conception of the amount of a billion can be gained by remembering that if a man stood on a street corner giving out dollar bills at the rate of one every minute, it would take him 1902 years to give away a billion dollars. And at the same rate it would take from the earliest dated event in history (4241 B. C.) until now, four times over, to pay out our annual crime bill. 

We spend more on our unavailing efforts to curb crime than we spend on all our public schools, the work of our churches, and for all our private social agencies. Two years without crime would enable us to pay off the national debt and balance the budget.

EUTHENIC (environmental) CAUSES 

Our national lawlessness reflects our historical past ("Wild West"), our racial mixture, and our industrial conflicts. It is a composite characteristic in which symptom and cause are commonly confused. The many environmental factors that are associated with crime are assumed to be causes because of this association. These same factors can as appropriately be classed as symptoms, together with crime, of underlying biological defects which are the basic cause of the entire associated group of social inadequacies. 

The fallacious reasoning which mistakes symptoms for causes might well be extended to include small mouths and V-shaped palates as responsible for crime, for at the 1935 conference of the Eugenics Research Association it was reported that over 80% of the delinquent boys sent by the courts to a Philadelphia psychiatrist had them. This formation is often found with feeblemindedness, so the association is primarily with mental defect. 

Environmental conditions are important agents as secondary causes of crime and as such must be dealt with and improved. But honest searching thot shows that primary causation is innate and as such is even more important and must be dealt with and improved. Behind the chronic offender is the problem child; behind the problem child is the inadequate home and the problem parent; and behind the problem parent is poor heredity. Yet we have fought crime and dependency only by social methods and have utterly neglected to use biological checks, tho it is the old story of the weeds which spread faster than the grain and can best be stopped by not letting them get started. These checks are equally indicated from the standpoint of heredity and environment. It is unnecessary to decide how far heredity and environment each contributes to conditions. 

When an increasing proportion of children is being born in families of the social problem group, it is unnecessary to prove that they all start life physically or mentally defective in order to establish the fact that we are not making good use of our human resources. Ignorance, poverty, overlarge families, in short, slum conditions, whether symptoms or causes, are not fit surroundings for the injection of new human life.

ALCOHOLISM 

Mental disorders most frequently associated with crime are, in the order of their prevalency, (1) drunkenness, (2) feeblemindedness, (3) epilepsy, (4) paranoia (systematized delusion). Of 236 commitments of alcoholics to the

Trenton State Hospital in a two year period 85% of the cases showed alcoholism in one or both parents. The report of the New Jersey Commission on the Care of Mental Defectives states: "Alcoholism is the most prominent single causative agency with reference to crime, pauperism, mental disease, and degeneracy." 66 

LACK OF EDUCATION 

Under our compulsory school laws all except the lowest grade of feebleminded come under the supervision of the teachers. The defective paupers, criminals, unmarried mothers, alcoholics, prostitutes, hoboes all have school histories. When these histories are elicited, it is found that they were the chronically 'left back,' the truants, the disciplinary problems. Typically they drift thru school as repeaters, till at the age of 16 years they have reached about the 5th or 6th grade. Then they drop out, being no longer within the limits of compulsory school age."-L. S. 

Hollingworth, The Psychology of Subnormal Children. 

A study of 500 men in Sing Sing revealed that 45% had left school before sixth grade. Warden L. E. Lawes assures us that this percentage would hold for the entire I prison (over 2400 inmates) and his solution is to build more schools. No suggestion of our cutting off the supply of boys incapable of acquiring an elementary education! 

POVERTY 

In Chicago a study of 420 delinquent boys showed that over 20% had been in dependent institutions. An enormous number, if we think of the boys as coming from the general population!

In Cleveland during 1934 the prosperous area had one delinquent boy to every 910 inhabitants, while the poorer area had one to every 183 inhabitants. For the city as a

whole 70% of all delinquents came from families which received help from charitable agencies or whose incomes were just large enough to cover the bare necessities of life. 

We are constantly told that "the great cause of crime is poverty." No consideration is given to the fact that this country with the greatest material prosperity in the world has at the same time the highest crime rate of any civilized country, while in other parts of the world there are communities of law-abiding people who live their whole lives on the bare necessities without recourse to crime. 

LARGE FAMILIES 

In a study of 3000 delinquents, made by Dr. Wm. Healy, it was found that the larger the family the greater was the percentage of cases with more than one delinquent in the family. Dr. J. L. McCartney of Elmira Reformatory 

(N. Y.) states that in a study of 400 prisoners he found that over 59% came from families of 5 or more children and 10% from families of from 10 to 21 children. He concludes that "Large and unwarranted families are a major factor in the cause of crime." They are also indicative of the type of parents whose offspring become criminals. This is true only of unwarranted large families, for where parents are healthy, moral, responsible citizens, large families are of the utmost value to society. 

HOUSING 

Among 2000 repeated offenders it was found that only 7.6% were living under reasonably good conditions for the upbringing of a child. "We have no other figures showing such high correlation between conditions and incidence of delinquency. Where to direct a strong attack for prevention stands out with striking clearness" (Dr. Wm. Healy).

So the New Jersey Housing Authority was created in 1933 and has gotten approval for two sites involving an expenditure of five million dollars. It has a five year program indicating that 12% of all residential structures in urban areas are slums and should be removed. The chairman, S. S. Holmes, states: "The influences of the surroundings are responsible for the high percentage of crime in the slum areas. 

10

The crux of the problem lies in the question, "Do the slums make the man or does the man make the slums?"?

Let this be answered by excerpts from the Brock Report on Sterilization to the British Parliament (1933): “A considerable proportion of defectives do come from slum surroundings but inquiries into the family history of such cases show that in the majority there is evidence of morbid inheritance." "The inefficiency of the defective tends to depress him to the lowest economic level. They drift to the slums and like marries like . . . . It would be idle to expect of this group a proper sense of social responsibility. 

But many would be glad to be relieved of the recurring burden of parenthood, for which they are so manifestly unfitted." "Sociologists generally accept the view that there is a concentration in the lowest social stratum of the physically and mentally defective, the chronic unemployables, the habitual recipients of relief, and a delinquent element of a mentally subnormal type." "During the past few decades very great improvements have been made in housing and general conditions . . . . yet no diminution of the incidence of defect has occurred, but there has in all probability been an increase. There is an association between slums and mental defect, and it may be that where hereditary predisposition is present slum conditions act as a contributory factor in accentuating the results of this predisposition." "There are genetic factors which will produce a given character in any environment in which the subject can live. There are other genetic factors which will only produce a given character in an environment favorable to its production."

In Florida there are many families of 10 or more children living in one-room, windowless huts with dirt floors. 

In 1935 the government distributed 8000 mattresses in a misguided effort for improvement. It was found that the mattresses were soon put out with the chickens. 

Is it strange that broken homes are closely associated with crime? A 1936 study of 1700 boys who had been paroled from the Newark City Home at Verona found almost half of them to have come from broken homes. (Among school children generally the incidence is 19%). Many of these homes were broken by institutionalizing the parents. 11

If a criminal is the child of bad parents, he has not had the right start and society should prevent a child from getting into such a home rather than at great expense trying later to remedy his defects and create for him a new environment.

RELIGION 

Irreligion of parents has been found to be connected with "practically every case" of juvenile delinquency, according to the Director of the Police Juvenile Aid Bureau of New York City.

Why has no one risen to proclaim the importance of various brands of religion in producing crime and dependency? Not a single Quaker has been on relief rolls during the national depression. Clearly all that is needed to eliminate relief rolls is to get the recipients of relief to join the Society of Friends. Unitarians have never numbered more than 100,000 in the United States, yet one-third of those in the Hall of Fame are Unitarians. In New Jersey the Catholics are supplying 100% more of the socially inadequates than their number in the state's population warrants. What could be plainer than that the way to be rid of our burden of defectives is to convert our Catholics to Unitarianism!

The environmental factors cited are those usually believed to be the chief causes of crime, but there exist in the United States conditions more directly responsible for the widespread prevalence of crime which are not so often discussed, namely, the alliance between our police authorities and certain criminal elements, the accepted standards of morality in the legal and banking professions, and the practice of self-service so rampant among our political representatives. This exhibition of dishonesty among persons screened by a cloak of respectability affords ready selfjustification to the underworld and prevents any sense of shame among those who might be influenced by the moral tone of their environment. 

J. Edgar Hoover recently declared that if every person in public office in the United States were fingerprinted and records of law violation were spread before the nation the 12

results would "amount to a scandal which would rock the country." When he took charge of the Federal Bureau of 

Investigation in 1924 to reorganize it, he did so only on the condition that there was to be no politics in it, and there has been none in this Bureau since. 

Warden Lawes is very vocal concerning crime, but he never was so near the truth as when he recently said: "What we need today more than anything else is the right kind of officials, the right kind of prosecutors, and that means an intelligent and informed electorate, which will pick that kind of officials."

As in ancient times it was said that all roads 'lead to Rome, so today all theories of crime lead back thru the nursery to the parents, and we are brought squarely to the fact that no nation can rise higher than the innate quality of its people. The most drastic way to alter conditions is to alter the people who create them. 

Let us then consider our racial degeneration; first, thru immigration and, second, thru heredity-by means of an adverse differential birth rate. The 20% of our people on relief produced three children per family, while the 80% who were supporting them produced but two children per family.

EUGENIC CAUSES IMMIGRATION 

Until 1914 our restriction on immigration was limited to the exclusion of certain classes of defectives. For some years southern and eastern Europe had sent 85% of the million aliens entering our ports each year. There was little discrimination as to type or quality, so we became the dumping ground for the criminal and subnormal classes of all nations. A Princeton professor who built his home in 1934 was told by his Sicilian stone mason that letters from his brother, who is a police commissioner in Sicily, were full of the problem of what to do with their criminals since they could no longer ship them to America. No wonder that today, among all coming before the Children's Court in New York City, the Italian children are lowest in 13

intelligence (Negroes are very little better), tho "mental retardation is the rule among the 12,000 children arraigned yearly." 

Slightly more subtle methods were used in Germany and England to get rid of their criminals. Bail would be made low and the judge would warn: "I shall pass sentence two weeks from tomorrow; it will probably mean five years for you." The convicted man usually did what was expected-jumped his bail and sailed to America.

Our acceptance of aliens'in the false belief that all men are born equal has led to results such as (1) Of the 4771 feebleminded children in the ungraded classes of New York City's schools in 1921, three out of four had foreign-born parents. Probably the same proportion is true of the 10,- 

000 defectives in these classes today. (2) Of the 631 mental defectives committed to the Children's Hospital on Randall's Island in New York in 1930, 78% had foreign-born parents. These proportions would be more significant, if comparative percentages could be given on the number of children with foreign-born parents in New York City's general population; but there is no census computation for parental nationality of children in New York City.

Racial Comparisons: All studies are mutually supporting concerning the representatives of other countries in the United States, but these facts do not necessarily apply to the general population in their native lands.

Prof. E. A. Hooton of Harvard University, in referring to his researches on the American criminal in ten states, says: "It can be stated positively that the biological inferiority of the criminal is no less marked than his economic ineffectiveness and his general stupidity. We need a biological New Deal which will segregate and sterilize the anti-social and the mentally unfit." He adds that when our social engine stalls someone has watered the gasoline. Our motive power is our citizenry; how are we guarding its quality?

Our Present Policy: The quota law allows 153,714 aliens to enter annually, but does not apply to the western hemisphere. This means that Mexicans and West Indians have free access, to increase our present color problem. 

Successive bills to restrict immigration from the western hemisphere have been defeated in Congress by raising the cry "Latin-American Trade." Let us remember what became of Esau who sold his birthright for a mess of pottage. 

Mexican Children constitute the school problem in Texas,

New Mexico, and Colorado. In a group of 1004 the median 

Intelligence Quotient was 78 (normal I. Q. is 90-110).

Their fecundity is such that a Mexican couple making their northward trek into this country may expect to have 729 great-grandchildren, while a Nordic couple in the southwestern states may expect to have 27 great-grandchildren. 

Puerto Rico, where there has been an incredible mixture of races, sends an inexhaustible supply of her people to a complacent mainland. Her birth rate is 39 per 1000, while ours in only 22.5; already the Puerto Rican colony in New 

York City is the second largest Puerto Rican city in the world. A study just completed of Puerto Rican children in the schools of New York City shows that the majority are so low in intelligence as to require education of a man- 15

ual sort. What is true of those in Puerto Rico can not be determined, since 84% of all children in Puerto Rico leave school by the end of third grade! The report states: "Puerto Ricans are adding greatly to the tremendous problem of subnormal school retardates of alien parentage whence are recruited most delinquents and criminals. The majority of the 240 examined betray a family mentality which should not be permitted admission here, further to lower standards already seriously impaired by immigration."

In the Children's Court of New York City during 1934 far more delinquent Puerto Ricans appeared than any other foreign-born children.

Negro Children constituted 11.7% of the Children's Court cases in New York City during 1930, tho they (from 8 to 15 years) were 3% of the city's population. This ratio continues in adult life. According to the 1933 Decennial 

Census of County and Municipal Penal Institutions 22.8% 

of the prison population were Negroes, while those over 15 years are but 9.3% in the population at large. Negro blood is freely diffused thru the Puerto Rican population. 

We see the harvest being reaped from mass immigration from the lowest levels of many lands; what must we do to redeem our nation? It is urgent that our racial degeneration be checked and that we apply the same standards to all would-be immigrants from this hemisphere as from the rest of the world, not excepting our American dependencies (whence are legally admitted feebleminded and insane). In the last Congress a bill was introduced to make American citizens of the natives of the Samoan Islands and another to enable Hawaii to be admitted into the Union on an equal footing with the states. 

The Reynolds-Starnes Bill of the 1936 Congress provided adequate safeguards for our racial future that deserve the unqualified support of every true patriot when it is reintroduced into the 1937 session. It provides for registration and fingerprinting of all aliens now in the United States or who may hereafter be admitted. In addition it establishes a system of fingerprinting by American consulates as a means of permanently identifying legal immigrants and preventing substitutions and passport frauds. 

It requires prompt and mandatory deportation of alien 16

criminals and bars Communists and other foreign revolutionists and deports agents of foreign governments engaged in espionage.

In times of national emergency it permits deportation of pauper aliens in the same way as is now in force among other countries. (This would save available jobs for our own citizens.)

It requires that intelligence tests be made in foreign countries and the standards of admission be raised to those who pass a test "equivalent to, or higher than, a normal rating of an average sampling of native-born American white stock, or whose reputation or personal characteristics in the judgment of the consul would render the applicant readily assimilable among the preponderant element of the population of the United States." It further demands that "if any member of a family fails to meet these requirements that fact shall exclude the whole family from admission." HEREDITY 

"Do men gather grapes of thorns or figs of thistles? A good tree cannot bring forth evil fruit, but a corrupt tree bringeth forth evil fruit.” (Matt. 7. 16-18)

British studies have shown that the son of a convict is four or five times more likely to be a convict than a man picked at random. The obvious fact that anti-social behavior tends to run in families is conditioned by both heredity and environment. Because a man is defective does not mean that he is destined to be a criminal anymore than a given environment assures a uniform result. Crime is the result always of the interaction of several forces, but the biological inheritance is far more determining than anything external. As C. B. Goring, Deputy Medical Officer in the British Prison Service, maintains, "Crime is only to a trifling extent (if to any) the product of social inequalities, of adverse environment, or of other manifestations of what may be termed the force of circumstances." 66 

Dr. C. W. Burr, Professor Emeritus of Mental Diseases, University of Pennsylvania, in his presidential address to the Eugenics Research Association in 1925 said: 'Crime is not largely the product of economic stress. The criminal, as the psychiatrist defines the word, is born and not made .... They are not the product of the slums. They are incurable because they are not suffering from external stress and strain, but from an inherent defect in protoplasm." Illustrations of this are not hard to find. A child-placing organization in New York, after finding homes for a large number of children of criminals in rural communities offering a fairly ideal environment, found that the majority developed such bad behavior traits that it became impossible to persuade anyone in the same communities to accept more children. Miss E. S. Kite's studies in New Jersey exampled many cases of children placed 19

as infants in ideal homes, receiving every advantage and yet manifesting as adults their own family pattern. In short, "blood will tell."

There are many inheritable traits involved in asocial behavior. Tendency to deception underlies much bad behavior and it has been shown, in a study of 734 pairs of brothers and sisters, that children of the same family resemble each other in deceptiveness just as much as they do in intellect. It is also as independent of the range of home background as is the I. Q. Honesty was further found to go with emotional stability, which is largely inherited. Emotional instability is closely associated with suicide, which in turn is associated with homicide. 

Another manifestation of heredity's relation to crime is nomadism. A study in California of 24 boys who were markedly nomadic showed that 30% of their 312 relatives were likewise nomadic, while a control group of 24 boys had only 1% of their 318 relatives nomadic. "In communities where epilepsy is not subject to special public care it appears unduly frequently among delinquents."--White House Conference. Epileptic dream states lasting for days may follow a seizure so slight as not to have been noticed. In them the patient may commit crimes of violence noted for their ferocity and brutality; and when he comes to himself he has absolutely no recollection of what has happened. 

Judge Olson called an intelligence defect a misfortune, an emotional defect a calamity, a defect in both realms a catastrophe. Of the 27 murderers hanged in Cook County,

Ill. during 1914-1923 his laboratory tested 12 and found all to be defective both mentally and emotionally. Some had no emotional reaction to their own death. 

The power of inhibition is the most important factor in morals; this is commonly called self-control. In the normal man it is well-developed, in the weakling it scarcely exists. Most persons possess a social instinct which is the foundation stone of civilization. There are those who are born without capacity to develop this social instinct and a moral sense. The problem of crime is not so much a question of reward and punishment as it is a question of reduction or multiplication of hopelessly handicapped individuals.

34 prisons 76.6% were found to be abnormal mentally. "Several studies have shown that the proportion of criminals who are actually diseased mentally is several times as great as that of the population as a whole."-Paul 

Popenoe, Sterilization and Criminality.

RECIDIVISM 

A recidivist is the term used in all penal literature for one who relapses into criminal behavior, especially after punishment. 25

The extent to which repeaters constitute our crime problem can be seen from the fact that the Federal Census for 1932 reports on 39,530 inmates in state prisons as to previous commitment-53% were recidivists. The figures are even worse for inmates of county and municipal penal institutions, for the 1933 Decennial Census gives 58.5% with previous convictions.

In 1917 Sing Sing's population was 67% recidivist; of the psychopathic group 87% were recidivists. In his report Dr. Bernard Glueck stated: "The problem is sufficiently staggering to justify any radical departure from present procedure that might promise some degree of prevention."

The commitments of one year to Massachusetts' institutions were 57.4% recidivist, their previous commitments averaging six sentences each. And of those received in

New York City's penal institutions in 1931 (the last year of record) half were repeaters and 10% of these had served five or more previous sentences! What could be more ridiculous than the continuance of this blindly repetitious procedure, since this, the worst part of our crime problem, is due to biologically defective stock and hence is controllable at its source! 

Dr. Wm. Healy says: "Almost all recidivists show plainly their tendencies at least by late childhood.' Yet the commonsense policy of adequate control is seldom made and the boy goes on to develop a career of crime. Dr. Healy illustrates this with the following cases: 

1. The laboratory urged permanent commitment for a boy of 9 years who came from a feebleminded and criminal family. This being ignored, at the age of 15 he killed a man and left the city. 

2. Extreme nomadism marked a boy from 9 years of age. He had a defective heredity and by 16 was a chronic thief. The psychiatrist saw in him a thoroly unstable individual with marked anti-social trends. Even his mother urged a permanent commitment, but he was merely given short sentences until one of his crimes led another examing board to state that he was "a menace to public safety and irresponsible for his actions." In spite of this he was transferred to a general hospital from which he readily walked away. At 22 he shot five men, killing two. 26

Studies of 100 immoral women (court cases) showed 84% of the recidivists to be suffering from some form of mental handicap; and of 100 drunken women 82.2% of the recidivists exhibited mental abnormality. It is further established that the largest percentage of recidivism comes among males where there is a family history of abnormal mentality.

Friedrich Stumpfl published in 1935 the results of his investigation of two groups of criminals. One group consisted of habitual criminals and the other of offenders who had been punished once but not again for at least 15 years. 

Material concerning 18,400 persons was collected. It was found that among the recidivists hereditary factors were a great deal more important than environmental conditions. 

For the bulk of grave criminality an inherited psychopathic constitution was responsible. The number of recidivists who were married was as great as that of the light offenders. Features associated with criminality were found in many members of the recidivists' families, many of whom had become criminals of a similar type. It was found that among the unconvicted members of typically criminal families there was a marked excess of children. The elimination of the criminal himself does not, therefore, eliminate the unfavorable hereditary characters, since the other carriers in these families propagate more freely than the rest of the population. 

Dr. V. C. Branham says: "There can be no question but that the relationship of the psychopathic personality group to the problem of crime offers one of the most fruitful sources for investigation."

The 65-year-old New Yorker, who had held the record with 45 arrests and 37 convictions, was nosed out a few months ago by a younger competitor, who at 41 years appeared in court in his 46th charge, this time a stabbing affair. And how many times are criminals arrested and discharged with typical gangster regularity! The latest is an Austrian who had a record of seven convictions before he came to these hospitable shores. His recent conviction, as a master counterfeiter, came after he had bought or talked his way out of 36 previous arrests! 

No industry would tolerate such inefficiency and yet those who pay the crime bill continue to work on the cir- 27

cumference and hesitate to strike at the root. When a man and a woman are sent to prison for neglect of their four children, nothing is done to prevent repetitious misery. No; even tho the four children all had to be sent to a mental hospital (where they still are), the couple were allowed to produce four more children. Recently it has been necessary to prosecute them again in respect to these younger children, one of whom is on probation for theft.

PAROLE 

After a prisoner has served a part of his sentence he may apply to a Board of Parole for release. If granted, he spends the remainder of his sentence at large under supervision of a parole agent and is liable to be returned to prison if he violates the conditions of parole. This must not be confused with probation, which is an act of the court after conviction, where instead of commitment to an institution the offender is allowed to be at large with suspended sentence under supervision of a probation officer.

Parole is so lavishly granted that during the fiscal year ending June 30, 1935 only 2 of the 648 prisoners released from Sing Sing had served their full term. Of the 646 paroled 112 had been previously paroled and returned to prison. Thus it is not surprising to note that during April 

1936 there were 37 men returned to Sing Sing as parole violators.

The wholesale granting of parole and the liberal interpretation of indeterminate sentences rapidly returns to the community those who have been apprehended and committed at great cost to taxpayers and at great danger to enforcement agents. 

J. Edgar Hoover, in commenting on the political alliance with crime which exists in state after state, says: "It has placed more than one ex-convict in the police department and even elected ex-convicts to office while they still maintained their allegiance with the underworld. It enters our penitentiaries and swings wide the gates for men who otherwise would have paid their debt to society." 

And he recently reminded us that "Every 2½ hours a murderer is released and every second man of this released army of slayers has been freed by some sort of clemency 28

-- The average sentence served in the United States by persons convicted of murder is only 3 years and 7 months and the average term served by persons given life sentences for homicide is less than 10 years . . . . Largely because of this inadequate punishment, before the sun sets tonight, 35 persons will have been wiped out by the hand of a murderer.' 

Parole is sound in theory and if its administration were freed of sentimentality and politics it would ultimate in much good. But the indiscriminate way in which it is applied results in events such as these reported recently in 

New York newspapers: 

1. The New York State Prison Parole Board released a slayer five years before the end of his minimum sentence for murder in the second degree. He murdered another man just eight months after his release on parole. 

2. Of two men caught robbing a store one had been arrested twelve times and had served three terms in Sing Sing. He was on parole. The other had been sent to Sing 

Sing for 10 to 20 years, but was paroled.

John Dillinger was a parolee, as were both criminals in the Weyerhauser kidnaping. More recently we have been shocked at the rape-murder of Mrs. Titterton by a 24-yearold ex-convict, who had been sentenced three times for grand larceny but had never served more than a few months and was on probation at the time of the murder, and at the rape of a 17-year-old girl by a WPA worker while on parole. No wonder that J. Edgar Hoover says that parole in practice "approaches a national scandal . . . . It seems impossible that in an enlightened nation brave officers should be asked to face desperate criminals, to endure even loss of life, that those criminals should be captured, only to see them turned loose again to resume their predatory careers.' 99 It is curious that people claim to be fearful of sterilization laws being unjustly applied, despite the fact that in more than 23,000 operations performed under state laws there has never been a recorded case of misapplication.

At the same time they support parole laws that result in real danger to society without being capable of the incalculable benefits that sterilization offers.

"ONE THOUSAND JUVENILE DELINQUENTS" (1934) 

This is the first volume of The Harvard Survey of Crime and Criminal Justice in Boston, organized in 1926 with Prof. Felix Frankfurter as the general director. This exhaustive survey was made in order to supply scientific knowledge to replace a priori assumptions and generalities based on meagre data. 

The Sample: 1000 cases sent by the Boston Juvenile Court to the Judge Baker Foundation during 1917-1922 for clinical examination. 32

The Scope, like that of the first study, extends thru a five year period following the end of the period of prescribed treatment. The aim of the study is to assay the combined efforts of a court and a clinic that represent the best treatment work in delinquency in Boston.

The Boys: Average age when examined was 13 years and 

5 months.

Average number of prior arrests was 2.3. 

Average age at first delinquency was 9 years and 7 months. Many presented behavior problems at 6 years or less. 

84.5% were retarded 1-3 years or were in ungraded classes (12% of the boys in Massachusetts schools are retarded). Compared with Boston schoolboys in general, twice as many withdrew because of economic need but seven times as many withdrew because of inability to do the school work. This group contained nine times the proportion of boys of defective intelligence. 

73.8% did not belong to crowds or gangs. 

Their Families: 86.7% contained members other than the boy who were criminal. 

5 or more children were the rule and the mothers were still in the child-bearing age. 

76.1% had Catholic parents (the general population of 

Massachusetts is 66% Catholic). 

87.5% had had contacts with social welfare agencies and a total of 2318 organizations had dealt with these families. 

Only 13.6% (111) were free from mental disease or mental defect in the close relatives.

The homes were largely broken or distorted and in neighborhoods containing centers of vice. The picture is one of social inadequacy, poor heredity, and family criminality. 

Recidivism: 88.2% recidivated during the five year period following treatment, their arrests averaging 3.6 per boy, and 70% were convicted of serious offenses. 

There seems to be no association between the number of contacts with the clinic and later adjustment. The length of the treatment period has very little effect on recidivism.

The factors most closely associated with reformation 33

were: (1) where delinquency does not occur before 15 years or over, (2) where there is no retardation or misconduct in school, (3) where an affectionate relation exists between boy and parents, (4) where parental discipline is good, (5) where there is an absence of mental disease or defect in the family.

Comment: "The major conclusion is inescapable, then, that treatment carried out by Clinic, Court, and associated community facilities had very little effect in preventing recidivism."

Even tho it leads us to see the general bankruptcy of our remedies for crime and the crying necessity for an entirely new approach!

It is valid to question whether cases referred to a clinic would make a typical sample. To answer this question a subsequent study was made of non-clinic cases which passed thru the Boston Juvenile Court during the same years as the Gluecks' boys. This revealed as high a rate of recidiv- 37

Those who are personally involved and hence emotionally antagonized by findings so contrary to their previous assumptions have found it easier to attack this research than to adjust their point of view to its revelations. An illustration of the arguments to which resort has been made is that recidivism is not a fair test of treatment, as even doctors cannot guarantee permanent cures! One convincing reply to these attacks can be found in Mental Hygiene for October 1934. 

Dr. A. O. Squire of New York made statements to the

The probation service of Massachusetts is above that of most other states and the parole department has reached (comparatively) a very high standard. CONFIRMATION 

The Children's Bureau of the Department of Labor undertook a study of five state institutions for delinquent boys and a case study of 751 boys who had been inmates. 

It was announced, July 19, 1936, that the records of these boys showed a heavy incidence of school retardation and habitual truancy and that this instability was repeated 38

after release in their employment records. The vocational training received had been of little direct use, as 21% had held 10 to 19 jobs in their few years of work and another 20% had changed jobs 20 or more times. Records of convictions subsequent to first parole were found in 58% of the cases and only 32% had made a satisfactory adjustment to community life. Did the weak spots lie in the institutions or in factors beyond the institutions' control? 

The Gluecks found that the recidivism of boys was not influenced by whether or not the clinic's recommendation pertaining to where the offender should live was followed by the court. The factors most closely associated with reformation were mainly symptoms of the biological constitution of the boy or of his family (p. 34).

The Gluecks said: "From the popular discussions of the crime situation it is clear that naivete still dances in the land. Will it be finally acknowledged that to tinker with procedure is to be occupied with but a superficial feature of the crime problem?" In the very few instances "where the rehabilitation may be credited to the reformatory the peculiarly favorable make-up of these women largely contributed to the good result." And the opposite is true, "recidivism must be charged largely to the favorable characteristics of this group." "Out of a mass of young offenders all similarly characterized with respect to environmental factors, some are destined to ultimate reform, others to recidivism."

The Brock Report: "Tho there may be no certain prognosis in any particular case, we know enough to be sure that inheritance plays an important part in the causation of mental defects and disorders. EUGENIC 

Criminality indicates great emotional instability, lack of will, excessive suggestibility, etc. Each of its component parts is inheritable and runs in certain families. The hereditary factor has been recognized for centuries,—a traveler in Palestine during the thirteenth century reported that thieves were castrated there in order that they might not beget sons who would be criminals. 

Studies have shown that there is twice the proportion of feeblemindedness among chronic offenders as there is among occasional offenders. A survey quoted in the American Neurological Association's Report reveals that more criminals are found among relatives of habitual criminals than among relatives of those who commit only one crime. 

This is as true of cousins reared apart as of brothers and sisters brought up in the same environment,—another proof of the fact that breed is more potent than pasture. 

Madeiros was electrocuted on the same day as Sacco and Vanzetti. His father had been subject to fits, a paternal uncle and aunt were both feebleminded, his mother was epileptic and her sister died insane. He was a delinquent from childhood and had had 12 previous convictions. If 50

we permit children to be born of defective stock, we can never make it up to them thru charity.

Friends can do more harm than enemies, and so it is with those advocates of sterilization who, because of their academic habits of thot and their temperamental aversion to action, raise the cry, "More research! Do nothing until all uncertainties have been removed." All men of action, all experimental scientists know that the unknown is best apprehended thru the application of the known. Those who refuse to step forward can not advance their horizon. 

SOCIAL 

The Brock Report states: "It would be hazardous to forecast the genetic results of any particular union.. about the social results it is possible to be more certain, since it is beyond question that the vast majority of defectives are temperamentally and socially unfitted for parenthood."

The American Neurological Association's Report puts epileptics with those to be included in a sterilization law "mainly on the ground of the social situation" and further states: "It is obvious that in the case of the feebleminded there may be a social as well as a biological situation of importance. Certain feebleminded can only care for themselves and a family of children may prove an overwhelming burden."

The social situation can properly be made a criterion for sterilization, as in the Scandinavian countries, because it is so interwoven with the eugenic situation that it may be considered but another manifestation of innate defect. 

The families of offenders contain an unduly high incidence of broken homes occurring when children most need the guidance of an integrated family, but the prevalence of mental deficiency and mental disorder in these same families also constitutes a deleterious influence in the rearing of children. LEGISLATION 

The Supreme Court of the United States in 1927 stated: "It is better for all the world if, instead of waiting to execute degenerate offspring for crime, society can prevent those who are manifestly unfit from continuing their kind." Under this protecting decision 14 states now include certain types of prisoners in their sterilization laws. 

The provisions are variously phrased: "inherited tendency to criminality" (Conn.); "habitual criminals if mentally defective or mentally diseased" (Del.); "habitual criminals, moral degenerates, sexual perverts" (Idahothe constitutionality of this law has been upheld); the above three classes when "about to be discharged or paroled" (Neb.); "hereditary criminal with sexual tendencies" (Utah).

Alabama's 1935 bill extended the classes to include criminals.

California's law is not well phrased concering criminals and hence no operations are being performed under its terms, but more than 150 convicts in San Quentin have vol- 52

untarily submitted to the operation since September 1935.

Indiana: About 500 convicts in the State Reformatory had been sterilized, at their own request, before 1907 when the first sterilization law was adopted. One convict gave as his reason that he had considered the fact that his grandparents and parents were thieves and crooks and had concluded that "it must be inherited." 

Kentucky: A special prison advisory commission recommended (Nov. 1935) emasculation of desperate criminals and degenerates. They were convinced that this type "is more afraid of desexing than of death" and they suggested it as a substitute for "the ineffective and obsolete death penalty." For feebleminded prisoners sterilization was advised.

North Carolina: "At least 40% of the inmates of the penal institutions are feebleminded and should be sterilized before being released."

Oklahoma needed an amendment to define the term "habitual criminal" before her law could be actively used. In 

May 1935 this was passed.

Oregon: More general use of sterilization of criminals is being considered by state officials. The Governor decided that those committed for sex crimes must agree to sterilization before applying for pardon.

Utah: "Our law is satisfactory except that its application should include additional groups of criminals" (now only sexual criminals).

Wisconsin: "In every state there are hundreds in the pauper class free to bear children of whom a large percentage are certain to have vicious social traits. The private property loss and the public expense they cause is beyond computation and their menace grows with the spreading branches of their family tree. What is to be done? Why not try a sane compulsory sterilization law administered as in Wisconsin?"

CONCLUSION 

"Let us raise a standard to which the wise and honest can repair; the event is in the hands of God."-George Washington. 53

Our social vision is microscopic rather than telescopic. 

We see no farther than an individual in distress and exhaust our resources in alleviation. In criminology the sentence is based on the offense, while all the time it is the offender that is basic. This fanatical focus upon the statutory offense as the gauge of seriousness brings the satirical question, "Would you consider that traffic violators are suffering from an inherent defect in germplasm?" This grouping is superficial and requires the legal answer, “Yes and no." Among traffic violators will be found the entire range of humanity, from the highly developed individual, with such quick mental reactions that he can break regulations without incurring danger, to the dipsomaniac (caused by a deep-seated neuropathic taint), to the unstable thru mental disease and to the incompetent thru mental deficiency. The last type has been conspicuously numerous lately; within a month two cases appeared in the Brooklyn 

Traffic Court. One driver's mental age was placed at 7 years and the other the psychiatrist placed at 10 years. 

Two recent cases in Russia were settled by execution. 

In one the driver was intoxicated and caused the death of three persons; in the other the motorist was brought into court for his seventh offense as a hit-and-run driver. The judge's final words were: "You promise to be more careful. I, also, have resolved to be more careful. You say that it will not happen again. I shall make certain that it will not. You will be shot in one hour." We must cease dealing with offenses supposedly major and minor and instead deal with offenders. We should realize that a murderer might need only a few months' custody to drive home his remorse, while a traffic violator may need life-long custody or be a proper subject for sterilization (never as a punishment, of course). To deal with men rather than with deeds could at least be our objective.

The most serious part of our crime problem is due to the recidivism of defectives. By defectives are meant those who are subnormal either mentally or emotionally. Evidence has been offered from many states which amply proves this statement. We must take the practical means which science and experience have provided to check the reproduction of such of these defectives as are fit to be at large and to keep in permanent custody those who will ever 54

be a menace to society, all this regardless of the nature of their offense. The anti-social behavior of this class begins in early childhood and a trained observer could select most of them for sterilization before they produced offspring. The constant application of this intelligent and merciful remedy in every state of the Union would be of incalculable long-run benefit to our nation. It would have to be reinforced, of course, by a strict exclusion of all subnormal immigrants. These remedies sound Utopian, when one considers that even the remedies of the environmentalists, who are in the saddle, are blocked thru the self-interest of certain legal groups which batten on crime and are loath to part with it so long as it contributes to their exchequer. Sterilization has its bloc of obstructionists in another professional class, which is also motivated by thots of self-interest. And it is left to the minority in both professions to work for the good of society. 

Dr. Wm. J. Hickson, Director of the Psychopathic Laboratory of the Municipal Court of Chicago, had completed over 40,000 examinations by 1925. His statement is: "Environmentalists are to criminology what the anti-evolutionists are to science. . . . Their thinking is dominated by their feelings, wishes, and prejudices and their minds are closed against established scientific fact.” us, Let us put our knowledge into action for, as Jesus told "If ye know these things, happy are ye if ye do them.”

When the Baggs family first applied to the social service agency help was refused them because it was established that their home was a house of prostitution. This was in the family tradition, for Mrs. Baggs when a young girl had been invited to leave a small New Jersey railroad town. This occurred because of what appeared to be her ambition to act as hostess to the local payroll of the railroad. After this ejection she married Mr. Baggs, whose chief characteristic was a complacent disposition due to a vacuum in the head. She proceeded to have 17 children, *In adopting a tone of facetious irony in these case histories, the aim is to expose the ludicrous ineptitude of social service without population control. 55

all of whom Mr. Baggs allowed to pass as little Baggs, which the neighbors thot was most accommodating of him. 

These children have inherited their mother's excessive vitality; thirteen of them have made names for themselves, in fact the biography of each is a matter of record. Only four lie peacefully beside the departed Mr. Baggs. 

After the social service agency refused to contribute to the support of this household in December 1914, a world cataclysm operated to restore their fortunes. Five sons enjoyed a brief period in uniform, for which they have been receiving benefits ever since, and six daughters, while not exactly in uniform, tried to do for Camp Dix what their lone mother had aspired to do for the railroad. One result of these wartime activities was a trial brought by outraged neighbors lacking the docility of Mr. Baggs, who was locked out of the house when business was good. Furthermore, there was the matter of overcrowding; it was this to which the neighbors particularly objected. Summer nights saw three or four quilts spread upon the back yard, each quilt sufficing for a bottle of gin, a soldier, and a Baggs girl. It was not the shades of night that gave some of these visitors an ebony hue. By this time Mrs. Baggs had earned a promotion; her role was now that of general supervisor. 

At the trial several men complained of her habit of demanding an additional cash bonus. This has been returned to them by a big-hearted public. Another complaint was that "breaking out in sores" seemed to follow these visits. 

It was not until 1934 that Mrs. Baggs, then 78 years old, again applied for charity. Living with her was an alcoholic son who had served four 30-60 days sentences in the local jail. This modest beginning is due to the fact that he is the youngest of her 17 and has not gotten into his stride. 

Naturally he is being given every chance thru short sentences and $123.26 in cash the first year, besides $104.49 given to his mother during 1935. Her old age pension is $15 monthly. Her cancer treatments in New York City have cost quite a bit, but then could money be better spent than in restoring her to the promise of years in which to enjoy society's benefactions?

There is one other son who has not married and who lives in lodgings when not in jail. He has run up the neat record of 17 sentences to date but, as they are only from 5 56

days to months, he has plenty of time for fun between. 

His cash gifts were only $91.74 during 1935, but then be gets so much attention in other ways and so much is done for him in the way of medical care that we really can not accuse society of cramping his style unduly. As his biography states, "This situation is apt to continue indefinitely." 

The 11 Baggs children who have married have gone farther toward making their mark in the world than have their single brothers. The 7 girls are all fair of face and they have so far found ways to avoid being "on relief,” tho they have not surpassed their brothers in any other way. Let the record of one married son suffice for all.
