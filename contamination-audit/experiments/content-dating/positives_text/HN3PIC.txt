Jeremy Mouse Comes to the Studio 

It was midnight. Twelve times Grandfather Clock spoke in his soft old voice with his hands before his face. And then the Blue Barn began to wake up. 

Little creakings and murmurings came from the windows, the chairs and the floors. The stairs which led to the big loft above cracked and snapped as they stretched their old boards.

There had been a party in the Blue Barn that afternoon. It was a very gay party and all of them were tired. They had slept until Grandfather Clock waked them up. "Oh, I've lost a pane from my sash!" complained the Window. "What a crack

I got when that ball went through me!

I was so shaken that I know my weight dropped." "Never mind," said the Curtain, which was hanging a little bit crooked. "We'll all get straightened out before long. The old carpenter down the road probably will come along in the morning. He'll give you another pane." 

A sharp little voice came from a Needle which lay on the floor. "Will somebody pick me up, please?

When the Blue Barn was made over into a house the barn loft, which had always been full of hay, was turned into a Studio for an artist. Here she worked daily at her drawing and painting and downstairs she slept and ate and had tea parties.

Plexy, the Milkweed 

Caterpillar 

One fine day in August Jeremy Mouse got very tired of staying indoors. He walked out in the field to see if anything interesting was happening there. 

For some time Jeremy just strolled along, enjoying the pure air and bright sunshine; then he hopped upon a nice flat toadstool that grew beside a milkweed plant and began to brush the dust from his coat.

When he had it clean enough to suit him, he stretched out on the toadstool. "Well, this is a nice day!" he said to himself.

He lay there in the sun until his back was as warm as a hot biscuit. Then he 31

rolled over and lay on his back to get his underside warm, too.

Jeremy kept his eyes closed for about five minutes. Anyone looking at him would surely have thought he was asleep.

He was not. He was thinking of what a wonderful day it was, and of all the pleasant things there were for a fat little mouse to do. And he was hoping that he would have a fine adventure sometime during the day.

Suddenly he opened his eyes. He saw that his toadstool was right under a milkweed plant and there was a caterpillar eating his lunch on the underside of a leaf just over Jeremy's head.

The caterpillar was a handsome fellow, about two inches long, with a striped suit of yellow and black and white. He had three pairs of legs near his head, which 32

were his true legs, and five pairs of legs halfway down his body, which looked like tiny elephant's feet. Each of these little legs had tiny hooks on it, and that is why this caterpillar could walk on the underside of the leaf and not fall. 

A very handsome little fellow he was, with his striped suit and two long black horns on his head and two more down at the other end of him, adding to his beauty. 

When Jeremy had a cracker to eat it disappeared rapidly, but not so fast as the milkweed leaf seemed to go down this little caterpillar's throat. "Oh!" cried Jeremy to the caterpillar, "you certainly can eat! If you can do that now, what will you do when you grow up? Do you keep it up like this all the time? Oh! I forgot to tell you my name. I am Jeremy Mouse. I know your 33

last name is Caterpillar; what's your first name?"

The little caterpillar stopped munching for a minute. "I'm Plexy Caterpillar," he said, "one of the Milkweed Caterpillars. I really don't know how old my family is, but it goes way back; one of the first American families, you know. Milkweed is my very best food. In fact, I live on this plant. 

Plexy is only half of my name, but wait until I'm grown up. Then you'll see a big change. You'll hardly know me, for 

I won't be a caterpillar then. "As for eating, how can a fellow grow if he doesn't eat? Why, it's all I feel like doing, and I must say I've made a pretty good job of this leaf. That's a big hole 

I've eaten there, isn't it?" "Yes, sir, I should say it was," cried 34

Jeremy. "It looks like one I found in a book leaf yesterday. My leaf was drier than yours, though." 

Plexy Caterpillar yawned. "Don't you ever outgrow your clothes, Jeremy?" he asked. "My suit is getting tight, and this will make the fourth time 

I've had to have a new one. Perhaps 

I ate too much today." He yawned again. "I believe I will stop and make a mat instead of eating." 

With the greatest interest, Jeremy watched Plexy spin a thick mat of silk on the leaf. Plexy moved his head to right and left until he had a mat longer than himself. Then he turned around and spun back again, making a second layer. Next he turned around and made a third layer. That done, he hooked his last pair of legs in it and rested. 36

Jeremy waited a while for Plexy to say something. But not a word would Plexy say. So the mouse went home, leaving 

Plexy looking as though he were dead.

The next day, about the same time, Jeremy went back. Plexy Caterpillar was still there, but he looked different, somehow. His skin was loose and hung down over his head like a little black cap. 

Really, he seemed to have two heads, one behind the other. The fact was, as Plexy had said, his suit was too small and he was about ready to get out of it. 

While Jeremy watched Plexy, the union suit split and began to shrivel up over his horns, and his front legs, and down his back. 

Plexy rubbed his head back and forth and from side to side, until he rubbed off his little black cap; then he just walked 37

straight ahead out of his old, tight union suit, which was left hooked to the mat of silk. 

Now Plexy Caterpillar looked much larger. He kept very quiet for about an hour. Then, seeing Jeremy, he asked, "How do you like my new suit? It fits pretty well, doesn't it? There's more room in it, too. Well, now I'll have some lunch, after I put my old suit out of sight.”

He ate the old union suit and then started in greedily on a big leaf.

Jeremy, finding it was getting late, went home. 

Two days later Jeremy, running along a fence, found Plexy spinning another little carpet of silk under the rail. When he finished it, he hooked his little hind legs right in the middle where it was thickest. After a short time, he let go 38

with his other legs and hung from his mat, head down. 

Jeremy was scared. "Do you think you ought to do that after eating so much?" he asked.

Plexy looked just like a question mark upside down and seemed to be beyond talking. So Jeremy went home.

After a week it began to change color; a dark purple-brown began to spread over it. Then, one day, Jeremy saw that it was no longer a green jewel with gold spots on it. Instead, it was reddish brown with black lines and white spots. Suddenly, as he watched, it split and hung there like a piece of thin old paper. 

Jeremy was breathless. Out came a little black head, one "feeler" and then another, and six black legs which fastened to the paper case.

Caterpillar, now a Monarch butterfly, crawled out of the empty case and hung there, weak and helpless. His soft, crumpled orange-and-black wings grew slowly longer and less crumpled, and strength at last came into his body. 42

Plexy's tongue did seem very queer.

Mrs. Mud Dauber Wasp's House 

It was a hot summer noon, and Jeremy 

Mouse lay dozing on the shelf back of the cracker box, dreaming of cheese. All kinds of cheese: cream cheese, cottage cheese, and, best of all, nice smelly cheese with a rind. 

His nose twitched, and his whiskers wiggled, and still Jeremy dreamed of cheese. 

Suddenly, he opened first one eye, and then the other. And behold, his dream had come true! There was some cheese right on the shelf, smelly cheese, almost under his nose! To the end of his days, Jeremy couldn't imagine how he stayed asleep while someone laid that cheese down beside him. 48

He did just what any sensible little mouse would do. The cheese was just what he wanted; it was handy, so he helped himself and ate all he could. 

Then he washed his hands and face, and smoothed down his fur coat. Next he polished off his whiskers and his tail.

What do you think it was Mrs. Wasp had put into that house of hers? 57

Jeremy could hardly believe his eyes. 

Fifteen spiders, fast asleep, with their legs all folded up tight! 

Mrs. Wasp had never intended to live in that house herself. It was really a nursery for her babies. The spiders she had stung to sleep were all nicely packed away for their food. 

Mrs. Wasp had laid her eggs, each in a separate cell. If Jeremy had not pulled the house down, this is what would have happened:

After Mrs. Wasp had packed in all the spiders she knew her baby wasps would need, she would have sealed the house with a mud door. 

In the course of time, each egg would have hatched into a little white grub called a larva, with a very hearty appetite. It would begin at once to eat the spiders. 58

When its appetite was satisfied, the larva would have spun a soft little nightshirt about itself, and turned into a chrysalis. 

After some days, if Jeremy should be listening just at the right time, he would hear a queer little sound inside the house. Next a hole would appear in the roof, and out would come a full-grown wasp, that, in the course of time, would do just what her mother had done. 

Jeremy never knew that he had probably saved some one from being stung by a wasp. But that was what he really had done. 59

About the Book 

The little stories about Jeremy Mouse and his friends are all based on fact with the exception of the one about Timmy 

Turtle's Trip. 

Jeremy and his family really lived in my studio and I was fortunate enough one night, to find a frightened little mouse in the washbowl just when I needed him. He helped me to find out the number of his toes and was a "model" mouse, as he told the dolls.

Always interested in bugs and animals and watching for them, I did not pass them by when we met, but waited around with a sketchbook at hand to see what would happen. 

The caterpillar of the monarch butterfly I found in a Marblehead graveyard and transported it from its native milkweed to comfortable quarters in my studio in company with eleven others and a cricket, thereby affording an opportunity for many friends to watch the metamorphosis of the caterpillar.

In an aquarium the goldfish and the newt lived together until they had to be separated. And here Polly Wog found her legs, as related by Jeremy.

On a walk across Boston Common I stopped to feed the squirrels one day, and Chatty Squirrel really buried a nut down my neck and covered it with my scarf. That amusing experience, together with that of knocking the mud wasp's nest down and finding it was filled with spiders, and other interesting meetings with various and sundry small creatures, led me to write all the happenings into one story with a little mouse to relate them. Mr. Angelo Patri very kindly read them and his advice led to their publication.
