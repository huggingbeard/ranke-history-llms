He lived alone, with only his gray horses and his wolf dog, King, for companions. And although friendly, he was a lonely, reserved little man, who never mentioned his past. finally a few friends learned to their amazement that he was the famous border outlaw, the "White Wolf." But

HERIFF ED STOUT was sixty, fat and lazy.

SHE 

He had been sheriff of Hidden Valley County for close to thirty years. For twenty-nine years and six months of that time the office had been a sinecure. 

But lately a crop of undesirables had drifted into the valley, and disturbed the sheriff's repose. During the past six months he had been forced to spend more time in the saddle than in the whole number of years up to that time.

Hidden Valley lay in the center of the Black Stone Mountains, close to the Canadian border. It was walled on three sides by tall, almost impassable mountains. Only to the north, on its open side, was it possible to reach civilization, and then the trail twice crossed the Canadian border before it reached a rail head.

In spite of the fact that the valley was fertile, that its three mines were rich, and that its welltimbered slopes were amply watered and furnished an ideal range for several small cattle outfits, the character of the place had changed but little in thirty years.
