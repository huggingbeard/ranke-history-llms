Preface to the American Edition 

In the midst of this World War II, and at a time when victory over the Axis Powers is in sight, the people of America and the British Empire are planning for a Peace that will make sure that the sacrifices they have been making will not have been made in vain; they are seeking for a Peace that will make a World War III impossible. 

Unfortunately most of our peace planners are under the impression that as soon as the aggressor nations are defeated, Peace will not be far off.

Our statesmen are stressing the necessity that the "three great peace-loving nations"-America, Great Britain and

Russia, must stay united after this war to enforce peace. 

Is that all the hope our leaders have for permanent peace? 

Did Mr. Churchill and Mr. Cordell Hull like the "peace" they had in Russia since the Red Revolution started in 1918? 

Can there be Peace in a world one-half capitalistic and the other half communistic, as the prospects are now? 

Can there be Peace in Europe, with two dozen disunited nations striving with each other for their own national interests?

Can there be Peace in the world with 400 million people living in crowded Europe, while millions of square miles of territory are uninhabited in the other four continents? 

Can there be Peace in the world with 10% of its inhabitants wealthy, and 90% poor?

Can there be Peace in a world where selfishness and competition rule? 

Already the "peace-loving nations" are striving among themselves for the best bargain in shipping, export trade, oil, ports and air-lines, with Russia taking whatever she wants. 

Did we have Peace in America during the last 15 years, when more than 10 million workers were unemployed and their families lived in poverty?

Do we have Peace in America now, with strikes all over the country, even during this great war emergency? 

Will there be Peace in America when 30 million war work-

ers and 13 million soldiers and sailors are discharged, after the war drums have stopped beating?

Can there be Peace, when after the shooting is over, the warring nations will find themselves 1000 billion dollars in debt and financially bankrupt, with half of Europe devastated?

When we contemplate these great questions, we must realize that the problems of Peace are far more numerous and complex than the problems of defeating our foreign enemies. The problems of victory were mostly problems of mass production; of producing a preponderance of ships, guns, tanks, planes and ammunition, plus a preponderance of man-power to overwhelm our foes. Under the stress and excitement of war it was not difficult to raise all the billions of dollars necessary to finance this enormous war effort. 

But the problems of Peace are far more complicated, as already pointed out.

Let us face the startling fact that not until this World War II became a Global war, have all the workers of the “civilized" nations been fully employed. 

Is not this a fearful indictment of our capitalistic system, when millions of workers must remain idle and their families live in poverty, except the nations go to war and spend billions upon armaments?

That is what happened in Germany. Hitler rallied the millions of German unemployed, set them all to work preparing for war and then drenched the world in blood. 

If Hitler is the "incarnation of the world's Evil," then what about the "Evil", particularly the evil of unemployment and poverty?

Therefore, when the battle flags are furled, it will not be a simple matter to "beat our swords into plowshares", and our "spears into pruning hooks", until we learn to distribute those plowshares and pruning hooks and the thousand-andone other articles of peaceful living, which modern technology can produce in such abundance to over 1000 million destitute people in the world. 

For 15 years now, we have heard about this great paradox of poverty in the midst of plenty. Now that paradox must be solved by the democratic Western nations, if they wish to avoid a communistic revolution sweeping the world.

Some of our Church groups are carrying on big Peace Campaigns, urging their members to write to the nation's leaders, expressing their ideas on Peace. 

But why ask men what they think of a righteous Peace?

Why not study God's Laws for Peace on Earth, as given in the Bible and explained by Jesus Christ, the Prince of 

Peace? 

Millions of people in these dark days are turning to the Bible for comfort and guidance. But how many understand what they read and how few practice what the Book teaches? 

People of the Christian nations have tried to practice the Ten Commandments, but the economic laws of the Old Testament and Christ's Laws of the Kingdom of God have never been practiced by any nation and by very few churches in modern times. 

Now the harvest time has come and the nations are reaping what they have sown. As the English scholar and engineer, Mr. David Davidson wrote: "Having become forgetful of His admonition, 'Seek ye first the Kingdom of God and His righteousness, and all these things shall be added unto you'-civilization is now being stripped of 'those things' to prepare mankind to seek for the 'Kingdom of God'. 

For lacking the spirit, civilization is now failing, as our Lord Jesus Christ said it would fail. Bereft of His spirit, and acclaiming only His name-praising in word and despising and rejecting in spirit-civilization is tottering to its fall.” 

Mr. Davidson wrote this in 1926. Since then our materialistic civilization has been tottering to its fall and since 1939, is being shot to pieces.

I also wish to quote here a remark made by George Bernard Shaw, years before this war; "After reviewing the world of human events for 60 years, I am prepared to state that I see no way out of the world's misery, except the way that Christ would take were He to undertake the work of a modern statesman.”

Yes, Christ's way is the only way to Total Peace on Earth; and it is only a matter of a few years now before our clergy, our statesmen and politicians, our economists and editors, will be compelled to study His ways and recommend them to the people of America and the British Commonwealth.

Mr. R. S. Newcomb, the author of this book, has explained in it the Laws of God, which form the only basis for Total Peace. He has explained them better and simpler than any other author we know of and has applied these Laws to our age of abundance.

Mr. Newcomb is a citizen of New Zealand, where he is well known as a radio broadcaster. He is also known throughout the British Empire as a writer on Christian economics. His illustrations of social and economic conditions are mostly taken from England and New Zealand, but those same conditions exist in America also. The message of 

FROM TOTAL WAR TO TOTAL PEACE is universal; it is of paramount importance to the American people and to all those who are looking for lasting Peace. This book has had a large circulation in Australasia since its publication two years ago-long before a book with a similar title was published in New York.

We are pleased to have the privilege of bringing an American Edition of Mr. Newcomb's book before the perplexed people of our country. We recommend his book to all those who are planning for Peace; to all the Church Groups who are campaigning for Peace; and to all the millions of people in America, Canada, and the world over, who are praying and longing for Peace.

There can be no Peace on earth until we have put into operation the laws and principles of the Prince of Peace. 

F. Haberman.

Introduction 

Total War! For the first time-and let us hope it will be for the last time-the peoples of practically the whole of the world are engaged in Total War. Quite apart from the enormous numbers actively engaged in the Fighting Forces, owing to the nature of modern warfare the nations are organized as never before for the production of those things essential for the prosecution of the war. Practically the whole world is divided into two opposing camps-Allied or Axis. Due to the development of aerial warfare, civilians, as well as the armed forces, are now in the front line. The life of every one of us is affected; the life of every one of us is in danger. 

There is no escape. The whole nation-old men, young men, women and children-is engaged in this war. It is Total War. 

And because this is Total War as never before, and because the life of every one of us is affected as never before, we are being forced to think as never before. 

As we look back to the grim days of 1939, we realize that we had no alternative but to fight. We did not want war; we were tragically unprepared for war. Now we know that we British people were faced with war or slavery to a hideous order based on might and cruel power and greed. And looking back, notwithstanding all the sufferings and anguish of more than three years-the blood and tears, and toil and sweat-and notwithstanding all that may lie ahead, we are proud of our decision.

But while we are working and denying ourselves for this war as never before, we are thinking-thinking. We see the utter futility of war, the waste of precious lives, the waste of time, of effort and materials, the suffering. We see the heroism, the willing self-sacrifice of our people; we see the tremendous strain and effort almost gladly borne. And, as we think, we know that, notwithstanding all these heroic efforts, at heart we have a hatred of war. So in the midst of a war in which we are glad to give of our best, we are forming a determination that war must end. Somehow or other we must contrive not merely to end war, but we must

abolish even the fear of war, and not for ourselves alone, but for the whole world.

Yet, as we think, we do not want to go back to the pre-war conditions, to the state of affairs between wars. There was another fear then-a horrible, gaunt fear that haunted most of us day and night—a fear even worse than the fear of war.

There was the fear of want, the numbing fear of economic insecurity.

The war itself is adding to this pre-war problem. Great 

Britain and America are organized as vast units for the production of war material. New factories have been erected everywhere. Millions are now trained mechanics, machinists, all busily engaged in production of every kind. When peace comes, as it must some day, the millions of men and women in the forces will be freed from military duties, and they also must be absorbed into peace-time vocations. 

Here is a huge problem in itself. It cannot be solved by the 

Unemployment Dole. Already the fear of want, of unemployment, is looming ahead.

As we look back, however, we see that this Total War is leading us towards a spirit of mutual help, of self-sacrifice, and equality, and in them we see a gleam of hope for the future. These grand qualities fostered by war must be developed for peace.

It was probably a realization of this new spirit animating the British peoples which caused the Rt. Hon. Winston 

Churchill in his address before a joint session of Congress at Washington on 26th December, 1941, to say, "He must indeed be a blind soul who cannot see some great purpose being worked out here below, for which we have the honor to be the faithful servants.”

So in addition to abolishing the fear of war, we also desire to eliminate the fear of want. His Majesty the King put this thought in its simplest form when he said, “We must not only win the War; we must also win the Peace." 

INSTEAD OF TOTAL WAR WE WANT TOTAL PEACE 

To win the Peace, we have to deal with matters of far greater magnitude than the military peace. We have to win the Social Peace, the Economic Peace, the Industrial Peace. 

To win the Peace, we have to destroy all those things which contribute towards War. 10

So long as there is "Poverty amidst Plenty", we have not won the Peace. 

So long as there are slums, inequality, injustice, the fear of want, class distinctions and privilege, the lack of those things we associate with the term "Social Justice"-so long as these evils remain we shall not have won the Peace. 

It is now generally recognized that a faulty economic and social system is productive of more suffering and evil than is war itself. Authorities inform us that the depression of 

1928-36 produced more deaths-from suicide, starvation, etc. -to say nothing of disease, suffering and misery, than did the Great War of 1914-18. The military deaths and suffering are confined, more or less, to the period of the war, but the evil effects the deaths, suffering, misery-of a defective economic and social system continue day and night without ceasing through the centuries. 

So we see that Total Peace is more than a military objective. It comprises all the spheres of man's interest and conduct in this world. It involves reorganizing the world in such a manner as will secure freedom, happiness, equality, prosperity and security for everybody, and the taking out of our structure of all those things which lead to strife and war.

Total Peace means no less than a complete change in the hearts of men, of nations, and of the world. It is an enormous task, but it is a glorious one well worthy of our every effort.

Admittedly it is not going to be an easy task-this endeavor to form a new world. All kinds of obstacles and objections will be put in the way. Some will say that our first job is to win the war, then we can talk about this Utopia. 

Others will say let things alone, they will right themselves. 

But it will be found that the most active advocates of leavethings-alone are mainly the so-called "successful" men who fear the social upheaval after the war, and who find delay an excellent method of keeping the poor in their places and preventing inconvenient discussion about social reform. 

The difficulty of trying to plan for the future peace when we are in the midst of war may, to some, seem to make it useless. But it is not useless. Without faith and without hope that at the end of this dark tunnel of war there is going 11

to be a better world we shall lose courage and confidence. 

If we are to be sustained in spirit during the bitter tragedies and heavy sacrifices the task of defeating the Axis forces will impose upon us, we must have something really worth while to look forward to.

The war will cease one day, and for that day we must be prepared. We must plan now,for assuredly if we do not plan now chaos will again result. 

Is "Total Peace" just a visionary idea, another example of wishful thinking? That depends entirely upon us. If we think that we can treat these tremendous matters exclusively as economic and social problems, then we are doomed to failure. We must delve deeper than that. 

It is perfectly true that most of the evils in this world of ours are occasioned by a defective economic system. But the cure will not be effected by changing one economic system for another economic system-unless that new economic system be based on fundamental truths. Our New Order, if it is to succeed, is not to be governed by an economic system; but the economic system of the New Order must be based on fundamental ideals and principles which are founded on man's highest and noblest aspirations. It is not a system but a Crusade, which will reach men's hearts and minds and bring about Total Peace.

The survey published by the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace gets to the root of the problem when it says, "The real issue to be faced is not 'What is the most efficient formula to arrive at a new integration of society?' but rather 'What is the truth to be served by men?' That

Truth is to be found in the teachings of Jesus Christ. 

This book is an endeavor to lead us to that Truth-the 

Truth wherein lies "Total Peace." 12

THE PEACE THAT WAS LOST. 

CHAPTER 1. 

FROM WAR TO WAR 

In the years 1914 to 1918 we fought what was then called "The Great War". It was also termed the "War to end war". 

We fought "To make the British Empire and the United States a place fit for heroes to live in." But although we were victorious, the War of 1914-18 did not end war, nor can the most optimistic amongst us say before war broke out again in September, 1939, that England or America was a fit home for heroes. Why were these splendid hopes frustrated?

Let us bring to our mind what happened in those years between the wars. 

From soon after the War until 1928 we experienced what is termed "a boom." Owing to numerous causes money was plentiful. During the actual fighting of 1914-18 manufacturers of the thousand and one things required for the waging of war took advantage of the nations' necessity and made high profits-you remember the "War profiteers."

Labor also took advantage of the situation and demandedand obtained-high wages.

When the fighting ended a grateful nation paid gratuities to its discharged fighting men, made advances to those who married for their homes and for furniture. Advances were made for the purchase of farms and businesses. Oh, yes, money was plentiful, business boomed. The demand for labor exceeded the supply, and Jack not merely became as good as his master, but he thought himself to be a good deal better. Ah, yes, Peace was good after those frightful four and a half years of war.

It never occurred to us that there was anything artificial in the "prosperity" of those days. This state of plenty was to continue and be permanent. Inventions developed during the war were now to be turned to the requirements of peace. 

Machines would produce a tremendous proportion of our needs, and even luxuries, at reduced costs. Everything was going to be wonderful, and the world was not such a bad 14

world after all. So we indulged in an era of extravagant spending and wild speculation. 

Do you remember all those subdivisions of land and the high-pressure salesmen who assured us that we would double our money in two or three years, things were progressing so? Here in New Zealand butterfat rose to record prices; so up boomed the price of farm lands, until it appeared that there was more money to be made in dealing in farms than in actually farming them. Farms were sold and re-sold-but not for cash. Each succeeding purchaser paid a little cash and gave another mortgage for the balance of the new purchase price over the existing mortgages and this was repeated and repeated until some farms were burdened in extreme instances with five or more mortgages! The profit of each succeeding vendor was represented by a scrap of paper-a second, third, fourth and fifth-but we were all content. Was not this the best of all worlds, and were not things going to continue like this-well, maybe not forever, but for our lifetime at least?

Then in 1928, the bubble burst. It was called, very politely, the depression. It extended world-wide and became the greatest depression this world has ever known. Despite-or perhaps because of the efforts our politicians and economists for eight long weary years this world suffered as it had never suffered before.

Let us recall some of the things that happened in those years 1928 to 1936-nay, if we are wise, we will never forget them.

The Rev. P. T. R. Kirk in his book "The Cross and So ciety" states:- “In 1933, 2,400,000 people died of starvation, and another 

1,200,000 committed suicide through pressure of economic want.

In that same year it is reported that there were destroyed 

56,000,000 pounds of tinned meat, 267,000 sacks of coffee, 140,000 carloads of rice, 570,000 carloads of grain, and another 420,000 carloads were burned as fuel. Incalculable amounts of sugar, milk, cattle, and cotton were also destroyed "Yet our school children and expectant mothers lacked sufficient daily milk to keep them in health and strength.” 15

It would be easy to fill page after page with instances of the destruction of livestock, foodstuffs, etc.-millions of cattle and pigs slaughtered; millions of bags of coffee destroyed; millions of bushels of wheat burnt. In England milk was poured down the drain.

But we did more than destroy these products—or as some people put it in those days, "Throw God's bounty back in His face"-we also restricted production. In this chaotic period the British Government invited other nations to attend an Economic Conference to try to find a solution of our difficulties. The experts of 66 nations attended. And the only result of weeks of deliberation by these great economic experts was an endeavor to destroy the abundance! Restrictions were imposed on the growing of wheat, tea, sugar,etc. 

English farmers (including a former British Prime Minister, the Rt. Hon. David Lloyd George) were fined for producing too many potatoes. So destruction and restriction of foodstuffs went on, while Sir John Boyd Orr, the distinguished expert on Food and Health, calculated that one half of the population of Britain was inadequately fed. 

The United States also participated in this scheme of restriction, in fact the Government actually paid farmers NOT to produce! Up to the end of 1935, over 182 million dollars had been paid to farmers for not producing corn or hogs. 

Yet in 1933 there were nearly 13 million unemployed in U. S. 

A. If an average of two persons were dependent on each unemployed, this meant that practically a quarter of the population of U. S. A., the richest country in the world, was unprovided for and dependent upon charity. 

If any country in the world can literally consider itself a land flowing with milk and honey it is New Zealand. Yet do you not remember the suffering and poverty of those years, the drives for old clothes, etc.?

Let us summarize this period carefully. It was a period of intense suffering, mental and physical. There were more deaths from starvation and suicide than were caused by the Great War. Unemployment was on a scale never before experienced, in fact whole districts in England, now known as the Distressed Areas, were unemployed and remained so until war broke out again in 1939. The paper fortunes of the 16

boom speculator disappeared over-night. Fear and anxiety abounded everywhere.

Yet during those years Nature produced as abundantly as ever. There was such a surplus of foodstuffs and raw materials that it was destroyed. We destroyed when people were dying for lack of the very things we destroyed. 

Historians looking back upon this period will surely be justified in thinking we were insane. 

In 1936, however, economic conditions improved only to be succeeded by fears and threats of another war, a war which threatened to be far more terrible even than the Great War of 1914-18. War, actually broke out in September, 1939, and now the whole earth is involved in a life and death struggle of a ferocity and magnitude never before imagined. 

Thus we have completed the circle-war to war. Yet the amazing thing is that our economists told us these things would happen; and they tell us that in the same conditions 

THEY WILL HAPPEN AGAIN. 

Now if there is one thing upon which we who experienced the awful conditions of the last twenty-five years should have definitely made up our minds, it is that neither we nor our children should again go through such a terrible experience. We will not however escape a repetition of this evil by "wishful thinking,"-there must be clear, logical thinking. We must find out the root causes of our troubles, for it is only by finding out and eliminating the SOURCE of our economic difficulties that we can hope to find a new and better order of society.

Why, then, did we experience the economic disaster of 1928-36? 17

CHAPTER 2. 

CHAOS. 

The economist shows us that certain economic causes will produce certain effects and that those causes will always produce the same results under the same conditions. From the action of these economic causes and effects he derives certain economic laws. For instance, the economist tells us that if there is an over-production of any class of goods, the price of those goods will decline; on the other hand, if there is a shortage, the price of those particular goods will increase. On these simple facts is based the Law of Supply and 

Demand. Note carefully, however, the words "under the same conditions." The economist, as such, is not necessarily concerned as to whether our economic order is a good one or a bad one; his main concern is to show the effects that will be produced by certain causes, and he does so in the main from the viewpoint of our existing economic order. 

This explanation is necessary, as in the last chapter it was stated the economist knew that booms and depressions must occur, and that they would continue to do so. 

We are all aware of what is termed "the Trade Cycle.” "Thus the state of trade," to use the famous words of Lord Overstone-"revolves apparently in an established cycle. 

First we find it in a state of quiescence-next, improvement -growing confidence-prosperity-excitement-over-trading-convulsion - pressure stagnation - distress-ending again in quiescence." (A. and M. P. Marshall: Economics of Industry, pp. 152-3). 

Put in every-day language, the above statement shows that our present economic system is such that it produces and will ever continue to produce a succession (or cycles) of booms and depressions, booms and depressions, with all its attendant sufferings and evils.

Do not get the idea that the depression of 1928-36 was unusual. It only varied from previous depressions in intensity. 

In 1914-18 we had the greatest war of all time up to the 18

date. It was followed by the greatest depression. Let me give you an illustration of what then happened to our financial machinery. Suppose you had a motor car which just jogged along at twenty miles per hour. True you had occasionally to get out to oil it here and there and tighten an odd nut or two, still it got you there after a fashion. Then supposing there came an emergency when you tried to drive that defective motor at one hundred miles per hour: What would happen? An utter irremediable disaster, chaos. Now that is exactly what happened in our economic system. 

From the time (about the year 1700), when Britain first started to borrow, until 1914, the National Debt of Great Britain amounted to £700 millions. But in the four years of war, £7,000 millions were added to the National Debt. In other words, the defective economic system which jogged along with occasional breakdowns and which dealt with £700 millions in two hundred years, was suddenly speeded up to deal with ten times that sum in four years, and, as in the case of the defective motor car, the sudden speeding-up caused the breakdown of the economic machine with the resulting chaos.

Now we are engaged in an even greater war, and borrowing even more colossal sums, and it is impossible to escape the inevitable economic consequences if we continue under the same economic system. Already some people are beginning to be afraid of what will happen after the war. Remember the axiom; economic causes, under the same conditions, must produce the same results. 

During the slump period following the last war, a World Economic Conference was held in London at which sixtysix countries were represented by the world's leading economists. This World Conference was opened by the late 

King George V., and in his inaugural address His Majesty said, "It cannot be beyond the power of men so to use the vast resources of the world as to insure the material progress of civilization. No diminution in those resources has taken place. On the contrary, discovery, invention, and organization have multiplied their possibilities to such an extent that abundance of production has itself created new problems." 

Put in its simplest form, as is indicated by His late Maj- 19

esty's words, this is the problem the delegates had to solve —on the one hand we have a tremendous surplus of food and other supplies; on the other hand, there are millions of people starving for want of that food and those supplies. What shall we do? 

I can hear you say, "Do you call that a problem? Why, a child could answer it. Give that surplus food to those starving people." Ah, yes, that is what a child would answer. It is the obvious, the ideal answer. But the foremost economists of the world could not give that answer. And they could not give that answer because OUR ECONOMIC SYS- 

TEM WOULD NOT ALLOW THEM TO DO SO. The world's most highly trained experts could find no solution to the problem you tell me a child could answer, so the surplus food was destroyed and the people starved. You know this is true-it actually happened. We did it, or rather permitted it. Looking back, it seems madness. It was madness, yet we were forced to do it by our economic system. 

If all that surplus wheat, for instance, had been put upon the market, prices would have dropped below the cost of production and the producers would have been ruined. The whole producing, wholesale and retail organization, together with the banking institutions which financed them, would have been disorganized, and the financial consequences would have been disastrous. 

The World Economic Conference failed. But immediately afterwards, agreements were reached whereby production was restricted. Governments supported these restrictive and destructive schemes. Yet all the time millions of people were suffering from starvation and malnutrition. We definitely threw God's gift back in His face—and got the punishment we deserved. And it was done-and in the same circumstances will be done again-because under our existing economic system, money with its personal profits, vested interests and class privilege was considered to be of more importance than the bodies of men. 

Describing conditions in Britain between wars, Mr. Francis 

Williams in his book "Democracy's Last Battle" says— "Throughout the period between the last war and this war, the number of unemployed in Britain ranged about ten per 20

cent to twenty per cent of the working population. Even during the prosperous years of that period one out of every ten of the workers of Britain was forced to live on the dole. 

During the years of depression, two out of every ten were thrown aside as useless by the industrial system of this country. "During that same period, immense fortunes were, with the benevolent approval of society, made by speculators on the Stock Exchange, and the great commodity markets, by financial and issuing houses, by directors and shareholders of companies manufacturing luxury and semi-luxury goods. 

To travel from the West End of London to one of the distressed areas of South Wales or Lancashire or Durham during those years was to see not two nations but two worlds with no bridge between them." (p. 149). "During that period, the ability of the great industrial nations of the world to produce grew enormously. Yet this increased productive power failed altogether to abolish or even reduce the evils of gross inequality and great poverty. On the contrary, these evils increased. Because the development of great industries was left exclusively in the hands of those, who whether they wished it or not, were forced by the system they operated to think primarily in terms of profit-making.. .. we were altogether incapable of distributing to the men and women who so sorely needed it, the wealth we had produced." (p. 157).

The economist foresaw this difficulty of distribution quite clearly. In his book published in 1924, four years before the depression commenced, Professor B. E. Murphy wrote, "It (distribution), tends to be moulded in accordance with the interests of the dominant class for the time being. 

In production man wrestles with Nature; in distribution man fights man." (Outlines of Economics, p. 18).

Nobody can deny the truths contained in the foregoing extracts. And certainly no one would dare to suggest that an economic order which produces such results is a good system.

The economic system under which we function is known as "Capitalism." Let us then examine this system in order to ascertain if we can, the reason why it produces such disastrous results. 21

CHAPTER 3. 

THE CAUSE. 

When planning this book I determined at this point to include a number of extracts from books on economics to show that the defects inherent in our economic system, and consequently the ill-effects which must inevitably arise from those defects, were well known to the orthodox economist. 

But I have decided not to do so for two main reasons. First, to many of my readers they would not be interesting reading, and secondly and primarily, there are very few people who have given this matter even a modicum of thought who are not satisfied that our present economic system is a lamentable failure. 

If you have any doubts upon this matter I ask you to read again the preceding chapters and let your mind travel through the past twenty years, then ask yourself if you are satisfied with the system which produces and perpetuates such an evil state of affairs. Are you satisfied that vested interests and class privilege should be dominant--that wealth, and the power wealth produces, should be concentrated into comparatively so few hands-that the vast bulk of mankind should be in a state of perpetual fear, the fear of want, of old age? Of course you are not satisfied with such a state of affairs, and you cannot be satisfied with the system which produces them.

For the moment we are not concerned with details. We have a defective system-we want to know the cause of those defects, for it is only by knowing the cause that we can apply the remedy.

And the prime cause of the world's troubles lies in the fact-the acknowledged fact-that the motive of our present economic order is self-interest. "Self-interest" is, of course, a polite way of indicating selfishness and personal greed. It is not the needs of the people, but "what can I make out of it" which governs our actions. We know this to be true. Under our existing economic system a manufacturer will not man- 22

ufacture his product unless he can dispose of it at a profit. 

The needs of the people only interest him so long as he can make money for himself by catering for those needs. If, however, people are too poor to pay the price of his product, our economic system prevents the distribution of such goods, whether it be food or clothing, in utter disregard to the needs of the people.

Let me quote two examples of what this means. Through the advancement of science we are to-day able to manufacture very excellent aids for hearing. These instruments are a tremendous boon to those who unfortunately are deficient in hearing. With their aid, deaf people are enabled to converse with their friends, hear music, enjoy the cheerful laughter of children, the songs of birds. Without such aid, life may be an eternity of silence. The price of these helpful instruments, however, is such that only the comparatively well-to-do can afford them. No one would suggest for one moment that the need to hear of one deaf person is not so great as another's simply because he is poor. Yet thousands of our fellow beings are deprived of the benefit of these tremendous helps simply and solely because they cannot afford the price. In effect, our economic system says to these unfortunate people, "I do not care if you do not hear another sound as long as you live-I do not care if you fail to hear that warning shout which would save your life; you will not have one of these wonderful instruments which modern science enables me to produce unless you give me my price-my profit." The same motive applies to everything we produce. Profit, self-interest dominates. The needs of people, whether they be of food, clothing, or shelter, are of no consequence if there is no profit. 

Let me remind you again of the World Economic Conference and its unsolved problem: "A surplus of food, starving millions. What shall we do? The needs of the people were there, God knows; the food was there; but again our economic system said, "Unless I get my profit, I will destroy that food, and the people can starve." So, as the profit was not forthcoming, food-precious God-given food-was destroyed and people starved. Bitter words, but alas, the even more bitter truth!

CHAPTER 1. 

THE TRUE LAW. 

The whole vast creation is governed by, and acts in accordance with, the Laws of its Creator. Therein lies a definite and tremendous fact.

Science tells us of worlds, many times larger than our own, so remote from Earth that the distance is measured by the speed of light. Yet so perfectly do those worlds act according to the Laws of the Creator that their course and their position at any moment are known with exactitude. 

Halley's Comet, for instance, has an orbit of about seventy years, but so accurately does it obey the Law that scientists can tell us exactly when and where the Comet will again become visible. Coming nearer, we know the time of the rising and setting of the Sun to seconds; we know the waxing and waning of the Moon. We can foretell the eclipse of the Sun to seconds; we know exactly where the shadow of the Moon will fall on the Earth, and we know precisely how long it will last. Months before the actual eclipse, scientific associations send out expeditions to the most suitable localities to make observations and take photographs. The combined influence of the Moon and Sun creates the ocean tides, and so accurately do the Moon and Sun obey the Creator's Laws that we can calculate the tides for any part of the world for to-day, next week, next year, or the next century with absolute accuracy.

In contrast with the great worlds of astronomy is the atom. In an article in the "Readers Digest" for August, 1941, Mr. Bruce Bliven says:- "I know of no more vivid introduction to this atomic world than the figure used by Mr. Henry Schacht. Look at a handkerchief says Mr. Schacht, and imagine yourself getting miraculously smaller while the handkerchief remains the same size. You are standing in a great snowy plain of linen. As you grow smaller you find you are stand- 29

ing on one of the threads, with gaps on both sides of you. A minute later the thread turns into a bundle of fibres with open spaces around them. Smaller still, you fall between two of the fibres. Now imagine a tremendously accelerated shrinking and you are floating in space among what seems to be planets, suns and moons, furiously revolving. You are now in the world of the atom. "Each atom is probably like an infinitely tiny solar system. It is composed of a nucleus which corresponds to the sun, and much smaller electrons which revolve about the nucleus like planets. The nucleus itself is not solid; it is made up of protons and neutrons. Protons are particles with a positive electric charge, electrons are charged negatively and neutrons have no charge. "There are so many atoms in a single drop of water that if the entire population of the world started counting them busily, day and night, it would take 10,000 years."

The point I wish to emphasize is that these minute particles, so small that they are impossible for us to conceive, all act in accordance with the Laws of the Creator. 

We now arrive at this fact, that the whole creation from the infinitely great to the infinitely small and everything in between, is governed by and acts in accordance with the Laws of the Divine Creator-everything, that is, except man. And he alone out of all this vast creation has been given free will. But although God gave mankind free will,

He did not leave him without guidance. God has provided 

Laws for mankind just as He made Laws for the government of all the rest of His Creation. As a Loving Father He gave man guiding precepts and He even sent His Son to show us the way. Man can obey God's Laws, or he can disregard them; and man, in his pride and arrogance, has made his choice. "I have got brains, I have got intelligence," he says, "I know what is best for myself; I will make my own laws." So man, thinking he knew more than his Creator, made his decision. Do not say this is a fanciful picture. It is terribly true. Have not you and I said "Religion (the worship of God) has no part in business." "Religion and politics do not mix." Yes, we have made our choice, and we have chosen man-made laws in preference to God's Laws made for man. 30

The result, so far as human life is concerned, would be chaos, death. Again, imagine that freewill was given to the atom, the electrons, and as we take up a piece of bread, the electrons suddenly decide to change their formation and the bread becomes iron. Again, chaos, death. Yet this is exactly what we have done ourselves. We have abandoned the Laws of God. We have created for ourselves another god, mammon. Now we have to pay, and are paying, the inevitable price-chaos, death. The remedy, of course, is obvious. If we are to escape from the evils which at present dominate our lives and produce the chaos of wars, of famine and of poverty, and in their place attain to that happiness and freedom which is man's natural birthright, we, like every other part of God's creation, must obey the laws of our Creator.

THE APOSTOLIC COMMUNITY. 

In the early Chapters of the Acts of the Apostles there is described an economic and social community inuagurated by the Apostles of a nature entirely different to that of their time, which like ours was Capitalism. St. Luke, the author of the Acts of the Apostles, writing some thirty odd years after the event, describes this community with enthusiasm. Here is what he says: "And all that believed were together, and had all things common; and sold their possessions and goods, and parted them to all men, as every man had need. "And the multitude of them that believed were of one heart and of one soul; neither said any of them that ought of the things which he possessed was his own; but they had all things common. Now there is a most amazing thing about the establishment of this community. Briefly, the Acts of the Apostles commences with an account of the Risen Christ who spent forty days with the Apostles "speaking of the things pertaining to the Kingdom of God." Then there was the

amazing thing for such inspired men to do! Viewed in this light it becomes of the utmost importance that we endeavor to examine this system and try to find out their reason for establishing it. One thing is perfectly certain. The Apostles did not establish this community simply for the fun of the thing; there must have been a definite reason behind their action.

First let us examine the economic order established by the Apostles. From St. Luke's account it is quite clear that the early Christians sold all their possessions and gave the proceeds into a common fund from which each received according to his need. This does not necessarily mean that having surrendered his possessions, be they large or small, into the central fund, the early Christian was pensioned off for life and did not have to work any more. As a matter of fact, the vast majority of the inhabitants of Palestine at that time were very poor. They were subject to the double taxation of Rome and of the Temple, either of which would have been a burden in itself, but combined the burden was staggering. The result was that the great mass of the population was in a state of permanent poverty and destitution. 

If then, the Apostles had offered a kind of permanent pension without any obligation to work, all, or nearly all, of the inhabitants of Palestine would have wanted to participate in such a desirable scheme. For such a prize it would have been quite easy to profess to be a "Christian." As a matter of fact, owing to their poverty, the average contribution paid in to the central fund by each individual would be very small, certainly not nearly enough to keep him or her for life. In the vast majority of cases the most valuable asset of the convert would be his ability to work, and I think we are on quite sound ground if we consider that, in the new order established by the Apostles, the newly converted Christians continued at their normal work, but contributed their earnings into the common fund. 

The new order was, of course, terminated in Jerusalem soon after the martyrdom of Stephen, when "a great persecution was established against the Church at Jerusalem, and all were scattered abroad throughout the regions of Judaea and Samaria except the apostles." (Acts 8, v. 1.) 

While, of course, the dispersal of the early Christians 52

CHAPTER 11 THE FIRST STEP TOWARDS CHRISTIANITY 

The purpose of this book is to help us to become Christians. 

Christianity does not consist merely of singing hymns and psalms, of preaching and praying, of subscribing to missions and giving occasional sums to what we term charities. 

Christianity is something greater and grander than that. 

Christianity is the whole way of life-it is the one and only true way of life. Christianity is the way of life that must rule every moment and every thing throughout the whole of our lives. Christianity must govern the economic factor, the social factor, as well as what we now consider to be the religious factor. 

We cannot put Christianity in a water-tight compartment for use on special occasions only, and exclude it from the other functions of life. Yet that is what we try to do. "You cannot mix religion and business" we say. In our daily social and business intercourse with each other, which, of course, occupies the greater part of our time, we use a social and economic system which is acknowledgedly based on self-interest, and which not merely encourages but actually rewards the worst instincts of man-greed, avarice and covetousness.

Christianity, on the other hand, is based on Love-love of 

God and love of our neighbor.

We have tried, and are still trying, to reconcile the irreconcilable. We are trying to reconcile self-interest (which is the essense of mammon), with love. We are serving mammon, and at the same time trying to serve God-with the greater part of our time spent in the service of mammon. 

We are trying to do the impossible. And therein lies the reason for the apparent failure of Christianity. Christianity with its matchless ideals of love and service should sweep and dominate the whole world, but it has been checked and held back by the degrading spirit of self-interest, which is 88

the absolute negation of Christianity. Christianity, however, has not failed, it is our conception of it that has failed. Mr. G. K. Chesterton spoke very truly when he said Christianity has not yet been tried.

For this apparent failure of Christianity we ourselves are to blame. With the utmost clarity and definiteness Jesus told us, "Ye cannot serve God and mammon." We can serve one or the other, but not both. We cannot be followers of mammon six days of the week, then try to be Christians on the seventh.

Having told us, "Ye cannot serve God and mammon," Jesus did not end there. He told us what we are to do. "Therefore," said Jesus, "because you cannot serve God and mammon, Seek ye first the Kingdom of God and His righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you."

If we may paraphrase this portion of the Sermon on the

Love, and also riches or wealth, which is self-interest. Remember that where your treasure is, there will your heart be also. If you make riches your aim, it will absorb all your thoughts and time. CHAPTER 12 ORGANIZED RELIGION 

At the time of a Parliamentary election some years ago, I asked a clerical friend which party he anticipated would win the election, only to receive the somewhat lofty reply, "I am not interested. Politics and religion do not mix." 

Let us examine this answer. True politics (as distinct from party politics), is the science of living. True religion (as distinct from denominational theology) also is surely the science of living. Unfortunately, however, by divorcing religion from our way of living, many of our clerical friends have conveyed to us the impression that religion is the science of dying! In actual fact we see that true religion and true politics should dovetail into each other, and that our whole everyday lives should be lived in accordance with the laws governing our religious profession as Christians.

Nevertheless, my clerical friend did correctly describe the general attitude of Organized Religion. We also know that religion and business do not mix. In fact, if you come to think of it, religion does not seem to mix with, or fit into, any of the everyday activities of mankind. So while religion may be very commendable on Sundays, it must not be allowed to intrude into any of those activities in which we engage in the course of our ordinary business or social lives. Thus religion has been separated from those things whereby we live and have our being. 

This is not to say that in organized religion there have not been outstanding personalities, who, from time to time, have pitted their individual strength against the evils of the huge juggernaut system which we know as capitalism.

But apart from the personal efforts of these Christian men, there has been created an unfortunate impression that organized religion as such is not greatly interested in earthly matters and conditions. against these blots upon our social order, it has rather encouraged the idea that such things are the will of God; there must be poor and there must be rich in the world, but hereafter and during eternity the division of things will be different. "You cannot have things both ways," and so the poor have encouraged themselves with the thought that their good time is in the hereafter. "Don't cry. When you die, there's pie in the sky" is the term used to designate this attitude. "It does not matter much what happens to you now, it is the hereafter you must think about-it is not the body, it is the soul that matters," as if the body were given us by a different and inferior God to the God who gave us a soul!

Can you imagine that helpless, innocent children are deliberately placed amid the squalor and misery of slum conditions by the will of God? Of course they are not. Slums, and their inescapable suffering and misery, are the inevitable result of our man-made economic system with its motive power of self-interest where the weakest must go to the wall. Man, not God, must bear the responsibility; yet organized religion has in the past, and to a certain extent, as the 95

foregoing quotation shows, still is pouring soothing syrup over such conditions. Even yet it has not conducted a vigorous campaign against slums and the evil system which brings them about. Can it be that organized religion is afraid that if it did so it may lose the financial support of its wealthy members?

Fortunately, however, there are signs that the Church is awakening. At the Conference the evils that the Church had condoned were very freely discussed, and from those discussions evolved a new and more comprehensive conception of the status and responsibilities of the Church. The then Archbishop of Canterbury realized that the Church must assume the leadership towards the New Order we are all determined to bring about, and so resigned to make way for a younger and more active man to direct the Church's efforts in this time.

Without such an understanding, any such campaign can only result in pious platitudes, however eloquently they may be expressed. Today we do not want eloquence, we want facts. It is little help to be airily told that we must run our businesses on Christian ideals; we must be shown what those ideals are, how they are to be applied, and what is their effect. GOD'S FAMILY—THE NATION. 

It is, of course, quite obvious that the smallest unit in which the New Christian Order can be established is a nation or a country. We cannot change an economic order in our individual life. I may think, for instance, that a decimal system of coinage is preferable to our existing English system, but I cannot set up such a system for myself; it must be done by the nation of which I am a member. So the starting point in the establishment of the New Order is a New NATIONAL economy. 

From whichever angle we approach the question of the New Christian Order we inevitably and inescapably are driven to the fact that the Laws, and the only Laws, which are to govern a true Christian Order fundamentally are the Two Great Commandments. Jesus told us so again and again. It will therefore be advantageous if we gather together those fundamental economic and social principles which we have seen are contained in the Two Great Commandments and see what they mean as applied to our national economy.

Foremost, it is quite clear that all real wealth, in its original form, has been given to us by God; that each one of us has a duty towards the proper utilization of that wealth; and that each is entitled to his proper and equal share of the resulting product of God's beneficence and man's labor.

We have seen that interest and profit making are contrary to God's definite command. In the New Christian 

Order, therefore, goods must not be made for profit but for use. The needs of man and not the profit we can make out of him is the measure of production. 

We have further seen that the direction of the New Order in all its activities is through "men such as fear God, men of truth, hating covetousness"-that character and ability will be the test for leadership, not birth nor political pull. 100

Party politics as we understand them to-day, will disappear. 

It is apparent that the logical development of all this involves complete national or communal ownership of everything except personal chattels. And this, as we have already seen, is precisely what happened in the Community inaugurated by the Apostles. The establishment of such an Order is, as Jesus told us, the first and the essential step towards a true Christian life. 

We are, therefore, to apply to our national economy those principles we individually have already experienced and which are so satisfactory in the well-ordered family; in fact the nation is to live as though it were one big family-God's family. It will therefore be seen that this is not altogether a "new" Order; it is only applying old and well-tried principles, which we have all experienced, but on a larger scale. 

The basis of equal participation in the benefits accruing from the efforts of the whole national family or community must be the contribution by each individual member of that family of the best effort of which he or she is capable, such individual effort being exerted in the sphere where those abilities can be best utilized for the common benefit. That, of course, is only another way of expressing the Second Great Commandment. By replacing the profit motive with an economy based on service to others (the community) there will be created a realization of the dignity of work and a resultant building-up of a spirit of co-operation and craftsmanship which takes pride in the job for the job's sake and not for monetary reward.

All this leads us to economic and social conditions and principles quite different from anything we have hitherto known. They have to be different; if they were not entirely different they would be useless. If you think of it, the economic principles of the New Order, if they are to be successful, must be the exact opposite of those governing our existing order. For where the present system produces poverty, the new system must provide plenty. Instead of class distinction and privilege, there must be equality and justice; for fear there must be security; for selfishness and greed, love; for competition, co-operation. We have to abandon the unequal, degrading, man-made system based 101

on selfishness, greed and lust for power, for the just, uplifting, God-given system based on love and service to our fellow men. To effect that requires not merely a difference, but a revolution in our economic and social structure. "But," some may say, "this is all idealism. It is all right talking about these beautiful things, about God's Laws and all that sort of thing, but we are practical people, and we want to know if they will work? That is the test." Well, let us be practical and apply the test. Let us, however, first apply the test to our existing system!

It has produced every evil you can think of poverty, distress, crime, hectic booms and slumps, and finally it has landed us in the greatest tribulation the world has ever known. Do you call that a successs? Such a system, if it can be said to work, works not for God or man, but for the devil.

These Laws of God, what about them? First let us examine the credentials of God who made the Laws. God made this universe, and it runs quite satisfactorily according to its allotted plan and laws. The sun rises and sets at its appointed time each day. Regularly, each year, this earth has its allotted seasons of Spring, Summer, Autumn and Winter. In their season the little seeds germinate and blossom with a beauty that is entrancing. Yes, look where you will, you will see all creation "working" to perfection. 

And it does so because it is working according to the Laws of its Creator, God.

In the midst of all this success there is one failure only, and that failure is man. He alone has departed from God's 

Laws. God gave Laws for man's guidance, but man has declined them and made his own. 

By all means let us be practical. And the most practical common-sense question we can put to ourselves is this:- 

As God's Laws have been so entirely successful throughout the universe, are we not fully justified in believing that His Laws will be equally successful for the welfare of that part of His creation which He made in His own image and which is known as mankind? God created man. He has given His Laws to ensure happiness and love and prosperity to man. We will be doing the most practical, com- 102

mon-sense thing that mankind has ever done when we discard our “jungle” laws of self and accept the Laws of God. Yes, let us be practical and replace the Kingdom of 

Man with the Kingdom of God.

Our great difficulty in assessing the value of anything new is to divorce ourselves from existing conditions. We are apt to criticize any proposal for the betterment of our economic and social conditions from the viewpoint of our existing economic and social system. We say a thing cannot be done, when we really mean that particular objective cannot be reached under our present system. Our economic system, however, is not sacrosanct. The new economics must be the servant or medium to produce the desired effect, not the master to which all other efforts must be subordinated. In judging the merits of any proposal, the first and chief consideration is, will it be to the general benefit of the whole of mankind, and not merely to some individual or group of individuals. The true test is not, Is it best for ME? but Is it best for US?-not me, but my neighbor.

Judged by such a standard our existing economic order cuts a very sorry figure.

Judged by the same standard of human requirements and the satisfaction of human needs, the economic and social order of the Kingdom of God-God's family-survives every test.

When discussing the possibility of establishing a nation's economy on a communal basis, we are frequently told by the upholders of our present system with an air of finality, which is entirely unwarranted, that such a system must fail because "No one will do his best without incentive." 

By "incentive" is meant monetary reward. That such a remark can be made and believed only proves the accuracy of the statement made in Part One of this book, viz., that the motive force of our present system is self-interest. 

Such an appeal to "self-interest" is an appeal to man's worst qualities and therefore cannot produce the best results. 

If you look round the world you will see that it has not done so up to the present. In too many cases, this "incentive" of monetary reward results in getting someone else 103

to do the work for the least possible wage or payment and so increase the "incentive" for the investor.

Even under existing circumstances this statement is only partly true, for many, if not most of our scientific, mechanical, and medical discoveries have been made by men who were not thinking of financial gain. Such men were urged on by the desire to bring forth the idea they had conceived in their minds. Frequently the actual inventor has secured very little monetary reward; it is the commercial interests which secured the patent rights-and therby a monopoly-and exploited the invention for the purpose of profit making. But even in a communal economy there is no reason why rewards should not be given to those who merit them. In war time we recognize those who have performed acts of particular merit or bravery; and similar awards, and even titles, could, and probably will, be given to those who make a special contribution to the national welfare. We have a Distinguished Service Order for war; we can also have one for peace. 

When all is said and done, however, what greater incentive can there be than the knowledge that we are serving God and our fellow men. Money, and its power, as in our present economy, will have no place in the New Christian Order. Its place will be taken by a far loftier crown, the respect and love of our fellows. We can be millionaires in happiness.

In God's family, the Nation, it is obvious that all the members of that national family, are equal and are entitled to an equal portion of the national income produced by that family. No man can say, God gave me twice as much intelligence, or double the strength of some one else, because He wanted me to have double the income. It is, of course, quite true that all men have various gifts. Some heave special gifts in respect to organization, mechanics, production, and so forth. Others, we say, are "born" doctors, nurses, musicians, inventors, etc. Others again have great strength, and others have the gift of being able continuously to perform monotonous tasks.

The point is that these qualities are "gifts." We, ourselves, did not create these special qualities; all we can do is to develop our particular gift. The idea of a "gift" implies 104

a giver. It also implies a responsibility. For if the Great

Mr. Harold Fleming made the following statement:- "When the possibilities of a full-out economy are set out against a partially employed economy, the enormous yearin-and-year-out cost of normal peace-time economy in terms of lost production, lost consumption, and the lost creative satisfactions of regular employment, become obvious. To the loss of effort (or waste) caused by manufacturers 105

who cease or reduce production-not because the goods are not needed but because people have not the means of purchasing-may be added the loss (or waste) resulting from the policy of Trade Unions, another product of the capitalistic system, in restricting apprentices, membership, etc.

The old motto "To each according to his needs' is the only criterion which can be applied."

It must also be apparent that with the absence of financial worry brought about by the new economic security, with a better standard of living, and a higher standard of education, the general scale of intelligence, physique and ability will increase, with a consequent appropriate development of the national resources. 

In actual fact when we lift our economic structure from a material to a spiritual plane-when we release ourselves from the service of personal gain and plan all our efforts to the service of God and of our brother-it will unleash a force of creative and inventive energy hitherto unknown in human experience. The knowledge that we are working as a part of God's Kingdom for the benefit of His Family will be spur to all that is best in us such as this world has never previously known. With each using his or her best endeavors for the benefit of the National Family, the result must be a tremendous increase in the welfare of each member of that Family.

We have applied the principle of treating the members of one nation or country as being one huge family. But our economic system has dealt with countries exactly as it has done with individuals. The rich and powerful countries have exploited the weak countries. The natural resources of the weak or backward countries have been developed not to the advantage of that country, but for the benefit of the exploiting nation. Take as examples lands which are much in our thoughts at the present time. Malaya has been endowed with a climate, soil, etc., which are specially suited for the growth of rubber trees. These natural advantages have not been developed for the benefit of the native population, but for the advantage of investors mostly living in other lands. Java has natural oil deposits.

They show, however, that powerful nations, or the big corporations or companies of those nations, have taken the natural riches away from weaker and backward countries 109

without giving them equal value in return. This exploitation is dignified by the name of "foreign development," and the successful exploiters are greatly honored in their own countries.

This process is, however, only the natural and inevitable development of our capitalistic system. The system demands that we prey on one another personally, and the natural extension is for nation to prey upon nation. Herein lies the germ of war. For not only do the "have” nations prey upon the "have-nots," but the "have" nations grow envious of each other.

The economist's suggestion that Britain immediately after the war must develop a big export trade only means that we shall again engage in that national competition which has been a source of war. 

If we are to end war between nations, we must first end that state of things which creates war.

Each nation has its own particular gifts to offer for the wellbeing of the others. It is probable that when such a system is instituted the more backward countries will receive generous treatment in order to help them to attain the social development of more advanced countries. 

Now, I trust, we are beginning to see the effect of the development of the idea of God's World Family of Nations. 

We start with one nation, the members of which accept in spirit and in fact the ideals and principles of the Two Great Commandments as the basis of the whole national structure. 

This implies that it accepts the well-ordered family as being the ideal economic and social unit, and develops the principles of the family economy to cover the whole national economy. Here is the nation as a family. The nation in turn becomes a unit in the greater world family. 

By such means we get a world developed upon and living according to the principles implied in the Two Great Commandments, for such principles are as necessary in the 110

intercourse of nations one with another, as they are among individuals.

CHAPTER 15 TOTAL PEACE

We want, if possible, to win the individuals of the enemy countries from the evils of Dictatorships, and show them a better way of life. Invincible, because we would then be acting according to the will of God.

Now" among the English-speaking peoples.

Think what it would mean if the Allied Countries could adopt the economic and social structure of the Kingdom of God among ourselves NOW. Think what a difference it would make to our war effort. Today our production is 112

hampered by patent rights, secret formulas which one firm is frightened to divulge to another because of post-war competition, etc. Even during the war, when we are literally fighting for our very existence, our present economic system prevents many firms and companies putting their whole effort into full production for the war machine. Our economic system reminds business and manufacturing concerns that some day the war will be over, so they must prepare for post war trade. So many brains-possibly the best brains are being turned from the war effort to plan for post war trade. And to that end, the war is hindered and prolonged, and possibly more lives lost. We may think such action traitorous, but it is only the logical consequence of our present economic system.

The adoption of an order built upon the economic and social principles of the Kingdom of God would stop all these personal profit motives, and permit us to direct our energies into whatever direction circumstances may require.

CHAPTER 16 THE ATLANTIC CHARTER 

Following is the famous "Atlantic Charter." Please study it carefully, for you will find that its aims can only be achieved on the basis of the social and economic intercourse among nations, as with men, established in accordance with the conditions implied and contained in the Two Great Commandments. The President of the United States and Mr. Churchill, representing His Majesty's Government, being met together, deemed it right to make known certain common principles in the national policies of their respective countries on which they base their hope for a better future for the world: 

1. Their countries seek no aggrandizement, territorial or other. 

2. They desire to see no territorial changes that do not accord with the freely-expressed wishes of the peoples concerned. 

3. They respect the right of all peoples to choose the form of government under which they will live, and they wish to see sovereign rights and self-government restored to those who have been forcibly deprived of them. 

4. They will endeavor, with due respect for their existing obligations, to further the enjoyment by all States, great or small, victorious or vanquished, of access on equal terms to trade and to the raw materials of the world which are needed for their economic prosperity. 

5. They desire to bring about the fullest collaboration between all nations in the economic field with the object of securing for all an improved standard of labor, economic advancement and social security. 

6. After the final destruction of Nazi tyranny they hope to see established a peace which will afford to all nations the means of dwelling in safety within their own bounda- 115

ries, and which will afford an assurance that all men in all lands may live out their lives in freedom from fear and from war. 

7. Such a peace would enable all men to traverse the high seas and the oceans without hindrance. 

8. They believe that all of the nations of the world, for realistic as well as for spiritual reasons, must come to abandon the use of force. Since no future peace can be maintained if land, sea or air armament continues to be employed by nations which threaten or may threaten aggression outside their frontiers, they believe, pending the establishment of a wider and more permanent system of general security, that the disarmament of such nations is essential. 

They will likewise aid and encourage all other practical measures which will lighten for peace-loving peoples the crushing burden of armament.

CHAPTER 18 THE CHALLENGE 

Throughout this book it has been emphasized that the social, economic and political conditions of the Kingdom of God are practical conditions designed by God for our everyday life on this earth. The question now to be answered is how can these conditions be attained and brought into practical effect?

We must clearly and definitely realize that however worthy our motive may be, we cannot establish the conditions of the Kingdom of God on earth merely as an economic and social structure. We have need of a voice which shall remind us that we have souls as well as appetites. We must remember that "Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul and with all thy mind" is still the first and greatest commandment. Without a recognition of the Fatherhood of God it is sheer hypocrisy to talk of the Brotherhood of Man. Our service to our fellow man can only be built upon the secure foundation of our service to God. We cannot establish the Kingdom of God without acknowledging our allegiance to its King. 

If we are to build a New Christian Order based on justice and love, those values must reign in our own lives. 

It is also absolutely certain that the establishment of the Kingdom of God on earth can not and will not be attained as the result of party politics. "The Kingdom of God" cannot deteriorate into a party slogan; we cannot adopt it as the name of a political party. The Kingdom of God is far above party politics.

There is one way, and one way only, in which the Kingdom of God can be established. And that one and only way is by a "change of heart." "I will write my laws in their hearts, saith the Lord." As has been explained earlier, this Biblical phrase means that we must first experience a complete change of mind in our conception of our whole system of living, of our social and economic standards; that 120

we must get a grander and more embracing idea of what Christianity really means and stands for. 

It means that we must acknowledge that man by his efforts alone is unable to guide the affairs of this world. 

It means that we must be able to say from our very hearts and with an understanding of its application to our national life we have not hitherto experienced, "Almighty and merciful Father we have erred and strayed from thy ways like lost sheep. We have followed too much the devices and desires of our own hearts. We have offended against thy holy laws. 

We have left undone those things which we ought to have done; and we have done those things which we ought not to have done; And there is no health in us." It is remarkable how this General Confession in our Book of Common Prayer seems even more applicable to our national life than to our lives as individuals.

And finally, this "change of heart" means that having acknowledged the futility of our man-made laws ("the devices and desires of our own hearts") we must express our desire and willingness to accept and be ruled by the Laws of God ("Thy holy Laws") which, as we have seen, were epitomized by our Savior in the Two Great Commandments.

It will have been noted that while our outlook on our whole way of life must be altered, in this book we have dealt in the main with the economic structure. The reason is, of course, that under existing conditions we are dominated by financial considerations. We may think, for instance, that it would be an excellent thing if all children could be educated up to eighteen years of age, but our economic system says it cannot be done. The poorer people must get their children earning as quickly as possible. So under the existing order, our whole way of living is dominated and governed by our economic system. But if man is to live a full, free life, the economic system must be designed to produce the conditions we desire it to produce. 

To do that our economic system must cease to be motivated by self-interest and greed, and be based on love and service. 

In this book it is unnecessary to go to a mass of detail concerning the New Christian Order, the effect upon Education, Agriculture, Medicine, etc., or the means of transition 121

from the Old Order to the New. We must first of all agree upon, and demonstrate our willingness to accept, the basic principles on which that New Order must be founded. For this purpose we have developed in this book those basic fundamental truths on which, if it is to be successful, the New Order must be based-they are unchallengable and unchangeable. Also, under the heading "God's Family-the 

Nation," there is given a broad outline of the effect of these principles when applied to our national economic life. 

It is certain that, sooner or later, the New Chrisian Order will come into being, and that it will be built upon the Fatherhood of God and the Brotherhood of Man. We can hasten the time of its inauguration by accepting it in principle and working for its adoption, or we can retard it by indifference and opposition.

So, in the ultimate, as we have seen in the previous chapter, it becomes a personal decision. It is perhaps the most important and vital decision you will ever be called upon to make; for upon the aggregate of individual answers depends the future of yourself, your children, and mankind.

Yet for all its importance, the issue is a very simple one. 

It narrows itself down to one grand, vital question-The 

Kingdom of God or the kingdom of mammon?—Total Peace or total economic, social and military war? Do we want an economic and social order based on the teachings of 

Christ, love, joy, freedom, justice, equality, co-operation, service; or shall we continue in our present system of selfinterest, class privilege, greed, competition, fear and uncertainty? To such a question surely there can be only one answer. 

The matter of a New Christian Order depends not so much upon the Church or any other organization, but principally upon you and me and the thousands of "You and me's" like us. When all is said and done, these organizations are just aggregates of individuals like us. "But," you may ask, "what can I do?" The answer is clear and authoritative. Jesus tells us "This Gospel of the 

Kingdom shall be preached-proclaimed, expounded, taught -and then the end of the Age shall come." There are our instructions and, what is more, there is the promise of success when we carry out those instructions. If we believe 122

in and want the Kingdom of God on earth, we have got to strive for it and make our desire known. We are to be active, not passive. "Faith without works is dead." In simple terms, we are each to do our best, be that great or small, to educate and direct public opinion. And just as in 1940 when the British public demanded and obtained a change to a more vigorous prosecution of the war and replaced Mr. Chamberlain with Mr. Churchill, so public opinion can demand and obtain a change to a more enlightened economic and social order. 

This is a high purpose, a Crusade, to which each one of us can dedicate his or her enthusiastic endeavors. Tell your friends and acquaintances; have discussions; form committees; hold meetings. Be the seed sown on good soil who hears and understands, and bring forth, some an hundredfold, some sixty, some thirty.
