PREFACE 

The manuscript for this book was first prepared in February, 1940. The war clouds in Europe were hanging low. Germany had overrun some small nations by her might and was threatening others with a blitzkrieg, pointing out Warsaw as an example to all that resist. Russia was showing her hand in Finland, and Japan was warring against 

China. Britain and France were struggling for their existence against seemingly great odds.

Many were in doubt of the final outcome of the war. Without doubt Hitler had planned what he calls a new world order, and was proceeding according to plans. There was no question but that the overwhelming majorities of the people of the United States were against German aims and procedure. 

Many felt that we should be sending all possible aid to those countries fighting for democracy. Some thought that the dictators would win and that we had just as well submit to their terms. All true 

Americans did not want war, so the burning question was, and is, what is the best route to permanent peace and stability?

This writer did not believe then, and he does not believe now, that these dictatorial warmongers can win, but must fail utterly in the final outcome. When the final roll is called for national leadership, their names will be among the missing. God's plan for saving the world is against military aggression and He has declared that it shall not win. The spiritual forces of the world have become too great to be destroyed. The cry for peace around the world is too loud today to be hushed. The kingdom of our 

Lord shall not be defeated by militarism.

The plan of the book is to show that dictators ruined the ancient world politically and religiously, and that God gave a plan to destroy them and their evil works. The divine plan is succeeding, and every world-wide dreamer for nearly two thousand years has failed. Some of them marched with their blitzkriegs of fear, and success seemed to be just around the corner when they met defeat and were hopelessly crushed by the invincible forces that believe in righteousness. Present dictators will fail as all before them have failed. They may almost bankrupt the world (we hope not) before they are destroyed, but their end is certain. 

A fine thing about history is that it moves parallel with the Bible. A correct understanding of history is in accord always with a true interpretation of Biblical facts. Reading history with this in mind helps us to understand the Bible, and reading the Bible helps us to understand history. Each has a key that helps to unlock the meaning of the other. 

The reader will find in this book a recital of both history and Bible. Some of the ancient dictators and their deeds are portrayed to show how the people were oppressed and mourned their fate. 

Some of the later dictators and their deeds are given as proof that the Lord of heaven is going to win in the conflict of right against wrong. The people shall rule according to the divine plan, and the meek "shall inherit the earth.”

The manuscript has been revised and brought up to date concerning the war. The writer received much pleasure in the writing of this book, and he sends it forth with the hope that others may profit by reading it.

S. L. NOEL.

The Conflict

CHAPTER I THE CONFLICT 

"And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel”- 

(Genesis 3:15). 

After sin came into the world, God told Adam and Eve that there is enmity between right and wrong. This means that there would be a state of hostility, unfriendly disposition, hatred or ill will. 

Good and evil cannot get along together. No matter how hard the good try, evil will not allow it. All history proves this to be true. From the time that sin came till now, the conflict has been going on. 

Both seek to rule. The good wants to rule that evil powers may be curbed, that peace and prosperity may prevail and that people may know the right and have a chance to be good. Evil makes every effort to rule that it may have its own way regardless of what may happen to others. It never sleeps on the job and will use violence to get on top and remain in power. 

Good people may cry, "Peace, peace, when there is no peace." There is never peace when evil men rule. There is no use to try to appease or compromise with them, for they think it is an acknowledgment of weakness and will take advantage of it. The forces of righteousness must be firm in the face of evil, or suffer as a result.

The writer of Proverbs rightly says: "When the righteous are in authority, the people rejoice; but 11

when the wicked beareth rule, the people mourn." 

Since the world war, there has been a cry for peace. 

It has been preached from the housetops that war is wrong, therefore we should have nothing to do with war. Some go so far as to say that they would not fight under any conditions. Well, war is wrong; but for us to say we will have nothing to do with it only increases the danger. While we cry peace, evil men are thinking of war. "I am for peace; but when I speak, they are for war" (Psalm 120:5).

When they think that we are so peace possessed that we are not willing to fight, they get ready to start the conflict, for this is their opportunity. The democracies of the world do not want war today. 

They have been so placid that Hitler, Mussolini, Stalin and the militaristic party of Japan said that democracy is weak and dying, so this is our chance to fight, win and rule the world.

God has given right the advantage over evil. He said in the beginning that "It shall bruise thy head, and thou shall bruise his heel." In the conflict that inevitably rages, righteous people will get heel bruises; but, at the same time, they shall be able to smash the head of evil. This they can do always if they rise up in their might and do it. 

This writer was somewhat amused at an editorial appearing in The Commercial Appeal, entitled "God and the War." An English Bishop, inspired by the 

Biblican injunction, "If thine enemy hunger, feed him," introduced a motion in the house of bishops, urging that the lords use their influence to permit the importation of foodstuffs into Germany by the 

British blockade. No sooner was the motion made

13 than the head of the Church of England objected and said of two evils, starvation for the German people was less than a Hitler victory. The editor of The 

Commercial Appeal replied by saying: "The British believe God is on their side and the Germans believe He is with them, and both are, to our mind, slightly blasphemous in their thinking. It will be the quality of seamanship and gunnery which will determine the British blockade and not the blessing of the Archbishop of Canterbury, go which way it may. 

The side choosing in the present conflict will go on for some time yet, but for the sake of common decency, why do not belligerents at least avoid trying to drag God into it? The only ones He is going to side with are those who have to die when they don't want to die and those who have to kill when they don't want to kill."

We cannot agree with the editor. Surely, God is always on the side of right in its struggle against wrong. If in war one side is fighting that an evil power may be crushed and that righteousness may prevail, the Lord, we think, must inevitably be in sympathy with that side. This we think is in harmony with the divine voice speaking in the Bible from Genesis to Revelation. 

After deliverance from Egyptian bondage, Moses declared, "The Lord is a man of war." Solomon believed it and prayed: "If thy people go out to war against their enemies by the way that thou shalt send them, and they pray unto thee toward this city which thou hast chosen, and the house which I have built for thy name; then hear thou from heaven their prayer and their supplication, and maintain

their cause." The writer of Revelation so understood it and wrote to the seven churches in Asia saying: "And I saw heaven opened, and behold a white horse; and he that sat upon him was called 

Faithful and True, and in righteousness he doth judge and make war." The Amalekites were a warring people. They gave Israel plenty of trouble and that without provocation. So the Lord said to 

Moses: "Write this for a memorial in a book, and rehearse it in the ears of Joshua; for I will utterly put out the remembrance of Amalek from under heaven... Because the Lord hath sworn that he will have war with Amalek from generation to generation." The Lord finally ordered them completely destroyed. 

God calls good people to fight in defense of right.

The Bible leads us to this conclusion. Ezekiel tells about Gog, a mighty prince, in the latter days. He would come like a storm. He would say: "I will go to them that are at rest . . . to take a spoil, and to take a prey" (Ezekiel 38:11, 12). God would be against him, and in the thirty-eighth verse we find this: “And I will call for a sword against him throughout all my mountains, saith the Lord God."

It is not intended to say that Hitler is Gog. We do not know. All that is intended here is to show that God does come in behalf of right and that he calls upon his people to fight against the enemies of righteousness. His displeasure is against those who will not fight. "Curse ye Meroz, said the angel of the Lord, curse ye bitterly the inhabitants thereof; because they came not to the help of the Lord against the mighty" (Judges 5:23). Pacifists should

Cain was the first born. He was of that wicked one (I John 3:12). He was not interested in the good and was not a true worshiper of Jehovah. God did not accept his offering because sin lay at his door. After this Cain hated his brother and slew him. So early came martyrdom into the world; the first man slain died for religion. Jealousy was

Cain's motive "because his own works were evil, and his brother's righteous." 

Cain grew worse. Josephus, a Jewish historian who claimed true records from Adam to his own day, says of him: "However, he did not accept of his punishment in order to amendment but to increase his wickedness; for he aimed to procure everything that was for his own bodily pleasure, though it obliged him to be injurious to his neighbors. He augmented his household substance with much wealth, by rapine, and violence; he excited his acquaintance to procure pleasures and spoils by robbery and became a great leader of men into wicked courses." Cain built a city, fortified it with walls, and compelled his family to come together in it, thus laying the foundation of a world kingdom. 

While Adam was still alive, the posterity of Cain became exceedingly wicked. They were intolerable in war and vehement in robberies. They wanted to 19

rule, and they brought on cruel wars in order to get the upper hand and then hold it. 

Adam and Eve had many other children. Seth was born when Adam was one hundred and thirty years old. He was a good man. He was excellent in character, and his children were like him. History states that these inhabited the same country without dissensions They lived happily and without misfortunes. They also were inventors of that peculiar sort of wisdom which is concerned with the heavenly bodies, and their order. This is the natural state of things when righteousness prevails. 

Peace and prosperity abounds; people become wise in spiritual things, and the hidden mysteries of life are discovered and used for man's benefits. No telling how much wisdom discovered in the past has been lost because of sin. Now, the descendants of 

Seth continued to esteem God as the Lord of the universe for several generations, but in process of time they were perverted by the Cainites and became wicked. The world was fast plunging to ruin and autocratic rule.

Enoch was the seventh from Adam. He was a preacher who tried to stem the tide of wickedness.

He was also a prophet who told the people: "Behold the Lord cometh with ten thousands of his saints; to execute judgment upon all, and to convince all that are ungodly among them of all their ungodly deeds which they have ungodly committed, and of all their hard speeches which ungodly sinners have spoken against him."

Noah was a descendant of Seth. He remained true to the God of his fathers. He was a preacher

Ancient Dictators

CHAPTER III ANCIENT DICTATORS 

"Pride goeth before destruction, and a haughty spirit before a fall”—(Proverbs 16:18).

We give here a brief history of ancient dictators. 

This is done in order to show that dictators ruined the ancient world politically and religiously. Under that system cruelty was the rule and slavery abounded. All this abuse of human rights continued until the righteous forces of the earth were strong enough to overthrow and begin to root out the evils of the whole system. Autocratic rulers are the same in every age of the world and present dictators are no exception. If Hitler were allowed to succeed according to his aims, Germany would be the leading nation, and all others throughout the world would be slave nations. The clock of time would be turned backward two thousand years-ignorance, superstition, poverty, cruelty and slavery would prevail as it did of old. However, this will not happen. 

Dictators cannot win. The Lord and the righteous forces of the world stand in their way. If necessary, they stand with drawn swords in defense of the right. They have not failed in every crisis for two thousand years, and they will not fail this time, even though there is much cringing of the issue at the present time. Dictatorial methods have been tried and found wanting. God has written “finis” to their world-wide ambitions and has declared that they shall not succeed again. 25

NIMROD 

Nimrod was the first dictator after the flood. He was a descendant of Ham. The Hamites fast degenerated and soon departed from the faith of their father, Noah. Nimrod was a fine marksman and a great hunter. After the flood the wild animals multiplied very fast and became dangerous to the people. Nimrod took other hunters with him and killed and drove away the wild beasts. This made him famous. People were high in their praises. 

They dubbed him, "Nimrod the mighty hunter before the Lord."

Nimrod liked praises, even flatteries, and he took advantage of his popularity to fool the people and make himself their dictator. He began by talking unity. God had said that it was not best for all of them to dwell in the country called Shinar. He therefore directed that they send out colonies abroad, for the peopling of the earth, that they might not raise seditions among themselves as they had done before the flood, but might cultivate more of the earth and enjoy its fruits. Now it was 

Nimrod who advised otherwise. He said it did not stand to reason that it was best to send out colonies; and that it was his suspicion that they were ordered to send out colonies that, being divided, they might the more easily be depressed, if not destroyed. He argued that by standing together they had driven out the wild animals, whereas if each had been for himself, the wild animals would have killed all of them.

Now the multitude were very ready to follow Nimrod, since he had been a benefactor and shown

His next step was to pervert the true religion of his fathers. It is very necessary for a dictator, who wants his own way about everything, to control the religious life of the people if he remains in power. 

We need not be surprised that Stalin has tried to crush all religion in Russia, and that Hitler is trying to Naziize Christianity in Germany. This is an important means toward their end. Seeing no other way to turn men from the fear of God and to bring them into dependence on his power, Nimrod gave them a religion which afterward came to be known as Baal, and history states that he forced this religion on them by tyranny. He was cruel and he went to any and all excesses of cruelty to enforce his will. In this way he largely succeeded in turning the people of his day away from God. Baal was a religion that remained to curse the world for centuries.

Being reminded that God had destroyed the world with a flood because of wickedness, he said they would put one over on God by building a high tower; and, if a flood came, they would go above the flood. God and boast of their own power, He brings them

to confusion. Jesus said, "Whosoever shall exalt himself shall be abased." An eastern tradition pictures Nimrod as a heaven-storming giant, chained by God among the constellations.

KINGS OF ASSYRIA 

There is a tradition that when Nimrod was defeated at Babel he went north and founded Ninevah.

There is no historical proof of the tradition, so far as I know; but it is a certainty that the mightiest kings of Assyria patterned after Nimrod. History says: “The Assyrian kings gloried in being like the great Nimrod, a mighty hunter before the Lord." 

The records of those kings show that they imitated him not only in general plans, but also in detail. The spirit of the first dictator still lived and was exceedingly active in the Assyrian kings.

Tiglath-Pileser I was the first sovereign of renown at the head of Assyrian affairs. He was a great warrior. He made numerous war expeditions. He was a conqueror by force of arms.

Tiglath-Pileser II was a man of great military power. His empire extended over the greater part of Southwestern Asia. He was not perhaps as great a conqueror as his predecessor, but he was a great political organizer, and he had no scruples in organizing according to his own will. 

Sargon was one of Assyria's greatest conquerors and builders. He conquered the ten tribes of Israel and carried them into captivity beyond the Tigris. 

He scattered them among the Assyrian people, and we do not know what became of them. They are thought of today as the ten lost tribes.

BABYLONIAN MONARCHS 

The Babylonian people bore the Assyrian yoke very impatiently. For centuries there were violent efforts to throw it off. In several instances they succeeded and enjoyed home rule for a little while only to be conquered again. In 625 B.C., when troubles and misfortunes began to come fast to the Assyrian king, he entrusted to the care of Nabopolassar the provinces of the South. This viceroy now saw a chance to obtain a crown for himself, and this temptation was stronger than his fidelity to his master. He entered an alliance with the king of 

Media and overthrew Ninevah and the Assyrian Empire, gaining large accessions of territory and becoming the first dictator of Babylonia. Thus it is always in autocratic ridden territories-some man with an autocratic spirit awaits the opportunity to overthrow the dictator in power. It is reported, and perhaps with good reason, that Goering is waiting

a chance to destroy Hitler and his wild advisers and force his way to the head of the German government. 

Nabopolassar was succeeded by his son Nebuchadnezzar, who was a man of war. He conquered much of the known earth and builded his empire to the greatest heights of outward show of any before him. 

History states that Babylon became at once the scourge and the wonder of the ancient world.

THE ISRAELITES 

For nearly four hundred years the Israelites were ruled by divinely called Judges. During this time there arose a line of national heroes, such as Gideon, Samson and Samuel, who acted as arbiters between man and man. The Book of Judges narrates heroic deeds of valor and daring by these Judges. They wrought deliverance for the tribes of Israel from their foes and caused their names to be handed down in grateful remembrance to this day.

During the period of the Judges, the tribes were not united by any central government. The latter days of these Judges were at the time when Assyria was coming into power and warring against all nations. The Israelites thought they would be stronger against their enemies if they were united into one government with a king at the head. They believed they would be stronger if ruled by a king than by a divinely appointed judge. So they cried out for a king that they might be like other people, little realizing that they would be like them in many ways which they did not want. However, the cry was so strong that the Lord told Samuel to let them have a king; "for they have not rejected thee, but

CYRUS THE GREAT 

Cyrus was tributary king of the Persians. He led a revolt against the Medes. Being a military genius, he built up an empire more extended than any Oriental monarch, or as far as we know by any ruler before his time. His kingdom stretched from

Indus to the eastern limits of Asia Minor, and from the Caspian Sea to the Persian Gulf, taking in not only the territory of the Medes and the Persians but also the allied kingdoms of Lydia and Babylonia.

Cyrus lost his life while leading an expedition against the Scythian tribes of the north. Notwithstanding his love of war and conquest, he possessed a kindly and generous disposition. He issued a decree for the Jews to go back to Judea and rebuild Jerusalem. Universal testimony, I think, is ascribed to him as the purest and best of the Eastern monarchs. However, when we think of this ascription, we must think of it in comparison with the cruelty

When he defeated the Lydian army in the field and Lydia became a province of his empire, he caused a pyre to be erected on which to burn Croesus, the Lydian King, but at the last moment did not because of a certain report. The times were such that Solon, a 

Greek sage, had previously said to Croesus: "Count no man happy until he is dead."

ALEXANDER THE GREAT 

In a dictator ridden world, many nations sought pre-eminence through military glory. Nations that were otherwise peace loving became militaristic, either in self-defense or because of some military genius among them. Ancient Greece was a land of wisdom-seeking people. In time they had an undying hatred for the Persians because of the things they had suffered at their hands. A joint expedition was planned against Persia. This was encouraged by the march of ten thousand Greeks through the very heart of that dominion, making feasible the conquest of Asia. Philip was chosen the leader of the expedition. He was assassinated while attending a marriage festivity. Alexander was his son and successor. He was only twenty-one when he came to his father's throne. He set out with an army for the conquest of the Persian empire. Now commenced one of the most remarkable and swiftly executed campaigns recorded in all history. He conquered the Persians, the Egyptians, the Babylonians, North India, Arabia, Carthage, Italy, Spain and other countries. His aim was the Hellenizing of the world. Common laws and customs, a common language and a common religion were to unite the

world into one family. In the midst of successes, he was seized by a fever, brought on by his insane excesses, and died at Babylon in the thirty-second year of his age. He exposed himself like any of his soldiers. He shared with his soldiers the hardships of marches and the dangers of the battlefield. History says of him that he was self-seeking, foolishly vain and madly ambitious of military glory. Themistocles said of him: "He was greater in genius than in character." 

That Alexander the Great was a great military genius there can be no question, but the spirit of the times made it easy for him to spread his kingdom. The people had been dictator ridden for so long that they were ready to surrender to anyone who showed superior military power. There was nothing to lose by giving up one dictator to accept another. The swiftness of Alexander's conquests spread like wild fire, and when he marched into Egypt, the Egyptians made no resistance but willingly exchanged masters. When he marched to 

Babylon, the mighty city and once capitol of great monarchs, opened its gates to him without opposition.

THE CAESARS 

Rome had been a republic for nearly five hundred years. Dictators had ruled in Asia, the birthplace of the human family; but they had not been so successful in Europe. The pillars of the Roman Republic had been swept away by the tide of luxury, selfishness and immorality, produced by the long series of foreign conquest and robberies in which the Roman people had been engaged. After Catiline

ANCIENT DICTATORS 

35 was slain with his many followers in 62 B.C., the affairs of Rome were in the hands of three men, Caesar, Crassus and Pompey.

Julius Caesar studied out plans to seize supreme power. Beyond the Alps, the northern tribes were causing trouble. He saw there an opportunity to display his military powers and gain glory and prestige as Pompey had already done in other fields. 

Now began a series of eight brilliant campaigns directed against the tribes of Gaul, Germany and Britain. In these campaigns, Caesar subjugated three hundred tribes, captured eight hundred cities, slew a million people-one-third of the entire population—and took prisoners a million more. Great enthusiasm resulted in Rome, causing Cicero to say, "Let the Alps now sink, the gods raised them to shelter Italy from the Barbarians: they are now no longer needed."

Crassus led an army against the Parthians, hoping to rival the brilliant successes of Caesar; but his army was almost annihilated by the Parthian cavalry, and he himself was slain. It is said that his captors poured molten gold down his throat that he might be filled with the metal he loved so well. 

Crassus' death left Caesar and Pompey masters of the Roman world. The senate, hostile to Caesar, issued a decree that he should resign. Caesar did not resign; he declared war. As he crossed the Rubicon 

River he exclaimed, "The die is cast." This caused consternation in Rome. Caesar marched, and city after city threw open their gates to him. Legion after legion went over to his standard. Within sixty days he made himself the undisputed master of all

Italy. In a little while Pompey was defeated and 

Caesar was lord of the Roman world. The senate made him perpetual dictator. He was to sit in a golden chair in the Senate house, his image was to be borne in the procession of the gods, and the seventh month of the year was changed in his honor from Quintilis to Julius (whence our July). 

Augustus Caesar took over the head of Roman affairs after his predecessor was slain by Brutus. 

Mindful of the fate of Julius Caesar, who fell because he gave the lovers of the old republic reason to believe that he was an enemy of the republic, Augustus carefully kept under cover his real absolute sovereignty by certain outward forms of the republic. 

All the republican officials remained. All officers were elected as of old, but they were simply the tools of the emperor.

Augustus Caesar reigned for forty-four years. It was the most splendid period in the annals of Roman history. His was the "Golden Age" of Latin literature. He also encouraged architecture and art. Of 

Rome he said: "I found Rome a city of brick; I left it a city of marble." He was not a warrior but a statesman. SLAVERY 

Many baneful evils have resulted from autocratic rule. Not only has religion been blighted and depressed, but many social evils have been imposed. 

The world has staggered and reeled like a drunken man under its impulse. The best of people have been dragged down and sold into slavery. The upper crust has often become the lower, simply to satisfy the lust and greed of some one-sided, egotistic and self-imposed ruler.

Slavery has been one of the sour fruits of one-man rule. When a country was conquered, it was customary to force or sell many of the people into slavery. Ancient Tyre was the emporium of all the commerce of the then known world. The fleets of its merchants went to all parts of the earth. The prophet Isaiah says that Tyre was "the crowning city, whose merchants are princes, whose traffickers are the honorable of the earth." When the city fell into the hands of Alexander the Great, thirty thousand of its people were sold into slavery. Again, Greece had suffered at the hands of the Persians, so when Alexander took Persepolis, he wreaked his vengeance by massacring many and selling others into slavery.

Ancient Greece is admired because of her culture, but there is a dark side to Greek life. Her glory is spoiled to present day readers because of her slaves. 

Corinth was a city of 500,000 people and 400,000 of them were slaves. Almost every free man was a slave owner. Slave traffic was carried on with other nations. Criminals and debtors, too, were

often condemned to servitude. A child found and unclaimed was brought up as a slave.

The deep impression which Babylon produced upon her visitors was made by her vast architectural works-her temples, palaces, gigantic reservoirs, canals, vast systems of irrigation, etc. The Hanging 

Gardens which Nebuchadnezzar built to please his wife were considered among the wonders of the world. His temples were made, in the language of the inscriptions, "to shine like the sun." All this glory, however, dwindles into insignificance when we read that all this was made possible by the numerous captives of his many wars. These gigantic undertakings, which challenged the thinking of that day with its glory, required immense expenditures of human labor, and this work was done by people who were taken in war and forced to do it. 

No wonder that Jeremiah said: "And Babylon shall become heaps, a dwelling place for dragons, an astonishment, and an hissing, without an inhabitant." Also that Isaiah should say: "And Babylon, the glory of kingdoms, the beauty of the Chaldean excellency, shall be as when God overthrew Sodom and Gomorrah. It shall never be inhabited, neither shall it be dwelt in from generation to generation: neither shall the Arabian pitch his tent there: neither shall the shepherds make their fold there.

But wild beast of the desert shall lie there; and their houses shall be full of doleful creatures; and owls shall dwell there, and satyrs shall dance there. 

And the wild beast of the islands shall cry in their desolate houses, and dragons in their pleasant palaces." All this came to pass, and Babylon is no

more. The plain where that one of the largest and most splendid cities of the ancient world stood is today studded with vast mounds of earth and imposing ruins. 

The most demoralizing thing in Roman life was the institution of slavery. It is said that there were more slaves in Rome than free men. The captives taken in war were usually sold as slaves. The many conquests of the Romans brought so many slaves into Roman life that it caused a drug in the slave market. Slaves were so cheap that masters found it more profitable to wear their slaves out by unmerciful hard labor, and then buy others, than to preserve their health and strength for future days.

In case of sickness, they were left to die without attention, since they could buy others for less cost than curative remedies. Slaves were often bought for a few dollars. Some estates were worked by as many as twenty thousand slaves. That each owner might know his own, they were often branded like cattle. We find an almost exact parallel to all this we have been writing about in the feudal aristocracy of medieval Europe.

Africa and sold in the Mohammedan world each year. Slavery has not been abolished in Christian nations very long. The wonder of it is that intelligent people should ever think that slavery is not wrong. Even Aristotle, a Greek philosopher and one of Greece's greatest minds, approved the maxim that "slaves are simply domestic animals possessed of intelligence."

If dictators are allowed to win again, the old system of slavery will be revived; and, as of old, the best and most enlightened will be thus allotted. Democracy is a government in which the supreme power is vested in the people. As Abraham Lincoln said a "government of the people, by the people, for the people." These rights may be exercised directly where all the people meet and make the laws, or indirectly through a system of representatives with delegated authority periodically renewed, as in a constitutional representative government or republic.

In such a system of government there is wisdom.

The result is the people, sooner or later, get a fall-all progress is retarded, and militarism becomes the game while the people suffer and are often driven into despair. All history attests to this fact.

Where there is a multitude of counsellors, each being free to think and speak for himself as in a 99

Public oratory which as has been said, "is the child of political freedom and cannot exist without it," is the Roman Republic's greatest glory. AMERICA 

The United States of America is a republic. This government is but a little more than a century and a half old. She is young in comparison with the age of great nations, yet in that brief time she has become the talk of the world and the dream of millions born in other countries. Mr. Chamberlain, ex-premier of Great Britain, said that this country is the greatest nation in the world. 

We have never been aggressive. The country has grown in area and power, but this has not been gained by military achievement. Louisiana was purchased from France. The territory embraced in this purchase included the present state of Louisiana and all the country now occupied by Arkansas, Missouri, Iowa, Kansas, Nebraska, Oklahoma, North and South Dakota and the greater part of Minnesota. Texas joined of its own free will and accord. 

Many of the western states were acquired from Mexico by a treaty, the United States paying Mexico $15,000,000 and assuming the payment of the claims of American citizens against Mexico. Alaska

was bought from Russia. We helped to free Cuba. 

During that war our navy under command of Admiral Dewey defeated the Spanish fleet in Manila Bay. According to the rules of war-to the victor belongs the spoils the United States was entitled to the Philippine Islands. A warring nation would have taken them over without further discussion, but our nation bought them from Spain. We went into the World War against aggressive Germany, sending millions of men and spending billions of dollars; then, at the peace table, we asked for nothing and received what we asked.

GREAT BRITAIN 

Great Britain of old was an unlimited monarchy.

Many of her kings were military chieftains. King 

John was tyrannical, unscrupulous and wicked. His course led to an open revolt, resulting in his being forced to sign the "Magna Charta" in which the rights and privileges of the people were clearly defined. The Magna Charta was often set aside by despotic kings; but the people clung to it, and many times forced tyrannical kings to renew and confirm its provisions, and swear allegiance to its articles. 

The people finally won, and constitutional liberty was guaranteed to her people in all parts of the world. Today Great Britain is a limited monarchy in which the people rule. Today she is fighting for democracy. Mr. Chamberlain said that Britain is fighting a "crusade" for freedom and to abolish "the spirit of militarism and accumulation of armaments which is pauperizing all Europe and not in the least Germany herself." FRANCE 

France had her share of the "Feudal System,” the lower classes making up the greater part of the feudal state, owned and controlled by feudal overlords. The power of the feudal nobles was finally broken, and autocratic despotic Royalty seized the government which led the French people to the Revolution and the Reign of Terror. There was a great effort to destroy Christianity and the terrorist fought with the ferocity of a beast whose appetite had been whetted by the taste of blood. When Robespierre was sent to the guillotine, the people rejoiced. 

France was soon a republic and Christian worship was re-established. Napoleon Bonaparte set up an empire and tried to conquer Europe. The second republic was established in 1848, and it was destroyed in three years by Louis Napoleon, nephew of Bonaparte, which finally resulted in a second reign of terror. The people, tired of despots, decided to rule, and the third republic was established in 

1871. In 1940 she was beaten by Hitler's armies.

OTHER REPUBLICS 

After the World War ended, several nations became republics, many of them having been destroyed by present dictators. Finland engaged in a fierce struggle against great odds in behalf of her independence as a state and her freedom of religious worship.

CHAPTER XII WAR IS NOT INEVITABLE 

"And he shall judge among the nations . neither shall they learn war any more" — (Isaiah 2:4).

When we compare the trees of aristocracy and democracy and behold the vast difference in the fruit yielded, we are overwhelmed and disgusted at the cruelty and poverty of the one and elated because of the progress and richness of the other. One is the result of wrong; the other the outcome of right. We need not mistake which is which. Looking at it without knowing the reasons behind it, we Americans are too much inclined to think that war is inevitable on European soil, having gone on forever and will continue to the end. Little do we seem to realize that the democratic spirit in Europe is in a desperate struggle (perhaps the last one) to free the continent of dictators and dictatorial methods.

We have heard it said that both sides in Europe are fighting for a right. They say that it is the same old story over again-the right of conquest against the right of possession. If this is true, then Germany is as much in the right as the English, and right is not an absolute quantity. Who said that conquest is ever right? There are ways to get what is right, and conquest is never one of them. Anyone who says that Hitler is right in his conquest of nations is not learned in the vital facts of history, 109

is a poor judge of right and wrong, and does not discern the signs of the times.

In as brief a way as possible, the terrific struggle of the people to free themselves from dictators and establish democracies instead has been related. Today Europe is in the throes of a modern war because dictators are endeavoring to conquer Europe and then the world, having emissaries at work everywhere preparing the way. Wherever they have power, they are trying to destroy or else control religion, as many of their tribe before them have done.

Hitler speaks of democracy as old and ossified and said: "Germany's leadership in the reconstruction of Europe has been achieved without the aid of the leaders of democratic nations, and the reconstruction of the world will also be brought about without them." Thus it is easily understood how he means to reconstruct the world, and this he will do without the help of democracy.

Well, it is certain that the democracies will not assist him in his plan of reconstruction. It is certain that democracy will stand in his way of ruination, and that he will not reconstruct the world is certain. My guess is that he will not reconstruct

A Need of the Hour

CHAPTER XIV A NEED OF THE HOUR 

"Every kingdom divided against itself is brought to desolation"-(Matthew 12:25).

We should stand together today. Democracies need some form of alliance. All religious people who love freedom and peace need to understand that religion is on trial. Standing together, we can put an end to dictators. President Roosevelt delivered an address in Chicago, October 5, 1937, in which he said: "The overwhelming majority of the peoples and nations of the world today want to live in peace. 

Surely the ninety percent who want to live in peace under law and in accordance with moral standards that have received almost universal acceptance through the centuries, can and must find some way to make their will prevail." This seems to be the size of it.

We have traveled a long way from Nimrod and autocratic power when nine-tenths of the nations of the world want peace. Well, nine-tenths can make one-tenth behave. We are finding the way to do that now.

We have been inclined to stand apart, each hoping not to be drawn into the war. The isolationist theory among nations has contributed to Hitler's success. We know now that we cannot stand separately and have peace, neither can we be a world power and escape world responsibility. We do not expect children to accept responsibility for the welfare of the community, but when they are 123

grown they should help to keep the peace. When 

America was young, she did not need entangling alliances with European affairs. It is different today. We are a full-grown nation, and we must help in world affairs. We must do our part, as a big brother, to keep the peace of nations. To refuse is to fail in our responsibility and invite endless trouble at home.

Suppose nine men stand with hands folded and look on while a bully, beginning with the least one, takes them one at a time, with resting spells between. What would happen? All nine would get whipped, and the bully would brag about his great strength. If all nine stand together, the bully will hang his head and leave that is, if they give him a chance.

The above principle is true of dictators and nations. We have stood on the sidelines while small nations have been taken by military force. Dictators have been encouraged and they move on to destroy democracy and enforce their religion, or else no religion, on us. We are not neutral in spirit, yet we try to be in the letter. Why should we be in sympathy with China and lend her money to fight for her freedom, and at the same time sell Japan those things she needs to conquer China? 

All peace loving nations today should stand together and tell the other nations that we are against them and will let them have nothing, and that we will support those nations fighting for the liberty of themselves and others. When we do this, the war will be over in a short time, and dictators will be humbled, if indeed, they miss the potters field.
