PREFACE 

This book might very appropriately be called "The Complement of my book on 'Pand Sqs of Prime Order."

I am really very sorry that the two could not be published in one volume. But when I finished the former, I had no thought of ever writing this one. The real clue that led to this book came to me quite unexpectedly a little later. 

The second Type of 9x9 Sq given is not a result of my own investigation. Due acknowledgment of this fact is given in the text on page 16. I have been given due permission to include this discussion in this book. 

For this courtesy I wish here to express my sincere thanks. 

With this exception, the results found, and the methods of construction used, are new, as far as I know. 

In this, of course, I may be mistaken. But it is so much more fun to work a thing out for myself, than to look through the literature to find out what someone else has done, and how he did it. Besides I am working chiefly for the fun of it, just to keep myself alert and contented.

As usual I have given a large number of illustrations of all Types of Sqs, and all the processes of construction. In these I have always used the simpler cases, because they are easier to follow. For the same reason, whenever it was necessary to use a 5x5 Pand Sq, I have always used the same "Model Sq" given as No. 3 at the bottom of page 83. Likewise, I have always used the same "Model 4x4 Pand Sq" which is given in several places. Other Sqs could have been used. I did this in order to make it easier to read the book. In order further to facilitate the reading of the book, two related figures, such as an Aux Sq and the Pand Sq derived from it, have usually been placed on two pages that face each other, so they can be seen without turning a page. 

I hope, and really believe, that most undergraduates may be able to read the book. 111

INTRODUCTION 

It seems desirable, by way of introduction, to give a general definition, or to state the general characteristics that distinguish the special type of Pandiagonal Magic Squares that we intend to investigate in the following chapters. We shall state these characteristics in terms of an "Auxiliary Square" that is formed by making certain permutations in the Rows and Columns of the "Natural Square, (See Figs 1 and 2, p. 6) because the essential facts are more clearly revealed in this way. (1) The special characteristic of these Squares may be stated in a general way as follows: A certain number of Columns, and Rows of the Aux Sq go over into and form the same Columns, and Rows, in the Pand Sq. 

Obviously, this "Certain Number" must be a factor of the order of the Sq, and there must also be a number of such sets of Cols, and Rows. Also, the sum of all the numbers in such a set of Cols, or Rows, must be a multiple of the "Magic Number" of the Sq. m². 

For example, let the order of the Sq be n = m Then in the Aux Sq there must be M sets of Cols, and m sets of Rows, with M Cols in each set of Cols, and M Rows in each set of Rows, which go into and form the same Sets of Cols, and the same sets of Rows in the Pand Sq. And the sum of all of the numbers in each of these sets must be m times the Magic Number of the Sq. 

If the order of the Sq is N = pq, then we may have p sets of Cols, and Rows, with q Cols, or Rows, in each set. Or we may have q sets of Cols, or Rows, with p Cols, or Rows in each set. 

For example, in the 16 x 16 Sq we may use 4 sets with 4 in each set, 8 sets with 2 in each set, or 2 sets with 8 in each set. ix

(2) Other Characteristics.

Row of the Aux Sq.

Each Row of the Pand Sq has one number from each 

Col of the Aux Sq.

Each Diag of the Pand Sq has one number from each Row, and also one number from each Col of the Aux Sq.

We shall find later that there are also other Squares that have the same essential properties as the Natural Square. With these Squares we can also construct Pand Sqs of this particular type. 

In a Pand Sq of "Odd Order" the First Prin Diag, and each Diag parallel to it, has one number, and only one, in common with the Second Prin Diag, and each Diag parallel to it. This is not true of a Pand Sq of Even
