DEAR boys and girls, are you fond of hearing wonderful things? Do you love to listen, when people tell of great dangers and surprising escapes? Some young people are so fond of these marvels, that they will sit and read books, which all the while, as they know, do not contain one word of truth, nor any real instruction; which is very wrong. Our time was given to us for better purposes; and we cannot have much regard for truth, if we take delight in reading falsehood and folly. I shall not tell you a fairy tale, for this good reason, that there are no fairies, nor ever were such beings in the world; and I would not affront my readers, by supposing that they are so very silly as to be amused by such nonsense. Neither shall I invent a story about some person who

never existed-no: I have better things to write about; and can begin my history by assuring you that every part of it is true, quite true. More than two thousand two hundred years ago, there was a very mighty king, who reigned over Babylon, then the greatest empire in the world. The king and his people were alike ignorant of God, and worshiped all sorts of foolish idols. They were, of course, wicked and cruel; for the dark places of the earth are full of the habitations of cruelty'-the thoughts of man's heart are only evil, and that continually, from his youth up; and nothing but the love of God shed abroad in his heart by the Holy Ghost, through the mercy of Jesus Christ, can possibly alter his character. 

These Babylonians were very fond of war, very powerful, and very covetous: so when the Lord God, angry at the obstinate sin of the Jewish people, put it into their hearts to go up against Jerusalem and to destroy it, they were very glad to conquer that fine rich country-to slay many of its inhabitants, and make slaves of the rest: to rob the magnifi-

cent temple that King Solomon had built, many hundred years before, of all its treasures; and then to destroy both it and the city, carrying away the king and all his princes as prisoners into Babylon. You know that, when Pilate slew the Galileans at their sacrifices-and the tower of Siloam fell, killing eighteen persons-our Lord Jesus told the people who talked about it to take warning, saying, 'Except ye repent, ye shall all likewise perish.' So, when we read or hear of God's judgments upon sinful nations, let us take heed, and stand in awe, and commune with our own hearts; that we may know in what we also are offending Him, and amend our evil ways, before His wrath overtakes us likewise. 

Now, you are to suppose the great King of Babylon at ease in his possessions-the conquered Jews being quite unable to give him any farther trouble. If you wish to know what was their state, read the 137th

Psalm, which is their sad complaint in their captivity. However, there were among them four young princes of the Jews, who had been chosen from the rest, to be kept in

the king's palace, and instructed in all the learning and in the tongue of the Chaldeans, or Babylonians-even as Moses was brought up in Pharaoh's house, and learned in all the wisdom of the Egyptians. You know how

Moses afterwards chose rather to suffer affliction with the people of God, than to enjoy the pleasures of sin for a season-esteeming the reproach of Christ greater riches than the treasures of Egypt, Heb. xi. 25, 26.
