I. INTRODUCTION Scope of Guide 

This guide is designed to provide all information, regulations, general conditions and instructions for completion of the forms required for the application and administration of planning and/or action grants awarded under Parts B or C of 

Title I of the Omnibus Crime Control and Safe Streets Act of 1968. 

Subgrantee Requirements THE CONTENTS OF THIS GUIDE ARE MANDATORY REQUIREMENTS TO BE ADHERED TO BY ALL RECIPIENTS OF PLANNING OR ACTION SUBGRANTS AWARDED PURSUANT TO THE ACT WHETHER REFERRED TO AS CONDITIONS, REGULATIONS, INSTRUCTION, OR BY SOME OTHER TERMINOLOGY, UNLESS OTHERWISE SPECIFICALLY PROVIDED IN THE PLANNING OR ACTION SUB- GRANT AWARD, GCCA Form G 1. 

FAILURE TO COMPLY WITH ANY SECTION OF THIS GUIDE SHALL BE SUFFICIENT CAUSE FOR THE PLANNING OR ACTION SUBGRANT TO BE TER- MINATED. IN SUCH EVENT, THE SUBGRANTEE SHALL IMMEDIATELY REIM- BURSE THE GOVERNOR'S PLANNING COMMITTEE ON CRIMINAL ADMINISTATION OF THE STATE OF WYOMING FOR ALL FUNDS ADVANCED UNDER THE PLANNING OR ACTION SUBGRANT AWARD IN QUESTION. 

Statutory Requirement 

The Act poses a number of special financial limitations on subgrant recipients. These include: (1) State is obligated to make 40% of all federal planning grant funds and 75% of all federal action grant funds available to local units of government or combinations of local units. (2) Prescribed ratios of federal participation in funding of action programs (75% for programs or projects dealing with the training, education, or equipment procurement related to the prevention, detection and control of riots or other civil disorders or programs or projects related to combating organized crime, 50% for programs or projects for the construction of buildings or other physical facilities related to criminal justice needs, or -7-

60% for programs and projects related to criminal justice needs in all other areas) and of planning programs (90% 

Federal share). (3) Ceilings on use of action grant funds for compensation of personnel (not more than 1/3 of the state's Federal funds may be expended for personnel compensation, exclusive of time engaged in training programs, and this must be matched by equal contributions from the subgrantee toward increased personnel compensation). The 1/3 limitation shall apply to the total grants of Federal funds awarded by the State of Wyoming and not to the individual subgrantee. In other words, an individual subgrantee may spend more than 1/3 of its Federal funds for compensation of personnel so long as the total funds used for the compensation of personnel in the state as a whole does not exceed 1/3 of the action funds. (4) The state is obligated to insure that federal funds "are so used as not to supplant state or local funds but to increase the amount of such funds that would in the absence of Federal funds be made available for law enforcement".

General Prescriptions of Financial Administration are set forth in the Act as well: (1) Each recipient of assistance under this Act shall keep such records as the administration shall prescribe including records which fully disclose the amount and disposition by such recipient of the proceeds of such assistance, the total cost of the project or undertaking in connection with which such assistance is given or used and the amount of that portion of the cost of the project or undertaking supplied by other sources, and such other records as will facilitate an effective audit. 

In addition, states are obligated in order to qualify for action grants to provide for such fiscal control and fund accounting procedures as are necessary to assure proper accounting of funds received under the Act.

In support of the foregoing, the State Planning Agency has established appropriate conditions, regulations, rules, and procedures as provided in this guide to facilitate these requirements. -8-

Audit of Subgrantee 

Pursuant to the requirement placed upon the State Planning Agency by the Federal Government, the State Planning Agency will administer, either directly or through other units of the government of the State of Wyoming, a continuing program of financial audit of Title I Planning and Action subgrants. The primary objective of the audit will be to determine that the funds awarded were used pursuant to the approved application for those funds. 

It is the intention of the State Planning Agency to conduct the audit on at least an annual basis. -9-

General Statement: The publication entitled "Guide for Application and Administration of Planning and Action Grants,"

Reports: The subgrantee shall submit semi-annual narrative and financial reports, indicating activities undertaken, expenditures, and general progress of the planning or action proposal for which the subgrant was awarded. B. FISCAL ADMINISTRATION Basic Responsibility of Grantee: The basic responsibility of the subgrantee is identical with that of the Committee. 

Each must so order its affairs that it may faithfully discharge its public trust which accompanies the authority to expend public funds. The subgrantee will therefore establish and maintain fiscal control and accounting procedures which assure that subgrant funds available for the conduct of the programs and projects covered by the subgrant are properly disbursed, adequately controlled and accounted for in a separate account. The accounting records as hereafter -12-

2. 3. 4. described in this Guide (or their equivalent) shall be maintained by each subgrantee. Additional records may be maintained at the discretion of the subgrantee. 

Records and Documentation: The subgrantee is responsible for keeping records which fully disclose the amount and disposition of the proceeds of the subgrant, the total cost of the programs or projects for which the subgrant is awarded, and the amount and disposition of that portion of total costs provided from non-federal sources. The procedures of the subgrantee must provide for the accurate and timely recordation of the receipt of funds, expenditures, and unexpended balances. Adequate documentation of each transaction shall be maintained to permit the determination, through an independent audit of the accuracy of the records and the allowability of expenditures charged to subgrant funds. If the records or documentation are inadequate, the questionable cost will be disallowed. The subgrantee shall reimburse the state for the amount of any disallowed items.

III. APPLICATION INSTRUCTIONS 

Prior to being considered for funding under the Omnibus Crime Control and Safe Streets Act of 1968, an application in proper form must be submitted. Two copies of the application are to be sent to the respective Regional Committee no later than March 1, 1971, and two copies should at that time be sent to the state office in Cheyenne, Box 468, 82001.

All applications improperly submitted will be returned to the applicant for correction and will not be considered until properly completed. In order to prevent unnecessary delays, it is suggested that all applicants contact their Regional Committee or the Cheyenne office if any questions should arise as to how to properly prepare the application forms. 

All applications will be reviewed by the Governor's Committee on Criminal Administration or its designated examiner and the final decision of the Committee will be communicated to the applicant. 

Procedures for appeal from the decision of the Governor's Committee are provided in the "Guide for Application and Administration of 

Planning and Action Grants". 

If the application is approved and an award granted, certain other forms hereafter described will be forwarded to the applicant. 

The regulations and conditions of the grant award will be contained in the award. The general regulations and conditions of the award are set forth in Part II of the "Guide for Application and Administration of Planning and Action Grants". Additional conditions and regulations will be contained in the individual grant award. -19-

A. B. General

This section of the guide deals with the rules and principles applicable in determining costs properly chargable to planning and action subgrants awarded to regional planning agencies and local government subgrantees under Title I of the act. It is based on the standardized cost allocation and allowability principles prescribed for all federal grant in aid programs dealing with state and local government in Bureau of the Budget 

Circular No. A-87 issued on May 9, 1968 as well as the "Financial

Guide for Administration of Planning and Action Grants" issued by the Law Enforcement Assistance Administration of the United 

States Department of Justice, dated May, 1969.

Circular A-87 was promulgated to provide the basis and standards "for a uniform approach to the problem of determining costs and promote efficiency and better relationships between state and local grantees and their federal counterparts." By subgrant conditions Circular A-87 has been incorporated in all individual awards of planning grants as well as action grants. Accordingly, and notwithstanding other "effective dates" contemplated by Circular A-87, that directive is to be regarded as applicable to all subgrant recipients receiving Title I planning or action funds and will guide the Governor's Planning Committee on Criminal Administration as well as the LEAA Administration in audits, and cost determinations with respect to such funds and their use by state agencies, local governmental units, or regional planning groups.

Basic Principles 

The policy guides and general allocability and allowability principles set forth in Circular A-87 adequately define the policies and assumptions concerning cost determinations in connection with Title I planning and action subgrants and the programs and projects to which they relate. The basic LEAA and 

State Committee focus in determining or examining allowability of costs within the A-87 framework will be the extent to which cost items contribute to the purposes and executions of the subgrant program and are so applied. It will be assumed: (1) That federal, state, and local units of government will each bear their appropriate share of allocated costs as allowable under Circular A-87 as well as under state and local law or regulations. (2) That the subgrantee will accept responsibility for expanding and accounting for federal funds in a manner consistent with (i) pertinent agreements and program objectives and (ii) policies and procedures that apply uniformly both to federally assisted and other activities of the subgrantees units of government. -39-

That costs pertinent to carrying out unrelated functions of government (i.e., unrelated to law enforcement programs receiving grant support) are not allowable and there can be no recognition of "profit" or other increment above true cost to the individual subgrantee in executing Title I subgrants. VIII. 

GRANTEE CONTRIBUTIONS AND MATCHING SHARES 

A. 

General 

The Act stipulates that planning and action grants awarded under Title I be matched by grant recipients on the basis of prescribed formulas and that federal funds not be used to pay the entire cost of authorized grant programs and activities. This section of the 

Guide sets forth instructions and principles for determining the amount of state and local contributions required as matching shares and for determining the value and appropriateness of state and local contributions actually made. Guidance as to specific items and relevant accounting rules is included. B.

Records of Contributions 

Since the requirement for state and local matching of federal grant funds is statutory, records must be maintained which show the amount and timing of contributions. These records are subject to audit and exceptions in the same manner and to the same extent as books and records dealing with the receipt and disposition of federal grant funds. As with other aspects of fiscal administration of action and planning subgrants, it is the subgrantee to which the Committee will look for primary compliance with requirements regarding matching shares.

The records of the amount and timing of contributions can be easily included within the accounting records prescribed by the Committee.

C. Timing of Contributions 

Contributions need not be made in exact time concurrence and proportion with withdrawal and expenditure of federal funds. During the first months of program or project operation, for example, the subgrantee may spend a greater proportion of its matching funds to meet program expenses than may be required by the applicable statutory ratio - or it may spend a larger proportion of federal funds. 

However, the full subgrantee matching share must be contibuted by the end of the period that federal funds are available for obligation or expenditure under a given subgrant and in no event later than the date at which the complete federal award has been expended. -53-

D. Determining Applicable Contribution Formulas 

Depending on the purpose for which a subgrant is awarded, one of our statutory ratios will apply in determining the amount of matching contributions required to match federal planning or action grants. (1) Planning Grants. A single ratio, 90% federal funds and 

10% state and local funds, applies to all planning grants awarded under Part B of Title I. For fiscal years 1969 and 1970 no matching funds are required for planning grants from regional or local planning committees since the legislature of the State of Wyoming appropriated sufficient funds to pay the 10% requirement for the local units for these two fiscal years. However, beginning with fiscal year 1971 beginning July 1, 1970, all regional or local planning groups desiring to receive funds for planning must make provisions for the contribution of the 10% matching funds. (2) Action Grants. One of three ratios applies to determine the relative Federal-State or local shares of the cost of action programs funded under Part C of Title I pursuant to comprehensive law enforcement plans approved by LEAA. This determination shall, where contrary to the applicants views, be binding upon the applicant, subject to correction or adjustment in accordance with the appeal procedures set forth herein. The three statutory ratios are: a. 

Priority Programs - Riots, Disorders and Organized Crime.

The minimum grantee contribution ratio for costs of programs within

b. Construction Programs. 

The minimum grantee contribution ratio for costs of programs for construction of law enforcement facilities within Section 301 (b) (4) of the Act is 50% state and local funds to 50% federal funds.

E. 

Identification of Construction Programs 

Any program or project, or component thereof, which involves the erection, acquisition, expansion, or repair of new or existing buildings or other physical facilities should be deemed a construction program for purposes of applicability of the minimum 50% grantee contribution to costs Section 601 (f) of Act. This is true irrespective of whether such construction is part of, or to be solely used for, an agency, operation, or program related to the control or prevention of riots and civil disorders or of organized crime. the extent that construction is included in any law enforcement action program submitted for a Part C action grant and such construction is not described as a separate program, the applicant must specify in its Program Description that a dual contribution ratio will be applied to construction costs (50-50) and other program costs (60-40 or 75-25) within the larger program.

F. 

Identification of Riots and Disorders or Organized Crime Programs 

An organized crime or riot control program may have elements or aspects which would not qualify for the 75% - 25% federal aid ratio. 

Nevertheless, their inclusion in an otherwise qualifying program may render them eligible for the priority ratio. The critical test is whether the program, project, or activity is principally and primarily designed to reduce organized crime or to prevent and control riots and other violent civil disorders. 

If non-priority activities or components are readily separable from a priority area program, this should be done. 

Where programs seem to serve both priority area and general law enforcement needs, determination will be made on a case-by-case basis as to applicable ratios at the time of grant application review. 

Additional justification may be requested by the Committee to permit a proper determination. Applicants are encouraged to request advance determination in difficult cases.

Treatment of Specific Items 

Set forth below are rules governing treatment of specific items. 

Generally, the cost allowability principles and standards of Bureau of the Budget Circular A-87 are deemed applicable to contributed subgrantee costs. 

1. Voluntary Services or Donated Goods. The value of services voluntarily rendered by private groups or individuals to the State Planning Agency or their subgrantees may not be counted as a grantee matching share or contribution to program "costs." This would include the contributed (i.e., unreimbursed) time and expenses of volunteers to planning or action efforts, either as staff, advisory board members or consultant resources, regardless of how valuable such contributions may be. Goods or property donated by private organizations may, like cash donations, be counted as matching contributions to program costs if such items are necessary for project purposes, involve a fair valuation per paragraph I above, and either (i) will be consumed or have their useful life fully expended in project use or (ii) the State Planning Agency or subgrantee agency is given permanent title to the property. 

2. Depreciation, Use and Indirect Cost Allowances. As indicated in Circular A-87, depreciation, use and indirect cost allowances constitute allowable costs in grant projects. J. Special Matching Requirement on Personnel Compensation 

Section 301 (d) of the Act specifies that not more than one-third of any action grant may be expended for compensation of personnel, except for compensation of those engaged in training programs. (See 

Section VII of Guide, paragraph Q, for interpretation). It further provides that amounts so expended from grant funds for personnel compensation: .shall not exceed the amount of State or local funds made available to increase such compensation."

A subgrantee shall be deemed to meet this requirement to match federal fund outlays for personnel compensation if its expenditures to increase compensation of personnel during the subgrant period at least equal the personnel compensation charged to federal funds under the action subgrant. Thus, if a subgrant project conducted in a particular law enforcement agency involves a personnel compensation outlay of $10,000 from grant funds, the subgrantee will be deemed to have met the special matching requirement if local funds of $10,000 were made available during the project period to increase total personnel compensation outlays by the agency. The subgrantee would, of course, also have to meet the regular grantee cost contribution requirements for the project (75-25, 60-40, or 50-50 as applicable) 

prescribed in Section 301 (c) of the Act (see Paragraph D supra). 

Planning grants are not subject to this requirement.
