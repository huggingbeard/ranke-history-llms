PREFACE 

`ROM the dawn of history to the present time the world has been cursed by an unceasing struggle for power. 

Power and still more power has been the grand objective of kings and emperors, popes and dictators. Some, more aggressive than others, have embarked upon vast schemes of self-aggrandizement, each one envisioning himself as lord of the world and supreme arbiter of the affairs of men. With reckless disregard of the rights and feelings of their fellows they have ruthlessly crushed all opposition and marched defiantly on toward their goal.

The bloodstained pages of history are crowded with the records of men possessed with this mad lust for world dominion; they are filled with sordid reports of the tragedy and sorrow they have caused. From the human viewpoint the story is confused and meaningless, but in the light of divine prophecy, which so marvelously illumines the past, the present, and the future, it takes on new significance. 

Prophecy is history in advance, and those who take time to study it will discover with what amazing accuracy it outlines the general course of world events down to the final consummation. 

In this book we have touched upon a few of the more important questions arising from the latest phase of the struggle for power, now reaching its climax. Everybody wants to know whether one nation will rule the world, whether some church will achieve supremacy, whether or not there will be one world ruler, and if so, who it will be. Only divine prophecy can answer such questions; and we trust that the reader will agree, as he peruses these pages, that it has answered well.

CHAPTER ONE SIY BATTLE OF THE AGES 

HE mightiest struggle for power this world has ever seen is surging about us today. The battle of the ages is on.

Great nations are locked in deadly combat, fighting for their lives. Everything they possess of money, effort, genius, and courage they are flinging unstintedly, and with staggering prodigality, into the stupendous conflict. 

Old empires, softened with age and security, have girded themselves to grapple with the new aspirants for dominion. 

Like giant wrestlers in the ring,-but with all the world their stage, they heave and lunge and smite with all the force they can muster, realizing to the full the terrific stakes and the dread destiny that failure must involve. 

No ordinary warfare is this, resulting from frontier disputes or supposed insults to national honor. This is a gigantic battle of ideals, of principles, of ways of life. It is a struggle between democracy and autocracy, between freedom and tyranny, between those who exalt the individual above the state and those who exalt the state above the individual. 

Consequently the conflict has developed into a vast international civil war, leaping over national boundaries and dividing erstwhile united peoples into opposing camps, with both sides trying not only to conquer the other by the sword but to convert it by the most lavish and extensive propaganda. 

No wonder the utmost confusion prevails and diverse opinions are heard on every hand. No wonder everybody is

World Catastrophe 

Back in the far-off days of peace, when rumblings of the approaching storm were becoming more and more pronounced, a farseeing world statesman warned that an avalanche of evil was poised above us, only waiting for some raucous voice to bring it crashing down upon our heads. 

That voice has now been heard. That avalanche has fallen; and in falling has brought appalling devastation over a vast and widening area.

The whole world has suffered incalculably from the shock of this titanic disaster, as if it had been struck by some mighty meteorite or shaken by a colossal earthquake.

Had a hundred volcanoes suddenly erupted they could hardly have wrought more destruction, and struck more terror to mankind, than the vast human upheaval we have witnessed these past few months.

In the thunderous convulsion whole nations have crumbled and collapsed like castles of sand before the crashing breakers of a tidal wave. Famous cities have vanished overnight. Ancient landmarks have been swept away in a moment.

Our worst fears have been realized. Modern war has proved to be more fearful than we dared to dream. With its screeching dive bombers, its magnetic mines, its huge tanks and long-range guns, and, worst of all, its utter ruthlessness, it has exceeded the most gruesome prognostications of the prophets of doom. It has demonstrated, moreover, to what depths mankind will sink when well-nigh unlimited power is seized by ungodly hands.

Long-pent-up forces of nature, unleashed by men of science, and combined with the untamed passions of ambitious and unscrupulous men, have precipitated us into this unexampled cataclysm, and turned what might have been a golden age into a raging hell. 

Sudden Disaster 

How quickly the blow has fallen! It seems but yesterday that expectations were running high that the long-hoped-for era of world peace and brotherhood was about to begin. 

With the founding of the League of Nations and the convening of the World Disarmament Conference after the Great War of 1914-18, it was almost universally believed that earth's happiest days were at hand. Though fears were not completely removed, there was a spirit of buoyancy and optimism about, and many a preacher made much of the text about beating swords into plowshares.

All that has gone now. It seems like a fast-vanishing dream. Some even find it difficult to admit they ever held such views.

Certainly the world outlook has completely changed, and with a suddenness that is startling and ominous. Leading statesmen, writers, and preachers are now boldly heralding further calamities of incalculable proportions. "Thoughtful people everywhere," says the Weekly News

Review, "are in a very serious mood. They look anxiously to the future, wondering what the history of the next ten years will be. They realize that the world is in danger of slipping into a period which later historians may describe as 'dark ages.' There is real danger of this, danger that war and the destructive forces which follow it may spread; danger that communism or fascism may be further extended; dan-

ger that the peaceful, democratic way of life which a large part of the world has enjoyed may vanish. Not to realize these possibilities at a time like this is nothing short of foolhardy."

Not long ago the Atlanta Constitution published the following striking passages in its editorial column: are "Never before in all the history of civilized man has the world faced a future so dark and ominous. These days to try men's souls. It has devolved upon our generation to bear the heaviest burden that has yet descended upon the spirit of humanity. "It seems as though we are witnessing the blood-red dawn of Armageddon. Women and children, old men and boys, wait within homes no longer secure for the blasting death which may descend, momentarily, from the once serene skies.

The people look to the heavens no more for succor, but cower in fear of the brutal death which lurks behind the clouds. "He who prophesies the complete eradication of civilization as we know it is no longer regarded as a fanatic alarmist; his words are heard with the foreboding that they may well be true."

Falling Night 

Grim forebodings there are in abundance today. Widespread is the fear that night, dreadful and terrible night, is falling over the world.

Already almost all of Europe is engulfed, and the shadows are lengthening farther and farther out to the ends of the earth. ← THE PICTURE: It is surely a sign of the times when peaceful old English churches are destroyed by bombs. International

Practically every nation in the Old World today lives under an ironclad tyranny. Liberty has vanished. Justice has fled. Only the law of the jungle prevails; the strongest takes the prey.

We have entered an epoch more cruel, more heartless, more terrifying, than any the world has known before. And in the fearful darkness we can hear the frightened cries of a great host of refugees fleeing before invading armies; the pitiful weeping of thousands of orphaned, homeless, hungry children; the despairing groans of countless multitudes who have been dragged from their homes and hiding places by secret police, kicked and bludgeoned in concentration camps. 

And the end is not yet; for it is inevitable that all the frightful carnage, all the colossal waste of wealth, must result in famines and pestilences, civil strife and revolutions. 

Asleep in the Storm

Strangely enough, some people are still unaware of the mighty issues at stake in the present international struggle, and its tremendous possibilities of further disaster. 

They are asleep in the greatest storm that ever struck the world. They can be heard discussing the most trifling matters while great nations are hurled into oblivion, a whole continent reverts to barbarism, and the world shudders with the shock of battling empires.

Equally careless and ignorant of the stupendous possibilities inherent in this gigantic conflict are the multitudes who are thronging the places of amusement, flocking to the picture theaters, drinking, dancing, and carousing, while civilization crashes about their ears, freedom perishes from the earth, democracy is contemptuously overthrown, and Christianity itself is challenged as never before in history.

They seem like foolish children playing on a river, shouting and laughing together, oblivious of the fact that their little boats have already been caught in the rapids and are being swept on relentlessly toward the falls. 

Never were the words of the apostle Paul so applicable as in this solemn moment: "Knowing the time, that now it is high time to awake out of sleep." Romans 13:11.

This is no ordinary hour. It is the greatest moment of time. It is the climax of history. Today the stage is being set for the final scenes in the drama of the ages. The very upheavals we have already witnessed are paving the way for the fearful time of trouble, terror, and persecution that is to sweep the earth in its final crisis. 

God Still Reigns

Confusing as the present situation undoubtedly is, however dark and forbidding though the future may appear, we must not permit ourselves to doubt that behind all the dini and horror of war, behind all the bewildering claims and counterclaims of contending factions, back of all the incalculable personal sufferings of innocent people, the God of heaven still lives and reigns.

Such a belief will not only afford wondrous comfort at such a time; it will assist to an amazing degree in clarifying the issues at stake, in allaying our fears for the future, and in answering our questions as to what is to happen next. 

Listening hour by hour to broadcasts of stories of invasion after invasion, of gigantic forces crushing the weak, of whole armies being annihilated, of whole cities being destroyed, of horrible cruelties being perpetrated on a scale hitherto unimagined, we are all liable to lose both faith and courage simultaneously, and plunge desperately into despair. Eternal Watchers

Therefore there is no need to worry overmuch at the transient tragedies of our time, nor be disturbed by the loud boastings of those who have seized power for the moment. 

Their triumphs will fade, their scepters fall. Rather, let us turn our eyes trustingly heavenward, striving to watch God "working His purpose out." With perfect vision, perfect understanding, and perfect judgment He is directing the course of history to its final consummation. With infinite wisdom. 

He weighs each move and motive; and with inflexible purpose and unfailing power He plans and guides in everything toward the final triumph of goodness, the victory of every righteous cause.

When such a faith is ours, and such a perspective has brought tranquillity to our troubled souls, then it shall be said of us, as it was once said of another in the long ago, “He shall not be afraid of evil tidings: his heart is fixed, trusting in the Lord." Psalm 112:7.

E ARE witnessing today one of those rare phenomena of history, the rise and progress of a would-be conqueror of the world. These turbulent times have, in fact, produced several contestants for this questionable honor.

As a result a great fear has entered the hearts of multitudes in every land on earth, fear for their little homes, for their boys and girls, for all their treasured possessions. For when restless, insatiable leaders set out on a career of conquest they usually stop at nothing in their zeal to reach their goal; and it is the common people who suffer most. 

However, such men have risen before-strange, mystical, ambitious men—and blazed a fiery trail through the nations like some comet sweeping on its dazzling way across the sky. But, like comets, they have faded from view as suddenly as they have appeared.

There is little difference in the essential elements of these occasional eruptions of dissatisfied humanity; only this time science has provided the modern Attilas with additional weapons of destruction.

One reason why so many are profoundly distressed at this latest international tragedy is that they are living in the very midst of the harrowing scenes, and are unable as yet to appraise them in their relation to the past and the future. God and the Nations

Back in the dim beginnings of time upon this earth, when the seat of world power was still in the sun-baked Euphrates Valley, and Europe was to men an unknown land, God knew that the course of empire would move toward the west. 

When as yet Athens and Rome were insignificant villages, He saw their rise to glory, and all the glittering parade of later kingdoms down to our day and beyond.

Because of this perfect vision,-and His full knowledge of human weakness,-world power does not mean the same to God as it does to man; and those who understand God's mind, as revealed in His prophetic word, will share His own calm attitude concerning it. "Behold, the nations are as a drop of a bucket, and are counted as the small dust of the balance: behold, He taketh up the isles as a very little thing.” "All nations before Him are as nothing; and they are counted to Him less than nothing, and vanity." In the sixteenth century, Charles V became ruler of most of Europe, and had visions of completing the conquest of the Continent. It was said of him that "no monarch until 

Napoleon was so widely seen in Europe and in Africa." Yet, in 1555, because of failing health, he was compelled to abdicate and to sign away his vast possessions to others.

Little more than a hundred years later, Louis XIV of France became the dominant figure on the Continent; he reached out in all directions for more and more authority, overrunning the Netherlands, laying waste the Palatinate, and exclaiming: "There are no longer any Pyrenees." Nevertheless a combination of opposing forces finally brought his grandiose schemes tumbling about him like a castle of cards.

Napoleon Tries and Fails

Then, less than a century and a half ago, came Napoleon, perhaps the greatest of all these would-be lords of Europe and conquerors of the world. He was thrown up, as it were, by the French Revolution, and from 1795 to 1804, when he was proclaimed emperor, he consolidated his hold upon the people of France. Then for eight fearful years he swept over nation after nation, crowning himself king of Italy, placing his brother Louis on the throne of Holland, and his brother Jerome on the throne of the new kingdom of Westphalia, which he created. His brother-in-law Murat he made sovereign of his newly established Grand Duchy of Berg, and he gave his brother Joseph the throne of Spain. No other conqueror ever made such thorough preparation for the establishment and perpetuation of a united Europe. 

Nevertheless, even before his plans were completed, rumblings of the coming disintegration could be heard.

In 1805, the French fleet was defeated at Trafalgar. In 

1812 came Napoleon's Russian expedition, his retreat from 

Moscow, the subsequent defeat at Leipzig in October, 1813, followed by his final crushing overthrow at Waterloo in 1815.

In 1914, well within living memory, a similar attempt at European domination was made. The kaiser's armies swept over Belgium, rolled on into France, into Italy, into the Balkans. Their shattering blows seemed, for many a long day, to indicate that the final triumph would be theirs. Those who lived through those dark and evil days will not soon forget the gloom that settled over the world as retreat followed retreat before the advancing forces of the conqueror,

nor the tremendous feeling of relief as, miraculously, the course of events suddenly changed, and, before one could scarcely appreciate the magnitude of what was taking place, it had all ended at Versailles and Doorn.

Thus has it happened in the past. Thus will it happen again. How, no one can tell. One hesitates even to suggest.

It might be by some internal revolution; it might be by the discovery of new weapons; it might be by the outbreak of some fearful pestilence; it might be by a belated combination of the remaining free nations in an effort to save themselves from slavery; it might be in some altogether different way that no one has as yet envisioned. But of one thing we may be absolutely certain, and that is that the prophecy will not fail. When the present seemingly irresistible force of the great invader is spent, Europe will revert to its old divided state. After the overspreading eruption of iron has ceased, the elements of clay will reappear, cracking and breaking the mass of metal into its old-time divisions. Boundaries may be altered here and there, but the main lines of the ancient frontiers will be restored.

CHAPTER THREE WILL ONE CHURCH DOMINATE THE WORLD? 

ETTLED forever is the question whether one nation will rule the world. In the light of the great prophecy we have just examined it is clear as noonday that no such development is possible. Every attempt to set up a universal empire of this sort, under one centralized government, or in obedience to the will of some supreme potentate, is foredoomed to failure.

But what of the possibility that the whole world situation, perhaps as a result of war weariness, or for some other good reason, may take a religious turn, and that the power which no nation can successfully grasp may be seized by a church? 

This may seem a strange question to ask at a moment of world confusion and catastrophe, when the churches, both Protestant and Catholic, were never, apparently, so impotent as now. Attacked on all sides by the forces of modern atheism, their financial income reduced, their spheres of activity curtailed, their inner convictions weakened by doctrinal dissensions, their fortunes-so far as outward appearances go -could hardly be at lower ebb. Nevertheless the idea still persists in many minds that one result of the present world upheaval may well be such an overwhelming demand for new leadership of a different, more spiritual order as to cause a mighty turning toward the church which boldly offers to provide it.

Stealing the War 

In one of his latest books, The New World Order, H. G. 

Wells, the famous novelist and historian, whose understanding of the trend of events has so frequently been proved correct, makes the remarkable statement that the Vatican "is trying to steal the war."

He means, presumably, that the powers that be at the Vatican are making the most of the present world conflict to advance the interests of their particular church and to see to it that when the war ends this organization will exert a much stronger international influence than at present. 

Such an effort would not be unnatural, nor unwarranted, nor indeed unexpected. In fact, the most casual observer of recent events must admit that there is more than a little evidence to support the famous novelist's bold assertion. 

Every few weeks the newspapers headline new pleas for peace uttered by the pope. More and more the prestige of the ruler of the Vatican is being “built up" by his vast publicity system as the great peacemaker of our time. And no one needs to be told that the person who can lay claim to any measure of responsibility for bringing peace to this warwracked world will be assured of widespread and unstinted appreciation.

Moreover, certain very significant events have been taking place of late in Rome itself. Back in 1929, as we all well remember, a concordat between the Italian state and the Vatican removed most of the grievances between the two powers. 

The "prisoner of the Vatican" was released. A new Vatican state was organized, with its own territory, radio station, postal system, railroad depot, and all essential signs of complete independence. The pope became a king again-king of the smallest, but perhaps the most influential, dominion in the world.

For more than ten years now there have been two kings in Rome, but no matter what political differences may have separated them in the past, their present friendship is unquestioned. The two kings have met twice, first in the Vatican, secondly in the Quirinal.

"This extraordinary visit," wrote Richard G. Massock of the Associated Press, on December 28, 1939, referring to the call of Pope Pius XII at the Royal Palace, “in return for the call made by King Vittorio Emanuele and Queen Elena on the Holy Father at the Vatican a week ago, symbolized complete reciprocal recognition of the two great sovereignties established on opposite banks of the Tiber."

The prophet is still in Babylon, but time has left its mark upon him. Nebuchadnezzar has been dead some years. Belshazzar has come to the throne, the last of this imperial dynasty. The empire is tottering to its fall. Despite its colossal defenses, but seventeen years remain before its overthrow. 

The golden head of the great image is about to give place to the breast and arms of silver.

It is night. The prophet is dreaming. He seems to be standing upon the seashore watching the wild waves lashed to fury by a mighty wind. Only one power has arisen in history which has met in complete detail every one of these specifications—the papacy. 

The Papacy's Rise to Power 

This interpretation is not inspired by personal prejudice. 

It is merely the statement of an obvious and unchallengeable fact of history.

To those who resent the suggestion, and its unavoidable implications, we would say at once that it is made in no spirit of personal antagonism. It has been our privilege to visit distinguished fathers of the church in the headquarters of the Jesuits in Rome; we have enjoyed a most pleasant hour with the Vatican astronomer in the pontifical palace at the Castel Gandolfo; we have talked with the Jesuit representative at the League of Nations in Geneva. Always in such contacts we have been treated with the utmost courtesy, which has been gladly reciprocated. Not for a moment would we willingly hurt the feelings of any of these good men, or the many others like them.

When we mention the papacy we are thinking of an ecclesiastical system, not of individuals; and when one is

seeking to interpret the great prophecy of Daniel 7:25 there is no possible way of avoiding the conclusion that it applies directly to this vast, powerful, mysterious, partly religious, partly political, organization.

Trace the papacy back to its dim beginnings in the martyred church of Rome; note the gradually increasing prestige of its elder, or bishop, because of his location at the seat of empire; recall his growing claims-challenged at first by other bishops, but eventually conceded-to leadership of the whole Christian church, and you have a sequence of events marvelously fitting the rise of a "little horn," or a new world power, amid the ten other kingdoms contending for the territory of the Roman Empire. 

Even in the days of the apostle Paul, unfortunate trends were already making themselves manifest in the church, and he wrote to the Thessalonians: "The mystery of iniquity doth already work: only he who now letteth will let, until he be taken out of the way." 2 Thessalonians 2:7.

Some power at that time was "letting," or hindering, the expansion of the little horn. The Caesars still ruled, and as long as they should do so the papacy could never develop as foreshadowed in the prophecy. 

Centuries rolled by; and as the power of the Caesars waned, so the power of the bishops waxed. When the last of the emperors passed off the scene and the great Empire of Rome came to its end as a world power, the way was opened for the almost unlimited aggrandizement of the pope. The vacant throne presented an opportunity he was not slow to grasp, and it was not long before he had assumed much of Caesar's surrendered authority and had begun a career of despotism and "power politics" to last a thousand years.

James A. Wylie, in his book, The Papacy; Its History, Dogmas, Genius, and Prospects, writing of the establishment of papal supremacy, says: "At last the empire of the West was dissolved. The seat which had been occupied so long by the master of the world was now empty. . . . The overthrow of the empire contributed most materially towards the elevation of the Bishop of Rome; for, first, it took the Caesars out of the way. 'A secret hand,' says De Maistre, 'chased the emperors from the Eternal City, to give it to the head of the Eternal Church.' 

Second, it compelled the bishops of Rome, now deprived of the imperial influence which had hitherto helped them so mightily in their struggles for pre-eminence, to fall back on another element, and that an element which constitutes the very essence of the papacy, and on which is founded the whole complex fabric of the spiritual and temporal domination of the popes. 

With Rome would have fallen her bishop, had he not, as if by anticipation of the crisis, reserved till this hour the master stroke of his policy. He now boldly cast himself upon an element of much greater strength than that of which the political convulsions of the times had deprived him, namely, that the Bishop of Rome is the successor of Peter, the prince of the apostles, and in virtue of being so, is Christ's vicar on earth. In making this claim, the Roman pontiffs vaulted at once over the throne of kings to the seat of gods: Rome became once more the mistress of the world, and her popes the rulers of the earth.”—Pages 

33, 34 (1852). Cardinal Manning (1808-1892) himself recognized this transfer of authority from pagan to papal Rome, and wrote as follows: 

"The abandonment of Rome was the liberation of the

pontiffs. Whatsoever claims to obedience the emperors may have made, and whatsoever compliance the pontiffs may have yielded, the whole previous relation . . . was finally dissolved. . . . The providence of God permitted a succession of eruptions, Gothic, Lombard, and Hungarian, to desolate Italy, and to efface from it every remnant of the empire. 

The pontiffs found themselves alone, the sole fountains of order, peace, law, and safety; and from the hour of this providential liberation . . . the chains fell off from the hands of the successor of St. Peter. "A power had grown up in Rome, far more imperial over the reason and will of man than the iron despotism of the Roman Empire. 

This interior and supernatural power of direction and government over the actions and hearts of men flowed from one center, and was embodied in one person, the Bishop of Rome. The floods which swept all the other authorities away threw out into bolder relief and more conspicuous prominence the supreme pastoral authority of the vicars of Jesus Christ." 

Three Horns Uprooted 

Three "horns" were to be removed by the "little horn."

This happened also. As the papacy rose to power three of the ten kingdoms were eliminated at her instigation and by her machinations. These were the Heruli, the Vandals, and the Ostrogoths. 

Firmly established at last in his strategic position in “the 

Eternal City," proclaimed by Justinian a. D. 533 as “head of the church and the corrector of heretics," the Bishop of Rome began to assume powers, prerogatives, and titles no emperor, in the height of his ambitions, ever conceived. He not only styled himself "Vicegerent of the Son of God," and

"Lord God, the Pope," but approved such adulation as the following in an address given at the Fifth Lateran Council in 1512: “Thou art the shepherd, thou art the physician, thou art the director, thou art the husbandman; finally, thou art another God on earth.”—Labbe and Cossart, History of the Councils, vol. 14, col. 109. 

Words of Blasphemy

In an article on the pope, to be found in Ferraris's Ecclesiastical Dictionary, are these words: "The Pope is of so great dignity and so exalted that he is not a mere man, but as it were God, and the vicar of God. "The Pope is of such lofty and supreme dignity that, properly speaking, he has not been established in any rank of dignity, but rather has been placed upon the very summit of all ranks of dignities. "He is likewise the divine monarch and supreme emperor, and king of kings. "Hence the Pope is crowned with a triple crown, as king of heaven and of earth and of the lower regions. "Moreover the superiority and the power of the Roman Pontiff by no means pertain only to heavenly things, to earthly things, and to things under the earth, but are even over angels, than whom he is greater. But when is this vast change in world affairs to take place? We might be tempted to place it in the far-distant future but for certain clear evidence to the contrary. 

Seven Great Facts 

First is the fact that the great prophetic time periods, already mentioned in this book, have already elapsed. The 

1260 years of Daniel 7:25 ended in 1798. The 2300 years of 

Daniel 8:14 ended in 1844. Nearly a century has passed

since then-the greatest, most momentous century in all history.

Second is the fact that we are living "in the days of these kings"—or kingdoms-that superseded the Roman Empire, during which period the kingdom of God is to be set up. 

For more than fourteen centuries Europe has been in this divided state, and we may well believe that the fulfillment of the divine promise cannot much longer be delayed.

Third is the fact that we find ourselves in the midst of the most remarkable scientific era of all time. This is the age of invention and discovery. Everywhere we are confronted with some laborsaving, time-eliminating device unknown to previous generations. Moreover, every continent has been mapped, every ocean charted. As Jules Verne wrote, “There are no more impassable deserts, no more unfathomable seas, no more inaccessible mountains." The explorer's task is done; while the airplane, radio, and television have annihilated distance and brought "the ends of the earth” nearer than they have ever been before. All this is more than coincidental, and when viewed in the light of the prophecy that in the time of the end "many shall run to and fro, and knowledge shall be increased," it becomes freighted with deep significance.

Fourth is the fact that after the tragic failure of all man's best and noblest efforts to establish enduring peace, the world is being torn, battered, and ruined by the most terrible war of history, which is rapidly producing an intensity of fear, horror, and suffering abundantly fulfilling Christ's prediction concerning "distress . . . with perplexity" among the nations and “men's hearts failing them for fear, and for looking after those things which are coming on the earth." 

Fifth is the fact, dreadful and ominous, that liberty is

passing. All man's struggles for freedom have been largely nullified during the past few years. His immense and incalculable sacrifices to establish "the rights of man" have come to nought. Millions who have tasted the sweets of liberty for a few brief decades are now ground under the iron heel of tyrannies more terrifying than any that cursed the earth before. Secret police swarm amongst them, ready to stifle the first mutterings of independence or revolt. Concentration camps are crowded with courageous souls who have dared to speak their minds. Liberty's light is flickering out, and the darkness of midnight is fast engulfing mankind. 

Remembering the multitudes who have suffered inhuman tortures, confiscation of property, imprisonment, and exile under one or other of the new authoritarian regimes, there seems little doubt that we have come to that "time of trouble, such as never was since there was a nation" foreseen by Daniel as one of the closing phases of history. 

Sixth is the fact of the moral deterioration which confronts us on every hand today. This is to be seen not only in the increase of crime, racketeering, and corrupt politics, but also in the decay of home life, the multiplication of divorces, and lowered standards where virtue, truth, and honesty are concerned. How often have we heard people say, "You can't trust anybody any more," or, "You can't believe anybody any more." Many practices considered improper only a few years ago are now carried on openly, even being tolerated in Christian communities. Men are "lovers of pleasures more than lovers of God; having a form of godliness, but denying the power thereof," as Paul said would happen in “the last days." 2 Timothy 3:4, 5. All of which is typical of the downward trend toward a state of affairs "as it was in the days of Noah" and "as it was in the days

of Lot." Everybody knows what it was like then, when "every imagination of the thoughts" of men's hearts "was only evil continually." But the Lord warned that when similar conditions should reappear we would know that the day of the Son of man had arrived. 

Seventh is the fact that to a very large degree the gospel has now been preached in all the world as a witness unto all nations. A century ago this would not have been true. 

At that time only a handful of missionaries had ventured forth into heathen parts. The Bible was available in very few languages. During these hundred years, however, a mighty change has taken place and a glorious work has been done. It has been the age of missions. Doors into long-closed lands have opened mysteriously in rapid succession, and into all these challenging opportunities the messengers of the cross have hurried with one consuming purpose to serve and sacrifice for their Lord and Master, and to finish His work. 

Pentecost has been outdone a thousandfold as the gospel has been preached in every nation under heaven. Translations of the Bible have followed one another until the list of languages has reached and passed a thousand. This world-wide proclamation of the gospel during the nineteenth century down to the present time constitutes perhaps the most remarkable religious phenomenon in history. And Jesus said that when this should happen, when the gospel of the kingdom should have been preached as a witness unto all nations, "then shall the end come."

Considered separately any one of these facts might be discounted and shown to have no special reference to any coming change in world affairs; but viewed as a whole they comprise the most convincing and startling evidence that the oft-predicted, long-expected "end of the world" cannot now

be far away. In fact, Jesus Himself said, "When ye see these things come to pass, know ye that the kingdom of God is nigh at hand." Luke 21:31. 

Yes, the new world order—and the new world ruleris near, “even at the doors."

And because this great change in world affairs is to come suddenly, with the speed of lightning, with the rapidity of a heavenly blitzkrieg, the Lord left us this warning to maintain ceaseless vigilance and preparedness: “Take heed to yourselves, lest at any time your hearts be overcharged with surfeiting, and drunkenness, and cares of this life, and so that day come upon you unawares. For as a snare shall it come on all them that dwell on the face of the whole earth. Watch ye therefore, and pray always, that ye may be accounted worthy to escape all these things that shall come to pass, and to stand before the Son of man." Luke 21:34-36.

There will be no compulsion save the compelling power of love; but overflowing gratitude for "so great salvation" will ever seek to express itself in gladsome obedience, joyous co-operation, and devoted loyalty. The Secret of the Kingdom 

It sounds too good to be true!

It does, and yet it is true. It must be true or the last ray of hope would die out in the soul of man and the darkness of utter despair would settle upon him. 

But if it is true, if this new world order is indeed going to be established on this war-scarred planet, then there must be some secret about it. How can even God Himself bring about such a state of perfection, of unalloyed happiness and contentment, with the type of human material with which 

He has to deal?

There is a secret about it. It has to deal with such elementary things as the state of mind, the outlook, and the direction of purpose of those who are going to dwell in the coming kingdom.

God knows the difficulties. After watching the course of history for six thousand years He has no illusions on that score. With aching heart He has seen the whole human race plunging through the morass of sin, hating and lust-
