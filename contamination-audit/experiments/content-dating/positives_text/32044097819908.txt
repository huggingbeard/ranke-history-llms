PREFACE 

24 This manual is prepared and published in conformity with the law by the Elections Division of the Department of State. It is an attempt to give in brief and plain language an outline of the duties of election boards and the proper methods for the conduct of elections. It is believed that the use of this manual will result in better understanding and in more uniform procedure. While every effort has been made to check with the laws on elections, errors are always possible. Should differences be found, it is the law itself that must be followed. When there is any doubt, consult your local election officials.

This office will always be happy to have any suggestions for corrections or improvements of this booklet from you who use it.

In order for a person to vote, he must be registered in the precinct in which he offers to vote. To register, a person must have the following qualifications by the next primary, general, or special election: (A) Citizen of the United States. (B) 21 years of age. (C) Resident of Michigan for 6 months. (D) Resident of township or city for 30 days. $44

If an elector moves from a city or township in which he is registered to another city or township within Michigan during the 30-day period just prior to an election, he may vote in that election only in the precinct of the city or township from which he has just moved. When applying to vote, such an elector shall in addition to signing the application for ballot form, sign an affidavit stating that he has moved from the city or township in which he is applying to vote to another city or township in Michigan within the 30-day period just prior to the election.

Appointment of Inspectors

NOT MORE THAN 50%, AS NEAR AS MAY BE, OF EACH BOARD SHALL BE OF THE SAME POLITICAL PARTY. 

The election commissions in cities and townships shall appoint a board of election inspectors for each precinct in their jurisdiction. 

Each of such boards shall consist of at least 3 inspectors. More than 

3 shall be appointed if needed for proper conduct of the election. All the inspectors must be qualified and registered electors of the city or township in which they serve, but they need not be registered in the precinct in which they serve. No person shall be appointed an elec tion inspector unless he shall have completed an application on a form approved by the state director of elections for such position and shall have a certificate showing that he has been properly instructed in the operation of voting machines. If a majority of the board is not present at any time while the polls are open, the electors present shall choose enough qualified electors as inspectors to fill the vacancies.

POLL Display of Flag 

The chairman of the board or some other member of the election board must see that a flag of the United States is properly displayed at or in each polling place during the progress of primaries and elections. If the flag is hung in the horizontal or vertical position, the blue field must always be on the left when one is facing the flag.

Checking Counters 

After the machine has been opened, the various counters must be checked to make sure that a proper count will result. There are three types of counters to be checked. 

1. The Protective Counter: 

The protective counter shows the total number of votes that have ever been cast on a machine. This number is recorded on the key envelope or key tag. Check to see that these numbers correspond. 

2. The Public Counter: 

The public counter records how many votes have been cast at a given election. In the morning, before the polls are open this counter must register zero. 

3. The Individual Counter: 

These counters record the vote the individual candidates received at a given election. In the morning, before the polls open, these counters must register zero. On the Shoup Machine these counters 22

are found on the face of the machine. On the Automatic Machine they are located in the rear of the machine. On the newer Automatic Machine a movable light can be found in the rear of the machine to give added light in reading the counters. f any of the counters do not register as indicated above, notify your lerk or custodian and receive instructions from him. Seals On Machine 

Voting machines are sealed with numbered seals. The numbers on these seals are recorded on the key tag or envelope. these numbers correspond. 

On the Automatic Machine, the seal is located in one of the following places: (1) On the voters operating lever, or (2) On the lower left hand corner of the face of the machine, or (3) On the entrance latch.

In all cases the seal is plainly evident. 

On the Shoup Machine are two seals, both located on the key shelf in the rear. One is attached to the knurled knob and one to the ballot strip bar.

If these numbers do not agree with those on the key envelope or tag, call your clerk or custodian. Do not use the machine until clearance is received as either an error has been made or someone has tampered with the machine. 26

Ballot Application and Registration Record $254 Before permitting a person to vote, the following procedure must be followed: 

1. Have the voter sign a ballot application. A voter must sign this application in the same manner as he signed the registration card. 

2. Locate the voter's registration card in the registration book. The record of each voter must be in the official registration record. If you cannot find the card, check the address on the application to see if the voter is in the proper precinct. If so, check the record to see if the 33

card has been misplaced. If a voter resides in your precinct and maintains that he is registered and the record cannot be found, call the clerk of your city or township. 

3. Compare the signature and address on the application with the voter's signature and address on the registration record. Be sure they agree. Take nothing for granted. a. 

If the address differs, determine the location of the new address: (1) If the new address is located within the same precinct as is the old address, the address shall be changed in the manner your local clerk directs. The voter shall be permitted to vote as he is a registered elector of the precinct. (2) If the new address is located within the same city or township as the old address is but in a different precinct, the voter shall sign a transfer of registration form and shall be allowed to 

(3) If the new address is located in another city or township, the voter shall not be permitted to vote unless he shall have moved within 30 days of the election as discussed on page 9. b. In some instances a voter may have changed his name for such reasons as marriage, information contained in the birth record, correction in spelling or by court order. In such cases the registration record needs to be changed to meet the new circumstances. Be guided by the rules of your city or township clerk who handles registration of voters.

Challenge and Oath

Any challenger, or registered and qualified elector of the precinct may, and any inspector of election present shall, challenge a person's right to vote if they know or have good reason to suspect that such a person is not a qualified elector of the precinct. A challenge may also come from the city or township clerk and will appear on the registration record of the person to be challenged. The challenged person must take the following oath orally before he can vote. 

He need not, however, sign any statements or affidavits: "I solemnly swear (or affirm) that I will well and truly answer all questions put to me touching my qualifications as a voter." 

Any inspector may administer the oath after which any inspector, challenger, or qualified elector of that precinct may question the chal lenged person as to his qualifications as an elector. The inspector must see to it that the challenge is courteously and promptly handled. 

If the challenged person shall have shown by his answers that he is qualified and shall have taken the oath, he shall then be permitted to vote. If the person challenged refuses to take the oath, refuses to answer under oath or shows by his answers that he is not qualified to vote in that precinct, he shall not be permitted to vote.
