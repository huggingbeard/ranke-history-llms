B-The object of a verb is in the objective (accusative) case. 

C=The object of a preposition is in the objective case. 

D=The predicate noun is in the nominative case. 

E=A verb should agree with its subject in person. 

F-A verb should agree with its subject in number. 

G=A pronoun should agree with its antecedent in number. 

H=A pronoun should agree with its antecedent in gender.

DIRECTIONS TO TEACHERS 

Three pronounced advantages may be claimed for this series of tests. In the first place they afford an unusual opportunity to measure the progress of individual pupils. This arises from the fact that the eighteen tests of the series are of approximately equal difficulty. In the second place they offer a direct comparison of one grade with another. Each test contains items ranging from easy to hard. The series may therefore appropriately be used in several successive grades. Since, when so used, the work is "constant," the group-score at once indicates the status of the grade in question with respect to the abilities measured by the test. In the third place the abilities of individuals and of classes may be compared with expected abilities. This opportunity is afforded by the grade norms which have been provisionally worked out and which are presented on the inside of the back cover. 

It is recommended that the first test be given during the first week of school in the fall, and that a test be given every two weeks throughout the year. Thirty-six weeks would be required to complete the series.

In each test certain questions are starred. The star means that the question may contain an error. 

Many starred questions are, however, correct; therefore the pupil must first decide whether or not there is an error. Directions for these starred questions are given before each test. Each page of the tests contains also a group of Key Sentences. 

It will be found that certain questions in each test are based on these. Still other questions call for a knowledge of the rules of grammar found inside the front cover of the test book. 

The following procedure is a good one to follow in using the tests: 

1. Pass to each pupil a copy of the test book, having him write on the front cover his name and other data required. 

2. Explain that the answers should be very brief, one word often being enough, and that complete sentences are not required. See the brief answers in the Teachers' Key. 

Explain that it is better not to guess at answers when one is in much doubt.

In general the more difficult questions come last, so that after a pupil passes several questions in succession that he cannot answer, it will be of little use for him to try the remaining questions. 

3. Have all pupils start at the same time. Then the teacher should stroll about the room two or three times to see that every pupil understands how to proceed. Have them stop at the end of thirty minutes. If all pupils have written all the answers they know before the thirty minutes are up (and this will often be true in grades V and VI), the papers may be collected. 

As some pupils will finish several minutes before others do, it is well to announce that if a pupil finishes before the time is up, he may close his test book and read from some book in his desk. 

4. If the teacher is to score the papers herself, have the test books collected. In scoring do not give credit for answers partially correct. 

5. A day or two later, after the tests have been scored, pass the test books to the pupils. Each should turn to the score-card on the inside of the back cover and record his score by making a big dot opposite the number he had correct and in the column that is numbered the same as was the test he took. After the first test each dot should be connected to the preceding dot with a line (graph). 

6. Call on individual pupils for the correct answer to each question in turn, proceeding in the test only as far as your curriculum outline for the year demands (unless the curiosity of a group of superior pupils induces you to go somewhat further). Explain all difficulties or have them explained. 

7. Compliment pupils who have made gains rather than pupils who have made high scores. 

Also bear in mind that a small gain made by a slow pupil is equivalent in effort to a much larger gain by a superior pupil. 

8. Collect the test books promptly each time after using them. 

9. Drill may be provided by reviewing any test that has already been taken this year and having the answers given orally. In this oral drill do not have the incorrect forms of the starred questions read aloud. Only the corrected form should be read aloud. 

10. Some teachers feel that with care better results are secured if the pupils exchange test books (or each keeps his own if the room morale is very high) and score the answers as the teacher reads the correct answers from the Key. This scoring should be followed by an explanation of difficulties. 

11. The papers will be somewhat easier to score if the teacher suggests to the pupils that their answers be written in the right-hand portion of the blank spaces when the answers require less space than is provided.
