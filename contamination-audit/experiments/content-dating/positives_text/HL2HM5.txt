Introduction 

This report gives statistics on all Parole Board votes resulting from parole, parole rescission and parole revocation hearings held at state institutions during 1976. Office votes are not included. 

There were 1234 state parole release interviews during 1976 (see 

Table 1). Of this total, 778 were First Interviews (see Table 2). First 

Interviews are those where the person is seeing the Board for the first time on this sentence. First Interviews consist of Regular Order Interviews, One-Third Interviews and Early Consideration Interviews. During 1976 there were 694 Regular Order Interviews (see Table 3), 49 One-Third Interviews (see Table 4) and 32 Early Consideration Interviews (see Table 5). 

1976 also saw the introduction of the concept of contract parole in In December 1976 the Parole Board entered into contracts Massachusetts. with three inmates who were given parole dates contingent on their fulfilling the terms of their contracts. In 1977 the pilot projects of Victim 

Restitution and Mutual Agreement Programming (MAP) will result in a higher number of contracts being entered into with inmates. 

The 1976 paroling rate was 58%. The paroling rate is the percentage of all First Interviews voted parole or reserve. In the 1975 report the "parole" vote category included both paroles to street and to custody. the 1976 report these are two separate categories. 

In 1976, 451 of the In 778 First Interviews received a parole, reserve or parole to custody vote; 

56% of all Regular Order Interviews, 71% of all One-Third Interviews, and 91% of all Early Consideration Interviews received a parole, reserve or parole to custody vote. Concord-Impact cases accounted for one half of all 

Early Consideration Interviews.

While 58% of all 1976 First Interviews were given a definite parole date, only 17.5% received a "deny" vote. An additional 1.2% were "denied at their own request." So in actuality the proportion of 1976 First Interviews receiving some type of positive consideration by the Board was 81.3%. 

In addition to the parole release hearings and contract parole hearings, 55 rescission hearings and 335 revoke hearings were held during 1976. Twentyfive of the 55 rescission hearings (45%) received a parole or parole to custody vote (see Table 11). Eighty-seven of the 335 revoke hearings (26%) were voted re-release or parole to custody (see Table 12). 

The Parole Board held an average of 112 release hearings, 5 rescission hearings and 30 revoke hearings per month at state institutions during 1976.
