To the Teacher 

THE MATERIAL in this workbook may be used either as teaching or testing mate- Trial. If the pupils have not yet studied the content with which the exercises are concerned, the teacher may regard the various pages as teaching devices. If, however, the pupils have already studied similar material, then the exercises may be used as diagnostic tests to reveal the strengths and weaknesses of the pupils with reference to the items tested.

When the exercises are used for purposes of diagnosis, the main value of using them as tests is that the results obtained point with a fair degree of certainty to the causes of individual difficulties, and thus give the teacher valuable assistance in improving his instruction - a much more valid aim of modern testing practice than the standardization of pupils' accomplishments for purposes of comparison with other school systems.

In the first part of the book there is a comprehensive survey of the essential features of Informal Geometry, including: 

1. Intuitive geometry, in which the pupil looks at a figure and says that something is so because it could not be otherwise, as, for example, "If two straight lines intersect, the vertical angles are equal." 

2. Experimental geometry of a simple type, such as cutting out a paper triangle, tearing off the angles, and placing them adjacent so as to show that their sum is 180°. 

3. Observational geometry, in which the pupil recognizes objects in the world about him as typifying certain geometric forms, and sees certain relationships that exist between them. 

4. Fundamental geometric skills, such as constructing a perpendicular from a given point outside a line to that line. 

5. A simple approach to demonstration through informal proofs of theorems, such as, for example, "The sum of the angles of a quadrilateral is 360°" after the pupil has learned that "The sum of the angles of a triangle is 180°."

Such a survey covers the questions that a person may ask about an object, namely: "What is its shape?" (Geometry of Form), "What is its size?" (Geometry of Size), "Where is the object located in space?" (Geometry of Position).

In studying the geometry of size, the pupil is introduced to simple formulas (the language of size), simple equations, and methods for solving them. 

In the latter part of the book there is a review of the basic arithmetic skills usually taught in the first six grades of the elementary school together with the application of these skills in problems relating to life situations. 

The informal geometry is presented in psychological and logical order - instead of being interwoven with arithmetical topics in order that the teacher may locate more easily the material he wishes the pupils to study. 

The actual time that it will take to do the various exercises in arithmetic will vary from page to page and in general it will take longer to do the pages on arithmetic than those on geometry. So, although the space given to geometry, in so far as the number of pages is concerned, is as great as that given to arithmetic, the time necessary to do the geometry will not be so great.
