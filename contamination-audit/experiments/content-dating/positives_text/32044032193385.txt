THE SECOND STATE YOUTH CONFERENCE I. PLANNING THE CONFERENCE 

During its early meetings in the fall of 1958, the Oregon Youth Council discussed a wide range of subject areas for a state conference among themselves, with youth from the organizations each represented, with parents, teachers and school administrators. 

Based on these discussions, the conclusion was reached that the area of counseling in the high schools should be the focus for a state-wide youth conference. The Youth Council wanted to provid an opportunity for youth to express ideas and attitudes on what they wanted from the counseling program in their high schools. 

To center a state-wide conference around the subject of counsel was an ambitious task. Early in its planning, the Youth Council made it clear that the purpose of the conference was not to crit cize counseling programs in the high schools. The statement of purpose as developed by the Youth Council, which was distributed to all of the delegates at the conference, is as follows: "We are trying to give counselors a students-eye view of what we would like to receive in the line of counseling. 

We are not trying to say who the counselors will be-or of what sex, for we recognize these generally have little to do with counselors' effectiveness. We want to help our counselors help us."

The next step was developing areas for discussion, questionnaire on each of the areas, detailed plans for the conference, and inviting each high school student body to name two delegates to attend.
