THE RULES OF THE ROAD 

THE READER will, I hope, understand at the outset just what this booklet purports to be. It is a road map for club programs. It does not even try to tell you where to go or whether the contemplated journey is the one which your club should make. And it furnishes neither gas, oil, nor a smooth-running machine for the trip.

The points covered are those in which we believe women's clubs are especially interested. But no attempt has been made to include every destination women turn eager, expectant eyes. upon which

Nor do the following pages indicate all the roads by which any place may be reached. To attempt to do so would defeat the very purpose of the booklet. For 

The Program Road Map has been prepared primarily for the inexperienced traveler, who desires to reach a famous place but has no idea how to get there. Multiplicity of directions would only confuse and discourage her. I am aware, however, that the more experienced could suggest many other places and other routes. 

In using our map may I offer travelers a few road directions. 

Before starting, fix definitely the point of destination. A club often decides to study "World Peace." That subject represents an undiscovered continent in quest of which scholars have spent their lives. A women's club can hardly expect in a dozen afternoon meetings to solve problems which have long baffled scientists and statesmen. But there are many related subjects any one of which may be, in the time allotted, profitably approached. Among these are the political or economic conditions under which other nations live, the causes of the last great war, the United States policy of neutrality, or what individuals or women's clubs can do to aid in bringing world peace. Define clearly the journey in order that in due time you may arrive.

Do not be tempted into too many interesting bypaths. The world is filled with strange and fascinating things, to study, to discuss, to learn. No one can encompass all of them. If the club is to reach its chosen destination on time, it must follow a fairly straight line and leave other points of interest for another season. 

It is suggested that you consult frequently the explanatory paragraphs which follow. The booklet is divided into three parts:

I. Services offered club women by state institutions; the kinds of material and services available, and the conditions under which these may be obtained. This information has been compiled from replies to a questionnaire distributed by us. 

States are listed alphabetically. Such agencies offer a most effective service for club women and should be used freely.

II. A guide to sources of inexpensive material classified by subjects. Subjects have been arranged alphabetically. No attempt has been made to list the sources of assistance in the order of their importance or of the value of their material, except that we have placed first under each subject a source having a variety of pertinent material.

III. Addresses of organizations and publishers to which reference has been made in the second section.

For the reader's convenience, the following symbols have been used in the second section: * indicates that the organization has upon the subject a substantial amount of material in the form of booklets, pamphlets, reprints, and other inexpensive printed matter; +indicates that a free price list is available upon request; indicates that a certain amount of free material is available. 

Each source listed has material upon the subject indicated. 

Something, however, is left to the imagination and powers of deduction of the reader. If, for instance, a peace society is listed under the general topic Agriculture, the reader must be able to surmise that the material, while related to the farm or the farmer, will just as surely be related to peace. Because their number is legion and their expense a factor to the club member, we have but rarely included books. 

For these the reader will do well to consult the local or state library. 

In writing to any of the supply sources, may we suggest that you be very specific in your requests. Do not, as sometimes happens, say "Send us a program on Economics." Unless you know what you want, a good preliminary step is to ask for free price list and free material, perhaps inclosing a modest check for sample literature. Or you may state the general subject of your interest, the number of meetings to be devoted to it, the approximate average attendance, the caliber of mentality and experience (that is, college graduates, business women, or homemakers), and whether your club is rural or urban. 

In preparing this booklet, no attempt has been made to indicate any opinion concerning the validity or authenticity of the material distributed by the agencies listed. Nor do we recommend or assume responsibility for any particular booklet or its content. Great care has, however, been used in the compilation of information; and in addition to the examination of much material, our own listing has been checked against similar authoritative listings. 

A final word of road direction - the club itself must furnish the power of locomotion and its leaders must control the steering wheel. Road maps save time and effort. They indicate passable roads which lead in the right direction. There is, however, nothing mystical about them. Without club effort they are useless. 

The club itself must, under its own power, make the journey. 

Our parting wish is not, as in olden times, "Safe journey"— but a daring and profitable adventure for those club women who use The Program Road Map.
