DURING THE WAR YEARS 

San Diegans experienced, for the first time, the problems that arise when individuals or families or communities attempt to live together under crowded conditions Many of the problems with which your municipal government has been faced in recent months have had their origin in the city's spectacular growth in population Streets have been congested Our facilities for the collection of garbage and rubbish have been overtaxed Our sewerage system has proved to be inadequate More people have required fire, police, and health protection than ever before. There have been greater demands on our harbor department, our engineering staff, our libraries, our airports

Water consumption has risen to a point where it exceeds our reservoir supply

Superhighways have been built. Modern traffic controls and safety lighting have been installed New trucks and crews have been assigned to the collection of garbage and rubbish and the frequency of collections has been stepped up Sewer lines have been extended. More efficient methods of sewerage treatment have been employed

New fire and police equipment, a health clinic and an all-weather airport have been added to the city's fast growing list of municipal services The improvement that has benefited the greatest number of San Diegans this past year, however, is the Colorado River Aqueduct
