Littauer Suggestions for Using This Script 

This script is published as a Texas Education Agency bulletin because students and teachers may be expected to derive educational benefits from this type of presentation. 

Dr. Hubert B. Jones of the Grand Jury Association of Travis County, Texas, prepared the script. It may be used in junior and senior high schools in connection with units involving a study of city and county governments. The dramatization is designed to inform boys and girls of the purposes, procedures, and responsibilities of the Grand Jury. 

The Grand Jury is an effective means of protecting individual rights and for preserving peace. The Travis County Grand Jury Association has expressed the following hope for all persons using this script: "When the time may come for any of them to serve, they will do so willingly and with pride, knowing that they are sharing the responsibility of maintaining the dignity of their community. By informing boys and girls of the Grand Jury system, our schools are rendering a real service in good citizenship."

It is suggested that social studies teachers may make this presentation more valuable by defining some of the legal words before undertaking this dramatization as a class project.

To the Classroom Teacher: 

The Grand Jury in Action is intended to be enrichment material that might be used in the social studies program either at the junior or senior high school level. It is suggested that the teacher and a class committee study the text carefully before attempting to name the cast that will present it to the class. A citizen in the local community who has served on a grand jury would be an effective resource person to use in the preliminary study or at the time of the presentation.

Junior high school classes in several schools of Texas have used the material and reported favorably concerning its effectiveness in stimulating interest and conveying concrete information on services of a grand jury to a local community. 

The Travis County Grand Jury Association, Austin, Texas, would be glad to have any comments you might make concerning the success you have with this material. -vi-

THE GRAND JURY INTRODUCTION, BY DR. HUBERT B. JONES 

In the judiciary of the State of Texas the district court is that of original jurisdiction for all felony cases. A felony case is one in which the punishment to be assessed may be imprisonment in the penitentiary. 

To secure action in the district court against one who has committed a felonious act, this person must have been accused of crime by a legal body - a Grand Jury. 

A Grand Jury is a body of twelve reputable citizens of the county in which the crime was committed. Any citizen may appear before the 

Grand Jury and accuse a person of wrongdoing. If sufficient evidence can be produced to sustain such an accusation, the Grand Jury then returns an indictment (a charge of wrongdoing the commission of an illegal act) against the person accused. Ordinarily the charges against such wrongdoers are brought before the Grand Jury by the District Attorney the law enforcement officer charged with this duty. District courts have terms, provided by statute, when they shall be in session prepared to examine charges brought against individuals. The law also provides that before each session of the district court, a Grand Jury, appointed by the court, should have heard all of the reports from the District Attorney of the crimes committed in that county since the last term of that court. This is the body which returns the indictments that are the first steps in the institution of criminal proceedings.

The Presiding Judge of a district court appoints a Grand Jury Commission of from three to five men who choose sixteen men of that county to serve as grand jurors. From this list of sixteen men the district court, acting through its chief officer, the district judge, chooses twelve men to constitute a Grand Jury for that term of the court.

We are about to enact a scene that presents a Grand Jury in session. 

The district judge organizes and charges the Grand Jury. This body then, in regular session, examines the witnesses whose testimony supports a charge brought by the district attorney against one Elmer Upton, who is charged with taking an automobile without the owner's permission.

This presentation, including all of the names used, is fictitious, but is representative of any real case of similar nature that the Grand Jury handles. -1-

"You solemnly swear that you will diligently inquire into, and true presentment make, of all such matters and things as shall be given you in charge; the State's counsel, your fellows' and your own, you shall keep secret, unless required to disclose the same in the course of a judicial proceeding in which the truth or falsity of evidence given in the grand jury room, in a criminal case, shall be under investigation. You shall present no person from envy, hatred, or malice; neither shall you leave any person unpresented for love, fear, favor, affection or hope of reward; but you shall present things truly as they come to your knowledge, according to the best of your understanding, so help you God.''

Gentlemen of the Grand Jury: 

The office of grand juror is of ancient origin and is one of high honor and respect among our people. It is considered by free men as the bulwark of liberty, because no dictator can flourish in a land where a man can be placed on trial for his life only upon an indictment by a grand jury of twelve of his neighbors. 

So, for the time being, you will represent the conscience of this community. If our laws have been violated by the commission of a felony, no officer of your government can call on anyone to answer for it, unless such violation shocks your conscience upon the oath you have just taken. The standard of law enforcement for the term of this court cannot be higher than the standard set by you. 

You and you alone have the right, power and duty to say what person shall be true-billed, and what person no-billed. You may investigate anyone without reservation and without hindrance. But, I charge you to remember that an indictment must reflect the true judgment of the Grand Jury upon your oaths. Do not permit anyone else who comes before your body, either private citizen or public officer, to exercise that discretion for you. -3-

Your success as grand jurors will not be measured by the number of indictments you return, but by the wisdom of those which you do return. In the discharge of this duty, the members of this community rely entirely upon your sound judgment, your courage and your sense of justice and fair dealing.

The punishing of such persons who have violated the law is the responsibility of the Courts, but before the Courts can try any person for such a violation an indictment (a charge of wrongdoing) must have been returned by a Grand Jury. The indictment of the violator of the law can only be made by a group of citizens selected and organized as we have been, and only then after a full investigation of all of the facts and circumstances surrounding such violation has been made. State of Texas vs. Elmer Upton is as follows: 

District Court. On the

The case was set, in regular order with the other indictments returned, on the criminal docket of the date of the trial, Elmer Upton and his attorney appeared before Judge Hanover, the presiding Judge of the court, and plead guilty" to the charges returned by the Grand Jury against him and placed himself on the mercy of the court."

This action of Elmer Upton was doubtless done on the advice of his -22-

attorney. This was a sensible thing to do for all of the evidence, as shown by the examination of the witnesses by the Grand Jury, was all against Elmer Upton, and his chances of winning an acquittal were practically nil, 66 Judge Hanover, following the mandate (or direction) of the law in accepting Elmer Upton's plea of guilty," assessed against him a sentence of two years of confinement in the penitentiary. However, the assessment of such punishment is subject to another law which gives the judge who has to mete out such punishment the authority to give the guilty party a suspended sentence. This was done by Judge Hanover.

In pronouncing the sentence it was carefully explained by the court that a suspended sentence--while relieving the guilty party from the obligation of serving the time assessed against him now--if he committed and was convicted for another felony within the time of the punishment given him in this case, that these two years would be added automatically to the punishment given him in the second felony case. But if Elmer 

Upton lives properly and within the law during these two years, at the end of that time the Court which convicted him will give him a clear bill of conduct" and he will be privileged to exercise all the rights of a free citizen. 66 Elmer Upton, from that day on, has lived an upright, honorable life and is making of himself a good business man and a worthwhile citizen. -24-
