ELEGY FOR A HOUSE

THAT blackened stub was once a purple beech 

And that grey scrag a mighty maple tree,—

I close my eyes and see a locust reach Out of that empty space a tracery Of whispering leaves held close against the sky. 

I smell the blossoms where no blossoms are, I hear the droning bees where no bees fly 

And dream of days more distant than a star.

Here stood a home,-a thing of oak and pine Rich with a memory of a of a hundred years.

This was not built by hands, this spirit shrine 

Made holy ground by laughter touched with tears. 

Forever in my sight the mordant flame 

Will leap to leave its beauty but a name.

IJ I was not there to watch that beauty die 

And yet I saw each blackened timber fall.

I saw the windows glare and I stood by 

Helpless beneath the smoke that was its pall.

The bed in which my mother slept, the stairs That mounted creaking to an attic room, The gilded mirrors and the ancient chairs,— 

All, all, I saw them meet their dreadful doom.

It seemed almost as though a tragic spell Had fallen on each long-loved treasured shape Else it had risen ere the rafters fell 

And won its way to freedom and escape.

Now there is nothing but the gaping ground,— 

Yet no star stays upon its ordered round!

IIJ The axe that hewed the roof-tree and the sill Lived in the gashes of the virgin wood. 

The craft that shaped the frame was present still that looked and understood. 

To every eye

The rambling gables and the arm-thick vine, The sunken flagstones' worn uneven floor, The rocks where meadow rue and columbine 

Brought the cool woodland to the open door,—

All sang of gracious living that survived 

Out of a world of hearthstones and of hope,

A world of sure belief, a world contrived Of simple faith, too simple now to cope 

With all the tragic tumult of the hour? 

Or have we lost the vision and the power?

IV 

Even in my brief life I saw it all, I caught the pageant of a hundred years.

My eyes have seen the writing on the wall, My heart has known the grief of broken spears.

All of the ache that comes from destined death, All of the hope that hallows each new birth, All of the joy of lovers breath to breath,—

They have been mine beside this blackened earth. 

They have been mine, but they are more than mine, They are the heritage the human race Shares with creation, sacramental wine Lifted through time on life's high altar place. 

I know it cannot fall, time's course will run

When I and mine have found oblivion.

V What is this pageant of a hundred years 

That passed before my life to end in flame?

Nothing of history, no crowding cheers 

Wakened these walls before the terror came!

I saw a woman bent with time whose eyes

Were deep with wisdom and whose blue-veined hands Were sure at homely things, mincemeat and pies 

And quartered apples laid in fragrant bands 

Across the sunlit flagstones at the door.

I saw the red shawl on her shoulders laid I saw her mounting to the upper floor 

Holding her candle calm and unafraid,—

She did not come again nor ever waken And my young heart with its first grief was shaken.

VI My mother's mother lived beyond his doom Still sure of faith, still absolute in trust. 

She had met grief before an open tomb, But not the bitterness of dust to dust.

Serene and calm and gracious to the end Her world moved with her like an aureole. 

As surging seas upon a shore descend 

Time's mounting tumult broke before her soul. 

Around her island spirit all her days

This vanished house was like a fortress wall, Shielding from time her dear accustomed ways;

Peace and content and beauty over all:- 

City of refuge where the weary came 

To pause and rest and worship at her flame.

VIJ Clearer than any ghost there walks among These blackened stones the figure of a man. 

I scarcely knew him, but from every tongue That taught my growing mind, the story ran 

Of one who was a dreamer born before

His time, whose vision and whose searching heart 

Leaped out to life as through an open door,—

Living legend, a prophet set apart. 

And so life caught him in a tangled mesh 

Of broken faith that killed him ere he died, The triumph was the triumph of the flesh,—

He kept his honor and unsullied pride. 

He is a ghostly figure, and yet still I move within the circle of his will.

VIIJ 

My mother caught the banner from her hand 

And taught her sons the loyal word and true,

Born of old worlds she lived to understand The courage and high valor of the new. 

Her open mind and ranging spirit saw The worth and wonder of the changing scene 

But never once forgot the changeless law 

That keeps the heart secure, the soul serene.

Within these vanished walls she watched death come 

Stealing upon her like a rising tide, No sounding trumpet and no throbbing drum 

To keep aloft the banner of her pride. 

Yet none who saw her clear eyes ever guessed

The sign of death was dark within her breast.

IX Beyond this lawn a spreading apple tree Once held aloft a secret cedarn seat. 

My father and my mother said to me "We planned our lives within that cool retreat, 

And where this maple grew they pledged their faith

With that enduring trust that never died. 

Here moves no strange intangible dim wraith,— 

My father passing with his lovely bride!

To this warm hearthstone they returned to dwell 

Building their lives in wonder day by day 

Until the winter when the shadow fell 

And from these too dear walls he fled away. 

Age came, passion endures, though the heart grieves,—

He laid him down beside these vanished eaves.

Brother, my brother, how few hours ago

We waked to find our old world shining new. 

The past is present,—we must have it so. 

It was not even yesterday we grew Within these vanished walls that loom again When the eye closes or the dark returns,— Our spirit refuge from all grief and pain 

A haven with a beacon blaze that burns 

Brighter than any noonday summer sun. 

For us light glows from long remembered days,—

Will the light fail when our brief days are done 

And alien eyes watch alien cattle graze

Upon the weed-grown level of this lawn 

Where we have loved the sunset and the dawn?

XI 

Brother, my brother, it was yours to bring

Out of the West a stranger to this land 

With her own dreams borne on the beating wing 

Of her proud spirit. Ours to understand

The loyalty, the courage and the will That shaped her life to unaccustomed ways, The tireless search for beauty to fulfill

The long lost dreams of unforgotten days. 

This house was hers and was a part of her, She touched these ancient walls with tenderness, Beneath her hand they felt again the stir 

Of youth that was the boon of her caress.

Beauty she brought from fields her spirit ranged Changing, she left the changeless still unchanged.

XIJ The ancient floors beneath your children's feet 

Echoed with laughter and with sudden tears 

And life's sure round, so terrible, so sweet

Renewed the wonder of its vernal years. 

The same warm earth my grey-eyed mother knew,

The same cool stream that closed my body over, The same green lawn all shimmering with dew, The same wide fields of timothy and clover,— This their heritage, their unconscious pride 

Inevitable as the rising sun

Or the long mountain ridge's purple side Against the afterglow when day is done. 

This was their being, in their living veins One with their blood its glory still remains.

XIIJ Set in its circling forest lands and farms, At peace among the hills that time reveres, The old house held its surely sheltering arms 

Wide for their welcome for a score of years.

It stood until the ancient maple tree 

Once more looked down upon a star-eyed bride

Deep-freighted faith and holy revelry,- 

Bravest of earthly hopes and tear-wet pride. 

And we who watched in tenderness and mirth

Saw the linked pageant of the past reborn Unchanged as though the swiftly sweeping earth 

Halted forever at a single morn

Life halts not so-all dreams must change or die 

Knowing no fate but travail and birth cry.

XIV 

Scarcely the crackling flames had died away,

The last ghost curl of blue smoke disappeared, The last ash cooled and all the little play Of life resumed its sure routine,-desks cleared, Beds made, meals bought, clothes changed, accustomed things 

That falsely mark the passage from a grief 

That follows after on slow-beating wings 

Close as the wind upon the stricken leaf; 

When your first made music in fond ears, cry

Your petalled hands reached upward to enfold 

The universe. If joy seemed kin to tears

It was that you were never to behold Even the shadow of those walls that were 

A brave world's cradle and its sepulcher.

XV 



Are those dear walls, too dear for us to see

That we have shared what no walls may confine? 

And all we loved, a spirit moving free 

Of ancient hearthstones, and of bowered vine? 

Are we too deep in grief to understand 

That beauty has no mortal tenement, No sanctuary built on shifting sand 

No sunken tomb or ashen cerement?

O Anthony the faith is hard to hold 

For us who knew the blown smoke and the flame,

For us who saw the blackened earth enfold The glory that has shrunken to a name; 

Yet for your eyes we set this shining star:— 

Remember what we loved,-and what you are!
