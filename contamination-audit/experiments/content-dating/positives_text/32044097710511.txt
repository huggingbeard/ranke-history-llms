Introduction 

The general appropriations bill (S.B. 372), adopted by the 

Thirty-Seventh South Dakota Legislature and approved by the Governor, for the fiscal biennium ending June 30, 1963, contains $28,532,430 in line item appropriations for salaries of officers and employees of state government. This amount is exclusive of salary appropriations for departments and agencies not incorporated in the general bill, and is also exclusive of local and endowment funds allocated for salaries.

The salary appropriations for the fifty-six departments and agencies included were, for the current biennium, $23,130,651, and for the biennium which ended June 30, 1959, $22,119,656. It should be noted that several departments and agencies listed were not in the general appropriations bills in these earlier biennia, but the amount appropriated for salaries is shown if the department or agency has been included in the 1961 bill. Where the 1961 bill does not set out salaries as a separate line-item (e. g., Division of Forestry and Parks), the department or agency is excluded from a the tabulation.

This memorandum ignores all salary supplementations to the ~ general appropriations bills made in special appropriations bills.

FACTS ABOUT THE SOUTH DAKOTA LEGISLATIVE RESEARCH COUNCIL. 

The State Legislative Research Council is a permanent, joint committee composed of every member of the South Dakota Senate and House of Represent atives. During the interim between sessions, advance study and consideration is given to problems expected to confront the next Legislature. The Council conducts most of its work through an Executive Board, and through standing and special committees and their subcommittees. The Council staff serves every member of the Legislature in a fact-finding capacity and assists the Executive Board and the committees in developing a program for consideration at the next session.

In its work, the Executive Board: 1) acts as a clearing house for ideas on current legislative problems by receiving proposals for study from any member of the Legislature; 2) determines and directs, through its committees and staff, the study and research necessary for proper consideration of all proposals; 

3) disseminates advance information on these problems to legislators and to the general public by means of reports and newsletters and by discussion during and between Board and committee meetings; and 4) reports directly to the Legislature, in advance of the regular session, making recommendations in the form of bills or otherwise, and summarizes the material prepared for use of the Legislature in considering the Council program. 

Since 1951 the State Legislative Research Council has provided an open forum to which citizens and organizations have submitted their proposals for consideration. As a result of the Council activities, problems of South Dakota state and local government receive continuous attention and study by members of the Legislature. Research Service
