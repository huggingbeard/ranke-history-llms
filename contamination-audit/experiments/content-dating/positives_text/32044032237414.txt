INTRODUCTION 

Federal assistance to local redevelopment and urban renewal projects is conditioned upon, among other things, a feasible plan for relocating the site occupants who must be displaced. Section 105(c) of the Housing Act of 

1949, as amended, provides that contracts for financial assistance to projects under Title I must require that: "There be a feasible method for the temporary relocation of families displaced from the project area, and that there are or are being provided, in the project area or in other areas not generally less desirable in regard to public utilities and commercial facilities and at rents or prices within the financial means of the families displaced from the project area, decent, safe, and sanitary dwellings equal in number to the mumber of and available to such displaced families and reasonably accessible to their places of employment."

In administering this provision of the law, the Urban Renewal Administration requires the local public agency to assume the responsibility for: (a) demonstrating that existing and anticipated local housing resources meeting the aforesaid requirements are or will be adequate to meet relocation needs; (b) formulating an acceptable plan for orderly relocation; and (c) providing competent staff services, generally described as a family relocation service, to assist families in obtaining decent, safe, and sanitary housing. 

Assistance through the family relocation service of a local public agency is available to all families residing in project properties at the time of acquisition by the local public agency. As a preliminary step, the family relocation service interviews each family and evaluates its relocation needs in terms of composition, income, race, place of employment, and special problems and preferences.

-- - 2 - These factors are considered by the local public agency in helping families through referral service and other types of assistance to achieve rehousing in accommodations meeting approved local standards of "decent, safe and sanitary." Families eligible for Federally-aided low-rent housing are given help in preparing applications and are given preference for admission to these projects. Families interested in purchasing are given advice and guidance in securing homes and financing, including the use of the special mortgage insurance provisions (Sec. 221) of the National Housing Act enacted by the Congress to facilitate the rehousing of low-income displaced families. 

As encouraged by URA, many local public agencies establish Relocation Advisory Committees to broaden community awareness and understanding of relocation problems and to enlist community assistance for the solution of problems. Vacancies in the private housing supply are inspected to determine their acceptability as a rehousing resource to which families can be referred. 

In certain instances financial assistance is given to defray the expense of moving and the first month's rent. *Local housing authorities are required to extend the preference for admission to units in Federally-aided low-rent housing projects constructed under the Housing Act of 1949, as follows: "First, to families which are to be displaced by any low-rent housing project or by any public slum-clearance or redevelopment project initiated after January 1, 1947 or which were so displaced within three years prior to making application to such public housing agency for admission to any low-rent housing; and as among such families first preference shall be given to families of disabled veterans whose disability has been determined by the Veterans' Administration to be service-connected, and second preference shall be given to families of deceased veterans and servicemen whose death has been determined by the Veterans' Administration to be service-connected, and third preference shall be given to families of other veterans and servicemen..."

Under the Housing Act of 1954, the first preferences identified above are continued and broadened to include families displaced by projects and other governmental actions of a public body or court.

- 3 - Housing to which families move on their own initiative is inspected to determine whether or not it meets the local standards of "decent, safe and sanitary." An attempt is made to trace families who move without informing the local public agency of their new address so that their new accommodations can also be inspected to determine whether they meet local standards. All families moving on their own initiative to substandard dwellings are offered assistance in locating standard accommodations, including referral to acceptable vacancies.

URA policies permit local public agencies to use legal eviction action to accomplish the removal of families from a project area only as a last resort if any of the following situations exist: failure to pay rent, maintaining a nuisance, refusal without adequate reason to accept suitable accommodations referred by the local public agency, or persistent failure to cooperate with the local public agency in its efforts to provide relocation assistance.

In certain instances the local public agency may arrange for temporary rehousing, frequently on the project site, while suitable accommodations are being sought. For example, dwellings in the project area may be unsafe for continued use, or demolition schedules may require that structures be vacated before suitable rehousing is available to the occupants. Minimum standards for temporary rehousing accommodations require that they be generally not less desirable in character than the dwelling vacated, and that they be in a safe and habitable condition. The local public agency continues to be responsible for assisting temporarily relocated families to be rehoused permanently in dwellings that meet local standards.

· 4 · Although the relocation requirements of Title I are confined to the relocation of families, local public agencies are encouraged to extend their relocation services to single persons and to business establishments. 

Individuals maintaining separate households are eligible for the same type of financial assistance as site families. 

Under the terms of the Federal grant contract, the local public agency is responsible for keeping records on the relocation of site families and for reporting monthly to the Urban Renewal Administration on relocation progress. 

Monthly reporting was first standardized in December 1952 with the issuance of Form H-666, Report on Relocation Progress. In February 1955 the report was expanded to provide information on such additional items as the inspection and certification of rehousing accommodations and the tracing of families who move voluntarily and without the knowledge of the local public agency. 

As of September 30, 1955 the local public agencies conducting 80 Federallyaided urban renewal projects in 53 cities in the United States, Hawaii, and Puerto Rico had started relocating families from project lands already acquired. 

This report summarizes the information on Form H-666 submitted for the 80 projects as of September 1955. 

All data refer only to families living in properties to which the local public agencies had obtained title as of September 30.

FINANCIAL ASSISTANCE: ALL PROJECTS 

Local public agencies may furnish financial assistance to needy families and individual householders being displaced when such assistance facilitates achievement of the relocation and site clearance objectives of the project. 

Assistance is limited to a reasonable amount for moving expenses or a reasonable amount for the first month's rent or both. 

Financial assistance may be given only to families and to individuals maintaining independent households. Families temporarily relocated within the project site may receive additional assistance in connection with relocation outside the site. A family moved off the site, however, may not receive further financial assistance, even though the accommodations are classified as temporary and a further move is indicated.
