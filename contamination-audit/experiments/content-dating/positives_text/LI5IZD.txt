PAST AND PRESENT . . . The Story Told in Pictures 

We present herewith a graphic report on the progress of Richmond, and how it grew in a few short years from a small town of 23,000 to a metropolis of over 100,000 population. 

The Chamber of Commerce began compiling a record of airplane pictures back in 1928, and continued at intervals up to the outbreak of the war at which time flights were forbidden.... The moment restrictions were lifted the cameras were again sent aloft to capture the pictures of PROGRESS. Everyone knew there had been great changes in Richmond. We could see that from the ground. But it remained for the lens to reveal the full import of what had happened. ... We have gone back into the files for the earliest airplane pictures. We have matched these as closely as possible with the new pictures, all of which have been taken within the last six weeks. The contrast tells the story. Richmond grew up between 1940 and 1945, and the Chamber of Commerce uses this method to tell the world.

GROWTH OF INDUSTRY 

REPORT FOR 1945 By EDGAR W. DALE, President 

The program of national advertising and promotion to bring new industries to Richmond progressed satisfactorily during 1945. Under the Industrial Committee, Chairman Dr. C. E. Rutledge, with Pere Woodcock as industrial director, we are using $30,000 this fiscal year in advertising in national publications. Mr. Woodcock made a trip to eastern cities this year. Our files show a growing number of prospects. The Richmond area actually secured seven new industries in 1945. 

WORK PILE COMMITTEE The Work Pile Committee, Chair man Francis A. Watson, engaged Dr. Chilton A. Bush for a survey of the expected postwar home building volume and the anticipated additions and renovations, and the amount of buying of household appliances. Dr. Bush reported that $20,000,- 

000 worth of home building is a conservative estimate. Purchase of household appliances will run to $5,000,000 in accordance with the figures from this survey. Improvements and additions to homes are figured at $4,274,137. (Copies of this report are available upon request.)

NEW AIRPORT FOR RICHMOND 

The Chamber of Commerce is working closely with the City on the matter of an airport for Richmond. This project is in the early stages. Details will be made public as they develop. 

INDUSTRIAL RECONVERSION Many of our industries com pleted their reconversion work this year and are under way with civilian production. A strong demand for labor continues, and employment is at a satisfactory level. Richmond suffered no important economic shock as a result of the ending of the war. This was due in large part to the fact that the shipyards had been reducing their working personnel on a gradual basis ever since 1943. 

NEW ROAD PROJECTS The Chamber of Commerce support ed, and will continue to assist, the project of the City of Richmond for inclusion of the "missing link" of route 69 in the State Highway system and the new construction necessary to make the connection with the Richmond-San Rafael Ferry over a new route without passing through the Standard Oil Plant. There is also a movement for a new low-level, modern road to Point San Pablo to which we give our support. The plans for the 

Freeway through the eastern part of the City are being given careful consideration. 

PUBLIC HOUSING REMOVAL – It is hoped some definite action will be forthcoming in 1946 for removal of at least part of the public housing which now occupies vast areas of land in our city. We advocate the development of a proper city plan which will open these acres to industry and privately built residential projects in accordance with the best long-range interests of Richmond. Removal of the public housing will continue to be the Number One item on the Chamber agenda.

RICHMOND NEEDS NEW BUSINESS 

The Industrial Depart ment compiled a report showing that a great number of new businesses could be expected to operate profitably here. These are of many kinds. In particular, the lack of warehousing and wholesaling facilities in Richmond was stressed. (Copies of this report are available upon request.)

MEMBERSHIP SHOWS GAIN - We are glad to report that 108 

new members joined the Chamber during the year. This is a satisfactory gain and shows that our organization continues to extend its influence in the community. 

RICHMOND TAKES FORWARD STEPS Passage of the bonds for the new Civic Center was probably the most important step taken by Richmond in many years. It means we are to have a new set of modern buildings in keeping with the city's size and prestige. Modernization of the business district has been undertaken and will be carried forward in a big way during the coming year. Our congratulations to the citizens of Richmond for their constructive policy.

TRANSPORTATON IMPROVEMENTS 

mitments for two new important bus lines during the year. One starts at the bus terminal in the downtown section, and covers Tenth, Thirteenth and Seventeenth Streets and Broadway, terminating at the pottery works of the American Radiator and Standard Sanitary Company. The other starts on Fairmont Avenue and traverses Panhandle, Pullman, Cutting, Forty-seventh and Tenth Streets, ending at the bus terminal. The Chamber of 

Commerce gives continuing attention to the transportation question, both local and inter-city, and expects to report further progress and improvements in 1946. 

CHAMBER AIDS MERCHANTS The Merchants Division has had a busy and constructive year. The Chamber has supplied them with many important services, including publication of a monthly bulletin devoted to their interests. 

PORT GETS "WAR DISCHARGE" The Port of Richmond was released from war service and again becomes available for commercial shipping. The Parr-Richmond Terminal Corporation inaugurated a campaign of advertising and promotion to increase tonnage.

PROGRAM FOR COMING YEAR The Chamber of Commerce during 1946 will continue to function in the interests of the community, not merely for its own members, and will carry on a constructive program of leadership designed to serve all groups. 

Commendation is due to the committee members for their diligent service and to the directors for their high record of attendance at meetings and careful consideration of the problems of the community and business of the Chamber. May our organization and community go forward to bigger and better things in the new year.
