SUGGESTIONS ON OVERCOMING 

In meeting together for meditation, let us first give ourselves fully and wholly to God that He may redeem the negative tendencies which usurp our strength and narrow our mind's vision, leaving little space for the soul's growth. 

With this giving of ourselves, let us definitely and purposefully take our days more quietly, with less strain, less pressure simply doing what is before us, not wasting valuable time arguing with ourselves, unwilling to be guided by the Indwelling Intelligence. As we live more quietly, we shall become more conscious of our false tendencies and deal more directly with them. 21

When antagonistic feelings causing condemnation or even irritation or impatience arise within us, take them at once into the Light of Love and thank God that He has made us aware of what needs to be released. Or when some special fear returns to the mind, hold it out in the radiant Light of His Love, there to be melted and redeemed and be thankful that the fear, the adversary, is turning us to Him. Thus when a troublesome condition looms before us, give it wholly and completely to God and in the Name of the Father, in the Name of the Son, in the Name of the Holy Spirit, leave this condition in His Holy 

Love. 

Then must we be at ease, not looking for results. We must be normal, balanced, joyous, not evading our work, not withdrawing from one another.

Habits of a lifetime cannot be erased by a few affirmations and denials. Therefore be not troubled if a negative trait, a racial inheritance returns, only be grateful that we may leave it in the Redeeming Light of Love. For we are ever in the Father, in His Love; ever in the Son, in His Resurrected Life; ever in the Holy Spirit, the Breath of the Almighty. 22

MANIFESTING GOD 

No man hath seen God at any time! If we love one another, God dwelleth in us, and his love is perfected in us. *The profound, the esoteric, conception of the object of human life is unfolded to us in this utterance of St. John. It is that 

God, the Universal Originating Spirit, is seeking to realize Himself, to express Himself, to manifest Himself through us. God needs man for purposes of self-realization and self-manifestation. There is a sense in which humanity is as necessary to God, as 

God is to humanity. God is pure Spirit; pure Spirit can only realize itself. . . through the individual, and can only be known in its manifestations.

I repeat the axiom which I have made familiar to you. God, the Immanent Spirit, "sleeps in the stone, dreams in the plant, awakens in the animal, and is self-conscious 

* Ven. Basil Wilberforce, D. D. 

43

in man;" to which I add, "and is perfectly manifested in the Lord Jesus Christ." But we are responsible for manifesting what 

He is, what God is in Himself. What is God in Himself? Again and again is it repeated by the Apostle of Love, God is love. It is love that God seeks to realize, to manifest, to express, through us.

Why do even good and religious people so rarely manifest God thus? Because we do not wholly believe that God is Love; because it is not yet a conviction wrought into the verities of our being that God, the Universal Substance, the causal basis of all that is, is Love. 

And yet if we did believe it, we should be in possession of the one golden clue that can save us from losing our way in the tangle of puzzles and paradoxes around us; the one truth which can irradiate with 

Divine assurance the darkest problems of life; the one contemplation which would ennoble our dealings with our fellow men; 44

which defines clearly our relation to the Eternal, encouraging us to endure patiently, hopefully, because measureless love united to measureless power holds and watches over our ultimate destiny, and assures us that whatever our trouble, whatever our sorrow, Love is in everything that happens to us.

Now before we can manifest God as Love, this conviction must be won in the silence of our own soul. There will be times when the insoluble riddle of this painful earth, and the infinite confusion that perplexes human life, seem utterly to contradict the affirmation that "Love is the root of creation, God's essence." 99 

I am sometimes asked does this conviction enable you intellectually to reconcile the anguish of the world with the goodness of God? I answer, I do not find that necessary. When you are beginning to know God, . . . you will believe in a God who is Immanent as well as Transcendent, 45
