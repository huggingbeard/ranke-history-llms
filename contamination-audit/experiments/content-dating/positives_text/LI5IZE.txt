Shipyards Now Ready for

Private Industrial Use. 

Outstanding among the industrial developments of 1947 was the transfer from the War Assets Administration of yards 1, 2 and 4 and the prefabrication plant to the Parr Richmond Industrial Corporation and to the Santa Fe railroad.

The Parr interests took over Yard 1 and the "prefab" plant, and the others went to the Santa Fe. Both began at once making plans for the intensive industrial development of the properties for use by private operators. The transfer of the industrial lands, buildings and equipment, and their immediate availability for private production, took Richmond a long step ahead as one of the outstanding manufacturing centers of the Pacific coast. An important fact in this connection is that buildings with a total of nearly two million square feet of space are already erected, and can be used by industries, many of them with only minor alterations and adjustments. 

Industries were standing in line for these shipyard locations during the two years that the Chamber of Commerce, City Council and labor unions pooled their efforts to get the properties released for private use.

Mr. Fred D. Parr, before the main transactions were consummated, had already placed the International 

Harvester Company in Warehouse A from which it will serve the western states. Other industries now placed in the shipyard lands are the Smith Enton Bag Company; R. J. Prentiss Company, importers, exporters and processors of drugs and insecticides; Beer Distributors, Inc.; Fortier Transportation Company; Montgomery Ward Company, processing and shipment of rugs and carpets; Protective Coatings, Inc.; Pacific 

Paint and Varnish Company; Thermet Fire Extinguisher Company, and M. J. Ryan Co., steel fabricators. 

The Parr Richmond Industrial Corporation placed a staff of men at Terminal No. 3, headed by T. A. 

Goodrick, to make a detailed study of the properties acquired by this company, so that a long range, basic plan could be drawn up for the placing of utilities and for the whole industrial arrangement involving present and future buildings, so that Richmond can expect to achieve the ultimate in employed personnel when the program is in full operation. Standard Oil Keeps on Building 

As Richmond grows and prospers, Standard of California's refinery keeps pace.

Improvements now under way or being completed this year by Standard as part of a program of general expansion amount to several million. dollars. 

Completion of the "Long Wharf" modernization work is proceeding rapidly. The new truck causeway with its two wide lanes has been finished, the office building, and a machine shop are now in use; the warehouse is yet to be completed. A concrete "barge strip" along the inner face of the loading pier is in use.

Modernized Long Wharf with new and wider truck lanes to provide more adequate loading facilities for waterborne shipments. The main laboratory building of the California Research Corporation, which was finished in 1947.

When completed, the modernized Long Wharf will provide berthing space for five tankers at one time and will rank as one of the best such facilities in the country. 

In the refinery itself there is being completed another unit which, when in operation, is expected to boost the refinery's output considerably. Known as "No. 3 Battery Crude Unit," this new addition comprises two huge, natural-gas-fired furnaces each with its own exhaust stack. There is a large atmospheric pressure column, and a vacuum tower, plus all the other equipment necessary to the unit's operation. 

In construction now is a new grease plant and packaging warehouse to replace present outmoded facilities. 

This plant, covering, roughly, two square blocks, will centralize the manufacture, packaging and shipping of a wide variety of package products, ranging from chassis greases and lubricating oils to fly sprays and cosmetic white oils.

One highly important project recently completed is the new Industrial Relations Building. All industrial relations activities at the refinery, including employment, medical service, employee relations, and safety, are centered in this building.

City Gained 25 New Industries in 1947 

Aside from the shipyard properties, the most important industrial development now going forward in Richmond is at the Parr Industrial Tract, in the North Richmond manufacturing zone north of Road 20 and running parallel with the Giant Highway and the Santa Fe and Southern Pacific railroad tracks. This is owned and operated by the Parr-Richmond Industrial Corporation, and Mr. Fred D. Parr, the President, has said it will ultimately provide employment for approximately 10,000 Richmond men and women. It consists of nearly 500 acres of land, well served by rail lines and streets and highways. A new street plan which will put this tract in a still more favorable position is now being worked out with the aid of City and 

County officials.

Now operating on this property are the Niagara Sprayer and Chemical Company, Giant Mill and Lumber Company, and the Atlas Foundry and Manufacturing Co. Ground has been broken for the new plant of the Eastman Tag and Label Company. The Rheem Manufacturing Company has purchased 56 acres for a new plant. Sites have been acquired by the I. F. Schnier Company and the Pacific Coast Aggregates. In all sections of the City, Richmond gained 25 new industries in 1947.

REPORT FOR 1947 By EDGAR W. DALE, President Richmond Chamber of Commerce 

The year 1947 was notable in the growth and development of Richmond as can be seen from this eight-page booklet, which is prepared in such a manner as to give you the outstanding facts at a glance. The Chamber of Commerce in recent years has followed the policy of making the annual report largely pictorial, and response from the membership, as well as from individuals, organizations, and libraries throughout the United States leads us to believe that this type of report is favorably received.

Airport Definite steps have now been taken to provide 

Richmond with an airport. Mr. Robert H. Miller, Jr., chairman of the Aviation Committee of the Chamber, and his committee members, give the following progress report: A site has tentatively been selected adjacent to the plant of the California Cap Works. It consists of 30 acres that properly developed, would give Richmond a Class 1 airport with 2,000-foot runways. Application has been made to the C.A.A. to be included in the Federal budget for 1948. The city of Richmond is taking steps to acquire the land from the various owners. 

The airport is to be included in the Richmond Master Plan. 

It will be the best located airport in the Bay region, only five minutes from Richmond and closer to downtown Oakland and 

San Francisco than any existing field.

Industries---The Industrial Department, directed by Mr. 

Pere Woodcock, was extremely active and aided in securing a total of 25 new industries in 1947. Re-activation of the shipyard property was the largest single achievement. Combined efforts of the Chamber of Commerce, the City Council, and the Labor Unions were exerted before the War Assets Administration turned over the great shipyard properties to private operators for use by private industries. We hereby express thanks to all who assisted in this vital matter. 

Membership The Chamber of Commerce gained 81 new members during the year. Plans are now under way by Mr.

Del Thompson, Chairman of the Membership Committee, and Dr. C. E. Rutledge, Chairman of the Industrial Promotion 

Committee, and their committees, for a large scale membership campaign which will provide the Chamber of Commerce with funds to operate on a much larger scale than ever before. 

Richmond Independent - The largest commercial business transaction to take place in 1947 was the sale of the Independent Publishing Company by the Galvin interests to Mr. 

Leo Owens of Harlingen, Texas. We welcome Mr. Owens to the business life of our community. We appreciate his recog. nition of the worth of one of Richmond's young business executives in the appointment of Warren Brown, Jr., as associate publisher. We note with a great deal of satisfaction that Mr. 

John F. Galvin, is to remain in Richmond with offices in the 

Galvin Building on Macdonald Avenue, and that Mr. John

Galvin, will continue in the active management of Richmond's own Radio Station, K.R.C.C. We feel that a great part of our present day prosperity is due to Mr. Galvin's sound editorial policies and wise counsel throughout the years. 

Merchants Division-As a result of a co-operative plan between the Merchants Division and the Richmond Independent, a Shop-in-Richmond campaign has been carried on since last April, and is producing such satisfactory results that it seems likely to continue. Mr. Victor Paolini is chairman of the special committee directing the campaign. The resignation of 

Mr. J. P. Dunning as chairman of the Merchants Division was accepted with regrets. Mr. Dunning found it necessary to dispose of his business owing to ill health. Under his direction the Merchants Division has been most active and we believe has been responsible for helping to solve many of the post war problems of our local merchants. Mr. Robert G. Reid, prominent druggist, was named chairman to replace Mr. Dunning, and was also made a director of the Chamber of Commerce to fill the unexpired term of Mr. T. A. Bedford, resigned. 

SEW AGE DISPOSAL-In compliance with the directive received from the State Department of Public Health that all communities facing on San Francisco Bay must cease from discharging raw sewage into the Bay waters, Richmond and the contiguous area are studying the matter of sewage disposal, and are expected to adopt a definite plan in the near future. The Chamber of Commerce has retained the services of a qualified engineer to make preliminary study and recommendations covering particularly the question of disposal of industrial wastes. Upon the completion of this study, the report will be submitted to the Board of Directors of the Chamber of Commerce and to the Richmond City Council for their consideration.

Traffic--The Traffic Committee, under the chairmanship of Mr. Malcolm Meyer, held several meetings to protect the interests of Richmond in rate matters which came up during the year. This committee is made up of traffic experts, and they are vigilant in all transportation matters. 

Membership Meetings Two open dinner meetings for members were held during the year. The first meeting was held primarily to acquaint the members with the operations of the Chamber of Commerce and to outline the many services available. The second meeting was called to hear a progress report on Richmond's Master Plan. It is hoped that this practice of open meetings will be continued in 1948 as we feel that an occasional get-together by the entire membership is beneficial not only to the Chamber of Commerce itself but to the community at large.

Cheaper Industrial Water-We regard this as the No. 1 

item on the community agenda. To insure our enviable position from an industrial standpoint, it is absolutely essential that water be obtained at lower rates, from some source, than those now being charged. This matter originated in the Chamber of Commerce over two years ago and is still receiving the full attention of the Industrial Water Committee, and we feel certain that a report showing definite results can be made public in the near future.

Open Golf Tournament 

The Third Richmond $10,- 000 Open Golf Tournament was held at the Richmond Golf Club under the sponsorship of the Richmond Chamber of Commerce. This annual event tends to bring the attention of millions of people throughout the world to Richmond. Clippings covering the two previous Tournaments have been received from as far away as New Zealand, Australia, England, and South and Central America. Chairman Francis A. Watson and Professional Pat Markovich and their entire committee deserve the thanks of this community for providing us with this fine entertainment and publicity media. 

School Bonds-With the school bond election scheduled for February 10, and as one of the sponsoring organizations of the campaign, we desire to call your attention to the abso lute necessity of our voting approval of this bond issue. With several hundred more children in our schools today than we had total population in 1939, the need for additional facilities is most obvious. We must do away with the double shift system that has been necessary for the past several years. The quality of our educational system is in danger. Good educators can do little unless we can give them the proper tools. By all means vote "Yes" yourself and urge your friends to do likewise on 

February 10.
