FOREWORD 

We rise early and work late day in and day out- year in and year out- - - always in a hurry, denying ourselves pleasures we might have in order to save for the future and yet, an unwise decision taking only a second's time can disrupt a life-time's plan. 

It has often occurred to me what a terrible calamity it would be should I suddenly be taken by death and my little son who naturally means more to me than even life itself be unable to benefit by his father's experiences in business.

It is the natural desire of every father that his sons benefit by his experiences. Many fathers do not live to guide their children in financial matters, and therefore the children must go through life paying dearly for experiences which could have been avoided, had their fathers lived to give them the benefit of their experiences.

Should I pass on to the great beyond the minute after this manuscript is finished, my son would have the benefit of his father's experiences. And in later years, before investing one dollar, he could carefully study this manuscript, and would undoubtedly thereby be saved endless worry and financial loss.

Therefore, this is written for the benefit of my son, and for the sons of all fathers who do not put in writing their experiences for the benefit of their children.

The experiences in the following few pages cover nearly twenty years and millions of dollars. 

In reading the following few pages, under no conditions lose sight of the fact that my basic reason for writing about financial problems is that I believe the dollar, outside of your Mother, is the best friend you have in the world.

Back Kicks

There is no deviation from the above statement in any way, shape or manner. 

The first dollar you make in business until the last one before death will be tried to be taken from you by fair means or otherwise. You will be offered get rich quick propositions, opportunities to invest in every conceivable proposition.

Literally thousands of people will be doing everything humanly possible to separate you from your money.

The law of averages proves conclusively not one out of a thousand ever turn out to be worth a single red cent. 

Without fear of contradiction, I will say that nine hundred and ninety-nine men out of a thousand past fifty years of age look back figuring what they could have done with the money had they put it in a bank rather than in worthless investments. 

As stated above, your dollar is often your best friend. Read the following pages carefully and realize only a fraction of the many ways to have your money taken away from you, and then profit by the experiences of one who has had them.

After you leave school and enter the business world your future success will rest entirely upon yourself. Your future happiness, standing in the community, prosperity, and I might add, even your health, will depend entirely upon the decisions you make. 

It has been said many times, "listen to everybody's advice, but act on your own judgment." This simply means to treat everybody who offers advice courteously. Weigh their advice from every standpoint, think it over, consider it, but when it comes

Back Kicks to making a decision - · no matter what the consequences may be, act on your own judgment. 

In acting on your own judgment, you will make your decisions blindly, just as I and millions of others have done who have had no knowledge upon which to base their judgment. And in the great majority of such cases everything has been lost, not only money, but peace of mind and even health. 

How well I recall 1913. Business was at a low ebb. Wheat, corn and oats were going lower right along. Industrial plants were laying off men every day. One particular instance, I remember vividly. We advertised for an employee. The next morning about eight o'clock when I stepped from the elevator, there was a line of men from the elevator running all the way down the hall. There must have been a couple of hundred. To my astonishment the line led right to our office door. They all wanted a job. There were salesmen, bricklayers, carpenters, plumbers, etc. all wanted work, any kind at almost any salary. 

The Worlds' War. Condi- 

Then came 1914 tions changed overnight. The demand for labor exceeded the supply. Commodities advanced rapidly flour from a few dollars a barrel to more than fifteen dollars a barrel real estate, ordinarily worth five thousand dollars to eight, ten, twelve and fifteen thousand dollars. Fairly good used automobiles worth today a couple of hundred dollars brought easily a thousand or more. It was a question of finding the products, and not the products finding the buyer.

Then came the Armistice in 1919, and with it a period of unloading surplus stock at declining prices. 

The late Mr. Armour's fortune, for instance, was nearly entirely wiped out. In his own words, he stated

Back Kicks he lost a million dollars a day for more than a hundred and forty straight days.

This period lasted until about 1922. Immediately following it prosperity gradually returned and continued at a terrific pace until the great market smash of October and November 1929. 

During all these years, I was actively in business, buying and selling, importing and exporting. 

Dealing not only in grain, cotton, sugar and in fact practically all food products and raw products, but also in stocks, bonds, etc. Price changes were fluctuating to the extremes.

This is being written when I am thirty-five years of age. Before I was twenty-one, I had made a fortune of a quarter of a million dollars. Before thirty,

I had made a quarter of a million several times and a million dollars once. During those years, our business ran into the millions of dollars per year. 

For these reasons, I cannot help but feel that my advice and opinions on matters that will come up in your life after leaving school will assist you in forming fundamentally correct decisions on these matters. 

By giving a few minutes thought as to whether or not you should buy a mortgage, buy stocks on a margin, play the grain market, invest money in mines, oil wells, or other ventures, you may profit by my actual experiences and probably not only save hard earned money, but days, weeks, months and years of worry and regret.

Before You Play The Stock Market 

It is my opinion more suicides, more broken up homes, and more heartaches can be laid to the door of the stock exchanges than all other gambling places combined. 

You scarcely pick up a paper but that you see where some man has been indicted for defrauding a bank of money to play the market, or where some man in utter desperation has committed suicide, due to his financial predicament, caused by Stock Exchanges.

However, you read only about a fraction of the actual cases. There is no way to determine the thousands or hundreds of thousands of people who every year lose everything in the market. 

What are Stock Exchanges? They are Exchanges composed of members to buy and sell securities. They serve their purpose to a certain extent. If you own securities listed on stock exchanges, you can immediately convert these securities into cash and from day to day, you know the value of those securities. 

If the Stock Exchanges were limited to buying and selling securities for cash and delivering these securities, allowing no margin trading, and if its members were prohibited from bringing out stock issues which frequently cause great loss to the buyers of the securi-

Back Kicks ties, then the losses sustained by the public would be greatly minimized.

The great majority of the Public, however, do not invest in securities in the market, they speculate, or putting it very plainly, gamble. 

They hear about the fabulous fortunes made in Wall Street. They know less than one in a thousand ever make a dollar profit in the end. However, we all feel we are more or less lucky, and we feel we will be the lucky one to beat the market. But, alas! only after losing everything, and after endless anquish, worry, heart-aches, and wasted time, do we realize the uselessness of it all. 

The market is composed of bulls and bears.

A bull is a man who believes in higher prices, and a bear is a man who expects lower prices. 

No one ever enters the market expecting to lose everything. Naturally we all expect to make money. Usually a newcomer in the market goes in cautiously. Frequently he has "beginners luck." 

A man deposits with his broker one thousand dollars. This will enable him to buy a hundred shares of stock on a ten point margin. 

By this I mean if a stock is selling at thirty dollars a share, a hundred shares out-right would cost three thousand dollars. However, the man can buy the hundred shares with a thousand dollars down and while the stock is

Back Kicks 13 his, nevertheless it remains in the brokers possession, as a collateral loan on the two thousand dollars balance owed the broker. The balance is called the debit balance and the broker receives interest on the money according to the prevailing rate of interest, which is as low as 12% or as high as 15% according to the money market.

Understand, however, any dividends received from the securities are applied to the stock owner's account.

If a man buys a hundred shares of stock at thirty and a day or two later the stock advances to thirty-three, the owner can immediately sell the stock at thirty-three and has made three points, or $3.00 per share profit, less commission paid to the broker, on the hundred shares. The man will have an approximate profit of three hundred dollars. 

What easy money! Three hundred dollars profit in a few days without working for it. 

The original thousand is now increased to $1,- 

300.00. Instead of buying 100 shares the man can now buy 130 shares on a ten point margin.

The broker explains that a man can "sell short" in the market just as easy as he can "go long" or buy stocks. In other words, if you think a stock is going down, you can sell that stock without owning it, and when the stock declines you can buy it in and make the difference between what you sold it at and what you bought it in at.

300.00 in the one deal or $1,600.00 profit in both deals within a few weeks.

About this time the man usually has his mind ten percent on business and ninety percent on the stock market. It takes so much effort to make money by working for it, and it is so easy to make in the market.

Many times a day the man finds himself calling the broker to ask the condition of the market. If he is handy to the brokers, he finds himself drifting in occasionally during the day. He cannot wait until evening to see the latest paper to find out how his stocks closed. 

These visits to the brokers office usually become more frequent and generally more time is spent in the board room as time goes by. 

If the man is fortunate he may run up his original investment to ten, fifteen, twenty or even fifty thousand dollars.

The real money made, however, is really only a secondary consideration. The main thought is how much stock can be traded in with the money on hand.

That is, he can buy so much stock with the funds available and every three to five points advance the new profit will enable him to keep on buying stock all the way up. The same condition applies to selling stock short. 

The man pyramids the stock and the first thing he has a comfortable fortune made in the market. 

If the man could only look ahead six months, a year or two years, he would take his money and forever let the market alone. But making money on the market is so easy and there is so much to be made, the man finds it next to impossible to give it up. 

If the man could only have the money in his hands in thousand dollar bills and realize it is real money, there would be more men drop out at this point. 

But the man doesn't want the money in bills. All he figures is how much stock can be bought with what he has.

You so often read of bank failures due to the cashier losing the bank's funds in the market. The cashier usually goes in the market in a small way and with his own funds, with no intention in the world of speculating with anybody else's money. However, he is no different from the other 999. Only those who have lost their all in the market can really sympathize with a man of this kind. At heart he is not a criminal. He is the victim of circumstances. Playing the market is just like opium. If it once gets you in its clutches in 999 cases out of 1000 you are its victim until you have lost everything. 

As long as the stock exchanges are allowed to exist under the conditions operating today, just that long will experiences as related above continue, causing endless mental and financial suffering. 

Under no conditions speculate in the market. Some men last longer than others. Some make and lose on and off for years, but the result is inevitably the same.

The market has such a hold on some men they save a few thousand in their own business and lose it in a few weeks in the market, and repeat the experience year after year.

Before You Entrust Your Funds With Any Broker

The stock market does have its advantages. 

Stock Exchange seats are worth from a few thousand to nearly a million dollars according to the price a member is able to sell his seat at which is generally governed by the prosperity of the country.

Without exception usually only the highest type of men are elected to the exchanges. A man can buy a seat but before he can trade he must be admitted and no one of questionable character is passed by the exchange. 

The stock exchanges have rules to protect as much as possible the customers of its members. These rules are scrupulously enforced and 

Stock Exchanges allow no irregularities or the member is suspended or expelled.

However, nothing is perfect, and even with their rigid rules, occasionally a member is expelled.

I have read from time to time where they have discussed the matter of binding together and making all the members liable for the failure of any member and other times of discussing insurance covering losses to the customers.

In my estimation, this should be done. As 

I have followed the market for the last seventeen years, there have been many failures and

Back Kicks 23 some of the failures were concerns seemingly as strong as the government itself. 

Thousands of men have had all their money up with brokers, or have been carrying stocks or bonds, either on a margin or outright, and when the broker failed the men have lost everything, just as they would have in a bank failure.

In my business life I have seen so many brokers fail, I would not advise anybody to carry accounts with any of them, unless the money was insured, providing they failed. 

A man I have known for years was manager of the Cleveland office of the E. W. Wagner & Co. E. W. Wagner & Co. were not only members of the New York Stock Exchange, but were members of the Chicago Board of 

Trade and many other exchanges. They had offices in principal cities and were considered absolutely sound. Year after year they went along and a man felt his money was just as safe with them as with a bank. Out of a clear sky they failed. 

Prince & Whitney, another stock exchange firm, one of the oldest and soundest firms in 

Wall Street, out of a clear sky failed.

These are only two instances, but they simply confirm my point that no matter how good they look or how sound they seem, there is always a possibility of their failing.

When You Receive Inside Information Or Tips 

On Any Stocks

A famous millionaire once said that a sure paying little business is worth more than all the tips in Wall Street. 

A tip is information either reliable or unreliable about certain stocks or grain. The tip can either be favorable or unfavorable. Without exception those giving the tips usually feel they have inside information that for certain reasons the stock or grain will go up or down. 

Anyone interested in securities hears certain tips at all times and at all places. Playing bridge, at clubs, on the Pullman trains, most of all in Brokers' offices.

Some years ago I made a thousand dollars on a shipment of Copra Cake we imported from the Phillipine Islands. The check for the $1,000.00 profit was mailed to me and I put it in my pocket and started across the street to put it in the bank. 

I had no sooner stepped on the sidewalk than I met one of the Federal Auditors. We talked about various stocks, etc., and he advised me he was auditing the books of the 

Grant Motor Co. in Cleveland and he felt sure their profits would warrant an increase in their dividend. 

The Government Auditor was sincere in

Back Kicks 25 his statement. 

He being a Federal Auditor made me feel sure the tip or information was surely inside information and the check never saw the bank.

I immediately took it over to the broker's office and purchased as much Grant Motor 

Stock on a margin as the $1,000.00 would buy.

Within a few weeks their business started dropping off --- within a few months they did not raise their dividend but passed it entirely. 

The stock dropped. I had to put up one thousand right after another in order to hold my original stock and finally the company failed all together, and I lost several thousand dollars in the transaction.

Another time I was introduced by a mutual friend to the sales manager of the Smith Form-a-Truck Company of Chicago. We had lunch together at the Hollenden Hotel in Cleveland.

At lunch the sales manager told me about the prosperity of the Smith Form-a-Truck Company. He said they had more orders on their books than they could fill. Their stock was selling on the Curb Exchange at around five dollars a share. 

He said he felt the stock surely should go to at least twenty-five dollars a share. 

This, I thought, was the best inside infor-

Back Kicks mation possible to receive, a real tip from the sales manager himself.

It was a wonder I did not have indigestion, because I was in such a hurry to finish lunch and have my broker buy five thousand shares at approximately five dollars a share. 

It is true I knew I would have twenty-five thousand dollars tied up in the stock, but nevertheless if it only went up to twenty it would make me $75,000.00.

A few weeks after buying the stock they became entangled in litigation. The stock dropped steadily. They went into bankruptcy and a year or two later when I needed a little money I took the five thousand shares to Worthington Bellows & Company and had them sell it for me and received just exactly 1/32 for the stock or three and one-third cents a share that less than two years before had cost me $5.00 a share. All the result of an inside tip.

Before You Purchase Any Securities For 

Permanent Investment 

I cannot impress upon you too strongly the effect the ever changing world conditions have on the security market.

It is folly and dangerous to invest all your money in any one or two securities. In the first place, fifty years ago prohibition was almost unthought of. At that time probably even the most optimistic prohibitionist did not expect to see prohibition for a hundred or two years. 

Good sound brewery stocks were considered a gilt edge investment. Many men died leaving their families brewery stocks returning a good yield, assuming the income would support and provide the necessities of life for their loved ones.

The Worlds' War brought prohibition almost before we realized it. The brewery industry was wiped out from its roots up. Most of the brewery buildings became dilapidated. Dividends were cut off and not only the stocks but the bonds became worthless.

Practically the same conditions existed in the Interuban railway industry. Twenty years ago the Interurban railways were flourishing and new lines were rapidly spreading over the country like a network.

Then came the extensive use of the auto-

Back Kicks mobile and better roads and the bus, and with it the decline in interurban railways profits. 

Today most of them are in receivership and very few are making money.

This condition applies not only to the brewery and interurban companies, but to many others as well.

Some years ago I bought approximately fifty thousand dollars of Columbia Phonograph first mortgage gold bonds. The phonograph industry in my estimation was a safe and staple business. Their profits might vary of course due to good and bad business years, but nevertheless the bonds looked like a dependable investment. 

I figured these bonds would bring me and my heirs several thousand dollars a year indefinitely.

Within six months the radio became much improved. The Phonograph industry became demoralized and my bonds practically worthless. . 

Of course, one might say there is a certain risk in everything. That is true, and for that reason one should diversify his investments, realizing that any one of them due to changing world conditions, modern inventions, etc., might become worthless.

I have mentioned about certain securities

Back Kicks 29 today which are very high grade and yet in years to come may be worthless.

These securities are not confined at all to companies who produce specialities or staple commodities for the market but in fact any firm under certain conditions today may be perfectly sound and tomorrow unsound. 

Take for instance the New York, New

Haven and Hartford Railroad Company, practically dominating the transportation of New England. Any man would naturally feel he could leave securities of this kind to his estate and the income would provide them a steady certain income. 

However, mismanagement, or any number of other causes, can put a company on the rocks in short order. 

The New York, New Haven and Hartford

Railroad Company, at one time one of the soundest and best system in the country, had to be reorganized. 

The once proud and mighty Royal Mail

Steam Packet Company whose securities climbed above seven pounds per share last year dropped to zero. In fact a sale was actually made at below zero. The owner paid the transfer tax. 

The once gilt-edge 62% preferred stock of the company closed the day at the ignoble price of zero.

The reason for the common stock dropping

Back Kicks to nothing was due to the fact that certain attorneys stated the company was not limited and the share holders would be liable for their company's debts.

Of course laws in various states differ. but there is always a possibility unless you know just what you are buying that you might be liable, being a stock-holder, if the company should fail, for the debts pro-rata to the amount of stock you hold.

It is never wise to put all your eggs in any one basket. The above instances are conclusive proof.

When Buying Securities Of Any Kind 

The safest way to buy securities is to buy them outright and put them into your security box. Then it does not matter whether the market goes up or down. You own the stock outright and can never be called on for margin. 

If you own good listed stocks or bonds outright you can always borrow sixty or seventy percent of the market value from your banker.

Remember, it is always better to own ten shares of a stock outright than a hundred shares on a margin.

I know of a case where a man owned approximately two hundred shares of a good stock outright. He took the shares to his broker to sell and the broker persuaded him to put up the shares as a margin and buy considerably more stock.

The man followed the broker's advice and with the inevitable result. The market crashed.

The man not only lost the full value of his original stock but more than ten thousand dollars besides. 

The last time I talked to him he said he was through forever with the market. (Since the above was written, the man entered the market again and committed suicide).

When Making Sales Or Closing Contracts 

We are all salesmen. No matter what our position in life, we all make our living by selling. Professional men must sell their services.

Clerks and laborers also. The better our work, the more pay we receive. 

The greater amount of service we render the greater compensation. This not only applies to individuals but also to corporations as well. 

No matter what our vocation in life there is one rule which if rigidly abided by will pay the biggest dividends --- and that rule is, "Always stick to the truth.” 

As is carved on the library at Fifth Avenue and 42nd Street in New York City "But above all things, truth beareth away the victory." Never were truer words penned and how fittingly they are carved in stone to be a guide for all generations and to endure thru the ages.

A man or corporation can lose every dollar, but the good will created can never be lost. 

Good will is only created by fair and honest dealing. 

Frequently in life temporary monetary gains can be had by slightly deviating from the truth, but in the end the loss will far outweigh the gain. 

If the officials of every company from the

Back Kicks 33 Chairman of the Board of Directors to the Sales Manager would abide by and could burn into the minds of every employee just two words, and they and the employees would live those two words to the best of their ability, the losses saved would run into hundreds of millions of dollars per year, not mentioning endless law suits, misunderstandings and loss of good will. The two words are, DON'T MIS- 

REPRESENT. 

No matter what you are selling, whether it be life insurance, real estate, food products, specialities or anything else, never intentionally misrepresent one single fact in order to make a sale.

This, of course, is exceedingly hard to do. 

Many of us occasionally slightly deviate from the true facts in order to close a deal. 

By misrepresenting, I am not referring to those who will deliberately lie in order to close the sale. This manuscript is only intended for those who want to transact business in a fair and honorable manner. 

Anybody intentionally lying and deliberately and knowingly misrepresenting in my estimation is just a plain crook and deserves the same treatment as a thief who robs your home or steals your money.

There are, of course, a certain class to

Back Kicks

whom the truth means nothing. This class will not last long in any business. 

However, the over anxious salesman might be inclined to make statements, slightly incorrect and often times causing no harm, but nevertheless leading eventually into lost confidences.

By this I mean a buyer might ask if delivery can be made on a certain day. The salesman feels that delivery cannot be made for a day or two later, but assures the buyer he will receive his goods on a certain day. The day arrives and the buyer does not receive his goods. 

The goods arrive a day or two later and he accepts them but nevertheless the buyer has lost some of his confidence in the salesman.

What I have to say will probably be challenged by the majority of real estate dealers, bankers and by even anyone allied with the building business. 

However, I am simply stating my experiences.

There has been much talk to the effect that if you buy a good piece of real estate now, it will keep you later. Very true, but where is the good real estate? And who knows but what good real estate now will be worth a fraction of its cost five, ten or twenty years from now? 

There are exceptions, of course. Desirable real estate in New York, Chicago or other metropolitan cities probably will continue to enhance in value. Even small growing towns and cities have real estate that will double, triple and even be worth a hundred times its present value ten years from now.

On the other hand, while there is one making money in real estate, hundreds of others are buying lots or other real estate that in ten or fifteen years will be taken over for the taxes or mortgages. 

I really believe there is not one case in a thousand where it pays a man to even own his own home, if there must be a first and second

Back Kicks 71 mortgage on it. In the first place, generally it keeps a man sweating blood to meet the interest and refinancing charges on second mortgages. Then there is hanging over his head like a mighty sword always the fear of losing the property after he has spent time and labor in improving it. 

In buying a home, first save enough money to buy the home outright, or at least to pay all but the amount of a reasonable low interest first mortgage on it. With ready cash there are always exceptional bargains. If you own your own home and are happy there you do not care whether the property goes up or down in value. It is your own home. You are the king of your domain and no one can put you out. 

However, if you own a home with a second mortgage, you can't feel the satisfaction of ownership as much as you could if you were renting. A landlord generally endeavors to please and retain satisfied tenants whereas second mortgage holders simply hold a club over your head all the time. If you have a second mortgage on your home, figure up the interest on both mortgages, plus the taxes, plus the interest on your equity, and you are very apt to find that you are paying more than you could have rented the place for. 

Business property is not always a sound investment either. I know of a property within a

Back Kicks stone's throw of the Union Terminal Development in Cleveland. The property including the building can be bought for about five thousand dollars. Years ago the property was in the heart of the Cleveland business district. Today it is practically worthless.

When one considers owning apartment houses or stores another problem enters. It is a difficult matter to fill the places with desirable tenants. I mean tenants who are prompt with their rent, etc. Then there are always tenants moving. The places must be fixed up, redecorated, etc.

The time a man spends on a fairly good sized place almost equals his net return from his property. In this I am speaking from experience, because at one time I managed approximately twenty apartment houses in Cleveland.

It is as difficult to pick good real estate as it is to pick good stocks. Years ago brewery stocks, interurban railway stocks were considered A 1 investments. Today most are worthless. The same thing may occur in real estate.

The Great Masses of working people are never sure of their future. A young man will start in a business and work hard for years then when he has reached middle age, he will be replaced by a younger person. I have seen the time when all these commodities were selling at Peak prices and the future pointed to even far higher prices and yet within a short time the bottom dropped right out of the prices, and there did not seem to be any limit as to how low the prices would fall.

However, it is well to remember that the prices on all commodities can only be governed by the law of supply and demand. 

There never has been, nor will there probably ever be, any artificial method to stabilize the prices on any commodity.

The world at the present time, due to modern inventions, such as the radio, telephone, telegraph, fast methods of transportation, etc. is fast becoming more of one big family, than of separate isolated nations.

Back Kicks 101 The prosperity of one nation reflects in the prosperity of all nations. 

In order for nations to be prosperous, it is necessary for the basic commodity of any nation to sell at a fair average profit. When the nations of the entire world are selling their goods at a cost below production, there can be no world wide prosperity.

For instance, the main crop of Cuba is sugar. It costs so much per pound to produce sugar. When sugar does not sell for the cost of production the people cannot even afford to pay their taxes or replace worn-out equipment. 

They cannot afford to buy clothes, radios, automobiles, etc. Consequently, all manufacturers suffer and a state of depression exists. 

The state of prosperity, therefore, is an imaginary or "cost of production line." When basic commodities produced by civilized nations such as sugar, grains, coffee, cotton, meats, metals, etc., sell at less than the cost of production depression is bound to exist. 

When these basic commodities sell at just the cost of production then conditions should be normal and the higher they sell above the cost of production line the greater the prosperity.
