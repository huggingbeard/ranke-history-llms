OTHER MATTERS 

Standing Timber and Timberland. In reviewing the laws of other tates, the Commission has found that timber-producing states have noved away from local ad valorem taxation of timberland and standing timber. This has been done in recognition of the economic mportance of timber products. It has been the purpose of the new tax laws to encourage good forest management practices. Ad valorem caxation of standing timber and timberland is difficult because of the length of time required for the invested capital to produce a return. This means that taxes are paid annually by the taxpayer over a period of years when there may be little cash recovered from sale of timber; and this passage of time increases the hazards of maintaining the investment (e.g., fire, insects, disease) and the uncertainties of price at the time of harvest. 

Development of an alternative to the present method of ad valorem taxation of standing timber and timberland would require working out a number of difficult technical problems. Continued sound financing of necessary public services in the timber-growing area is essential. 

This will require careful analysis of information from taxpayers and taxing units.

COMMISSION RECOMMENDATION NO. 16 

It is recommended that the Legislature provide for a study which would develop a workable alternative to the present method of ad valorem taxation of standing timber and timberland. [19]
