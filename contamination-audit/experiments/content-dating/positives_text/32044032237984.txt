STATE OF NEW YORK EXECUTIVE CHAMBER ALBANY, March 16, 1959 To the Legislature: 

Rehabilitation is the process of restoring disabled persons to lives of greater usefulness and self-sufficiency through medical treatment and training.

Handicapping disabilities interfere with the ability of adults to work, of children to attend school, and of many persons to be self-sufficient in their daily activities. The human values of rehabilitation are inestimable.

While the well-being of our citizens is our prime interest, nevertheless we cannot afford to overlook the savings in costs of care and the actual return to our economy from increased productivity and tax dollars which result from the rehabilitation of handicapped persons into employment. The aggregate earnings of those rehabilitated last year through our vocational rehabilitation program increased from an estimated $2 million annually to over $11 million annually. These rehabilitated persons will pay an estimated $1.4 million in State and Federal income taxes on their earnings for the first year after rehabilitation. It should also be noted that they were receiving public assistance payments at the rate of $800,000 annually before rehabilitation. 

Present Status of Our Rehabilitation Programs 

Certain aspects of New York's public rehabilitation programs have failed to match the State's record in other social fields. The administrators now responsible for these programs have recognized deficiencies, and last year, through the Interdepartmental Health Resources Board, they completed an inventory of all State programs involving rehabilitation. This inventory helped to lay the groundwork for current planning. 

Among the more important State programs involving rehabilitation are: the general vocational rehabilitation program of the 

State Education Department's Division of Vocational Rehabilita tion; the special vocational rehabilitation services for the blind provided by the State Department of Social Welfare; the several medical rehabilitation services administered by the State Department of Health; and the Selective Placement Service of the State 

Department of Labor's State Employment Service. 

While these programs serve a significant number of disabled persons, there are many others who could benefit from rehabilitation services but who are not yet being served. It is important that we expand our goals to reach these individuals. 

The Road Ahead 

To improve rehabilitation services will require vigorous effort, sustained over a long period of time. The crucial need of the [3]

present is to set in motion the machinery designed to produce longrange results. A number of approaches are essential. While most of these approaches can be undertaken without legislation, I wish to report them to you as part of an integrated program. 

1. Leadership and Coordination 

The State's rehabilitation effort is necessarily distributed among many agencies of government. This fact places a premium on strong leadership and coordination.

The unit primarily charged with this responsibility is the State 

Interdepartmental Health Resources Board. This Board is com posed of the heads of Departments involved in health or healthrelated functions. I shall continue to look to the Board to help in the coordination, joint planning, development and evaluation of programs.

It should have the staff assistance necessary to carry out these functions, including the assignments referred to in this Message.

In addition, I believe it is essential that our rehabilitation effort have the benefit of the continuing advice of objective experts from outside the State government. Rehabilitation in New York does not need a one-time, large-scale study so much as it does stepby-step evolution and adaption of new methods and improved procedures. 

Accordingly, I shall designate a small group of consultants, to be called the Governor's Council on Rehabilitation. This consulting group will serve in an advisory capacity to both the 

Governor and the Interdepartmental Health Resources Board, with staff services provided by the latter. I believe that this 

Council can help provide the stimulus necessary to assure continued progress. 

2. Rehabilitation Personnel Needs 

If the State of New York is to strengthen and expand its rehabilitation services, it is obvious that our various State programs must have available to them the necessary trained personnel to work directly with our disabled citizens. 

A recent survey of professional rehabilitation positions such as rehabilitation counselors, physical and occupational therapists, clinical psychologists and social workers showed that of 1409 such positions, 299 or 21% were unfilled and 427 or 30% were filled by temporary appointments. Thus, less than half of these important positions were occupied by permanent appointees. This acute shortage of professional skills hampers our programs and is of great concern in our State. 

Therefore, I am asking the Interdepartmental Health Resources

Board to give the highest priority to an analysis of rehabilitation personnel needs and resources in New York State. This study will be carried out with the active cooperation of the Department of Civil Service and the Division of the Budget. The study

should include examination of the large potential for the training of rehabilitation personnel which lies in the State University and in privately supported institutions of higher education. 

Upon conclusion of the study, the Interdepartmental Health Resources Board and the Governor's Council of Rehabilitation will present to me their recommendations as to how the urgent rehabilitation personnel needs of New York State can best be met. 

3. Expansion of Vocational Rehabilitation Services 

Vocational rehabilitation consists of the services which enable a handicapped person to engage in a remunerative occupation. 

4,455 persons were rehabilitated last year through the vocational rehabilitation program operated by the Division of Vocational Rehabilitation in the State Education Department, and 209 persons were similarly rehabilitated through the special program for the blind. These persons represent only a small portion of the number of our handicapped citizens estimated to be in need of vocational rehabilitation. We should expand the program to a point at which we are assured we are serving all disabled persons who are capable of benefiting thereby. 

To help make the necessary expansion possible, I have included in my Budget an increase of $435,700 for the Division of Vocational Rehabilitation in the State Education Department. These funds will be used primarily to meet the costs of medical and training services for those undergoing rehabilitation. 

4. Revision of Vocational Rehabilitation Law 

In 1954 the Federal Government revamped and modernized its vocational rehabilitation law as a basis for a projected nationwide program expansion. A number of States have adopted new statutes since then.

I recommend the adoption of legislation which would bring New York's rehabilitation law up to date and reflect current needs. This legislation would include these features: 

1. Broadening the definition of vocational rehabilitation services to conform with the Federal provisions and providing express authority for all such services; 

2. Promoting flexibility of operation by substituting general authority as to administrative procedures for the outmoded procedural provisions now in the law; 

3. Providing authority for research and training to improve program techniques and to increase the availability of trained rehabilitation personnel; and 

4. Authorization for the Division of Vocational Rehabilitation to provide services to those in State mental and correctional institutions who may be benefited.

5. Integration of Rehabilitation with Disability Income Programs 

We have today broad social insurance programs designed to help replace lost earnings when disability occurs. Our ultimate objectives for disabled persons should not, however, be financial support. Our purpose is to help insure the opportunity for each individual to attain his fullest potential and to achieve the maximum development of his own capacities. 

I shall, therefore, make it a goal of my Administration to provide a closer integration between the vocational rehabilitation program and the various disability income programs. 

(a) Workmen's Compensation and Temporary Disability Benefits 

At my direction, action has already been taken to give greater emphasis to the vocational rehabilitation of disabled workers receiving workmen's compensation. Specific procedures and personnel assignments are being established for more quickly identifying those who may be benefited by vocational rehabiliation services and for providing those services. 

Two years ago your Honorable Bodies appropriated $135,000 for a study of rehabilitation of injured workers. After some preliminary work and unfortunate delays, in December of 1958 the 

Workmen's Compensation Board contracted with New York Uni versity for this study. The study should produce very valuable results, with specific recommendations for administrative and legislative action.

The Workmen's Compensation Board will then explore ways of applying the same principles to our program of temporary disability benefits. 

(b) Federal Old-Age, Survivors and Disability Insurance 

In 1954 the Congress enacted provisions granting protection, analogous to the "waiver of premium" provision in private life insurance, which protects the social security benefits of totally disabled workers. Then, in 1956, the Congress provided for the payment of monthly cash benefits to totally disabled persons over age 50. 66 The findings as to whether individual applicants are eligible for the Federal benefits are made by the Department of Social Welfare under contract with the Federal government. It is extremely important that maximum use be made of this process as case finding" for rehabilitation. I am asking the Interdepartmental

Health Resources Board to review and report to me as to how the opportunities for rehabilitation can be increased for applicants for 

Federal benefits. 

(c) Public Assistance 

Continuing attention should be given to reducing public assistance caseloads through rehabilitation.

In the last three years a pilot project has been conducted at The 

New York State Rehabilitation Hospital at West Haverstraw, de signed to demonstrate how persons on the aid-to-disabled relief rolls could benefit from rehabilitation. The results were highly successful. I believe, therefore, that this project should be continued as a regular part of the program at West Haverstraw, and the present special rates applicable for disabled persons on public assistance should be continued. My budget includes the necessary funds for the Health Department for this purpose. Encouraging local welfare departments to use rehabilitation services will assure savings to the State and its localities over the long run. 

I am also asking the Commissioner of Social Welfare, in cooperation with the Commissioners of Health and Education, to prepare a plan whereby the knowledge acquired from this demonstration project can be brought to bear for as many as possible of the recipients of aid-to-the-disabled in the State. I also believe these same principles can and should be applied to the disabled parents of children receiving assistance payments under the aid-todependent children program. 

6. Improved Medical Services Under the Vocational Rehabilitation Program 

The provision of medical services to alleviate disability is a basic part of the comprehensive service to the individual vocational rehabilitation client. The continued provision of these medical services in adequate quantity and in accordance with the the highest professional standards available to us is essential. 

The State Department of Health has had considerable experience in the administration of medical rehabilitation services through its program for physically handicapped children and for adults paralyzed by poliomyelitis, and through its operation of the New 

York State Rehabilitation Hospital. This experience includes the establishment of standards for medical personnel, services and facilities, determination of medical fee schedules, evaluation of medical disabilities and the potentialities of corrective services, and relationships with medical societies to assure the cooperation of the medical profession.

There are essentially two approaches to better utilization of the Health Department's resources in the vocational rehabilitation program: 

(1) The Health Department would work closely with a medical administrator in the Division of Vocational Rehabilitation, who would supervise the provision of medical services in accordance with Health Department standards; or (2) The Health Department would perform for the Division of Vocational Rehabilitation the function of obtaining medical services for persons undergoing rehabilitation.

In order to determine which of these patterns is likely to prove more fruitful in the long run, I am requesting the Commissioners. of Health and of Education to establish pilot projects to test the

relative merits of these approaches. The Commissioner of Mental 

Hygiene will participate in the psychiatric aspects of medical service. 

7. Rehabilitation Field Laboratory 

Rehabilitation programs must reflect constantly changing conditions and knowledge. Changes in the age distribution of our population, shifts in industrial situations and development of new clinical, training or administrative techniques are variables that may indicate a change in focus and re-alignment of our programs and procedures.

To help redesign our rehabilitation programs as may be needed, I propose we establish in an appropriate community setting a rehabilitation "field laboratory." By concentration of effort in a specific area, we can evaluate current programs, determine the extent to which needs are being met, demonstrate new techniques in providing services, provide opportunities for training professional personnel, conduct research, and study the costs and the methods of meeting the costs for these activities. Such a laboratory" should be established in an area in which there is a university medical center and adequate community resources and interest. 

I am requesting the Interdepartmental Health Resources Board, working with the Governor's Council, to formulate a detailed blueprint for this project. 

I can report that discussions have already been held with representatives of the Federal Government concerning this proposal, and they have suggested that the project might be utilized for a number of studies of national interest and significance. The State will, therefore, make an application to the Federal Government for a special project grant to assist in carrying out this proposal. 

8. Rehabilitation Services for Physically Handicapped Inmates of the New York State Correctional Institutions 

There are many inmates of correctional institutions who are disabled. They should be prepared for their eventual return to their communities. The Interdepartmental Health Resources Board has made a special study of this problem and has submitted recommendations regarding the improvement of rehabilitation services. 

Of particular importance is the recommendation for a special center for epileptic prisoners to provide for their medical, vacational and social rehabilitation needs. As a beginning, I am requesting the Commissioner of Correction to proceed with a demonstration project in one institution, in consultation with the interdepartmental Health Resources Board, to determine how present resources and modern methods can be brought to bear on this problem. 

9. Rehabilitation Services to Adults with No Vocational Potential 

There are a significant number of disabled adults who unfortunately have no identifiable vocational potentialities but who may

achieve important increases in self-care through a medical rehabilitation service. There is currently considerable interest in Congress in this area, with a number of bills pending which would offer Federal aid for rehabilitation of such persons. 

At present there is no comprehensive State program for assisting such individuals to receive rehabilitation services. 

The only exceptions to this are adults with poliomyelitis and those who are eligible for public assistance.

These persons could be provided for in either of two ways: 

1. Extension of the program for physically handicapped children operated by the State Department of Health to include adults; or 

2. Extension of the medical services of the public assistance program to encompass rehabilitation services for the medically indigent.

I am requesting the Commissioners of Social Welfare and Health, in conjunction with the Commissioner of Education, to confer with representatives of the medical profession and to make a joint study to determine the best way to reach and serve these persons and the costs involved. Certain aspects of the problem might be examined through the rehabilitation field laboratory I have proposed.

Conclusion 

Rehabilitation is a heartwarming process; it achieves for the individual a greater degree of independence, satisfaction and dignity. No program could be more deserving of the State's strong support.

The measures I have outlined will pave the way for a major advance in enlarging opportunities for rehabilitation for disabled citizens of New York. My Administration will work closely with you and with public and private agencies throughout the State to expand our efforts in this important area of human welfare. 

(Signed) NELSON A. ROCKEFELLER
