SYNOPSIS 

When we think of the numerous problems facing this upset world, dictators and war stand out in dire prominence. How about the many other tragedies which also continue to relentlessly stalk humanity unabated, some of which have directly caused more death and suffering than have all the combined wars of history! 

In view of religious leaders, the Oxford group, and the President's failure so far to obtain peace on earth, good will to men, can anything more tangible be done about it?

Most assuredly indeed! Your perusal of the plans outlined between the covers of this book will bring you to a firm conclusion that man's inhumanity to man, the main cause of it all, can and will be abolished, clearing the way for inevitable world democracy, the one and the only solution. 

Here is a frank and open treatise which carries a moral that is vital, one conducive to progress and prosperity for all humanity. Nor is it an effort to try and reform the world in a day. The author is no religious fanatic and there is no reflection cast upon millions of good devout Christians, but there is frank disclosure of the sacrilegious practices of many who would cavort through life under the guise of piety, leaders of nations, crooked politicians and too many millions who aid them by their own apathy toward illicit conquest. 

On Christmas Eve last, the President and religious leaders of the world cried out for some specific plan for the cessation of war and eventual peace to the world. Prompted by this announcement in the papers, and through the kindness of his congressman, Mr. Adams was able to have delivered to Mr. Stephen Early, secretary to the President, his plans written on Christmas Day. Mr. Early promptly acknowledged receipt of them and although no personal word from Mr. Roosevelt has been received these many months later, the author lives in hopes.

Herein is the plan as written to the President. One definitely specific and as definitely terrific to heathens, but calling for little sacrifice from humanity as a whole. With its adoption by the Christian nations of the world, and ultimate participation by those nations which by the will of God we cannot ignore, the plan will not only bring about the demise of dictators, but will truly give us the solution to ALL major problems facing this world when chaos is heading us straight for bedlam!

PREFACE 

In formulating plans to write this book, I found vast difference in simply having an idea and then writing of it when an inferiority complex compels caution. To enhance this handicap some friends intimated because I had never written a book I was licked at the start. 

To be a success I must be a genius in the field of literary expression, my vocabulary was too limited, I had had no college education and because I was not an author with a big name I had better forget the whole idea. 

However, seemingly at the psychological moment, other friends highly intellectual, of broad culture and of responsible position, told me my plans were ideal, the book could do nothing but good and worthy of trial. I grab at those kind tributes, all in the face of adversity, for the courage to find out through the medium of this book, if I at least have sufficient ability to put over my philosophies, stress their importance and still be able to make it interesting reading matter. 

Heretofore I have dwelled too long with apathy on the entire thought. The first bombshell which urged me with alacrity to wake up and DO something about it was a tragic incident in my own life. 

The final shot I needed was the refusal of three prominent papers to publish a letter disclosing my plans for a very possible and highly probable solution to, or at least improvement in, the multiple problems of war, alcohol and crime.

It is the expression of a personal philosophy, evoked by sad experience combined with those of other mortals trying to solve the mysteries of life. To place my story before the public, sheer persistency is my only recourse, and I pray it will prove a virtue, genius or no genius!

If I had used one of the following for a title to this book, "Christianity is Slipping." "All who are Evil, Beware." "Thy Will be done, Thy Kingdom Come." "Protestanism is Slipping." it would from the beginning be a flop, as a title commanding much attention.

Today the world does not want a book bearing of religious fervor or interesting only to pious people. Gone are the days when the popularity of a book having tendencies to try and reform the world in a day, although to say the least they were read by thousands of good Christians. Nor can we in flowery language, gild a book which between the covers is only literary value of tin. 

If I had selected for my title one of these, "This is a Hell of a World.” "Why only the dictators-Beware!" "The World is Screwy!" "Jitterbug to Bedlam." 17)

it would materially help if I was looking for large sale through exploitation and tempting titles. They are the kind which would attract attention, and if you were gullible you might purchase one. 

If you were not gullible it would still have its attractions whereas the former ones would draw but passing and casual inspection.

Well after all, in the vernacular of modern day language, the world is screwy, it's a hell of a world to most, protestantism is slipping, all who are evil might readily profit by being beware, and jitterbugging to bedlam might hit the nail on the head as a very appropriate modern day title. Why should only the dictators be beware, when there are corrupt civic governments galore, crooked politicians, crime abundant and plenty, plenty and then some more, lack of a devout Christian spirit!

Well, the subject and contents of this book were never in the first place meant to become a money-making proposition. It was the refusal of several prominent newspapers to print my letter to the editor, a letter portraying through print, my plans for the possible solution to, and at least improvement in, the too numerous problems of the world. It burned me up, these papers were representative of the more conservative ones and they are published in New York, Chicago and Los Angeles. When they refused to print it even in condensed form that the public might read of it, there was nothing but to write the President, as I felt that he would not pass it up. It was delivered to the White House and I received acknowledgment from Mr. Early, the President's secretary, and while I have as yet not heard from Mr. Roosevelt, I live in hopes. Meanwhile, my effort, that the public will know of it anyway. 

I took for my title the one I thought most adaptable, although frankly, I believe any one of those listed would cover the contents in so far as those who prefer that type of title. I chose for my comedy and tragedy, love and hate, spice and sex and the numerous other phases of life, from the simplest of sources. Life as it is, and I sincerely trust that the many good pious people, and thank God there are still some left, will bear with me from cover to cover. 

That the public might read it, and after all the common people are the world, and as they go, so shall the universe; and that those who require a modern vein to their reading along with the more pious, I have the courage of my convictions and have strived to make it interesting reading matter to all. I have tried all through, when 18)

quoting some who have confided their hopes in, and others who have condemned it, to express to the best of my ability the nearest to exact manner in which they before expressed it to me. 

Nor is there anything so vulgar, or so sexy, or so downright scandalous but what you who patronize the talkies will see and hear. 

And if you read a lot, you have seen in print in the papers and magazines the like of which would make my revelations seem like a Sunday school paper in comparison. And if it were not for rigid censorship, you would hear it over the air. As it is now, the devil's concoctions are holding their own in broadcasts telling you of the merits of liquor, when there are none. I wonder if this is the only blight to the genius of the immortal Marconi?

Since talkies have been perfected, I have attended but ten or a dozen pictures, and they of the type of Maytime, The Firefly and the Great Victor Herbert, pictures which truly prove what a wonderful thing the talking pictures can be, when they want to be. 

However, if I were interested in the spicy, sextype of picture or magazine, I would neither have to attend the theatre or buy the book. 

In wanting to get the significance of the latest in ribald presentations, I would simply go into a beer joint, sit in a booth adjacent to one occupied by a group of modern youngsters, and hocus-pocus, I'd get the real low down. I would not have to eavesdrop either, for usually in these days youth does not whisper, as in the days gone by, of things we were forbidden to discuss even in private. Of course the movies picture these things as being done in the best of families, so why not they?

The Eternal cycle of life; How like two circles which do not have a space between; They touch, and we must stay within one or the other. One the vicious cycle and the other the pious. Wonderful sometimes to reminisce and picture our happiest days of childhood and adolescence. How big we felt when first we went to school and later how boresome it became. If we wanted to and did quit to go to work, how quickly we woke up and realized that teacher wasn't so bad after all. 

School days, school days, dear old golden rule days, when our first puppy love experience seems now so trivial, but then so mammoth to our emotions, and dreams of real love, visions of happiness in marriage and a home and children. Then adolescence had all too soon turned into adulthood and we were at once at the crossroads of life. {9}

Here, some of us paused and carefully surveyed the landscape bordering those two roads at the turn. One the road down the vicious cycle of temptation and evil, the other up the hill to success and the rewards of piety. Sad to relate, what with modern day temptations, that I fear too many of us failed to study those signs, but perhaps we didn't know we were lost and simply took the road that looked best. 

Down the hill was easier, the line of least resistance, and it required less effort to maintain our moral stature. How easy to create a cycle of temptation which grows in ever widening inducement to sin. Much to my sorrow, I found it so, but thankfully have come to the realization that no matter how far down the road one gets, there is a way back.

And here I am again at another crossroads. In the face of adversity 

I found the publishing of this book to be but another obstacle, one not easy to overcome. It is only by the grace of God who allowed the sheer persistency I was forced to summon, and be able to survive the nervous tension and the discouragement necessary and attendant to preparation and achieving my goal. Financial backing was a main obstacle, but through all the bewildering complexities and many days of disheartenment, it was friends who came to my rescue and enabled me to carry on. Then and only then, did I appreciate all the worthy tribute to friendship by Saintly poets. No longer was I under the delusion that a dog was my only friend, a pal forever, and I lived anew in hope.

Feeling at this time that the opinion of five intellectuals whom I knew, men of broad culture and men of experience in various vocations, would be apropos, I sought their sanctums, gave them the synopsis, and eagerly but nervously awaited their sentiments. 

One was the editor of a newspaper, one a lawyer and a judge, the third the principal of a large school, another the minister of a large church and the last a man representative of labor. 

The variation in their response was indeed startling, to say the least.

One said I was crazy and if not quite, overzealous; it had wonderful possibilities and was an ideal plan; it's too real and too much of bitter truth and that hurts, especially politicians; it's mighty interesting, but of no value; it's magnificent, ideal and don't let anything stop you from getting it off to press.

Well! I was in a dilemma of no mean proportions. For propriety's (10)

sake I deem it advisable to refrain from telling who was who and who said what. Suffice to say, if this book turns out the opposite of any individual prediction, think how mortified the particular man would feel.

I figured they were all smart men and that one's opinion was as valuable as the others and their concensus only served to decide me to make the most of my own ability and write as I thought best. If 

I were to act upon the advice of some, the book would end up far different from the way I mean it to. Why should its grammatical construction be without a flaw, and why should I change it and solicit favor from the critics? If it's condemnation they give me, should it ever reach the stage when they will be interested enough to read it, then by confining myself to my own limited vocabulary and ability, said condemnation can truly be aimed at me, eliminating any chance of my ever saying to an advisor, "I told you so," or "you steered me wrong."

I was told by some that I would have to reach perfection in the line of literary expression these days to put over a book, but I felt that if I were to wait until I could achieve that, well, the public would never get my plans, and by the looks of things at this writing, with poor little Finland gone the way of dictator's desire, the sooner my plans are sponsored by group leaders, the sooner peace will come. 

Mr. A. R. Lesage says: "Authors in general are stark mad on the subject of their own works," and I heartily agree with him. If I have any initiative or worthy individuality you are getting it now, for this book is in sum and substance my first attempt and I trust it is not garnished with superflous literary seasoning which could spoil any book.

If I knew the ropes or were a dyed in the wool author I might have built it up where you would be kept in suspense until the last chapter, such as a love story where they live happily ever after. Perhaps in the style of a good crime story, where the solution is never revealed until the final clue has been digested. 

Well I concluded to reverse this custom and give you my solution at the beginning, hoping that you will find in it desire to continue on, that you may read of the crimes which warrant in these days the seeking of a worthy end ere they envelop us all. 

A too serious vein would remind you of an evangelist or a religious fanatic, but after all there is one subject which we must admit war- (11)

rants the interest of mankind more now than at any other time in the history of the world. That is conditions in this complex world today, with its relationship to Christianity. We know that man's attempt to remedy any of the world's problems without the aid of a Divine being have been futile, and all this even in the face of modern civilization, which offhand we would judge to broaden our intellect to the extent of at least knowing enough to settle nation's problems without recourse to war. Day by day we are absolutely approaching closer to virtual bedlam. 

With modern competition of the talkies, night-clubs, beer joints and what have you for entertainment, the churches are up against it, especially evening services, as witness meagre attendance. Is the day coming when they will resort to huge neon signs, a la the great white way of Times Square, to pull in their congregations? Maybe featured movie stars, radio headliners or shapely girls in a dance act will prove the drawing attraction.

Even then, just because it happens to be a church, I'd bet you'd find no S.R.O. signs on the door. Weekly dances and movies and parties are already coming with the demise or resignation of some of the staid deacons of the old school who have failed to wake up and realize that this is the 20th century and 20th century tactics are needed.

With science ever opening up new vistas of life, there has come a too definite trend by millions to believe that with death comes the absolute end and immortality is a myth. With this view also comes the philosophy, "why take life seriously anyway?" and "why the hell not drink myself to death, why worry about morals and sin, what if I do go out with married women, what if I do cultivate greed and cheat the government out of a few thousand in taxes by subterfuge?" 

Yes indeed, there is much of this going on, probably right in your midst, even of course if you yourself don't practice it. And Christian spirit, oh boy! What a lack of it these days in proportion to a pro rata comparison of just only a quarter of a century ago!

The next time you go to church, (if you do), gaze about you during the prayer and will you, or will you not be surprised to notice that you are also being given the once over by more than one. Perhaps they are however only sizing you up, rather your style of dress, and they are doing anything but reverently praying or concentrating on spiritual things. (12)

Do you in these days of bewildering excitement among the worldly wise find much of Christian fellowship? In your daily contact with your brethren do you ever discuss God and prayer? Are there still group gatherings at the homes of friends where you pray weekly as you did, or knew of others who did only thirty years ago? At your home, or when visiting friends, how often is Grace said before partaking of your daily bread? Well think it over, perhaps I do take life too seriously, perhaps I am crazy but to me they are significant of man's growing neglect of God's presence and they certainly do not help to stop the spread of Atheism, in fact they breed it after a while. 

It seems today that only in times of dire stress, war or destruction, the loss of loved ones, calamity, chaos and tragedy, do we take refuge in a higher source. Must it always be that famine, flood, disaster and grief must come before we seek refuge, succor or divine aid, or wouldn't it be better to be worthy of the prevention of God's wrath when man's negligence, greed, moral degradation and his inhumanity to his own brethren cause God's wrath? Indeed a God all mercy would be an unjust God.

Only three years ago in New London, Texas, the greatest school disaster in our history was cause for mourning of the world. When we think of that tragedy in which 294 pupils and teachers lost their lives, when we think of the suffering and heartaches of the parents, relatives and friends, who were left behind in their grief, when we think that somewhere there was negligence or greed of man to tap the gas which caused it, and when we think that this terrible tragedy is but an infinitesimal part of what's going on in the world, and which can cause tragedies which would make that insignificant in comparison, then perhaps without urge you might try and realize the plight of Austria, Czechoslovakia, Poland and Finland and do something about it! And Norway, Belgium and Holland. Who next is your guess, but action is paramount for us all.

How this town in Texas recovered, how the pastor of the church witnessed a religious revival when grief stricken parents turned to prayer and the scriptures for solace, is but a reminder to us all that in similar circumstances we know there is only one source of refuge. 

The world at large, good people, criminals, and yea, even gangsters of the lowest type like dictators then turn to God with alacrity, no matter how they professed non-belief before. 

And so in war, nations as with individuals, find in defeat the will (13)

to live on anew in hopes that past chaos by the grace of God will never return. Always it seems that after something happens there is abundant of God in the air, but in comparison very little of it before. 

You will shortly read the letter which I sent to President Roosevelt on Christmas day, remembering that a lawyer and a judge because of this plan said I was crazy, and that a famous doctor said I took life too seriously. Whether their opinions of me are worth while, I am afraid you shall decide after you have read the book, and should you too say I take life too seriously, and also that I am crazy, then I shall have to take consolation in these words of John Dryden; "Great wits are sure to madness near allied, And thin partitions do their bounds divide."

THE WHY AND WHEREFORE OF MY LETTER TO THE PRESIDENT 

That the reader may refresh his memory, and for the benefit of those who on Christmas Day were suffering the consequences of sacrilegiously celebrating the birth of Christ in the too ignominious style of inebriety, but who, before they have digested thoroughly the contents of this book, will I HOPE, realize there could be error in their ways, I here include Mr. Roosevelt's Christmas message to the

Pope, in the form as printed in some of the Sunday papers of December 24, 1939. 

In a dramatic Christmas Eve move to promote "Peace on earth, good will toward men," President Roosevelt today raised high the standard of all western religions:-Protestant, Catholic and Jewish, and asked their leaders to co-operate with those in government who share a common purpose to end war's destruction. 

The President announced he had sent Christmas letters carrying the thought to the Pope as head of the Catholic Church, to Dr. 

George A. Buttrick, president of the Federal Council of Churches of Protestants in America, and to Rabbi Cyrus Adler, president of the Jewish Theological Seminary of America, a leader of the Jewish faith.

The faith of common men will yet bring peace despite, “the grief and terror of the hour," the President told all three. Because the

Protestant and Jewish faiths possess no such central seat of authority as the Vatican, the President requested Dr. Buttrick and Rabbi (14)

Adler to visit him from time to time to discuss with him the problems arising out of the world situation. "When the time shall come for the re-establishment of world peace on a surer foundation, it is of the utmost importance to humanity and to religion that common ideals shall have united expression," the President wrote. "I trust that all the churches of the world which believe in a common God will throw the great weight of their influence in this great cause.' 

"The defense of religion, of democracy and of good faith among nations is all the same fight. To save one we must now make up our minds to save all."

Not since George Washington had a President of this nation linked religion and democracy so closely. He testified movingly to his belief in the common man and a common faith. He reminded the three religious leaders that "the Christmas Star was first seen by shepherds in the hills, long before the leaders knew of the great light which had entered the world." "I believe that while statesmen are considering a new order of things, the new order may well be at hand," went on Mr. Roosevelt. "I believe that it is even now being built, silently but inevitably, in the hearts of masses whose voices are not heard, but whose common faith will write the final chapter in the history of our time." "It is well," he continued, “that the world should think of this at CHRISTMAS." 

The President admitted that at the moment No Spiritual or Civil Leader could move forward on a specific plan for peace, but confidently asserted that the hour will surely come for spiritual and civil leaders to build anew. "It is well," he continued, "that we encourage a closer association between those in Every Part of the World:-Those in religion and those in government:—we have a common purpose." 

To the Pope the President phrased his invitation: "I am, therefore, suggesting to your Holiness that it would give me great satisfaction to send to you my personal representative in order that our parallel endeavors for peace and the alleviation of suffering may be assisted."

That in brief is the President's message on Christmas to his Holiness, the Pope, and knowing that a President receives a deluge of [15]

mail, also with the knowledge that much of it because of minor nature quickly ends in the waste basket, I felt secure in writing my congressman asking him if he would do me the favor of transmitting my letter to the White House.

I was gratified to shortly receive his answer, in which he wrote that he had taken means to see that Mr. Stephen Early, secretary to the 

President had received it. I also received acknowledgment from 

Mr. Early and I felt that my efforts thus far had been amply rewarded. 

It is months since that letter was received and I live in hopes that Mr. Roosevelt will personally answer it, but meanwhile I have been just about sick trying to present it to the public. Last summer I sent in sum and substance the same letter to prominent papers in New York City, Chicago and Los Angeles, but none of them printed it and my recourse to the book my final attempt that people may read of it, digest the thought, and decide for themselves if it is a crazy notion or a thought worth while. To me it is the only way, especially in view of mans previous failures to do much about the too many vital problems of an upset world. 

A thought I would leave with you before you read my letter: 

Would our great, great Grandfolks thought one crazy had someone told them that such things as electric lights and the telephone would come?

Would our great Grandfolks thought the person crazy who told them that the turn of the century would bring the horseless carriage and that Henry Ford was NOT crazy?

And now in 1940. Who at the turn of the century would have believed that machines heavier than air would actually fly, that China clippers would transport humans, express, and mail across the vast Pacific? 

Would Professor Goddard of Clark University be called crazy if he told us that man will ultimately fly to the moon in rockets? Would it be entirely fantastic to visualize trips to Mars and Venus in centuries to come?

In view of past strides in the field of invention and science, we would probably believe this latter prediction a great deal quicker than would our ancestors have believed the then called fantastic prophecies but which have actually come to pass. 

So what? How often in view of the fact that every thing you see other than that produced by nature, every building, locomotive, [16]

motor car, radio, ships in the sky, liners of the seas, unlimited achievements of man, have you said to yourself, anything is possible? 

And who in this world many times, what individual, what nation, what color, creed or race faced with adversity, calamity, discouragement and with their backs to the wall, would not desire to start at that land of beginning again, as so graciously written by Mrs. Booth 

Tarkington:-

In disclosing my plans as outlined in this book, many have remarked that it is too stupendous and that human nature cannot be changed. However, to abide by my suggestions it requires no huge sacrifice by humanity and millions of individuals have conquered in proportion just as great obstacles as the present barriers to peace between nations.

Who is there to say that when we set out to achieve some goal that it will fail to come to pass? If so, I would remind you that before

And we have the plans completely drawn, we have the motive and we have ample means to propel this machine which would trample

And as the machine rolls on, it will automatically end poverty and destitution, famine for millions when others have plenty, revive sincere Christian spirit and a Democracy of the world will become as real as have the radio, the aeroplane and television. 

World democracy or bedlam is the option. Which shall we close on? What, if it's not bedlam approaches the world at a fast clip, in view of the chaos now rampant? God's will be done or bedlam is not too fantastic for us to vision, and that in the near future, if present trends are a criterion. Surely if what I suggest in the following letter to the President were to come to pass, War and destruction, with all the major evils of the world would perish and bedlam would cease forever!

The situation looms realistically before us, as witness the unsolved problems of the entire world. They DEMAND recourse to a higher source for solving, in view of man's feeble efforts to find the remedy which is available, but which he has failed to try. 

I earnestly plead with the reader that after each paragraph of the following letter to Mr. Roosevelt, you pause for mental exploration into their significance as you yourself may see them. Surely you are aware of the power of prayer as an individual, then just as surely are you aware of the mighty power of a simultaneous prayer to God by millions upon millions, embodying virtually all mankind. Multiply the power of your prayer by a billion and a half, and there you have approximately the sum of the power of a prayer sincerely and reverently partaken in by these multitudes. 

After all previous efforts of mankind had failed, would God neglect us in this mighty appeal? How possible, if we are unworthy, that he would have much reason to be indifferent, but also how probable if we proved worthy, that he would, in his infinite wisdom, give us succor and help us to overcome the obstacles which are barriers before success?

These things, dear readers are for YOU to dwell on and seriously consider.

May it give you worthy cause to give it the due consideration it deserves, in all its potentialities! [19]

If my ideas are crazy, then I must be foolish, and until you decide, I shall derive consolation in:- 

"The most foolish book is a kind of a leaky boat on a sea of wisdom; some of the wisdom will get in anyhow." 

OLIVER WENDELL HOLMES. 

MY LETTER TO THE PRESIDENT:- 

Christmas Day, 1939.

His Excellency, Franklin D. Roosevelt, 

President of the United States of America; 

Washington, D. C. 

My dear Excellency: 

In view of no specific plan at present for the solution of the many tense problems existing in this upset world, I respectfully take this opportunity of presenting to you, one which was inspired by a dream during the Czech crisis, one which seemed so realistic as to bear token of actuality if it inspires the interest necessary by you, to promote its inference.

I felt I was too humble, and because I was not a great man, I feared the world would pay little attention to me for a suggestion inspired but by a dream. I was reluctant to write you or the various religious leaders, until reading in yesterday's Sunday papers that you had no specific plan. This prompted me to write, hoping you will take sufficient interest to sponsor such a plan. There is so much to it all that it would require a letter tantamount to a book, however I shall here condense the thought your Excellency, that you may not be burdened with a too lengthy letter.

When we think of the evils of the world the dictator's and war stand out predominantly, but if only my plans were to be fulfilled, I sincerely believe it would eliminate through Divine aid, much of other evils too rampant and too numerous to mention here. We 

Christians believe in God, we believe in devout prayer and sincere faith, but do we expect many times that He will answer these prayers when we have proved unworthy? (20)

Your Excellency, I humbly crave your ardent perusal of the following synopsis of these plans, which were developments created following the dream. I resolved to try and interest the world at large through the offices of a great man, what seems to me to be the solution to, or improvement in, conditions existing in this tense world. 

If we find consolation and help in prayer, if we feel that in it there is a Divine inspiration and a guidance obtained from no other source, something I have found to be absolutely true during a crisis in my life, I pray it will attract your attention, and in turn that you will gain approval of religious leaders throughout the world. I have classified them as plans A, B and C:—

Plan A:-To be put into action as soon as humanly possible that it may avert further disaster to humanity, and that the aggressors future war strategy may go awry, and while we still have enough Christian morality left to WARRANT asking God's aid.

It would be a silent prayer by all Christian peoples, this at a SIMULTANEOUSLY stated hour, set by religious leaders. A prayer that would engage the fervent pleas to God by nearly seven hundred million Christians at the same identical moment would be a prayer of such magnitude as to bear testimony of Christianity's faith, and proof of the power of this Divine faith would SURELY be forthcoming!

Plan B: An improvement on Plan A in that it would be a prayer led by different religious leaders of Christian Nations at a simultaneous moment, by means of a huge radio broadcast. His Holiness the Pope to direct the Catholics, Rabbi Adler for the Jews, and I sincerely hope that the Protestants would not tarry too long in choosing their representative. In view of their objection to your choice of an envoy to the Pope, my memory stimulates youthful reminiscences picturing some as "hard shelled." I'm a Baptist myself, but this reference is implied solely as a reminder. 

The time most desirous by Christians would in all probability be Easter or Christmas, and due to the difference in time in different countries, this important consideration could be ironed out and an agreeable hour suitable to all could be arranged. 

God is ever ready to listen to devout prayer, and Christmas eve, Bethlehem time would be fitting and significant. It would give ample time to awaken the world to the mighty significance of a simultaneous (21)

prayer of nearly seven hundred million Christians, led by Christian Leaders through this radio broadcast. 

You may be sure Excellency, that meanwhile people would wake up to the fact that it would be advisable for them to correct their own ills, that they be worthy to participate in this mighty gesture and supreme effort for permanent peace on earth, good will to men. In case of a sudden crisis threatening cataclysm, people should see in this suggestion that it was time to act and not think. In this event, such a prayer could become reality in a short time, and as a last resort if necessary, what person would fail to take opportunity of appealing to the only source which COULD help us? In the time it takes to announce plans over the air, via the press and by word of mouth, religious leaders could ably set a time and a last world crisis would be avoided.

Plan C: On the impulse might in its immensity seem impossible, but can we, your Excellency, neglect in plans for world peace, ignore the more than a billion other souls who are not Christians? There is not a nation, color, creed or race who do not believe in a Supreme being and a form of Divine worship. 

Be they Catholic or Protestant, Jew or Mohammedan, Hindu or

Buddhist, Confucianist or those of other miscellaneous beliefs; whether they worship at home, be it ever so humble, in a church, a synagogue, or be it Ethiopians in the jungle or Arabs on the desert, ardent and devout prayer has been a source of Divine inspiration from time immemorable. While it be Allah to millions, or Buddha to countless others, it is God to us and we all worship the same God. 

The masses of these nations crave peace as do we, and ultimately a democracy of the world in my opinion is not too fantastic to aspire to. 

Therefore, after a series of conferences, the religious leaders of these various creeds could be included in this huge vast broadcast of prayer for the peace and happiness of all humanity, which God meant us to have, but which as yet, man has so miserably failed to achieve. 

A plan of this immensity has ramifications which are multiple, but unless we find a remedy for the listlessness with which the world takes this ever widening breach between nations, something catastrophic to all humanity looms more than vaguely before us. 

Diverse opinions as to its practicability might be rampant, Atheists might jump at it and claim that if permanent peace was not achieved [22]

through this appeal that it was sure proof there was no God and bedlam would be the final result.

But: When final preparations of leaders chosen to sponsor this plan had been completed, you can just bet that the vast majority of all humanity, all nations, colors, creeds and races would enter into it with sincere reverence. Flocks of Arabs would journey to Mecca, millions in India, China and Japan, other millions of peasants would assemble in Churches, all had joined in bowing in allegiance to their various Gods which are one. Think of it your Excellency, hundreds upon hundreds of millions of souls! 

The mighty significance, the immensity of it, great enough to create a God for those who did not worship because they lacked faith. 

Millions who a few months before had felt unworthy, had in the intervening time abided in God, his words had abided in them, and knowing this, they asked what they would and it was given them. 

With continuance of this yearly appeal to God, Eternal peace to mankind was granted because those unworthy had made themselves worthy, they had become God-fearing, Allah conscious or Buddha minded to the extent of bending low in reverence to the one God who rules supreme. At a time of such stupendous moment, more than one feared that God might arise in his wrath and a day of retribution might be in the offing. Because of this much of evil had vanished. 

It has great possibilities for the salvation of the human race, especially in view of mankind's futile effort. It also packs dynamite, and a mighty repercussion could be caused if by chance God paid no heed to this mighty plan for guidance. However there would be slim chance of this would we prove worthy.

Dictators would, if we proved unworthy, use its failure as a bludgeon with which to destroy civilization, but they along with the lowest type of criminal, even if they defy all religion, would in time of dire crisis when death stares them in the face, turn to God with a rapidity they never before knew was possible. Therefore any serious alarm on their account would be nil against the fervent and ardent desire of millions in their own country. 

Your Excellency, during the dream which causes me to try and make real its potential possibilities, the beautiful music of Franz 

Schubert's Unfinished Symphony and his Ave Maria, continuously rendered a sublime background of indescribable soul inspiring music and melody. {23)

So, in realistic fulfillment, should it come to pass, what greater inspiration as a background for a prayer of this kind? Music hath powers to soothe the savage beast, so also hath it the power to soothe the savage breast of men who would slaughter, enslave and torture their own blood. Those decadent in Christian spirit would relive the days of youth when they were good. Through music God has given us a medium of expression comparable only to those of Divine heritage. It has the abundant power to make more realistic and inspiring those prayers in which representatives of God would lead us. Through the masters we have music to sway the hearts of all men, as have the voices of Saintly men who speak.

Through unemployment and the discouragement that went with it, a near tragedy in my own life, brought about by indulgence in alcoholic drinks, caused me to strive my utmost to atone. As we sow, SO shall we reap, had became a vital philosophy to me. I had sowed badly and I had reaped badly, therefore I have sincere faith that I am now sowing well, and shall reap well. With ardent prayer as my main effort. I have been able to conquer this evil which has killed more humans than have all the wars of history combined. 

If I, as an individual, can conquer this, the greatest tragedy of man, then also nations like men, can conquer and overcome all obstacles via the same method, devout prayer and a sincere Christian spirit.

God in his all seeing wisdom does not mean for man to sacrifice all his pleasures, nor for the person who can intelligently drink in moderation, to give it up. He does not mean to abolish such things as Sunday baseball, nor a myriad of other harmless pleasures which are not soul destroying, but I firmly believe he means for us to abolish those evils which he so graciously demands in the ten commandments. 

If this plan is adopted, elimination of lesser evils will come of man's own desire to be worthy of grant, and the major problems of alcohol, war and crime will come through fear of God's just retribution. 

Would it be asking too much your Excellency, for your sentiments on my suggestions as herein outlined in condensed form? If you approve, would you divulge its contents to various religious leaders. 

Excellency, the world today looks to you for guidance and suggestions for obtaining world peace and happiness. Great leaders of nations, great men of religion, and the masses even of the totalitarian states seek refuge in you as the most important man of the moment {24)

in the world. If only you will see the potentialities of this plan to bring "Peace On Earth, Good Will To Men," I know that my efforts have not been in vain. 

Sincerely your humble servant,

GEORGE W. Adams

Please excuse the length of this letter, but I so wanted to explain it as fully as possible in condensed form, and while I see it as a hope for salvation from the present tense world situation, I would be proud to hear your own sentiments.

I truly feel that in presenting it to you, I have also presented it to the only man I feel could gain ardent attention to any plan to end war's destruction, and bring about a renewed Christian spirit with a universal desire for permanent peace to mankind. 

A United States of Europe would be a move in the right direction, but a democracy of the Universe would be a better one. NOTHING is impossible via the RIGHT way, your Excellency, and here's hoping that one dream did inspire that which in embryo will become full grown.

That in view of these facts, also in view of the whole world's belief in general in a Supreme Being and prayer, with knowledge beyond all doubt that there is unknown power of inconceivable magnitude in our humble faith if only we are worthy, that as a last resort if necessary, religious leaders and leaders of nations, statesmen and fraternal organizations get behind this suggestion and as quickly as humanly possible, select a sponsor! UNEMPLOYED; AND SOBER 

I am one of the army of unemployed. I crave no riches, I desire no mansion and neither do I expect or want a life of luxury. What I do want, above everything else at the present time, is work. I am not particular as to what kind of work so long as it is honest labor. For wages I want only what will enable me to marry, to have a home be it ever so humble and enough to live modestly. To be able to donate conservatively to my church and to charity, to give my wife and family those things which I think God meant them to have, and through the happiness that comes with it, to maintain my Christian. spirit which at times through discouragement is in jeopardy. 

I am not envious of the rich, nor do I covet the prosperity of my neighbor. When I stand on the curb of millionaires row and see them go by in custom built cars with liveried chauffers, I do not envy them, rather do I admire their sagacity in having acquired their riches, especially if they are self made men and honest. [34]

As I look about me, as I try to keep up through reading of current events, and as I listen often to world famous commentators on foreign entanglements, I am quite content that my country seems to prosper as a whole in comparison.

However I can see as well as others, and expressed by countless members of union organizations, that there is an increasing silent and subtle influence permeating our traditional and successful system. 

This army of unemployed, of which I am a full fledged member, has no suggestions as to what shall be done to remedy this situation, that it is in the hands of the government which has had greater obstacles to overcome than any administration in our history. 

We know that there is an army with whom we at present are unequipped to triumph over. That is the army of politicians, and God grant that they may soon be replaced with great statesmen. We need them, and oh how we need them.

Until then, I pray that before man's inhumanity to man is abolished through Divine guidance which can only come through your plan, that people will realize its full significance, and act on it, when they remember that all past attempts to cure the ills of the world have been futile.

Good luck, and here's looking forward to the half century mark when if your plan is adopted, the world will be a better place to live in, and the common people of Germany and Russia, who are good people, will for the first time in decades, enjoy life and know it's worth living.

INANIMATE BUT ANIMATE DICTATOR 

I am in the hands of a dictator, a silent sinister dictator, one which in an all conquering desire for power works in a manner equally potent with living dictators.

First, it is quite easy to fall victim to its insidious lure, falsely helping you to find kind refuge, especially when you are discouraged, melancholy or down-trodden. It temporarily gives you relief from your woes, whether or not it kills you and thousands of others in the process. To appease you regardless of how slight, or how immense are your troubles, it offers hilarity you can find from no other source. 

Of course, we know it kills more people than have all the wars of history, but that dire fact lessens its destruction but little, therefore [35]

showing you the vastness of its domain. Its powers of helping me to evade the real issues at stake ranks equally with the subtle intoxicating lust of living dictators, who also will never be satisfied with just enough. Everything or nothing is its motto, and probably the living dictators knowing of its success in the suffering and destruction of mankind, are trying to duplicate it, for they too care nothing about killing men if it serves their purpose. 

For a time, however, I had youth and I was able to combat it with physical and mental stamina, and it allowed me to see the world through rose colored glasses as a very fine world to live in. At times when it controlled my system to its limit, I thought it the fountain of youth and the elixir of life and as an earthly Utopia, but always with the morning after discovery that it was all illusionary. 

But after all, thank God I am a man, and I begin to see the futility of enslaving myself to a dictator which craves absolute power over my earthly rights. I am arising in my wrath and am sick and tired of its tactics, I am awakening to its wiles and the duplicity and treachery with which it inveigles me, and its absolute desire even if not necessary, to slay me, but while I am still able I will turn on it, as will my brethren also revolt against those living dictators who have forced to succumb the good people who never did want their wars.

I am on the offensive and shall conquer this demon in disguise. The awakening of my spirit was obtained only through prayer, and I have pledged myself to this king alcohol's downfall, that I may live the rest of the days of my life much as God intended that I should. 

I am but a lowly mortal, but I am another who knows that if I, but an individual force in this world, can conquer and obtain victory over such a Gargantuan dictator as alcohol in all its wicked forms, then also I am confident simultaneous prayer by many millions of mortals will surely bring about a victory for peace to the world through the downfall of dictators, who like alcohol never let up until their goal of world domination is true fact. 

INTELLECTUALS FALL TOO? 

When men of my type, men in the higher professions and of the arts and sciences think well of your idea, but a bit skeptical as to its results if adopted, it seems to me you have something which at least [36]

gives us serious food for thought. It demands attention, especially when several of my colleagues feel a lot like I do regarding their own lack of a preparation to be worthy of a material answer for their part in the consummation of your plans.

I am a lawyer and I rate high in my profession, but I can plainly see that if your plans are accepted, that a tension would be bound to exist, worrying not only the dictators, politicians and munitions makers, but millions who up to now had thought but little of being fit. Take me for instance, when I tell you of one case which bothers me no little at the present time due to qualms of conscience created by your suggestion.

I have defended a boy who was being tried for murder. It was my life, my ambition to be a super criminal lawyer and in my mad desire for conquest on this, my most important case, I relentlessly squelched his accusers. I made the district attorney and the whole prosecution for the state look sick, and the feelings of the mother, the father or relatives and friends of the slain girl, did not at the time bother my scruples of conscience.

Now as I think the whole affair over, your plans have given me the shivers and I feel necessity of changing my mode of defending or prosecuting clients and confining myself strictly to the defense of those whom I know to be innocent. This case of which I speak is one which now brings tortures of conscience which during the trial were unstricken, even when I knew my client to be guilty of that murder. Many hours I spent in thought after he was acquitted, thinking mainly that I had won a victory which meant a rapid rise to fame as an outstanding criminal lawyer, and I was made. 

Witnesses for the defense who had taken oath to tell the truth, and nothing but the truth lied deliberately at my own instruction. I had coached them and diligently spent hours in telling them how to cope with traps and answer questions when cross-examination by counsel for the prosecution third degreed them, and I had won. Yes indeed, Injustice triumphed! It's no source of satisfaction that I am but one who casually does these things.

It's no consolation now when I look back on it all and it's no solace I find in going to church every Sunday. Verily, I am beginning to see it is anything but. I accepted fifty thousand dollars for handling that case. His parents are wealthy and I cater mostly to that class, refusing many cases where I knew the prisoner to be innocent because ₤37)

they could not afford my services. Alas! I now see to just what extent Gold was my God, but I also fear I am typical of too many hundreds of men in my profession throughout the world. 

Many of my intimate friends are corporation counsel who are rich, and they and I always felt we were respected and of high character even during victory of dubious nature. 

When I now think of some of the deals that they have put over, of the millions of dollars that through their advice and handling have been diverted to investments which were literally phony, of the suicides and suffering which can directly be attributed to their legal advice and suggestion for investment during the crash, it is easy to see that they rank equally low with me in morals. 

The way things are going on in the world today makes it seem that inevitably war will come to us. A war which might end war this time by ending civilization, therefore as there has as yet been no solution, surely your plan is worth while for trial, and as far as I am concerned we might mention in preparation, the betterment of my kind, as well as all who are in collusion with crooked judges, corrupt officials and politicians who are stooges for the underworld. 

And I would impress upon the minds of the readers of your book in case you include my sentiments on the matter, that I intend no reflections to be cast upon the thousands of honest law abiding members of the bar associations. This simply expresses my own opinion and I firmly believe many others of us are conscience stricken all of a sudden because of the possibilities and potential retribution which might develop during the fulfillment of your plans. 

Dictators will worry, but so will we unless we atone as far as possible for past mistakes!

IS RELIGION A FARCE? 

I welcome your plan with more than just ordinary interest for many reasons. In the first place I do not believe in religion or any of its ramifications for the simple reason that in all the years of history, nothing of a substantial nature has proved that there was a God, or that Christ was the son of God. I do not believe in the slightest that firm believers find it a remover of sin and suffering in their own ranks, or that its followers are blessed with divine happiness any more than do atheists receive what gifts there are to mankind. {38}

Real Christians throughout the world do not outnumber those of other beliefs, along with those who are of doubtful faith, and if so there are too many would be Christians who sadly neglect to live up to half of their Lord's Ten Commandments, and this more than makes up for the good people who do. Any thought of realistic help from your plan is a farce.

On the other hand, we atheists and all of doubtful reliance in this modern day religion, welcome prayer in unity as a means to find out from results if there is a Supreme being and if he will pay any attention to you. You can be sure that if there is a God he would not be a just 

God to ignore the pleas of many millions of those made in his own image. 

And it wouldn't be a very hearty laugh that would brighten the faces of Christians if it proved a fiasco. I am not hard hearted enough to wish its failure, for I love living, and by no means do I desire a negative result, but because many of my friends have tried to influence me toward faith and religion with no basis of fact to stand on, I have been indifferent these many years. 

If there were enough real honest to goodness people in this world, enough to be worthy of a God such as we picture one could be, it seems to me that they could create through this plan a more worthy reason for a just God to act. If I were a Christian I'd think seriously of brushing up on my own morals before expecting any just God would answer me. Dictators alone are not the only ones you need to stop, and I do believe your imagination holds great possibilities of causing a panic the like of which has never before been seen, if people are dumb enough to attempt this massive gesture without proper preparation. Believe me, from what I have seen in half a century, they'd better hurry because their sins can't be obliterated in a week, or a month or a year, unless they take this idea with grave import, and cut out their sacrilegious living. 

Woe to us all if no good comes of it, and as for myself it will make no material change in my philosophy, but I am inclined to think millions will join with the atheist doctrines and it will mean the loss of countless believers in Christ and an invitation for a rapid ride down the open road that leads to hell for millions! 

Withal my doctrine, I am forced to admit as Edmund Burke said, that writers against religion, whilst they oppose every system, are wisely careful never to set up any of their own. Well I'm in that 139

DO YOU DRIVE A CAR SOBER? 

Alcohol as a beverage in relation to Health and Longevity has been a source of debate and a most discussed subject since time immemorable. Countless books have been written and numberless lectures delivered by critics, evangelists and the clergy at large. 

It is a contention with me that there are many factors entering such a broad issue as to the merits or evils of. alcohol, that certain facts should be born out by those who have actually been in contact with the problem. After all is said and done, and believe me that's plenty, it is no more than right that the drinker should be included with the tee-totaler, the clergy or any critic, when it comes to denunciation of alcohol as a beverage.

A tee-totaler or members of various dry organizations cannot possibly tell us in a convincing way how to combat the evils of drink when their argument is based solely upon theory or statistics. 

However, the confirmed addict, the so-called lowly drunkard and the millions of people who imbibe in moderation, along with periodic drinkers can intelligently give us information based on experience. 

Surely experience is the greatest of teachers, and would that we all could profit more by those of others rather than to have to go through them ourselves before learning thoroughly, the truth. Those experiences which hold such an element of danger as the drink question would not be ones which the youth of our country would hanker after as at present, if broader education in the schools would be nationally adopted.

In justice to every type of drinker, be they a nipper, periodic, chronic or a confirmed drunkard, there are amongst them many whose powers of intellect were formerly unquestioned, and this in itself shows the subtle intoxicating power of a devil personified. 

While critics seem divided on the evils or merits of drinking in moderation, there is a vast difference of opinion as to just what constitutes this so-called moderation. Witness in your own experiences, the different amounts of liquor which will be consumed by various persons, each claiming the boast of being well able to handle it in moderation. What is one man's food is another man's poison, aptly applies to the problem of alcohol as well. [40]

There is, however, one angle of this world problem upon which all writers, critics, tee-totalers and the addicts themselves are wholly agreed upon. That is the fact that chronic over-indulgence is a time proven and well-known short cut to the cemetery. Periodic sprees are but the rudiments in the course leading to your certificate in the chronic club, and when you are a full fledged member, you also are eligible to join the suicide club from which the grave beckons. 

Albeit the claims of countless makers of alcoholic beverages, with their resort to artifice in advertising, the grim realistic reminder of numberless tombstones over the graves of the victims of this Satan in disguise, bears ample testimony of its tragedy. Perhaps you can number the bodies of some of your own relatives and friends which lie under the sod that alcohol built. 

Perhaps you are intimately acquainted with a Doctor or a Lawyer or a Banker who have successfully coped with the problem common to alcoholics, and you often refer to this thought and find in it a solace, strengthening your own philosophy that after all if they are able to do it, why shouldn't you also? Perhaps after a day of tense mental work at the office, or after a day of strenuous labor, you yourself will indulge, seeking a welcome relief which seems a glass of liquor will bring.

For every person who can successfully do this there are countless thousands who cannot stop with a glass, or two or three, and they daily increase this moderation, with the final result they find they have been subtly deceived. What was formerly sufficient is no longer enough and a vicious cycle has been established. 

A famous doctor once told me that he no longer prescribed any alcoholic beverage as a tonic except in extremely rare cases. He was also reluctant to prescribe patent medicines containing a too high percentage in alcoholic content. He found through actual treatment of his patients that these tonics were more apt to be habit forming than cures.

There are numerous articles in the papers of people going suddenly beserk, and tragic happenings follow. Very often it states that the person had previously suffered from a nervous ailment or recently been discharged from a hospital for mental diseases. Nine out of ten times there is no mention that drink was encountered in any of the phases of investigation. The more prominent the name the less likely you would see any mention of the victims weakness for liquor. {41}

Originally I meant this to be but a letter, but when I dwell on the problems of alcohol as a beverage, there comes into my mind so many tragic memories that I verily believe I could write a goodsized book on the question, however I shall from here on confine myself strictly to its relation to drunken driving, the shame and crime of which counteracts the casualties we don't have because we are lucky enough not to be at war.

It is indeed a paradox to read among obituary notices a beautiful eulogy on a prominent person involved in a tragic auto accident, the cause of which was the drivers, and you are personally aware that he was drunk. You will invariably find that the radio announcer will seldom speak of, nor the paper print reference to the drivers state of inebriety. If they only would remind us more often that many of these accidents were the direct result of indulgence in liquor, albeit WHO, there would sooner become instilled in our minds a fear of potential death every time we step into a car to be driven by a driver who drinks even in so-called moderation.

Statistics beyond all criticism have proved conclusively that even one or two glasses of beer or wine dulls the perspective and the mental alertness necessary in a time of crises which you are bound to meet when driving.

The saddest tragedy of drunken driving is the involvement of persons who are entirely innocent of any part in it. How often in a collision, the fault of a drunken driver who goes head on into a car, containing people who drank in their lives not a drop of alcohol? 

Reliable statistics of past occurrences of this nature would shock you in their toll of injury and death. You may rest assured that were the man or woman drunk and responsible for any of these gruesome tragedies yet able to, they would I believe gladly proclaim to the world this warning that through alcohol, the grim reaper had caught up with them, and had cut off the lives of other good people, ere it was ready for harvest. In the hopes that it would prevent similar accidents they would surely feel some sense of atonement in broadcasting and publishing this warning that it would save a life in the future, even as you and I would do.

There is no doubt in my mind, but what slowly but surely a decrease in the number of accidents of this kind would be in the offing if in every case of a tragedy through drunken driving there would be [42]

forcefully driven home to us the element of danger in mixing alcohol and gasoline.

If we could read unbiased articles which stated the truth and nothing but the truth, reminders of this kind would soon awaken us to the sinister influences of driving when drinking even in moderation. 

If you realize the deep significance involved, that any plan for improvement in world conditions would be futile without some interest shown in liquor problems, I will be happy in knowing that I have driven home my point, and a prayer to the Almighty through your plan would be lacking plenty, if it did not include reference to the ever increasing evils of alcohol as a beverage.

Would that the multitudes now a border case on the liquor problem, and would that those youth who are tempted, read and digest the significance of the immortal verses of Edward Fitzgerald in his Rubaiyat of Omar Khayyam, "Ah, my Beloved, fill the cup that clears

DICTATORSHIP IN THE AMERICAS? 

What if not dictatorship exists in parts of our own Americas?

While making ourselves worthy that we may expect Divine aid in solving the many problems of the world it would be well for us to consider the sad plight of millions in our own hemisphere. I speak especially of our neighbors in Latin South America. 

If only one would stop to think about it more seriously, it is a crime that seven per cent of the entire population own all the land and completely control the governments of the ten countries which make up South America.

Democracy means nothing to them, they are not allowed to vote and while given the use of a little land to raise what crops it is absolutely necessary for them to live on, they receive scant pay, if any. 

They make all their own clothing and are grossly neglected in any mode of advancement in education.

As in medieval times there is virtually no middle class, the peons own no land and work on the great estates as did the serfs of the middle ages. The huge peasantry is not far removed from serfdom. 

Illiteracy is rampant throughout these countries and the true 56)

Peruvian culture has vanished. The Spanish conquerors who demolished the wonderful Empire of the Incas have left in their wake one of the saddest declines in history. The most advanced civilizations of the western world, the Inca Empire in Peru and the Mayan culture in Mexico are gone. With man's inhumanity to man abolished, and establishment of educational and social culture inaugurated even at this late day, the rehabilitation of the Indians could plan a major part in making Latin America a worthy rival of North America in the field of true Democracy.

It is only with shame that our own United States should look at its past record in dealings with South America. It is our big business with enormous capital invested which has helped to control the existing forms of government. Governments which ignore the plight of the masses, and many members of which bask in the sun on the Riviera and gamble at Monte Carlo, while millions back home endure a life of virtual slavery. 

It is no wonder that there has been an immense feeling of ill will toward us when we realize that we enjoy the liberties of democracy but have done nothing to encourage improvement in the condition today existing in South America for the lowly masses. 

Efforts toward Pan Americanism and good will tours have no doubt helped to weld all the Americas into a strong bond of friendship, but I sincerely hope that due consideration of the abominable state of culture and living conditions for the poor will become a part of our appeal to the only source which will prevent inevitable bedlam. 

If in your itinerary you could include visits to Germany, Russia and then some of the countries in South America, compare living conditions and what if not dictatorship exists on that score in all three countries?

CHRISTIAN SPIRIT IN WASHINGTON? 

When you intimate there is much of evil in this world besides. dictators and war, why not consider what I believe to be plenty of lack of sincere Christian principle in some past, and the present administration of our own country?

This, luckily so far, is a free country and a free press and free speech is at your disposal. The men in power may be big, they may be famous, but technically they are our servants, they are but human [57]

as you and I. While we all make mistakes, some of which are trivial and probably not worthy of mention, there are many more which are virtually tragic in their consequences.

When I mention Mr. Roosevelt, I do not literally mean to cast reflections alone upon him, for by now the world knows that he is far from being solely responsible for the administration's failure to keep practically every major promise they made during the hectic campaign days preceding election.

And the present government is not alone if it comes to one of unquestioned integrity. Mr. Hoover as a humanitarian during the world war endeared him to the hearts of not only Belgium, but the world at large. At present he again comes into the limelight and truly deserves the respect and love that multitudes have for him. On the other hand, he might believe an ounce of prevention may be better than a pound of cure, but it seems to me that he failed to heed that philosophy and the handwriting on the wall in the late twenties must have indeed been hard to decipher. He also was not alone, capital and politicians knew that prosperity was on the decline in plenty of time to have done something more specific about it. The subsequent decline of republican support in 1932 proved beyond all doubt the extent of the massive blunder of unpreparedness. 

Neither can we say that Mr. Hoover created this condition. Mr. 

Coolidge who so ably administered law and order in Boston with only the police system at stake, could it seems to me have done more about law and order in Wall street. Essentially as important there, it seems to me that a president should have bucked capital and the wolves and bears of the street, and whatever else may be said of Mr. 

Roosevelt, give him plenty of credit for showing his cards on that stock market deal. 

Was President Hoover asleep at the switch with two tracks open, one to maintain prosperity and the other which was apparently easier to take, sliding into the down grade toward depression? Surely men so great should have the foresight to see calamity ahead when there were plenty of warnings and dire predictions openly made by men in the know and many of whose prophecies have truly come to pass!

As in the war, why did Norway and Sweeden refuse the help of the allies for little Finland? Now in the throes of battle themselves, they realize too late their lack of foresight. As with the whole set-up since (58)

Munich, it's always too late, so in government here Whittier's "For all Sad Words of Tongue or Pen, The Saddest are These:-It Might have been," are words which if conscientious, the present administration must often think of. Is Humanity going to neglect taking up the option which your plans would offer them, or is it going to be too late when chaos claims us all and we sadly lament, “it might have been?"

When we think of the huge amount of scrap-iron and other materials which we have sold to Japan, all for the sole purpose of manufacturing machines of destruction to be used against China, can we connect this with Christian motive on our part? 

Quoting President Roosevelt, he says "No greater thing could come to our land today than a revival of the spirit of religion." 

These are his own words, and can you tell me if this is sincerely the way he feels, why you have not heard from him on your plans, the receipt at the White House of which you have received acknowledgment from Mr. Early? I am of the opinion that you should have received at least some personal word from Mr. Roosevelt, regardless of his attitude, for in a gesture as magnificent as it seems to me, no man could easily pass it by without comment. 

A man who had had much experience in politics once told me that Republican or Democrat, politicians are all alike. Said he, "put them all, the whole conglomeration in the Rose Bowl and shake 'em up, yes give them a damn good shaking up, there's only a sprinkling of real honest to goodness statesmen among them anyway. Shake thoroughly, don't pick but draw one blindly, and hocus-pocus, if you draw a statesman, anything but a wily politician, you are actually pulling a miracle act and your future as a magician, should you desire to become one, is made." If we could only shake them up hard enough to jar some moral stature into those who lack it, there would soon be an end to unemployment and prosperity would come as sure as does sunshine again after a rainy spell.

In 1932 after the Republicans had had their banquet, Mr. Roosevelt came in to clean up, confronted with the greatest task any President has had to face in the history of the United States. Did he not gain the presidency through the slickest political maneuvering ever practised, and did he not soon show appreciation of gaining that (59)

pedestal where he wished to reign supreme, by dealing out patronage to those who were mainly responsible for his election? 

That irked him after a while and he finally had to ignore pleas for even more partiality. He was forced to use a little judgment of his own if he was to maintain his popularity with millions of republicans who had voted for him. He was well aware of the fact that many of the rival party had voted for him because of their own leaders failure to bring the country out of a depression the like of which we had never before known.

He aspired to become another Jefferson, but those ideals were too high for any man of the present day to attain. Times had changed materially and modern policies and newer tactics were absolutely necessary. In attempting to change some of our tried and time proven doctrines, he realized with the failure to enlarge the supreme court that he had ventured a grave error. Had it been successful no doubt he would have strived to become a dictator, but of course a different kind of a dictator, one that would want only about 95% control instead of 100% such as European dictators not only want, but get?

That venture left an aroused populace of no small number, who from then on started to oppose him. Congress was forced to back their constituents, who were not shy in letting them know how the folks back home felt about it. We must at this point give congress credit for their former allegiance to the president. They had been real liberal in their support of the administration, but he was unable to cope with a situation demanding an acute acumen, so he called in some mighty brilliant intellectuals, college professors, mixed in with some of the best financial geniuses of our time; but woe and begone, their difference of opinion and their diverse theories so complicated the situation that he finally had to call in his cabinet, much as he might lack faith in some of them. His right-hand advisor had died and he was lost like a man in a fog. His lust for greater power had diminished with the defeat of the supreme court bill, and then criticisms from members of his own party were being spilt with much abandon. 

They increased in keeping with his failure to keep most of the promises he had made to we, the people. 

The financial structure of our country is in a deplorable state after the promiscuous spending of billions in comparison with all other orgies in the field of spending. Unemployed number more at (60)

the present time, than at any time in history, and this after eight years of complete failure to remedy it.

In answer to any queries on that score, leave it to Mrs. Perkins to come across with the weakest alibi it has been my displeasure to run up against. She says, and very recently, give the democratic party time and they will prove their capability. Good Lord, if eight years are not enough, has she the gall to insinuate they would like another four? If so, there's less chance of the same gang going back than there would be chance of a snowflake surviving in hell? I hope!

If it's the right man either party wants, why the devil don't they let us, we the people, select and elect the delegates to the national conventions instead of this infernal handpicking system, which is anything but government of the people, by the people, and for the people!

Hardly one of the nation's vital problems have been solved and at times certain proceedures have virtually smelled of communism. 

IF only I were writing a book instead of telling you briefly about these things which bother me no little, I'm afraid it would be a mighty thick book. I could cite error after error and one of the most significant ones would be the administration of the Work's Progress Association, a project which the President claimed would not mix. with politics and that party lines would be nil in picking the unemployed. A fiasco, and a mighty one, and abundant proof that if ever crooked politics, patronage and nauseating partiality was dished out, is available.

It is the administrations' own contention that one-third of our people are ill-housed, ill-fed and ill-clothed; Why in the face of this was that colossal blunder of destroying surplus crops and pigs possible?

Why, when the banks are bulging with money, do they withhold investment in private and small industry. Is it because there is little opportunity for a fair return when taxes on industry are so enormous that they are in the red before they can guarantee the bank a conservative profit, and that the banks in turn can't warrant the depositors a fair rate of interest?

Yes, these things are indeed a vital problem, but in the final analysis on the situation before we decide pro and con, it would be little less than malicious propaganda to pass by without mentioning some of the finer things which our President has given us. When a (61)

man is in the limelight there is always more condemnation for his wrong doings than there is praise for the right. Picture yourself stepping into the White House in 1932, and to realize to the best of your ability the situation then existing. You nor any other one man in the world could have cured all our ills and if there were such a man, let him speak for himself and present to us worthy and specific means with which to cure the condition. 

People always hollered soak the rich and President Roosevelt has soaked them plenty. He knows that if it were to go much farther that it would abolish a staid and traditional system which has prospered under able administration. If we were robbed entirely of the capitalistic system, it would also rob us of creative genius, and socialism or one of its brother isms might ensue. So let us be thankful that he did soak the rich, but more thankful that he knew when to stop. 

The national income has increased 71%, and tremendous strides have been made upward in the categories such as wages, salaries, farm income, dividend receipts, non-agricultural employment and exports, but this is not sufficient while expenditures remain so colossal, and there is much to be done. Democrat or Republican, lets sincerely hope and pray that something will be done and that that something will not be in the too distant future.

Social security will prove a wonderful help and the day when you are old and in no better condition than you are now, you might appreciate that Mr. Roosevelt did something for you even if he did lack the proper humanitarian principles in destroying crops and millions of livestock when thousands in our own country were ill fed. 

If first we would endeavor to create a democracy of the world that other nations might share in peace and happiness to all mankind, which God meant all humanity to have, would it not in the face of failure here, bring about inevitable prosperity to all?

In closing I would cite the lack of Christian spirit throughout all these past years of administration in Washington. 

When millions of pigs were destroyed, surplus grain burned, millions of bushels of it, with millions in China starving, malnutrition rampant in our own country, ill-clothed and ill-housed multitudes taking it all with expectancy. Is that Christian spirit?

It would be a wonderful thing if the very foundation of government could set us lowly mortals a pattern of good clean Christian administration. {62)

The arrest of seventeen members of the Christian front for planning the overthrow of the government is immeasurably trivial perhaps, but will this radical trend always be so minute in significance? 

There are men in our country, democrats worthy of Jefferson, and republicans worthy of a Lincoln, and may we hope that party lines be forgotten when and if they are found. Let political strategy be arrested long enough that we, the people will eventually be able to appoint our own delegates to the national conventions. 

If the President does not answer your letter within a reasonable time I fear it was too much for him. You think him the greatest man of the moment in the world today, but frankly I and millions of others do not. IT HAPPENED IN HOLLYWOOD 

I know that you are interested only in stories bearing of the truth when you desire opinions on such a magnificent theme as simultaneous prayer by mankind. Here is one that tells only a small part of the wickedness which has seemingly gone on unabated in Hollywood.

I would have you and all who read of it, know that there is meant no reflections cast upon the many good people of Hollywood and its environs. In fact I have been frankly told by many good people residing there that they wish with fervor, that the entire picture industry were anywhere but in their own fair city of Los Angeles, and they also assumed that before the advent of pictures it could hold its own with any city in the country for decent and upright living. {63}

While these few true incidents which I quote date some few years back, I judge from reading current developments that conditions are not much improved, if at all. The fact that the Dies committee is about to give us some of the low-down on certain activities there will bear witness that all is not well, if as stated in the papers an investigation is under way. 

My first experience dates back to the early thirties when on my route as a salesman, I was driving along a lonely coast road some miles from Los Angeles in the early morning of a mild spring day. 

For half an hour I had passed no cars and neither a pedestrian, but in suddenly rounding a curve I spotted a lonely girl apparently trodding a weary way. Even if only a girl, I at first was skeptical of picking up a hitch hiker as there were rumors afloat that some of them worked in league with shady characters and foul deeds were feared by many motorists.

As I drew closer I readily surmised that this girl was no ordinary hitch-hiker, for as I slowed up she made no attempt to beckon me for a lift, or to even avert a glance toward me. That in itself showed me that there was probably something materially wrong. Here it was barely dawn, the morning mist had not yet vanished and it was a darn long way to the next town of any size. A young girl, well dressed and carrying only a small overnight case, wearily walking on a lonesome desolate road near the sea, and not soliciting a ride from the only car she had seen in hours.

I stopped, and while she was by no means afraid, she was fidgety and reluctant to engage in conversation and wished I would go on and leave her to her original intentions. This stirred my intuition however, and it bid me fair to use every ounce of persuasion I could diplomatically summon, and by adjusting myself to the situation, succeeded in finally gaining her confidence and she consented to allow me to give her a ride.

During the next hour, through talking of cheerful things and trying my utmost to snap her out of a seeming state of lethargy, I was able to gain her acquiescence in joining me for breakfast at a wayside inn. 

During this period which led to subsequent acquaintance, I was with much tact and sincere desire, able to obtain her story which she told in frank and candid manner.

Well dressed, young, beautiful, fine of figure with a perfect carriage and beaucoup intelligence, would in short, describe this girl. On the [64]

surface, all that would make most girls very happy that nature had so bestowed herself upon them. 

On the contrary this girl was a very sad picture of modern youth, melancholy, pensive and dejected to the point of tears. During breakfast for the first time I could not help but notice that she was pregnant and this in turn gave me an inkling of what possibly might be behind the situation. After a hearty breakfast, and with renewed confidence she confided in me the following story which made me realize more than ever that there was more truth than poetry in many of the things I had heard about Hollywood.

It seems she had won a beauty contest back east some three years previously and became the subject of much admiration and suggestions that she hit for Hollywood where she would be bound to achieve success. She was a modest girl and had gained some measure of success in amateur theatricals but did not seriously consider the talkies. 

At the time she was only eighteen and things at home were none too harmonious after she graduated from high school and her future was really one problematical. It was this psychological time that into town blew an exploitation manager pretending to have great influence in Hollywood, and with a great gift of gab, and adept at throwing the salve, she fell hook, line and sinker for his line. A break one way or the other was at hand when this bird flew into the situation, and needless to say under existing conditions and after some consultation with her parents, she was off on the first rung up the ladder to stardom, at least so this prince of publicity claimed anyway. 

Her folks staked her to a couple hundred bucks and carfare, but this sheik pretended she would not need it and paid all her expenses on the trip, and also for the first few weeks her hotel bill upon arrival. 

He was to do great things for her and in return she was to pay him a measly 50% of all her earnings and she was dumb enough to sign a drawn-up contract which he showed her. It automatically made him her manager and she later found out that it represented no picture company, but a coined up paper having all the flowery headlines of what she thought a reliable booking agency. Slick would be a word which wouldn't start to describe his acumen at this sort of subterfuge. 

I know you don't want a book out of a letter so I'll try and condense the story as much as possible. He made her all right, but oh how! What a guy, this bird, and how many more he and others like him have seduced in his meanderings, is beyond us and the Lord only (65)

knows. If he has not yet met his Waterloo, you can feel with confidence that when he hears of your plan for the showdown of humanity, he with all birds of a feather will do everything in their power to subdue the thought and openly condemn it. You may later on guess why.

After only a month he started to get fresh, all in the midst of telling this innocent but slightly dumb girl of how he had talked with this or that executive and that a screen test would be given her soon. But she was not all dumb and she felt he was giving her the old hokum and jumped out on him to room with a girl she had met and who seemed friendly and more worldly and wise than she. They became steadfast friends and she learned more and more the ways of Hollywood, but not quite enough.

Her money did not last long, and while awaiting a break she took a job in a restaurant as waitress, still resolving to remain virtuous no matter what luck she would have in aspiring to crash the talkies. It was not long before the bluebeard in disguise located her, reminded her he had her contract and that he had obtained through his pull an audition and screen test. He really seemed repentant for his former advances and admonished her with new hopes and here at last he claimed was the spark which would spread to a flame of publicity and fame.

She accepted his offer and was successful in getting a couple of minor spots in a crowd scene, all the while this bozo telling her that is the way all stars got their start. She was naturally excited and at the time firmly believed he was representative of the industry. He seemed not such a bad chap after all, he was certainly good looking and he had a pleasing personality which drew people to him in spite of his gusto. She finally came to believe him sincerely in love with her and she reciprocated.

With these developments came hopes for better parts and she began attending night clubs with him, in order to meet the proper higher ups who were absolutely necessary in any plans for advancement. 

She for the first time started sipping champagne, with of course increasingly large doses the usual outcome. He seemed to have plenty of cash and she figured after all he must be something in the setup, although he seemed to avoid known stars and producers, most of the people she met in various night clubs she could not seem to remember as ever having got very far even if he claimed they were big shots. (66)

Meanwhile her girl friend had gone back home to marry her schoolday sweetheart, having seen the handwriting on the wall and to retain her virtue in the complex social strata of her set was too problematical for her to cope with. 

The subject of my true story however fell violently in love with her self appointed manager, and more and more he taught her the value of stringing along with a gay set and liquor, a bohemian life was the life and she believed him. He told her he would yet make her and it was not long before he did. One night when both were under the subtle insidious hypnotic influence of alcohol's intoxicating prowess he seduced her and from then on until she became pregnant they lived as man and wife. 

When she imparted news of this condition to him he all of a sudden flew the coop and the morning I met this girl she was heading for the nearest appropriate cliff from which to leap into the Pacific and oblivion with merciful death rather than to face her people and the public. All this after that short but too steep decline to degradation via the wiles of something calling himself a man, the like of which are plentiful in this playground called Hollywood.

Through encouraging discourse and cheerful advice, I was able to persuade her to accompany me to the home of a very dear middled aged lady who I felt would be able to give her worthy suggestion under reigning conditions. This was accepted with little deliberation by all other Colors, Creeds and Races who were not Christians, in keeping with the majority of the world's religious believers desire to make it more significant on the anniversary celebrating the birth of Christ, in the most worthy manner, simultaneous prayer of millions.

The feeling of deep emotion inspired by the contemplation of the presence of God was in itself a tremendous influence for advancement in the moral standards of all humanity. This betterment of conditions was so great in extent, that millions made it a prayer of thanks, and pleaded to God for continuance of mankind's efforts for eventual permanent peace on earth, good will to men.

Many who had as yet not found themselves, asked for Divine aid in throwing off the yoke of habit formed detriments to clear conscience, to live life anew, to love thy neighbor as thyself, and to aid in the moral rehabilitation of lost brethren who were yet floundering in the sea of life. The birds in the wood, the flowers in the garden, the plants on the window sill, the very grass on the lawn, the shrubbery and the trees in the forest, verdant and inspiring, were alive and a part of God's mighty world which some of your ancestors failed to appreciate.

The mountains and the dales, the valleys and the brook, and even the vegetable garden, the immense wheat fields and the boundless (99)

seas were something you thought about in your explorations into the mysteries of nature. And the sun and the planets and the stars became things of more than minute interest to you in your study of astronomy at times when you sought diversion from your daily tasks. Exploration into the mysteries of this mighty cosmos became a hobby with some as did the following of sports with others. 

And there was no lack of excitement and pleasure. Friendly rivalries in sports continued and there was renewed interest in the Olympiad. There was great increase in the love of true sport and they were all conducted on a higher scale. A cleaner administration in all branches of boxing and wrestling, and in college athletics all shady negotiations were combed out for the better interest of youth who were star athletes. They chose the school of their own desires and competition was as ever keen. 

The turnstiles of league baseball clicked with greater frequency and softball for men of 70 and 80 came into its own. 

Followers of the sport of kings were amply appeased. The Irish 

Sweepstakes and the Kentucky derby were controlled by local governments. Hialeah was run by the city of Miami and the state of 

Florida conducted and regulated the other tracks there. This system was adopted universally. 

They all prospered and tax on profits of individuals as well as corporations all went into the fund derived from government controlled lotteries throughout the world. This enormous revenue was pooled and old age pensions and unemployment insurance were the recipients where formerly it mostly went for luxuries of dubious nature. By this time people were becoming well able to pass the last days of their life in great comfort, and poverty and destitution were nil.

Fans who played the races, and people who sought diversion and thrill in lotteries found there were fewer and fewer who squandered promiscuously until they became destitute. They were checked and double checked, and if neglecting any vital needs of family were barred from all gambling activities. This was a new duty for former sheriffs and jail guards who with decrease in crime were forced to seek newer outlets for their efficiency as upholders of law and order. 

Records in sports made back in the middle century were shattered and newer ones made which it seemed would be impossible of shattering, but with man's abundant improvement in physical prowess {100}

there came a race of super humans and continuance of new records was conceivable. Both boys and girls were bigger, stronger and healthier. 

Aviation furnished a chief source of new and greater thrills for the common people. Perfection of detail, instrument flying and mass production had brought light safe planes within the reach of all and they were as plentiful and as cheap as the ancient flivver. There was no let down because of improved Christian life, for those who sought excitement and pleasure, gambling and numerous other diversions and hobbies. Friendly rivalries existed in all branches of aviation and new found sports, but there was no lack of Christian spirit in their administration.

Music became a new medium for inspiration. Its entrancing background during all the annual refuge in simultaneous prayer, now via television, had proved a magnificent source for emotional ecstasy which could only come through Divine assistance. Now it was possible to see as well as hear the Saintly personalities of those who led us in prayer and to see in the background a wonderful orchestra of hundreds of the finest musicians in all democracy. Here the genius of Marconi was blended with the genius of the old masters. Only by means of the results of their genius could prayer be held on such a magnificent scale.

A greater and stronger more intellectual race of all mankind was possible and became a reality through rigid adoption of birth control. Those of all nations who formerly had been against it, found that quality in men was as in everything, preferable to quantity. 

And there developed the philosophy that birth control was within the tenets of good religion. They felt that God would not want brought into the world, children by parents who for some reason denoted a desire to avoid them because of past syphillis inherency and feeble mindedness or for other reasons.

By advanced methods in teaching the value of mental hygiene and physical cleanliness we advanced in the abolition of venereal disease. 

There was no more syphillis or gonorrhoea and the mystery of its origin remained, but mental hygiene developed the will to practice body cleanliness, and science agreed that this would eliminate all possibility of these sinister plagues returning. 

From production to consumption, the picture industry was cleansed as were all. Monopoly vanished and talkies no longer appealed to (101)

youth through insidious pictorial propaganda of morals and drink. 

Also gone forever were the days when people thought human nature could not be changed. They knew the author of this book did not intend to try and reform the world in a day, but that through experience in his own life he had found straying too far from the straight and narrow path brought nothing but misery. Like all who likewise found there was only one way, he endeavored through the medium of this book to further his cause and prove that the diverse problems of the whole world could be laid to greed and lack of 

Christian Spirit.

People realized again and again as time went on that these Utopian feats were possible, and human nature was bound to be changed with the biological and mental improvement which came with humanity's ardent desire to become the highest form of animal in truth, but with intellectual powers to control it. No longer were there the scum of men who bragged of their conquests over virgins and married women. 

Broad education on the sex problem and the vanishing of illiteracy had given the world a firmer foundation on which to build a higher moral life.

No longer were there men who gave to the women of their temporary desire the old salve, "my wife just doesn't understand me, it's necessary that I seek sexual pleasure on the outside," and no longer were women susceptible to this line, or gullible to men with bank rolls and Rolls Royces.

Sexual intercourse became truly what it was meant to be and was not practised promiscuously, but between man and wife, and strange but true, animal instinct was only of animals, but with higher moral stability, sexual pleasure and ecstasy was even greater in wedlock, true devotion and the higher love which made these things all the more wonderful to experience.

The greatest crime which could be committed in this newer day was the seduction of a virgin or the infidelity of a man or wife. It rarely happened and those who would do such things were so ostracized from society that they invariably committed suicide rather than face the populace with disgrace written all over them. 

Abortion was also a thing of the past, there was no resort to it and people did not even have to think twice, for loose morals and drunken indiscretions vanished with improved morals. There was less and less of tainted blood due to past sins of our ancestors which science had (102)

formerly claimed responsible for many human ailments. This all came about by the cleansing of the soul which in turn brought cleanliness of mental and physical desire. Here again broad education on the relationship between alcohol and sexual desire made youth sense more quickly the danger, and temptations were nipped in the bud through mental hygiene which was adopted in childhood with increasing desire to retain high morals throughout life. 

No person any longer thought religion a farce, it had become one religion to the entire satisfaction of all creeds who formerly at times differed in doctrines. Church remained as ever a good place to go, and congregation was truly one of spiritual help and people did not go to be in style or to witness their neighbors Easter clothes. 

This was the time when people of all the world, after centuries of combatting evil had finally come to the full and material realization that alcohol was a food, but in the same sense that dynamite was a fuel, and they profited thereby.

There was a vast and broad education on the evils attendant to all alcoholic drinks as beverages. National and international school systems adopted a universal code of instruction from the A, B, C age up, with the gratifying result that children were aware of its insidious subtle lure before they reached adolescence and were suddenly confronted with it sans knowledge. 

While drinking in strict moderation was allowed, laws were more rigid and the days were ended when negligent booze hounds were on the rampage and a menace to all decent living. Drunken drivers were but a memory and the love of life and good health through proper education was too strongly imbedded on the minds of the newer intellectual world. Education and experience had taught men the futility of trying to cope with the destructive forces of the demon rum. Consumption of liquor diminished so greatly that finally the government controlled the few remaining breweries, wineries and distilleries.

Insurance rates were materially and automatically reduced, because with decreased consumption of all alcoholic drinks, there was great increase in life expectancy. In the few years necessary for proof of added length in life, insurance companies published reliable statistics, showing that even the few who drank and lived as long as 60 or 70 in the fifties, were now living 80 to 100 years. 

There were staggering amounts in the decrease in deaths caused by 103)

alcoholism and its affiliate diseases directly attributed to it. Heart and kidney diseases and cirrhosis of the liver, former ailments directly traceable many times to indulgence in alcohol had disappeared in entirety. All mental torture and suffering caused by the devil's brew was a thing of the past, and no longer was there competition between alcohol and war as to which was the grimmest of reapers, for the very simple reason that there was none of either. 

At the turn of the century there was none of battleships and submarines, there were no navies, and all armaments, shells and massive machines of destruction lay forever stilled at the bottom of the sea.

To gain knowledge of the immensity of this burial, timid souls feared their sinking would raise the water line.

Memories of war were vague, only revived by display of their spoils in the parks, but there a few reminders remained at the choice of the people, that their descendants in future generations might see how dumb the people of previous ages were. It was easy to visualize how man was once a predatory animal in parts of the world, but it was difficult to realize that with the coming of civilization man had continued to slay each other, and how men like Hitler and Stalin could survive even in the short time they reigned was beyond the comprehension of 21st century intellect.

People told their children of the days when foreign wars were an easy device to divert our attention from our own serious domestic troubles, and how it was so wonderful now that there was none of either. By experience we grew bigger and got a keener and deeper insight into life, and its mysteries were no longer unsolvable, because of the huge advance in science and invention. 

For centuries man had tackled the problem and while they used the mighty forces of electricity, they had been unable to tell the world just what it was, or to define it satisfactorily. With the coming of radio and television there developed a science founded on the thought that if men's voices could be sent through the ether waves, and images transmitted through the air, that the souls of all earthly people could also very easily be carried afar to other and greater planets where a new and better life would be born. Those worthy would find themselves on the greater and nobler planets where life on this earth would be but a vague and not too ideal memory. On lesser planets would be found the recreation of those earthly souls whose life had warranted their future on a lower basis, but with the (104)

chance through atonement to finally scale the endless spaces of the cosmos and join their earthly relatives, ancestors and friends. Life was electricity and all earthly uses of its millions of currents was but as a blood cell to the body.

A code of ethics in advertising was adopted, and it was rigidly adhered to by all participants, thereby eliminating all past infringements and artifices which were once used. False indorsements and tendencies to lead youth astray, those who were susceptible to pretty pictures and flowery versed inducements to drink and smoke non nerve calming cigarettes, were no more.

Married women, because of their desire to work, no longer blocked the acts of nature, and became pregnant and did not rob the world of potential Presidents and men of high moral stature. There was always some capable girl to take her place, this proving that when nature takes its course, at once a home and children was made and a new job created for a girl just out of school. There was no need for wives to financially help their mates. With the maintenance of prosperity, had come more desire to have a home and children, but when women of exceptional intellectual ability desired a career there was nothing to stop them and they took their place in the administration of world democracy. They however married, and took time out to have children and they were looked up to by all the world. 

Jails and prisons were mostly vacant or torn down to be replaced by recreational centers. Hospitals were a rare sight as the 25th century rolled around. Science had conquered disease and hardening of the arteries and 500 years was the average length of life and people took death but as a new and greater experience, science having proved to the satisfaction of even the skeptics that the hereafter was but the continuance of life with but the cloak gone with earthly end, and even after eventual disrobement there would be the body, which after all was future life in all its glory. 

Victoria Falls in Africa had been successfully harnessed and a network of electric power was distributed over the vast domain of Africa. In South America, Iguassi Falls was likewise the power plant which brought radio and television to the masses who had been in earlier days without these wonders of God and man.

Illiteracy was stamped out and in all Latin America serfdom was but a memory. The high Inca Culture was not past reviving and true democracy had arrived and complete happiness was no longer a dream. (105)

Millions no longer starved in China. Negroes and whites alike the world over, including our own south, no longer were compelled to endure life on meagre wages, and poverty stricken areas were taboo. 

Shacks and pig pens were replaced with modern housing and surroundings approached the ideal. No more were there tenement slums in the larger cities of the world, and the bowery became a beautiful street where museums made the public gasp when pictures showed them where people used to get a kick out of "Let's go slumming!"

Ignorance of God could have been excused the neanderthal man in the early stone age and down through the quaternary period, but with the birth of civilization, man through Divine instinct and intelligence came to know that there WAS a God, and in many forms they worshipped a Divine being. There was no excuse when civilization had reached the heights it did in the middle of the 20th century, and man's unworthiness was the sole reason for man's unhappiness. 

True there had been famines, pestilence and floods, but who is so highly intellectual to tell us there was no God because of this? 

Those of devout faith can tell you with conviction that God works in mysterious ways his wonders to perform and another immortal, Edward Young, expressed in few words the significance of many acts of God. "A God all mercy is a God unjust." 

Brilliant men predicted that through intermarriage that eventually the white races would be one, this eliminating much of racial hatred and friction between former warlike peoples. The yellow races had also became one while the negro race remained as separate, they were treated as equals with the exception of intermarriage. They desired to remain separate races for with the advance of education each competed in intellectual conquest and entirely friendly rivalry was taking the place of war. 

And the wild horses in the great west and British Columbia were no longer hunted and killed as they had been. They were given the freedom of public parks as were elk and buffalo that they shall not become extinct.

Promises were made in these days, and promises were kept. A standing army and a large navy were only in vogue for a century, merely a reminder to one or two nations which found it extremely difficult to get into the new regime, but finally they saw it was for the best and acquiesced to terms universally adopted. 106

With the harnessing of atomic energy and the solar ray, came the broadcasting of power; automobiles were as quiet as a mouse, but with power unlimited and massive trucks pulled trains of trailers but were confined to specially built roads and pleasure driving became a pleasure.

No more unsightly billboards marred the countryside and the landscape if ugly, was made beautiful with planting of shrubbery and trees and all panorama was free of man-made detractions to nature. All these things furnished employment for many thousands. 

One of the greatest phenomena came to pass with the final conquest of speed in rocket ships and trips to the moon became as frequent as China bound clippers in the 20th Century. It served to stimulate interest in the proposed non-stop flights to Mars, Jupiter, Mercury and Venus, with eventual new worlds to discover inconceivable. Inter-planet radio communications were as casual as form cablegrams from Europe were in the days of the forgotten President F.D.R.

It was a mighty good thing for the world that they had become as one huge nation, for with radio messages from Mars, there was much speculation as to whether or not they were a war-like people, but with a world united, they found that indeed in union there was strength. While navies and armies were no longer, scientists had discovered new ways of dealing with any attempt by Mars to conquer the great democracy of the world, and fear was unknown. 

Mammoth ships of the sky carried as many passengers as did the former Queen Mary, and breakfast in New York with dinner in

London was a common occurrence. 

No longer was this Utopia a mirage, no longer a day dream, a vision or an illusion or irrational hopes, but real, alive and material, the supreme happiness which could and did come. And when the 

25th century arrived in great splendor, there existed on earth the nearest perfection of ideal life it was humanly and Divinely possible to achieve. The dream of a humble but persistent humble person had helped to inspire the necessary public opinion to take up humanity's option. It was not allowed to expire and bedlam was in the discard. 

To you who doubt that such things could come to pass, I pray if after spending an hour and a half or two hours in ardent perusal of 107)

this book, that you will devote but another five minutes as to the why and wherefore that earthly Utopia is no idle dream, but can in all its glory, become simply another impossibility come to pass. 

If the whole world marched serenely forward to God's challenge "Christians front, but CHRISTIANS;" would that not be a start in the right direction?

If sincere faith in God, ardent prayer and renewed Christian spirit were practiced religiously; would that be a detriment to man's achieving a firm foundation on which to build?

If man's adherence to the Golden Rule, inspired by simultaneous prayer annually were universally adopted; would that not in itself furnish much incentive to live right and be rewarded for it? 

If sincere reverence for the Divine precepts of life as found in the gospel of Christ was manifest; would that bar future happiness bearing token of Elysian fields to come? |

If with awed wonder multitudes would conscientiously try to fathom the infinite wisdom of God; would he deny us through this faith in Him, the wonders of life which we had been denied for centuries because we were formerly apathetic toward his divine power? Would not continuance of those enjoyments which man seeks for relaxation, sports, gambling and lotteries, night-clubs and drink in strict moderation for those who are unable to resist, all under improved conditions as outlined in this postlude, prevent the sudden reforming of the world, but yet improve the outlook for maintenance of Christianity without the kind of piety which mankind likes to shun?

Isn't it what we shall do now, that will make for our descendants an easier task for improvement in world conditions that eventual 

Utopia on earth shall not prove a fantastic goal to reach?

If only when you close the covers of this book, you will in the future during many lulls in the grind of your daily routine, or nightly when you pray, give serious thought to your own problems, try to remedy them in these suggestions, the right way, and think of the problems of others which you can help in many ways if you have the will to; and if others will do likewise, it won't be long before you will have a new outlook on life.

If in turn you would seriously try to spread these doctrines, remembering that if you are really bad, that improvement might have to be gradual, but that no matter how slowly and how little gain each 108)

day brings, reminisce to your youthful days when you took a small snowball and rolled it down the hill, and how it grew to be a monster in size; so likewise will your moral stature become mammoth, and rewards will be yours which will make you a finer person. Last but not the smallest in significance will be the happiness which you will be able to pass your remaining days on earth, and to die will be but to part from your loved ones until you meet again. 

Surely all these things are worth while trying, and your scruples of conscience will be reborn, and you will become again in heart children, and your good deed for every day will make you a good scout and the whole world will admire. 

Life will be good again to live, and to you who have been derelicts on the sea of life, a port of haven from out the mist and through the storm of adversity will be found through the clearing skies of spiritual revelation.

In closing, lest we have been swayed by our emotions and fear again coming back to the world of materialism, such as we always do when carried away by happy dreams and then wake up to the fact that life is not as it looks through rose colored glasses, I leave with you a thought which will prove worthy of much concentration. It was handed to me by a friend who knows so well that the world is fast slipping on the down-grade and that I have strived with persistency to present to the public, those plans as outlined to the President. 

It is an article by Mr. Charles Wells, and I think it warrants including in my final chapter to a book which I have prayed will receive due consideration and ultimately inspire public interest in adopting these plans as the final and only recourse for humanity in the face of today's problems. | The Only Way: Throughout the world men are desperately hoping we will emerge from the despair and discontentment of the present, into a happier more equitable tomorrow. 

But everywhere we look, yawning chasms of racial and class hatred spread before us, merged with depths of greed and poverty. Time and again we have stretched out some frail structure across the barriers, hoping for a way of deliverance, but collapse and wreckage mark 

MAN'S efforts to save himself. (109)

Yet there is a way and someday men will find it. 

Anchored in divine will, paved with sacrificial love, it offers a victorious fulfilment of man's dearest hope for a world of liberty and justice. 

Surely those words of Mr. Wells carry a vital thought. 

To me at this time when chaos is rampant, regardless of party lines, would that the next President of the United States religiously adhere to the doctrines of our immortal Abraham Lincoln, and with but one word changed; "That this world (nation), under God, shall have a new birth of freedom, and that government of the people, by the people, for the people, shall not perish from the earth.”
