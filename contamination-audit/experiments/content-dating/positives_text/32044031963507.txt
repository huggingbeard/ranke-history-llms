Out of Town Relatives a. If letter will not get results, ask corresponding agency to visit or contact. b. Friends 1. 

Often deserter may be visiting or living with these -- there is a tendency to go to towns in which relatives or friends reside especially when there have been differences between deserter's family and deserted spouse. 

Try to contact close friends and acquaintances especially so-called "buddies" with whom deserter spent considerable time -- and who may have some information as to deserter's plans. 

2. Sometimes mutual friends of the family can help give a clearer picture of the social situation, so as to enable one to determine the possible course of the deserter.

Other Marriages 

C. 1. 2. D. 3. 

It is important to contact spouse and children of previous marriages, since deserter may get in touch with them.

If there is a divorce in the marriage in which you are interested, the deserter may have remarried. In such cases, try to contact the present spouse.

In all cases, the in-laws of a past or present marriage may prove helpful in giving information or leads.

Trade Associations and Unions 

1. Try to contact union or association to which deserter belonged, if known. 

Try to get exact local number, since in most large unions the national office does not have a record of membership. Some, such as Painters Union, do. 

2. Many unions will not give information, as a matter of policy, but some will forward mail to the last recorded address. 3. 

Look up directories issued by unions for list of locals, e.g., Directory of Labor Organizations in New York State (issued by N.Y. Department of 

Labor, Albany).

Societies and Lodges. Get the name, branch, or lodge name or number of group, and name of secretary or other officer. Determine when and by whom deserter's dues last paid. Find out if any benefits were collected. 

If membership has been transferred, get new address.

U.S. State Department, Passports, Foreign Residence 

1. It is possible to learn if a passport has been issued to a particular person by writing the Passport Division, Department of State, Washington, D.C. 2. 

If deserter is known to be living in a foreign country, the State Department may be requested to have its Consul contact the deserter or in some cases a relative of the deserter, who may know where the deserter is located. (This applies only if deserter or his wife is a U.S. citizen.) 

Court Records 

1. 2. 3. 

If deserter is known or believed to have a criminal record, check should be made with the authorities in the locality where crime took place and/or with the probation or parole officials.

If deserter is known or believed to have been involved in a civil court proceeding, the local court should be contacted for recent activity and/or last known address.

If deserter is known or believed to have been in touch with any attorneys, the attorneys might be contacted (although in most cases, attorneys will not disclose information without consent of their clients; but even in such cases it may lead to voluntary appearance or contact).
