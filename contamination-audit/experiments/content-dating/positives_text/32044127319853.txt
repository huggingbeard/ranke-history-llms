"THE PSYCHEDELIC WORLD 

There is a world far better than the one we have always known. This world is sprinkled with little shining crystals, and a silent song goes on unendingly. 

Little droplets of molten silver drop from the heavens.

A blending of sound interwinds and merges to become pure light. 

Each object exists in infinite perfection. A sharp, knife-like distinction seems to separate each spectacle. Perfect cogs spinning in opposite directions with no other purpose than BEING. 

A huge round dome which encircles the universe... millions of replicas of the same world, but each representing a different plane of consciousness.

Worlds missing each other by inches, as we move them up a degree, where they meet and the perfect state of order

becomes. Where we now see our friend and enemy as one: where all things we recognized as sins in our fellow beings we now see as our own manifestations: for only our considering it sin has made it so. 

A timeless pulsation seems to take us across all barriers. 

Eternities are reached; infinity is felt. 

The relation of all things is known. 

Each object seems complete: the world we see in each has objects of its own which are seen as worlds and objects endlessly. Interest exists everywhere:

Everything is in flux and seems newer than the new it was an instant ago. 

All the senses act as one as they seem to caress and encompass whatever they perceive.

A thousand sensual feelings are emitted, at once sending out waves of climaxes which thrill the mind to even greater heights. All mysteries are exposed as upon each revelation vast explosions of vibratory color 10

emerge as flowing liquid. A consummation of you, your purpose and creator, unfolds as your mind is seething to heights undreamed-of.

A tremendous surge of bliss is then realized. 

This accumulates in a tiny shell that expands double its size, emotions at their highest to the unbearable breaking-point and then release as the shell bursts and a huge burning-white flower grows at a slow unceasing rate until the petals reach out to their extreme, and then they once again close to rest and you float in a heaven of satisfaction and contentment.

I. 

There is a center path which is treacherously narrow, and to walk this path is no mean task. The present condition of society makes this doubly difficult. If those who have become accustomed to confusion and disharmony were exposed to the "perfect" situation, they might prove to be too far gone to salvage. He who could not achieve his goals in that direction has compromised for "second bests"-even poor substitutes. Once man has lived too far off to the right or the left of this path, he is confronted by factions that always live on the opposite of either choice. 

This is the beginning of conflict, and as long as we cannot see that the two opposites are the same, we are bound to discontent. Many 

Set up Systems dedicated to bucking the opposites and erroneously consider their maneuvers as being a "challenge". There is a realm of mind that knows no good and evil, and sees them both as man's invention.

Once we reach this state-where we are no longer attached to sense objects, and 13

no longer identify ourselves with people or groups-an objective way of thinking develops. 

How do we know that an objective way of thinking is ever possible? Only because the inherent states of mind are exposed to us under certain conditions, and by the nature of this phenomenon we realize it exists only to reveal an objective truth to us. There is such a similarity that exists in these states, that it appears that primitive and "modern" man alike experience the same thing, once the barriers of attachments and shams are stripped away. 24

Occasionally some of these senses are defective but may be corrected by mechanical means or various exercises designed to strengthen the weakened muscles. 

One of the last states of hypnosis is referred to as hyperaesthesia (the intensification of the senses). One of the reasons for this intensification is that the subject's attention is directed entirely in one unwavering direction. 

The mind acts as a huge amplifier; the slightest tinkle rings inside and expands in quality and quantity. The messengers (senses) now send a less distorted message to the mind, which can interpret it more 15

This is one of the main factors dealt with here: how to expand the existing senses to their fullest potential.

In the world of science there are few sciences which are infallibly exact and predictable. Math, for example; chemistry, to a degree; clear-cut facts that work the same for all. With all of the variables of each understood, all sciences are as exact, each science as predictable as math. 

Unfortunately this is not the case, and most of our knowledge is derived from theory and postulates. One of the least known and most inexact and unpredictable sciences is psychology. Both psychology and psychiatry have retained their antiquated methods since their origin. While we are stuck in the fantasy sex-world of Freud, our political competitors- the Russians-have advanced in their extensions of Pavlov.

We maliciously call conditioning a reflex "brainwashing". Now I am not trying to argue the methods of either system; yet parts of the Russian method are indisputable and completely predictable, while none of our methods are in any way predictable except 19

for the predictable fact that the poor victim will be at the mercy of the psychiatric world for many "sick" years and hear their nonsense until he is blue in the face. 

With the advent of "tranquillizers", few cures were invoked, but tens of thousands of "tranquil" psychotics emerged. The socalled "snake-pit" situation in our mental hospitals was somewhat alleviated. Nobody really was cured, but it was much easier on the nerves of the doctors and orderlies. 

It is apparent that we are on a completely hopeless track, and new methods and procedures must emerge.

This world is for the most part conservative, each individual holding on violently to the old-fashioned, time-honored methods which are so familiar to all. When these stabilizing reference points are in any way threatened, there is a rebellion and a frantic attempt to avoid change.

Head-on political differences, rather than denial of the Russian achievements, have forced us to neglect the obvious. There have been many aids employed by both branches of psychiatry, 20

specifically the psychedelics. The few Russian references to them are in relation to conditioning a reflex in a dog, with and without mescaline. They established that "conditioning is grossly speeded up" with the use of psychedelics.

I will add a system that I have worked out, putting this to a practical application. 

With this method, I have mastered a few demonstrable feats, the most significant of which has been the learning of the German language, rapidly and fluently. I also learnt typing in an incredibly short time; and my most satisfying and personally rewarding results have been in the revelation of classical music. You see, before my psychedelic experience I was TONE-DEAF. Now I am told by musicians that I hum Brahms, Wagner and even Bartok in PERFECT PITCH!

I have witnessed many people undergo the psychedelic experience, and even the few who seemed to have a less pleasant experjence have agreed that it has changed their lives in many ways. The majority, however, have only praise for the states of mind induced. 21

The Battle for Religious Freedom as waged by the Native American Church of North America 

Poyote had been used by the Aztecs since years before the Spanish conquest of Mexico. As far back as the Spanish Inquisition there are recorded writings in documents of peyote being used by Pueblo Indians near Santa Fe, New Mexico.

In more recent times peyotism has moved rapidly from one tribe to another. Many tribes in Oklahoma had incorporated peyote into their religion by the late 1800's. In the early Twentieth Century an association known as the "Mescal- Bean Eaters" was formed. This formidable name was changed to the "Union Church", for obvious reasons.

In 1918 the Bureau of Indian Affairs attempted to have Congress pass an "anti-peyote"

Caw. This led to a proposal for an intertribal peyotist association, that could combat any such action, if need be. James Mooney, famed ethnologist of the Smithsonian Institute, advised the Indians to change the name of their Kurch to the "Native

American Church". This was accomplished on 

October 10, 1918. By 1944 it had spread 43

throughout the nation and became known as the Native American Church of the United

States" When it spread to Canada in 1955 it was given its present name, "Native American 

Church of North America".

There were many Indians opposed to the spread of peyote. Primarily its spread threatenea the existing religious groups, of which those in control foresaw the possibility of losing their stronghold. This problem was settled when an agreement was reached by both factions; the agreement proposed the following: 

1 Affirmation by the Dueblo of the principle of complete religious liberty. 

2. Affirmation by the Peyote group of its determination to maintain the old religion, be faithful to the kiva life, participate in the ceremonies and do community work. 

3. Recognition by the Peyote Church of the authority of the Governor and Council to set up conditions governed by public welfare and convenience, which would be limiting upon the activities of the Deyote Church. 

4. These conditions to include the following: a. The Peyote Church ceremonies to be 44

A form of muscaria in Mexico is called Nanacatl, and is usually prepared as an extract from mick and mixed with agave cactus brandy. It has also been used by various tribes of Siberia. 

An extract was obtained by soaking the mushroom in hot water for about an hour, then drinking the amount needed to induce the desired condition.
