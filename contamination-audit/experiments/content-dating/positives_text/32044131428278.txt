Marijuna Is Poison 

MARIJUANA (a name in itself bearing no meaning, and referring to plant material called HASHISH, "locoweed," Indian hemp, etc.) has but lately made its appearance in the American lexicon of familiar terms. 

What it is, what does it refer to, and why has it so lately become known? Marijuana distorts the brain, and any suggestions, no matter how absurd or diabolical, may seize them. It is the "Weed of Madness." 

It is called "Reefer," and its users become absolutely crazy. 

The MARIJUANA piant has for years led a strange 

Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde existence. Under the name "HASHISH" and similar terms, it has been known for years in the Orient as a powerful drug. The plant, growing wild in the Western United States, became known as "loco-weed" by the cattlemen when they observed that live stock eating it often became temporarily demented. As hemp, on the other hand, the plant has been cultivated usefully for years for the manufacture of strong and durable rope, and thousands of people have handled it extensively without ever knowing that it was identical with the Indian drug, HASHISH. -36-

As recently as five years ago MARIJUANA was scarcely known even to persons who were devoting their lives to the control of the trade in narcotics. But in that brief period it has spread in the form of cigarettes to every part of the United States.

The effects of the use of the weed are various and not universally harmful, but the specific effect on ny individual is almost completely unpredictable. As one of the common results of indulgence is a temporary paralysis of inhibitions and moral controls, the results are all too often very serious. 

Some months ago a Chicago high school boy was accused of an exceptionally brutal and bestial crime on a child. Apparently up to the time of the crime he had led quite a normal life, and was by no means marked as the dangerous degenerate the crime seemed to indicate. Though the newspaper reports did not indicate that such was the case. it was the common opinion among his classmates that he was under the influence of 

Marijuana when he committed the crime.

The Beautiful Snow 

The following poem is a mute testimony to the powers of sin and the underworld when a person becomes chained by dope. This poem, was written by a very cultured young girl, who became a victim of dope, and having spent her young life in disgrace and shame, died the melancholy death of a broken-hearted outcast. 

Oh! the snow, the beautiful snow, Filling the sky and earth below,

Over the housetops, over the street, Over the heads of people you meet; 

Dancing-Flirting-Skimming along 

Beautiful snow! It can do no wrong: 

Flying to kiss a fair lady's cheek, Clinging to lips in frolicksome freak; 

Beautiful snow from heaven above, Pure as an angel, gentle as love! 

Oh, the snow, the beautiful snow, How the flakes gather and laugh as they go 

Whirling about in maddening fun;

Chasing-Laughing-Hurrying by, It lights on the face and it sparkles the eye; 

And the dogs with a bark and a bound 

Snap at the crystals as they eddy around;

The town is alive, and its heart is aglow, To welcome the coming of beautiful snow! 

How wild the crowd goes swaying along, Hailing each other with humor and song; 

How the gay sleighs like meteors flash by, Bright for a moment, then lost to the eye; 

Ringing Swinging—Dashing they go, Over the crest of the beautiful snow; 

Snow so pure when it falls from the sky,

As to make one regret to see it lie To be trampled and tracked by thousands of feet 

Till it blends with filth in the horrible street. 

Once I was pure as the snow, but I fell, Fell like the snow flakes from heaven to hell,

Fell to be trampled as filth in the street, Fell to be scoffed, to be spit on and beat;

Pleading Cursing-Dreading to die, Selling my soul to whoever would buy; 

Dealing in shame for a morsel of bread, Hating the living and fearing the dead. 

Merciful God! have I fallen so low! 

And yet I was once like the beautiful snow. -46--

Once I was fair as the beautiful snow, With an eye like a crystal, a heart like its glow; 

Once I was loved for my innocent grace- 

Flattered and sought for by the charms of my face! Fathers-Mothers-Sisters-all, God and myself I have lost by my fall; 

The veriest wretch that goes shivering by, Will make a wide sweep lest I wander too nigh,

For all that is on or above me I know, There is nothing so pure as the beautiful snow. 

How strange it should be that this beautiful snow 

Should fall on a sinner with nowhere to go! 

How strange it should be when the night comes again. 

If the snow and the ice struck my desperate brain Fainting-Freezing-Dying-alone, Too wicked for prayer, too weak for a moan 

To be heard in the streets of the crazy town, Gone mad in the joy of snow coming down; 

To be and to die in my terrible woe, With a bed and a shroud of the beautiful snow. 

Helpless and foul as the trampled snow, Sinner, despair not! Christ stoopeth low

To rescue the soul that is lost in sin, And raise it to life and enjoyment again, Groaning Bleeding-Dying-for thee, The Crucified hung on the cursed tree! 

His accents of mercy fall soft on thine ear. "Is there mercy for me? Will He heed my last prayer?" 

O God! in the stream that for sinners did flow 

Wash me and I shall be whiter than snow.

A Personal Letter 

Dear Christian Friend:

After a number of years our prayers, as well as the prayers of thousands of Christians, have been answered, and the Sunshine Mission for Girls, at Sixth and Wall, has become a realization. 

This Mission for Women is one of the most needed works in the City of Los Angeles. Here there are many missions for men, which have accomplished. much good work and cared for thousands of down and outers, but until the Sunshine Mission opened Los Angeles did not have any place where women and girls could be given care. 

Thank God, there is now a Mission for women and girls in the heart of Los Angeles, and since the opening, New Year's Day, 1942, many women and girls have been cared for. Many have been saved; young girls have been rescued from the underworld, rehabilitated and sent back home to their loved ones; they have been fed, bathed and dressed, and some placed in good jobs, giving them a new outlook on life.

One girl walked the streets until her feet were so swollen and blistered she could not walk, and was brought into the Sunshine Mission. Today she has been saved, her feet completely healed, and she is happy in a good job, and going on with the 

Lord. She attends every meeting at the Mission.
