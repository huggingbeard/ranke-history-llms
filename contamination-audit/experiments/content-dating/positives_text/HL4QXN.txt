FOREWORD To the Class of 1925, Harvard Law School: 

The Class Report submitted herewith is the work of Arthur and Susan Sutherland. For this labor of loyalty to the Class, all of us are greatly in their debt.

The Class at graduation in 1925 had 246 members. Of these gradu-` ates, 19 have died; 19 non-graduates of the Class have died. 

The living graduate and non-graduate members of the Class will meet for the 25th Anniversary Reunion in Boston and Cambridge on April 28 and 29, 1950. This will be the sixth reunion since graduation, for the Class, or a portion of it, has heretofore met in 1935 in New York, in 1936 (Harvard Tercentenary) at Boston, in 1940 at Philadelphia, in 1942 informally in Washington, and in 1946 in New York. We hope to be able to continue these reunions in the future.

The Secretary will appreciate it if classmates will keep him informed of any changes in their addresses, of changes in the firms or in the organizations with which they are severally associated, and of other important developments affecting them or any members of the Class. The information contained in this report has been compiled from the best available sources, but as some members of the Class failed to answer their questionnaires, inaccuracies have doubtless crept into the text. 

Please call these to the attention of the secretary. 

R. Ammi Cutter 

53 State Street 

Secretary 

Boston 9, Mass. 

[Ed. Note: At the 1946 meeting of the Class, Ammi Cutter was elected secretary to succeed Whitney Debevoise who had then resigned.

] 1

This Classbook was produced under the supervision of a Committee consisting of Samuel Becker, Harrison Dimmitt, Hugh Fullerton, Raphael 

Koenig, Francis Plimpton, Robert Sloan, Leonard Wheeler and Arthur

Sutherland. The Committee decided that some biographical material should be included, and devised a questionnaire which was sent to each of the 388 men still living who started at the Law School with us the fall of 1922, or who joined us thereafter. Returns were somewhat slow and a printed follow-up postcard was mailed to everyone. Thereafter by individual correspondence, by telephone, and by the action of local committees in Boston and New York, other strenuous efforts were made to get from each classmate a short account of how he spent the last quartercentury. In all, 230 completed questionnaires were returned. The information they brought, necessarily somewhat condensed, appears in this 

Classbook. 

That every member of a group of nearly four hundred men, busy and widely scattered in the world should send in news of his doings was not to be expected. So, to make the book as complete as possible, every classmate's name with his last known address was included, regardless of whether he returned a questionnaire.

The preparation of these pages has been a pleasure. Young faces, cheerful voices, and great days come to mind as one reads well-remembered names. Some of those voices will no longer be heard, and this is sad news. But surely few groups as small as ours have shown more solid achievement; surely none has reason to feel more cordial ties.

Classbook Committee.
