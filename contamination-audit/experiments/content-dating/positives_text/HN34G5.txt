PREFACE 

The Strathmore Plan is an educational program based on the use of the formula test-teach-practice-test-reteach. It will supplement any course of study in English or Arithmetic which you are using. Grade levels are not indicated on any of the materials, so that a seventh-grade or an eighth-grade pupil who has failed to master any of the skills which are taught in the fifth or sixth grades, for instance, can work on them without feeling that he may be doing work on a lower grade level. 

The Teacher's Manuals of the Strathmore Plan contain a copy of each test, exercise, and practice sheet which the pupil will use for the testing, learning, and practice of English and Arithmetic skills. The areas of subject matter in the Teacher's 

Manual are divided into units of work, each of which is preceded by an inventory test to determine what skills in English or Arithmetic the pupil has not mastered. 

The pupil's record on each of these inventory tests reveals the skill on which he needs further study and practice. The pupil's work sheets which provide this necessary practice are indicated on the inventory test, and may also be found by consulting the Table of Contents or the Suggested Grade Placement Sheets in the Teacher's Manuals. 

Since the practice exercises are keyed to the inventory test, the pupil studies and practices only those skills on which he needs help. Following the practice exercises, there are reviews and a self-test to enable the pupil to determine his progress and his readiness for the mastery test. The mastery test completes the final check of that unit for both the pupil and the teacher. The program is thus flexible and timesaving. It meets the needs of all pupils on any grade level, and pupils with exceptional ability do not spend time on what they already know. 

How to Use the Strathmore Plan 

There are eight procedures suggested for each unit of material: (1) Give the inventory or pre-test (2) Teach to obtain understanding (3) Use appropriate practice material (4) Use review exercise (5) Use additional practice sheets if necessary (6) Use the self-test (7) Use additional practice sheets if necessary (8) Give the mastery test

Each teacher will find the best way of presenting the material to her group. It iii

is suggested, first of all, that the inventory test be given to all pupils to determine what learning steps have been mastered. The results of the inventory test will determine how the class may be divided for teaching and practice exercises. Pupils may work individually, they may work in small groups, or they may work as a class. 

For example, in Unit I on Inventory Test No. 1 in Capitalization, Robert made errors on Parts II and III which clearly indicated that he needed further study and practice on the capitalization of names of persons. As the exercises where Robert's errors were made are numbered and keyed to definite pages of practice and remedial material, the teacher can tell exactly what procedures to follow next, and Robert will practice only those exercises on which he needs help. As he made errors on 

Part II on Inventory Test No. I (Capitalization of names of persons) he will turn to pages 3, 4, and 5 (Exercises 2, 3, 4) in the Teacher's Manual for Capitalization where the remedial material for the correction of these errors is found. The practice exercises for Part III on the use of initials is found on pages 6 and 7 (Exercises 5 and 6). Jimmy, Marilyn, and Edward made errors in sentences 1 and 2 of Part VI.

These children need to study only pages 13, 14, 15, and 16 (Exercises 12, 13, 14, and 15) which involve the capitalization of names of pets. They may work together as a small group on these exercises. All of the pupils may make errors on Part III, and if this happens, the entire group should work together on the particular learning step where capital letters are involved. Reviews are given after pupils have completed the teaching and practice exercises. These are followed by Self-Test No. 1, and Mastery Test No. 1. If the pupil did not make any errors on the Inventory

Test, he may omit the teaching and practice exercises on that particular unit. Each page in the Teacher's Manual for Capitalization has a page designation in the upper right hand corner. The same page number is used on the File Guide for each page so that there is no waste of time in looking for materials. Either Robert or the teacher can find them without difficulty.

A good score on the mastery test assures both teacher and pupil that understanding and mastery of each learning step in those particular skills has resulted. Since a new skill should not be attempted until such understanding and mastery have been accomplished, the mastery test is keyed to definite exercises which makes it possible for the pupil to find the exercises on any learning step that he needs to study and practice again.

Accuracy should be emphasized. When an exercise is first presented to the pupil, the teacher should stress the importance of reading and carefully studying the teaching material at the top of each page. Before the pupil starts to do an exercise or a test, he should be sure that he knows exactly what is expected of him.

Do you see how the use of a sentence makes the meaning clear?
