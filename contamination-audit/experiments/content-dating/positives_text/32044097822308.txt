PREFACE 

During November of 1958, this Commission made its first report to the Legislature and the people of Minnesota covering Minnesota Statutes 1957, chapters 160 through 164 and chapter 166. Many who did not previously have an opportunity to review the work of this Commission were given an opportunity at that time to examine and analyze the proposed procedural and substantive changes in the highway laws. As a result, suggestions were received proposing changes in the first report. Some of these changes were subsequently adopted and are included within this supplemental report under the heading of 

"Amendments to the Commission Report Dated November 1958." 

Numerous suggestions were received during the last few meetings of the Commission proposing amendments to existing laws governing traffic regulations. A number of these suggested changes were adopted and are included herein. Other changes relating to the Safety Responsibility Act, implied consent, and arrests on reasonable grounds for traffic violations were proposed to the Commission; however, because of the seriousness of these proposed changes and the lack of time within which to conduct a detailed analysis, recommendations covering these areas were not adopted. 

In addition to the need for a study of highway traffic regulations, other areas which should be examined in order to complete the revision of Minnesota highway laws include motor vehicle registration and taxation, driver license and safety responsibility, and eminent domain. 

Also, suggestions and recommendations contained in the report of the U. S. Department of Commerce on the Great River Road through the state of Minnesota warrant further study and consideration. Within these areas a highway laws commission to serve during the 1959-60 biennium could accomplish constructive revisions benefiting highway users and state highway agencies. This Commission, therefore, recommends that the 1959 Legislature consider the creation of an interim commission to continue the study of Minnesota's highway laws. iii

SUPPLEMENTAL REPORT OF THE MINNESOTA LEGISLATIVE INTERIM COMMISSION ON HIGHWAY LAWS I. SALARIES OF HIGHWAY PATROLMEN. 

The present basic salaries of members of the highway patrol are below salaries offered for similar positions under local governmental divisions within the Twin City metropolitan area. For many years it has been the practice of highway patrolmen to encourage legislation for salary adjustments independently of civil service adjustments for all state employees. This practice has proved undesirable. The state department of highways has suggested that salary increases be provided for members of the highway patrol to establish salary ranges similar to that of local government and that future adjustments for longevity of service be patterned after salary adjustments for other state civil service employees. This Commission is in accord with the suggestion of the highway department and recommends that legislation be adopted to provide salary increases for highway patrolmen and that salary adjustments be established by law similar to other civil service salary adjustments.

II. USE OF CONTROLLED ACCESS HIGHWAYS. 

The maximum use of controlled-access highways can only be obtained in conjunction with the regulation of the types of vehicles or modes of travel permitted on the highway. In order to control the use of highways with limited access it is recommended that the following authorizations and prohibitions be adopted into law: (a) No person shall drive a vehicle onto or from any controlledaccess roadway except at such entrances and exits as are established by public authority. (b) The Commissioner of Highways may by order, and any public authority may by ordinance, with respect to any controlled-access roadway under their jurisdictions prohibit the use of any such roadway by pedestrians, bicycles or other non-motorized traffic or any vehicle. 1

III. HIGHWAY AND TRAFFIC TERMINOLOGY. 

Recent changes in highway terminology and the lack of definitions concerning various terms used within present statutes requires the addition of the following definitions recommended for adoption by this Commission.

Amend M. S. 1957, section 169.01 by adding the following sub divisions: 

Subd. 54. "Controlled-Access Highway" means every highway, street or roadway in respect to which owners or occupants of abutting lands and other persons having no legal right of access to or from the same except at such points only and in such manner as may be determined by the public authority having jurisdiction over such highway, street or roadway.

Subd. 55. "Implement of Husbandry" means every vehicle designed and adapted exclusively for agricultural, horticultural or livestock-raising operations or for lighting or carrying an implement of husbandry and in either case not subject to registration if used upon the highways.

Subd. 56. "Stand or Standing" means the halting of a vehicle, whether occupied or not, otherwise than temporarily for the purpose of and while actually engaged in receiving or discharging passengers.

Subd. 57. "Stop" means complete cessation from movement. 

Subd. 58. "Stopping" means any halting even momentarily of a vehicle, whether occupied or not, except when necessary to avoid conflict with other traffic or in compliance with the directions of a police officer or traffic control sign or signal. 

Subd. 59. "Urban District" means the territory contiguous to and including any street which is built up with structures de-

voted to business, industry or dwelling houses situated at intervals of less than 100 feet for a distance of a quarter of a mile or more. 

IV. MAXIMUM SPEED LIMITS. 

The marking of highways with "absolute" speed limits has resulted in confusion regarding the meaning of the various speed limits. 

Numerous complaints and inquiries have been received by the department of highways to the effect that many drivers interpret "absolute” as prohibiting a lower speed. This inadequacy in existing law can be changed without difficulty by providing that all speed limits shall be maximum limits and speeds in excess of such a limit shall be unlawful. It is recommended that this change in the law be adopted as outlined below: 

1. Amend M. S. 1957, section 169.14, subd. 2.

Subd. 2. Except when a special hazard exists that requires lower speed for compliance with subdivision 1 of this section, the limits specified in this section or established as hereinafter authorized shall be maximum lawful speeds, and no person shall drive a vehicle on a highway at a speed in excess of such maximum limits: (1) 30 miles per hour in any urban district: (2) 65 miles per hour in other locations during the daytime: (3) 55 miles per hour in such other locations during the nightime.

Daytime means from a half hour before sunrise to a half hour after sunset. 

Nighttime means at any other hour. 

2. Amend M. S. 1957, section 169.14, subd. 2.

Subd. 4. When the commissioner determines upon the basis of an engineering and traffic investigation that any speed set forth in this section is greater or less than is reasonable or safe under the conditions found to exist on any trunk highway or upon any part thereof, he may erect appropriate signs designating a reasonable and safe speed limit thereat, which speed limit shall be effective when such signs are erected. Any speeds in excess of such limits shall be unlawful. 

3. Amend M. S. 1957, section 169.14, subd. 5.

Subd. 5. When the local authorities believe that the existing speed limit upon any street or highway, or part thereof, within their

respective jurisdictions and not a part of the trunk highway system is greater or less than is reasonable or safe under existing conditions, they must request the commissioner to authorize, upon the basis of an engineering and traffic investigation, the erection of appropriate signs designating what speed is reasonable and safe, and the commissioner may authorize the erection of appropriate signs designating a reasonable and safe speed limit thereat, which speed limit shall be effective when such signs are erected. Any speeds in excess of these speed limits shall be unlawful. Alteration of speed limits on streets and highways shall be made only upon authority of the commissioner. 

V. DRIVING REGULATIONS. 

Clarification is necessary in the responsibility of drivers making left-hand turns, approaching yield signs and using multi-lane roads. 

Under present law slow moving traffic is not required to use the right lane of multi-lane highways. Secondly, regulations governing the proper use of yield-right-of-way signs do not include protection of pedestrians legally crossing streets marked with such signs. Also, although driver manuals explain the manner of making left-hand turns, the existing laws governing driving regulations do not include directions for proper left-hand turns. It is recommended that the following changes be incorporated into law to govern in these instances. 

1. Add the following subdivision to M. S. 1957, section 169.18:

Subd. 10. Upon all roadways any vehicle proceeding at less than the normal speed of traffic at the time and place and under the conditions then existing shall be driven in the right-hand lane then available for traffic, or as close as practicable to the right-hand curb or edge of the roadway, except when overtaking and passing another vehicle proceeding in the same direction or when preparing for a left turn at an intersection or into a private road or driveway, or when a specific lane is designated or posted for a specific type of traffic. 

2. Amend M. S. 1957, section 169.19, subd. 1 (2): (2) Approach for a left turn on other than one-way roadways shall be made in that portion of the right half of the roadway nearest the center line thereof, and after entering the intersection the left turn shall be made so as to leave the intersection to the right of the center line of the roadway being entered. Whenever practicable the left turn shall be made in that portion of the intersection to the left of the center of the intersection;

3. Amend M. S. 1957, section 169.201: 

169.201 The driver of a vehicle approaching a YIELD RIGHT- 

OF-WAY sign shall slow to a speed that is reasonable for conditions of traffic and visibility, and stop if necessary, and yield the right-of-way to any pedestrian legally crossing the roadway on which he is driving, and to all vehicles on the intersecting street or highway which are so close as to constitute an immediate hazard. 

VI. OJBECTS SUSPENDED BETWEEN DRIVER AND WINDSHIELD. 

The practice of many drivers in hanging ornaments and medallions from rear-view mirrors and sun visors prevents clear vision of the highway. Because of the inherent danger in obstructing the drivers view, it is recommended that this practice be prohibited by law. The following amendment to M. S. 1957, section 169.71 is proposed: 

169.71 No person shall drive or operate any motor vehicle with a windshield cracked or discolored to an extent to limit or obstruct proper vision, or with any objects suspended between the driver and the windshield, other than sun visors and rear vision mirrors, or with any sign, poster, or other non-transparent material upon the front windshield, sidewings, side or rear windows of such vehicle, other than a certificate or other paper required to be so displayed by law, or authorized by the state director of civil defense.

VII. EQUIPMENT ON VEHICLES. 

Motor vehicle design and equipment are constantly subject to change by the manufacturer. Within recent years many additional safety devices have been added such as two tail lamps, reflectors and stop lamps. Under present law some question is raised as to the legality of these changes on vehicles registered and operating in Minnesota. In order to include these additions in vehicle equipment within present authorizations it is recommended that the following amendments be adopted. 

1. Amend M. S. 1957, section 169.50, subd. 1.

Subdivision 1. Every motor vehicle and every vehicle which is being drawn at the end of a train of vehicles shall be equipped with at least one tail lamp, exhibiting a red light plainly visible from a distance of 500 feet to the rear. And further, every such above-mentioned vehicle, other than a truck tractor, registered in this state and manufactured or assembled after January 1, 1960, shall

be equipped with at least two tail lamps mounted on the rear, on the same level and as widely spaced laterally as practicable, which, when lighted as herein required, shall comply with the provisions of this section. 

2. Amend M. S. 1957, section 169.50, subd. 3.

Subd. 3. On and after January 1, 1960, each new motor vehicle trailer, or semi-trailer, hereafter sold and each such vehicle hereafter operated on a highway, shall carry at the rear either as a part of the rear lamp or separately at least two reflectors. The reflectors shall be of a type approved by the commissioner and shall be mounted as close as is practicable to the extreme edges. of the vehicle at a height not more than 60, nor less than 24, inches above the surface upon which the vehicle stands. Each such reflector shall be so designed and maintained as to be visible at night from all distances within 300 to 50 feet from the vehicle, except that on a commercial vehicle the reflectors shall be visible from all distances within 500 to 50 feet from the vehicle, when directly in front of a motor vehicle displaying lawfully lighted headlamps. 

3. Amend M. S. 1957, section 169.57, subd. 1.

Subdivision 1. (a) Any vehicle may be equipped and when required under this chapter, shall be equipped with at least two stop lamps on the rear which shall emit a red or yellow light and which shall be actuated upon application of the service (foot) brake and which may, but need not be, incorporated with the tail lamps and which shall be plainly visible and understandable from a distance of 100 feet to the rear during normal sunlight and at night. (b) No person shall sell or offer for sale or operate on the highways any motor vehicle registered in this state and manufactured or assembled after January 1, 1960, unless it is equipped with at least two stop lamps meeting the requirements of this section, except that a motorcycle, motor driven cycle or truck tractor manufactured or assembled after said date shall be equipped with at least one stop lamp meeting the requirements of this section. 

4. Amend M. S. 1957, section 169.59 by subdividing the present section and changing subdivision 3. 

169.59 Subdivision 1. Any vehicle may be equipped with not more than two side cowl or fender lamps, one on each side which shall emit a white light without glare.

Subd. 2. Any vehicle may be equipped with not more than one running board courtesy lamp on each side thereof, which shall emit a white or yellow light without glare.

Subd. 3. Any vehicle may be equipped with not more than two back-up lamps, either separately or in combination with another lamp, except that no such back-up lamp shall be continuously lighted when the vehicle is in forward motion, nor shall it project a glaring light.

VIII. OPTIONAL VEHICLE LIGHTS. 

The requirements of Minnesota laws regarding auxiliary lighting equipment are somewhat vague. For clarification purposes it is recommended that the following changes be adopted: 

Amend M. S. 1957, section 169.56, by deleting the second paragraph of the first subdivision, and adding three additional subdivisions.

Subd. 2. Any motor vehicle may be equipped with not to exceed 

2 fog lamps mounted on the front at a height not less than 12 inches nor more than 30 inches above the level surface upon which the vehicle stands and so aimed that when the vehicle is not loaded none of the high-intensity portion of the lights to the left of the center of the vehicle shall at a distance of 25 feet ahead project higher than a level of four inches below the level of the center of the lamp from which it comes. 

Lighted fog lamps meeting the above requirements may be used with lower headlamp beams as specified in section 169.60.

Subd. 3. Any motor vehicle may be equipped with not to exceed two auxiliary passing lamps mounted on the front at a height of not less than 24 inches nor more than 42 inches above the level surface upon which the vehicle stands. The provisions of section 169.60 shall apply to any combination of headlamps and auxiliary passing lamps.

Subd. 4. Any motor vehicle may be equipped with not to exceed two auxiliary driving lamps mounted on the front at a height not less than 16 inches nor more than 42 inches above the level surface upon which the vehicle stands. The provisions of section 169.60 shall apply to any combination of headlamps and auxiliary driving lamps. 

IX. EMERGENCY LIGHTS. 

Minnesota is one of the few states which prohibits the use of flashing lights, except on certain types of vehicles, for the purpose of

warning other highway users of a dangerous condition. The Interstate Commerce Commission has proposed a requirement that all vehicles operating under its jurisdiction be equipped with flashing lights to be used in emergency situations. The following changes and additions to Minnesota law are recommended to permit the use of this type of additional lighting equipment. 

1. Amend M. S. 1957, section 169.59 by adding a new subdivision.

Subd. 4. Any vehicle may be equipped with lamps which may be used for the purpose of warning the operators of other vehicles of the presence of a vehicular traffic hazard requiring the exercise of unusual care in approaching, overtaking or passing, and when so equipped may display such warning in addition to any other warning signals required by this act. The lamps used to display such warnings to the front shall be mounted at the same level and as widely spaced laterally as practicable, and shall display simultaneously flashing white or amber lights, or any shade of color between white and amber.

The lamps used to display such warnings to the rear shall be mounted at the same level and as widely spaced laterally as practicable, and shall show simultaneously flashing amber or red lights, or any shade of color between amber and red. These warning lights shall be visible from a distance of not less than 500 feet under normal atmospheric conditions at night. 

2. Amend M. S. 169.64, subd. 3.

Subd. 3. Flashing lights are prohibited, except on an authorized emergency vehicle, school bus, road maintenance equipment or on any vehicle as a means of indicating a right or left turn, or the presence of a vehicular traffic hazard requiring unusual care in approaching, overtaking or passing. 

X. ENGINEERING SURVEYS AS PRIMA FACIE EVIDENCE. 

As authorized by existing Minnesota law records of engineering surveys and related matters made by engineering departments of any municipality are prima facie evidence of the correctness of the statements and facts contained therein. The proposed amendment outlined below would extend this law to include the state and its agencies. 

599.14. Records of surveys made by the engineering departments of the state or its agencies or made by the engineering department of any municipality, including field notes, profiles, plats, plans, and other files and records of such departments shall be prima facie evidence in all courts of the correctness of the facts shown and statements made therein.

XI. SAFETY RESPONSIBILITY ACT. 

The Safety Responsibility Act currently requires a minimum insurance amount for property damage of $2,000. This amount is deemed entirely inadequate under present conditions and should be increased to $5,000. It is recommended that M. S. 1957, section 170.21, subdivision 10 be amended to provide for $5,000 coverage for injury to or destruction of property, and that similar changes be made in section 

170.25, subdivision 3, and section 170.40, subdivision 2.

The requirement of proof of financial responsibility by the deposit of a cash amount of $11,000 does not correspond to the present requirement that insurance as evidence of financial responsibility be in an amount of $22,000. It is recommended that M. S. 1957, section 170.44, subdivision 1 be amended to require a cash deposit of $25,000 as evidence of financial responsibility. 

Although the term "license" is used throughout the Safety Responsibility Act in reference to drivers of motor vehicles, no definition of this term appears in the act. Existing ambiguities caused by the lack of a definition can be overcome with the adoption of an amendment to M. S. 1957, section 170.21, subdivision 4 as follows:

Subd. 4. "License to operate a motor vehicle" means any operator's or chauffeur's license or any other license or permit to operate a motor vehicle issued under the laws of this state including: (a) Any temporary license or instruction permit; (b) The privilege of any person to drive a motor vehicle whether or not such person holds a valid license; (c) Any non-resident's operating privilege as defined herein.
