FOREWORD 

How many of my readers have had the experience of learning to drive a car? If you have not, you have at least seen others, or know the process necessary. Before the pleasure of driving came the tedious mechanical process of learning to start the car, shift the gears, regulate the gas, keep the car on the road, and observe traffic signals. Could you have learned to drive in any other way? It would have been much pleasanter-unless you appreciate the joy of conquest-to have had the reward without the effort, but that does not seem to be the plan of life. 

Have you

You would not expect to attain great success in any type of work if you had not first mastered the technique. ever seen a workman begin a building without making a definite, accurate and suitable plan? He proceeded so far and found he could go no farther. His windows did not fit, the doors would not swing, and his material was not suited to the task at hand. 

He found he must take down what he had done, make a plan and start over again. Now, he is ready to progress without loss and with assurance of success. That is our purpose in trying to give you a view of the goal to be reached and in building a foundation before giving the more practical, personal, and pleasurable part of our study.

The same principles are applicable to the building of a personality that we find in driving a car or constructing a building. 

The plan, the technical instruction, lays the foundation for what is to follow and, I feel, could not be reversed without losing something of value. Dealing with the subject from this standpoint stimulates, organizes, and unifies the mind which is, in itself, an important part of the process of development. If you knew knew you could not fail to attain success if you followed the rules, would you not make the effort to complete your study and apply its principles to your life? It is true that you cannot fail to increase your capacity for success, health, love, and happiness, if you persevere. A positive purpose and ideal in life, in itself, gives strength and adds a magical influence to your every endeavor. 

Do you know how to form that purpose and direct that ideal? Merely reading the lessons through will enlarge your vision and open the door to a new and thrilling adventure in life, but the degree to which you attain mastery will be determined only by your understanding perseverance.

Since this is the most vital subject to which thinkers can give their attention, one who does not have the interest to carry him through the technique will not have the strength of character and purpose to carry him far in life. In any event, after the rain comes the sunshine. After the work comes the reward. 

I have purposely kept this work as direct and concise as possible. I hoped to make it possible to get the gist of the matter, the perspective before your mind in an evening's study for immediate application. For this purpose it may be read entirely through, then start again and meditate on each thought until its deeper meaning is revealed to you. You will then be able to make a plan for your individual application of the principles outlined.

Read this book over again and again. 

Your subconscious mind will grasp truths that your conscious mind cannot yet comprehend, and the expression of these things will come into your life as springs of water from a hidden source- -even powers not mentioned here for you will find your own hidden source or fountain of life.

If you will take my word as guarantee and faithfully apply the suggestions you will soon find a mystical power beginning to work in your life dissolving barriers and problems, and establishing an inner radiance of joy and life that you have never dreamed possible. The application of these principles not only helps you to live more sucessfully now, but prepares you for the life hereafter. Anything that brings greater harmony in human relationships brings us nearer the kingdom of heaven.

PREFACE 

How many people are conscious of their personal limitations and have a sincere desire to improve their personalities but do not know just how to begin? This was my experience as a very young child. I longed for the finer things of life, poise, charm, beauty, refinement, the ability to meet people with ease and the consciousness of being correct in manners and acceptable in person, but I seemed not to know just what to do about it. I often determined in my heart that, when I grew up, I would find the secret of these things and teach mothers how to help their children in their search for the finer things of life; how to develop their personalities that they might enjoy the richness and fulness of life. The school takes care of the education of the child very largely, but in the development of personality the mother has a definite opportunity and duty. 

I was reared on the farm where life was, at that time, more or less crude. We were not in as close touch with education and the cultural side of life as we are in this day and age. We had no radio, therefore, lectures, plays and music were not readily available. My soul seemed starved for the things that were apparently not a part of my environment and I was often dissatisfied and resentful. It grated on my sensitive nature to hear people make grammatical errors and speak in provincial language, to have to wear coarse clothes, use oil cloth and colored table cloths on the table, and see old fashioned furniture in our homes. If 

I could only have looked forward to see that some of those very styles are the height of fashion today! I found that the fault lay, not so much in my environment, as in the lack of understanding of the values of life and my attitude toward it. Now, I can see a coarsely dressed person and whatever his language may be, find something beautiful and lovely in his soul. In the soul, the inner self, lie the finer values of life. Then I despised to see men smoking or chewing and now even many of the girls smoke. 

I was a delicate child with high ideals, so men's ways often seemed crude and rough to me, which prevented forming friendships with them. This environment left such a critical attitude in my mind that I lost all consciousness of the sweetness and beauty of life. Now I am able to release these imperfections from my thought and find the riches, sweetness, and fulness of life. 

How cramped and stunted a child's early years often are because he does not know how to meet the problems of life. It is my hope

that this book will not only help older people to adjust and prepare themselves for a fuller life and a more successful and pleasing personality, but will aid them in helping the children and youth of today to meet and adjust themselves to life's problems as they come. Thus they may be free from false concepts, conflicts, and inhibitions which warp their lives and hinder the progress of civilization. Professor Ralph Tyler Flewelling of the University of Southern California says, "The greatest gift to society is the gift of great personality, for in realizing himself the individual brings the greatest progress to the social-order.” 

It is hoped you, who are parents, teachers and educators of today realize the responsibility and opportunity that is yours in teaching the individual how to become a social being. In my school days I was usually at the head of my class and always graduated with honors, but knew less about life than the most uneducated. The ability to meet life's problems with peace, poise, and assurance is the greatest education that can be given to any individual, and the development of personality the greatest asset to suscess in any undertaking. 

It is said "In the higher sense personality is the power to turn life in any desired direction with effectiveness . . . If this be true we will surely agree with General Smuts that it is "the most important and fruitful problem to which thinkers can devote their attention." 1

WHY HAVE IT 

PERSONALITY? 

"That Something" which gets you what you want and makes you what you want to be-Personality-is, perhaps, the most coveted of gifts. You may not have recognized "IT"" by that name, nevertheless, it is the basis of the highest success and happiness whether it be personal, social, professional, or even spiritual development. Though this question was answered in part in the introduction it will be of advantage to consider it more fully at this time.

Every success or failure can be traced back to some personal trait, and in truth the only complete failure is the one who fails to develop his own inner power and gifts. You want to be successful, to develop more vital magnetic power and a better personality. But what are you doing about it? 

Would you like to turn your life in the desired direction and make of it a thrilling adventure? Then let us sit down together and awaken within you the magician that can do this very thing. 

That magician is within you. It needs only to be discovered and put to work. It is "That Something" which gives us personality. 

Now what to your mind is the measure of a great personality? 

Your concept will be determined largely by your aim in life and measured in terms of what you wish personality to give you. 

Is that wish merely to be pleasing? Or is it to have the power to win from others the admiration, love, affection, kisses and caresses, favors and gifts you crave? Do you desire to hold the love that is yours and establish a happy home relationship? Would you attract the health, the wealth, the friends, the opportunities that make life worth while? Is your idea of success and happiness social distinction, political honor, supremacy in the business world, or do you "feel quite intuitionally that the growth of the soul is the crowning act of personality?" 

Would you succeed in your profession? Perhaps you aspire to be a movie star. Jimmie Fiddler says Hollywood complains that it is hard to find material for great actors that Hollywood should be looking for great personalities rather than great actors, for it takes great personalities to make great actors. 

It is true that when you have found "That Something" we call "IT" the ability to act becomes the spontaneous expression of life. However this power in not mere sex appeal as it is commonly declared to be as we shall see later. 

Some may call success or failure "luck" or "fate" and we may hear the argument that great personalities are born, not made-you either have IT or you don't have IT. Although it is

true a native capacity influences the ease and rapidity with which we attain success, those who understand the laws of creation know there is cause back of every effect, and that a study of these factors will make it possible to transform the most unpromising individual. So whatsoever you desire in life, if you wish the highest success, begin now to take account of your assetsyour traits of character or personality. 

H. C. Spillman says, "If that 'far-off event toward which creation moves'-be the perfecting of human society; if man is the ultimate effort at evolution: then Personality is the answer to 

Henry Drummond's question, 'What is the greatest thing in the world?' After a century of intense study of the materialistic sciences man turns again to psychology as the mother science because it treats of the greatest creation of all the ages—man himself."

Love can be said to be the greatest thing in the world only when it is translated into action-Being-Personality. It is then the Living Word of God through which and by which all things are created, and through which the human is translated into the Divine.

WHAT IS PERSONALITY? 

Yes, personality is without question one of the most coveted of gifts as well as the source of the highest success and most satisfying attainment, but we have not yet determined just what personality is, for to be truly successful no one faculty must be developed out of proportion to all the other faculties. 

Have you ever met a person who on first appearance made a very pleasing impression but on closer acquaintance the flaws in his character caused you to lose your esteem for him? Napoleon, though one of the greatest, one of the most outstanding charasters in the history of the past, is not thought of today as one of the most admirable characters. His love of power was developed out of all proportion to his love for humanity, which in the end brought about his downfall. William H. Burman says, "The most fundamental characteristic of normal personality is wholeness, unity, integration. Summed up in one phrase we might classify these qualities as integrity of character. Yet this does not define personality.

Personality has been said to be a difficult characteristic to define as well as to develop, and the secret of it has often eluded the most diligent student of its mysteries. Many will not attempt to define it. They say it is a mysterious something that is indefinable. That is, in a sense true, for the revelation of its secret so fills one with awe, words are inadequate to describe it. Yet, let us again emphasize, there are certain principles that may be outlined which will help to lead us to this wonder-waking faculty. 

There have been many books written on the subject of "Personality". However, after all is said, people can teach us more about this vital subject than many books. Seeing is believing. People are more convincing. At least that was my experience. I had always desired and hoped to develop a pleasing personality, but what I read did not seem to penetrate or take hold of my consciousness until I had met a number of people with an unusual development of this power. Studying the secret of their power convinced me unreservedly that the secret of personal magnetism or personality can be summed up in a few vital points, and that these are attainable by all who will make the persistent and consistent effort to attain them. 

After I had become convinced of this fact my study became more profitable. Sometimes we are not so fortunate as to be in the environment of those possessing this remarkable gift for direct study, but, in any event, books are a valuable supplement to direct

observation. An analysis and definition of personality, however imperfect, will be of great assistance in directing our energies toward our goal.

To state our purpose, unify our thought, dispel any questioning in our minds, and enlist our whole hearted support to the task at hand, it will be necessary to study for a moment the differences between individuality and personality. 

Have you ever engaged in a controversy as to whether individuality or personality denotes man's true nature? Are you a

Metaphysician who is inclined to think of personality as the selfish, carnal nature of man? If you are indiviuality to you signifies man's true self. Metaphysically, individuality is defined as the I AM, or Christ Principle. We may accept the definition but we should go farther and think of personality as the Divine 

Principle personified. 

Individuality suggests species, or principle of Being; personality the expression of Being. Anything that touches so deeply the springs of character and creative power as the traits necessary to the development of true personality must be spiritual in its nature. Individuality is defined by Webster as: separate and distinct existence; distinctive character. Personality is defined as: that which constitutes distinction of person. 

John Herman Randall in "Culture of Personality" expresses the thought that we are born individuals. We become personalities. Each individual's expression of life builds his consciousness of life or personality. Each person, in his opinion, becomes a personality at the birth of "self consciousness.' Many people are able to look back to their childhood days and remember the moment when this awakening came to them. Life took on a new meaning. They seemed to look at the world with new eyes. 

New life seemed to permeate every experience, and they felt a keener consciousness of their personal identity. 

If this viewpoint be correct only an order of development higher than the animal can have personality. Therefore personality would signify a higher state of development than individuality. 

Perhaps you are one of those who have felt that animals and flowers do have personality. Perhaps you too have looked into the face of a pansy and felt that a human face was looking back at you. I think there is no other flower that comes so near causing us to question if flowers do not have personality. Dogs sometimes look at us with such an understanding attitude we hesitate to deny their right to claim a place with other personali- A bird, an animal has separate and distinct existence. A ties.

H. C. Spillman says: "Personality is the very ego of man.

We might say, Personality is the very BEING of man. It is more than individuality. It is more than self-consciousness. It is the growth of self-consciousness through relationships-human and divine.

Personality is not a set condition. It is a "growing thing." 

If you will study those about you, I believe you will see what is meant. Many people have individuality and have attained selfconsciousness who do not have that something we usually term "IT." It was my contact with two young men who were so very different in their expression of life that caused me to ponder over the question, "Why does one person have 'that something' which instantly marks him as having personality while another does not?"

One of these men impresses one as having individualitythe other as having personality. In the first, idealism is the expression of character. In the second, it is LIFE expressed. Do you get the point? It was necessary at first, to ponder over the reason for the difference in these two people. The first is equally as fine as the second in intellect, initiative, and ability, and the intellectual understanding of Truth and idealism of life is even greater. But there is something lacking in his personal appeal. 

He is liked, but there is not the compelling presence that would command undying love and loyalty. There is an impersonal attitude that places a barrier between himself and those he meets. 

As a result he does not radiate the human sympathy, the vital force, and radiance of life that compel the wonder and admiration of those about him.

The secret of power in the second is his consciousness of life expressed in relationship to others—his realization and radiance of life, peace, poise, reserve, animation, human sympathy, and service.

Personality includes individuality, but individuality does not include personality. "Individuality suggests the unsocial" (separation of oneself from others) "personality the social" (responsiveness to others.) "It is born of the very contact of man with man." And I would say the very contact of man with God. It represents love and service, the basic principles of being. 

Nearly every person has, at some time in his life, asked himself the question, "Why am I here and whither am I going?" If

Many of the Masters of the far East believe and teach that the highest good is attained by seclusion. Their objective is 

Nirvana, Oneness with God or The All. We of the Christian world believe that through love and service the highest development is attained. That was the example given us by the Master

Life is three-fold. Personality is the expression of the complete individual. To be complete each person must express himself through the human touch with others as well as the divine, and through the divine as well as the human. In this lies the secret of immortality, continuance of existence as an individualized being. That, I believe, was Jesus Christ's concept of immortality. Was not that His objective in returning to earth after 

His crucifixion-to prove the immortality of the individual soul?

If your conception of the goal of man is the perfecting of the soul, attaining conscious self-awareness of BEING or essential 

God-hood, you will not condemn personal development. To deny the personality of either ourselves or God, as some do, is to deny the creative power of expression as an individual. Oneness with God should be a oneness of agreement, oneness with beauty, love, life and perfection, a harmony of purpose-not fusion of person or loss of identity. God lives in us as life, as love, as beauty, as perfection. We will then recognize God both as the principle of life and love, and as that principle personified in each person. This does not mean that we vizualize a personal God sitting on a throne but it does mean that we recognize ourselves as the likeness of a God or Universal Being having the attributes of person. 

Ralph Tyler Flewelling, Professor of Philosophy of the 

University of Southern California says, "If the cosmic intelligence is conscious of a developing world-order, then it must be conscious of itself in relation to that development and in some measure its experiences must fall under the temporal form. It can be held truly to transcend the time series while it works under it, and the existence of purpose in the world-order as truly calls for transcendence of the temporal order as for immanence in it. This peculiar demand is met only when we look upon the cosmic Intelligence as a creative and self-conscious personality."

It is true that the wrong use of personality-for the purpose of attraction or possession-is carnal, temporary and unfavorable to spiritual growth. Napoleon may again be used as an example. 

His objective was personal power, personal gain-not service to humanity. His biographers quote him as saying 'Peace is opposed to my interests.' And Madame de Stael describes him as "neither good nor bad, neither gentle nor cruel. He is unique; he can neither inspire nor feel affection; he is more and less than a man. Character, mind, speech-all have a strange stamp. This very strangeness helps him to win over the French. "He neither hates nor loves; for him, no one exists but himself; all other people are merely 'number so-and-so.' A great chess-player, for whom humanity-at-large is the adversary he hopes to checkmate. His success is quite as much due to the qualities he lacks as the qualities he possesses ... Where his own interest is involved, he pursues it as the just man seeks virtue; if his aim were good, his perseverance would be exemplary He despises the nation whose applause he seeks; there is not a spark of fervor intermingled with his craving to astound mankind... I have never been able to breathe freely in his presence."

We can readily see that this type of development is not favorable to spiritual growth, but development through radiation -service—is spiritual, eternal, and the basic principle of all creative expression in life. Contrast the life of Napoleon with that of Jane Addams, one of the most outstanding personalities among women in America, whose whole life was devoted to the service of humanity.

We see, then, that personality may be either good or bad according to its use. We, therefore, do not deny the personality, we seek to understand and spiritualize it. We do not condemn the personality, we seek to purify it, to transform it into the 

Divine Ideal. 

Many people who are not conscious of their spiritual power or the spiritual nature of their Being, may have wonderful personalities which they may use for material and selfish ends for the time being. But there is no material expression without a spiritual power back of it, even though it be unrecognized and misdirected. Those who have attractive personalities must be more creative and constructive in some way than those who have not. 

Someone made the remark that it is usually the irresponsible person-the person who refuses to take responsibility-that has personality. Underneath this characteristic lies a creative principle-he refuses to worry. We can learn a lesson from him and even improve upon it. We can keep our sense of responsibility

The law will, sooner or later, catch them and trip them up, if they disobey it. If we wish to avoid ultimate failure and loss of creative power we will work from the standpoint of Principle or Universal Law-not merely the desire for gain.

THE SUPREME ACT OF CREATIVE LIVING Choosing Your Consciousness 

How is consciousness of life brought into expression? What is the secret of creative living? 

If we go back to the history of creation we find that the creative process began with the WORD or creative idea as the germ or seed. God said- was the first step, then Spirit moved in the idea or acted on the seed thought and brought life into Being through living experience. So the material from which our consciousness of life or Being is built springs from our thoughts and actions. If the thoughts that habitually occupy your mind are consctructive and creative you have found the secret of creative living—the channel through which desired conditions are brought into your experience. The stone has been rolled from the door of your earthly tomb and you now dwell in the temple of the living God. But most of us have not yet rolled away the stone. 

Of what is your consciousness built?

If you were to ask yourself the question and attempt to answer it you would probably say, "Well, my consciousness is built largely of questioning 'How shall I ever forget that experience? Why do my friends treat me so? Don did not seem so attentive tonight, I wonder if he still loves me. Will Sue be able to win him from me? What shall Ido if I lose my position? Am 

I in my right place, my right vocation? I just feel I am going to be ill. I find much of my time occupied with regretting the past, feeling dissatisfied with the present, and fearing the future.' 

Do you wish your consciousness to be built of these thoughts? Are they true, wise, and creative-success producing? 

Are they purposeful? Do they have the power to recharge your mind, body, and soul with revitalizing energy-or do they ground the batteries of life? 

I hear you answer: "In my present mental state I must admit there are true ideas and false. There are thoughts of wisdom and thoughts of ignorance. There are idle, purposeless thoughts, and there are constructive, creative thoughts. Yes, I can see on reflection these thoughts and ideas produce success and life, or failure and death. "When I stop to consider, no, I do not wish my mind to be occupied with negative, idle thoughts. But how am I to avoid this condition? Is not the content of consciousness determined by the daily needs and demands of life? Do I determine my consciousness, my life, or is it fore-ordained to be as it is? "Just how do we determine the content of our consciousness?"

Will has been called the supreme act of personality. It is often thought of as the power to force, but its true meaning and use lies in the power to choose. Life must be a spontaneous process and not a forced expression if it is to complete its fullest growth and most perfect manifestation in the individual. The first act, therefore, in creative living is the willing or choosing to accept or reject any concept of the mind or impulse of the emotions that is presented to the consciousness. For instance: I can choose to be loving and gracious or I can choose to be spiteful and hateful. I can choose to think common, sordid thoughts or 

I can choose to think beautiful, elevating thoughts. If I allow myself to think I am unattractive, I will form that picture or vision in my mind, and I will see unattractive people and sordid things whereever I go. If I choose to think of the beauty of the Christ

Spirit within me and others, I will form that picture or vision in my mind, and I will see beauty everywhere and in all things.

When accepted, an idea becomes a nucleus or mould about which and through which substance, energy of life and power take form and develop into that which we have cherished and fed with our thoughts, ideals, dreams, and actions. 

John Herman Randall says, ". . . Our minds seem in some real sense to be nourished by the raw materials of knowledge that we take in from the outside world. There is a close parallel here to the physiological processes. The raw material of thought is taken into the mind through the senses, and there, is digested by the thought processes and is actually assimilated by the mind until it becomes a part of the mind's permanent possessions, and is given out again in countless forms. This means that as we go through the experiences of every day, we can by the powers of observation gather much or little of the raw material out of which thinking is made . . . . It becomes of vital importance, therefore, how we choose and what we choose to allow to occupy the mind. 

Like attracts like. And like produces like. "By their fruits ye shall know them." 

If the fruits of your thoughts are not what you wish them to be, stop and analyze yourself. Take an inventory of the fruits of your past life and then determine what you wish the fruits of your future life to be. Plan to feed and cherish those you wish to grow and begin to drop from your thought those that no not strengthen your character and help you to express your highest aims. Are you thinking thoughts of life, love, joy, beauty, loveliness and success? If so, these are the things of which your life will ultimately be built. By choosing our thoughts and actions we are able to transform our natures and cause our personalities to radiate and attract that which we desire. 

If we wish our manifestation of life to be complete and perfect the soil in which it grows must be well fertilized, watered, and the planting cultivated and given time to grow. Be patient. Anxiety and haste project your thought energy out of the body instead of letting it develop and build that which you wish within. Never force a demonstration or experience you desire. You will save yourself many sad experiences in life if you will remmber this. 

Choose your ideas, cherish and feed them, but allow the subconscious mind time to gather substance, life, and power to bring harmonious, complete, and perfect manifestation. For instance, if you are seeking to develop a consciousness of love, think love and respond to every opportunity to express love, but do not try to

force, either yourself or others, to feel love. The impulse or inspiration to express from within is your cue for action--not a forced expression of the will. It is then not merely action, but action impregnated with life and creative power. It is important that you get this point for it touches deeply the secret of the magnetic power to have and to hold that which you desire in life. These impulses will be strengthened as you rely more fully on the guidance of the inner self.

This secret I learned from one who has the most beautiful magnetic power I have ever contacted. A friend of mine often exclaims "What heaven must be!" And that is how I feel when I contact the Spirit of this person. My troubles seem to vanish as clouds before the sunshine. You, too, can develop this power if you will let the inner self reveal the way. Act on your higher inspirations which come from the Christ in you. 

Ideas are accepted by the conscious mind and carried into action by the sub-conscious mind or Spirit. There must, therefore, be harmony between the conscious and sub-conscious minds. Just how can we do this?

In a sense, the way to begin is just to begin exercising faith, decision, and love, but I have found there are handicaps that will be more readily eliminated by a better understanding of the

creative processes of life. All life is dual in nature, or has opposite poles of expression, as: hot and cold; high and low; ebb and flow; inner and outer; negative and positive; idea and action. 

The action and reaction of one to the other produces an energizing and equalizing power which, in human life, will best be under stood as ATTITUDE and RESPONSE. 

Attitude and response is the method by which we shift the gears of life from low to high, or reverse. When we understand this process we can throw ourselves into high gear and lift ourselves out of the ruts of habit and circumstance. Remember, when you get into a rut you do not need to stay there. Adjust your mental attitude toward the situation and you will speedily be lifted up and out onto the success paved highway of life again.

In attitude and response lies the energizing, vitalizing, generating power which constitutes the human dynamo, as well as the directive, formative and creative power of thought. By our attitude of mind, we change fear to faith and courage, and the response from within is increased energy of life. By fear, we reverse the life processes, and the reverses are outpictured in our minds, bodies and affairs as failures, ill health and unhappiness.

Many have failed to develop this dynamic power because they have not understood the relationship of idea and action, or attitude and response, and have not worked from a true basis of interpretation. There are many attitudes in life that need to be corrected and our basis of interpretation must be one of impartial and unselfish judgment. We must be able to detach ourselves from situations and look upon them from the standpoint of Universal Law or Universal Love rather than preconceived opinion, habit, or the selfish desires of a narrow mind. We will then be able to adjust ourselves to life and release within us that inner sense of power and inspiration that overcomes worry, fear, criticism, inharmony and actually lifts us and sweeps us onward to our goal.

Most of the inharmonies of life seem childish and absurd when viewed impersonally from the standpoint of love, law, order, and justice. Or when we get far enough from our problems to see ourselves as others see us. It would seem quite amusing to the onlooker to witness a woman pack up her goods and "go home to mother" because her mate uses the pronoun "I" too much, yet that is what often happens. It would seem that such a trifling matter could be harmoniously adjusted if there were not unreasoning pride and stubbornness in either or both parties.

SUMMARY 

In every person there are two selves, the higher and the lower, the inner and the outer, the human and the divine. Our task is to bring these into unity of purpose, harmony of action, and completeness of expression. We will then possess a supreme personality.

For years I tried to express more life and to develop more originality, self-confidence, initiative, creative power, tact, and radiance but I seemed somehow to be missing the point of contact until I began to bring myself into mental peace and harmony with the Universal Law and to understand the true values of life. 

Now, I no longer strive for these things. They are gradually becoming the spontaneous expression of the inner life or soul. It is no longer a natural tendency to procrastinate, to shrink from life, to take life too seriously, to be self-conscious, to teach others what they should do, or to take the responsibility for their lives on my shoulders. Yes, all these faults were mine and many more. So do not feel discouraged if the list seems at first appalling. Remember, it is not through effort that we change these conditions, but by changing our attitude toward life and our response to it. 

This may take constant vigilance but it will not take mental effort if we just mentally release the condition, figuratively lay it on the table before us, and view it with a desire to understand and adjust ourselves to it. This attitude not only solves our problems, but builds that inner sense of power and capacity which brings supreme development.

ASPIRATION 

Aspiration: a yearning desire for something higher and better than that already possessed. 

Is there a living soul who does not aspire to what to him seems higher and better? If it were not so, would there be any progress, any higher development? By conscious, intelligent direction of our aspirations, our spiritual development will be much more rapid, complete, and harmonious. Aspiration, setting the attention on a certain definite goal, is an attractive, organizing, and integrating power. Aspiration bears the same relation to man that a sugar bowl bears to ants. Ants will travel miles to a sugar bowl, so man's aspirations will attract from the far reaches of the universe that which he sincerely desires to attain. 

Desire the greater gifts.

FAITH 

"Faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen." (Heb. II:I.)

Faith is not merely belief; it is the substance of things hoped for and the magnetic power which brings into evidence things not seen. Faith is increased by use. Every day brings opportunities for the exercise of faith. 

Paul says, "Faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God."

When we hear and accept or believe in a truth, we have taken the first step in developing the power of faith. Accept the truth that you are potentially perfect and complete in BEING. 

This attitude of mind we call belief. Faith is the attitude, plus the vision and creative power to bring things hoped for into being.

We know that we must have the inner vision, or knowing, which gives us the courage to take a step into the unseen, before faith is rewarded. Then we must be steadfast to our vision, we must keep our eye single to our purpose, for "If thine eye shall be single thy whole body shall be full of light," the light which leads you to your goal. The single eye of faith develops psychic vision, gives power to peer into the future, and lights the path to attainment. Do not let doubt, discouragement, or neglect cloud your vision or impede your progress. The rapidity and degree of attainment will depend on your singleness of purpose. 

The third step in developing faith is action. We may believe that a thing is possible or true, but we must act on that belief to give it creative power. James stated this truth quite graphically when he said, "Thou believest there is one God; thou doest well: the devils also believe and tremble. "But wilt thou know, O vain man, that faith without works is dead?"

So if our faith has not been rewarded, we may know we have not kept our eyes actively and faithfully single to the truth.

Gentleness takes the strain out of life, adds lines of softness and loveliness to form and features-soul and quality to the voice. 

A gentle nature is always loved and adored, however humble its station in life may be. Cultivate gentleness of nature.

GRACIOUSNESS 

One of the most beautiful qualities of character is graciousness. "Graciousness" is the essence, or fragrance, of the Grace of God mirrored in, and reflected from, your mind and soul. It is as elusive, ineffable and indescribable as the fragrance of the rose. Basil King says, "The nearest I can come to it is that it was the outward expression of beauty within, the action of GRACE in expressing and making ourselves lovable . . . . the place to look for grace is... in trying to cultivate a beauty which is so obviously that of God."

Perhaps there is no place where graciousness is needed more than in a home, especially where it is necessary to live with those who are not members of our own immediate family. Compare this experience, where graciousness solved the problem, with the case of the proverbial Mother-in-law: "I recall a woman whom I knew in my youth, whose beauty, charm, and indescretion had become legendary, up and down the

country-side. Well, the time arrived when, old, penniless, and helpless, she came on the hands of a son-in-law who didn't approve of her, and a severe, tight-lipped daughter. Husband, lovers, beauty, means were all in the past, and the only refuge was one which would not have opened could it have stayed shut. 

But one thing she hadn't lost, and that was graciousness. Into a grim household, she brought the light of sweetness. Charmed by everything done for her, she soon charmed those who were doing it. Son-in-law, daughter, grandchildren, all came to delight in her, and when she died-contrary to all rules of poetic justice, considering her career—she went down lamented into an honored grave. "Graciousness begets graciousness, as a smile begets a smile." 

SWEETNESS OF DISPOSITION 

Many people are keeping from themselves their heart's desire by attitudes of jealousy and intolerance, and a crude and dominating manner. Jealousy can never give you the power to have and to hold the love you seek. Through understanding, you can have the power to hold the love that is rightfully yours.

Sweetness and gentleness of nature have greater power to inspire love than fame, beauty, or money, and anyone can have these qualities who will make the effort to cultivate them. Yes, butwill not take us anywhere or do anything for us. Just get busy and you will be rewarded.

COURTESY 

The value of courtesy should be too well recognized to need comment here, yet many people are not obliging or thoughtful of the happiness of others. "Give and ye shall receive" is the Universal Law.

We have often heard that "Politeness is to do and say, the kindest thing in the kindest way." When in doubt as to what to do, the rule to do the thing that seems kindest to you will carry you a long way. But a study and application of the laws of etiquette will add to your comfort, your self-confidence, and help you to establish a feeling of equality with anyone with whom you may associate. Knowing the right thing to do and say at all times will give a feeling of wealth a million dollars cannot give. 

Today, do something to increase your knowledge and use of etiquette.

ANIMATION—ENTHUSIAM 

Animation and enthusiasm radiate the spirit of progress, growth, and illumination. They are as necessary to the growth of the soul as sunshine to the plant. We turn naturally to those things that are inspiring and life giving, and away from those things that depress. Animation and enthusiasm preserve the 

Spirit of Youth, which is always a magnet of attraction—and rightly so, for Youth is an attribute of God. We can neither be youthful and attractive, nor express the attributes of the Divine Nature, if we allow discordant emotions to control us. 

Animation, enthusiasm, love, and cheerfulness are the leaven of life and youth. A little of this spiritual leaven in a darkened room, or an unyielding circumstance, will lighten the whole condition. 

Affirm: The Radiant Light of Spirit animates and inspires me, awakening life, light and love in all it touches.

HUMOR 

How often a sense of humor proves to be the saving grace of a tense moment or trying circumstance! To many, it does not come spontaneously or naturally. It must be cultivated. Plan to read some good jokes frequently, and learn a few to use when the psychological moment arrives. It is a splendid means of opening the way to take part in conversation. One radio lecturer gives ability to engage in interesting conversation as one of the nine most important factors in personality development. "The character and state of culture stand revealed in the type of humor enjoyed." (Abraham Myerson, M. D.)

CONVERSATION 

To develop the power to converse well with others, observe the following:

Strict attention to what is being said. 

Assume an attitude of thoughtfulness and responsiveness to others.

Maintain a keen and varied interest in life and people. 

A conservative and tactful use of questions, to learn your associate's interests in life-especially if he needs drawing out. 

Be a good listener when your companion is the versatile one. 

Have a store of anecdotes, jokes or illustrations to enliven the conversation where appropriate. 

Be gracious-and tolerant of others' opinions. 

Do not force your opinions and ideas on others.

Be well read-particularly on current topics of interest.

RHYTHM People who are awkward, self-conscious and lacking in vitality usually lack a sense of rhythm. Life is vibratory actionrhythm. When one becomes responsive to the inner life, he contacts the heart beat of the universe, and feels its streams of vibrant life flowing through him in joyous cadence. 

This rhythm of life may also be found in music. Whenever you hear music, listen for the melody and rhythm in it. It is one of the finest ways of learning to respond to the highest and most beautiful vibrations of life. If you play, sing or dance, it will strengthen, to an even greater degree, your sense of rhythm. 

There is nothing better than music for bringing the whole body into harmonious action. When you are tempted to let thoughts mill over and over again in your mind, substitute a song in their place. Inharmonious thoughts subtract from life. Harmonious rhythms add to youth, beauty, and the radiance of life. 

Music may be found in many walks of life: in the swish of the trees in the breeze, the rippling brook, singing birds, the beat of the tide, the rhythm of poetry and prose, the voice of a friend, Page Ninety-nine

or the emotions of the soul. Lend yourself to the rhythms of life, and you will be rewarded with increasing vitality, restfulness, poise, grace, buoyancy of spirit, and inspiration..
