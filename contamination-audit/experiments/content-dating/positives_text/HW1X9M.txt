CHAPTER XXIII  

Candace Belle had resumed her school work when the new building opened in September, but the village was still fearful that, unless a way could be found to provide the piano for the auditorium, she would yet resign in favor of her offer in Archer. The probability was discussed in all the social circles of the village, even to the assembly gathered about the stove in the Hayford store, as November gales gave the regulars an excuse for being there and likewise invited customers to tarry.

Gage overheard such a conversation one dismal afternoon, but he ventured no opinion as to what Miss Corwin might or might not do. He smiled, but his thoughts were of a serious character. He had visited the store expressly for a talk with Walter Bartlett but found him busy with trade. "Stick around; I want to see you," Bartlett said. His urgent work at Roaring Brook was to close up the last crack and crevice in the old barn for the greater comfort of his Guernseys. And, as the early darkness settled down, accompanied by chilling mists and drizzling rain, it was a satisfaction to know that all creatures in his charge were well fed and comfortably housed. 

That evening he sat before a wood fire in the snug old living-room, thinking of the future. Candace Belle was willing to give up her school work to come to Roaring Brook at the close of the first semester, in January. 

There was much he must do to put the dwelling in good order. Yet how much was it wise to do, in face of the fact that Walter Bartlett could give him no assurance as to the future of the farm?
