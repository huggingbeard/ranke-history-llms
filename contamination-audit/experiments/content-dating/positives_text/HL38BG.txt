FUNDAMENTALS OF THE DISTRICT POWER LAW Washington Public Utility Districts 

1. The right of the people in a local county to form a Public Utility 

District by majority vote with local autonomy on District affairs. 

2. The right of eminent domain whereby the will of the majority may be exercised. 

3. A locally elected commission to form and administer the Public 

Utility District and thereby provide and maintain the "home rule" fundamental of a democracy in utility service. 

4. Mandatory appointment and employment of qualified utility management. 

5. The right to finance by revenue bonds on a business basis with private funds. 

6. Development based on a district-wide nature to the benefit of all inhabitants rural and urban. 

7. Establishment of rates and services on a fair and nondiscriminatory basis. 

8. Payment of Taxes to local and state governments to prevent loss of tax revenues in the transfer of utility operation from private monopoly to public district ownership. 

9. Cooperation by mutual agreement among Districts on utility operations to the benefit of the individual Districts. 

10. Placement of service above profit in utility service.

Reproduction of Title 54, Revised Code of Washington, which is the law governing Public Utility Districts, has been made possible through cooperation between Secretary of State Earl Coe and this Association. 

The law, PUD directory, and other information are furnished in response to many requests from residents of our State. "County light" systems, or as legally known, "Public Utility Districts", have been organized by majority vote of the people in 29 of the State of Washington's 

39 counties.

These local, publicly-owned utility systems are based on the initiative law passed in 1930 by a majority vote of the people of the entire State. 

At present there are 24 active Districts which operate electric and/or water utility systems in 23 counties (two systems are in Mason County). 

PUDS were formed to (1) combat the high electric rates charged by the private utilities in the 1920's and 1930's, and (2) provide rural electrification which was then being denied many farm families. 

With the excellent example of local public power present in the city light systems of Seattle, Tacoma and nearly twenty other Washington cities, county people established their own county controlled and operated utilities. 

Although operating a very short period as compared to most of the other utilities in the Northwest (first county-wide PUD operation was in 1940, and a majority of present PUD customers are served by systems which started after 1945), the record of the local publicly-owned county electric and water systems is very good on rates, services and financial stability. 

This Association, as the voluntary service organization of these Districts, will be glad to furnish further information or answer any questions within our province upon request.

54.04.030 Restrictions on invading other municipalities. This title does not repeal or affect any existing law relating to the construction, operation, and maintenance of public utilities by irrigation or water districts or other municipal corporations. 

A district shall not include therein any part of a municipal corporation when the municipal corporation already owns or operates all the utilities herein authorized; if it does not own or operate all such utilities it may be included within a district for the purpose of establishing or operating therein such utilities as it does not own or operate. No property within an irrigation or water district or other municipal corporation shall ever be taxed or assessed to pay for a utility, or part thereof, of like character to a utility owned or operated by the irrigation or water district or other municipal corporation. 

[1931 c 1 § 12; RRS § 11616.]

54.04.050 Group employee insurance. A district engaged in the operation of electric or water utilities may contract for group insurance for the benefit of ten or more of its employees, and pay all or any part of the premiums therefor out of the revenue derived from the operation of its properties. If the premium is to be paid by the district and employees jointly, and the benefits of the policy are offered to all eligible employees, not less than seventy-five percent of the employees may be so insured. 54.04.060 District elections. The supervisor of elections or other proper officer of the county shall give notice of all elections held under this title, for the time and in the manner and form provided for city, town, school district, and port district elections. When the supervisor or other officer deems an emergency exists, and is requested so to do by a resolution of the district commission, he may call a special election at any time in the district, and he may combine or divide precincts for the purpose of holding special elections, and special elections shall be conducted and notice thereof given in the manner provided by law. 

The supervisor or other officer shall provide polling places, appoint the election officers, provide their compensation, provide ballot boxes, and ballots or voting machines, poll books and tally sheets, and deliver them to the election officers at the polling places, publish and post notices of the elections in the manner provided by law, and apportion to the district its share of the expense of the election. 

The manner of conducting and voting at the elections, opening and closing of polls, keeping of poll lists, canvassing the votes, declaring the result, and certifying the returns, shall be the same as for the election of state and county officers, except as otherwise provided herein.

The district commission shall certify to the supervisor a list of offices to be filled at a district election and the commission, if it desires to submit to the voters of the district a proposition, shall require the secretary of the commission to certify it at the time and in the manner and form provided for certifying propositions by the governing board of cities, towns, and port districts. 54.04.090 Minimum wages. Each contractor and subcontractor performing work for a public utility district or a local utility district within a public utility district shall pay or cause to be paid to its employees on the work or under the contract or subcontract, not less than the minimum scale fixed by the resolution of the commission prior to the notice and call for bids on the work. The commission, in fixing the minimum scale of wages, shall fix them as nearly as possible to the current prevailing wages within the district for work of like character. [1955 c 124 § 4. 54.04.100 Rates for wholesale power-Procedure. Whenever a decree of public use and necessity has been entered in condemnation proceedings conducted by a district for the acquisition of electrical distribution properties, or whenever it has executed a contract for the purchase of such properties, the district may file with the public service commission, a copy of the contract or a certified copy of the decree, together with a petition requesting that the public service commission cause a rate to be filed with it for the sale of wholesale power to the district, and thereupon the public service commission shall order that such a rate be filed with it forthwith. The public service commission may enter such an order as to any public service corporation which owns or operates the electrical distribution properties being condemned or purchased or as to any such corporation which owns or operates transmission facilities within a reasonable distance of the distribution properties and which engages in the business of selling wholesale power. The rate filed shall be for the period of service specified by the district, or if the district does not specify a particular period, the rate shall apply from the commencement of service until the district terminates it by thirty days' written notice. 

[1945 c 130 § 2, part; Rem. Supp. 1945 § 10459-12, part.]

54.08.010 When district includes entire county-Petition-Notice-Ballots. At any general election the board of county commissioners of a county may, or on petition of ten percent of the electors of the county based on the total vote cast in the last general election, shall, by resolution, submit to the voters of the county the proposition of creating a public utility district coextensive with the limits of the county. The petition shall be filed with the county auditor, who shall within fifteen days examine the signatures thereon and certify to the sufficiency thereof, and for such purpose the auditor shall have access to all registration books in the possession of election officers in the county. If the petition is found to be insufficient, it shall be returned to the persons filing it, who may amend or add names thereto for ten days, when it shall be returned to the auditor, who shall have an additional fifteen days to examine it and attach his certificate thereto. No person having signed the petition may withdraw his name therefrom after it is filed. When the petition is certified as sufficient, the auditor shall forthwith send it, together

54.08.020 When district includes less than entire county-Petition-Hearing-Boundaries to be fixed. A petition for the formation of a district may describe a less area than the entire county in which the petition is filed, the boundaries of which shall follow the then existing precinct boundaries and not divide any voting precinct. If such a petition is filed, the county commissioners shall fix a date for a hearing thereon, and publish the petition, without the signatures, for two weeks prior to the date of the hearing, together with a notice stating the time of the meeting when the petition will be heard. The publication and all other publications required by this title, shall be in a newspaper published in the district, or, if there is no such newspaper, then in a newspaper published in the county in which the district is situated, and of general circulation in the county. The hearing on the petition may be adjourned from time to time, not exceeding four weeks in all. If upon the final hearing the county commissioners find that any lands have been unjustly or improperly included in the proposed district and will not be benefited thereby, they shall change and fix the boundary lines in such manner as they deem just and conducive to the public welfare and convenience, and shall enter an order establishing the boundary lines of the proposed district: Provided, That no lands shall be included within the boundaries so fixed lying outside the boundaries described in the petition, except upon the written request of the owners thereof. Thereafter the same procedure shall be followed as prescribed for the formation of a district embracing an entire county, except that the petition and election shall be confined solely to the lesser area. 54.28.010 Definitions. As used in this chapter: "Tax commission" means the state tax commission; "Operating property" means all of the property utilized by a public utility district in the operation of a plant or system for the generation, transmission, or distribution of electric energy for sale; "Taxing districts" means counties, cities, towns, school districts, and road districts; "Distributes to consumers" means the sale of electric energy to ultimate consumers thereof, and does not include sales of electric energy for resale by the purchaser. [(i) 1941 c 245 § 1, part; Rem.

Supp. 1941 § 11616-1, part. (ii) 1949 c 227 § 1 (f); Rem. Supp. 1949 § 11616-2 (f).] 

54.28.020 Tax imposed-Rates. There is hereby levied and there shall be collected from every district a tax for the act or privilege of engaging within this state in the business of operating works, plants or facilities for the generation, distribution and sale of electric energy. With respect to each such district, such tax shall be the sum of the following amounts: (1) Two percent of the gross revenues derived by the district from the sale of all "distributed energy," i.e., electric energy which it distributes to consumers but neither generates nor purchases from generating districts; (2) five percent of the gross revenues derived by the district from the sale of all "self generated and distributed energy," i.e., the electric energy which it distributes to customers and also generates; and (3) five percent of the gross revenues derived by the district from the sale of "distributed energy purchased from another generating district," i.e., electric energy which it distributes to consumers and also purchases from another district which generated the same. [1949 c 227 § 1(a), last am'ds 1941 c 245 § 2(a); Rem. Supp. 1949 § 11616-2 (a).] 

Severability: "If any section, subsection, clause, sentence or phrase of this act be for any reason adjudged unconstitutional, such adjudication shall not invalidate the remaining portions of this act, and the legislature hereby declares that it would have enacted this act notwithstanding the omission of the portion so adjudicated invalid." 54.28.070 Municipal taxes-May be passed on. A city or town in which a public utility district operates works, plants, or facilities for the distribution and sale of electricity may levy and collect from the district a tax on the gross revenues derived by the district from the sale of electricity within the city or town, exclusive of the revenues derived from the sale of electricity for purposes of resale. The tax when levied shall be a debt of the district, and may be collected as such. The district may add the amount of the tax to the rates or charges it makes for electricity so sold within the city or town. 

[1941 c 245 § 3; Rem. Supp. 1941 § 11616-3.] 

54.28.080 Additional tax for payment on bonded indebtedness of school districts. Whenever any district acquires an operating property from any private person, firm, or corporation and a portion of the operating property is situated within the boundaries of any school district and at the time of such acquisition there is an outstanding bonded indebtedness of the school district, then the public utility district shall, in addition to the tax imposed by this chapter, pay directly to the school district a proportion of all subsequent payments by the school district of principal and interest on said bonded indebtedness, said additional payments to be computed and paid as follows: The amount of principal and interest required to be paid by the school district shall be multiplied by the percentage which the assessed value of the property acquired bore to the assessed value of the total property in the school district at the time of such acquisition. Such additional amounts shall be paid by the public utility district to the school district not less than fifteen days prior to the date that such principal and interest payments are required to be paid by the school district. Public Utility Districts may operate water utility systems as well as electric utilities. (RCW 54.16.030.) 

At the present time eleven of the PUDs have water systems in operation. largest and most extensive system is operated by the

The 

PUD water systems, like electric operations, are financed initially primarily through issuance of revenue bonds. However, local communities within a PUD county have the right to form local utility districts to permit support of a water system through special tax levies where initial sales revenues will not support needed construction financing. (RCW 54.16.120.) A number of suburban areas adjacent to some of the 

State's leading cities have organized PUD local utility districts in order to secure the advantages of a publicly owned and operated water utility service. 

PUDS may also own and operate irrigation systems, but so far no District has exercised this right. (RCW 54.16.030.)

Facts The following facts on Public Utility Districts are emphasized because of their major importance and interest: 

TAXES 

The basic principle of the laws covering PUD taxes is to protect local and State tax funds from any tax void which might occur by the transfer of the utility property from private ownership to public ownership.

Public Utility Districts pay the following local and State taxes: 

1. The PUD privilege tax. (RCW Chapter 54.28.) This replaces the property tax paid by the privately-owned utilities. Since the privilege tax is based on gross revenues, it grows automatically as revenues increase. 

2. The State utility excise tax. A tax levied against gross revenues of all electric utilities in the State. 3. 4. 

Unemployment compensation tax.

Other miscellaneous State taxes and fees such as the retail sales tax, the compensating tax, the gasoline tax, etc. 

5. Local business and occupation taxes. The PUD law permits taxation of PUDS by incorporated cities within such Districts. The PUD either absorbs this tax as an additional business expense, or it passes the tax on to the customers residing in the city levying the tax as a separate tax item added to their power bills. (RCW 54.28.070.) 

6. Payments to school districts. Contributions to schools with bonds outstanding at the time properties are taken over by the PUD are mandatory. Additional voluntary payments may also be made to schools. 

These payments are in addition to funds received from distribution of the privilege tax. (RCW 54.28.080.)

REGULATION 

Regulation of PUD affairs is local and direct. 

It is provided through the democratic processes of free speech and free elections. 

42

The operation of a PUD is under the direction of a three-man commission elected from and by the citizens within the District. (RCW 54.12.010.) All official actions of a PUD commission are by resolutions which are matters of public record. Their meetings and the records of the District are open to the public. (RCW 54.12.090.) 

The term of one Commissioner expires each two years. Rates, service and management thus become subject to public scrutiny and approval at each general election. (RCW 54.12.030.)

In addition, the annual audits of the Division of Municipal Corporations, Office of the State Auditor, provides State supervision of expenditures and accounting practices and insures compliance with the requirements of State law.

While regulation of rates by an outside agency is necessary to protect the customers of a privately-owned utility, it is not needed in the case of PUDs (or city light and cooperative systems). Utilities, public or private, are natural monopolies; they usually have no direct competition in the areas they serve. Since the restraining influence of competition is absent, government regulation is needed to prevent excessive charges by the private utilities. However, the pressure to keep rates high in order to enhance stockholder earnings, such as is found within the private utilities, does not exist where the utility is owned and operated by the public served. Any "overcharge" which might occur in PUD (or city light and cooperative) rates automatically reverts to the credit of the local customer-owner. 010.)

RATES 

The PUD law requires that rates must be fair and non-discrimina- (RCW 54.24.080.)

Since the purpose of a PUD is the best possible utility service at the lowest possible rates, substantial rate reductions have been made by the PUDS since commencing operations.

The State of Washington has the lowest electric rates in the nation. This was not always the case. In 1935 the lowest State average residential rate was to be found in the State of Oregon. Oregon, like the State of Washington, benefits from the low-cost Pacific Northwest Federal power projects. Oregon, however, is served in the main by privately-owned utilities, while Washington has had the benefit of substantial rate reductions by PUDS, city light systems, and cooperatives, even in recent years. Washington has thus replaced the 

State of Oregon as the lowest in the nation, while Oregon has slipped to third place, being preceded now by the State of Tennessee. on Federal Power Commission statistics.) (Based Because the authority to establish rates rests with the local PUD commission, many adjustments in rates are being made, including special rate reductions to tax-supported bodies, special irrigation pumping rates, and more equitable rates to commercial establishments. (RCW 54.16.040.)

EQUITY 

The businesslike PUD practice of retiring bonded indebtedness, as opposed to perpetual private utility indebtedness, continues to improve the financial stability of the PUDS and contributes towards low rates to consumers by reducing interest expense.

The financial strength of the PUDS has been demonstrated by their ability to make most of their system extensions and betterments out of current earnings. The result has been a growing, debt-free equity in the local systems. It is the basic foundation for providing and continuing low rates in the future.

MANAGEMENT 

The PUD law specifically requires appointment of a qualified manager by the Board of Commissioners. (RCW 54.16. 100.)
