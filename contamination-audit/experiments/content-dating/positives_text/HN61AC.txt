DRILL TWELVE I. SOLVING TWO-STEP PROBLEMS 

A two-step problem contains a main problem and a hidden problem. 

This hidden problem must be found and solved before the main problem can be solved.

What you are asked to find in the main problem is always stated for you, but one, or more, of the facts necessary for its answer is missing. 

Finding this missing fact is what makes the hidden problem. 

The following two-step problem is solved for you. Study it and solve all other two-step problems by the same plan. 

1. A grocer bought 24 sacks of sugar for $12.00. If each sack weighed 10 lb., what was the price per pound? 

MAIN PROBLEM-What are you asked to find? The cost of the sugar per pound. 

What facts are given? The grocer bought 24 sacks of sugar for $12.00.

Each sack weighed 10 pounds. 

What facts are missing? The number of pounds of sugar in the 24 sacks.

This becomes the question of the secondary problem which must be solved before the main problem can be solved. 

HIDDEN PROBLEM-What is asked? How many pounds of sugar did the 24 sacks weigh. 

What facts are given? The grocer bought 24 sacks of sugar. Each sack weighed 10 pounds. 

What process should be used? Multiplication. When you are given a number of sacks of sugar and the weight of each sack, you multiply to find the number of pounds.

SOLUTION OF MAIN PROBLEM-What is asked? The price of the sugar per pound. 

What facts are now given? The grocer bought 240 pounds of sugar for $12. 

What process should be used? Division. When you are given the cost of a number of pounds of anything, you divide the cost by the number of pounds to find the price of one pound. 

Check the answer. Is the answer reasonable? Yes, because the grocer bought 240 lb. of sugar. 

Is the answer correct? Yes, because 5 × 240 is 1200 and 240 10 is 24.
