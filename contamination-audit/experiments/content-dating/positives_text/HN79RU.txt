A Message From Our President 

As President of Congregation Rodeph Sholom, I am pleased to extend my personal congratulations and the felicitations of our trustees. and members to Rabbi Louis I. Newman upon his 25th Anniversary year as our Rabbi. I consider myself fortunate to be the President of our great Congregation during this celebration. Two great personalities in our Rabbinate proceeded Dr. Newman during the recent decades of its 113 years of history. One was the late Rabbi Aaron Wise, who for 

21 years from 1875 to 1896-ministered to Congregation Rodeph Sholom. The other was the late Rabbi Rudolph Grossman who was our spiritual leader from 1897 to 1927, bringing added distinction to our Temple during the thirty years of his Rabbinate. Rabbi Newman has ministered to us for 25 years and our prayers are that the Almighty will permit him to continue his ministry among us for many years into the future.

SAMUEL FALK 

It was my privilege to come to know Rabbi Newman almost from the first months after his return to New York after serving as Rabbi of Congregation Emanu-El in San Francisco, California. We have worked closely together in many aspects of the Temple life, in the founding and development of the Parent-Teachers Association; in the religious educational program of our Temple; and in the varied phases of our Congregational work. During my period as President, Rabbi Newman has maintained the tradition of fellowship and cooperation between the Ministers and lay leaders of the Temple. We have always found it possible to find common ground on behalf of the Synagogue's welfare. I should also like to express my appreciation for the friendship and comradeship that has always obtained between Rabbi Newman, the Cantors, Ministers and staff members of Rodeph Sholom. 

Rabbi Newman, it seems to me, has always had a sincere and profound appreciation for the services rendered to the cause of the Temple and of Judaism by the laity. He never overlooks an opportunity to convey his thanks to those who work side by side and hand in hand with him for the advancement of the Temple and the Jewish cause. He has always stood for a Reform Judaism that is not static: To him liberal Judaism is dynamic, vital, progressive and ever forward-looking. He has made many contributions to the aesthetic and ceremonial activities of the American Synagogue, and we take pride in the fact that other congregations have emulated the programs we have presented as pioneers in our Temple. 

It is entirely fitting that we should tonight celebrate in a traditional Siyyum ha-Sefer the completion of the manuscript of Rabbi Newman's Modern Guidebook on Jews and Judaism, "The Jewish People, Faith and Life". 

Many confirmants, newcomers into our faith, and adults have benefitted by the contents of this volume in the past quarter century. Now at last, Rabbi Newman is placing it in final form as a printed and illustrated book. 

We are happy that amidst the heavy and demanding daily schedule of Rabbi Newman's life as pastor, he has found the time to prepare and complete this volume. Though the printed book itself will not, we understand, appear until next Autumn, we deemed it appropriate that we should celebrate the finishing of the manuscript by an occasion just twenty-five years after Rabbi Newman delivered his first sermon in our Temple. It was in 

March, 1930 that he participated in the Dedication of our new Temple and Temple house on West 83rd Street. 

And now after a quarter of a century, we have the opportunity to signalize another memorable occasion of creative and constructive quality. May we today, and those who come after us, find in this memorable volume the enlightenment and the practical guidance which will enable us to live as well informed, believing and devout 

Jews in American and world life.

May the years of achievement and progress which have been ours with Rabbi Newman in the past twentyfive years be the prelude to new decades of service and accomplishment in keeping with the proud heritage of 

Congregation Rodeph Sholom.

SAMUEL FALK, President of Congregation Rodeph Sholom

Twenty-five Years 

In Rodeph Sholom

By RABBI LOUIS I. NEWMAN 

It was in March, 1930 that I had the privilege of sharing in the Services in Dedication of the new Temple and Temple House of Congregation Rodeph Sholom, and while my family and I did not come to New York City until August, the March occasion symbolically marks the commencement of my association with the Temple. A quarter century of labor in the vineyard of Rodeph Sholom life has brought many experiences, both within and outside the Temple. We began our work together in the days of the economic revival after the financial breakdown in the United States of 1929; we witnessed the rise of Hitler, the Nazi persecution, the advent of the Second World

War, the victory and the peace, and now, in recent years, the "Cold War" between the West and the East, with the Korean War as one of its climactic events. We have together been granted the Zekut of witnessing the establishment of the Third Hebrew Commonwealth in the Holy Land. 

In Congregation Rodeph Sholom we have seen the development and extension of our Temple and Temple House program; the clearing away of the Mortgage, the building of the handsome Sixth Floor Auditorium, and the increased influence and the heightened repute of our Congregation. 

To the laity of Congregation Rodeph Sholom, permit me at this Anniversary occasion to express again my warmest gratitude. Many have been called into the "Holy Congregation on High"; happily multitudes of those who were with us in 1930 are healthy and active today, their enthusiasm and devotion unabated. It is my conviction that Rodeph Sholem has one of the finest Staffs in the entire synagogue community of America; it is my belief, also, that we have a laity of talent and devotion, unparalleled in the land. It is no accident that we have contributed many of the finest religious, community and philanthropic workers to the Jewish community and the commonwealth. It is no accident that we number in our ranks some of the leading personalities in the political, cultural and economic life of our republic. To my Rabbinical colleagues who have ministered with me in Rodeph Sholom throughout the years, I voice my appreciation; to the Presidents and Officers, the members of the Board of Trustees, the heads and members of our Auxiliary Organizations, and to all who have made up the personnel of Rodeph Sholom, I rejoice to offer my hearty thanks. A Congregation is not judged by its Rabbis and Cantors, but by its lay leaders and Congregants. If Rodeph Sholom is what the community knows it to be, we can find the reason in the partnership between us all throughout the two and a half decades we have striven together for the faith of Israel and the advancement of the general good. 

Rodeph Sholom is a center from which radiate forces for the betterment of religious and community life. We have pioneered in many fields of endeavor; we have regarded Reform Judaism in terms of a tradition which "sees and welcomes all truth, whether shining from the annals of ancient revelation, or reaching us through the seers of our time." We have therefore encouraged new forms, new ceremonies and new expressions of beauty, melody and drama in our synagogue program. While we regret deeply the migration of multitudes of our young people to the suburban areas, we are always delighted to learn that Rodeph Sholomites are in the forefront of service and leadership in the new congregations. Many of our Confirmants now heads of families, are 

Trustees, Sisterhood, Brotherhood, P. T. A. Presidents, and the rank and file have affiliated with local synagogues, giving to Reform Judaism greater depth and scope.

It is well, therefore, for us to pause and "consider the days" which have elapsed since 1930. We have been together through manifold experiences of joy, sorrow, aspiration and, I trust, accomplishment. And now, in 1955, you do your Rabbi the courtesy of sharing with him in happiness at the completion of the manuscript of "The Jewish People, Faith and Life". I have instructed many hundreds of Confirmants both verbally and through the experimental mimeographed versions in the contents of this Modern Guidebook on Jews and Judaism. Hundreds, too, of non-Jews have been taught what it means to be a member of the Jewish people by voluntary affiliation; an adherent of Jewish beliefs, and a participant in the Jewish life. While it will not be possible to place the printed and illustrated book in the hands of the reading public until later in the year, nevertheless, it has seemed to us that we can celebrate this stage in the preparation of the three-fold volume. 

When it appears in the monumental form it has assumed, the reason for the delay in its appearance will be understood. I have worked on it for nearly thirty-five years, and the creation of the State of Israel has had a profound influence upon its material. An entire revision has been necessary as a consequence of the remarkable changes wrought in Jewish religions and community life since 1948. I bespeak your patience, my friends, until at last the printed book is off the press and made available. It is my earnest hope that you will feel rewarded for your understanding.

To President Abram L. Sachar of Brandeis University who has come to us for the "Siyyum-ha-Sefer" (Conclusion of the Book) on March 11th, I extend my heartfelt thanks. To Rev. Dr. John Haynes Holmes, whose friendship, with that of the late Rabbi Stephen S. Wise, has been one of the great privileges of my life, I express my gratitude for his written message. May he have health and strength to "one hundred and twenty years!" To the Committee and all who have had a role in this Anniversary celebration, goes the warm appreciation of Mrs. Newman, my family and myself. Within the past quarter century, we have stood shoulder to shoulder in our aspirations and efforts! May the harvest of our labors be a continuously fruitful and nourishing synagague life! And may Rodeph Sholom be an "Eternal Temple" ever holding aloft the "Ner Tamid", the "Eternal Light" of Israel's message. 

DR. ABRAM L. SACHAR President of Brandeis University

SAMUEL S. SAMPLINER Chairman Ways and Means Committee 

The Ways and Means Committee of Rodeph Sholom salutes you, Rabbi Louis I. Newman, on this twenty-fifth year of your rabbinate for devoted service far beyond the call of duty, for your indefatigable spirit and for your inspiring leadership.

When our committee was created in 1937 with Joseph Pulvermacher its first Chairman, you had been at the helm of our Congregation for only seven years; however your book, "The Jewish People- 

Faith and Life", the manuscript of which is being dedicated on March 11th, was already on its way to completion. During Joseph Pulvermacher's regime which ended in September 1944, the amortization of the mortgage was begun. When he stepped down and handed over the leadership to Albert M. Kahn, the sum of $100,000 had already been raised. To Joe Pulvermacher, Rodeph 

Sholom owes a debt of gratitude as it was due to his wisdom and foresight that the $450,000 mortgage was ultimately liquidated. 

SAMUEL S. SAMPLINER 

During Albert M. Kahn's chairmanship, the Ways and Means 

Committee launched the Fund Drive for the new sixth floor, and under Benjamin Siegel's leadership, the Tablet Fund was begun and flourished. 

Also during Siegel's regime the creation of the Foundation Fund was proposed by Walter W. Weismann and actual work on it was started. 

The Ben Siegel era was dynamic, as both Siegel and Kahn worked together as members of each other's committees. They were assisted by a large group amongst whom were such stalwarts as Walter W. Weismann, Joseph Eisner, Arnold Becker and Samuel Staff. The net result of the activity was the realization of a dream long cherished-the beautiful Sixth Floor. As a result, our Sunday School facilities have been enhanced and today we have one of the finest institutions of its kind in the Country. 

With the mortgage out of the way and our building program completed, the Ways and Means Committee of today has dedicated itself to bringing our members closer to our Temple. Under the present 

Chairman, a series of Sabbath Seudahs has been inaugurated and these have taken hold in a firm manner. 

Over 100 Congregants have participated on several occasions at these luncheons where visiting Rabbis or Educators have honored us by remaining after their lectures to form a symposium with Rabbis Newman and Ilson.

At the present time a sub-committee under Samuel Kolin is discussing an enlarged program of activities for our youth, and their further integration into our Congregational life. 

While fund raising has played an important part in the work of the Ways and Means Committee, other facets mentioned above and others to be explored in the future will play an equally important part in the development of New York's most outstanding Reformed Congregation. We hail our Spiritual Leader, Louis I. Newman, and wish him many more years of inspirational service.

A Message From Our Sisterhood President 

On this happy occasion, the 25th anniversary of Rabbi Louis I. 

Newman's association as the spiritual leader of Temple Rodeph Sholom, the Sisterhood extends its heartiest congratulations. His whole-hearted Likewise cooperation with the Sisterhood has endeared him to all of us. the organization has been enriched with the membership of his wife, Lucille Newman, whose wise counsel and suggestions have always been. welcomed at Board and Committee Meetings. 

During Rabbi Newman's twenty-five years with us, the Sisterhood has celebrated its 40th, 50th and 60th anniversaries, having been founded in 1892 by the late Rabbi Aaron Wise. The programs of many of our 

Sisterhood meetings have been arranged with the help of our Rabbi. 

He has brought a great number of leading personalities of the day to us. 

The Round-Tables which he has helped conduct have been both stimulating and enlightening, and we all remember the delightful one act plays: "The Quality of Mercy" and "Daughter of the Queen" which were first presented at Sisterhood gatherings.

MRS. IRVING J. NETHE 

The continuity of our Sisterhood has been maintained and its program has been broadened. Our Donor 

Luncheons have been wonderful occasions, and our Judaica Shop was one of the first in American synagogues. 

In fact, it has proved a model for others throughout the country. Our Sewing groups have done magnificent work; our Golden Age Club is recognized as one of the strongest and most successful in the city, and, like the 

Judaica Shop, is setting the standard for others. We have been happy to welcome new workers and leaders who have received training in synagogue work during their years in the Parent-Teachers Association when their children were in the Religious School. We are proud, too, of the role which our Sisterhood plays in the State and National Federation of Temple Sisterhoods, as well as in other community organizations. We have tried to awaken the generosity of our members towards all worthwhile charities, and we know that the name of Rodeph Sholom has prestige in the philanthropies and welfare organizations of our city. 

Thus the legacy we have received from our predecessors in the Rodeph Sholom of 63rd Street has been carried on by all of us in our Temple and Temple House on 83rd Street. During these twenty-five years of service and progress, Rabbi Newman has been our friend and advisor. The officers and members join in extending to him and to Mrs. Newman and all the members of their family our hearty congratulations and best wishes for many more years of happy and fruitful service to our organization. 

MRS. IRVING J. NETHE, President,

Rodeph Sholom.

Rabbi Newman's interest in our Association has always been a very vital and interested one. He guided our early years as a fond parent guides a child. During those twenty-five years, he has seen us grow and mature and accept the responsibilities that a mature person, and mature groups must learn to accept. The program that the P.T.A. has set itself to follow has been a rich and satisfying one, always guided by the rabbi's wise counsel. it

The primary reason for the existence of the P.T.A. is now, as has always been "to bring parent and child, and home and school closer together". Besides purchasing books and film strips for the Sunday 

School, the P.T.A. has established a yearly series of Breakfast Workshops. 

MRS. EMIL ADELAAR

MRS. EMIL ADELAAR, President, Parent Teachers' Association of Rodeph Sholom.

Highlights of a Quarter Century 

Congregation Rodeph Sholom was founded in 1842 and its Temple and Temple House at 7 West 83rd Street is its third major home. The first was on Clinton Street, the second at 63rd Street and Lexington 

Avenue. It was the late Rabbi Rudolph Grossman who envisaged an adequate and beautiful new Temple edifice on the West Side, but he did not live to see its completion. On March 16, 17 and 18, 1930 the Dedication Services for our new building were held, and we were launched upon a new epoch in our synagogue history.

These have been vivid and crowded years of activity. For nineteen years Sunday Morning Community 

Services were held, and the guest speakers including many of the most notable personalities of our time. In

the year 1949, it was decided to concentrate upon the Friday Evening and Saturday Morning Services in order to emphasize personal and family Sabbath piety among our Congregants. Our Sabbath Services have also been graced by the presence and message of distinguished guest preachers. We have instituted the periodic Oneg 

Shabbat (Delight of the Sabbath) Reception and Social Hour, with opportunity for Forum discussion. Another recent innovation has been the Sabbath Morning Seudah Luncheon following the Temple Services, at which a guest preacher discusses a vital religious theme. Thus we have been able to develop even greater solidarity and fellowship among our Congregants. 

We have had a number of Anniversary Celebrations during these twenty-five years. We celebrated our 

90th Anniversary in 1932; our 100th Anniversary in 1942, and our 110th Anniversary in 1952. In 1945 we observed the 15th and in 1950 the 20th Anniversary of Rabbi Newman; in 1941 we commemorated the 25th and in 1951 the 35th Anniversary of Cantor Nathan G. Meltzoff.

Its devoted and indefatigable members, with the contributions of the Temple and the results of our Theatre Parties, were able to gather sufficient funds over a period of years to clear up our mortgage indebtedness; they then proceeded to assemble enough to build the handsome Sixth Floor Auditorium which has proved so beneficial to our Religious School, our Youth Groups and all the units of our synagogue life. 

During the years of the Nazi oppression and the War, we helped conduct a School for the teaching of English and American life, and hundreds of newcomers from lands of persecution were thus introduced into the language and ways of American life. Recently a Golden Age Club was established which has proved to be one of the most pleasing interests for those who are growing old.

In 1930 the Rodeph Sholom League for young adults was created, and it has had a continuous and unbroken career since then. Its meetings, forums, dramatic productions and welfare activities have proved a source of stimulus to thousands of young people, many of them now leaders in synagogues of New York, its suburbs and other sections of the United States. In 1930-31 the Parent-Teachers Association was established as a means of enlisting the cooperation of the fathers and mothers of pupils, and it has proved a tower of strength to our School and youth groups. A Junior Society was founded which later took the name of the 

Rudolph Grossman Society, and, in cooperation with the Aaron Wise Post-Graduate Society, has conducted a fine program for our high school and early college young people. A Young Married Guild has been active for nearly two decades, despite baby-sitting and other problems. The Men's Association has had a notable quarter century of service and work, and, with the Jewish Chautauqua as one of its activities, has done much for the social, cultural and religious life of the Temple. The Sisterhood, with its Sewing Groups, it Donor 

Luncheons, its periodic meetings, and its many welfare and philanthropic causes, remains, as always, a pillar of strength in our Temple. The Judaica Shop has done much for our Sisterhood and Temple.

Our Adult Education Courses have attracted many hundreds of persons; there have been classes in Jewish 

Customs and Ceremonies; the Bible; Jewish Problems; Psychiatry and Modern Life; Jewish History, Jewish 

Music and allied themes. 

The Religious School has flourished, and it is our earnest hope that we will not be too greatly affected by population changes in metropolitan life. No effort has been spared to take advantage of the new pedagogical facilities which Jewish education today affords. We have had large Confirmation Classes, and the 

Cantatas at Shavuot Services have been original and creative in character. 

In the field of Pageants, Cantatas and plays we have done much to give new artistic forms to Jewish ceremonial observance. At Hanukah we have had "Ein Bereirah: No Alternative"; The Candlestick of Spears"; and "The Light of Liberty"; at Purim we have given "The Miracle of the Scrolls" and "Star Over Shushan"; at

We have extended hospitality at our Temple House to community organizations, including the 65th Street, Alumni (formerly the U.S.O. group of World War II days), and numerous other groups. Our Temple and 

Temple House have been a mecca for thousands of persons throughout the years, and our Temple "Chronicle" has been closely studied by others for program suggestions. 

Congregation Rodeph Sholom has taken an increasingly active part in the work of the Union of American Hebrew Congregations, and its affiliated groups, including the Gerater New York Federation of Reform Synagogues, the Metropolitan and National Federation of Temple Brotherhoods; the State and National Federation of Temple Sisterhoods. Many of the leaders and members of our synagogue occupy places of honor and responsibility in the philanthropic, communal and civic life of our city, state and nation.

Our Temple and Temple House are today as beautiful and useful as on their Dedication day. We have seen the changing panorama of world events since 1930, and they have been mirrored in our Temple life: the 

Service of Intercession for German Jewry in November, 1938; the D-Day Service on June 6, 1944; the V-E and V-J Day Services of 1945; the Celebration in honor of the Partition Decision of the United Nations on 

November 29, 1947; the Services honoring the establishment of the State of Israel on May 14, 1948, and its admission as a member of the United Nations. We have shared actively in the affairs of Jewry and the commonwealth, and we have maintained the respected tradition of our Temple.
