ANONYMOUS GIFT

ADAM AND TWO E VES 

THIS startling true story deals frankly, but with remarkable delicacy and restraint, with the problem of unnatural love. It is all the more poignant because the conflict is between normal and abnormal love in the same person, a young widow, who is taken into the household of a married couple where both the husband and the wife fall in love with her. She becomes the lover of both but it is a love without joy, a hunted, tortured experience. 

How she escapes this strange situation and is restored to normal happy love is revealed with a dramatic power, and with an inspired understanding that lifts this book among the finest in recent years.

FOREWORD 

This strange story has happened to me and has given me such an unusual slant on life that I feel I would like to tell about it. It has caused me many hours of pain and suffering but it has also brought me a certain knowledge of human nature that is valuable and interesting. The word "love" always has been and always will be used frequently and casually. I thought I knew what it meant, but it was only after having gone through this strange phase of my life that I realized how many different kinds of love there are. 

I have tried to be absolutely honest and to tell only the truth; therefore I prefer to remain anonymous. I ask you to try to understand my story, not because I want sympathy but because it broadens one's mind and heart to realize the strange relations that can exist between human beings. Some of you may criticize me and instantly dismiss the possibility of such an "unnatural" situation. But though

I know it was an unusual relationship, I have tried in this book to convey the spirit of it, which was that of real love. It is possible to love two people at the same time, in different ways, and it is possible to

I wanted to throw my arms around him and kiss him-show him my gratitude. Then he stepped in front of me and said, very quickly, excitedly: "Carroll, I am very guilty. I realized it only a moment ago. I condemned you when I heard about your strange relationship. I was thunderstruck. You had disappointed me. That I had made a mistake about you, that you were not what I had considered you to be. You saw me leave here in a rage . . . or rather in a silent demonstration of disgust, perhaps even loathing. probably jealousy. Then you called me up and I was hostile to you. It was very touching that after I had hurt him so much, as I had, he should ask me to forgive him!

I realized that in his soul he was searching for something to excuse me . . . he wanted to forgive me.

The thing I had done was ugly, low, horrid, in his eyes-the only way he could get over it was by making himself believe that he was guilty. It was utterly untrue-but I understood his desire to forgive me. "Darling Alfred-you asking me to forgive you? 

How can you be so generous? No, you have nothing to do with it, my dear, dear boy. I was very sad, that is true, and probably weak. I was in a daze... I didn't realize then that it was an impossible situation. I was sincerely fond of them. I still am they are terribly nice people . . . not at all wicked, spoiled Continentals . simple, earnest bourgeois people. It is a funny thing, this threesome of ours.

Saturday morning! This was Wednesday night.

I had to dress. I vaguely remembered the plan we had made to see the new "Scandals." It all seemed so remote ... so far away.
