TO THE GOVERNOR OF THE STATE OF OREGON: 

The Judicial Council of Oregon acting pursuant to ORS 1.840, herewith submits its Third Annual Report.

The report is presented at a different time and in a different manner than in past years because the Council during 1958 very largely devoted Its time to cooperating with the Interim Committee on Judicial Administration and only working on projects of its own to a small degree. 

Pages 1 through 8 report the final determinations of the Council on recommendations of the Interim Committee. Page 9 contains a summary report of some extra projects worked on by the Trial Courts Committee, and beginning on page 10 is a report of the only Judicial Council Committee on Courtroom Design and Courthouse Facilities, whose work continued uninterrupted through the year.

Plans for a vigorous approach to the problems of judicial administration In 1959 have been made by the Council and committees to study them are already at work.

SPECIAL REPORT OF TRIAL COURT COMMITTEE 

Judge Hammond, Chairman, had been specifically asked to study ORS 14.270 relating to disqualification of judges and attempt to avoid the abuse of attorneys filing disqualifications after unfavorable rulings. Judge

Reid, on behalf of the committee, outlined the historic development of the statute and presented a proposed amendment which would in essence require disqualification to be filed prior to rulings on questions of law. 

Questions from the floor brought out that the proposed amendment was probably deficient in not providing against jointly indicted defendants shopping for a trial judge, and providing against attorneys absenting themselves from the assignment call and then disqualifying at a later date. 

Question was also raised regarding whether clients should be required to be present at time of assignment, since attorneys habitually refer to wishes of the client. Attention was called to Washington's disqualification procedure, and that the procedure should also apply to rulings on questions of fact. It was then moved that a committee be appointed to work with the draftsman to take into account the suggestions made at the meeting, and the fact that Multnomah County because of its size offers special problems. 

Judge Reid then moved to the second matter which his committee had under consideration, namely, the proposed repeal of the limitations on confidential use of presentence reports contained in ORS 137.110, and the substitution of section 5 of the Standard Probation and Parole Act to protect parole officers from libel, and to specify that the court may in its discretion permit inspection of the report, or parts of it, by the defendant. Judge 

Reid asserted that much of the valuable information on social history in a presentence report is unverified and, if the persons giving the information were aware that it would be turned over to the defendant, they would be afraid to speak. After extensive discussion regarding the merits of passing sentence on the basis of material not known to the defendant, the Chairman observed that the practice has been upheld by the Supreme Court of the United States. Judge Bohannon moved that the bill be referred back to committee for further study. The motion carried by a standing vote. -9-

REPORT OF COMMITTEE ON COURTROOM DESIGN AND COURTHOUSE FACILITIES 

Judge Fort made the oral report of the committee noting that it was the only Judicial Council Committee whose task was not primarily devoted to study and cooperation with the Legislative Interim Committee on Judicial 

Administration. 

Judge Fort mentioned that the Committee had been active regarding new courthouses or courtrooms in Linn, Lane, Multnomah and Malheur Counties during the year.

He said that substantial changes were made in the Malheur Courthouse after consultation with the committee; and in most of the other counties, committee work was in the area of ideas for designs prior to the drafting. 

Judge Fort introduced Judge 01liver to tell of the new courtroom in Linn County which was patterned after the Lane County experimental, moveable courtroom. Judge Olliver described the new courtroom as having a solid ceiling of light and no windows, to avoid street noise. The judges bench, counsel tables, witness chair and jury box are arranged in a circle to focus attention and voices. He mentioned though, that because the courtroom was put in an existing building, it was somewhat cramped, but that basically the design is excellent, and seems to have won approval of all lawyers and judges who have tried cases in it. Judge Sulmonetti spoke on the Multnomah project of cutting their high-ceilinged courtrooms in half horizontally to double available courtrooms. A 3rd and a 5th floor courtroom are serving as guinea pigs. Judge Sulmonetti invited inspection of the partially-completed 3rd floor courtroom and its twin, Judge Langtry's new 4th floor courtroom. He deplored the paucity of information available on courtroom design and courthouse facilities, and then pointed out that the 3rd floor courtroom would only be 11 feet 8 inches high because of the thickness of the structural steel, whereas courtrooms on higher floors would have higher ceilings. He also expressed the great need for having adequate waiting-rooms comfortably furnished for jurors, and described recent strides in Multnomah County to provide such facilities.

Judge Langtry, of the Domestic Relations Department whose third floor courtroom was cut in half, remarked that he will take over the new courtroom on the 4th floor and that he cut down the length of the courtroom by building in facilities for his family counselors and secretarial staff. The working part of the courtroom is built in a circle, as in Linn County, with a circular aluminum railing separating the public portion of the courtroom from the rest. In case of an emergency a jury could sit inside the railing. 

He noted that his courtroom would have a circular recess in the ceiling over the well of the court similar to Judge Dickson's new Probate courtroom, which would be for acoustical purposes and lighting. The outside walls are of glass block rather than windows to cut down street noise and prevent distraction. Judge Dickson described his specially-designed probate courtroom on the 2nd floor of the Multnomah Courthouse. This new courtroom, which has been occupied for several months, was placed on the 2nd floor to give ready access to probate records, and all allied offices of the court are right across the hall from the courtroom. The new courtroom is substantially smaller than a jury courtroom and everything in the room is moveable. Acoustics are helped by a hard, reflective surface behind the judge, a lighted circular recess about eight feet in diameter in the ceiling in the well of the court, wall-to-wall carpeting, and attractive panelling. -10-

Counsel tables, witness chairs, etc., are arranged in traditional fashion. 

He objected to two major faults in the courtroom. First, a one story courtroom when occupied by a large number of people, requires excellent ventilation and air-conditioning. The second fault is that the back wall at the far end of the room from the bench was covered with a plywood and reflected too much sound. The plywood is presently being removed and replaced with acoustic plaster. The courtroom swinging door is made of clear glass because of heavy foot traffic at certain hours.
