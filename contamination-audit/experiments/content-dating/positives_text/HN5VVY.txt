FOREWORD 

The death of Aron Nimzowitsch on March 16, 1935 deprived the chess world of one of its most noted masters; and of its greatest innovating spirit since Wilhelm Steinitz. Paradoxically enough, Nimzowitsch did more than any other master of his generation to make chess more abstruse (in his games) and more popular (in his books)! His games, now that they are no longer distorted by the contempt and derision which were once so liberally heaped on them, are a precious heritage which it is our duty to collect and preserve.

It is for this reason that I have selected some of Nimzowitsch's finest games and brought them together in one book. 

Fragments of some of them appear in My System and in Die Praxis Meines Systems, but most of them have never been printed in book form, and all of them are inaccessible to the average reader. 

To lend additional interest to the volume I have incorporated the annotations of several eminent masters, whose personal opinions of Nimzowitsch's play often add a piquant touch to the games themselves. The book is also considerably enhanced by the material quoted from Die Blockade, a little-known work which Nimzowitsch used as the basis for My System. 

Game No. 29 was inadvertently included in the present collection because of a mistake made by Ludwig Bachmann in his 1924 Jahrbuch. I discovered too late that the entire game is given in 

Die Praxis Meines Systems. All translations have been made by me, except in the cases of games taken from the Yearbooks. 

The present collection will be supplemented by a volume in the 

MODERN CHESS LIBRARY containing Nimzowitsch's finest games dur ing the period 1928-1934, with annotations by Nimzowitsch, Tartakower, Ko stich, Kmoch, Spielmann and others.
