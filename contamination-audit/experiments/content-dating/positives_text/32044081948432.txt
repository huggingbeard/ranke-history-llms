FOREWORD 

This booklet, the Monetary System and Depression", is an attempt to propose a common program for all people, regardless of class or position in society. All those who are victims of depression and of economic insecurity can come together on the program here proposed, and work in common for the great cause of doing away with depression and obtaining economic security.

THE MONETARY S.STEM AND DEPRESSION IN THE PRESENT DEPRESSION 

In the present depression, we see and hear wherever we go the crying slogans "Buy now, Invest 

Now, and Bring Back Prosperity." This cry is coming from the smallest merchants all the way up to the President of the United 

States of America. Now, after years of hammering these slogans, depression is still weighing upon the people of America more heavily than ever before, and the unemployed problem still remains unsolved. Were the slogans to be obeyed completely both in letter and spirit, it would no doubt be a great benefit to the people. Unfortunately, it is certain that these slogans are reaching the empty pocketbooks of the unemployed and the unheeding ears of those who have money. This opinion is based upon undeniable facts.

TYPICAL SCARCITY The present Monetary System has succeeded in producing a type of character different from that of the average person, inasmuch as that individual would rather deny himself the satisfaction of a complete meal than separate himself from that which is his best and most certain friend: his money; not to speak of purchasing with it comforts beyond the absolute necessities of life. 

Perhaps every person came in contact with such a character, but if there are any people who are not personally acquainted with that type, then we will take for an example from the daily newspaper.

Mr. B. Curtis, from a town known as Branford, was brought before a judge and was found guilty of unbearable scarcity (stinginess). The plaintiff was his wife; she complained that her husband, who earns a good salary and also has money in the bank, is so stingy that he refuses to give

The society complained that this man is so stingy that he does not feed his dog, and he keeps his poor dumb animal hungry.

From the same facts, we can also learn that the strongest appeal and the most effective slogan for "buying now" would be to modify the Monetary System where hoarding or holding of money for a long or everlasting time would not pay.

HOLDERS OF MONEY A MENACE AND MISFOR- TUNE TO SOCIETY. 

Accumulated money in possession of each individual is a menace and misfortune to society as a whole. Sometime ago, the writer of this booklet read an article where it was demonstrated statistically that the average actual producer receives only seventeen cents out of every dollar's worth of wealth he produces, the other 

83% going to profit, rent, interest, taxes, and other nonproductive expenses. That means that the producer creates $100 worth of commodities and consumes only the $17.00, which he receives in wages. The other 83% has to be consumed by the other people, who are participating in receiving the 83% of the commodities, so that when the 100% commodities would be fully consumed by the people, a depression like the kind we now have, caused by under

consumption, would never be possible, for the reason that for every unspent dollar which is being piled away in a safe, or anywhere else for the purpose of holding, means at the same time that the same amount of commodities is being left over unconsumed on the shelves of the stores and in the pileds of warehouses.

So we are getting the final result of all this unspent accumulated money. The stores and warehouses are piled up with the unconsumed commodities. The shops, factories and plants of all kinds are closing, with many millions being thrown out of work and the depression grows from bad to worse.

THE INJUSTICE OF OUR PRESENT MONETARY SYSTEM. 

The fact that money functions as a medium of exchange which is being given as a reward for participating in service of creating or distributin necessities of 10

life, comfort and pleasure. Again money is being given for the purpose of consuming the created things which have been mentioned. So we find that almost everything is being created for the immediate disposal, and not for many years later. As we all know the fact that when products are not being disposed of within a short period after they have been produced, they begin to depreciate in value. For an illustration, we will take, for instance, the commodity of eggs.

We all know how eggs depreciate in value with increasing age.

We also know the difference in prices between fresh eggs and stored, how the value of eggs has been depreciated since the time when the eggs were fresh. Again, the people who participate in the production of the eggs usually receive their reward in comparison to the value of fresh eggs, and not according to the depreciated value of old stored. The desire for hoarding and preserving money is essentially a manifestation of that everlasting and terrible fear of the misery and helplessness that comes from economic insecurity. Further, it is this Monetary System which is · principally responsible for the present crisis with its concomitant evils. Again, it is the same

Monetary System which drives every moneyless person to hunger, misery, beggary, desperation, crime or sucide. - 14

UNDERSTAND- 

If it is just and proper to expect every person to make a sacrifice for the good and welfare of the country, is it not equally just and proper to expect that the country take care of each individual who makes that sacrifice and becomes destitute through it? 

Obviously, some sort of understanding towards mutual aid should be formed between the 

Government and each individual.

Such an end would not be at all difficult to accomplish, were our present Monetary System modified. The modification must be made so that hoarders of money may spend their savings without running the awful risks of future poverty.

A MODIFIED MONETARY SYSTEM. 

How may the present Monetary System be best modified for the good and welfare of the people? The suggestion we make is this: 15

First, that all money issued by the Treasury retain its face value as an exchange medium for only a specified period from date of issue. As an illustration, money issued in 1932 shall retain its face value for the first two months, then it shall gradually begin to decrease at the rate of 1% every two months. A more practical demonstration is seen hereby, a drawing representing a dollar value, backed by general commodities, which is similarly subject to decrease in value. These illustrations explain how it is possible to decrease the value of circulation money with the increase of time since the date of issue.

The apparent loss in value to the owner of money will not be a real loss, but a gain on the part of the people as a whole and a benefit to the community. The loss in value of the money to its individual owner, due to its increasing age, will revert to society as a gain. Each successive decrease in the value of unspent money is

THE PROBLEM OF UNEMPLOYMENT 

The problem of unemployment would solve itself under this modified monetary system. The first reason for this is that the people would be compelled to spend their money and thus facilitate the quick consumption of commodities. Secondly, the people will cease being afraid of insecurity, which fear has prevented their spending of money, knowing that they are fully protected against poverty, it will no longer be necessary for them to preserve and hoard their money 

19

against insecurity, old age and sickness. The State will then be in a position to take care of all this through the income derived from the decreasing values of money. Thirdly, employers will also cease to accumulate money by the payment of starvation wages for long hours of hard and dangerous labor. As for instance, we might take the miners in the mining districts.

The accumulation of money will be made impossible by the modified monetary system explained before, due to the decreasing value of money with increasing age.

THE FARMER PROBLEM 

The farmers who are the producers of all our agriculture commodities are certainly entitled to a decent living in return for their investments and long hours of hard labor. But so long as the evil of competition continues, the farmers will be unable to derive a living for their families from their farms. We know that the 

20

markets for agriculture commodities are dependent upon the consuming powers of the dwellers in the industrial centers. If all the working people were fully employed at fair wages, they would become great consumers of farm products and their families would not go hungry as a result of being unemployed. Neither would they be limited to consume only the cheapest and inferior grades of the products. On the other hand, the improved labor conditions caused by the modified monetary system which would also help to decrease competition in the following manner. It is well known that many industrial workers become farmers through unemployment or starvation wages, in the hope that they might draw the means for existence from the farm. But many of this type of farmers would surely return to their industries if working conditions would improve to the extent that a living would be more secure. 21

MARKETS CONFLICT IN FOREIGN LANDS 

On account of the modified monetary system which will make good domestic purchasing power, there will be no need for creating new foreign markets and as this desire for new markets is the principal cause of war marketing, conflict and arming will be automatically done away with, together with the oppression of people in foreign lands. 

It is understood that such modified monetary system can only become possible when gold will no longer function as a medium of exchange or circulating money.

While gold may still remain in the Treasury as backing for the paper money in circulation, not one individual, or group of individuals shall be able to redeem their paper money for gold; as is now possible. It is easily understood, that should the Government continue to pay out gold on 

22

demand to everyone presenting paper money, as it must have been. It would be quite natural for many persons to take advantage of this opportunity and exchange their circulating money which is subject to depreciation, and take gold which may not depreciate in value.

Gold as an exchange medium might still be a necessity in internationaly trade, where the direct exchange of commodities cannot take place, and gold must be used as an exchange medium. 

International trade of this sort can best be managed by the

Government, who is sole possessor of gold, where the direct exchange of commodities cannot take place. In such an instance, the export of gold would become an absolute necessity and therefore justified.

INDIVIDUALITY 

The modified monetary system · hereby described should appeal to 

23

all 

The conscientious people, especially to those idealists who stand for individuality. reason for this is that the new monetary system is not intended to kill the spirit of individual intentness for private enterprising, wherever there is such a possibility. It would not force anyone to become a small part of a large public institution, or, as an individualist would express it a screw in a big machine. It would yet be a new monetary system where Individuality and Independence would really become possible, together with economic security. 

A PRACTICAL METHOD 

What is there to do now? Be fore the monetary system herein described can be brought into effect, what would be the most practical method to prevent starvation at present? The answer is that the more progressive farms and the industrial workers may get together and discuss their 24

problems. Using this method, they would surely come to an understanding by which they would jointly form an industrial and agricultural organization for the exchange of commodities. As a form of immediate relief, the progressive elements of the workers would then have to install workshops, so that the unemployed members may get an opportunity to secure some little earnings by working in these shops at least a few days during the week, to keep them from actually starving until the unemployed are re-employed.

Industrial commodities pro- duced by the unemployed should exchange with the farmers for agricultural commodities and in this way the farmers and workers would both benefit by it. It should no doubt be in the best interest of the farmers to develop an organization of this kind, which would open a new market with the opportunity of disposing 

25

part of their products in exchange for valuable industrial commodities, and also at the same time save lives from starvation. The workers for their work and the farmers for their product might be paid by their mutual organization with credit checks, which should be good for buying agricultural and industrial commodities produced in their own jointly agricultural and industrial organization. Such a method of mutual aid between farmers and industrial workers would be an ideal demonstration of solidarity which may develop into great effective service in their common struggle for economic freedom.

AN APPEAL 

As one of the people, I appeal to all the people and to all their representatives, asking only that they give careful thought to it. 

It is the great ideal desire and hope of all right thinking people in all nations that the suffering people, who are the great major- 

26

ity, will once and for all come to their senses and recognize that it is a disgrace and a crime to tolerate so insane and criminal a social system, as is our present one. Where millions of people and innocent children must starve because there is too much food, millions of people are homeless because there are too many houses and apartments, millions of people wear ragged clothes and worn-out shoes because there is an abundance of clothing and shoes in the country, there must be a vital defect in that system. Again, I ask you people and your representatives to think of all this and think intelligently; think from the viewpoint of justice and conscience as well as from a logical point of view. And so thinking in this direction, all of you must admit that the system as it now stands is bankrupt and that a better social order based upon justice and economic security must take its place.

REPLIES 

Dear Professor :- 

Regarding your statement that "money is only a part of the mechanism by which goods are exchanged"; The statement does not seem to be very clear, because in our life experience, we see no other medium of exchange except money or a promise to pay off in money, like mortgages, bonds, notes or checks. All this is nothing more than a written promise to pay off in money. So it is with everything else, from a box of matches to a house, from a wheel-barrow to a motor truck, 31

from a doll carriage to a pleasure car, from a hand shovel to a steam shovel, from a needle to a machine, from a handkerchief to a fur coat, from a loaf of bread to a turkey, from plain water to a bottle of champagne, from a kitchen chair to an electrical refrigerator, from a cheap movie to grand opera, from a ride in a street car to a trip around the world. From small to large, from . the lowest to the highest, for anything and for everything, we see no other medium of exchange except money.

The professor has touched upon a more important problem when he mentioned "production, capital and labor." The question of capital and labor is old and popular. The struggle between employers and employees has gone on for ages, yet we still do not see any possible change in the near future. Why is it so? 

The answer is simple. First "it takes two to make a bargain." 32

Second, it is the indifference and ignorance of the masses. Third, it is our competitive system. When we look into all this, we find how largely our present monetary system is involved when organized labor is raising its voice against wage cuts or for better working conditions. On that case, the main argument of the employer is that he cannot afford to maintain wages or improve conditions on the grounds of competition, and we see how fearful is the competition, especially among unorganized working people and also the competition among the capitalists.

WHAT IS COMPETITION? 

Competition is nothing more than a large supply and a small demand. An abundant supply of commodities and a very small demand for them; a large supply of labor power and a very small demand for it. Under our present monetary system, we have no 33

means of preventing people from producing tremendous amount of commodities with the aid of competitive starvation wages and competitive child labor, and to dump said commodities on the competitive market. The dumpers and producers themselves consume very little of it. The dumpers cannot consume enough as they are small in numbers; the workers cannot consume enough because they are being paid starvation wages or are unemployed; the farmers cannot consume because of the low prices they receive for their commodities. For example, take the dairy farmers; they receive 2c to 3c per quart for milk which costs them 5c per quart to produce. A similar situation exists with many other agricultural commodities. This question will be discussed in greater detail in the letters of the social worker. 

Dear Social Worker :- 

Your opinion about lending money gives the impression that 34

all that is necessary to have prosperity is to get enough money to produce more wealth. (Never mind about the many millions of consumers.) They are not involved in this situation. Commodities are only being produced for angels somewhere in Heaven and not for the great masses here on earth. 

Money used for producing more wealth may help some for a short time until again the competitive markets become overcrowded. But when more money is used for consuming, then it helps to eliminate competition, and it brings more and steadier employment. In this way, the entire business throughout country would benefit. the

The depression is by no means the result of a shortage of credit for the running of industries. Bankers and industrialists admit that there is an abundance of money in the banks, which is not being used. In the vaults of the New 35

York City banks, alone are laying billions of dollars in idleness. The

Federal Reserve System has also made bank credit possible to the amount of $7,250,000,000, but the mere willingness to lend money for running the industries cannot make the industries run, so long as the great majority of people are not given the possibility of consuming. 

A big money lender is frequently a small consumer and what the industries and agriculture really need is a large number of good, paying consumers.

For instance, in the building industries, we know of many builders who have lost their money and have stopped building because of their inability to find buyers for their houses. Landlords have lost ther apartment houses because of a large number of vacant apartments. That doesn't mean that they have lost their houses because of a shortage in credit but that they have lost their pro- 36

perty because of a shortage in good, payable consumers, for we know that tenants also means consumers. They consume in the form of using apartment houses. 

The same applies to factories and all other businesses which are not operating. Businesses and factories are closed because they cannot get enough buyers for their commodities. We cannot get away from the fact that the means of buying all commodities, comforts and pleasures is money. So, if we really want to do away with the depression and all its concomitant evils, we have no other way out but to make the buying power accessible to all people and not only to a few individuals, as is now the case. Obviously, a few thousand people cannot consume as much as many millions would consume were they to get the opportunity. 

This is where the mistake lies.

We think too much of money in terms of an instrument through 37

which to produce more wealth. 

We always forget that money is necessary in order to be in the position of a consumer. We should rather think of money more often in terms of a consuming power, through which the created wealth can and should be consumed. Money is similar to a stream of water in a community which has to make use of the stream. In order to keep the water fresh, clean and wholesome, it is necessary to allow the water to run as freely as possible.

When even a part of the stream is dammed anywhere, the entire stream is polluted. Money works under the same influence.

Money is the consuming power of society. Its nature is to move from one place to another, for its main purpose is that each and everyone in society should get the possibility of making use of it. 

Money is an instrument for obtaining created wealth in society. 

The instrument is similar to the 38

water in the stream. It must always be kept in movement from one person to another to serve its purpose for the common benefit of society without being stopped by private individuals, just as the stream of the community.

The opinion of the social worker that money as a medium of exchange "is highly desirable and that its value should remain as nearly constant as possible." 

Yes, to some extent we can all agree with the social worker that money in its present "constant form" is highly desirable. To a large number, very highly desirable. That is why we have so many holdups, for the sake of getting that money. Money is very desirable to swindlers, rackateers, grafters and kidnappers. 

Money is sometimes so highly desired that people will go to any length for it; as in the notorious "Snyder Case" and many other similar cases. Money is also 39

highly desirable to many public officials. To some of these officials, money is so greatly to be desired that they cannot withstand the temptation and they sell the confidence and trust that the people have vested in them for this desired money. 

To whom should not money be so highly esteemed as the great majority who are getting very little of it, or none at all. To those people money in the vaults of the banks is the same as money on the moon. We cannot reach the moon, neither can we reach the money in the vaults of the banks.

Dear Educater : 

It may be true that in a depreciated monetary system, people may like to hoard gold, or stones, instead of actual money. 

However there will be many forces to counteract the dilligence of hoarding gold and stones. The first will be the law of supply 41

and demand. If people will buy gold and stones for the purpose of hoarding, the price of said article will increase. Second, it will be paid for with depreciated money. This will be another reason why the price of gold and stones will become too high. 

Again, the merchant who will receive the depreciated money will be forced to spend it otherwise, the outstanding money will become a social wealth. As such, it will be spent in the form of social insurance. In other words, every dollar received by anyone for participating in the production of goods, no matter whether by actual earning or profits or through some other ways. This money will have to be used up in consuming the creative goods, which the money represents. It makes no difference by whom the money is being spent, whether by private individuals, or through social insurance, as long as it serves its main purpose. 42

Also gold in the bar form, can be easily made inconvenient for the average person to buy through control of the gold by the government. And there may not be any other way of buying it except in the form of manufactured articles and the high costs of selling it with profits and perhaps a special tax on gold, will all have to be added on the price. That will again make the price of gold so high that people will lose their desire to buy gold for the purpose of hoarding. 

There may be other reasons why people will not like to hoard gold and this is the loss of bank interest. Obviously a depreciated monetary system does not mean the abolition of banking, and people will certainly want to regain through bank interest part of their loss caused by depreciation. 

But the main reason against hoarding will arise from the fact that people will no longer find the necessity for hoarding, as 43

every person will feel that he is providing for "needy times" like the popular expression "for a rainy day," through social insurance, with the money which will be derived through depreciation and also for the greater possibilities of obtaining employment to earn a living.

PREAMBLE OF THE LEAGUE FOR ECONOMIC SECURITY By the Author 

Due to the fact that the present existing Monetary System is a failure, and carries in itself the cause of economic insecurity, it makes human lives unsafe. It denies people their natural, moral and religious right to earn an honest living through labor and useful occupations. 

Furthermore, seeing that the existing monetary system has brought to many millions of human beings, hunger, misery, crime, desperation, and suicide.

Hence, looking upon all of this, I voluntarily join the League for 

Economic Security and I obligate myself to support the League with all possible and peaceable means.
