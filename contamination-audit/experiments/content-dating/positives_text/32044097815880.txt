SUMMARY The 

Under the police power of Massachusetts, the Commonwealth may regulate the sale and installation of lightning rods. At present, vendors of lightning rod equipment in the Commonwealth are controlled neither by state legislation nor by local ordinances. persons who install such equipment must conform to no specific state requirements; in some instances, they are however, governed by local laws relative to the erection of antennae. Such regulations are concerned with structural strength rather than electrical aspects. The state, in the absence of specific legislation on lightning rods, may exercise some restraint through its building inspection laws.

Under these circumstances, the insurance companies at this time provide the only real regulation of lightning rod equipment in Massachusetts. This is accomplished through those companies declining to write fire insurance policies until it is clear that proper installation of such equipment has been made. Proper installation calls for the use of only those materials which have passed a thorough test showing that the equipment meets the minimum standards of organizations such as Underwriters' Laboratories, Inc., or Factory Mutual Laboratories. In addition these materials when installed must be properly inspected whereupon the label is attached showing that all requirements of the Underwriters have been met. Premises with non-insured equipment remain almost wholly outside any form of regulation.

Most lightning rod companies in Massachusetts which manufacture or sell such equipment comply with the national standards because insurance companies reduce premiums by 10% when the equipment which is being insured bears the National Board of Fire 

Underwriters' label. 

Those states having statutory regulations are concerned principally with approved materials and proper installation, relying on tests and inspections by the nationally recognized research laboratories for such approval. Some states that require license fees to sell equipment make no attempt to regulate or inspect installations. On the other hand, one state (Kentucky) has no statutory regulation but does exercise some control through departmental regulations.

The Province of Ontario, in addition to the type of controls in use in the United States, has an effective governmental inspection program to ensure adherence to the law.

REGULATION OF THE SALE AND INSTALLATION OF LIGHTNING RODS INTRODUCTION 

Senator Ralph Lerche of Hampden, Hampshire and Berkshire, has requested the Legislative Research Bureau to make a study of the regulations in use of the sale and installation of lightning rods in Massachusetts, and the practices followed in other states. 

Legislation regulating the sale and installation of devices is designed to protect life and property. It is required primarily to protect the public (1) from individuals who lack sufficient technical knowledge and experience to install such equipment properly, and (2) from those who use unethical practices. 

Carelessly installed protective equipment can be a grave danger to the public. Unfortunately, there is no certain means of determining whether fires caused by lightning that damage or destroy "rodded" buildings are the result of careless installation or simply beyond man's preventive efforts. Damage reports indicate whether the buildings concerned were equipped with rods but do not show the condition of the installation. Fire marshals and insurance investigators concur that there are many defective installations, adding that this faulty work contributes in large measure to the loss of rodded buildings by fire.

In past years, the lightning rod business has acquired a somewhat unsavory reputation due to the many unscrupulous operators in the rural areas taking orders for such equipment. They would vanish after collecting their money, leaving in their wake a sub-standard job, or perhaps no job at all.

- 

Whether or not the lightning rod business has a larger share of unethical operators today than other businesses, various state and local governments have instituted minimum controls to protect persons purchasing this protective equipment. 

REGULATION BY GOVERNMENT 

State Regulation in Massachusetts

Under their police powers, states may regulate the sale and installation of lightning rods. In Massachusetts, no statute regulates the sale or installation of lightning rods. The State Fire 

Marshal administers and enforces chapters 148 and 266 of the General Laws, but these chapters contain no references to lightning rods. 

Moreover, the State Fire Marshal's office does not have any departmental rules or regulations on this subject. In the same Department of Public Safety, the Building Inspector and Supervisor of Plans has no knowledge of any state regulation of the sale or the installation of lightning rods within the Commonwealth.

Although the state does not regulate such sales or installations, the cities and towns may regulate lightning rod installations through building ordinances and by-laws enacted by them under authority of 

General Laws, chapter 143, section 3. 

The State Board of Standards completed a proposed uniform building code a few years ago to be submitted to cities and towns throughout the Commonwealth for approval; it did not refer to lightning rods. 

The Chairman of that Board, has no knowledge of any local code containing regulations governing lightning rods. Local codes are concerned with the structural strength of antennae installations rather than electrical aspects.

The General Laws of the Commonwealth in Chapter 143 authorize cities and towns to establish local building codes which may restrain careless or improper installations of lightning rod equipment.

Regulation in Other States 

Few states have exercised much control over lightning rod sales or installation.

A questionnaire which was sent by the Legislative

Research Bureau to 36 states and the Province of Ontario, asked what statutory requirement or departmental regulations were in effect relative to the sale and installation of this protective equipment. 

With practically all of the states reporting, the answers clearly reflect a state practice of leaving such regulation to the local level of government where municipalities exercise regulation in the form of building and electrical codes. 

(See At least seven states have, however, passed some form of legislation in this field. Of these, three New England states, Maine, New Hampshire and Vermont have the most inclusive controls. 

Appendices for the full texts of relevant statutes which are summarized below.) 

Maine and New Hampshire require a license of any person, firm or corporation engaged in the sale of lightning rods while Vermont's statute refers only to persons selling lightning rods. Licenses are granted in Maine by the Insurance Commissioner, in New Hampshire by the State Fire Marshal, and in Vermont, by the Commissioner of Public 

Safety. 

Alabama licenses all who sell outside their regularly established business. Louisiana levies a fee on travelling vendors of lightning rods. Tennessee requires a privilege tax from a dealer or agent selling lightning rods.

Several conditions enter into the granting of a license. Maine will not grant a license until the Insurance Commissioner approves the equipment and the manner of installation. Vermont requires that the Public Safety Commissioner approve the equipment after a test conducted by either the National Board of Fire Underwriters or by any

ct e: agency making equal tests. In New Hampshire, the State Fire Marshal must also approve the material which bears the master label indicating prior approval of the Underwriters Laboratories, Inc. 

Some statutes contain bonding provisions. In Maine, a manufacturer or agent must file a $10,000 bond to guarantee refund of the cost of equipping a building damaged by lightning or he must repair the damage. In New Hampshire a dealer must file a $5,000 bond to guarantee that materials used and the installation of materials have the prior approval of Underwriters Laboratories, Inc. If Underwriters Laboratories does not approve material within 90 days of completion of the installation, the dealer must reimburse the owner for the cost of the installation, labor and materials. There are further provisions in both the Maine and the New Hampshire statutes which permit property owners of rodded premises to bring suit on the bond.

Agents are licensed separately from dealers and their fees are much lower. Licenses must be exhibited to purchasers and upon demand, to public officers. Maine and New Hampshire require that copies of the law be furnished the purchaser. Statutory violations call for varying penalties with a maximum of $200 or 6 months imprisonment, or both.

REGULATION BY THE INSURANCE INDUSTRY 

In the absence of state control, fire prevention groups, motivated by severe property damage and threat to life from fires caused by lightning, have developed a comprehensive code for protection against lightning. The Underwriters' Laboratories, Inc., a national research non-profit organization, sponsored by the National Board of Fire Underwriters, has developed a field inspection program covering nationally recognized standards. Its "Master Label", placed upon approved equipment and installations, indicates a thorough and tested inspection. Insurance people usually insist upon either the Master 

Label or some equivalent type of inspection.

- Any lightning protection system which is to bear the Master Label of Underwriters' Laboratories, Inc. must meet definite standards established by the Underwriters' Laboratories. The Master Label is evidence that the installation of the lightning protection system: 

1. has been made by an accredited installer of a manufacturer of listed lightning protection equipment, 2. 3. materials used are subject to factory inspection service by 

Underwriters' Laboratories, Inc. and are labeled, and is subject to a field inspection program covering nationally recognized standards.

These requirements, published in a thirty-page manual, on file with the Bureau, are intended as a guide for ready reference by installers and inspectors and are in general accord with the above mentioned Code for Protection Against Lightning. 

It is an essential requirement for Master Label installations that all materials which are necessary for correct installation work, be of types listed for qualified manufacturers. Parts other than those subject to factory inspection by Underwriters' Laboratories, Inc. are not acceptable. Sale of Lightning Rods 

Sec. 307. Manufacturer selling lightning rods licensed. No manufacturer, whether a person, firm or corporation, shall sell or offer for sale material used for the protection of buildings from damage by lightning until licensed to do so by the commissioner as hereinafter provided. 

Sec. 308. Conditions of license; bond; guarantee. No such license shall be issued until the commissioner has approved of the material made by such manufacturer for protection from lightning and of the manner and system of installing such material, nor until such manufacturer has filed a bond with the commissioner in the penal sum of $10,000 with surety or sureties satisfactory to the commissioner, conditioned for fulfilling the guarantee agreement provided for by this section together with a written stipulation that legal process affecting such manufacturer or his agent, served upon the commissioner for the time being, shall have the same effect as if personally served upon such manufacturer or his agent within the state. The manufacturer shall also file with the commissioner a copy of the guarantee agreement to be issued by him, which shall be in a form approved by the commissioner and must provide in substance that in the event of damage by lightning to property equipped by said manufacturer or his agent, any money paid for the equipment of said building shall be returned to the owner thereof or the damage to said building repaired. 

When the manufacturer has complied with the foregoing requirements and the commissioner is satisfied that the manufacturer is safe and reliable as to assets, business standing and methods and is entitled to confidence, the commissioner shall issue a license to such manufacturer to continue in force 1 year from date of issue. The license may be revoked at any time by the commissioner for good cause after a hearing. 

Sec. 309. Agent's license. Upon written notice from a manufacturer, licensed under the provisions of the preceding section, of the appointment of a suitable person, who must be a resident of the state, to act as his agent in this state, and upon the presentation of a certificate of the good reputation and moral character of such person, signed by the mayor or selectmen of the city or town of which he is a resident, the commissioner may, if he is satisfied that the appointee is a suitable person and a resident of this state, issue to him a license as such agent. For the purposes of the provisions of sections 307 to 312, inclusive, "such agent" shall be construed to mean the duly licensed resident of this state who purchases, sells and installs such lightning rod material. Such license shall continue in force until the 31st day of December following the date of issue but may be revoked at any time by the commissioner for good cause after a hearing.

- Sec. 310. Agent to exhibit license when requested by public of ficer. Every agent shall, upon demand, exhibit his license to any mayor, selectman, sheriff or his deputy, constable or police officer and to any person to whom he sells or offers to sell lightning rods, and shall furnish a copy of sections 307 to 312, inclusive, to every person to whom he sells such lightning rods. If he neglects or refuses to do so, he shall be liable to the penalty provided by the following section for acting as such agent without a license. 

Sec. 311. Selling without license; license not transferable.

Whoever sells or offers for sale such lightning rods or other material without being licensed as provided by section 308 or section 309, shall be punished by a fine of not more than $200 or by imprisonment for 6 months for each offense. The licenses provided for by sections 

308 and 309 are valid for only 1 person, firm or corporation and are not transferable. 

Sec. 312. Holder of guarantee agreement may bring suit on bond. 

The holder of any guarantee agreement issued under the provisions of section 308 may bring suit in the name of the commissioner upon the bond provided by said section and have the same procedure and remedies thereon as in the case of official bond of sheriffs, but the amount of damages need not be first ascertained. Whenever legal process against such manufacturer is served upon the commissioner, he shall take such action as is provided in the case of the service of legal process against foreign insurance companies.

APPENDIX E BALTIMORE CITY, MARYLAND BALTIMORE CITY CODE (1950 Edition) ART. 5 PARAGRAPH 4994 

"I. Lightning Conductor Systems. 

All masonry and reinforced concrete chimneys over 50 feet in height shall be provided with a lightning conductor system. 

Lightning conductor systems for chimneys less than 200 feet in height shall have at least one vertical conductor while chimneys 200 feet or over in height shall have two vertical conductors, diametrically opposite each other.

The vertical conductors shall be connected at the bottom to the ground and at the top to a horizontal cable. These conductors shall be continuous, without splices, and shall be fastened securely flat against the chimney with screw clamps spaced not over 10 feet apart.

The conductors shall be not less than 5/16 inch diameter copper conductors for chimneys less than 60 feet high and not less than 7/16 inch diameter copper for higher chimneys.

The horizontal cable shall be of the same size as the conductors and shall be fitted with rods around the periphery of the top of the chimney, not more than 6 feet apart, and projecting vertically not less than 6 inches above the top of the chimney. The rods shall be of copper properly secured. All splices and contacts shall be brazed or soldered. When required to resist acid gases, the entire conductor system above a point 25 feet below the top of the chimney shall be coated with chemically pure lead 1/16 inch thick. 

Lightning grounds for masonry chimneys shall consist of ground rods or girdle around the foundation of the chimney. Each vertical conductor shall be connected by a soldered joint or copper covered ground clamp to a ground. Ground rods shall be steel rods 7/8 inch in diameter, covered with a substantial copper sheath and shall be driven at least 8 feet into the ground. The ground rods shall have not less than 30 per cent copper by volume. The girdle shall consist of a copper strip 1/8 by 2 inches, or equivalent, placed not less than 18 inches below finished ground level."

APPENDIX F COMMONWEALTH OF KENTUCKY STANDARDS OF SAFETY Section 2613: LIGHTNING PROTECTION. 

(a) 

Where necessary, protection against lightning shall be provided in accordance with recognized safe practices, for all schools, churches, hospitals, and asylums, hotels and apartment houses, factories, department stores, and all places of assembly, including, but not limited to, motion picture theatres, auditoriums, open-air theatres, tents, and recreation halls; all oil or gas tanks; and all nonmetallic or improperly grounded smokestacks and chimneys. 

The Code for Protection Against Lightning, 1945 printing, as published by the U.S. Department of Commerce, shall be deemed safe practice. Particular attention should be given to isolated schoolhouses and churches, or other similar places where people may congregate during thunderstorms. 

Such protection is also recommended for all farm buildings and, where necessary, for all other buildings in which people live, sleep, congregate, or work; and for all overhead electric lines and the apparatus connected thereto. (b) In determining whether or not protection against lightning is necessary, due consideration shall be given to the frequency and severity of thunderstorms for the locality in question, the local building condition (whether on high or low ground, whether in isolated, partially built-up or closely built-up areas), the occupancy of the building, and other pertinent factors.
