Introduction Estate Taxes and Legacies and Successions Taxes 

Estate Taxes and Legacies and Successions Taxes are excise taxes on the exercise of privilege. To tax the privilege of an owner of property to pass it at his death, either by will or by intestate succession, is an Estate Tax, and estate taxes are levied on the estate as an entity, not upon distributive shares therein. On the other hand, a Legacies and Successions Tax is a tax levied on the privilege of beneficiaries under a will, heirs under the law of intestacy, surviving joint tenants, donees or grantees of transfers within one year of death, or donees or grantees where possession vests at death of the donor or grantor to take property by reason of death of the owner. The Kansas inheritance tax statute is a Legacies and Successions Tax Law. Taxes levied thereunder are computed on beneficial shares and are collectible from distributees personally, whether they take by will, by law, by gift or grant in contemplation of death or taking effect in enjoyment or possession at death of the donor or grantor, or by survivorship.

The first Kansas Legacies and Successions Tax Law was enacted in 1909 

(L. 1909, ch. 248) but was repealed in 1913 (L. 1913, ch. 330). Thereafter 

Chapter 357, Laws of 1915, was passed, but provided for taxation of indirect and collateral successions only. It was amended by Chapter 305, Laws of 

1919, to include direct successions of surviving spouses and all lineal ancestors and lineal descendants, and the law as amended is still in force. The Kansas law follows similar legislation in other states and the sum levied thereunder is not a tax but an exaction upon the right to take property by will or descent under the law of the state.

The Kansas law is therefore an excise tax rather than a property tax. Accordingly the constitutional rule requiring uniformity in rate of assessment of property prevailing in this and other states does not apply. 

Property Subject to Tax 

The Kansas Legacies and Successions Tax Law, being a tax law and not an escheat law, is imposed on the right to succeed to property through death of the owner. To impose such a tax Kansas must have jurisdiction over property so taxed. The state has jurisdiction over all real or tangible personal property situated within its boundaries, whether passing by will, by descent, by gift in contemplation of death, by gift made to take effect in possession at death, or by joint tenancy with right of survivorship.

Chapter 400, Laws of 1955, amended the Kansas act to include as tangible personal property taxable by this state oil and gas leases on Kansas land and all interests created thereby or arising therefrom. 

It has been a universally accepted rule that intangibles such as bank accounts, mortgages, stocks and bonds are taxable at the domicile of the decedent, even though physically located elsewhere. However, the decision of the 

Supreme Court of the United States in the case of the State Tax Commission (5)

Introduction of Utah v. Aldrich (316 U. S. 174; 62 U. S. Sup. Ct. Repts. 108, 86 U. S. Law 

Ed. 1358), give the various states power to impose inheritance taxes on stock of domestic corporations held by nonresident estates. Kansas, because of reciprocal legislation with sister states, (K. S. A., 79-150le) levies taxes on domestic stock held by nonresidents only when the decedent was domiciled outside the United States, but it is necessary to obtain written consent of the Director of Revenue before stock of a Kansas corporation can be transferred out of a decedent's name, whether involved in resident or nonresident estates. 

Bonds of domestic corporations held by nonresident estates do not come within the scope of the Kansas law and should be transferred by the corporations without written consent of the Director.

Kansas Estate Tax 

K. S. A. 79-1501a is applicable only to estates subject to Federal Estate 

Taxes wherein a credit for state death taxes is granted under Title 26 U. S. C. A., sec. 2011, 1954, Internal Revenue Code. When taxes imposed on distributive shares are less than the Federal Death Tax credit, Kansas levies an estate tax in the sum necessary to make its taxes equal the credit. Such credit is carried by the Internal Revenue Department as a deficiency tax pending proof of payment of state inheritance taxes in a like amount. Failing such proof, the credit is payable to the government as a deficiency Federal Estate 

Tax. 

K. S. A. 79-1501d concerns only estates in which no tax is payable under 

79-1501, but in which a death tax credit is due under Title 26 of the Internal 

Revenue Act of 1954. By virtue of 79-1501d Kansas levies an estate tax equalling the credit.

Where estates of Kansas decedents own real or tangible personal property in other states, and where a nonresident decedent owns real or tangible personalty in Kansas, the death tax credit is prorated to Kansas in the ratio assets within the jurisdiction of Kansas bear to the gross Federal valuation. 

K. S. A. 79-1501b provides that estate taxes levied by Kansas under the provisions of 79-1501a and 79-1501d shall be prorated to beneficiaries of the state in proportion to the share in the net estate received by each, even though no tax may otherwise be due from the beneficiary. 

Imposition of Tax 

K. S. A. 79-1501 provides that "All property, corporeal or incorporeal and any interest therein, within the jurisdiction of the state, whether belonging to the inhabitants of the state or not" which shall pass within the following classes shall be taxed. 

1. Property passing by will. 

2. Property passing by intestate succession. 

3. Property passing by deed, grant, or gift made within one year of death or in contemplation of death. 

4. Property passing by deed, grant, or gift made or intended to take effect in possession or enjoyment after the death of the grantor. 

5. Property held in joint tenancy passing to a surviving joint tenant.

Discharge of Lien 

Property of which a decedent died seized or possessed is subject to the lien of all inheritance taxes and interest thereon which may become due, and any property acquired in substitution therefor is likewise charged with such lien. However, the lien so imposed does not affect the title to personal property after it has been sold or disposed of for value by an administrator, executor or trustee.

The lien charged upon real and personal property is discharged by payment of inheritance taxes and interest to the County Treasurer. It is the duty of the County Treasurer to file a copy of the receipt for such taxes with the Probate Court, and when the receipt is placed on record in the Probate Court it serves as evidence of the discharge of all inheritance tax lien by payment. 

The county treasurer may not refuse to accept partial payment of tax but where partial payment is tendered the lien remains until all tax liability and interest have been paid.

When taxes are levied upon successions involving future or remainder interests under K. S. A. 1504 the remaindermen may elect not to pay the taxes until they come into possession and enjoyment of the property, providing a bond approved by the Probate Court is filed to secure payment. The bond, if a personal bond, shall be in a penal sum not less than three times the tax so secured, and if a corporate bond shall be for a sum not less than one hundred twenty-five percent of the tax imposed. Taxes so secured bear four percent interest and the bond must be renewed every five years. 

The lien may be satisfied in relation to a separate parcel of real property in an estate if payment of the tax to the state is secured by bond or deposit, or by substitution of other real estate therefor as security with the approval of the Probate Court having jurisdiction.

Where it is necessary for an estate to dispose of securities prior to the time when a complete report of the case can be made for determination of tax liability, the Director of Revenue authorizes issuance of consent to transfer such securities upon certification by the Probate Court of a preliminary inventory setting forth the property it is desired to transfer and describing and valuing real estate to act as security for taxes to be determined later. 1. Property Passing by Will 

Property taken by bequest or devise is liable for inheritance taxes imposed on the exercise of the right of succession under the will. It is immaterial if the persons receiving the property have established their right to the property by virtue of a contract or as beneficiaries. 

In the case of The State v. Mollier, 96 Kan. 514, 519, 152 Pac. 771, the court held: 

"Section 1 of chapter 248 of the Laws of 1909 (Gen. Stat. 1909, Sec. 9265), imposed a succession tax upon the right to take property by will or by the intestacy laws or by transfer made in the contemplation of death. The provisions of section 1 exempting from the operation of the act the case of a bona fide purchase for full consideration in money or money's worth' was intended to apply solely to transfers by deed or grant made in contemplation of death, and has no application to the transmission of property by will.' 

The Court in its opinion on page 919, said: "It has been universally held by the courts that statutes of this character impose a tax not upon the property but upon the succession, the right to take the property by will or by the intestacy laws or by transfer made in contemplation of death." (Citing authorities.)

The foregoing decision was followed in the case of The State v. Gerhards, 99 Kan. 462, 162 Pac. 1149, wherein the court held: "Where persons assume to take property by virtue of a will bequeathing it to them, they are liable for the inheritance taxes imposed on the exercise of their right of succession under the will, and it is immaterial that they might have otherwise established their right to the property through a contract or as 'cestuis que trust.' (See, also, Daum v. Inheritance Tax Commission, 135 Kan. 210, 9 P. 2d 

992; Rooney v. Inheritance Tax Commission, 143 Kan. 143, 146, 53 P. 2d 

500, 502.)

Introduction 

9 The imposition of the tax is not limited to property passing gratuitously by will, but is extended to all property transferred in such manner. Where property passes by will and not by contract, it is subject to succession taxes as imposed by the inheritance tax law. (See the case of State v. Mollier, 96 Kan. 514, 152 Pac. 771, heretofore cited under this subject.)

In the case of Rooney v. Inheritance Tax Commission, 143 Kan. 143, 53 P. 

2d 500, the court held: "The proceedings considered in an action to abate succession taxes and to obtain a refund of succession taxes, on money which, in fact, passed by will, and held, there is no basis for application of the exception contained in the succession tax statute relating to deed or grant intended to take effect in possession or enjoyment after the death of the grantor, to a bona fide purchaser for full value in money or money's worth.” 

2. Property Passing by Intestate Succession 

All property passing by operation of the law of descent and distribution, is subject to legacies and successions taxes. Heirs are determined by the law of intestate succession as of the date of decedent's death, regardless of subsequent decease of such heirs. If heirs agree that property shall pass in a manner different from that prescribed by the law of intestate succession, the agreement may be enforced, and will be followed if such an agreement is approved by a Probate Court or District Court. Informal family settlements cannot be recognized for tax purposes. 

In the case of Erwin v. Erwin, 153 Kan. 703, 113 P. 2d 349, the court held: "The probate court has jurisdiction to settle and distribute an estate in accordance with the terms of a written contract of final settlement and distribution fairly entered into by all the heirs, the validity of which is not questioned and which is properly presented to that court, upon due notice, for final settlement and distribution."

For inheritance tax purposes, however, this may be done when the agreement does not alter or change the tax liability or the taxable shares as determined by the inheritance tax law.

In the case of The State v. Mollier, 96 Kan. 514, 152 Pac. 771, the court held: "A will was executed in 1901 bequeathing all the property of the testator to his niece, in pursuance of a contract entered into between them many years before, by which she agreed to live with and care for him as long as he lived. When he died in 1911, the contract had been fully performed. 

Held, that the property passed by the will and not by the contract, and is liable to the succession tax imposed by the inheritance tax law of 1909.” 

See the case of State v. Gerhards, 99 Kan. 462, 162 Pac. 1149.

If the decedent leaves a will and the surviving spouse elects to take under the law of intestate succession, then, so far as the surviving spouse is concerned, there is no will and her share of the estate is taxable in accordance with the tax liability measured by the law of intestate succession and not by the terms of the will. 

In the case of Tomb v. Bardo, 153 Kan. 766, 114 P. 2d 320, the court stated the facts and held as follows: "A testator in items one and two of his will made specific devises of real estate; in items three and four bequeathed specific legacies, and in item five devised specific real estate to his widow. Item six provided that after items

Introduction one, two, three, four and five have been fulfilled, the widow was to have the income from all the property, real and personal, during her life. Under item seven the remainder in certain real estate, after the death of the widow, was devised to a niece of the testator. Under item eight it was provided, that after the death of the widow, and item seven was fulfilled, 'the net proceeds of all the remaining property' was given to the persons therein named. The widow elected to take against the will. “In an action to construe the will and to determine the rights of the devisees and legatees thereunder, the record is examined, and held: (1) By the election of the widow to take against the will the attempted life estate in the widow failed in its inception; (2) that by reason of the failure of the life estate the remainders were accelerated; (3) that while the testator is presumed to know his widow had the right to renounce the will, the renunciation makes only such changes in the will as the law compels; (4) that it was the intention of the testator that the specific devises and legacies in the first four items of the will should be fulfilled and have priority over the remainders; (5) that real estate devised to the widow in item five of the will should be sold and the proceeds applied to make up the loss sustained by the devisees and legatees under the first four items; (6) that the remainders should be charged with the amount then necessary to compensate the specific devisees and legatees for the depletion caused by the widow's renunciation; (7) that an express power of sale of the real estate in item eight was given to the executrix; (8) and that distribution should be made per capita to the forty-two beneficiaries under clause eight." 

3. Property Passing by Deed, Grant, or Gift, "Made in Contemplation of Death"

Property is presumed to be transferred by grant or gift in contemplation of death when the grant or gift is executed within a year prior to the death of the grantor or donor. This presumption is not conclusive however and may be overcome by sufficient evidence. 

The phrase "in contemplation of death" has not been uniformly defined by the court. However the Supreme Court of the United States in the case of 

United States v. Wells, 283 U. S. 102, 51 S. Ct, 446, defined the phrase as follows: "It cannot be said that the determinative motive is lacking merely because of the absence of a consciousness that death is imminent. It is contemplation of death, not contemplation of imminent death, to which the statute refers. It is conceivable that the idea of death may possess the mind so as to furnish a controlling motive for the disposition of property, although death is not thought to be close at hand."

A gift is made in contemplation of death within the meaning of the legacies and successions tax law if the motive inducing it leads to a testamentary disposition. Since the purpose of the "contemplation of death" provision is to reach substitutes for testamentary dispositions in order to prevent evasion of the tax, the statute is satisfied where a donor or grantor becomes concerned over disposition of his property at death and takes steps to effect its devolution by contract or otherwise. Even though the donor or grantor was unaware death was imminent the transfer may have been so motivated. "A gift made in contemplation of death' when the motive inducing it is of the sort that leads to testamentary disposition, but not when the motive is merely to attain an object desirable to the donor in his life, as where the immediate and moving cause of transfers was the carrying out of a policy, long followed by the decedent in dealing with his children, of making liberal gifts to them during his lifetime." (U. S. v. Wells, 283 U. S. 102, S. Čt. 446.)

Introduction 

11 In the case of Bank v. McGowan, 323 U. S. 594, 65 S. Ct. 496, the court held: "A transfer is 'in contemplation of death' within the meaning of section 

302 (c), where the thought of death is the impelling cause of the transfer.” (See, also, the case of Allen v. Trust Co., 326 U. S. 630, 66 Sup. Ct. Repts. 389.)

Every donor knows that gifts effected during life are not includible in his estate at death. In the statutory sense such gifts are not made in contemplation of death. Many gifts are motivated by purposes associated with life rather than in anticipation of death. They may be made in order to recognize special needs or to discharge moral obligations. The gratification of such desires may constitute a more compelling motive than the thought of death. The dominant or compelling motive is a question of fact in each particular case. 

In the case of Rosenthal v. People, 71 N. E. 1121, 211 Ill. 306, the court held: "The evidence in a proceeding by the state held to justify a finding that a gift by a husband to his wife 33 days before his death was made in contemplation of death, and subject to tax under Laws 1895, p. 301 providing for the taxation of gifts made in contemplation of death." 

In the case of People v. Kelley, 75 N. E. 1038, 218 Ill. 509, the court held: "A trust deed, not made in contemplation of death, took effect on delivery for the benefit of the beneficiaries, except that the grantors reserved to themselves out of the income of the fund $2,400.00 annually for life. Held, that so much of the estate conveyed as was necessary to produce the income provided for was subject to the inheritance tax." (See, also, People v. Porter, 123 N. E.

4. Gifts to Take Effect in Possession After Death 

An irrevocable trust agreement whereby the trustor fully parts with the legal title to the property, reserving the income therefrom for life, and named beneficiaries are to succeed to the remainder upon his death, constitutes “a deed, grant or gift made or intended to take effect in possession or enjoyment after the death of the grantor."

On April 22, 1940, the Supreme Court in the case of Russell v. Cogswell, 151 Kan. 14, 98 P. 2d 179, held: "The Kansas Inheritance Tax Law contains no provision under which a tax may validly be imposed upon property which passes by deed, grant or gift, ‘made or intended to take effect in possession or enjoyment after the death of the grantor.""

The legislature thereafter enacted chapter 369, Laws of 1941 which remedied the law by inclusion of the deferred possession clause of the statute. The foregoing case, decided before the statute made such gifts taxable, is applicable to the present law. (See Russell v. Cogswell, 151 Kan. 793, 101 P. 2d 361.)

An agreement whereby the owner of property is to devise to another's next of kin has the same effect.

In the case of Daum v. Inheritance Tax Commission, 135 Kan. 210, 9 P. 2d 

992, the court held: "D and wife had no living issue, but had substantial property. They agreed between themselves they would make no disposition of it while both were living, but the survivor would, by will, leave one-half of it to his next of kin and the other half to her next of kin. D survived his wife and on his death left a will giving all the property to his next of kin. The next of kin of Mrs. 

D sued and recovered one-half of the property less expenses of administration. 

Held, the property was liable to an inheritance tax for the reasons, that in effect it passed by will, and by grant or gift in contemplation of death, or by

Classifications and Exemptions 

K. S. A. 79-1501 provides three classifications for successions-class A for direct heirs; class B for indirect heirs; class C for collateral heirs and all others. 

Class A consists of the surviving spouse; lineal ancestors; lineal descendants; legally adopted child or its lineal descendants; and wife or widow of a son, or husband of a daughter.

Class B is limited to brothers, sisters, half brothers and half sisters. 

Class C includes relatives of all degrees of consanguinity not included in 

Classes A and B and strangers in the blood.

Bond to Secure Tax on Future Interests 

K. S. A. 79-1503 sets forth procedure whereby bond may be filed with the county treasurer to secure taxes computed upon contingent interests after the expiration of one or more life estates or estates for years. This section of the statute has not as yet been resorted to by remaindermen. 

Computation of Less Than Absolute Interests

K. S. A. 79-1504 provides that taxes shall be levied on the present value of less than absolute interests and remainder interests, computed according to the American Experience Tables at five percent compound interest. In 

Kansas less than absolute interests involving more than one life are determined by actuarial calculations based on algebraic formulae set forth in Wolfe's "Inheritance Tax Calculations." Taxes so ascertained remain a lien upon the entire property until paid. It is further provided that a remainderman may elect to pay the tax when he comes into possession of the property, in which case bond approved by the Probate Court must be filed to secure payment of the tax. Personal bonds must be not less than three times the amount of tax and corporate bonds may not be less than one hundred twenty-five percent of the tax. Bonds are renewable every five years and provide four percent interest on the tax, but the tax may be paid at any time and the bond thus discharged. Should bond not be renewed the conditions shall be deemed broken and action may be taken on the bond to recover the taxes. Vested estates in remainder are defined as estates wherein the remainderman would take at once if the life tenant were to die. 

The court held in the case of McLean v. Stanley, 134 Kan. 234, 5 P. 2d 

839, that the statutory definition of a vested remainder in construing a will has reference to a postponement of enjoyment rather than postponement of vesting of the estate. In the case of In re Estate of Grueter, 158 Kan. 88, 145 P. 2d 152, the court held that payment of a bequest from proceeds of sale

Introduction of land after one or more life estates therein does not prevent it from being vested for computation of inheritance taxes. The court further held in this case that the definition of vested remainder applies whether the estate in remainder is fee or less.

Compromise Settlement of Tax 

K. S. A. 79-1505 provides that the tax upon interests or estates in expectancy which are contingent shall be determined on the value thereof when the persons entitled thereto come into possession, with no diminution for valuation of estates limiting such interests. It is provided, however, that when it is desired to pay the tax on future or contingent interest immediately, with the approval of the attorney general a compromise settlement of tax liability may be effected, based on the present value of the interest in expectancy after deduction of present value of the limiting life interests, computed on actuarial algebraic formulae based on the American Experience Tables at five percent compound interest.

Collection of Tax 

K. S. A. 79-1506 charges executors, administrators or trustees with responsibility for collection of Legacies and Successions Taxes. They may deduct the taxes from beneficial shares before delivering property, or may collect such taxes from heirs at the time distribution is made. If unable to collect taxes otherwise, they are authorized to enforce collection by sale of real estate. 

Final settlement of an estate may not be made nor may executors, administrators nor trustees be discharged prior to payment of all inheritance tax liability and interest.

In the case of In re Moseley's Estate, 100 Kan. 495, 164 Pac. 1073, the court held that where the tax was not paid and the executor was discharged, the order of discharge was invalid.

Legacy Charged Upon Real Estate 

K. S. A. 79-1507 provides where a legacy is charged by will against a devise of real estate, the devisee must pay the tax to the executor, administrator or trustee, and the tax shall remain a lien upon the real estate until paid. Payment of such tax may be enforced in the same manner as payment of the legacy itself could be enforced by the executor, administrator or trustee. 

Taxes Paid From Other Property 

K. S. A. 79-1508 relieves property charged with payment of taxes on specific bequests or devises from any tax upon the amount necessary to pay such taxes. 

Many testators charge taxes upon specific bequests or devises to the residue of the estate. In such instances residuary shares in the estate are computed after deduction of the sum required to pay taxes on specific devises on bequests charged thereto.

K. S. A. 79-1515 outlines procedure whereby a person who has paid tax may recover such payment where property is afterward surrendered or the tax is judicially determined not to have been chargeable.

K. S. A. 79-1516 provides valuations and taxes determined by the Director of Revenue shall be certified to the probate court and the county treasurer.

Should parties in interest be aggrieved at any time within three months after certification they may petition the Probate Court for reappraisal. Reappraisals certified by the court constitute ground for recomputation of tax liability. The Director of Revenue has discretionary power to increase valuations, when in his opinion such increases are justified, and valuations so increased are final unless reduced by reappraisement. Costs of reappraisement are payable by parties to the proceedings.

Introduction Abatement of Tax and Review 

K. S. A. 79-1517 as amended by Laws of 1963, Chapter 468, limits debts and administration expenses deductible in determination of taxes to those certified by the Probate Court. After certification of tax liability to the court, the county treasurer and the parties in interest, the tax when paid shall discharge the lien on estate assets. At any time after payment of tax, and prior to final settlement of the estate, an aggrieved executor, administrator or trustee may petition the Director of Revenue for abatement of the tax or any portion thereof. On denial of the petition written appeal for a hearing before the 

Board of Tax Appeals may be filed with the Secretary of the Board within thirty days. Upon considering the appeal the Board of Tax Appeals shall enter an order either sustaining the finding of the Director or ordering abatement of any portion of the tax adjudged wrongfully exacted. 

In the case of State v. O'Leary, 144 Kan. 563, 61 P. 2d 1325, the court held that the statutory procedure for redress of a taxpayer's grievance against an excessive succession tax is exclusive and that it must be invoked before resort to the courts for such limited relief as they can give is invoked. 

Appeals 

K. S. A. 74-2426 provides that upon denial by the Board of Tax Appeals of an appeal for abatement of legacies and successions taxes, appeal may be taken to the District Court of the county where the estate is administered. If the estate is not being administered appeal shall be made to the District Court of the county where decedent was domiciled, if a resident. Nonresident estates owning no land shall appeal to the District Court of Shawnee county. The act also provides for appeals from the District Court to the Supreme Court. 

For decisions concerning the bringing of a mandamus action pursuant to the above section of the statutes, see the cases of Frick v. State, 139 Kan. 572, 32 P. 2d 221; Kittredge v. Boyd, 136 Kan. 691, 18 P. 2d 563; Boston Safe

Power of Appointment 

K. S. A. 79-1520 provides that property passing through exercise of a power of appointment shall be taxed as though it were the absolute property of the decedent exercising such power. If the donee of the power of appointment fails to exercise it, either in whole or in part, the persons or corporations thereby becoming entitled to property to which the power related, either in whole or in part, shall be taxed as though they succeeded by a will of the donee of the power taking effect at the time of such failure. 

In the case of Howard's Trust, 65 Ohio St. 191, 29 N. E. 575, the Court quoted Thompson on Wills, 2d ed., sec. 400, as follows: "A power of appointment is a power of disposition given to a person over property not his own, by someone who directs the mode in which that power shall be exercised by a particular instrument. It is not an absolute property, nor an estate, but authority to appoint an estate; in other words, to indicate to whom the estate shall pass. The donee of the power may have a limited estate of his own in the property, such, for instance, as a life estate. In such case the power is distinct from the limited estate, and its exercise is necessary to dispose of the inheritance. A power to dispose of property by will may be given the donee by a direction in the donor's will that the property shall go to anyone whom the donee may by will direct.'

Final Settlement by Representative or Trustee 

K. S. A. 79-1522 provides that no final account of an administrator, executor or trustee shall be allowed by the Probate Court unless a showing is made that all inheritance tax liability has been determined and discharged by payment.

79-1501. Imposition of tax; classification of distributees; exemptions and rates. For the purposes of this section, and of the act of which this section is amendatory, oil and gas leases on lands in this state and all interests created thereby, or arising therefrom, shall be considered as tangible personal property having an actual situs in this state. 

All property, corporeal or incorporeal, and any interest therein, within the jurisdiction of the state, whether belonging to the inhabitants of the state or not, which shall pass by will or by the laws regulating intestate succession, or upon the death of a joint tenant, or by deed, grant or gift made in contemplation of death, or made or intended to take effect in possession or enjoyment after the death of the grantor, to any person, absolutely or in trust, except in case of a bona fide purchase for full consideration in money or money's worth; and except property to or for the use of literary, educational, scientific, religious, benevolent and charitable societies or institutions: Provided, Such use entitles the property so passing to be exempt from taxation; except property to or for the use of the state, a county or a municipality for public purposes, shall be taxed as herein provided.

Distributees of estates, whether they succeed to the ownership of their respective shares by reason of the provisions of a will or under the law of descents and distributions, or as a surviving joint tenant, or by deed, grant or gift made in contemplation of death, or made or intended to take effect in possession or enjoyment after the death of the grantor to any person, absolutely or in trust shall be classified as follows:

Class A shall consist of the surviving husband or wife, the lineal ancestors, lineal descendants, adopted child or children, lineal descendants of any adopted child, the wife or widow of a son, or the husband of a daughter of the decedent.

Class B shall consist of the brothers and sisters of the decedent. 

Class C shall consist of relatives of all degrees of consanguinity, except those excluded in classes A and B, and shall include also strangers in the blood of the decedent.

From the value of the shares, as ascertained under the provisions of this act and succeeded to by the several distributees, exemptions shall be allowed as follows: To the surviving spouse, $75,000; to each other member of class A, $15,000; to each member of class B, $5,000; and the tax herein provided for shall be charged only upon the excess value of the shares over and above the exemption herein declared: Provided, That when one or more of the shares of an estate shall consist of property within and property without the state only such percentage of the exemptions above named shall be allowed as is the (24)

percentage within the state of the total value of the shares: And provided further, That any gift, legacy, inheritance, transfer, appointment or interest which shall be valued, for the purposes of this act, after exemptions are allowed, at a less sum than $200, shall not be subject to the tax herein imposed: Provided further, That all property transferred by a decedent to any person described in class A, providing the same was transferred to such decedent not more than five years prior to his death by another decedent such as described in class A and a tax paid thereon to the state of Kansas, shall be exempt; but the value of said property shall be taken as of the date of death of the first decedent; and the provisions of this proviso shall not apply to a tax paid under the provisions of sections 79-1501a or 79-1501d of the General 

Statutes of 1949, or any amendments thereto.

Upon the value of shares succeeded to by members of class A in excess of the exemptions herein declared, the following rates of tax are hereby imposed: 

On the first $25,000, or fraction thereof, one percent; on the second $25,000, or fraction thereof, two percent; on the next $50,000, or fraction thereof, three percent; on the next $400,000, or fraction thereof, four percent; on all over $500,000, five percent: Provided, That upon the share of the estate passing to the spouse, only one-half of the foregoing rates shall be charged. Upon the value of shares succeeded to by members of class B in excess of the exemptions herein declared, the following rates of tax are hereby imposed: On the first $25,000, or fraction thereof, three percent; on the second $25,000, or fraction thereof, five percent; on the next $50,000, or fraction thereof, seven and onehalf percent; on the next $400,000, or fraction thereof, ten percent; on all over $500,000, twelve and one-half percent. 79-1501e. Foreign estates; reciprocity; personal property. The tax imposed by this act in respect to personal property of nonresidents (other than tangible personal property having an actual situs in this state) shall not be payable (1) if the decedent at the time of his death was a resident of a state or territory of the United States which at the time of his death did not impose a transfer tax or death tax of any character in respect to personal property of residents of this state (other than tangible personal property having an actual situs in

such state or territory), or (2) if the laws of the state or territory of residence of the decedent at the time of his death contained a reciprocal provision under which nonresidents were exempted from transfer taxes or death taxes of every character in respect to personal property (other than tangible personal property having an actual situs therein) provided the state or territory of residence of such nonresidents allowed a similar exemption to residents of the state or territory of residence of such decedent.

In no case shall the provisions of this section apply to the intangible personal property of nonresident decedents unless such tangible [intangible] personal property shall have been subjected to a tax or submitted for purposes of taxation in the state of the decedent's residence. This section shall apply only to estates of decedents dying subsequent to the effective date of this section. 

For the purpose of this section the District of Columbia and possessions of the United States shall be considered territories of the United States. (L. 1941, ch. 369, sec. 5; June 30.) 

79-1502. Payment of tax; lien, how discharged. In cases where property is transferred by deed, grant or gift made in contemplation of death, or made or intended to take effect in possession or enjoyment after the death of the grantor to any person, absolutely or in trust, and in cases where property is held in joint tenancy, the tax thereon shall be due and payable at the time of other inheritance taxes.

If the taxes contemplated by this act are not paid when due, interest at the legal rate shall be charged and collected from the time the same became payable. When the determination of such taxes is delayed beyond eighteen months after the death of the decedent and the director finds that such delay was due to the inability of the executor, administrator or trustee to determine the distributive shares of an estate or the proper recipients therof, or to litigation, or the neglect or failure of the probate court having jurisdiction of the estate to furnish the proper reports of such estate to the director, interest shall not commence until the date that the determination of the tax is made.

79-1504. Valuation of property; bond for tax upon estates in remainder, renewal. The tax on the several shares of the estate, including shares of which vested estates in remainder may be a part or all, shall be due and payable to the treasurer of the proper county, as hereinbefore provided, and said tax shall remain a lien upon the entire property of the respective shares until paid: 

Provided, That the person or persons, body politic or corporate, beneficially interested in the estates in remainder charged with the said tax may elect not to pay the same until the right to come into actual possession or enjoyment of such estates accrues, and in such cases said person or persons, or body politic or corporate, shall give bond to the state of Kansas as hereinafter provided.

Vested estates in remainder, as used herein, shall include all estates where the remainderman, being alive, would take at once if the life tenant were to die. (L. 1941, ch. 369, sec. 8; June 30.)

79-1505. Valuation of future estates or interests; settlement and payment of tax. 79-1511. Probate judge to furnish inventories and appraisal of estates to director; basis of tax; reinventory and reappraisement. The probate judge shall within thirty days after any inventory or appraisal of any estate has been filed in his office, as provided by law, send by mail to the director every such inventory and appraisal, or a copy thereof. The values so reported shall be the basis on which the director shall base the tax, unless the director shall find that not all of decedent's property was inventoried, or that the values fixed in the inventory and appraisal are incorrect, in either of which cases the director may order that assets of the estate reinventoried and reappraised, except that in case the amount received by an executor or administrator for any item in the inventory within 18 months after the death of the decedent and prior to the payment of the tax is greater or less than the value so reported, the director may upon certificate of the probate judge or such other satisfactory evidence as the director may require, showing such fact, use such amount as the basis on which he shall base the tax. Said reinventory and reappraisement to be made under oath by three qualified householders appointed by the probate court on application made by the director. 

Ten days' notice of such order shall be given to the executor, administrator, or trustee of the estate or in case there is no such personal representative, to the person filing the original inventory and appraisal with the director. Such notice when given shall be notice to all interested parties who shall have no opportunity of being heard on the matter of the inventory and appraisal. The probate judge shall also, within the same period, send by mail to the director a copy of the will of the decedent, if such has been allowed by the probate court. The probate judge shall also furnish such copies of papers in his office as the director shall require, and shall furnish information as to the record and

files of his office in such form as the director may require. The director shall excuse the probate court from filing inventories or copies of inventories and of wills of estates no part of which appears to be subject to a tax under the provisions of this act. (L. 1941, ch. 369, sec. 11; June 30.) 

79-1512. Determination as to taxability or amount of tax; certification by director; recordation; estate of nonresident; probate court expenses. Whenever, for the purposes of obtaining a determination by the director as to the taxability of and the amount of tax, if any, which is chargeable against any distributive share or shares of the estate of a decedent under the provisions of this act, any person interested in such an estate shall make it to appear by his duly verified application filed with the director that administration upon such estate is unnecessary for any other purpose than for such determination as to such tax, and shall set forth in the application the names of all persons known to have or claim any interest in the estate, the director shall advise all the parties in interest of the filing of such application, and that after a given date the director will make an investigation as to the value of the estate and of the distributive shares thereof. Before making such valuation the director may require from any party in interest or from any county officer, upon forms to be prepared by the director, such information as will disclose fully the condition of the estate; and after the value of the shares shall have been ascertained, and the tax chargeable against any of the said shares, if any, shall have been determined, the director shall make an order determining such value and the tax, and shall file a certified copy thereof with the probate court of the county in which is situated the estate in question, and shall certify the amount of the tax to the county treasurer of such county for collection. 

Should any person be aggrieved by the action of the director, application may be made to the probate court having jurisdiction of the estate, as provided in sections 79-1516, 79-1517 and 79-1519 of the General Statutes of 1935, as amended, and the said probate court shall in all such cases exercise the authority conferred by said sections in order that any grievance may be remedied. In case it shall be found that no tax is chargeable against any share of the estate being passed upon, the director, through the secretary of the commission, shall issue a certificate to that effect, and such certificate, when recorded in the office of the register of deeds of any county in which is situated real property belonging to such estate, shall be evidence that no lien exists against such real property because of any statute providing a tax upon legacies and successions.

If no person having an interest in the estate shall make application to the director within three months after the death of the decedent, the director may demand of some one of the parties in interest the filing with him of an application in form to be prescribed by the director. In case no application shall be filed as herein provided within three months after a demand therefor, the probate court of the proper county, upon the application of the director, shall immediately appoint an administrator for the sole purposes of ascertaining the value of the estate and the shares thereof, in order that the tax may be determined.

The value of any property, the ownership of which passes by deed, grant or gift made in contemplation of death, and the tax, if any, chargeable thereon,

shall be determined upon information required under the provisions of this section, but in default of such information the director may of his own motion determine the value of all such property and the tax, and certify the same to the probate court and to the county treasurer for collection. When the estate of a nonresident decedent shall have among its assets property within the jurisdiction of the state of Kansas, but which is physically without the state, and no letters testamentary or of administration shall have been taken out in this state, the tax upon the distributive shares of such property shall be determined by the director and in all such cases the director may require that the tax shall be paid directly to the state treasurer. 

Any expense incurred in the probate court under the provisions of this section shall be borne pro rata by the distributees according to the value of their respective shares. (L. 1941, ch. 369, sec. 12; June 30.)

79-1516. Computation of value of property; appraisement. The value of the property upon which the tax is computed shall be determined by the director and notified by him to the person or persons by whom the tax is payable and to the probate court and county treasurer of the proper county, and such determination shall be final unless the value so determined shall be reduced, by proceedings, as herein provided. At any time within three months after such determination the probate court shall, upon the application of any party interested in the succession, or on application of the executor, administrator or trustee, appoint three disinterested appraisers, who, first being sworn, shall appraise such property at its actual value in money as of the day of the death of the decedent, and shall make return thereof to said court.

79-1517. Determination and certification of amount of tax; abatement; review by commission. The director shall determine the amount of tax due and payable upon any estate or upon any part thereof, and shall certify the amount so due and payable to the probate court and to the county treasurer and to the person or persons by whom the tax is payable; but in determination of the amount of any tax said director shall not be required to consider any payments on account of debts or expenses of administration which have not been allowed by the probate court having jurisdiction of the estate. Payment of the amount so certified shall be a discharge of the tax. Any executor, administrator, trustee or grantee who is aggrieved by any determination of said director may, at any time before said estate shall be finally closed, and after the payment of such tax to the county treasurer, apply by petition to the director for the abatement of said tax or any part thereof. The director, after considering such petition, shall enter his order thereon and shall give notice thereof to such executor, administrator, trustee, or grantee by mailing to him a copy of said order, and if said director shall adjudge that said tax or any part thereof was wrongfully exacted it shall order an abatement of such portion of said tax as was assessed without authority of law. 79. Receipts of county treasurer; record in probate court; certificate; recordation. 79-1519. Jurisdiction of controversies by director; counsel for state; order by probate court for payment of tax; actions for tax. And if it appears that there are no goods or assets of the estate in his hands, the court may assess the amount of the tax against the executor, administrator, or trustee, as if for his own debt, and may enforce compliance with such order by proper procedure, as authorized by probate practice; but the administrators, executors, trustees and grantees hereinbefore mentioned shall be personally liable only for such taxes as shall be payable while they continue in the said offices or have title as such grantees, respectively. In the cases where the tax is due and payable by and collectible from the beneficiary, all actions shall be prosecuted by the attorney general or the county attorney of the proper county or the attorney for the director in the name of the state, and such actions may be brought in the same courts as other actions for money. 79-1520. Exercise of power of appointment. (L. 1941, ch. 369, sec. 19; June 30.)

Chapter 74, Article 24.—BOARD OF TAX APPEALS; DEPARTMENT OF REVENUE 

74-2443. Creation of department of revenue; continuation of divisions; director; appointment; employees; expenses. The department shall be a continuation of the divisions operating under the supervision of the director of revenue at the effective date of this act. The director of revenue shall be appointed by and serve at the pleasure of the governor, and shall receive an annual salary to be fixed by the governor with the approval of the state finance council. He may appoint an assistant director, a secretary, and such attorneys, accountants and other employees as may be necessary for the efficient administration of the department, which employees, except the assistant director, secretary and attorney, shall be in the classified service of the Kansas civil service act. The director and all employees shall receive their actual and necessary expenses incurred in the performance of their duties. (L. 1965, ch. 458, sec. 11; July 1.) 

74-2426. Board of tax appeals; final orders; appeals to district court, when; venue, bond; notice of appeal; equity proceedings; referees; evidence; appeals to supreme court; regulations not enforced, when. Whenever the board of tax appeals shall enter its final order on any appeal, said board shall mail a copy of its order by registered or certified mail to the person, firm, corporation or association who was a party to such appeal. Within thirty (30) days after the mailing of the final order of the board, any party to such appeal may appeal to the district court from any order determining, approving, modifying or equalizing the assessment of property for property tax purposes. Appeals from orders made under the taxation of legacies and successions act shall be to the district court of the county where the estate is being administered, but if the estate is not being administered in Kansas, the appeal shall be (1) to the district court of any county where real estate belonging to the estate is located, or if it owns no real estate, then to the district court of Shawnee county if the party appealing is a nonresident, or to the district court of the county where the party appealing resides if the party appealing is a resident of the state of Kansas.

Appeals from orders made under any excise tax act shall be to the district court of the county in which the party appealing resides, or has his principal place of business, or if a nonresident of the state of Kansas, to the district court of the county where he maintains his principal place of business in the state, or if he has no place of business within the state, then to the district court of Shawnee county. Appeals from any final order made by the board as to ad valorem tax liability shall be to the district court of the county in which the property involved is located, or if located in more than one county, then to the district court of any county in which any portion of the property is located. At the time of filing the appeal, the party appealing under any excise tax act, shall give a bond for costs in the sum of twenty-five dollars ($25) conditioned that he shall prosecute the appeal without delay and pay all costs assessed against him, and, in the event that the appeal is from an order determining the amount of tax assessable and the tax has not been paid, an additional bond in an amount equal to twice the amount of the tax assessed. 

The appeal shall be taken by filing, with the clerk of the district court of the proper county, a written notice stating that the party appeals to the district court, and alleging the pertinent facts upon which such appeal is grounded. 

Upon filing of the notice of appeal, the director shall be deemed to be a party to such appeal, and the clerk of the district court shall docket the cause as a civil action, and shall forthwith and without praecipe issue summons and cause the same to be served upon the director, in accordance with the manner now provided by law in civil cases. Jurisdiction to hear and determine such appeals is hereby conferred upon the district courts of this state. Such an appeal shall be heard as an equity proceeding, and shall proceed as an original action. Trial may be had or any order made in term or vacation.

The district courts are empowered to appoint referees, either standing or special, to hear the evidence and recommend findings of fact and conclusions of law. The transcript of the proceedings had at the hearing before the board of tax appeals may be admissible in evidence. Appeals may be taken from the district to the supreme court by the party appealing or by the director as in civil cases, except that the director shall not be required to give bond on appeal. The final decision made in such appeal shall be entered as a judgment as in other civil cases against the party appealing or against the director, as the case may be. In hearing and considering any such appeal, the court shall not enforce or give effect to any rule or regulation which it shall find to be unreasonable, arbitrary or capricious. (L. 1943, ch. 290, sec. 12; L. 1957, ch. 429, sec. 12; L. 1958, ch. 21, sec. 1 (Special Session); May 8.)

Waivers of notice of the pendency of the proceeding shall be required only from distributees whose shares are subject to tax. (Authorized by K. S. A. 79-1512, K. S. A. 74-2437, 74-2443, L. 1957, ch. 429, sec. 5, sec. 10.) 

92-2-17. Nonprobated estates in which determination of descent proceedings are had in Kansas as provided in K. S. A. 59-2250 and 59-2251 of the 

Kansas Probate Code, may be reported to the Director of Revenue on Form 27, with full information relative to the entire estate wherever situated, as of the date of decedent's death, with advice as to the court in which such proceedings are pending. (Authorized by K. S. A. 79-1512; K. S. A. 59-2250, 59-2251; K. S. A. 74-2437, 74-2443, L. 1957, ch. 429, sec. 5, sec. 10.) 

92-2-18. Nonresident estates owning land or tangible personal property, or intangibles with business situs (except intangible personal property coming within the provisions of G. S. 1949, 79-150le) within the jurisdiction of Kansas shall be reported on Form 12-T, whether ancillary administration or Descent Proceedings are instituted in a Kansas court, or whether reported under 79-1512 to the Director of Revenue for proceedings without the intervention of the Probate Court. Schedules required by Form 12-T shall be prepared and attached to the blank and a copy of the will in testate cases shall accompany the report. (Authorized by K. S. A. 79-1501 (e), 79-1512, 79-1517; K. S. A. 

74-2437, 74-2443, L. 1957, ch. 429, sec. 5, sec. 10.) 

92-2-19. Marshalling of assets in nonresident estates owning Kansas property, or in resident estates owing property outside the jurisdiction of Kansas, shall not be permitted. Where a direction as to the manner of payment of legacies is lacking in the will of a decedent, the tax shall be assessed against each legatee according to his interest in the fund or property not specifically bequeathed, and not against the total value of the same as a part of the residuary estate. In such estates the property in Kansas when not specifically devised shall be considered as an integral part of the entire net estate, wherever situated, passing in the proportion in which such entire estate not specifically devised wherever situated devolves. (Authorized K. S. A. 79-1502, 79-1512, 79-1517, 79-1519; K. S. A. 74-2437, 74-2443, L. 1957, ch. 429, sec. 5, sec. 10.)

TRANSFERS 

92-2-24. All property transferred or conveyed by the decedent within one year preceding the date of his death shall be considered, included and taxed as a part of the decedent's gross estate, subject to the taxpayer furnishing sufficient proof that the gift was not made in contemplation of death. (Authorized by K. S. A. 79-1501; K. S. A. 74-2437, 74-2443, L. 1957, ch. 429, sec. 5, sec. 10.) 

92-2-25. Transfers of property by deed, grant or gift made or intended to take effect in possession or enjoyment after the death of the grantor to any person absolutely or in trust are taxable. (Authorized by K. S. A. 79-1501; 

K. S. A. 74-2437, 74-2443, L. 1957, ch. 429, sec. 5, sec. 10.) 

92-2-26. The following should be considered as gifts made to take effect in possession or enjoyment at or after the date of the death of the grantor: Any deed, grant or gift wherein the decedent reserves to himself any interest of the estate or the power of disposition of such property during his lifetime. 

Any deed, grant or gift made absolutely or in trust to a grantee after June 30, 1951, but not recorded until after the date of the death of the grantor, shall be considered taxable as a gift made to take effect in possession or enjoyment after the death of the grantor. Likewise, any property, real or personal, held in the joint names of two or more persons as joint tenants with right of survivorship shall be considered a part of any decedent tenant's estate and taxable in its entirely except where proof of contribution toward purchase thereof is filed by the surviving joint tenant or tenants. (Authorized by K. S. A. 79-1501; 

K. S. A. 74-2437, 74-2443, L. 1957, ch. 429, sec. 5, sec. 10.) 

92-2-27. All property within the taxing jurisdiction of the state of Kansas which shall pass under any trust agreement whether revocable or irrevocable,

Regulations if executed subsequent to June 30, 1941, and some annuities, depending upon the provisions of the annuity contract, shall be includible in the gross estate of the decedent within the state of Kansas and be taxable as gifts to take effect in possession or enjoyment at or after the date of the death of the grantor or trustor. (Authorized by K. S. A. 79-1501, 79-1502; K. S. A. 74-2437, 74-2443, L. 1957, ch. 429, sec. 5, sec. 10.)

GENERAL 

92-2-28. Insurance payable to a named beneficiary absolutely or insurance payable to a trustee for named beneficiaries is not taxable under the Kansas law. Insurance payable to an estate of a decedent, whether absolutely or in trust, is considered a part of the assets of the decedent estate. Refund annuities wherein proceeds revert to an estate at death of the annuitant, are considered a part of the estate assets. (Authorized by K. S. A. 79-1501; K. S. A. 

74-2437, 74-2443, L. 1957, ch. 429, sec. 5, sec. 10.) 

92-2-29. Property passing to Class A beneficiaries from an estate within five years after it has been taxed in a previous Class A succession, and any accretion in value, shall be taxable in the second estate and deduction for previously taxed assets shall be limited to the sum actually taxed in the prior estate, if the amount so passing exceeds in value the sum upon which taxes were paid in the prior estate. (Authorized by K. S. A. 79-1501; K. S. A. 791512; K. S. A. 74-2437, 74-2443, L. 1957, ch. 429, sec. 5, sec. 10.) 

92-2-30. Where a will bequeaths personal effects or other personal property to a beneficiary and directs such person to retain a portion thereof and distribute the balance among others at his discretion, the entire bequest shall be taxed to such beneficiary. (Authorized by K. S. A. 79-1501, 79-1512; K. S. A. 742437, 74-2443, L. 1957, ch. 429, sec. 5, sec. 10.) 

92-2-31. A life estate coupled with a power of disposal is not to be considered the equivalent of a fee simple estate. (Authorized by K. S. A. 79-1501, 79-1502, 79-1504; K. S. A. 74-2437, 74-2443, L. 1957, ch. 429, sec. 5, sec. 10.) 

92-2-32. A bequest by will to a subscribing witness to such will who would not be entitled to any part of the estate in absence of such will, shall be void, and consent of the residuary legatee or legatees under such will filed with the probate court to payment of such bequest shall not make good the attempted gift by will. (Authorized by K. S. A. 79-1501, 79-1512; K. S. A. 74-2437, 74-2443, L. 1957, ch. 429, sec. 5, sec. 10.) 

92-2-33. Notes or other evidence of debt forgiven by will shall be considered assets and deductible from the share of the debtor in distribution of an estate and taxable to such debtor, even though action thereon is barred by the statute of limitations, as the lapse of time does not extinguish an obligation nor satisfy the debt. (Authorized by K. S. A. 79-1501, 79-1512; K. S. A. 74-2437, 74-2443, L. 1957, ch. 429, sec. 5, sec. 10.) 

92-2-34. Where estates contain assets held in joint tenancy with right of survivorship and the joint tenants execute a joint and contractual will, for computation of legacies and successions taxes the surviving joint tenant shall be considered to have surrendered survivorship rights to jointly held assets, and to take under the will. (Authorized by K. S. A. 79-1501, 79-1512; K. S. A. 

74-2437, 74-2443, L. 1957, ch. 429, sec. 5, sec. 10.)
