Palestine Our Holy Land. By "Palestine 

I mean that part of Turkish Syria once occupied by the Philistines from whom the name Palestine derives, by the Hebrews from whom Jews were later descended, and by the Canaanites who were there long before either Philistines or Hebrews and who most nearly resemble the present native Semitic inhabitants of the country. 

By "our" I mean all religious people whose faith has historic and sentimental relation with the little country itself. 

By "Holy Land" I mean the memorable land of early Hebrews, Jews, Christians, and Moslems. Because one is a Jew, Christian, or Moslem does not mean that one is to go with his tape-measure and knife and take a slice of the territory, nay but that one associates the Holy Land, mentally, spiritually with one's Savior and sacred scriptures somewhat as an admirer of Shakespere associates the poet with English scenes. 

Palestine as world-country is to be regarded as Finland, Holland, Greece, and others, but as soul-country may be revered and visited as a shrine. To grab and develop its water power, its salts, and its industrial possibilities is questionable from the point of view of religion, morals, or politics. There should be no conniving to dispossess the native inhabitants or to submerge their style of life in western "improvements." The nearest that wisdom would permit would be to take a careful census of the peoples' choice in all matters which outsiders would recommend for the native consideration.

It is very unfortunate that armies should cross the country, or that the physical weakness of the population should subject it to strong-arm methods by politicians, or industrialists from abroad. Indeed it is most unfortunate that any small country should be ravished by the larger powers. For smallness has certain advantages in the organization of the cultural world and the most difficult problem today is how to secure the neighborly consideration of little countries by greater. The riches of true civilization are increased by the contributions of small societies and pre-eminently by noblesse oblige. 

Even in our urban communities we have zones of industrial, business, and residential development and where the children go to the schools we placard the road with cautions against undue risks to their small bodies. So among the nations of the world there should be a grown-up ethics which would ensure the [3]

An American Friend of the Arabs. It becomes us to interpret the special genius of each of the components of our American blend so that no one will misunderstand any of the citizenships which have surrendered the eastern past to make the new West. One of the least aggressive of the new citizens is the 

Arab. Who would guess that there are hundreds of thousands of former dwellers in Arabian lands, Egypt, North Africa, Arabia itself, Iraq, Syria and Palestine now living in the United States, not to mention the far greater number in the western hemisphere? We continue to have polite relations with the countries from which they came, by travel, commerce, and treaty. And it is necessary in order to complete our knowledge of those countries that we draw on the memories and relationships of those among us who once lived over there, or whose kindred still belong over there. Better than travel, commerce, or treaty in any effort to appreciate the countries of the East is the knowledge which these former children of the East possess and can share with us. In that way they can prove their new loyalty by dropping the disadvantages of the old and conserving the advantages of the new world. If they could not better themselves here they would probably return. If they do better themselves here then they owe it to each of us to live and die for U. S. But if they were to leave behind them all the good of the historic past they would be poor material out of which to build the American nation.

Our nation is not built on race as certain backward politicians of the old world seem bent on re-building their nations since the World War. We are built on a new principle of democracy which even the democracies of the old world do not seem to understand. They are tearing Europe apart in order to constitute new ambitious racial groupings which they call nations but which are really grandiose tribes. It would disrupt our nation to recognize any such racial hatefulness. We are engaged in a more human and liberal business, to make 

America the land of the free, free from racial prejudice and all ingrowing insanities.

It is difference as well as similarity that enriches a civilization and it is humane civilization which makes the new and greater nation of the West. War is a [4]

divider and we want no war. War is a backward device and we wish to go forward. War is worse than bestial, worse than folly and we wish to be wisely human. No peace with war nor with the hateful things that make it! 

What the unthinking might call pro-Semitism roots in humaneness, in a sense of justice and mercy. We have a conscience which revolts against oppression. 

Our debt to Semites, whether Hebrews, Jews, Christians, or Arabs is long. We speak for these not because of their name but because they are humans who have been oppressed. If they ever become oppressors we should speak against them and for their victims. Having been ever appreciative of the richeness of 

Semitic contributions to civilization and ever aroused by the wrongs done to them when they were in position of disadvantage the writer notes that all related questions have entered a new phase which he deplores and the Semites are being persecuted by invaders who call themselves Semites par excellence. We think that there is no excellence in the modern, political Zionism, nor in the schemes of the gentile politicians who saw their opportunity linked with it. The chicanery has been exposed in such standard works as George Antonius' The 

Arab Awakening (Lippincott, 1938) and J. M. N. Jeffries, Palestine: The 

Reality (Longmans, Green & Co., 1939). These works state authoritatively what many of us have come, after years of painful experience and investigation over there, to believe is the right in the Palestine matter. 

Who is interested in the corner of the world known as the Levant? Every one, certainly, who is even remotely connected with those three great religions so closely related to each other and to the eastern homelands of the Mediterranean. 

Having traveled and lived in Palestine, been schoolman, excavator, and admirer of the many values issuing throughout historic time from the birthquarter of Hebraism, Judaism, Christianity, and Islam, the writer who is retired. and is independent of all material concerns over there feels moved to pray for the peace of this world with its multiple woes and blessings and is determined to keep on looking for the kingdom of God in the hearts of human-kind. There is so little one mortal can do to help! But that little let us persist in doing. 

It is my personal confession that for unworthy me there is none to compare with Jesus of Nazareth. If you will share your confession and your dear hope

I shall feel honored and pray that we may yet see a fairer world built according to our finest dreams for our brother-sister humanity.

A Christian Christianity will deal mercifully, even in thought, with the faithful of all faiths. But it is high time that we urge a tender thought for the tens of thousands of the remnant Christian natives in Palestine who are seldom mentioned in this busy world. These and their country have a symbolic significance far beyond their numbers. They are the native remainder of the early [5]

Church and for fifteen centuries have persistently held to their allegiance in spite of unimaginable pressures and misfortunes. Those of their Semitic race who became Moslems over a thousand years ago were, generally, Christian Palestinians before that change. Now, the two faiths of one race work side by side in agricultural pursuits and equally do they deplore the inroads of European politics upon their peasant life. 

Good Americanism then, demands that we understand the many different races and peoples who make up the citizenship of the United States. We are building the Nation and our citizenship is based not on racialism but on this. ideal of nationality. It would be the greatest mistake to try to separate the various elements in America and to return each to its former home even if we could do so. That is what Europe tries to do and see what wretchedness it brings. We are committed to the idea of differences which are to be harmonized, of values in all the races, and of mistakes to be avoided. All peoples have their better and their worse qualities. Our tragedies and our crimes come from those worse qualities. Our politics often increases the dangers of those bad tendencies in every racial group which members of each group recognize as quickly as any one else. To reduce and remove those bad qualities is necessary for the health and happiness of all. To increase civic goodness and loyalties to our country, America, is the privilege, yea the necessity of us all. 

Each racial element ought to try to bring the best from its rich past and to curb any bad inheritance for the common good of our new America. It is someone's duty to bring to our attention the riches of the past as related to each of our racial groups. It is our privilege to survey those riches as helping to explain the genius of our fellow citizens and likely to broaden our own culture and our sympathies. This will become education on the grandest scale ever known to history. And each racial element may well take pride in the classic past of its people while contributing along with the rest of us to the sum total of the New World's opportunity. So we correct our narrow view of western modern man by researches into the humans of Greece and Rome and Jerusalem and not only of those three pasts but also the long ages that built up modern France, Britain, Germany, Scandinavia, Russia, Italy, Egypt, China, Japan, Persia, Arabia, India and a score of other seed-beds of human endeavor. 

We have peoples among us loyally seeking to make America great and good for human kind from all those named and unnamed races and nations. Foundations such as the Scandinavian Foundation, for one example, have been provided with funds and are doing much good to make for an understanding of those cultivated peoples of northern Europe who are represented by so many thousands of loyal Americans. A hyphenate who adds to our national culture of the good and [6]

glorious things of his ancestral past and devotes himself with single citizenship to our living, present America is the most successful addition to our capital stock. Let other racial groups bring to us similar riches from the old countries and the former days and a similar determination to make America great in the finest qualities. Other groups may have gone far already in this combining of the old and the new. God's blessing upon them! They are the best Americans and the best friends of the wide world of humans who serve this new world best. 

By the same token we shall not meddle with the political life of the old countries. We are out of that and should stay away. Salvation for every one of those countries must very largely arise from within. We belong to another nation, a nation that does not seek to set one race above another, a nation that has proved to be a haven of opportunity for those who left parental shores for our land and, having left over there, should devote their energies to grateful service to the new country. This is the true and only feasible internationalism. We have all the races here and if we succeed in this blend and liberty we shall have done the whole world our best possible service: But not by meddling in their wars or their peace. Both feet must be on the shore of our choice. Let that choice be clear, final.

Democracy has different meanings for different peoples. We know the measure of our freedom and the responsibility of our liberty. School, press, radio, religion, decent utterance and a decent respect for the opinions of all who seek that liberty which we value: these must be forever free. Call it democracy if you like but do not confuse it with what is often called democracy in the old world. It is our kind of democracy and it builds men and women into a citizenship unique. Our faults are many but they are the faults of growing, learning people, not of a dying nation. They will be pointed out and they will be cleansed away. We need not boast of virtues but simply cultivate them until they are the second nature of a progressive people. If your heart is as my heart in this give me your hand. My ancestor was an immigrant from east of here; probably yours was also, what difference whether in the second or the eleventh generation! 

One of the desirable practices in a large family or society is that of discouraging back-biting. We are in effect, in America, a society of former nationalities but we are trying to form a new nation all the time. If we have a double standard of talk, one manner of speaking to a man's face and another behind his back, we are headed for a social toboggan. We have laws enough against libel. 

We do not need more laws but a higher standard of individual feeling and behavior. If my immigrant ancestors were from the north of Europe and yours from the south, or if mine were from the west of Europe and yours from the east, there will be very keen perception of your faults and a dull perception [7]

when it comes to my faults, except of course when quarrels break out among blood relatives and then the same bitter possibilities take place on a smaller scale: 'how queer the whole world is except thee and me and sometimes I think that thee is a little queer!' The point is just here, that what breaks up cordial family relations will as surely break up the national harmony if we do not adopt a higher code of fellowship. If I am too quick to see the faults of an 

Italian, or a Jew, and slow to see how I might improve myself in my social relationships, then I am in danger of being a bad citizen. Or, if I think the East of Europe is the major contributor to national decline and am slow to realize how my kind is failing in its privilege of making America grand then I am a very poor piece of white trash and unworthy of this America. 

Let there be conference and confession, exchange of notes and even of secret thoughts and let us see whether we can put into the rubbish heap all those worse traits which cause failure. Let us bring forward the best we have, in ourselves, or from our ancestry, which will be somewhere, somehow available and give of our best to the greatest blend of human desirables that the world has seen. There will be the best of law, medicine, statecraft, trade, philosophies, ideas, and mind you, practices, of present possibilities as well as historical experiences from all the ages and from all the world. Who could wish to be in a perfected and perhaps already decaying nation when we have this opportunity of being partners in a nation that is still becoming? Let us be generous in our praise and in our adoption of those sterling qualities of other racial origin and let us be keen to lay aside the vices of our own racial past. 

It will take lively imaginations, as we can see, for all Americans to be confessional of their personal defects as well as conscious of the undesirable characteristics of other Americans. One thing should be understood; we are to be frank, courteous, thoroughgoing, willing to be educated as well as willing to educate. Were such a sincere mood to overtake us all some fine morning there would be plenty of humor in the air and there would be need for excellent sportsmanship. Some one for example bursts into eloquent opinion of his Jewish neighbor and says in effect that we are plentifully supplied with knowledge of his ancestral past in Asia and that while we acknowledge great debt to the ethical and religious genius of the Hebrew and the Jew we have need to avoid the grosser evils of that past lest its virus be injected into our modern society. 

Think of the horrible wars waged in the name of a national God who was supposed to exult in the crushing of alien peoples. Think of David, how he drove harrows over the backs of his prisoners of war until he tore in shreds all semblance of humanity! "Yes," retorts the Semite whose forbears are thus pilloried, "how about your more recent military measures which were punctuated [8]

Then the Red Indian, the frontier Indian fighter, the French Voyageur, the sly legalist of the Middle Border, the Yankee trader, the share-cropper, Mexican wars, the slave block, 'The Mutiny on the Bounty," prisons south and north, not forgetting "Dartmoor," opium wars, ivory trade, and by that time the knout, the kurbash, cannibalistic rites whether in remote islands or financial districts, political chicanery, or white slavery, these and more whether read out from the first chapter of Romans, or in police courts, or smothered history, these are of a world we'd like to lessen in America.

To extenuate, to disallow, or to eradicate within this present are three methods possible and all three will be necessary. To extenuate; we shall say "but those were other days, ages of darkness, we know better now." To disallow; we can say of King David, "but he was not a Jew, no more a Jew than King Alfred was a Yankee. He was in the racial line perhaps but had not arrived." The word "Jew" is too loosely applied. Strictly, there were no Jews in the proper sense before the Babylonian Captivity and the Second Temple, that is near or in the 

Persian Period. There were Hebrews, Israelites, idolaters many of them: look up the record. Even the great monotheists as Isaiah, and Jeremiah, while in the racial line, lived long before the evolution of that ecclesiastical product which we properly call the Jew. King Charles I and George Washington may have been racially connected but only one of them was an American. One could carry these analogies interminably and disrupt much foolish historical error and at the same time help to explain much foolish politics. 

One of the chief qualifications for full humanity, for full citizenship indeed, is the capacity for assimilation. If you and I have the capacity for taking on more and more desirable characteristics, humane education in short, and for sloughing off the grossest of our unlovely traits, purification you may call it, then we qualify for citizenship in the republic. But sloughing should take place throughout the nature as well as on the surface. Those who cannot assimilate, who would set up as it were little nations within the nation are likely to be the unfit.

We allow religious freedom in this country. It is a window open to the divine sky of surprise whence we draw inspiration for a better and better humanity. 

We allow freedom of expression as "the press," education, and the ballot, for examples. It is the circulatory system of our democracy. These and all our freedoms depend on our humaneness.

What a splendid preparation it would be for all our publicists to be deeply learned in the racial backgrounds of the many peoples who constitute our [9]

citizenry! We have many business men who spend a considerable portion of their lives in foreign countries. How sympathetic they are with the cultures of those countries is an important question. Missionary families have long been among our chief interpreters of other countries to our own. Specialists in the languages, faiths, folk-lore, mental attitudes of foreign lands are with us. We might found schools of living languages such as exist in several European capitals. Our diplomatic service depends naturally, to a degree, on such familiarity with other modes of thought than western. But why do we not depend on the recently foreign, the new-comers, for such understandings? Because they are so seldom conscious of the best of their fatherlands or of the methods of organizing such knowledge even when it is implicit in their mental equipment. 

Their children are often too shy to admit the foreignness of their origins, often reluctant to keep up with the languages, the arts, sports, folk customs of their ancestors. Much of cultural value is lost through undue self-consciousness of the differences between the old and the new. Newness is so entrancing that one may seek to cast off the old fashions entirely though not able entirely to divest oneself of the physical and psychical traits of the fathers. A horde of school children is not promising ground for emphasizing the finest assembly of differences in the finest manner. The much abused politician is usually able to appraise for his political advantage the prejudices of variant groups in his constituency. We have public officials who are acquainted with the racial points of view of the voters. 

The mayor of one of our largest cities is qualified to appreciate and to set forth for good administration the best features of his Jewish and Italian fellow citizens. The proportion of Irish, French, Teutonic, even Asiatic-American which holds important public positions is worth our reckoning in this problem of understanding. These successful hyphenates give large attention to our less well digested, that is to say poorly assimilated groups of recent immigrants. With our school systems, entertainments, politics, radio, and press they work to make for greater unity of purpose. The military draft added its clumsy help in the total result. Laughter, scorn, funny stories, clubs, associations, all work their unconscious leavening effect.

It is inevitable that in a movement toward unconscious assimilation, so little appreciated for its tremendous importance, there should be huge waste. Even so, the wastage in deliberate partisan propaganda under any degree of dictatorship would be more tragic. Unless a free people is freely persuaded of the better values we have not progressed far in a right direction. is What is better and what is right? In our style of life in America that must be discovered by communities and proven by living tests. Not because a way different is it better. Not by novelty or indulgence are values approved but by [ 10 ]

An Italian may have valuable secrets, superior ways in cuisine, a Russian in the dance, an African in merriment, an Irishman in humor, a German in research, a Scandinavian in seamanship, an Arab in hospitality, an Armenian in trade, an Indian in philosophy, a Chinese in handicraft, and so on indefinitely, not to deny that experts in each of these and many other desirable skills may be sprinkled amazingly among the races, so that the dance may come to perfection in one or another of those named or unnamed. We ask from all immigrants deep sympathetic appreciation of the riches of their previous homeland and of the good liberty of their present homeland. We want no juntas here, no double-intended citizenship, but a marriage to their new love. Else are they but sojourners, not citizens truly. 

The writer would consider himself a happier, more useful man did he know the lore of many of the lands represented in our American population, or if indeed he knew a few of them and would consider himself better prepared to act constructively for the good of his patrimony. The fact that his immigrant ancestor settled here long ago makes him mindful of certain values brought along on that humble boat. But the more magnificent fact of this haven of humanity and the unexplored possibilities of the new world are reasons for a reverent patriotism. Perhaps it was an early bible training that led to an appreciation of civilization's Semitic and classical past. That and an instinct for literary values led on to the history and languages of the Christian past and finally to travel, residence, and labor in the Near East. The sufferings of Semitic peoples drew his championing concern and in the wider Semitism came the vision of the Arabs of whom we have now some hundreds of thousands in our America. Scarcely aware at first of the ground-swell of renascent Arabism which has led to calling the population of all countries where the Arabic language is spoken by the one unifying name of "Arab," yet the writer was impressed by the history, philosophy, science, and the truly majestic language of the Arabs. In the little country of Palestine as in the rest of Syria, the province of which it was a part, was a peasantry of considerable agricultural skill, passionately devoted to the land of its birth as had been its farmer ancestors for thousands of years. Before the World War there was no safer place in the world for a Jew than in Palestine. And we have plenty of evidence of the change among our own American people. 

Thousands of the more religious Jews in England and in America struggled against the secularization of their faith by the political scheme which was engineered by Balfour and Lloyd-George with their aides. These more typical Jews and the majority population of Palestine which is always Arab are against a modern invasion which would turn them into a foreign society. The Arabs have for twenty years sturdily opposed the innovators by every known method of libertyloving farmers and citizens.

Before 1915 there was no Palestine problem. There were European problems and they were thrust upon Palestine shortly after that date. One of the evils of this treatment of Palestine was to bring division here in America and a new kind of anti-Semitism over in Palestine where Europeans began to persecute the Arabs. It is necessary to go to the bottom of this Semitic question and to expose the false bottom of a materialistic and often atheistic Zionism which threatens to disrupt decencies both in Palestine and in America, not to speak of the menace to Jewry everywhere. The seeds of war are in this racialism, this blood cult, which separates between American Jews, good citizens, on the one hand, and those who would claim not to be American Jews but Jews in America.

We do not believe in a Bourbon irreconcilability to the rest of the human race.

The early Christians who were converts from Judaism sought valiantly to break with the bigotry of this inhumane treatment of humanity. Paul, the Jew and Christian, wrote the new platform of faith in his letter to the Galatian churches of Asia. Paul saved his

AN AMERICAN TO AN ENGLISHMAN 

My dear Sir Ronald Storrs: 

As said to you in Philadelphia, you have made one of the best short statements of the Arab case in Palestine that I have heard. 

It is clear that your experience, your sympathy, would indicate that you are one of those who esteem the Arab, that you believe his case to be the strongest and that he has a major right in his patrimony in Palestine. As an Englishman of culture and fair mind you care for the Arab, his rights and his happiness, as deeply as do any of us other outsiders who are not natives of Palestine. 

But in your summing up at the close of your speech in the four points of your attitude to the Palestine Problem, permit me to say, dear Sir Ronald, that you show a clearly discernible cleavage between the Englishman and the imperial Briton. Your empire interest over-topped your English sense of history and of justice and the American believes it right to controvert your conclusions. He is [22]

Taking up the four points which you as an Empire citizen hold and which as an Englishman you may deplore as much as I do, Î quote them in rough form as taken down at the moment of your utterance. 

1) That terrorism must stop and that it must be broken as with a rod of iron: 

Well, perhaps, eventually, but you by your policies are responsible for the start of terrorism and those who uncork the bottle are not always able to crowd the trouble they release back into the bottle. So perhaps you are not elected to say just when this phase of terrorism shall stop, especially if you have no other method than the rod of iron (and have you not been toying with the rod of iron in Palestine, in your dealings with your pledged allies, the Arabs?). Do you really believe that you have in these so-called Arabs, Canaanite farmers, Christian as well as Moslem, united in a peoples' movement of despair, do you think that you have one of those groups which you have sometimes broken with "rod-ofiron" methods? Have you not learned any other way to deal with a resident people? Do you think thus lightly of the possibilities of Arab and Moslem friendship? Have you not in two hundred years learned any other way? Do you at all recognize the elements of British terrorism, or the British gift of inciting to revolt and terrorism in the last twenty years?
