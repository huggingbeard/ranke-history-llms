RÉSUMÉ OF BILLS REPORTED BY THE COMMITTEE ON VETERANS' AFFAIRS, EIGHTY-FIRST CONGRESS 

During the Eighty-first Congress 619 bills and resolutions were referred to the Committee on Veterans' Affairs for consideration. 

The full committee conducted 47 hearings and subcommittees held 17 hearings (resulting in 2,355 pages of printed testimony); 34 executive sessions were convened. A total of 30 House bills and 14 Senate bills were reported favorably to the House. Of this number, 23 are now public law and 1 became a private law, 19 have passed the House and are pending in the Senate, 1 remains on the House Calendar for consideration, 1 was recommitted, and 2 were vetoed. Of the 2 vetoed, 1 was passed over the veto and enacted into law. 

During this Congress a new system of numbering the pages of hearings in cumulative fashion was initiated so as to permit compilation of a comprehensive index at the end of each session and at the end of the Congress. In this manner those working in the field will be able to have a general index, subject and individual, to all the hearings of this committee.

Also inaugurated was the policy of supplying the Members of both the House and Senate with letters, prepared by the staff advising the provisions and status of pending legislation and other matters pertinent to the field of veterans' affairs. Several such letters were distributed, on subjects involving insurance dividends, the War Claims Act, compensation and pension, as well as education and training under the Servicemen's Readjustment Act. 

One of the most important bills to be passed was H. R. 9911 which provides for $10,000 gratuitous indemnity to the survivors of all men killed in the Armed Forces after June 27, 1950, without cost to the servicemen. It has been estimated that such a program would have saved several hundred millions of dollars in comparison with the national service life insurance program. 

The service-connected compensation proposal, Public Law 339, enacted a new disability compensation schedule based on $150 for total disability. This represents an increase of 8.7 percent over the former compensation schedule. 

The committee also adopted the following resolution:

The staff of the committee is directed, during the recess of the Congress, to consult with the Veterans' Administration, the Department of Defense, the General Accounting Office, the Bureau of the Budget, and any other interested Government agency, and the four service organizations chartered by the Congress, with a view to presenting to the Committee on Veterans' Affairs in the Eighty-second Congress, recommendations for a new law providing insurance protection to members of the Armed Forces and veterans of such forces; the staff to utilize, to whatever extent practicable, previous surveys or investigations made on this subject. 

J. E. RANKIN,

Chairman, Committee on Veterans' Affairs, House of Representatives.

Public Law 339. Veterans' omnibus benefits for service-connected disabled veterans. A sectional analysis follows: 

Section 1: Restores to World War I statutory presump tive cases full compensation of 100 percent in lieu of the present 75 percent. Original law provided for any veteran contracting disability prior to January 1, 1925, would be presumed to have incurred the disability in the service. 

Subsequent laws reduced the amount of compensation for this group to 75 percent of the rates prevailing for other disabled veterans.

Section 2: Present law permits compensation for arrested cases of tuberculosis (service-connected) at the following rates: Total up to 2 years; 50 percent for balance of 5 years; and 30 percent for further period of 5 years.

This section makes mandatory total rating for 2 years and increases 50 percent rate period to 4 years.

Section 3: Subsection (a) establishes a new disability payment schedule based on $150 for total disability. 

Thus a veteran with 50 percent disability would be entitled to $75 per month. The increase is approximately an 8.7 percent over the former schedule of $138 for total disability. Peacetime veterans receive 80 percent of the wartime rates.

Subsection (b) increases rate for widow and one child from present $100 to $105 and raises the rate for each additional child from $15 to $25. 

Section 4: Dependency Allowance Act provided that for those veterans 60 percent or more disabled there shall be paid a proportionate additional allowance for dependents; this law reduces the requirement to 50 percent. For example, a veteran 100 percent disabled and having a wife and one child receives $35 additional compensation. 

A veteran 50 percent disabled with wife and child would under this section receive $17.50 additional-50 percent of the total disability rate.

Section 5: Liberalizes line-of-duty requirements with respect to a service person whose disease, injury, or death was incurred without willful misconduct while in confinement under sentence of court martial or civil court, unless sentence involved an unremitted dishonorable discharge or the offense for which convicted involved a felony. Under prior law disability incurred while in confinement under sentence of court martial or civil court was held not to have been incurred in line of duty and both the veteran and dependents were barred from compensation. (Hearings May 17, 18, 19, 24, 25, June 7, 8, 9, 15, 16, 21; H. 

R. 5598 reported July 14; H. Rept. No. 1063; passed House 

Aug. 2; reported in Senate September 23; S. Rept. 1103; passed Senate September 27; approved October 10, 1949.)

Provides pensions for World Wars I and II veterans. 

A sec- 

tional analysis follows: 

Section 1: Any veteran meeting service requirements and income limits less than 55 years of age who has a single disability of 60 percent or more, or two or more disabilities, one of which is 40 percent or more, making a combined rating of 70 percent or more, and who is unemployable, shall be entitled to $60 a month pension. The same rate applies to a man aged 55 who has disability of 60 percent or more, single or combined, and upon reaching age 60, the disability requirement is reduced to 50 percent. (NOTE. 

The benefits applying below age 65 are now provided by VA regulation known as extension 5 to the 1945 Disability 

Rating Schedule.) At age 65, the veteran would be presumed to be permanently and totally disabled without a medical examination, and regardless of unemployability, would receive a pension of $72 per month. 

Section 2: Provides rate of $100 in lieu of the $60 and $72 rates mentioned above for those veterans who are so helpless or blind as to need the regular aid and attendance of another person.

Section 3: Raises the annual income limitation for a veteran without dependents from the present $1,000 to $1,200. The $2,500 limit for veterans who have dependents is left unchanged.

Section 4: Raises the annual income limitation for widows without children from the present $1,000 to $1,200. 

The $2,500 limitation for widows who have children is left unchanged.

BILL REPORTED, PENDING ON HOUSE CALENDAR H. R. 8235_____ Contains 10 sections of amendments to the National Service Life Insurance Act of 1940. An explanation of each section follows: 

Section 1 authorizes waiver of health requirement as to compensable disability in application for insurance or reinstatement of insurance made prior to July 1, 1951 (present provision expired January 1, 1950, and requires disability must result from performance of active military or naval service).

Sections 2 and 3 extend the time for applying for continuance of $5,000 gratuitous insurance in certain cases to July 1, 1951.

Section 4 permits the commuted value of insurance maturing prior to August 1, 1946, and remaining unpaid at the death of the last person within the permitted class of beneficiaries, to be paid to the estate of the insured. 

Section 5 provides that in any case where the insured provided for the payment of premiums by deductions from his service pay or where premiums were paid by the Government, such insurance shall not lapse or be forfeited so long as the insured remained in active service prior to August 1, 1946. This would be true despite the fact that such deductions may not have been deducted because (1) the insured was discharged to accept a commission, or (2) the insured was absent without leave but restored to active duty, (3) the insured was sentenced by court martial and if he was restored to active duty or required to engage in combat or became totally disabled or was killed in combat. Premiums in such cases would be deducted from the proceeds of insurance.

Section 6 provides for application for waiver of premium for total disability on any policy due after October 8, 1940, and prior to date of enactment of this bill; premiums paid during the time in which the waiver is effective shall be applied to payment of subsequent premiums, waiver of premium to cease upon notice sent by registered mail by the Veterans' Administration after the insured ceases to be totally disabled; in the event of the death of the insured without filing application for waiver of premium, beneficiaries may file application for waiver of premiums which were due after date of the death of insured; permits waiver
