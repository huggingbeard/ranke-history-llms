Subject: Hearing, January 19, 10:30 a. m., on complaint of Pine 

Bluffs Rural Electric Co., Wyoming and Nebraska, re proposed contract with Department of the Interior. 

Senator Joseph C. O'Mahoney, Wyoming, has brought to my attention correspondence with the Department of the Interior relative to its rejection of certain features of a proposed contract with the Pine Bluff Rural Electric Co., a cooperative group and preference customer for power under the reclamation law. The Pine Bluffs organization operates in Laramie County, Wyo., and Kimball County, Nebr.

At Senator O'Mahoney's request, I am calling a hearing on the complaint for Thursday, January 19, 1956, at 10:30 a. m. in room 

224, Senate Office Building. In the event I am unable to be present and Senator Anderson, chairman of the Subcommittee on Irrigation and Reclamation, is otherwise engaged, I shall ask Senator O'Mahoney to preside as acting chairman. 

The proposed contract, it appears from the correspondence, was negotiated in the field by representatives of the Bureau of Reclamation, with the Pine Bluffs cooperative. The draft was accepted by the board of directors of the organization and recommended to the Department by the Casper (Wyo.) projects manager and the regional office of the Bureau of Reclamation at Denver, I am informed. 

The clauses of the proposed contract rejected by the Department of the Interior in Washington related to the lease of transformation equipment to the Pine Bluffs company. The equipment was purchased by Reclamation some years ago for another installation, but now appears to be lying idle at Casper, Wyo. 

In his letter of October 17, 1955, to Assistant Interior Secretary 

Fred G. Aandahl, Senator O'Mahoney stated that by the rejection of the recommendation to lease the transformation equipment "the Department of the Interior at Washington is putting itself in the position of making it more difficult for the preferred customer to utilize the power to which it is entitled," etc.

Notwithstanding the representations made by Senator O'Mahoney on behalf of the cooperative, and by spokesmen for the Pine Bluff organization in person, the Department adheres to its position of rejecting the field recommendations. 851035 1

Assistant Secretary Fred G. Aandahl and any members of the Department or Reclamation staffs who may throw light on the situation have also been invited. 

I mentioned in my letter of January 9 to Senator O'Mahoney that there was a resemblance between the manner in which the Pine Bluffs power contract was handled and the repayment-construction contract problem on the Helena Valley unit in Montana. In each case recognized representatives, engineers, of the Bureau of Reclamation negotiated contracts which were accepted by the affected local organization. When each of the contracts came to Washington, essential features were rejected by the Department. In the Helena 

Valley case, the draft of a repayment contract has been "approved" by the Commissioner of Reclamation for use in the formation of an irrigation district.

I recognize, of course, that the final responsibility for the approval of power and irrigation contracts rests in the Secretary of the Interior. 

However, we had been told in effect that the present administration intended to place more authority and responsibility in the field engineers of the Bureau of Reclamation in order to bring the reclamation program closer to the people. 

In any event, the manner in which the Pine Bluffs and Helena Valley contract negotiations on essential points have been rejected in Washington is disturbing to the affected organizations. Its effect on the reclamation program and the morale of the Bureau's engineers and other responsible officials in the field who must continue to deal with the people at the grassroots certainly should give concern.

DEAR SECRETARY AANDAHL: I find from conference with officials of Pine Bluffs REA Cooperative that on September 6 representatives of the Bureau of Reclamation held conference with Pine Bluffs manager and board of directors to discuss new contract to replace existing contracts Nos. 181r-1229, 181r-1239, and 181r-1269. These contracts were to be combined into a single contract under which the Reclamation Bureau would provide a new circuit for the use of the cooperative at the Pine Bluffs delivery point. The contract was to increase the rate of delivery of firm power to 4,000 kilowatts from 

3,500 now being provided. 

Pine Bluffs Rural Electric Co. needs this additional power to stabilize its service at Kimball and elsewhere, solely for the use of cooperative members. Increased power may be utilized only through the installation of special facilities at the Bureau of Reclamation substation now existing at Pine Bluffs. These facilities are presently the property of the Government and are stored without present use with the Bureau of Reclamation at Casper, so there would be involved no additional purchase of facilities by the Government. A provision was made in a proposed contract drafted by the Bureau of Reclamation personnel at Casper and approved by the Bureau of Reclamation personnel at Denver. It was accepted by the board of directors and the manager of the Pine Bluffs Cooperative. The new contract, that they are after, was submitted to Washington for final approval. 

It is entitled: "Projects Office Draft 8-22-55, United States Bureau of Interior, Bureau of Reclamation, Missouri River Basin Project, Western Division, Contract for Electric Service to Rural Electric 

Company."

It contains the provisions for the furnishing of the necessary special facilities by the United States under an agreement that it shall remain the property of the United States, but that the Pine Bluffs Cooperative will pay rent therefor. These terms are set out in paragraphs 

9 and 10.

Under date of October 13, R. M. Sensintaffer, Chief of the Power Division of the Bureau of Reclamation at Casper, Wyo., transmitted to Mr. B. E. Lyons, manager of the Pine Bluffs Rural Electric Co.,

LETTER FROM ASSISTANT SECRETARY AANDAHL TO SENATOR O'MAHONEY DEPARTMENT OF THE INTERIOR, OFFICE OF THE SECRETARY, 

Washington 25, D. C., November 9, 1955.

Hon. JOSEPH C. O'MAHONEY, 

United States Senate, Washington 25, D. C. 

MY DEAR SENATOR O'MAHONEY: This is in reply to your letter dated October 17, 1955, on the proposed new circuit for the rural electric company, Pine Bluffs, Wyo., under the proposed contract between the company and the Bureau of Reclamation, and our recent telephone conversation.

We have been informed by the Bureau that Rural Electric Co. is presently served through three contracts for power, each contract providing for a definite quantity of power at a specific delivery point. 

Also, each delivery point is at a different voltage. The proposed contract is a consolidating contract covering the company's total power requirements. It provides for delivery of variable portions of the power, together with some increase at the same delivery points and at the same voltages as heretofore. 

In effecting delivery of power at Pine Bluffs, the Bureau has a 6,000 kilovolt-ampere capacity substation stepping down transmission voltage from 115 kilovolts to 12.47 kilovolts. The company has requested that the Bureau provide additional transformation facilities to step up voltage from 12.47 kilovolts to 33 kilovolts for a portion of the Pine Bluffs' delivery, in order to permit the company to interconnect for emergency purposes its Pine Bluffs' system with that portion of its system which receives power at 33 kilovolts from the Bureau's Sidney substation. 

The Bureau has made a major investment in substation facilities at Pine Bluffs to provide service to the company and its present capacity of 6,000 kilovolt-amperes at 12.47 kilovolts is more than sufficient to deliver the power desired by the company at that point. 

While there is no question as to the advantage to the company of supporting the end of its 33-kilovolt system which is energized from Sidney, the investment required should be made by the company and not the Bureau.

The request for the delivery at Pine Bluffs of a part of the customer's power at a different voltage comes about because the company desires to overcome adverse service conditions that have arisen on its own system. The Bureau undertakes, at the time service is initiated, to provide whatever facilities may be required and are financially justified to meet the customer's then existing system conditions. On the other hand, it is a Bureau policy of many years' standing that major changes requiring substantial equipment installations arising after service has been established which result from changing conditions on the customer's system are a customer responsibility. To adopt a different policy would involve the United States in the necessity of constantly changing its equipment installations as conditions upon its customers' systems undergo modifications. The fact that in this instance there is on hand equipment which could be utilized by the Bureau to effect the desired modification and that

PINE BLUFFS RURAL ELECTRIC CO., WYOMING, ETC. 

the customer would compensate the United States therefor does not justify a departure from this policy. While there may be no present need for the equipment on the Bureau's part, in the event such need arises in the future, it would then become necessary for the Bureau to procure additional equipment as the result of its having undertaken to accommodate the company. Moreover, whether the accommodation be in the form of the use of equipment already on hand or the acquisition of new equipment, in either event the net effect would be the equivalent of the United States having made a loan of capital funds in order for the customer to meet conditions arising upon its system after initial service.

You have raised the question of comparability of Rural's request with the Bureau's plan of installing presently available substation equipment to provide service to the city of Chappell, Nebr. These situations are in no wise comparable. Chappell is now initially contracting for power and the Bureau proposed to purchase a permanent substation for this delivery. However, the city, due to a present power emergency, desires immediate service and is paying to the Bureau an amount to cover the up and down expense of a temporary substation. The temporary substation as well as the permanent substation when installed will provide only one delivery voltage to this customer.

We regret that we cannot accede to Rural's request for the Bureau to provide, either as an investment or on a repayable loan basis, the equipment useful in overcoming a limitation within its own distribution system. As we have indicated above, such required investment should be the responsibility of the Rural Electric Co. 

We appreciate the opportunity to furnish you this information. 

Sincerely yours,

HELENA VALLEY CORRESPONDENCE LETTER FROM CHAIRMAN MURRAY TO ASSISTANT SECRETARY 

AANDAHL 

JANUARY 10, 1956. 

Hon. FRED G. AANDAHL, 

Assistant Secretary of the Interior, Department of the Interior, Washington, D. C. 

DEAR GOVERNOR AANDAHL: Please refer to your letter to me of

September 23, 1955, relating to the Helena Valley Irrigation District repayment and construction program. To that letter you attached a request to Senator Hayden, chairman of the Senate Appropriations Committee, and the chairman of the House Appropriations Committee, asking their counsel and guidance "in this matter."

On September 29, Senator O'Mahoney, acting chairman of the Senate Committee on Interior and Insular Affairs, held a hearing on the subject. The Department was officially represented by your then assistant, Edward Frye, and Floyd E. Dominy, of the Bureau of 

Reclamation. 

A full disclosure of all the facts and circumstances, together with correspondence, was incorporated in the transcript of the proceedings or in a committee print preceding the hearing. I have made a careful review of the transcript. Department spokesmen relied principally on statements made before the Senate Appropriations Committee as justification for the position of Interior with respect to requiring additional repayment contract coverage before beginning construction of the Helena Valley unit. The record showed that the Commissioner of Reclamation had "approved" a draft of a repayment contract for use in the formation of an irrigation district as the Congress had been advised formally in the budget justifications, the Helena Valley district had agreed to the terms, after negotiations conducted by reclamation engineers in Montana; a Montana court had, largely on the basis of the contract draft, entered an order forming the irrigation district; and there was every reason for all concerned to rely on the authenticity of the procedure, as Judge Fall has written to me. 

Senator Hayden, in his letter to you of October 7, 1955, states unequivocally that the Bureau should proceed with construction upon the execution of the contract referred to in the justification presented to the committee in support of the budget estimate. For ready reference I am attaching a copy of Senator Hayden's letter to you, and a copy of his letter of that date to me. 

Although more than 3 months have elapsed, I understand you have received no comment from Chairman Cannon of the House Appropriations Committee to your letter of September 23. The House situation with respect to Helena Valley construction funds was as follows: 

The House Appropriations Committee reported the public works appropriation bill to the floor with the statement in its report that "additional funds (for the Helena Valley unit) denied in view of the fact that a repayment contract has not yet been executed and that approximately 86 percent of the project repayment must come from power revenues of the Missouri Basin project." When the bill came before the Committee of the Whole of the House, the budget estimates of construction funds for the Bureau of Reclamation were restored in full, including $3 million for the Helena Valley unit. The vote was 103 to 70.

While I am not authorized to speak for him, it is my understanding that Chairman Cannon takes the position that the House of Representatives voted funds for the Helena Valley unit notwithstanding the adverse recommendation of its Appropriations Committee on this item. The Senate concurred in the action and the President signed the bill into law. The Department, therefore, would appear to have its instructions from the Congress. 

The availability of funds to begin construction of the Helena Valley unit was cleared by the Bureau of the Budget in approving the apportionments recommended by your Department for fiscal year 1956.

Under the circumstances I have outlined and, with especial reference to Senator Hayden's letters to you and to me, I respectfully recommend that the repayment contract, as originally negotiated and "approved" by Commissioner Dexheimer, should be executed promptly.

Advertisements for construction bids should be expedited so as to get construction underway promptly. Negotiations with the city of Helena for a supplemental water supply from the Helena Valley system and with landowners for supplemental irrigation supplies should, of course, be pressed to increase the repayment coverage as 

Senator Hayden recommends. 

Senator Mansfield and Representative Metcalf concur in this letter.

Please acknowledge receipt of this recommendation and advise as promptly as practicable of the Department's course. Sincerely,

JAMES E. MURRAY, Chairman.

Assistant Secretary of the Interior, Department of the Interior, Washington, D. C.

MY DEAR MR. SECRETARY: Reference is made to your letter of 

September 23, 1955, in which you requested my advice on the question that has arisen with respect to repayment coverage on the Helena Valley unit, Montana, of the Missouri River Basin project. 

I have reviewed the history of the appropriation of funds for the initiation of construction of the unit in the Supplemental Appropriation Act of 1955, and for the continuation of the unit in the Public Works Appropriation Act, 1956. It is my opinion that the budget estimate of $3 million for this unit was approved in the Public Works Appropriation Act, 1956, with the understanding that prior to construction a repayment contract would be executed with the irrigation district in the process of being formed to provide for a repayment of approximately $800,000. That a contract to provide water for the supplemental lands would be considered at a later date. 

Therefore, it would seem to me that the Bureau should proceed. with construction upon the execution of the contract referred to in the justification presented to the committee in support of the budget estimate. However, the Bureau should make all proper efforts to obtain additional repayment contracts so as to provide for a minimum repayment coverage of 12 percent on the unit. 

Very sincerely yours,

Chairman.
