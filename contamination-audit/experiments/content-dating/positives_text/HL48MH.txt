OF HUSBAND AND WIFE RCC 86: Marriage as a civil contract 

The law considers marriage in no other view than as a civil contract.

RCC 87: Regulation by law The laws prescribe: 

1. The manner of contracting and celebrating marriages; 

2. The legal effects and consequences of marriage; 

3. The manner in which marriages may be dissolved. 

RCC 88: Validity of marriage 

Such marriages only are recognized by law as are contracted and solemnized according to the rules which it prescribes.

RCC 89: Duration of marriage-Dissolution for causes other than death

Marriage is a contract intended in its origin to endure until the death of one of the contracting parties; yet this contract may be dissolved before the decease of either of the married persons for causes determined by law. 

HOW MARRIAGES MAY BE CONTRACTED OR MADE RCC 90: Essential requisites of marriage 

As the law considers marriage in no other view than that of a civil contract, it sanctions all those marriages where the parties, at the time of making them, were: 

1. Willing to contract; 

2. Able to contract; 

3. Did contract pursuant to the forms and solemnities prescribed by law. LO

RCC 91: Vices of consent

No marriage is valid to which the parties have not freely consented. 

Consent is not free: 

1. When given to a ravisher, unless it has been given by the party ravished, after she has been restored to the enjoyment of liberty; 

2. When it is extorted by violence; 

3. When there is a mistake respecting the person, whom one of the parties intended to marry. 

RCC 92: Minimum ages-Penalty for celebrating marriage of parties under age

Ministers of the gospel and magistrates, entrusted with the power of celebrating marriages, are prohibited to marry any male under the age of eighteen years, and any female under the age of sixteen, and if any of them are convicted of having married such persons, he shall be removed from his office, if a magistrate, or deprived forever of the right of celebrating marriage, if a minister of the gospel. 

Provided, that this act shall not apply, when on application of either of the parties to a proposed marriage, any district judge, except, in the Parish of Orleans, any judge of the First City Court of the City of New Orleans, may upon satisfactory evidence being presented to him, in case of extraordinary circumstances when the parents or guardians of the parties to the proposed marriage give their consent, provided, however, that said evidence shall be presented in chambers, and provided also, that said order shall not contain the reasons for authorizing said marriage, and the application shall not be a matter of record but the order shall be attached to and made part of the marriage certificate. (As amended Acts 1954, No. 398.)

RCC 93: Impediment of an existing marriage

Persons legally married are, until a dissolution of marriage, incapable of contracting another, under the penalties established by the laws of this State. 

RCC 94: Impediments of direct line relationship-Mis cegenation

Marriage between persons related to each other in the direct ascending or descending line is prohibited. This prohibition is not confined to legitimate children, it extends also to children born out of marriage. Marriage between white persons and persons of color is prohibited and the celebration of all such marriages is forbidden and such celebration carries with it no effect and is null and void. (As amended Acts 1894, No. 54.)

NOTE: Article 79 of the Louisiana Criminal Code defines as criminal "the marriage or habitual cohabitation, with knowledge of their differences in race, between a person of the Caucasian or white race and a person of the colored or negro race.”

R.S. 9:201: Marriage between Indians and colored per sons prohibited Marriage between persons of the Indian race and persons of the colored and black race is prohibited, and the celebration of all such marriages is forbidden and such celebration carries with it no effect, and is null and void. 

RCC 95: Impediments of collateral relationship-Mar riage out of State-Affidavit concerning relationship

Among collateral relations, marriage is prohibited between brother and sister, whether of the whole or of the half blood, whether legitimate or illegitimate, between uncle and niece, between aunt and nephew, and also between first cousins.

That no marriage contracted in contravention of the above provisions in another State by citizens of this State, without first having acquired a domicile out of this State, shall have any legal effect in this State. 

No officer whose duty it is to issue a marriage license shall do so until he shall have received an affidavit from one of the parties to be married to the effect that he or she is not related to the other party within the degree prohibited hereinbefore. (As amended Acts 1900, No. 120; Acts 1902, No. 9.)

RCC 96: Other impediments abolished 

All other impediments on account of relationship or affinity are abolisted. 

RCC 97: Parental consent for marriage of minor

The minor of either sex, who has attained the competent age to marry, must have received the consent of his father and mother or of the survivor of them; and if they are both dead, the consent of his tutor. 

He must furnish proof of this consent to the officer to whom he applies for permission to marry.

RCC 98: Proof of majority for issuance of license 

Those who have attained the age of majority, on their demanding permission to marry, must furnish the officer proof of their having attained that age.

RCC 99: Authority to issue marriage license-Minimum delay before celebration

License to celebrate marriage in the Parish of Orleans shall be granted by the Board of Health and Judges of the City Courts in and for said Parish, and in other Parishes

of the State by Clerks of the Courts, unless the Clerk himself should be a party to the marriage, when the license shall be granted by the District Judge. 

All marriage licenses shall be issued in duplicate. 

No marriage shall be performed by any officer or minister of any religious sect presently empowered by law to perform marriage ceremonies in this State before Seventytwo (72) hours have elapsed between the time of the issuance of the license and the proposed ceremony, except when in the opinion of the officer charged with the performance of the marriage there is serious and grave reason for performing the marriage within a shorter period of time than seventy-two hours after the issuance of the license, in which cases such officer may perform said ceremony within said period of less than seventy-two hours from the issuance of the license upon first obtaining the written consent of the Board of Health for the Parish of Orleans, or of the Clerk of the District Court for all Parishes of the State other than the Parish of Orleans, which written consent shall be retained as a part of his public records by the officiating officer or minister of any religious sect, and by the license issuing authority. 

Upon violation of the provisions of this article, the authority of any minister of religion, or public officer granted power by the State to perform marriages, shall be revoked for a period of one year upon the complaint of any person that said marriage has been so performed, when said complaint is made to the Board of Health of the Parish of Orleans, or the Clerk of Court of any of the other Parishes of the State. Acts 1948, No. 312, Sec. 1.)

RCC 100: Place of issuance of license 

Licenses for marriage can only be granted by the person authorized to issue the same in the parish in which one at least of the parties is domiciliated.

R.S. 9:245: False statements in certificate-Disclosure of facts pertaining to examination-Penalties

Any physician who knowingly and wilfully makes any false statements in the certificate required in R.S. 9:241, and any person who discloses any facts relating or pertaining to the medical examination provided for in R.S. 9:242 except as it may be otherwise permitted by law, shall be fined not more than one hundred dollars or imprisoned for not more than six months.

R.S. 9:261: Marriage licenses-Reports to be filed with board of health

On or before the tenth day of every month, all officers authorized to issue marriage licenses in this state shall file a report with the secretary of the state board of health, upon suitable blanks to be provided by the board, showing the number of marriage licenses issued by them during the preceding calendar month. These statements shall specify in each case: (1) The date of the license; (2) The full name, residence, race, ages and occupation of each party; (3) The names of the parents of each party, and whether dead or alive, and their residence, if alive; (4) The number of former marriages of each party with the name of the spouse, whether dead or alive, and whether divorced or not; and (5) The relationship of each party to each other. 

R.S. 9:262: Duplicate records of marriage licenses is sued-Preservation-Filing of duplicate copy with state board of health-Penalty for failure to file

Each officer authorized to issue marriage licenses in this state shall keep a duplicate record of all marriage 13

licenses issued, on which they shall note the date and place of the marriage, and the name of the person who performed the ceremony.

One copy thereof shall be kept in a loose-leaf book until it has been filled, at which time it shall be permanently bound, and shall be kept open to the inspection of the public during office hours.

The other copy shall be filed with the secretary of the state board of health within ten days of the expiration of each month, and the failure, neglect, or refusal to do so shall be punished by a fine of not less than ten dollars nor more than fifty dollars.

R.S. 9:263: Tabualtion of marriage statistics—Annual report of Secretary of Board of Health 

The Secretary of the state board of health shall annually prepare, from the information filed with him under the provisions of R.S. 9:261 and 9:262, abstracts and tabu lar statements of the facts relating to marriages in each parish, and embody them with the necessary analysis, in his annual report to the state.

R.S. 40:317: Marriage Certificate-Compulsory regis tration of marriages

The marriage certificate is the record prepared for every marriage on a form approved by the state board of health. It shall contain the information prescribed by the board. On the face of the certificate shall appear the certification to the fact of marriage, signed by the parties to the marriage and by the witnesses, and the signature and title of the officiant. 

Every officiant of a marriage ceremony performed in this state shall sign a certificate of marriage in triplicate. 15

R.S. 40:318: Disposition and recordation of marriage certificates

The officiant shall give one copy of the marriage certificate to the parties married. Within ten days after the ceremony, he shall file the other two copies of the certificate of marriage with the officer who issued the marriage license. 

Upon receipt of these copies, this officer shall sign them with his signature and title and shall note thereon the date the certificate was recorded by him.

He shall forward to the state registrar, on or before the fifteenth day of each calendar month, one copy of each certificate of marriage filed with him during the preceding calendar month.

R.S. 40:319: Recording fee to be paid officer issuing marriage licenses 

Every officer authorized to issue marriage licenses shall receive a fee of fifty cents for every marriage certificate filed with him and forwarded by him to the state registrar. 

The fee shall be paid by the applicant for a marriage license and shall be collected together with the statutory fee for the license.

R.S. 40:255: Registration of marriage 

Every marriage celebrated in the parish of Orleans shall be recorded in the office of the local registrar. 

R.S. 40:256: Return of endorsed license to registrar

The person celebrating the marriage shall return the license authorizing the marriage to the local registrar after having endorsed thereon the fact and date of the celebration of the marriage and the registrar shall preserve it in his records. 16

R.S. 40:257: Marriage certificate-Contents 

The marriage certificate shall contain, as a minimum, the following items: 

(1) The names of the contracting parties prior to the marriage. (2) Their age. (3) Their nativity. (4) The names of the father and mother of each or of their tutors, if any. (5) The date of their license. (6) By whom the license was issued. (7) The name of the person celebrating the marriage. (8) The date of its celebration. (9) The names of the witnesses to the celebration. (10) The official signature of the local registrar and the date of filing of the certificate in his office. 

R.S. 40:258: Fee for registration of marriage certificate

The local registrar shall charge one dollar for recording a marriage in his office.

RCC 102: Ministers and priests right to solemnize

Any minister of the gospel, or priest of any religious sect, who having been first registered with the Clerk of the District Court for the Parishes in which they are officiating, as an authorized person to perform marriage ceremonies, whether a citizen of the United States or not, may celebrate marriages within this State, upon complying with the regulations of the law. R.S. 9:202: Powers of judges of districts and city courts 

Judges of district courts and of city courts may celebrate marriages within their respective jurisdictions. 

RCC 103: Authority to celebrate marriage—Judges and justices of the peace Parish judges and justices of the peace may celebrate marriages within their respective parishes, upon complying with the regulations of the law. 

RCC 104: License to celebrate marriage

No minister of the gospel, or other person, shall celebrate any marriage in this State, unless he shall have obtained previously a special license to him directed, issued by the person appointed by law to grant licenses in the parish. wherein the marriage is to be celebrated, authorizing him to celebrate such marriage.

RCC 105: Formalities-Witnesses 

The marriage must be celebrated in presence of three witnesses of full age, and an act must be made of the celebration, signed by the person who celebrates the marriage, by the parties and the witnesses. 

This act must be made in duplicate and appended to the license issued in duplicate; one of these acts, appended to the license must be returned within thirty days from the date of celebration by the person celebrating the marriage to the person who granted the license, who shall file and record the same in his office.

RCC 106: Opposition to marriage 

In case of an opposition to the marriage, if it be supported by the oath of the party making it, and by reason sufficient in the opinion of the judge to authorize a suspension of the marriage, it shall be notified to the parties, and a day shall be assigned for a hearing thereon. 18

RCC 107: Hearing on opposition 

The time fixed for the hearing of the parties and the decision on the opposition, shall not exceed ten days, from the day on which the opposition shall have been made. 

RCC 108: Persons entitled to make opposition 

Any person may make opposition to a marriage, but if the opposition be overruled, the party making it shall pay costs.

RCC 109: Marriage by procuration 

No marriage can be contracted or celebrated by procuration.

OF THE NULLITY OF MARRIAGES RCC 110: Forced consent- -Mistake in person 

Marriages celebrated without the free consent of the married persons, or of one of them, can only be annulled upon application of both the parties, or of that one of them whose consent was not free.

When there has been a mistake in the person, the party laboring under the mistake can alone impeach the marriage. 

RCC 111: Cohabitation after recovery of freedom or discovery of mistake

In the cases embraced by the preceding article, the application to obtain a sentence annulling the marriage, is inadmissible if the married persons have, freely and without constraint, cohabited together after recovering their liberty or discovering the mistake. 

RCC 112: Minor's marriage without parental consent

The marriage of minors, contracted without the consent of the father and mother, can not for that cause be annulled, if it is otherwise contracted with the for- 19

malities prescribed by law; but such want of consent shall be a good cause for the father and mother to disinherit their children thus married, if they think proper. 

RCC 113: Action of nullity-Marriages celebrated out of state

Every marriage contracted under the other incapacities or nullities enumerated in the second chapter of this title may be impeached either by the married persons themselves, or by any person interested, or by the Attorney General; however, first, that marriages heretofore contracted between persons related within the prohibited degree either or both of whom were then and afterwards domiciled in this State, and were prohibited from intermarrying here, shall nevertheless be deemed valid in this State, where such marriages were celebrated in other states or countries under the laws of which they were not prohibited; second, that marriages hereafter contracted between persons either or both of whom were domiciled in this State and are forbidden to intermarry shall not be deemed valid in this State, because contracted in another State or country where such marriages are not prohibited, if the parties after such marriage, return to reside permanently in this State. (As amended, Acts 1904, No. 129; Acts 

1912, No. 54; Acts 1938, No. 426; Acts 1950, No. 242.) 

RCC 114: Parties entitled to bring action of nullity

But in all cases where, conformably to the preceding article, the action of nullity may be instituted by any person having a pecuniary interest, it cannot be brought during the life of the two married persons by the collateral relations or by the children born of a previous marriage, until they have acquired an actual interest. 

RCC 115: Ancient causes for nullity abolished 

The other causes of nullity which existed by the ancient laws are abolished. 20

RCC 116: Annulment of bigamous marriage

A married person to whose prejudice a second marriage has been contracted can sue for the nullity of such marriage, even during the life of the other party with whom he or she had contracted the first marriage. In case the second marriage has been contracted in this State and the defendant has left the State, an attorney shall be appointed by the court to represent the absent defendant. (As amended, Acts 

1906, No. 150.)

R.S. 14:76: Bigamy 

Bigamy is the marriage to another person by a person already married and having a husband or wife living; or the habitual co-habitation, in this state, with such second husband or wife, regardless of the place where the marriage was celebrated.

The provisions of this article shall not extend: (1) To any person whose former husband or wife has been absent, at the time of the second marriage, for five successive years without being known to such person, within that time, to be living; or (2) To any person whose former marriage has been annulled or dissolved at the time of the second marriage, by the judgment of a competent court; or (3) To any person who has, at the time of the second marriage, a reasonable and honest belief that his or her former husband or wife is dead, or that a valid divorce or annulment has been secured, or that his or her former marriage was invalid. 

Whoever commits the crime of bigamy shall be fined not more than one thousand dollars, or imprisoned, with or without hard labor, for not more than five years or both. 

R.S. 14:77: Abetting in bigamy 

Abetting in bigamy is the marriage of an unmarried person to the husband or wife of another, with knowledge 21

of the fact that the party is married and without a reasonable and honest belief that such party is divorced or his marriage annulled, or that the party's husband or wife is dead.

Whoever commits the crime of abetting in bigamy shall be fined not more than one thousand dollars, or imprisoned, with or without hard labor, for not more than five years, or both.

UNIFORM MARRIAGE EVASION LAW R.S. 9:221: Going outside state to contract marriage prohibited in state

If any person residing and intending to continue to reside in this state who is disabled or prohibited from contracting marriage under the laws of this state goes outside this state and there contracts a marriage prohibited and declared void by the laws of this state, the marriage is null and void for all purposes in this state with the same effect as though it had been entered into in this state. 

R.S. 9:222: 

Non-resident entering state to contract marriage which would be void in state of residence

No marriage may be contracted in this state by a party residing and intending to continue to reside in another state or jurisdiction, if the marriage would be void if contracted in the other state or jurisdiction. Every marriage celebrated in this state in violation of this Section is null and void. 

R.S. 9:223: Licensing officer to satisfy himself nonresident is not prohibited from marrying

Before issuing a marriage license to a person who resides and intends to continue to reside in another state the officer having authority to issue the license shall satisfy 22

himself by requiring affidavits or otherwise that the person is not prohibited from intermarrying by the laws of the jurisdiction where he or she resides.

R.S. 9:224: Issuance of license or celebration of mar riage knowing marriage to be prohib ited-Penalty 

Any officer who issues a license with the knowledge that the persons are disabled or prohibited from intermarrying and any person who knowingly celebrates such marriage is guilty of a misdemeanor and shall be punished by fine or imprisonment, or both. 

RCC 117: Civil effect of putative marriage

The marriage, which has been declared null, produces nevertheless its civil effects as it relates to the parties and their children, if it has been contracted in good faith. 

RCC 118: Persons entitled to civil effects of putative marriage

If only one of the parties acted in good faith, the marriage produces its civil effects only in his or her favor and in favor of the children born of the marriage. 

OF THE RESPECTIVE RIGHTS AND DUTIES OF MARRIED PERSONS 

RCC 119: Mutual duties of husband and wife

The husband and wife owe to each other mutually, fidelity, support and assistance. 

RCC 120: Obligation of living together

The wife is bound to live with her husband to follow him wherever he chooses to reside; the husband is obliged to receive her and furnish her with whatever is required for the convenience of life, in proportion to his means and condition. 23

OF THE DISSOLUTION OF MARRIAGE RCC 136: Methods for dissolution of marriage 

The bond of matrimony is dissolved: 

1. By the death of the husband or wife; 

2. By a divorce legally obtained; 

3. When the marriage is declared null and void, for one of the causes mentioned in the fourth chapter of this title; or when another marriage is contracted on account of absence, when authorized by law. 

Separation from bed and board does not dissolve the bond of matrimony, since the separated husband and wife are not at liberty to marry again; but it puts an end to their conjugal cohabitation, and to the common concerns, which existed between them.

CAUSES OF SEPARATION FROM BED AND BOARD AND DIVORCE 

RCC 138: Grounds for separation from bed and board 

Separation from bed and board may be claimed reciprocally for the following causes: 

1. In case of adultery on the part of the other spouse; 

2. When the other spouse has been convicted of a felony and sentenced to death or to imprisonment at hard labor in the state or federal penitentiary; 

3. On account of habitual intemperance of one of the married persons, or excesses, cruel treatment, or outrages of one of them toward the other, if such habitual intemperance, or such ill-treatment is of such a nature as to render their living together insupportable; 

4. Of a public defamation on the part of one of the married persons towards the other; 24

5. Of the abandonment of the husband by his wife or the wife by her husband; 

6. Of an attempt of one of the married persons against the life of the other; 

7. When the husband or wife has been charged with a felony, and shall actually have fled from justice, the wife or husband of such fugitive may claim a separation from bed and board, on producing proofs to the judge before whom the action for separation is brought, that such husband or wife has actually been guilty of such felony and has fled from justice; 

8. On account of the intentional non-support by the husband of his wife who is in destitute or necessitous circumstances, or by the wife of her husband who is in destitute or necessitous circumstances. (As amended Acts 1954, No. 617, Sec. 1.)

RCC 139: Grounds for divorce 

Immediate divorce may be claimed reciprocally for one of the following causes: 

1. Adultery on the part of the other spouse. 

2. Conviction of the other spouse of a felony and his sentence to death or imprisonment at hard labor.

Divorce may be granted to either spouse after a separation from bed and board in accordance with the provisions of Section 302 of Title 9 of the Louisiana Revised Statutes of 1950. (As amended Acts 1954, No. 618, Sec. 1.) 

R.S. 9:301: Continuous separation for two years ground for absolute divorce as When married persons have been living separate and apart for a period of two years or more, either party to the marriage contract may sue, in the courts of his or her residence within this state, provided such residence, shall have been continuous for the period of two years, for an absolute 25

divorce, which shall be granted on proof of the continuous living separate and apart of the spouses during the period of two years or more. 

R.S. 9:302: Judgment of final divorce after judgment of separation-Alimony-Custody and 

Care of Children 

When no reconciliation shall have taken place, the spouse who shall have obtained a judgment of separation from bed and board may apply to and obtain from the same court a judgment of divorce at the expiration of one year from the date of the former judgment shall have become final; the other spouse may do likewise at the expiration of one year and sixty days from the same date.

Whenever a judgment of divorce is obtained by the husband against whom the judgment of separation from bed and board was rendered, the wife shall have the same rights for recovering alimony as if she had obtained the divorce.

The provisions of this Section shall not interfere with the rights of the spouse who obtained the judgment of separation from bed and board to retain the custody and care of the children, as provided by law. 

R.S. 9:303: Curator ad hoc for absent defendant

In an action for separation from bed and board or divorce where the defendant is either absent from the state or his whereabouts are unknown, the court shall appoint, upon application by the plaintiff, a curator ad hoc who is an attorney at law, to represent the absent party and all proceedings shall be had contradictorily with the curator ad hoc, and any judgment or divorce may be rendered against him that might be rendered against his principal as if the principal were present in person in open court. 

Upon the trial of the cause upon the merits or upon confirmation of any preliminary default, proof shall be made of a diligent effort on the part of the plaintiff to locate the absentee. 26

OF THE PROCEEDINGS OF SEPARATION FROM BED AND BOARD 

RCC 140: Necessity for judicial proceedings

Separation is to be claimed, sued for and pronounced in the competent courts of justice; it cannot be made the subject of arbitration. 

RCC 141: Procedure against attorney for absent or incapable defendant

When the defendant is absent, or incapable of acting from any cause, an attorney shall be appointed to represent him, against whom, contradictorily, the suit shall be prosecuted. 

RCC 142: Special cases involving foreign causes for separation or divorce Whenever a marriage shall have been contracted in this state or elsewhere by parties either of whom are residents of this state and the matrimonial domicile shall have been established in a foreign country or in another state and if the husband shall have abandoned the wife, in the state of said marriage or elsewhere, or shall behave towards his said wife in said foreign country or in said other state, in such manner as will entitle her, under our laws, to demand a separation from bed and board or divorce, it shall be lawful for her, on returning to the domicile in this state, prior to said marriage, to institute a suit against her said husband for the purpose above mentioned in the same manner as if the parties were domiciliated in such place, any law to the contrary notwithstanding.

Whenever a marriage shall have been contracted under the laws of and in this state, and there shall be issue of said marriage, and the husband or wife shall leave this state and secure a divorce in another state through sub- 27

stituted service in such other state, and contract another marriage in another state of which latter marriage there is no issue, should the said husband or wife return to this state and remain and live separate and apart for a period of two years from the spouse acquired in said latter marriage, if the other spouse of said first marriage shall have remained single, either the husband or the wife of the latter marriage shall be entitled to immediate divorce upon said facts being established to the satisfaction of the court, provided both of the parties of the said first marriage shall make and execute a sworn affidavit and present same to the court, evidencing their intention to remarry with proof that there is one or more living and dependent minor children, issue of the said first marriage, dependent upon them for support. In such case suit may be filed by either the husband or the wife seeking to secure divorce from such latter marriage at their established place of residence, and such person seeking divorce shall be entitled to secure service on the dependent (defendant) either by personal service or by substituted process through appointment of a curator ad hoc to represent such defendant if absent from the state. 

In such cases an attorney shall be appointed by the court to represent said absent defendant; the plaintiff shall to entitled to all the remedies and conservatory measures granted by laws to married women, and the judgment rendered in such case shall have force and effect in the same manner as if the parties had never left the state or were both residents thereof. (As amended, Acts 1920, No. 113; Acts 1934, 2nd Ex. Sess., No. 1; Acts 1946, 

No. 347.) 

RCC 143: Abandonment

Separation grounded on abandonment by one of the married persons can be admitted only in the case when he or she has withdrawn himself or herself from the common dwelling, without a lawful cause, has constantly refused to return to live with the other, and when such refusal is made appear in the manner hereafter directed. 28

RCC 144: Absence for lawful cause

The absence of the husband or wife, which has had a lawful cause, although it shall appear that the absentee has not been heard of, cannot authorize a demand of separation, except so far as is provided in the title: OF ABENTEES. 

RCC 145: Proof of abandonment

In all cases where there has been no answer filed, the abandonment with which the husband or wife is charged must be made to appear by one summons issued to him or her directing him or her to return to the place of the matrimonial domicile within thirty (30) calendar days from date of service, and by a judgment rendered after the expiration of such period, which has sentenced him or her to comply with such request, together with proof that notice of such judgment has been served upon him or her no less than thirty (30) days before.

The summons and notification shall be made to him or her at the place of his or her usual residence, if he or she lives in this State, and, if absent, at the place of the residence of the attorney who shall be appointed to him or her by the judge for that purpose, at the suit of the husband and wife praying for separation from bed and board. 

In all cases where there has been an answer filed, the abandonment with which the husband or wife is charged shall be proved as any other fact in a civil suit and such case shall be set and tried as any other suit. (As amended, Acts 1928, No. 271; Acts 1932, No. 73.) 

R.S. 13:4452: Time for appeal from separation from bed and board and divorce cases No appeal shall lie from any final judgment of separation from bed and board or of divorce after thirty days from its date, but any appeal perfected within that time shall suspend the execution of the judgment until the appeal shall have been finally disposed of. 29

OF THE PROVISIONAL PROCEEDINGS TO WHICH A SUIT FOR SEPARATION OR DIVORCE MAY GIVE OCCASION 

RCC 146: Custody of children pending the litigation

If there are children of the marriage, whose provisional keeping is claimed by both husband and wife, the suit being yet pending and undecided, it shall be granted to the wife, whether plaintiff or defendant, unless there should be strong reasons to deprive her of it; either in whole or in part, the decision whereof is left to the discretion of the judge. (As amended Acts 1888, No. 124.) 

RCC 147: Repealed. (Acts 1928, No. 65.) 

RCC 148: Wife's alimony pendente lite

If the wife has not a sufficient income for her maintenance pending the suit for separation from bed and board. or for divorce, the judge shall allow her, whether she appears as plaintiff or defendant, a sum for her support, proportioned to her needs and to the means of her husband. (As amended Acts 1928, No. 130.) 

RCC 149: Inventory of property and injunction against alienation-Wife's right to demand

During the suit for separation, the wife may, for the preservation of her rights, require an inventory and appraisement to be made for the movables and immovables which are in possession of her husband, and an injunction restraining him from disposing of any part thereof in any manner. 

RCC 150: Encumbrance of alienation of community after institution of suit

From the day on which the action of separation shall be brought, it shall not be lawful for the husband to contract any debt on account of the community, nor to dispose of the immovables belonging to the same, and any 30

alienation by him made after that time, shall be null, if it be proved that such alienation was made with the fraudulent view of injuring the rights of the wife. 

RCC 151: Provisional proceedings in action for divorce

The action for divorce shall be accompanied with the same provisional proceedings to which a suit for separation from bed and board may give rise. 

OF THE OBJECTIONS TO THE ACTION OF SEPARA- TION FROM BED AND BOARD AND OF DIVORCE 

RCC 152: Reconciliation after separation 

The action of separation shall be extinguished by the reconciliation of the parties, either after the facts which might have given ground to such action, or after the action had been commenced. 

RCC 153: Causes arising after reconciliation

In either case the plaintiff shall be precluded from bringing his action; but he shall be at liberty to bring a new suit for causes arising since the reconciliation, and therein make use of the former motives to corroborate his new action. 

RCC 154: Exceptions to action for divorce

The exceptions to an action of divorce shall be the same as those to the action of separation from bed and board, established by Articles 152 and 153 of the Civil Code. 

OF THE EFFECTS OF SEPARATION FROM BED AND BOARD AND OF DIVORCE 

RCC 155: Dissolution of community upon separation- 

Re-establishment after reconcilation Separation from bed and board carries with it separation of goods and effects. Upon reconciliation of the spouses, 31

the community may be re-established by husband and wife jointly, as of the date of the filing of the suit for separation from bed and board, by an act before a notary and two witnesses, which act shall be recorded in the conveyance office of the parish where said parties are domiciled but which act shall be without prejudice to rights validly acquired in the interim. (As amended, Acts 1944, No. 200; Acts 1950, No. 

304, Sec. 1.)

RCC 156: Effect of separation on advantages and dona tion between spouses

In case of separation from bed and board, the party against whom it shall have been pronounced, shall lose all the advantages or donations, the other party may have conferred by the marriage contract or since, and the party at whose instance the separation has been obtained, shall preserve all those to which such party would have been entitled; and these dispositions are to take place even in case the advantages and donations were reciprocally made. 

RCC 157: Custody and tutorship of children

In all cases of separation and of divorce the children shall be placed under the care of the party who shall have obtained the separation or divorce unless the judge shall, for the greater advantage of the children, order that some or all of them shall be entrusted to the care of the other party. 

The party under whose care a child or children is placed, or to whose care a child or children has been entrusted, shall of right become natural tutor or tutrix of said child or children to the same extent and with the same effect as if the other party had died. (As amended Acts 1921, Ex. Sess., No. 38; Acts 1924, No. 74.) 

RCC 158: Rights of children of the marriage

This separation or divorce shall not in any case deprive the children born of the marriage, of any of the advantages 32

which were secured to them by law, or by the marriage contract of their father and mother; but there is no right to any claim on the part of such children, except in the manner and under the circumstances, where such claims would have taken place, if there had been no separation. 

RCC 159: Effects of divorce 

The effects of a divorce shall not only be the same as are determined in the case of a separation from bed and board, but it shall also dissolve forever the bonds of matrimony, between the parties, and place them in the same situation with respect to each other as if no marriage had ever been contracted between them. 

RCC 160: Wife's alimony after divorce

If the wife who has obtained the divorce has not sufficient means for her maintenance, the court may allow her in its discretion, out of the property and earnings of her husband, alimony which shall not exceed one-third of his income; provided, however, that in cases where, under the laws of this state a divorce is granted solely on the ground that the married persons have been living separate and apart for a certain specified period of time, and the husband has obtained a divorce upon the ground of such living separate and apart and the wife has not been at fault then the court may allow the wife in its discretion, out of the property and earnings of her husband, alimony which shall not exceed one-third of his income. 

This alimony shall be revocable in case it should become unnecessary, and in case the wife should contract a second marriage. (As amended Acts 1916, No. 247; Acts 

1928, No. 21; Acts 1934, 2nd Ex. Sess., No. 27.)

RCC 161: Nullity of marriage with accomplice in adultery In case of divorce, on account of adultery, the guilty party can never contract matrimony with his or her ac- 33

complice in adultery, under the penalty of being considered and prosecuted as guilty of the crime of bigamy and under the penalty of nullity of the new marriage.

R.S. 40:259 Report of divorces and annulments by clerk of court to state board of health-Penalty for failure

The clerk of the Civil District Court for the Parish of Orleans shall, on or before the tenth day of each calendar month, forward to the state registrar, on forms supplied by him, the following information relative to each divorce and annulment, except annulments of adoption, granted in the parish of Orleans: 

(1) Name of parties. 

(2) Domicile of parties. 

(3) Docket number. (4) Date of judgment.

If the marriage affected by the divorce or annulment was or should have been registered in the parish of Orleans, the state registrar shall forward the above report to the local registrar for that parish. 

Any clerk who violates the provisions of this Section shall be fined not less than ten dollars nor more than twenty-five dollars.

R.S. 40:320: Divorce certificate - Registration of di vorces and annulments

The divorce certificate is the record prepared for every divorce on a form approved by the state board of health. 

It shall contain the information prescribed by the board. 

On the face of the certificate shall appear the certification of the fact of divorce, with the identification and signature of the person preparing the report. 34

For every divorce and every annulment of marriage decreed by any court, the clerk of the court shall prepare, within ten days after the decree becomes final, from the court records, a certificate of the decree on forms furnished by the state registrar.

On or before the fifteenth day of each calendar month, the clerk shall forward the certificates which were prepared by him during the preceding calendar month to the state registrar. If the marriage affected was one which was or should have been registered in the parish of Orleans, the state registrar shall immediately forward the certificate to the local registrar for the parish of Orleans. 

R.S. 40:321: Fee of clerk of court for divorce and annulment report

Every clerk of court acting under the provisions of 

R.S. 40:320 shall receive a fee of fifty cents for each certificate prepared and forwarded by him to the state registrar as provided in that Section. This fee shall be included in the court costs for the divorce or annulment. 

R.S. 40:322: Delayed or altered certificates-Allowed subject to board regulations The acceptance for filing by the state registrar of any certificate more than six months after the time prescribed for its filing and any alteration of any certificate after it is filed with the state registrar is subject to regulations in which the board shall prescribe in detail the proofs to be submitted by any applicant for delayed filing or for an alteration of a certificate. 

R.S. 40:323: Handling of delayed or altered certificate

Certificates accepted for filing more than six months after the time prescribed for their filing and certificates which have been altered after being filed with the state 35

registrar shall contain the date of the delayed filing or the date of the alteration and be marked distinctly "delayed" or "altered."

After a certificate has been accepted for delayed filing or after the board has permitted an alteration of a certificate on file, the state registrar shall note on the certificate a summary statement of the evidence submitted in support of the acceptance for delayed filing or the alteration together with the alteration made. 

Evidence affecting delayed certificates or affecting the alteration of a certificate after it has been filed with the state registrar shall be kept in a special permanent file. 

R.S. 40:324: Illegal alteration of certificates-Penalty 

Any unauthorized person who wilfully makes or alters any certificate provided for in this Part, or any certified copy thereof, shall be fined not more than one thousand dollars or imprisoned for not more than six months, or both.

4. Of a public defamation on the part of one of the married persons towards the other;
