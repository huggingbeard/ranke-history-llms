EUROPE: CRADLE OF PEACE OR WAR? 

by N. Polyanov (Izvestiya, January 4, 1966) 

Recalling Europe's recent past and taking a close look at its present, one can't help asking himself this by no means idle question. 

Many countries of the West and North of the old continent have arrived at a historical crossroads, so to day: it is time to choose where to go further. The situation in Europe has never been as explosive as it 

The "holy alliance" of the West European and American reaction has never been so close to the nuclear armament of the Bundeswehr an adventure which can turn out to be irreversible. Never before and such is the dialectics of life have the real conditions for seeking the way toward European security been as favorable as they are today. is now. 

It would be stupid, of course, to close our eyes to the difficulties arising in that way. Europe is politically split. It is burdened with unsolved problems many of which will probably remain sucy for a long time yet. The planet's two most powerful military groupings are opposing each other on its territory, and the line of division between them is already called a "frontline" in NATO staffs. Besides, the road to security has to be sought in the maze of blind alleys created by those who spiked all reasonable initiatives. Be it as it may, no pains, no gains. And this should be borne in mind by all those who would like to turn Europe from the cradle of two world wars into the cradle of a lasting peace.

Are there any hopes of getting things moving? It seems to me that the historic transformations that changed the face of Europe and gave its states fixed borders answer this question in the affirmative. 

The modern balance of forces in Europe does not favor the militarists. 

Today the border between socialism and capitalism passes not along the Nieman and the Bug, as it did before the Thrid Reich's attack on the Soviet Union but along the lower Elbe and the Werre, that is almost - 1 -

a thousand kilometers to the West. Having given birth to the most destructive weapons mankind has ever known, the revolution in science and technology, at the same time, clipped the wings of the Western military doctrines. Their authors have to take stock of the fact that a takeoff with aggressive intentions can easily turn out to be a suicide. 

The attempts of transferring the "Vietnamese experience" to Europe promise them nothing good, either. This is evidenced by the recent war maneuvres of the Warsaw Treaty countries in the German Democratic 

Republic. 

In a word, any variant of nuclear adventure in Europe be it general or local -- is senseless for the invaders, although either could be chosen by them: the demon of war is blind. The only way out is to keep this demon from breaking loose. And this presupposes collective effort on the part of European countries. 

Another thing favors the search for ways to security: the 20year-long treaty on the Atlantic alliance expires three and a half years from now. In two and a half years time its participants will have tọ announce whether they intend to remain in the alliance. Meanwhile, the motto, "to live without NATO," proclaimed by the Left opposition in the

Western countries, also for the first time began to find favor in the ruling quarters of some of them last year. This is recalled by the example of France, Norway, Denmark and a number of other countries, who preferred to keep aloof from the most dangerous nuclear Atlantic ventures. 

11 It is still unknown whether NATO will remain in its present form, but Europe will remain. To tie the future with NATO means taking the risk of getting lost in a whole forest of question marks. To link the future with Eruope means having business with the realities of the Sixties and Seventies, but only under one condition, namely, if there is peace.

Hence there is the growing susceptibility of Western Europe to the ideas of collective security. While discussing them, it is hard to get away from the fact that the Western powers and the revanchists on the banks of the Rhine have planted big obstacles in the way of the reunification of Germany in the foreseeable historical period. Therefore, it is necessary to proceed from the real situation that is shaping up. 

We shall not enumerate in detail the numerous proposals that have already been submitted by the Soviet Union and the fraternal socialist countries (on the conclusion of a nonaggression pact between the Warsaw Treaty and NATO countries, denuclearized zones, freezing of nuclear armaments in Central Europe, etc.). They remain on the roundtable of Big Diplomacy, and it will not be an exaggeration to say that they indicate precisely the main direction to security in Europe. one should also not disregard the narrow paths leading to the coveted goal, more especially if the main road is piled up with rocks. This is confirmed, notably, by the successful development of bilateral contacts between the Soviet Union and certain Western countries. But -2-

There also are other paths, including untrodden ones.

For instance, let us turn to the idea of mutual guarantees of nonaggression that the states could exchange. The European nuclear powers -- the 

Soviet Union, Britain and France and also the United States, that keeps one-half of its atomic potential on European territory, would be compulsory guarantors. All the European countries could become voluntary participants in utmost flexible frameworks and forms: in unilateral statements by the powers with the subsequent transfer of the corresponding documents to the United Nations for safekeeping, in biand multi-lateral commitments, and, lastly, in both of these forms and possibly in a third form of guarantee. The most important thing is that they be given. As regards the forms, the Europeans, I think, will be most obliging: they can accept any form. 

Or take the idea of denuclearized zones. It is needless to explain why the Rhine militarists gritted their teeth when they heard about it. Indeed, denuclearized Central Europe would make the Bonn military policy meaningless. However, Europe is not only the Rhine valley but also the fiords of Scandinavia, the azure coast of the Mediterranean, the mountains of the Balkan Peninsula and the chalk rocks of Albion. What, for example, would Denmark and Norway, let alone neutral Sweden and Finland, say if asked to legalize by a treaty what statesmen of these countries have already declared on many occasions: there is no place for atomic weapons in Scandinavia. Denmark and Norway, as is known, refused to admit such weapons to their territories and ports. 

Finnish President Urho Kekkonen and Swedish statesmen also spoke more than once in favor of Northern Europe being turned into a zone of lasting peace. Iceland, another Scandinavian country, does not participate in any NATO nuclear projects. What is it then that makes it impossible to legalize the present situation and rejoice at the creation of the first denuclearized zone ever in Europe?

In reply, some Danish and Norwegian politicians say sometimes: suppose that denuclearized Scandinavia becomes a fact. But, the Soviet 

Union, a powerful neighbor of Scandinavia, remains a great nuclear power. 

Won't the Scandinavians put themselves in a vulnerable position if they proclaim their "denuclearization" in the absence of a general disarmament agreement?

However, unconvincing as it may seem to me, this argument does exist. If the absence of the corresponding security guarantees on the part of the nuclear powers is the only obstacle to signing the treaty on setting up a denuclearized zone in Northern Europe, is this obstacle insurmountable? Moreover, the example of Scandinavia receiving security guarantees from the nuclear powers in exchange for the legalization of the denuclearized zone by a treaty may, undoubtedly, prove instructive and infectious for other regions of the Old World. Is it not worthwhile for diplomats to come together for the sake of setting up the first denuclearized zone in Europe? · 3.

The present author mentioned only two possibilities which, in his view, should be borne in mind as a supplement to the known proposals concerning European security. Of course, there are a multitude of other reasonable possibilities of "moving the stone," and they should be used. The Europe of the last third of the 20th century was born in the crucible of the anti-fascist struggle, the gun powder smoke of fighting and class battles. Its security may be only ensured on the basis of what was won by the peoples and what firmly established itself on the political map and signifies a new epoch in the history of the 

Old World.

A-BOMB AND NATIONAL SECURITY by Fyodor Burlatsky (Pravda, January 10, 1966) 

From time to time, there are moments in the kaleidoscope of rapidly-flowing international life after which the course of events changes and begins to follow a different direction, a new channel. In such moments, political leaders, who are aware of their responsibility, should be able to rise above everyday matters, review the situation as a whole and assess its prospects realistically in order to make the proper choice.

I believe that at this moment the leaders of the Western powers face a choice of this kind in connection with the Soviet government's proposal on the prevention of the further spread of nuclear weapons. 

The question is whether it will be possible today through common efforts to throw up a dam in the path of the proliferation of these weapons, because tomorrow it will be too late: an ever greater number of states will get their hands on these weapons of mass destruction, and the process of their dissemination will ultimately escape any international control. 

Nuclear armaments are presently possessed by five states -the USSR, the United States, Britain, France and China. Four or five other powers have also declared at different times that they are able to manufacture weapons of this type. For all we can tell, still other countries can reach for the A-bomb. 

The picture is more or less clear as to the highly dangerous consequences of total nuclear armaments. The most authoritative experts nuclear physicists, missile designers and the military have expressed their conclusions. Hundreds of volumes have been written to prove the possibility of a gradual slide of mankind down to a thermonuclear war or its accidental outbreak. Even a special word has been thought up, - 5 -

escalation, escalation of a world conflict as a result of a chain of circumstances. We can imagine how a man would feel on earth if not five, but several dozen powers possessed A-bombs and if the threat of a thermonuclear strike were to constantly accompany international conflicts, big and small. Still, despite all this, there are some leaders in the capitalist world who are trying to gain access to these weapons. 

The question of non proliferation of nuclear armaments has grown particularly acute and urgent of late in connection with the schemes of giving Federal Germany a nuclear status in one form or another. No matter what the West says about the "reincarnation" of the present leaders of West Germany and their "peaceableness," it is impossible to refute the fact that the Federal Republic of Germany is actually the only state in the world which sets the task of revising the results of the Second World War. Who can give a real guarantee that the German militarists, who unleashed two world wars, will not, by their reckless action, lead matters to a fresh world conflict?

In recent times, the Western powers, and, first of all, the United States, are trying to pursue the tactics, on the one hand, of not rejecting talks about a treaty on the non-dissemination of nuclear weapons, and, on the other, of openly or secretly leading the matter up to the participation of Federal Germany in nuclear armaments in one form or another.

Commenting on Erhard's recent visit to the United States, The New York Times said: "The Soviet Union insists that the non-proliferation treaty close the door to a NATO nuclear force. But the Wilson- 

Johnson and Erhard-Johnson meetings in the past few days have produced firm agreement that the door will be kept open although no one has 

any intention of going through it at present...The effort to avoid a definite choice between a NATO nuclear solution and a non proliferation treaty may prevent either from reaching fruition. But the hope is, by giving neither priority, gradually to achieve both." 

This is a vain hope. The Western powers are trying in vain to find loopholes of some kind to combine what, in fact, cannot be combined. The Soviet Union, the socialist countries and other peaceloving states will never agree to allow the participation of West Germany in nuclear armaments in any form whatsoever. This would run counter to the fundamental interests of peace and security in Europe and to the Potsdam agreements that demand that the revival of German militarism not be allowed.

There is no use laboring under illusions: if the US and its allies try to provide the FRG with access to the atomic bomb in any form, they will thereby render impossible the conclusion of a treaty on the :nonproliferation of nuclear weapons. Such is the meaning of the choice which Western Europe and the United States have to make now. - 6 -

the There is now a realistic possibility of concluding a treaty on nonproliferation of nuclear weapons. All the peoples of the globe and the world public are for such a treaty. The will of the peoples was clearly expressed at the recent UN General Assembly session. The resolution, calling for states to work for the conclusion of a treaty on the non-proliferation of nuclear weapons, was supported by more than 90 UN member states. These included not only the socialist countries and the peaceloving states of Asia, Africa and Latin America but also most of the Western powers, including the USA. The resolution does not leave any loopholes that would permit "nuclear or nonnuclear powers to engage in the proliferation of nuclear weapons in any form, directly or indirectly." The time has come now for the peoples and states to join forces and to work for the implementation of this decision. 

It should be emphasized that there is not a single nonnuclear power, great or small, the national interests and aspirations of which would conform to the possession of nuclear weapons. 

What actually are the arguments used by the ruling circles of a given country for the desire to possess the bomb? There are usually two arguments used: the interests of strengthening national security and prestige considerations.

To have a clearer picture of the actual meaning of such arguments let us put ourselves for a moment in the position of a politician of any nonnuclear West European country, be it Italy, Sweden or the Netherlands. Naturally, we mean a soberminded politician who would think only of the country's genuine interests. 

Thus, can it be said that the atom bomb can help to strengthen the security of any such country? This question immediately entails another question: does anyone threaten that country's security? Even

Western politicians and statesmen, with all their obvious bias, will not take the risk of maintaining that the Soviet Union, the GDR, Poland or other socialist countries plan to attack anyone, now or in the future. 

Here are some of the characteristic admissions made in connection with the discussion of NATO's future destinies. The Dutch newspaper 

Haarlems Dagblad says: "The concept of the cold war is in our days more applicable to relations between the US and France than to relations between the Western powers and the Soviet Union. Relations between East and 

West, though rather unnoticeably, have quickly improved almost to a normal and, at any rate, safe competition in the diplomatic and commercial spheres." The French newspaper Figaro expresses doubts as to "the possibility of military aggression on the part of Russia, who proclaims ;eaceful coexistence." 

G. Garbo, Chairman of the Venstre Party in Norway, says that "developments in Eastern Europe in the past few years have changed the situation which formerly had made NATO necessary." Though one can -7-

dispute the reasons, underlying such statements, they nevertheless show graphically that the bug-bear of "Soviet aggression" can hardly mislead anyone nowadays.

Equally unsound are the so-called prestige considerations. 

From a moral aspect the desire to acquire the deadly nuclear weapons, not in the interests of defense, but for some kind of prestige considerations, is in itself inhuman. We know that moral considerations are least important in the calculations of the imperialist leaders. Therefore, we shall deal with the practical aspect of the matter: does possession of the nuclear bomb augment the country's prestige and its influence in the world?

Britain is a convincing example in this respect. What did it gain by becoming a nuclear power? Did it come up with the United 

States in the solution of international problems? Was it able to consolidate its position among its former colonies and dominions with the help of military strength? There is only one answer to these questions, and this answer is NO. The international position of Britain has been steadily weakening in the postwar period, and the manufacture of nuclear weapons by it could not alter this process. 

The true prestige of any state in the world arena does not depend on the possession of nuclear weapons. It depends on the contribution a given state makes to social progress and peace and on the assistance rendered by it to the peoples in the struggle for their goals.

As for giving West Germany access to nuclear weapons, this could merely cause a serious detriment to the national interests of its population, for the geographic position of that country makes its protection in the event of a thermonuclear war impossible. people living in glass houses throw stones? 

Why should

What can the United States, Britain, France and other countries of the West gain from giving West Germany a nuclear status? This can only create additional problems for them. Already now they have encountered serious contradictions in their camp on this issue. If we suppose for a moment that the revanchist forces of West Germany really managed to get hold of atomic bombs, this would create a direct threat not only to the Western neighbors of the FRG but also to the United States which may be involved by these forces in any venture. 

Considering this problem from the standpoint of the real national interests of the Western powers, one inevitably comes to the conclusion that the only correct solution for them would be to make a choice in favor of a nuclear nonproliferation treaty. This choice would fully meet the vital interests of all nations. 

It should be noted that voices against this treaty are heard - 8 -

not only in NATO. Some people describe the Soviet proposal for concluding a treaty on banning the dissemination of nuclear weapons as almost an attempt to "preserve the monopoly of the USSR and the United States" over these weapons. But whom can these inventions persuade? 

What kind of monopoly can there be if five, not two, powers possess atomic bombs?

The Soviet Union is known to have manufactured thermonuclear weapons as a means of defense, and these weapons have in its hands become a factor ensuring the defense capacity of not only the Soviet state but of the socialist community as a whole. It has become a factor contributing to ensuring the freedom and independence of all peoples and to preserving world peace. 

At the same time everyone knows that for 20 years the Soviet Union has been tirelessly working for the complete prohibition of nuclear weapons. It put forward this proposal back in 1946 and has been upholding it for many years, though it possesses a huge nuclear missile potential.

It goes without saying that a treaty banning the spread of nuclear weapons cannot solve all problems, that it is only a partial measure. However, the conclusion of such a treaty would lay the ground work for a step-by-step solution of other major issues agitating mankind, namely, the restriction of the nuclear armaments drive, the setting up of atom-free zones in different parts of the world and then the complete prohibition of nuclear weapons and destruction of all of its stockpiles. In other words, such a treaty could become an important step towards the relaxation of international tension. CE-46

THE PEACEFUL ATOM. 1966 Dubna Meeting by TASS Correspondent 

(Kommunist, Yerevan, 14 January 1966, page 2)

The regular session of the committee of official representatives of the governments of member states of the Joint Institute of Nuclear Research completed its work on 12 January in the Moscow suburg of Dubna. 

Having discussed the report of Academician N. N. Bogolyubov, director of the Joint Institute, the committee noted in its resolution that fundamental research in the areas of high energy and nuclear physics was successfully fulfilled in 1965. The institute continued to be supplied with the latest experimental equipment and computers. Cooperation with scientific research institutes in the socialist states and scientific ties with laboratories in other countries of the world were successfully developed. 

The committee of the official representatives in its resolution expressed gratitude to the Soviet government for offering the scientists of the countries participating in the Joint Institute the opportunity to conduct scientific research on powerful accelerators in other institutes in the Soviet Union. 

The scientists considered the chief trend in the work of the institute in 1966 to be the resolutionof the most important problems in the area of high energy neutron physics. CE-46

WEST WARNED THAT COEXISTENCE IS INDIVISIBLE Unsigned Editorial (Moscow TASS International Service in Russian 17 January 1966) 

The editorial in today's Izvestiya is devoted to Soviet foreign policy. It stresses that "this policy is becoming even more active and effective and it is achieving even more successes, since it is based on the wisdom of the party and its Leninist collective leadership." 

The chief aims of Soviet foreign policy, the article says, consist of insuring, together with all socialist countries, peaceful conditions for building socialism and communism in our countries, defending the cause of peace and progress, doing everything possible to strengthen the unity and cohesion of the socialist countries and their friendship and bortherhood, supporting national liberation and revolutionary movements, developing all-round cooperation with the liberated states of Asia, Africa and Latin America, and consistently defending the principles of peaceful coexistence in relationships between states with different social-political systems. "These days of January," Izvestiya writes, "have been marked by exceptionally warm meetings of emissaries of the Soviet (?party and government) with working people of the Mongolian People's Republic and the DRV...The visits of the Soviet delegations to the Mongolian 

People's Republic and the DRV are an integral part of the unceasing efforts of the Soviet Union directed toward the development of fraternal relations and all-round cooperation with the socialist countries and the strengthening of the common front in the anti-imperialist struggle." 

As the paper points out, "the struggle for the unity of countries of the socialist comity stands at the center of Soviet foreign policy activities. It meets with the full understanding of and support from the other socialist states. Among the important results of this policy, achieved during the past year, are the further strengthening of the military alliance of Warsaw Pact countries and the widening and deepening 

11.

of the cooperation of the socialist states within the CEMA framework and also on a bilateral basis. "The unity and cohesion of the socialist countries is not only an urgent requirement for the development of these countries themselves but also for the entire international communist movement. At the same time, it is the most reliable way of averting the threat of war which hangs over the world today," Izvestiya writes. It also notes that "the Soviet Union constantly supports the strengthening of friendly relations between young independent states and the solution of disputes which may arise between them by peaceful means and works actively for this end." For example, the Soviet government worked actively for the achievement of positive results at the Tashkent negotiations. 

Noting that an essential basic part of Soviet foreign policy is the principle of peaceful coexistence of states with different social systems, Izvestiya writes: "The policy of peaceful coexistence is unthinkable without an active struggle against the provocations of the warmongers, without resolute opposition to interference by the imperialists in the internal affairs of other people and without an energetic mobilization of all progressive forces against the threat of war from imperialism. 

The governments of certain Western powers should realize that the principle of peaceful coexistence is indivisible. One must not think that this principle can be supported in relations with one socialist state while it is being crudely trampled in relation to another. Yet, this is what Washington is relying upon in widening its aggression in Vietnam.

However, the blatant adventurism of American imperialism has not only met the determined opposition of the heroic Vietnamese people. 

The just struggle of the Vietnamese people for their vital national interests is today supported by all the socialist countries and all progressive mankind. The Soviet Union is providing the DRV with ever increasing assistance in repelling aggression. 11 Assessing the present international situation, the paper says: "It is not only US aggression in Vietnam that constitutes a threat to the peace and security of nations. Dangerous plans are being nurtured on the other side of the Atlantic for intervention against Laos and Cambodia, the implementation of which leads to extension of the large hotbed of war in southeast Asia. In Europe people are witnessing brazen claims by the Bonn revanchists who (?want to change) existing frontiers with the aid of atomic blackmail. Nor have the provocateurs quieted down in vicinity of the free Cuban shores while the Washington "hawks" add further billions of dollars to be furnace of (?nuclear) armaments."

In such circumstances, the paper says in conclusion, Soviet foreign policy, which is firmly upholding the gains of the Great October, the interests of universal peace and the unity and cohesion of the socialist system, acquires particular importance.

BONN REVANCHIST SCHEMES DOOMED TO FAILURE by Army General Yepishev (Moscow TASS International Service in English 18 January 1966) 

Writing in today's Izvestiya, Army General Yepi shev stresses that the militant task of the day, the task of all fighters against reaction and war, is to expose the schemes of the Bonn revenge-seekers and their aggressive policies." 

The general notes that the "rise of revanchism and the rule of the military in the German Federal Republic constitute a gross violation of the Potsdam agreements, which stipulated that militarism should be eradicated from German life. There are no existing international documents agreed upon by the principal members of the anti-Hitler coalition which would absolve the German Federal Republic from carrying out the Potsdam decisions." 

The revanchism of the Bonn militarists relies on a powerful military machine created contrary to international restrictions formulated after the defeat of Hitlerite Germany. General Yepishev says: "The

Bonn leaders have not only begun military preparations on a broad scale but also the ideological conditioning of the population in the spirit of militarism and revanchism." 

The article quotes pronouncements by Bonn War Minister Von Hassel, whose territorial claims "go far beyond the Oder-Neisse frontier." The 

Bonn revenge-seekers "would not only like to restore the boundaries of the Hitlerite Reich but even dream of heading an anticommunist crusade to destroy the socialist countries and of liquidating the great historical achievements of the October Revolution." 

Yepi shev points out that the criminal revenge-seeking policy of the Bonn militarists and their patrons, the US imperialists who have already turned West Germany into NATO's main nuclear missile place d'armes and a seat of war danger in Europe, cannot but alarm working 13.

people throughout the world. The aggressive plans of West German imperialism must be opposed by the joint might of the peaceloving states and people of Europe. Yepishev maintains that a major role in this respect belongs to the GDR. 

The article notes that this hopeless revenge-seeking policy is against the vital interests of Germans themselves. "It is doomed to inevitable failure. If the Bonn madmen try to violate the borders of the socialist countries, they will learn the strength of our retaliatory blow the blows of all Warsaw Pact armies." 

Referring to history, General Yepishev states that "German imperialism is a dangerous and insidious enemy. One must never let it out of sight. It must be exposed incessantly and actively, and the masses must constantly be warned of the danger of the military adventures which the Bonn revenge-seekers are preparing." -7000

US FORCES UK TO REVISE MILITARY STRATEGY by Melor Sturua (Moscow TASS International Service in English 18 January 1966) 

Izvestiya's London correspondent Melor Sturua writes today about plans to "revamp" British military strategy drawn up under US pressure. He contributes this article in connection with preparations in London to issue a routine white paper on military matters. 

As noted by Sturua, "the historical changes that have occurred in the postwar world have irreparably undermined the British Empire, which had once been regarded as omnipotent. The "East of Suez" policy now pursued by the Labor government is doomed. "It will be wiped out by the irresistible hurricane of the people's national liberation movement. Furthermore, its pursuance and continuation is simply beyond the power of modern Britain. Fearing that traditional British war bases are unreliable," the correspondent points out, "the Pentagon is persistently urging Britain to create a costly duplicate system, relying on giant aircraft carriers and tiny islands."

As for the official viewpoint of the Labor government on this question, Sturua says that the "reorientation to giant aircraft carriers and tiny islands will allegedly enable Britain to reduce its military expenditures." This, of course, is wishful thinking, the correspondent writes. In reality, this strategy will not enable Britain to either reduce its military expenditures or even freeze them at the present level. "The distance between connivance with aggression and complicity in it is not so great after all," Sturua continues. "Beyond the ocean, they are making every effort to see that Britain covers this distance as rapidly as possible. Here is one new example: It is now known that the Americans and British are creating military bases in Thailand spearheaded at the flanks of the DRV." 

Explaining the impatience with which the "Pentagon strategists 15.

are urging their British colleagues to switch over without delay from their traditional strongholds to the giant aircraft carriers and tiny islands," Sturua writes: "The Pentagon and the monopolies behind it are striving to kill two birds with one stone, first to (?take over) the positions of their British rival in Asia and "Africa and second to use the released British troops in their own interests, primarily for waging the Vietnam war.'

Turning to the recent suspicious activation of the aggressive ANZUS alignment, Sturua notes that "Britain is now being hitched to it more and more securely although it has not formally signed the pact." 

In conclusion, Sturua asserts that the revamped military strategy elaborated in Washington leaves Britain in a vicious circle, which threatens its economy and national interests with incalculable dangers.

SERMON ON THE POLICY OF FORCE by N. Karev (Krasnaya Zvezda, Moscow, 22 January 1966, page 3.) 

As usual January has become an outstanding month in the political life of America. Congress has renewed its work in the capital following the recess. President Johnson has already addressed Congress on the state of the country, outlining the course of the domestic and foreign policy of the government during the current year. An endless flood of speeches which will not be silenced until September and will perhaps be extended even further began under the dome of the capitol where the legislators meet.

What policy does the US President offer in his basic statement on the state of the country?

The Eighteen Nation Disarmament Committee has already gathered in Geneva eight times. Geneva conference.

The work of the Committee if now facilitated by the fact that most of these problems were discussed at the recent session of the 20th General Assembly which mapped out concrete ways of solving them in its disarmament resolutions. The Committee's immediate responsibility is to develop the positive results of the discussion at the General Assembly and to try to give them the form of a concrete understanding.

This refers above all to the problem of the non-proliferation of nuclear weapons. Its topical nature is chiefly determined by the 28 

---

fact that a number of countries are prepared in scientific and technical potential to start on the race for taking possession of the atomic bomb. Glenn Seaborg, Chairman of the US Atomic Energy Commission, named more than 10 countries having such potential.

US AND NATO ACTIONS BLOCK PROGRESS AT GENEVA (V. Men'shikov and G. Dragunov) 

(Pravda, 6 March 1966, page 5.)

The work of the 18-Nation Disarmament Committee has entered a new stage. The four-week general discussion has ended and the participants in the talks have begun to examine the question of preventing the proliferation of nuclear weapons. The common feature of the documents presented to the Committee on this question is that with all the differences in the approach to the essence of the question and in the methods for settling it in principle the urgent need to prevent the spread of nuclear weapons all over the world is recognized. 

The main task of the 18-Nation Committee was and remains the drafting of a program for universal and complete disarmament. The 

Committee was set up for this purpose and this was and remains the main trend of its activities. However, life itself advances to the forefront various questions from the general complex of measures on disarmament and security and concentrates the efforts and attention of the participants in the Geneva talks on it. That was the case, for example, during the preparations for and drafting of the principles of the Moscow treaty banning nuclear tests in three media. That is how matters stand today as well when the main point of the present work of the 18-Nation Committee is the problem of concluding an agreement on the nonproliferation of nuclear weapons. "If a limit is not put on the spread of nuclear weapons in the world, the danger of the outbreak of a nuclear war will increase manifold," stated the message sent by A. N. Kosygin, Chairman of the 

Council of Ministers USSR, to the participants in the Geneva talks. 

The keen attention of the world public is focussed on the work of the Disarmament Committee. Delegations of different countries are arriving in Geneva. The 18-Nation Committee and the heads of delegations are receiving letters from all corners of the world. Public opinion demands the speedy drafting of a realistic agreement on the nonproliferation of nuclear weapons, conforming to the interests of the 1

peoples. According to the estimates of extremely competent specialists, about a dozen countries already possess the scientific and technical means and the industrial potential enabling them to begin production of nuclear weapons.

The rumors persistently circulated here to the effect that West German and other foreign engineers are working in great secrecy to build up an atomic arsenal in the South African Republic show how great the danger of extending the "geography" of possession of this weapon of mass destruction is. 

One can readily understand the profound anxiety of the African peoples who realize against whom the racists in Pretoria may direct this weapon on the "black continent." "The remnants of colonialism in Africa," said Aberra, the Ethiopian delegate at the 

Committee meeting on 22 February of this year, "threaten the peoples of the African continent."

All of us have become witnesses to the fact that the world has not been spared dangerous accidents and incidents due to the possession of nuclear weapons. These accidents may result in serious consequences for the peoples. The echo of the accident with an American 

B-52 bomber with nuclear weapons on board off the Spanish coast resounds sharply in Geneva. To preclude the possibility of repetition of such accidents the Soviet delegate proposed that the Committee should immediately urge the cessation of bomber flights with nuclear weapons on board beyond national boundaries. 

In general, if we are to speak of Soviet proposals tabled in the 18-Nation Committee, that bear upon the many measures connected with nuclear disarmament and other problems and designed to remove the threat of war, to reduce tensions and to ensure peace, the Soviet Union, as frankly admitted by Western journalists, is firmly in the lead.

The proposals of the Soviet government allow the 18-Nation Committee to move from a standstill and to go ahead as far as nuclear disarmament and the prevention of nuclear proliferation are concerned. 

To facilitate understanding as regards the signing of a nuclear nonproliferation treaty, the Soviet government, as is known, proposed the inclusion of an article in the treaty prohibiting the use of nuclear weapons against non-nuclear powers, parties to a treaty, which do not have nuclear weapons on their territories. 

The reaction of the representatives from these countries in the Committee to the Soviet proposal was immediate and positive. 

After all the non-nuclear powers are still meeting with the attempts of the USA to impose its "nuclear patronage" upon them. This by no - 2 -

means corresponds to the national interests of these countries and gives rise to apprehensions that they may be embroiled in adventures of the nuclear imperialist powers. 

The delegates of India, Mexico, Nigeria and other countries, expressing the opinion of the nonaligned and neutral states, declared in the Committee that the Soviet proposal meets the vital interests of the non-nuclear countries.

The speeches by Western delegates in the 18-Nation Committee and the press of the NATO bloc pointed to a "great interest" in the Soviet views and admitted that they "may move the Committee's work forward," Promises "to study" them attentively and very earnestly were also made. However, weeks passed, and the Committee had not learned anything concerning the results of these "studies." 

Such prolonged "dilly-dallying" tallies poorly with the repeated statements by the representatives of the Western powers about the necessity "to solve the nonproliferation problem as soon as possible."

A recent secret meeting of NATO nuclear strategists at the session of the so-called "McNamara Committee" in Washington, in which, naturally, FRG Minister von Hassel participated, clearly shows that there is a discrepancy between the words and deeds of the Western leading political circles. NON-PROLIFERATION OF NUCLEAR ARMS STILL PRIMARY TASK (V. Nekrasov) (Mirovaya Ekonomika i Mezhdunarodnyye Otnosheniya,

The question of whether the circle of nations possessing the deadliest contemporary weapon will remain limited or whether this weapon will appear in dozens of hands is one of the central problems of international relations. Will fresh nuclear arsenals be propagated and will nuclear weapons become the property of those political forces that might attempt to use them for the sake of their mercenary motives without considering the consequences or will it be possible to halt the process of the spread of atomic and hydrogen bombs in the world and to arrest the development of tendencies dangerous to mankind? All this alarms the peoples and attracts the attention of statesmen recognizing their responsibility for international security. 

The world lives in an atmosphere of expanded international tension, The aggressive foreign policy course of US imperialism, which has found its most cynical expression in the war in Southeast Asia, aggravates the threat of a world thermonuclear conflict. The expansion of activities by the revanchist forces in West Germany, stubbornly soliciting nuclear weapons, also leads to a complication in the world situation. The delivery of nuclear arms in any form direct or indirect to West German militarism can lay the foundation for a new stage in the proliferation of this weapon. In such a case the risk of thermonuclear war would increase substantially and would almost automatically lead to a new round in the feverish rocket armaments race. 

Can the peaceloving peoples and nations and sober-minded politicians calmly view similar prospects? 

Many political and public figures who believe it absolutely necessary to adopt steps for the purpose of settling a number of international problems can be found in the leading capitalist countries. A characteristic example is the work of the so-called Arms Control and Disarmament Committee in the United States. At a recent conference - 5 -

The support now enjoyed in broad circles of the Western public by the proposal concerning the conclusion of a treaty for the nonproliferation of nuclear arms is evidence of the vital force of this idea. The problem of controlling nuclear arms, as noted in the

By 1950, five years after the explosion of the American atomic bomb over Hiroshima, there were only two powers in the world possessing nuclear weapons -- the United States and the Soviet Union, for which the forced development of nuclear weapons was a compelling matter, since it had no other choice in the face of the hostile policy of the imperialist states, hurriedly knocking together the NATO military block.

The adoption of the Soviet Union's proposals would signify a reliable obstacle to the spread of nuclear weapons in the world.

Atlantic politicians are now being overcome by increased nervousness. MELEKESS ATOMIC RESEARCH CENTER (B. Konovalov) (Izvestiya, 17 March 1966, page 4.) 

Melekess is one of the newest atomic centers in our country. 

Located not far from the city is the Scientific-Research Institute of 

Nuclear Reactors of the State Committee for the Use of Atomic Energy 

USSR a unique "testing ground" of nuclear energy. Various designs for nuclear plants and various materials for nuclear technology are under research and are tested here. 

Recently a new nuclear power plant was placed in operation in Melekess, The institute is located only a hundred kilometers from Ul'yanovsk. Therefore, the nuclear power plant was named Ul'yanovskaya. 

Of course, next to the giant Volga Hydroelectric Power Stations its capacity appears to be extremely modest, some fifty thousand kilowatts. 

However, it is appropriate to recall that the first Soviet electric power plant -- the Volkhovskaya GES --was of approximately the same capacity. The Ul'yanovskaya Nuclear Power Plant is experimental. It is one of those "bricks" which will be the foundation for the creation of largescale nuclear power.

When one speaks of the development of nuclear power, then, according to tradition, the atom is presented first of all as a powerful giant destroying the specter of the "power famine" on our planet. Yes, that all is true the thermonuclear power stations should remove the threat of power famine. However, this is a task for the distant future. 

Nuclear power, however, should be ready for active participation in the near future. After all, approximately eighty percent of the power resources of our country are located in the eastern areas and a large portion of industrial production in the western areas. In many areas it is economically more profitable to build nuclear power plants than to ship fuel there. One does not even mention the fact that in an age of synthetic chemistry the burning of its basic raw material -petroleum and coal is simply waste. After all, even out of the smoke 

. 49 -

of thermal power plants it is possible to "fashion" nylon socks, shirts, coats and similar synthetic materials.

However, in order to "overtake" the conventional fuel power plants it is necessary to achieve a sharp reduction in the cost of nuclear fuel. Computations show that with the already existing mockups of 400 to 500 thousand kw nuclear reactors it is possible to build power plants capable of competing with those operating on coal. 

Physicists and engineers continue to storm the economic "barrier" facing nuclear energy.

A search for the most efficient solution to the problems of nuclear engineering is being conducted on a wide scale throughout the world.

The Ul'yanovskaya Nuclear Power Plant is one of the important links in the investigations. Here a very promising so-called water boiling reactor which feeds steam directly to the turbine, is used, although the plant can also operate along the design of the Novo- Voronezhskaya Atomic Nuclear Power Plant. In this case the 3090 C and 100 atm water is fed from the reactor to the steam generator where it gives off its heat to the water in the second circuit; it is then transformed into a 237° C and 32 atm steam and is directed to the turbine.

Imagine for a minute the cylindrical body of the reactor with massive walls rated for a pressure of over 100 atm. Looking down into this cylinder, one sees a honeycomb arrangement composed of 90 hexagonal casings. Basically they are filled with nuclear fuel. Each of these two meter hexagons, constructed of zirconium-niobium, contains 126 fuel elements shaped like pipes, constructed of the same alloy and filled with "pellets" of uranium dioxide and carefully soldered.

Nineteen assemblies contain the neutron absorber and serve to control the reactor and to regulate the capacity of the neutron flux. Six of them are for emergency use. They will instantaneously shut down the operation in case of emergency.

Water supplied to the reactor through natural circulation rises, encircling the little rods, and is heated by the heat formed during the fission of the U-235 nuclei and finally boils. At the top, under the massive lid, hot steam under 100 atm pressure is collected. 

The steam is directed through pipes into the separator, where moisture and residual radioactive particles are removed and, throttled to 32 atm, passed through the separator once more, and eventually reaches the turbine blades. After giving off its energy to the turbine, the steam condenses into water; this water passes into the deaerator and is returned to the reactor. The cycle is completed. This design has been selected because the power plant is to be used for experiments. 

In principle radioactive steam which forms in the reactor could be led directly into the turbine. The advantages of a boiling reactor 50 -

would then be fully utilized. These advantages are extremely important. 

Utilization of steam under higher pressure increases the efficiency, reduces steam consumption for the production of the same amount of electric power, simplifies the technological design and decreases the capital investment. Computations show that the net cost of electric power, when operating at the simplest single-circuit system, may be reduced by 15-20% as compared to the water reactors of the Novo- Voronezh Nuclear Power Plant type. This alone is sufficient evidence of the vast possibilities for the improvement of economic factors that lie at the foundation of nuclear engineering.

A considerable share of the capital investments in the building of nuclear power plants is allocated to the various systems for the safety of the staff and the population in case of failure. 

Nuclear fuel must be handled extremely carefully. An Indian proverb says: "If the hand is not wounded it can carry poison. Poison will not damage those who are not wounded." During normal operation a power plant without "wounds" is absolutely safe. "If may seem paradoxical," states Oleg Dmitriyevich Kazachkovskiy, director of the Melekess Nuclear Institute, "but the thermal power plants' exhaust into the atmosphere of radioactive substances is greater than that of nuclear power plants. Basically these are infinitesimal uranium particles contained in the coal. Judge for yourself the reliability of the filters installed in a nuclear power plant."

US, SOVIET DRAFT TREATIES DEBATED AT GENEVA 4 April Session (Unsigned) (Moscow, TASS International Service in English, 4 April 1966) 

At a meeting of the 18-nation disarmament committee today, Polish delegate M. Blumstein analyzed the US draft treaty on nuclear weapons nonproliferation. The Polish delegate said that the first article of the treaty in the US version, despite all the amendments recently made to it by the United States, leaves a loophole for nuclear weapons proliferation through military alliances and, therefore, contradicts the resolutions of the 20th UN General Assembly session on this question. The Soviet draft of the first article, he said, closes all channels for nuclear weapons dissemination. Its superiority over the US draft is unquestionable, he stressed. 

Canadian delegate General Burns, who spoke at today's meeting, hesitated to advocate the US draft treaty. On the contrary, he emphasized what in his opinion were common points in the second articles of the Soviet and US drafts and suggested that the US and Soviet delegations meet to hammer out a common draft of this article. 

US delegate Fisher again spoke on the theme that without onthe-spot inspections there can be no guarantee of observance of the future treaty to end nuclear weapons tests despite expert opinion that national detection means are quite adequate for this purpose. 

5 April Discussions (Moscow TASS International Service in English, 5 April 1966) 

The main obstacle to solution of the problem of nuclear weapons nonproliferation is the effort of the United States to achieve a solution of this problem in accordance with NATO aims and its desire to satisfy the claims of the German Federal Republic for nuclear weapons, - 1 -

USA STRONGHOLD OF REACTION 

(by N. Yakovlev, Doctor of History) (Moscow, Izvestiya, 27 April 1966, page 3) 

Stories of My Life is the name of a remarkable book by that eminent Russian scholar and revolutionary Nikolay Aleksandrovich Morozov, which only recently has been published in a new edition here. In his book Morozov describes the dire plight of the Russian people under the autocratic yoke. In the hopeless gloom of the tsar's dungeons Morozov and his comrades gave much thought to the outer world. Meanwhile, time impartially ticked off the years that went by beyond the prison walls.

Then came the year 1876. "I cannot remember any particular event in our life for that year which would be worth mentioning with the exception of one happening that still deeply moves me," Morozov wrote. "This was the centenary of the birth of the great trans-Atlantic 

Republic. Over there, far beyond the ocean, preparations were in process festively to mark the first day of the beginning of the civil liberty of the New World... 'Will no one in Russia respond to this world triumph?' my friends and I wondered, 'Well, if no one at liberty wishes or dares to do that, we here in duress will do that!' And we did..." 

The prisoners managed to secure 200 large sheets of blue paper on which they glued white stars and stripes, thus manufacturing the starry flag.

The prison governors naturally ordered the flags to be taken down. The prison yard, which was like a deep well, took on its ordinary appearance. "But," Morozov concludes, "I felt so happy! An inner solemn voice declared: 'Now greetings have been sent to the great trans-Atlantic Republic from our land of Russia!"

Almost 100 years have passed since then. Today no realistically thinking person would call America a bulwark of freedom -- although the 

- 84 -

bourgeoisie's ideological handmaidens never tire of touting high and low their shop-soiled commodity of US "democracy." One, however, forms an opinion of the United States by what it does and not from the wellpaid outpourings of the US Information Agency. Meanwhile, the things that they do are shameful. "American imperialism," says the Resolution of the 23rd Congress of the CPSU, "is the chief reactionary force of our time and has assumed the role of world policeman. It interferes brazenly in the internal affairs of many countries and peoples in Africa, Asia and Latin America, scorning their sovereignty, seeking to crush the national-liberation movement by force of arms and to restore the colonial order..." Still this phenomenon deserves examination. How could it have happened that the torch of liberty, lighted across the ocean in the late 18th century, went out in the 20th century. It is smoking and the emanating fumes are poisoning the world. 

Measured against the history of other big states, that of the USA actually covers a very short span of time. Indeed, under two centuries have elapsed since the 13 British colonies merged to declare themselves the United States. It was this modest baby that has now grown into capitalism's present giant. The Pecksniffs in Washington are prone to regard the country's major technical achievements as proof of the vitality of the system evolved by the "founding fathers" of the United States. The US propaganda machine plays the same old gramophone record to assert that across the water there lies a land of righteousness and shining virtue. The myth, as is true of this genre, is indeed poetic. But like every myth it very faintly resembles the real state of affairs.

The USA is conspicuous for the exclusiveness not of its internal arrangements but of the external developments attending the appearance of the trans-Atlantic Republic. In their expansionist drive the 

Americans overcame their opponents, the scattered Indian tribes, feeble Mexico and declining Spain. In the mid-19th century the great European powers maintained armies hundreds of thousands strong. In 1861 the USA. had a force of only 16,000 men under arms. Even in 1914 US military spending, in relation to the national income, comprised a quarter of what was spent then in Britain, a sixth of what was spent in France, Japan and Germany, and an eighth of what was spent in Russia. 

The USA suffered no damage at all in either world war. On the contrary wartime business activity imparted in each case a powerful impetus to American economic advancement. In the 1914-18 and 1939-45 wars mankind lost 10 million and 50 million killed respectively. The 

USA lost 130,000 and 312,000 respectively. The Second World War caused tremendous damage in the USSR. The Soviet people lost 20 million lives. 

The country lost 30 percent of its national wealth. The USA, on the contrary, waxed fat.

External conditions provided the USA with hothouse development. - 85 -

Emerging from the flames of a war of liberation against Britain, the young American democracy once pluckily defied the despots of the Old World. However, because provincial America was so far away and of so little importance in the world balance of forces this challenge was not taken seriously at that time.

As for the American bourgeoisie, the philosophers of the moneybag had already minted the theory of "obvious designation," claiming that God had predestined the US to rule the world. The troubadours of the dollar reasoned that the Almighty would have never bothered to contrive such "perfect" institutes, were they not destined for all of mankind. 

Though this theory may evoke just a smile, it was peddled and modernized by generations of American ideologists. It is drawn upon to this very day to support Washington's aspirations to be supreme arbiter in world affairs. 

The 20th century has querried the truths of capitalism which at the turn of the century had seemed inviolable. The Great October - Revolution opened up an era of revolutionary changes throughout the world. The whirlwind of revolution has swept over the continents and the fresh wind has begun to penetrate the United States as well. 

US ruling quarters preserved in the changing world stonewall fidelity to the old. The peoples of the socialist countries are rapidly advancing towards the future. The diehard rulers of America see in this a retreat from the highroad of civilization. Declarations are issued on future calamities for the peoples who are advancing along unexplored trails. The ideologists of imperialism do not restrict themselves to words alone. They have transformed this former revolutionary republic into the main headquarters of counterrevolution. The circle has closed: the American bourgeoisie, starting its rule by recognizing changes in human society as being obligatory, has today declared that changes in the world in the interest of progress are an unnatural phenomena. As was pointed out in the report of the CC CPSU to the 23rd Congress the United States "would like nothing better than to subjugate many independent states and to erect barriers to the social progress of mankind. Time and again the course of history is bearing out Lenin's description of American imperialism as predatory, piratical and a strangler of the peoples, hated by all." 11 It is said that the dead cling to the living.

This is approximately what can be said of Washington's present policy. The US grip is a tenacious one the United States bases itself on its enormous military-industrial potential. The stars and stripes today fly over bases of aggression scattered throughout the world, over corrupt social regimes which are supported by American bayonets. 

Following the Second World War, American imperialism has time and again unleashed aggressive adventures, attempting to turn back - 86

the wheel of history. Today the United States is waging a dirty war against the Vietnamese people. Although official Washington insists on claiming that in distant Vietnam interests of the "free world" are being defended, the peoples see behind this phrase the frenzy of a plunderer who does not want to give up his booty. At the 23rd Congress of the 

CPSU Henry Winston, the leader of the delegation of the Communist Party of the United States, said that by their barbarous and dirty aggression against the Vietnamese people, the United States ruling circles had smeared the proud traditions of the American people. The criminal aggressive actions which the American imperialists are so brazenly perpetrating today in Vietnam were yesterday perpetrated in the Dominican Republic and the day before yesterday were directed against Cuba. They had a hand in strangling the Congolese people, and on the handle of the dagger which was thrust into the heart of Ghana, one also finds Washington's finger prints.

The shameful role which Washington took upon itself cannot but evoke the resolute condemnation of many Americans. Today there is a growing tendency among the American people to reappraise the situation of their country. The discussion on America's further road is assuming a nationwide nature, 

The real masters of America today are the 500 corporations controlling the national economy. In turn the leading role among the 

500 corporations is played by the main groupings of monopoly capital the Morgans, the Rockefellers, the DuPonts and the Mellons. It is precisely they who are vitally interested in preserving the present state of affairs, in the unbridled exploitation of the enormous majority of the people.

Mankind, however, is living in the age of revolutions which are of a socialist and anti-imperialist nature. These revolutions are washing away the foundations of imperialism and, naturally, in the first place that of US imperialism. To oppose the historical process in the nuclear age is to place the world on the brink of a catastrophe. 

Sober-minded Americans ever more frequently renounce such a prospect. As far as progressive America is concerned it believes that the monopolies which have made war their business can be bridled. The 

Communist Party of the United States stresses that the task is to unite into a powerful stream all the forces that come out against the monopolies. This is how a political majority which will challenge the domination of the corporations and will wage the struggle for radical reforms comes into being.

Such is the aim of the progressive forces of the American people. A persistent struggle will undoubtedly be required to achieve this. - 87 - 

---

The constant changes which are taking place in the world by no means leave the United States unaffected. As a result of the persistent struggle of the working class, certain reforms have been carried out in the country and definite changes have taken place in ensuring civil rights, etc. These reforms, however, reflect not the internal tendency of the development of American society but are the results, in the first place, of the successes of the socialist system. 

Despite all the ups and downs in the development of mankind, the new always scores victory in the final count. In the rapidly changing present-day world the United States, this bulwark and citadel of reaction, is to an ever greater degree losing its international influence.

BREAKTHROUGH ACHIEVED AT DUBNA IN RESEARCH ON TRANSURANIC CHEMICAL ELEMENTS (Unsigned) 

(Moscow, Pravda, 12 April 1966, p 4)

One of the most progressive and absorbing fields of current nuclear physics is the artificial synthesis of transuranic chemical elements that do not exist on earth. The international staff of researchers at 

Dubna achieved a new and extremely important success in this field: the mystery of the most superheavy 104th element has been deciphered. 

This is what the director of the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research, Academician N. N. Bogolyubov, told the TASS correspondent. 

In 1964, in this international scientific center of the countries of the socialist comity, the collective of physicists under corresponding member of the Academy of Sciences USSR G. N. Flerov for the first time in a laboratory obtained a new chemical element, not existing in nature, given in the periodic table as number 104. It was established that it is subject to radioactive decay and that its half life is 0.3 seconds. 

Following the completion of the investigation of the physical properties of the new element, a group of Czechoslovak and Soviet scientists, headed by I. Zvara, began a study of the chemical properties of the new element. The experiments were conducted on a unique accelerator of multicharged ions created by Soviet scientists, engineers and workers. 

The scientists were able to show reliably that the 104th element differs greatly from the other transuranic elements in its chemical properties and that it is chemically analogous to hafnium -- element number 72 and consequently opens up a new group of super heavy elements with specific chemical properties ad confirms the correctness of the periodic system of elements in the new field of transuranic elements.
