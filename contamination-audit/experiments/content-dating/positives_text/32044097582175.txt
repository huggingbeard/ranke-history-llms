It's a brand new wrinkle, Brother, in our good old Treasure State, This here Compensation statute that we're workin' by of late, An' my own exact opinion, if you rise an' want to know' 

Is that the same Law's a good un if you give it half a show.

If I'm workin' in the timber, if I'm in the mill or mine, If I'm sweatin' in the smelter, if I'm on the 'lectric line, There's a soothin', homey feelin' when I pause to cogitate That whatever else may happen, there's a law to compensate. 

If I'm mixed up in a mishap (which I hope may never come) An' I get a clip that floors me an' I'm crippled up quite some, Then I know the law's behind me, 'bout one thing I needn't fret: 

I'll be 'tended to while healin', an' I'll mock old Mister Debt!

Sure, the law may not be perfect-little done by humans is- But this law of Compensation, let the toiler call it his! 

It's the thing that spells Protection, makes sweet thoughts pervade his dome

When he knows it offers shelter to the wife an' kids at home! 

But I p'intedly don't hanker for to draw that kind of pay, An' I'll try to see, my Brother, that it don't bob up your way;

Every motion I'll make careful, to prevent the thing that's worst, An' I'll sing an' preach the doctrine that we're callin' "Safety First!"

PREFACE 

Since 1915, the Industrial Accident Board, and its successor agency, the Division of Workers' Compensation, has served Montana employers and employees through the administration of the Workers' Compensation Act and various industrial safety laws. Many changes have taken place since the original enactment of the laws under which the Division operates. 

In 1915, the weekly benefit amount for temporary total disability was 50% of the injured worker's wages, subject to a maximum of $10 per week. Medical benefits were limited to a two week period and could not exceed a total sum of $50. Today, weekly payments for temporary total disability amount to 66-2/3% of the injured worker's wages, subject to a maximum of $174 per week, and there is no limitation as to the time or dollar amount for medical costs.

The workers' compensation law both in Montana and throughout the United States now affects nearly every business operation and worker, and is an integral part of our industrial and economic system. 

This publication has been drafted in order to provide a comprehensive report of the activities of the Division of Workers' Compensation for fiscal year ending June 30, 1977. However, the Division wishes to recognize and commend the achievements of past administrations for their efforts in improving and refining the workers' compensation law in Montana. Through the insight of both the Montana Legislature and prior Industrial 

Accident Board and Division employees, a truly meaningful delivery system has developed. 

We hope that the information provided in this annual report is helpful, and if anyone has questions concerning the Division's functions and its operations, they may contact the Division at 815 Front Street, Helena, Montana 59601.

DIVISION ADMINISTRATION SECTION I 

The Division administers several separate Acts concerning the payment of compensation benefits for on-the-job injuries and contracted diseases, and the enforcement of industrial safety. The 1977 Legislature adopted several significant pieces of legislation which have added to and modified the Division's functions. Included in these legislative enactments were the creation of an Uninsured Employers' Fund to grant all employees in Montana workers' compensation protection even though certain employers had not abided by the law concerning mandatory coverage requirements; the removal of restrictive rate making requirements for private insurance carriers and allowing insurance carriers to utilize a competitive rating system; a complete revision of the Occupational Disease Act in order to streamline the procedures utilized to determine benefits under that Act, and to remove a substantial amount of superfluous and unneeded language in the prior law; the allowance of the writing of fictitious groups by workers' compensation insurers; and the creation of a victims of crime compensation system which will pay certain benefits to innocent victims of crime.

The following laws are administered by the Division of Workers' Compensation, with reference made to the title and chapter number in the Revised Codes of Montana, 1947: 

Industrial Safety and Health - Montana Safety Act, Title 41, Chapter 17; Metal and Nonmetallic Mine Safety Law, Title 50, Chapter 1; Montana Coal Mining Code, Title 50, Chapter 4; Boiler Inspection and Engineer's Licensing Law, Title 69, Chapter 15; 

Hoisting and Crane Engineer's Licensing Law, Title 69, Chapter 16; Compensation Laws Volunteer Firemen's Compensation Act, Title 11, Chapter 20; Silicosis Benefit Law, Title 71, Chapter 10; Crime Victims Compensation Act, Title 71, Chapter 26; Workers' Compensation Act, Title 92, Chapter 11; Occupational Disease Act, Title 92, Chapter 13; 

Rehabilitation of Injured Workers, Title 92, Chapter 14.

MAJOR ACTIVITIES DURING FISCAL YEAR 1976-1977 Administrative Program 

Proposed new legislation which clarified the language and established specific procedures for the Occupational Disease Act. Proposed new legislation which established the Uninsured Employers' Fund and assisted in the preparation of legislation to create the Crime Victims Compensation Fund. 

Participated in Small Business Clinics with four other agencies and met with over 1,200 Montana employers in twelve Montana cities. Conducted the

Annual Claims Seminar for representatives of the insurance industry, attorneys, physicians and other interested parties. The Rehabilitation Unit served 526 injured workers, and personnel of the Audit Unit reviewed 895 employer accounts. Internal cost savings and administrative decisions saved Montana employers about $700,000 in reduced assessments. 

State Insurance Fund Program 

As of June 30, 1977, 16,935 employers received coverage through the State Fund. Approximately 2,500 employers were included in the new experience rating system. Of these, about 500 having poor safety records paid an additional $900,000 in premium, while the 2,000 firms with good safety records saved some 2.4 million dollars in premium costs. A new program was designed to provide for automatic discounts based on premium volume, which will be implemented in fiscal year 1978. 

The State Fund paid $9,461,236 in compensation and medical benefits as a result of accidents to injured workers.

Insurance Compliance Program 

Personnel in this program processed over 20,000 new accidents reported to insurance carriers and self-insurers, and reviewed and monitored the payment of benefits to injured workers resulting from the submission of 3,100 new claims.

Safety and Health Program 

Activities under this program included 1,100 safety and health inspections and investigations affecting 20,938 employees; 100 training courses in first aid, safety, cardiopulmonary resuscitation, and defensive driving; 881 boiler inspections and investigations; and 1,600 inspection and investigation activities in the mining industry. fuck

INTRODUCTION SECTION II 

Many changes in legislation over the years have given the Division various new duties and responsibilities. In order to meet the financial requirements, revisions in accounting methods and reporting techniques have been necessary from time to time. 

The following statements reflect the financial activity and the condition of Division funds for the fiscal year ending June 30, 1977. Four types of statements are included, i.e., Balance Sheets, Statements of Changes in Fund Balance, Statement of 

Receipts, and a Program Cost Statement. These statements report the financial activity for each fund and each accounting entity within the fund administered by the Division. 

However, because of the magnitude of the State Compensation Insurance Fund, the financial activity of this entity is reported as a separate item in the following section of this report.

The Consolidated Balance Sheet, Consolidated Statement of Changes in Fund 

Balance, and the Statement of Receipts, compare the financial activity for the fiscal years ending June 30, 1976 and June 30, 1977.

While administering the various laws and regulations pertaining to workers' compensation, the Division incurs necessary operating costs. In order to fund these costs, state law requires the Division to assess Compensation Plan No. 1 self-insurers, Compensation Plan No. 2 private insurance carriers, and Compensation Plan No. 3, the State Compensation Insurance Fund, on an equitable basis. The amounts collected during fiscal year 1977 from these assessments may be found in the Statement of Receipts. 

The calculation of these assessments is published in a separate report which is distributed to the self-insurers and insurance carriers. 

Division operating costs are controlled by the Legislature through the appropriation process. The Program Cost Statement shows the actual operating costs for fiscal year 1977.

NEW HOLLAND STATE COMPENSATION INSURANCE FUND

INTRODUCTION SECTION III 

In 1916, after the first year's operation, 453 employers were enrolled with the State Fund. Today, enrollment approaches 17,000 firms, and new applications are processed daily. The State Compensation Insurance Fund provides Montana employers one alternative to obtain workers' compensation insurance coverage as required by law. The Division maintains a separate accounting entity in the 

State's Accounting System to record the activity of the State Fund. 

The Division maintains a sound financial and actuarial fund, credits investment earnings to the Fund, and each year analyses the assets, liabilities, reserves, income and expenses to determine whether enrolled employers receive a dividend. Based on fiscal year 1977 operations, the Fund will declare a dividend to eligible employers of approxmately 4.6 million dollars.

The Division employs an independent actuary to review payroll and accident experience and recommend increases or decreases in rates and reserves. 

Rates for fiscal year 1977 were generally lower than fiscal year 1976, due to the past and projected claims experience.

The following statements were prepared on the same basis as the previous year's statements. Premium earned was computed on the accrual basis, and the balances of the reserves and liabilities were reviewed by the actuary for adequacy. 

BALANCE SHEET 

The Balance Sheet found on the next page reflects the financial status of the assets, liabilities, reserves and fund balances as of June 30, 1977. 

In fiscal 1974, the State Fund began recognizing estimated premium due, but unbilled at the end of each fiscal year, as premium earned. Because of the accounting transactions used to implement this change in accounting procedures, unassigned funds are available for policyholders of record during fiscal years 1975 and 1976. The State Fund has now declared a supplemental dividend to its qualified policyholders. Therefore, two new accounts appear in this year's 

Balance Sheet. The first is a one-time liability account set up to pay additional dividends to policyholders of record during fiscal years 1975 and 1976. The second is "Unallocated Premium, due to Change in Accounting Methods", formerly "Prior Year Income Recorded in Current Year", which has been renamed and adjusted to reflect the purpose of the account. 

Investments are reported at par and adjusted to reflect the unamortized premiums, interest purchased, and unaccumulated discounts, which is in conformance with the reporting method in the Statewide Budgeting and Accounting System. DEGREE INJURIES AFFECTED THE EMPLOYEE'S ABILITY TO RETURN TO THE NEXT SCHEDULED WORK SHIFT 

With the implementation of the Division's new management information system, a new coding format was instituted to better identify the effect an injury has on an employee's work attendance. In past years, the accident was coded either as disabling, which meant the injured party was unable to return to the next scheduled work shift, or nondisabling, which meant that no work time was lost beyond the day of the accident. All accident information coded was from the initial report with no further adjustment effort. 

The new reporting format allows the Division more flexibility in identifying lost time injuries. "Lost Time Injuries" refers to those cases where the employee had a medical cost associated with the accident and failed to report to the next scheduled work shift. "No Lost Time Injuries" refers to cases where there was a medical cost involved but the employee attended the next work shift. The minor injuries column refers to cases where a minimal medical cost was initially reported and the employee lost no work time, and therefore considered a minor injury under the OSHA standards. Cases where a firm determination could not be made as to medical cost or lost time were classified as "Unknown". The reader should be cautioned against attempts to make valid comparisons between the two reporting formats. Follet OCCUPATIONAL SAFETY AND HEALTH ACT SURVEY 

Each year, the Division of Workers' Compensation has conducted a statistical survey in cooperation with the United States Department of Labor, Bureau of Labor Statistics. The purpose of the survey is to gather and develop data relating to industrial injuries and injury exposure within the state, which will conform with national standards. Gathered information will be used as a tool to direct state and federal education and inspection activity to areas where accident and illness prevention needs are the greatest.

The survey, conducted on a calendar year basis, includes employers of all private industries except self-employed individuals and agricultural firms employing ten or less employees. Federal, state and local government is not included. 

The data for certain mining activities, railroads, and federal reserve banks was supplied directly by the Bureau of Labor Statistics. 

Questionnaires were mailed to more than 4,200 employers for 1975, and more than 3,500 employers for the 1976 survey. Out of the questionnaires mailed, approximately 500 each year were found to be usable for survey purposes. The 

Division found it necessary to make follow-up mailings and telephone calls, which further validated the information received. Overall usable response rates amounted to 96.6% in 1975, and 97.2% in 1976. 5000 4000 3000 2000 1000 Questionnaires Mailed 76 75 Adjusted Sample 76 75 Usable
