Mrs. Henry D. Tudor Cambridge

In 1895 my father-in-law William Tudor had published the Diary of his great-grandfather, Deacon John Tudor, who came with his widowed mother to Boston in 1715 at the age of six. There were only 250 copies printed of this interesting volume, all long since disposed of. However, among William Tudor's papers were piles of loose sheets of the portraits reproduced in the Diary as well as a couple of pages from the geneological data appended thereto. 

As the Tudors have been a virile and prolific race, there are now many young descendants of the Deacon who might like to have this brief illustrated record of their ancestors. 

1. John Tudor, born in England, came as a child to Boston, where it is said his mother made the first white penny rolls in the town, which John as a child delivered to his mother's customers. 

The small street on which they lived was called, on account of this business of Mrs. Tudor's, "White Bread Alley"-later renamed Harris Street,-not far from the Copp's Hill Burying Ground in the North End. 

They brought from England an oil portrait of John, as a child, in a buff suit with white muslin collar and ruffles at the cuffs. This portrait was without a frame and was rolled up. 

Considerable work had to be done to it later. Also, a carnelian seal with the coat of arms and crest as shown. 

John became a prominent citizen of the fast growing town of Boston and was Deacon of the Second Church. He married 

Jane Varney with whom he lived happily sixty years, dying in 1795-six months only before her death. They had six children. 

2. Colonel William Tudor, born in 1750, was the youngest child of the Deacon. He married Delia Jarvis and they had eight children.

The Colonel was a noted swimmer, which served him well in his courtship of the Tory maid, Delia Jarvis. He was with the Patriot Army in Cambridge in May, 1775, and she was at Noddle's Island (East Boston). He, carrying his clothes neatly in a bundle on his head, swam across from the mainland to the Island and returned in the same way. 

He was Judge Advocate General of Washington's Army, with the rank of Colonel, a position he held for three yearsgoing with the Army to New York and to the Jerseys and staying with them at Valley Forge.

He was a charter member of the Society of the Cincinnati and its vice-president, and one of the founders of the Massa chusetts Historical Society. 

3. William Tudor (a bachelor), eldest son of the Colonel was a writer and a diplomat, dying in Rio Janeiro in 1830. He owned and edited the North American Review and was one of the founders of the Boston Athenaeum. 

4. Frederic-brother of William and son of the Colonel was known as the Ice King, owing to his successful sending of ice to the Tropics and India. He invented a method of packing ice and had a widespread trade. He amassed a large fortune-married 

Euphemia Fenno, by whom he had six children. He died in 

1864 in Boston. His sister, Emma Jane married Robert 

Hallowell Gardiner and had nine children. She died in 1865.

There is no picture of her in the Diary. 

5. Delia Tudor, youngest daughter of the Colonel, married Commodore Charles Stewart of the U. S. Navy, by whom she had three children.

Her daughter Delia Tudor Stewart married John Henry 

Parnell of Avondale, Ireland, by whom she had ten children, the sixth of whom, Charles Stewart Parnell-the noted Irish 

Patriot-married in June, 1891, the famous Kitty O'Shea, and died in October of that year. 

6. Henry James Tudor-the youngest child of the Colonel-had three children by his wife Fanny Foster, but no grandchildren. The lovely woodlands in the Medford Fellsway were given in memory of their daughter, Virginia, and are known as Virginia Woods. 

The six oil portraits were painted by the following artists: 

1. Deacon John Tudor-Artist unknown. 

2. Colonel William Tudor-Gilbert Stuart. 

3. William Tudor-Gilbert Stuart. 

4. Frederic Tudor-Francis Alexander. 

5. Delia Tudor Stewart-Gilbert Stuart. 

6. Henry James Tudor-Chester Harding.
