PREFACE 

Why I am interested in stimulants and stimulation I practiced the use of stimulants, mainly tobacco, for twelve years, when I became convinced that this was something more than an innocent habit. Instead of its being "just pastime", etc., I found it to be a very definite harm to my general health and also thinking capacity. I began an investigation, which is now eighteen years old, resulting in my conclusions as shown here. I observed that nothing is ever found in newspapers or magazines, nor on the radio, criticizing stimulants tobacco in particular simply because such discussions would injure their advertising revenues, regardless of the injuries such products do to ones health. 

I originally intended to discuss only tobacco but the more I studied the subject the more I became convinced that tobacco was "tied up" with coffee, tea, tobacco, and alcohol and while alcohol has been over-discussed, coffee and tea were under-discussed, leaving tobacco, approximately in the center and one of the main supports of the other three, practically ignored. If coffee and tea are eliminated, the desire for tobacco is greatly diminished. If coffee, tea and tobacco are eliminated, the desire for alcohol is negligible. Eliminate tobacco, leaving coffee, tea, and alcohol it becomes an easy matter to convince anyone regarding the other three, as these are easily controlled since the main support, tobacco, is what holds the desire for the other three, or in other words, maintains the desire. Simply another case of "the House that Jack 

Built" affair. A kind of four legged chair - cut off the two legs i.e., coffee and tobacco, and the chair (all four stimulants) will collapse. A very simple matter to control all four habits.

The reason people have not paid more attention to stimulation, is because they have not had the time or inclination to look into the matter but once the subject is brought to their attention in a logical, personal, and business-like manner without discussing the moral and spiritual side - they give it their attention with enthusiasm. I felt that here was a field big enough for me to work on; an outlet for my excess energy and enthusiasm, that would not be concluded in a few weeks or months, nor within my life time. A field in which I had much formidable opposition to keep up my interest, so I decided to make this my hobby, educational program, and contribution for having lived, by imparting to others the information that I had discovered and accumulated. I have been investigated and "depositioned", which spurred me on to greater effort. I have mailed copies of articles to physical and health directors of every good sized college and municipal board of education in the country, all at my own expense, without so much as a postage stamp in payment. I cannot boast of results so far, but 

I have hopes from the letters received. 

One of the largest universities in New York has a class in Personal Living and another requested permission to reprint my material in a class book; another is using extracts in civic classes. My activities cost me about $50.00 per year in postage and supplies and I get more satisfaction out of that $50.00

than any other similar amount I have ever spent. Time never hangs heavy on my hands nor do I have to sit through a poor movie to kill time - I do like movies, very much or go to bed early because "there is nothing else to do." 

I am never bored or lonesome. In other words, I have a real hobby, and I mean real in every sense of the word. I can always turn to mailing out these articles or compiling new ones and if anybody gets more satisfaction, or kick, out of life than I do, I would like to have their recipe. 

I have my critics. They are my best friends: they keep me accurate and keep up my interest. There are people, especially "educated" people, who object to every new suggestion or idea made. They "get mad" because it was not their idea; they dislike anyone else to have ideas. I appreciate there are many things wrong with these articles and I do not say this apologetically but simply to keep the record straight. The continuity is bad; but it was the best 

I could do and, after all, these are expression of ideas and not demonstrations of literature. They are an accurate compilation of facts as the writer sees and understands the facts to be and they are written in simple understandable language, interspersed with illustration, that young and busy people, who do not have much time for reading, can comprehend without any strain on their mental faculties. There is considerable repetition used and this is done for a specific purpose i.e. to drive home the salient points I wish to make. want this book to reach young people and working people and they are too likely to look-over and over-look, also misunderstand the true meaning of a statement made, hence, the reiteration. 

I maintain that tobacco is derived from a similar species of plant life, and answers the same purpose as marihuana, which is stimulation. Why kid ourselves that tobacco is harmless.

This book contains the kind of information, I feel sure, my father would have given me had he had the information to give. 

In discussing this subject I have tried to place the human element above everything else, the real purpose of which is to awaken an interest in the general subject of stimulation in order that others may "carry on" from where I left off or to improve upon what I have said. 

What I am about to say, and which I am going to use 27 chapters and 37,000 words to say it in, is substantially this: 

We are the result of what we eat and drink. We ought to "EAT to LIVE rather than LIVE to EAT."

I hope you like the idea even if you disagree with the way in which I say it.

I am very much interested in a new calendar, the present one having outlived its usefulness. I am also interested in preserving my teeth, through the process of seeing that my body is adequately supplied with calcium, or lime. 

Both of these subjects will be dealt with in the appendix.

CHAPTER I. 

IMPORTANT CONVERSATION, WITH OURSELVES 

The Brain and Stomach in a gossiping mode Have you ever stopped to think what the different parts of the body might say to each other if they could talk? We can, and do, through our sense of speech, express our views on many topics. If, in any way we are abused, we can and do talk about it. If the brain and stomach had the power of speech, would not their conversation be something like this? 

Brain: "Now, you listen to me, Stomach. That last mess of nourishment you sent up had nothing in it that a dog could use." 

Stomach: "Well, how could I help it? I did the best I could with the mess that was given me to digest, for your benefit." 

Brain: "Well, I don't see how I can be expected to produce good results if this is the kind of material I have to work with." 

Stomach: "No use complaining to me about it. Why don't you take it up with this man and have him supply me with better material - you do the thinking for him? Then I can pass the good stuff on to you."

Brain: "This man blames it all on you. He says you are always asking for more material to digest and he cannot resist the temptation to send it down to you."

Stomach: "Sure, I ask for material but not the mess of stuff he sends down. 

Furthermore, just because I ask for it doesn't mean he has to pamper and pet me like a spoiled child."

Brain: "This fellow is always complaining about his success in life. He cannot get the 'breaks' (whatever that means); he should have had more education, etc. Why, this poor sap has more alibis for his failures than the fellow who invented the word. The real reason is, that he doesn't use any sense in selecting the material he gives me to work with in order that I may produce mind or good thinking that will obtain for him the things he wants, really should have, and is entitled to." 

Stomach: "Well, he says all the other fellows eat and drink anything and everything they want and see advertised; he doesn't see why he can't too." 

Brain: "You tell him to look over those other fellows and see if they are in any better condition than he is. They may 'seem' different but right there the resemblance ends." 

Stomach: "You thought that last load of nourishment was bad, but wait until you get this one I am wrestling with now. Somebody invited him out tonight to play cards. They had a midnight 'lunch', consisting of shrimp salad, mince pie covered with a thick creamy sauce, topped off with coffee 'strong enough to float an egg I believe they call this demi-tasse; just a small cup of coffee to you and me. They sometimes have large cups and then put milk and sugar in it, to add to my troubles." 

Brain: "I thought something unusual was happening. I Just received the effects of some beer, high-balls, plenty of cigarettes, but no chewing tobacco. 

I wonder why he doesn't chew tobacco? This was a 'favorite dish' in the good old days." 

Stomach: "Oh, sure, I thought I told you about that. As soon as he arrived at the house, they had to have a high-ball; that starts the friendship off on the right foot. The fact that two or three of these will make them fight

like'l later on, makes no difference. It is all done for dear old friendship's sake and to let the other fellow know he is not a quitter nor is he limited in the capacity of his body to accept abuse." 

Brain: "What was that mess of stuff we been having all day long? I am becoming so hardened to abuse that I cannot keep track of it any more." 

Stomach: "I will try to outline it for you. For breakfast he had grape-fruit, a nice dish of ham and eggs, hot biscuits, fried potatoes and a couple of 'nice' cups of coffee. Oh, I forgot to ask you: how do you like the cigarettes we get each morning before breakfast? All right, let's skip it; that's a trivial matter compared to what follows. During the morning we had four or five cigarettes, and you will notice this man inhales the smoke; I wonder what his lungs think about that? The lungs should not kick. Look at me; I receive the full effect of the smoke through its action on the saliva from the mouth, whether it is inhaled or not, and in addition to this, I must wrestle with whatever action is caused by the smoke on the blood supply, from the lungs. To continue with the menu; for lunch, we had a nice cheap salad of stale vegetables, purchased in a cheap restaurant, because this man cannot afford to buy fresh food as he must save his money for high-balls, beer and cigarettes, in order to prove to the other fellows that he is a sport. 

During the afternoon we had four or five more cigarettes. For dinner (supper) He had a plate of soup, a nice great big beefsteak, with frenchfried potatoes and all the other cooked dishes, and when I say 'cooked dishes,' I mean about all the real nourishment has been cooked out and poured down the sink, with the cooking water. I would have helped some if they had made a meat gravy with the residue from the cooking; that would have saved some of the food value. For dessert: we had a nice piece of mince pie, made with 'pure' lard, which usually takes about five hours to digest. 

Then after dinner, we had four or five more cigarettes, which about fills out the day and seems to be an average day, except for a few variations. To top off this day, he went to a card party, the details of which you are familiar with."

Brain: "Say, what's the idea of this man always following up a meal with a cigarette or tobacco in some form, and then plenty of it between meals? That seems silly to me. Maybe he does it because the Indians did, eh? There is no nourishment in it. In fact, it simply retards and prevents what I am trying to do for this fellow." 

Stomach: "Gee, I don't know. I often wondered myself. It makes me sick.

It interferes with digestion, poisons the food, thus giving me a pain, and he then sends down some kind of pills. These pills are worse than the tobacco, but I don't suppose anyone could make this fellow believe that. He gets relief by deadening the nerve centers and then 'thinks' he is relieved. This fellow seems to be a 'dumb klux' in more ways than one, although he would be all right if he would take reasonable care of himself." 

Brain: "For his own benefit, tell him I am ready, willing and anxious to give him some real results, if he will only give me a little assistance. He doesn't have to be fussy of 'picky' in what he eats; using a little common sense in what he places in his mouth, is all that is necessary. Why, do you know, I am only working ten percent of my full capacity, primarily, because of the terrific handicap in nourishment you send up. If I were reasonably and

-- sensibly nourished, I could do a 100% better job than I am doing. 

Stomach: "OK, I'll take this matter up with him by producing an awful pain or sickness, as I know of no other way of appealing to him, but this won't do much good as he will only drink some 'likker' or send down some pills which are sure to be his old favorites - physic or aspirin.” 

Brain: "I suppose this fellow will be getting married some day, hoping to have as an heir, a boy who will be of the calibre sufficient for President of the U. S. Imagine his 'surprise' when he finds he is only the 'garden variety' kind of boy; lucky to be on this side of a jail." 

Stomach: "That is over my head. It's too deep for me. You will have to wrestle with that one."

Brain: "Well, I don't know what more I can say. It's up to you to do something if you expect me to do my work well. I like to work, the same as any healthy man's brain will, but I'll be damned if I like to fight against so great an obstacle as the material you are sending me. It's a wicked shame to expect me to do my work well. It just doesn't make sense. How the arms and legs can perform their duties well, is beyond me." 

Arms and Legs: "Well, we certainly are glad to see that you finally 'got around' to thinking of us. We not only felt neglected but forgotten. It is we who do all the hard work and certainly have a 'kick' coming, if anybody has, on the nourishment we receive. Is it any wonder our muscles are flabby and we tire easily and quickly? But, we do the best we can with the nourishing material supplied.”

Stomach: "I wish I could help all of you, but, you know, this fellow is the victim of habit and he seems to think that because he did a thing once, he must keep right on doing it even if it kills him. Because he and his friends do not die immediately following one or more of these gluttonous excursions, he assumes the disagreeable results are only a temporary inconvenience and that everything will be all right in a short time. The 'short time' may mean, to him, a couple of hours or couple of days while you and I know it is absolutely and positively of a permanent nature." 

Brain: "You are right. The poor sap has nobody to blame but himself. The question is, can we convince him of that? It doesn't seem likely. At any rate, the other fellows have not been told, but this doesn't mean you and I should not keep on trying. Rome was not built in a day; it took 22 years for Irving Berlin's song "God Bless America," to catch on, so don't lose heart. Why, it took centuries to convince man that the earth is round. Even today, there are many men who will tell you it is flat, for the reason that if it were round, the Mississippi River would be running up hill. They ignore the fact that the Tennessee River, which parallels the Mississippi 

River, runs in the opposite direction." 

Stomach: "OK, I'll keep on trying if you do. We, surely, ought to win when old age overtakes him. He ought to have sense enough, by that time, to quit or "let-up" a little; most of them do. The fact that it is too late then, to use the benefits gained, may never occur to them, but, you know, better half a loaf than nothing.” 

Brain: "OK, Pal. Goodnight. See you in the morning with a headache.

Incidentally, if this man would skip tobacco and alcohol, I could skip a lot of headaches that aspirin won't cure."

7 This is the beginning of life's activities; without responsibilities of any kind, and so recognized by courts of law. At this age, the child leans upon the parents only. 

8 to 15 A new era arrives. The child transfers his "leaning" from the parents to the school teacher. Law courts recognize them as "juveniles" and give them special care. 

16 to 23 Spring is here. The "sap begins to flow in the tree." Puberty has arrived. Everything appears rosy, now. This is the age of adolescence. Let's get going and show the "old-timers" how things should really be done. They "blossom out" from dependence to independence; begin to "know things." A new and wonderful conception of life begins. This is the dangerous age dangerous because it may lead in any direction. If these years are lived carefully and moderately, taking particular care of the health, nothing may be feared for the rest of life. This is the age of competition competing with friends and acquaintances to "out-do” them in dare-devil feats. Self-control, will-power, and good judgement are more necessary at this time of life than any other. 

24 to 32 A real working knowledge of life begins. Those who missed many things at school begin attending night school to "catch-up." They begin the forward "climb," and the result of this climbing depends exclusively on how well they have previously cared for their health. 

33 to 39 Employers are keen to obtain, and have in their employ, employees of this age. This is the best and most valuable part of the employee's life. They are now at the zenith of their power, physical strength and stamina. Hope of success surges high in the mind of these men. Very independent, hoping this will bring them the success they cherish. 

40 to 45 If not "good" now, the chances are slight. The form is made and the mold set. No turning back now. They cannot start a "new

business" or "get a fresh start" from here. Employers are now thinking of him as an "old man", and looking around for a replacement. It is too risky to leave his job as unemployment may be the result. This should be the ideal age, but can he make others believe it? Not likely. All dissipators should abandon hope here. 

Hope is only for those who have "grown" and improved. This is another dangerous age, socially. Here, a man is twitted about being old, and in order to satisfy himself and prove differently to others, he is likely to "step-out" with the ladies, who refer to him as the "old fool." Then his real troubles begin. It is a cruel situation, but that is the way it usually works out. 46 to 55 Beginning to "coast" and think about "taking it easy." They look forward to the time when they can do those things which they never before had the time or money. Have been saving-up for hobbies, rainy-days, etc. Find great interest in children all children. Hope children do not have "it as hard" as they did, not realizing that it is the "battle of life", if not too vicious, that gives us our rating in the eyes of others. 

56 to 65 Hope to retire any day now. Anxious to get started on some of those things they have had in mind for a long time. Know all about life that they want, or care, to know. Just want to do something to leave behind as a remembrance. Something real and constructive, as their contribution for having lived; something that others can use for their benefit. This man is heard to say "No man has a right to live without giving something of himself to mankind." He is glad he is done with "youth" (being young) and, in the absence of accumulated knowledge, would not go over the same ground again for anything in the world. Just to be given the opportunity to do the things he wants to do, is all he asks, and he must be the judge of what he "wants to do." 66 on In order to arrive at the age of 66 "safe and sound", this depends exclusively on the treatment of yourself. Now "floating along with the tide." Eyes and ears for everything, but let the other fellow do the talking and performing. He did his best. He did his best but realizes it was not quite good enough; he wanted to do better but could not "quite make the grade." Realizes he could have done much better if he had taken better care of his health in his younger days but then, it wasn't the custom, in those days, to take good care of one's health. Everybody leaned more toward the side of trying to get rid of the health he had. He is thankful for having lived; hopes we are not too disappointed with him, and that his having been here may be productive of some real constructive results that others may "cash in” on. It was an interesting journey, but how different it would have been, if he had "only known" more about it earlier in life. 10

CONCLUSION 

Of all the hundreds of things a man hears about and is told to do, for the "more abundant life", there are just four items that 99% of the people indulge in, one or more, which can be omitted and thereby obtain inestimable gain to themselves inasmuch as what we eat and drink determines how well the "engine will run" and what our self expression will be. The four items are: Coffee, Tea, Tobacco, and Alcohol and it is a very simple matter to do without these, even if we have inherited a taste for them. Simply look upon these as poison, dirt or filth, and you easily "poison" your mind against their use; the absence of which will prevent the body from being poisoned. We should feed ourselves with as much care as we do our dog, car or horse. 11

CHAPTER III. 

TOBACCO 

The great American weed and stimulant, with 25,000,000 users Tobacco was first "discovered" or grown in America. The Indians were using it long before the white man came. They used it as a symbol in treaty making. This is attributable to primitive man's worship of fire which could only be controlled to a limited degree. The Indians could control burning tobacco; it would smolder and not blaze. 

The only difference between tobacco and other weed is; tobacco will not "blaze up", it just smolders and thus "smokes", otherwise we could smoke most any kind of weed.

We, of today, are simply imitating the Indians when using tobacco but even the Indians had better judgement than to inhale the smoke and it is in the inhaling that the greatest harm occurs to the human body. 

All the tobacco to be found in other parts of the world is the result of seeds originally taken from America and these American seeds, planted in any other part of the world produce a different kind of tobacco than we can grow, the cause of which is the difference in soil. A good example of this is Turkish tobacco.

Tobacco and marihuana are derived from a similar specie of plant life. 

The difference, in effect, on the human body, is only in degree, none of which can, even remotely, be said to be beneficial to our health. We fear Marihuana. 

We tolerate Tobacco. Why? Is it simply to compliment or imitate the Indians? Why kid ourselves tobacco is harmless?

It would be repetitious to go further into the history of tobacco which can be found in any good encyclopedia. The purpose here is, to discuss some phases of tobacco and the effects of its use, by man, that we do not ordinarily hear about. The reason it has not been discussed is because the tobacco companies, enjoying such large profits from this business, have been able to silence opposition in the newspapers, magazines and radio, through their advertising accounts; the revenues being of such proportions that these "business men" will not jeopardize their revenue even if it is at the expense of your health. 

This is just another phase of the "good old days" when they would accept advertising of nostrums for consumption, cancer, paralysis, etc., until the 

Government had to step in and silence these fakirs in order to protect the public.

I appreciate that speaking and writing against the use of tobacco in America is like criticising the use of wine in France, Spain, or Italy; ale in 

England or beer in Germany, but who the'll wants to live now or any other time, in those countries and, more particularly, copy their habits. Our economic and social life, here, is becoming so complicated that we need every obtainable advantage that will produce clear thinking and, surely, tobacco, wine, and beer will not assist us any even if we do hear it said "nearly everybody uses these so they must be all right.”

Anything that is said here is not intended to reform anyone. This is not a "temperance lecture." It is not a fanatical tirade against the use of tobacco, 12

but more particularly the absurd and ridiculous practice of inhaling the smoke - the tobacco companies in their advertising say "everybody inhales at some time." Tobacco has been so little written and talked about that few people are aware that any real danger exists in its use. This book is an appeal to the intelligence of my readers to think seriously of something to offset some of the bunk we hear and see advertised, as well as the "opinions" of those who wish to justify their own bad habits.

By helping the other fellow, the reaction helps ourselves. We live for ourselves through living for each other. No one wants to deprive the other fellow of a real or innocent pleasure, nor do we want to see him deceived and buncoed. We want no laws passed preventing the use of tobacco. If there isn't enough information here to convince the victim, no means of force can possibly do the job. They must be convinced by their own reasoning. What is being said here, is in the nature of "throwing a rope to a drowning man" — he must "save" himself. To the non-user of tobacco this book simply serves - if at all additional information. To the "cigarette-fiend" it will serve no purpose as he or she has a fixed belief that they are immune and that all others are subject to danger but themselves. Their name is Mr. or Miss Knowitall. They grin at warnings, laugh at the advice of their best friends and turn your fatherly appeal into a joke. They set their foolish will against the knowledge and experience of the scientific and business world, all of which is but a symptom of their paranoic condition. They can think for themselves. They will walk a mile for a cigarette.

It is utterly outside the question to say that one person is just as good as another, who does not smoke. No comparisons can be made along this line, as we are all individual beings. But one thing is certain, i.e.: anyone is better for not having used tobacco and no matter how good they may be with its use, they would be still better without it. 

Not all the symptoms described here are to be found in every smoker, but every smoker has more than one symptom. 

Assuming that tobacco is, approximately, the middle of the ladder of injurious health practices, involving coffee, tea, tobacco, and alcohol, an interest awakened in this one will go a long way toward breaking down the others. 

Knock out the rung or prop of tobacco and the others are sure to be weakened, and may collapse which will be explained in detail later on. 

This subject has been discussed with many employees, strangers, casual acquaintances, and they took to it like the proverbial duck to water. They did not assume that I was trying to reform them, but rather that I was trying to be helpful by giving them some information that they should have had years ago. While you may not approach your friends and employees in the same blunt manner used here, it does seem possible that you can work up something along these lines that anyone will be glad to accept and by doing this you will not only improve their health, but increase their interest in life and productivity, with no more effort on their part, than at present. After all, they would resent being played for a sucker by the tobacco companies, if they realized what their real interest in this matter is, and the big money being made out of these habit-forming products at the expense of the consumers' health. 13

The majority of people believe that tobacco is harmless and should be taken for granted as an every day occurrence; a part of a man's necessary conduct; a kind of "personal distinction." If this is so, how is it that athletes discontinue tobacco when training, and the real intelligent athlete does without at all times? The average man, who has done without at all times, at the age of 60 looks like 45. 

When you consider the money you can save; the better health you can enjoy; willpower you can create, surely you want these benefits? If for no other reasons, these are important and of vital interest.

THE BUSINESS MAN'S CALENDAR 

A discussion of the calendar and proposed changes Our present twelve month calendar - from a business man's point of view - is one of the clumsiest and most ridiculous methods imaginable of measuring time, regulating and conducting one's business. It is so bad that 800 representative companies in America and England today, are using a 13 month calendar as a compromise. Our present calendar was never intended to be used in business, as we know business today. It is conceivable, could the originators visit us, they would laugh and sneer at our being so stupid as to even think of using it for comparative business statements. No two months are alike because of variations in the number of days or number of Saturdays and Sundays and the relationship one to the other. A quarterly statement cannot be made with anything like a reasonable degree of accuracy as the previous quarter may have 91 days while the current quarter will have only 90. 

To compare the current quarter with the same quarter of last year, will show variations because of the location of Saturday or Sunday. 

It is not necessary to go into the entire history of our calendar here but briefly, it may be said - in order to show how ridiculous our calendar really is, in this day and age that it was first brought into line, as we know it today, by Julius Caesar and in order to satisfy his vanity, one day was taken away from the month of February and placed at the end of Julius (July). When 

Augustus Caesar came into office, he did not want Julius to have "anything on him" so he took another day off February and placed it on Augustus (August) which was his name month. By 1732 the months were so far out of line with the rotation of the earth around the sun that they had to drop eleven days from the calendar. The months were formed and represent a compliment to the moon should be spelled moonth but even the moonths do not represent anything. 

The rotation of the moon is about 29 days and we do not have months of that duration. As you well know, some have 28 days, others 30 and 31 and these variations are caused in trying to satisfy the ego of ancient Roman Emporers plus a desire to make the months (moonths) fit in with the yearly rotation of the earth.

It is conceivable that man, antidating the Babylonian and Egyptian civilizations, paid considerably more attention to the moon than the sun, because the moon was continually changing its shape and position in the sky while the sun remained more constant.

This, together with the fact that man, in the daytime, could see his way around, whether the sun was visable or not, made the moon a vital part of early man's life. It is also conceivable that, to early man, the appearance of the moon from one stage to a return to the same stage of completeness, which we now call month, was early man's conception of a YEAR. If not, how can we account for the age of Methuselah being recorded as 969 years whereas 80 years would be more reasonable as we know "years" today; Adam approximately 77 instead of 930. Noah 79 instead of 950. The whole system of computing time was in the hands of the priests or spiritual advisors, no matter what country a man happens to be in, until the third or fourth century A. D. 76

and this was as it should have been, because the common people were absolutely unable to do this work or keep such records. 

The month to month record, apparently became monotonous as there is evidence that five months were combined into a "year" form of computing time into which we see Abraham aged 73 instead of 175 and Isaac 75 instead of 180 years. These five month periods correspond to the five fingers on a man's hand and the five toes on the feet, which was about all the mathematics one man could handle at that time. Later on, it evidently dawned upon the priests or "keeper of the records" that the five month "years" did not conform to the seasons of the year as they added one more month, making six "years" twice a year, which fits in much better with the physical year as we know it today. This would make Jacob's age 73 instead of 147 as stated in biblical lore. After the return of the Israelites from Egypt, you will find the ages of men recorded according to our schedule of today. 

The very early Romans used a ten month calendar. It was not until the seventh century B. C. that the Roman king Uuma added the months January and February, using 30 days for each month but even this was not satisfactory, because the year then, only consisted of 354 days and by the time Julius Caesar came on the scene, the calendar was a very confused method of recording time and history. When Caesar visited and conquered Egypt, about 46 B. C., he saw so many advantages to the Egyptian calendar, over his own, that he adopted it in principle but not in total as it was not consistent with Roman custom or superstition to tolerate even numbers; uneven numbers were considered lucky. 

As a compromise, every other month was made of 31 days whereas the Egyptian was 30 days plus five feast days or holidays, at the end of the year. 

This made the most accurate calendar civilization had up to that time as they were only one day off every three years considering the day we now call 

Leap Year, which was overcome by taking one day away from February. This changed the Roman conception of the calendar from a Moon system of calculation to a Sun method of computation, the problem now being to make these two methods blend or dovetail into each other. The sun was a very important part of man's activities because he saw, in its return from the winter solstice on December 22, the return of life - the "awakening of spring" as we refer to it today. This date of December 22 seems, in fact should be, the most logical and reasonable day to begin our new year as we, figuratively, get away with a new or fresh start at that time, but the Romans, from antiquity, had always started a new year with a "new" moon and, therefore, could not see any particular advantage to changing it at this time inasmuch as they did owe a particular homage to the moon for its light giving qualities at night and this was the only effective lighting system they had. The new moon, arriving on or about January 1st, was the beginning of the new month and year. There was also a system in effect which recognized March 25th as the year's beginning, because of the growing season being at hand. 

There is considerable evidence that the Babylonians and Egyptians experimented with an eight and ten day week, but to all intents and purposes the seven day week was used for historical purposes, this being adopted officially by Emperor Constantine in the year 325 A. D., at which time the Christian Sun (Sunday) was officially recognized as the day of rest although Sun- 77

day is the first day of the week while the Sabbath (Saturday) is the seventh.

This seems incongruous inasmuch as we rest before we start to work (start the week) rather than after our week's work is done which would be in accordance with the biblical admonition, but since that time the seven day week has never been seriously, if at all, questioned. 

As the knowledge of the people increased, it was noticed that the seasons of Spring, Summer, Autumn, and Winter were considerably out of line with the rotation of the earth around the sun, and while the straightening out of this feature had been talked of for a long time, it was not until the organizing ability of Pope Gregory XIII in 1582 came into play that the discrepancy was taken care of, which by this time was ten days too much. Naturally, the Catholic countries fell into line at once and dropped the ten days on Friday, October 5, 1582, or in other words, Friday, October 5th became Friday, October 15th, 1582. France adopted the change on Friday, December 10th which became 

Friday, December 20, 1582. Protestant Germany did not accept the change until 1700. England and the colonies, including the U. S., did not accept the changed calendar until September, 1752, which by this time accumulated eleven days, making it necessary to drop eleven days, which was done on 

Thursday, September 3rd, this day being corrected to read Thursday, September 14, 1752. Turkey, Russia, Rumania, Servia and Greece waited until

October, 1923, which by this time amounted to 13 days, they dropping the first thirteen days of the month thus, starting the month with Sunday, October 14, 1923, and this is the calendar the most of the world is using today. 

It may be interesting to note here, that while the family records will show Washington as having been born February 11th, apparently, the change in calendar reckoning was applied later inasmuch as we use February 22nd. Playing cards, of today, are simply the weekly time table or calendar of the ancient Egyptian Priests who found these more convenient to handle, since the early records were engraved on huge stone slabs. With the coming of papyrus, the weekly records were more easily handled and cared for in the temple. 

I have gone into the historical and factual side of our calendar at considerable length, simply to show how crudely it was constructed and especially how little part the month or moonth played in the whole set-up as applied to our doing business today or in other words, had it not been for the desire of primitive people to pay homage to and recognize time by the moon, the months would never have been heard of.

The Sun and Moon were man's first form of worship. When these failed to appear because of weather conditions, man asked (prayed) for their return. 

The moon was man's first "electric light." It was his guiding light at night, the first street lighting system. Aside from its alleged influence on agriculture and tides, the moon is the source of much poetry and romance. Other than this, it is hard to say what further importance we can ascribe to it. Incidentally, the only reason Easter is a roving date is; to permit ancient people to travel by night to Jerusalem for their Spring festival or holiday, by the light of the moon a discouraging factor to robbers and highwaymen. Not even the farmers of today pay serious attention to the moonths, so why should Business Men, in industrial lines, to whom the moon means absolutely nothing? 

The question, naturally, arises, "Why do business men need months at 78

all?" Why can't we talk in terms of weeks, ignoring the months entirely?

This would not interfere in any way, shape or manner with those social, political or religious groups desiring to use them as at present. The moon or month does not play an important part in our business world today and we can well afford to ignore it. It is just as easy to say, 26 weeks as 6 months. 

It is just as easy to understand that the first quarter means, and consists of 13 weeks as to say 3 months. It is just as understandable and convenient to say "the second 13 weeks", which represents the second quarter, as any other designation we have at the present time, and so it is with the third and also the fourth quarter. To designate our quarters by weeks, ignoring the month feature, is so much more accurate, each quarter having the same number of days, with Saturday and Sunday falling in sequence, which, as said before, is not true at the present time. These differences affect business materially, especially reports issued by large corporations and stock exchanges. 

Imagine a large Oil company trying to make comparative payroll or overhead figures with last month's or last year's figures? The number of days and the location of these days are different in each period, the result being too obvious to require discussing further. As for the employee, especially those on a monthly basis, he receives the same pay whether he works 28, 30 or 31 days. 

If the employee is worth his pay for 28 days work, then he is working three days for nothing in a 31 day month. On the other hand, if the employee is only worth 31 days pay, then he receives a bonus or gratuitous emolument equivalent to three days pay. The same thing happens with rents or anything else that is figured on a monthly basis, by business men. 

This being primarily a Business age in which we live, it is most fitting that we should have a calendar, form or method of computing time that is best suited to our business life, the age in which we live and not the Dark Ages. All business done today is done on a daily and weekly basis or multiple thereof and wherever a monthly term is used, it is more of a compliment to a worn out term, system, or respect for the aged, rather than a conviction that we are using an accurate system of conducting our business. 

Ignoring the months, in conducting one's business, will not interfere with any use which is being made of today's calendar in any line, other than business. Even the business man, by ignoring the monthly calendar in business, can still use it in his home and social life and certainly if Business. Men agree to do business on this basis, surely no one outside of business should object. 

There has been a movement under way, for several years, with the objective of establishing a calendar of 13 months which would mean four weeks in each month, making a total of 364 days for the year. One day must be provided for, which would not be a part of any week or month, the same to apply in the case of Leap Year. Another proposed change in the calendar is known as the "World Calendar", which provides 31 days in the months of January, April, July, and October, all other months having 30 days, making a total of 364 days for the year. This calendar would also make it necessary to provide an extra day at the end of the year which would be intercalated between December 30 and January 1st; the extra day of Leap Year being sandwiched in between June 30 and July 1st. There are many other schemes for rearranging the months but the two above seem to be the most popular and 79

while the former appears to be the most practical from a Business Man's point of view, both of these illustrate how easy it is for a business man to do business without a monthly calendar of any kind. 

Our present calendar begins each year with a named day later than the previous year (two days in Leap Year) which is the result of the impossibility of dividing 7 into 365, an equal number of times. This one and two day difference can readily be taken care of if some authoritative body such as a Stock Exchange or Chamber of Commerce will require all companies or corporations making reports, shall have an extra day on their last report of the year and for Leap Year, provide an extra day at the end of the 26 weeks at the end of one half of the year. This should be a very simple matter to place into effect and if the larger companies and corporations do this, the little fellows will fall in line without any trouble. i.e. Figuring time by months, in business or otherwise, belongs to a previous age, an age when the Do-Do bird, Ox Cart and the belief that the earth was flat, was popular. Figuring time by months, belongs to the poets, government employees figuring tides, lovers and song-writers, but not to the live, wideawake business man today. Not even the farmers of today, take our calendar seriously.

I believe every Business Man owes it to himself and business in general to see that this condition is changed and that we do not flounder around in the sea of uncertainty any longer.

It's up to you, reader, to talk this matter over with your friends and associates. Do this and your efforts will soon bear fruit.
