11. FRAMEWORK FOR A NUCLEAR PARK. 

In addition to an adequate site, the nature of which has aleady been discussed, a successful nuclear park requires a manageent in being, a plan of operations, and a legal basis for developent, operation and regulation.

Many excellent industrial parks have been developed in the nited States, particularly since the end of World War II. The uclear park will not differ fundamentally from these except that will be larger to provide physical dispersion and exclusion, it ill have some special features such as a plan for nuclear hazard ontrol, and it will be state-owned. Close attention should be aid to the experience of the United States Atomic Energy Comission in setting up its reactor testing station in Idaho, beginning 1951, and to that of industrial developers in establishing indusial parks such as those along Route 128 near Boston.

APPENDIX B. 

PRELIMINARY LOCATION ANALYSIS. 

PROFESSOR SAUL B. COHEN. 

Locating the proposed Atomic Energy Service and Manufactur ing Park within the Commonwealth presents unique problems. 

While the value of the park to industry and to the people of the Commonwealth in general can scarcely be overemphasized, the effects upon the economic and social well-being of the people in its vicinity are difficult to assess in advance. Even the acreage needs of the park are not rigid. They can be adjusted in accordance with a variety of factors, and certain limitations on land can be com pensated for by greater containment measures around the facilities The first decision to be made is that of the general location of the park. From an appraisal of the general location of existing and planned facilities of similar nature, it appears desirable that the park be located from thirty to fifty miles from a large metropolitan area. Advantage can thus be taken of industrial users, educational institutions, labor force, good residential areas and facilities for management and technicians, and integrated transportation net works. 

Within the Commonwealth, Springfield, Worcester, Lowell

Lawrence, Fall River-New Bedford and Greater Boston merit com sideration. But neither Springfield nor Worcester have the larg number of industrial and educational atomic energy users found in eastern Massachusetts. Nor are they as favorably located with respect to ports to service nuclear-powered vessels or to expor small power plants.

Locating the park in eastern Massachusetts is preferable becaus the general location requirements that have been cited can best by fulfilled there. Moreover, the Atomic Energy Park should help t attract new industry to Massachusetts, and the most favorable are for general industrial growth is eastern Massachusetts, especiall circumferential Route 128 and the major expressways radiatin outward from Boston. Thus, the park in particular and industr in general should stimulate one another if located to mutual ad vantage.
