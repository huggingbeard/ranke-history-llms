Change in Bank Control  Instructions The Change in Bank Control Act of 1978, Section 7(j) of the Federal Deposit Insurance Act, (12 U.S.C. 

1817(j)), as amended by the Money Laundering Control Act of 1986 and the Financial Institutions Reform, Recovery, and Enforcement Act of 1989 (FIRREA), provides that no person, acting directly or indirectly or through or in concert with one or more persons, shall acquire control of a national bank through a purchase, assignment, transfer, pledge, or other disposition of its voting stock unless the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) has been given 60 days prior written notice of such proposed acquisition and, within that time period, has not issued a notice disapproving the proposed acquisition or extended the review period. 

This information is filed pursuant to 12 U.S.C. 1817(j) and 12 CFR 5.50. To expedite processing of this notice by the OCC, all questions must be answered with complete and accurate information, which is subject to verification. The OCC may request additional information, clarification, or verification of any of the information submitted. 

Type or print clearly when completing this notice. If an answer is "no," "none," or "not applicable," so state. Failure to make an entry in response to any question renders your notice incomplete and delays the commencement of the 60-day period in which the OCC generally must act. To the extent possible, questions should be answered in the space provided; use insert pages as necessary. Information on insert pages should be keyed by number and letter to the appropriate question(s). Submit five copies of the notice and any attachments directly to the District Deputy Comptroller of the district in which the main office of the bank to be acquired is located. (Notices regarding multinational companies and their affiliates should be submitted to the Deputy Comptroller for Multinational Banking in Washington, D.C.) At least one copy must bear the original signature of the acquiring party. 

Material changes in financial or other conditions while this notice is pending decision and consummation must be reported promptly to the OCC. 

The notice must be filed in accordance with the publication requirements set forth in 12 CFR 5.50(h). 

Generally, within 10 days of receipt of the OCC's letter that the notice is technically complete, the acquiring party must publish an announcement of the proposed acquisition and within 20 days provide the district office with a copy of the published announcement and a newspaper publisher's certification or affidavit. Some notices may be exempted from the publication requirement as provided in 12 

CFR 5.50(h)(1)(i) and (ii). 4

Instructions for Filing an

Application To Relocate a Head Office Domestic Branch 

Refer to the laws, regulations, policies and procedures contained in 12 CFR 5, and particularly section 5.40 and 12 U.S.C. 36, in the Comptroller's Manual for National Banks. 

This application is used for proposals to relocate a head office or a domestic (brick and mortar) branch. It may not be used to request approval to relocate a CBCT branch. To request such approval, file an "Application to Establish a Domestic Branch, Seasonal Agency or CBCT" (see Domestic 

Branches, Seasonal Agencies and CBCT Branches section of the Comptroller's Manual for Corporate 

Activities). 

1. Answer questions in the spaces provided or by attaching additional pages as necessary. Please use 8 1/2" x 11" sheets. If questions are not applicable, please so indicate on the application. 

2. Submit to the Deputy Comptroller for the district in which your bank is located (multinational or their affiliates submit to Multinational Banking in Washington) a signed original and one copy of the application, with a check made payable to the Comptroller of the Currency to cover the filing fee. 

Refer to 12 CFR 5.5 and current fee schedule in Notice of the Comptroller of the Currency Fees. 

3. To facilitate processing, send application by hand, or by registered mail, return receipt requested.

Competitive Analysis 

Applicants for mergers that fail the OCC Quick Check Merger Screen and applicants that elect not to use the screen must provide their own analysis to demonstrate that the proposed merger would not have a substantial adverse effect on competition. 

The following outline may help applicants organize their data and analyses into persuasive arguments. Other formats and presentations may be used if applicants believe they better or more concisely state their cases. In some cases, applicants may need to address only some parts of the information outlined below to make persuasive arguments about the competitive effects of their mergers.

I. Market Definition 

A. Geographic markets

Define and justify what would be appropriate geographic market areas. You may consider alternative political boundaries, geophysical boundaries, business concentrations, residential concentrations, commuting patterns, and sociocultural concentrations. As part of your analysis, you should consider trends and events that will affect the size of the geographic market you identify, e.g., plant openings, new housing projects, and actual customer behavior. A market area should include that area from which at least 75 percent or more of the office's business is obtained.

B. Product Markets 

Define what products or services are appropriate for the analysis and what would be appropriate geographic markets for these products and services. Areas of indirect competition may be defined, e.g., where advertising and mobility in a large metropolitan area keep rates competitive. 

Applicants should support any assertions that they plan to specialize in unique product or service markets.

II. Competition A. Existing Competition 

Identify existing competition in the geographic market. Maps showing the location of competitors in geographic markets are required. If it is inappropriate to consider only commercial banks as competitors, state why and justify the inclusion of other institutions providing financial products and services in the analysis. The justification should relate to the product market you plan to serve. Thrift institutions will be considered as competitors when they are legally able to provide similar financial products and services and are able to affect the marginal prices of the relevant products in the market. Discuss recent product and service innovations and plans for new products and services within the market. If a particular set of products and services is to be emphasized, indicate dollar volume and market share projections for the next five years. 

B. Potential Competition 

If no significant legal or economic reasons prevent the entry of potential competitors into the markets, state why, give evidence of the market's ability to support competition, and justify your identification of potential competition. Show evidence of market size, the attractiveness of the market, the banks that could enter the market if they so desired, the history of market penetration, and drop-outs to justify your argument. 91

 Quick Check Merger Screen Overview 

A competitive analysis is required for all applications for mergers, consolidations, or purchase and assumption transactions between unaffiliated operating institutions. A competitive analysis is also required for transactions between affiliated operating institutions if the transaction which resulted in the affiliation was not subject to review by a federal financial regulatory agency under 12 U.S.C. 1828 (c), 1817 (j), 1842, or 1843.

The OCC Quick Check Merger Screen provides applicant banks and the OCC with a simple-to-use procedural device for quickly sorting out those mergers, consolidations, or purchase and assumptions that clearly do not have significant adverse effects on competition. Mergers that pass the screen's tests do not require a specialized OCC competitive effects review. 

All applicants are encouraged to test their proposed mergers against the Quick Check Merger Screen; however, they are not required to do so. Applicants filing proposals that would pass the screen's tests may elect to develop their own analysis of the competitive effects of a proposed merger. However, such applications will be subject to an OCC competitive effects review. 

There is no presumption by the OCC about the effects on competition of a proposed merger that does not pass the Quick Check Merger Screen's tests. However, applicants proposing mergers that do not pass the screen's tests must develop and submit their own analysis of the competitive effects of the proposed merger. In some cases, the Quick Check Merger Screen's tests supplemented by additional explanation or clarification may suffice for an independent analysis. For other cases, general guidelines are provided in "Guidelines for an Independent Competitive Analysis." 

Overview 

The Quick Check Merger Screen is divided into three parts. Parts 1 and 2 test for the competitive effects of a proposed merger. Part 3 records the results of the merger screen tests. 

Part 1, the Expansive Market Test, analyzes the level and changes in the level of concentration in broadly defined markets where the merging institutions both have offices. The market areas used are the predefined geographic markets developed by the Federal Reserve Board (FRB). Offices include head offices and branch offices. Market share of the resulting bank (plus affiliates) is calculated for each market. For those markets where resulting bank market share exceeds 20 percent, Herfindahl- 

Hirschman indices are calculated to measure concentration. If market share is less than 20 percent, or if market share exceeds 20 percent and Herfindahl-Hirschman indices are within Department of 

Justice Guidelines, the merger passes the Expansive Market Test. Part 2, the Proximity Market Test, measures competition in the area immediately surrounding each office of the merging institutions. 

Proximity markets are defined by drawing a circle with a radius of 5 or 10 miles, depending on population density, around each office. The applicant identifies those offices that have an office of the other merging institution within their proximity market. If there are no proximity markets where two or more merging institutions or their affiliates have offices, the merger passes the Proximity Market Test. If there are proximity markets where two or more merging institutions or their affiliates have offices, the applicant calculates the resulting bank's market share in those proximity markets. The merger passes the Proximity Market Test, if for those proximity markets, the market share of the resulting bank is less than 20 percent.

Market Areas 

The Quick Check Merger Screen uses two definitions of market area. The first definition is the FRB market area. It is used for the "Expansive Market Test". A list of current FRB market areas may be obtained from the Federal Reserve Bank for the area where the merging institutions' offices are located. 

In some cases, offices of the merging institutions may not be located in any predefined FRB market area. An independent market analysis must be developed for such cases. UNGIT AV 111

Quick Check Merger Screen Overview 

The second market definition considers the area that immediately surrounds each office of the merging institutions, the proximity market area. It is used for the "Proximity Market Test." For those offices located in a city, the radius of the proximity market area is determined by the population density of the city. For those offices not located in a city, the radius of the proximity market area is determined by the population density of the county. If the population density for an office is over 3,000 per square mile, no proximity market is identified. If the population density is 300 to 3,000 per square mile, the radius for the proximity market is 5 miles. If the population density is less than 300 per square mile, the radius for the proximity market is 10 miles. 

City and county population density is determined by the Census Bureau of the United States Department of Commerce every 10 years. Applicants should use the most recently published figures from the Census State and Metropolitan Area Databook or the Census County and City Databook. Both census books are available at most libraries. 

Deposit Data

To conduct the concentration analysis required in the Expansive Market Test and the Proximity Market Test, applicants must identify all commercial banks and thrifts that have offices in the market areas in which the merging institutions compete. This information generally can be obtained from the Summary of Deposits information in the Bank and Branches Data Book published by the FDIC for federally insured commercial banks and mutual savings banks and from the Office Deposit Report produced on tape by the Office of Thrift Supervision for SAIF insured savings and loans and federal savings banks. The same information also is available from several commercial vendors who republish it. Applicants should use the most recently published data in making their analyses. 112

New Bank Charters Operating Plan Instructions Application and Notices Operating Plan General Instructions 

The operating plan is prepared by the organizing group and reviewed by the OCC to determine whether approval should be given to organize a national bank. The plan identifies the bank's markets, its proposed services, the management capabilities the bank will have, and its strategies for profitability.

Organizing groups may submit for review operating plans in any format they wish. The OCC finds it useful when the information is presented in the following format (see Documents-OCC, Confidential Memorandum Checklist, New Bank Charters). This is the OCC checklist for the review of operating plans. It is included so you will know most factors in your operating plan that the OCC reviewers will assess. If the OCC finds that the business strategies are reasonable and coherent, that the proposed directors and management can successfully carry out the operating plan and operate in a safe and sound manner, and that the bank will be adequately capitalized, the OCC will grant preliminary approval to begin organizing a national bank. 

Some of the information listed in the optional format for an operating plan may be omitted for certain experienced organizing groups. For example, existing bank holding companies and groups that have been affiliated for three years with the same existing bank or chain banking group may not have to complete or submit Section V-Leadership and Management, Section VI-Market Analysis, and Sec tion VII-Plans and Objectives. Operating plan requirements for any organizing group should be confirmed with the OCC at the pre-filing meeting or over the telephone with the District Director for 

Analysis (DDA) or the Deputy Comptroller for Multinational Banking (DCMNB). The OCC looks to the holding company in deciding holding company sponsored applications. The proposal should discuss and disclose the activities of the holding company and the bank. The discussion should be submitted by established and newly forming holding companies. 

In proposals involving formation of a new holding company or an existing holding company where holding company debt will be used to capitalize the bank, the operating plan must clearly demonstrate that debt service requirements for the first few years of the bank's operations can be met without reliance on cash flows of any kind from the new bank. 

If existing trust operations will be merged into a new trust company or form the basis for the de novo trust company's operations, copies of the latest three years Trust Call Reports must be submitted with the application and factored into the operating projections. 

Groups forming a national bankers' bank must submit all parts of the operating plan except Section III-Community Reinvestment Act Statement. In lieu of individual personal financial information, participating banks should submit most recent quarter and year-end Reports of Condition and Income and Expenses, annual reports and 10Ks, if applicable. Proposed directors and executive officers for a bankers' bank should submit the biographical information in all instances and the personal financial information when the person is not currently affiliated with a national bank and known to the OCC. 

The operating plan should present data which accurately reflects the economic condition of the delineated market and addresses statutory and regulatory changes which may affect the operations of the proposed bank. Proposals should reflect the "realities" of the marketplace. An operating plan should contain sufficient information to demonstrate that the proposed bank has a reasonable likelihood of success. In this regard, a detailed listing of all assumptions used in preparing the operating plan should be attached to the submission (e.g., a margin analysis and cost of funds). After a technically complete application is filed, the OCC will not request additional information unless concerns arise that the applicants could not have reasonably been expected to address. 143

Biographical and Financial Reports Instructions  General 

The biographical and financial reports must be prepared by each person seeking entry into the national banking system as an organizer and proposed organizing director, executive officer, principal shareholder or manager of a federal branch or agency. Any person who proposes to assume such a position must file this report PRIOR to assuming the position. 

All information requested must be typed or printed. All questions must be answered completely. If the answer is "no," "none," or "not applicable," so state. If an item of information is unknown to you, so state. To the extent possible, questions should be answered in the spaces provided with insert pages used when necessary. Information on insert pages should be keyed by number and letter to the appropriate questions. All dollar amounts should be in U.S. dollars. An inaccurate or incomplete report will be rejected.

Confidentiality may be requested if the reasons are clearly and completely stated at the time the reports are filed. Determinations on questions regarding requests for confidentiality and discretion in releasing information under the Freedom of Information Act reside with the OCC. Generally, information of a personal nature will be held in strict confidence. Portions of the Biographical Report or Financial Report may be shared with the other federal financial institution regulators when deemed necessary. Confidential information should be bound separately from the public portion of the application or notice.

The OCC may request additional information, clarification or verification of any information submitted. 

The accuracy and completeness of the information will be verified.

NOTICE.

Organization Costs Documents-Applicant Organization Costs Incurred by Newly Chartered National Banks New Bank Charters Purpose 

This sets forth the policy of the Comptroller of the Currency on the accounting treatment for organization costs incurred by new national banks. Policy

Bank organization costs, incurred by newly chartered banks, shall be capitalized and amortized over a period not to exceed five years. Organization costs are defined as the direct costs incurred to incorporate and charter a bank. Examples include, but are not limited to, professional (such as legal, accounting, consulting) fees and printing costs related to the chartering or incorporation process, OCC filing fees, and the cost of economic impact studies. 

Pre-opening expenses such as salaries and employee benefits, rent, depreciation, supplies, directors' fees, training, travel, postage, telephone, are not considered organization costs, and should not be capitalized. Rather these items should be expensed in accordance with the Instruction-Consolidated Reports of Condition and Income. 

This policy does not permit capitalization of allocated internal costs (e.g., management salaries). 

Further, this policy does not alter existing initial minimum capital requirements or regulations (12 CFR 5.20(c)(4)(iii)) under which new banks may be prohibited from repaying organizing groups for expenses that are clearly excessive.

Reporting 

A listing of organization expenses should be attached to any proxy material to permit those shareholders voting by proxy to be adequately informed. 

Capitalized organization costs should be included with "Intangible Assets" in the Report of Condition. 

These costs shall be amortized over a period not to exceed five years using the straight-line method. 

The periodic amortization should be included in "Other Noninterest Expense" in the Report of Income. 265
