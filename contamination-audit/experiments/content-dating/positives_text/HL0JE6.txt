It is appropriate in this first annual report for the Commission to set forth its purpose and general area of responsibility. The 

Commission is a permanent organization, representing the public and all levels of government, established to increase the effectiveness of the American system of Federal government. As a permanent bi-partisan body with its broad and representative base, the Commission will examine areas of governmental responsibility and the major problems that arise in the division and sharing of this responsibility. 

It will give reflective, cooperative guidance to all levels of government in the important task of defining these responsibilities and coordinating actions within our Constitutional framework. 

This work of definition is important. A Federal system is a complex form of government. This complexity has grown greatly in recent decades and intergovernmental relations have changed in many ways. Further changes and mounting complexity are in evidence for the years ahead as population and urbanization grow rapidly and scientific developments raise new problems. In this context governmental responsibility will necessarily grow, at all levels. The more work that government does, the more important it is that areas of responsibility be clearly defined and properly shared within the framework and traditions of our Federal system.

5. Encourage discussion and study at an early stage of emerging public problems that are likely to require intergovernmental cooperation; 

6. Recommend, within the framework of the Constitution, the most desirable allocation of governmental functions, responsibilities, and revenues among the several levels of government; and 7.

Recommend methods of coordinating and simplifying tax laws and administrative practices to achieve a more orderly and less competitive fiscal relationship between the levels of government and to reduce the burden of compliance for taxpayers.

This is a broad mission. The Commission will approach its work selectively and with an objective point of view. A few guide lines may be suggested. 

1. The Commission will be equally concerned with the problems of the Federal, State, county, and municipal governments as they relate to each other. It will be truly inter-governmental. The public interest is paramount as the Commission undertakes its work. 

This may be stated simply: to increase the effectiveness of the 

Federal system in this country. 

2. The Commission will approach its work objectively. It is not dedicated to an increase or a decrease in the role of government as a whole, or to any particular level in this system. As it gives consideration to present functions and responsibilities and to emerging problems, the Commission will endeavor to frame its recommendations on the merits of the case as it sees them. 

Depending on the circumstances it might recommend expansion, or contraction, or transfer, or elimination of functions and responsibilities. The Commission is not a proponent of either centralization or decentralization, more government spending or less. will with objectivity examine facts, weigh issues, and advise appropriate action. The first meeting of the Advisory Commission, held on December 14, 1959, stressed the importance of a continuing examination of intergovernmental relations, both administrative and fiscal, to assure the maintenance of an effective Federal system, in keeping with our traditions and Constitutional framework. All members of the Commission present at this meeting voiced strong support for the Commission and emphasized the need for objective, realistic examination of present and emerging problems of an intergovernmental nature. Representatives of all levels of government indicated their eagerness to cooperate and to help in the Commission's work. This was a propitious start which augurs well for the important work this Commission will undertake. 

At its next meeting on February 10, 1960, the Commission will consider a tentative work plan outlining some immediate and -4-

longer range problems that need its careful attention. If this plan, with whatever modifications that seem appropriate, is adopted, the Commission will start work as quickly as possible. It will approach its work in a deliberative manner, yet in a way calculated to get realistic and practical answers to pressing problems, for action as soon as possible. This we consider to be the intent of Congress, the hope of the President, and the expectation of public officials at all levels and the public in general.
