9814. Department created. There is hereby created a department of social welfare in which shall be administered the laws relating to old age assistance, aid to dependent children, aid to the blind, adult blind, vocational rehabilitation for the blind, adult crippled aid, child welfare services including adoptions and Kinstead Home, committed children and state beneficiaries, including cooperation with the Federal Security Agency and acceptance of federal funds. 

1947, No. 187, §§ 1, 3. 

9815. Social welfare board created; members; term; duties. There is hereby created a board of social welfare consisting of three members who shall be appointed biennially in the month of February by the governor with the advice and consent of the senate. The members shall be appointed for a term of six years or for the unexpired portion thereof, in such manner that not more than one term shall expire in the same biennium. The governor shall designate the chairman of the board. The duties of the board are as follows: 

I. With the approval of the governor, appoint the commissioner of social welfare;

II. Confer with the commissioner on matters of policy; 

III. Act as a fair hearing board on appeals from decisions of the commissioner on old age assistance, aid to the blind and aid to dependent children; and 

IV. Make a biennial report with recommendations to the governor and to the general assembly. 

1947, No. 187, § 2. 

9816. Meetings of board. The board shall hold such meetings as may be required for the performance of its duties and may promulgate rules and regulations of procedure. The board shall meet at such times and places as may be designated by the chairman of the board. Such chairman shall call a meeting upon

ހހހހ the written request of any two members and upon the written request of the governor. 

1947, No. 187, § 2. 

9817. Commissioner; duties. With the approval of the governor, the board of social welfare shall appoint a commissioner of social welfare who shall hold office for four years unless removed for good cause, or until his successor is appointed and qualified. The commissioner shall administer the laws assigned to the department for administration by 9814. He shall make and promulgate such rules and regulations as are necessary to administer such laws. He shall make rules for the custody, use and preservation of the records, papers, files and communications of the department, restricting the use or disclosure of information contained therein to purposes directly connected with the administration of the department. 

1947, No. 187, §§ 3, 5. 

9818. Same; assistants, merit system. The commissioner of social welfare shall appoint all other necessary assistants and fix their duties and salaries in accordance with the established standards. He shall make rules and regulations for the establishment of or the participation in a merit system and for maintaining such system. All persons holding merit system status in the agencies administering the programs designated in this chapter shall retain and hold merit system status in the department of social welfare. 

1947, No. 187, § 5. 

OLD AGE ASSISTANCE. 

9819. Qualifications. Old age assistance shall be granted to any person who: 

I. Has resided in the state of Vermont, if a United States citizen for three out of the ten years immediately preceding application for assistance or if a noncitizen has resided continuously in the United States for a period of twenty-five years and in Vermont for three during the ten years immediately preceding application for assistance; 

II. Is not at the date of receiving aid, an inmate of any public institution;

III. Has not adequate income when joined with the income of such person's spouse to provide a reasonable subsistence compatible with decency and health; and has no person legally liable and able so to support him; 

IV. Has not made an assignment or transfer of property or income for the purpose of qualifying for assistance; 

V. Has attained the age of sixty-five years. 

1947, No. 193, § 1. 1945, No. 54, § 1. 1937, No. 65, § 1. 1935, No. 82, § 2. 

9820. Secrecy of lists. Whenever lists are made available under state law to other officials or agencies, such officials or agencies shall have adequate power to establish and enforce effective rules and regulations to prevent their publication or their use for purposes not directly connected with the administration of Old Age 

Assistance, Aid to the Blind, Aid to Dependent Children and Child Welfare Service. 

1941, No. 59, § 3. 113 Vt. 1. 

9821. Same; penalty. A person who uses or discloses public assistance lists and records for purposes not directly connected with the administration of the matters mentioned in the preceding section or contrary to the rules and regulations of the state departments shall be fined not more than $25.00. A person shall not be prosecuted for violation of such a departmental rule or regulation unless the same shall have been filed at least sixty days before such prosecution is commenced in the office of the clerk of the town or city where such person resides. 

1947, No. 202, § 10,247. 1941, No. 59, § 4.

9822. Local official. The selectmen of each town and city council of each city shall annually designate a legal voter therein to have charge of the applications for old age assistance therein and make such provision for his compensation as they may deem necessary. Immediately upon such designation, they shall inform the commissioner of social welfare of the name and address of the person so designated. 

1939, No. 72, § 3. 1935, No. 82, § 8. 

9823. Application and award. Except as hereinafter provided, application for assistance shall be made to the person so designated in the town or city in which the applicant is domiciled, and in the manner prescribed and on the form provided by the commissioner of social welfare. Such local official shall immediately investigate the applicant's condition, and within thirty days after receiving the application he shall forward it to the commissioner together with such supporting evidence, papers and recommendations as the commissioner shall require. An applicant for old age assistance may, if he desires, submit an application directly to the commissioner who shall in such event make a record of such and forward the application forthwith to such local official who shall act thereon as if such application had been originally received by him. The commissioner shall thereupon make such investigation as he deems necessary, and shall decide upon the application and fix the amount of assistance, if any. 

1939, No. 72, § 3. 1935, No. 82, § 8. 

9824. Appeal. An applicant or recipient aggrieved because of the commissioner's decision shall be entitled to appeal to the board of social welfare for a reconsideration of his action and shall be accorded reasonable notice and opportunity for a fair hearing. For the purpose of any such hearing on appeal, the chairman of the board shall have power to compel, by subpoena, the attendance and testimony of witnesses, and the production of books and papers. All witnesses shall be examined on oath, and any member of the board may administer such oath. 

1939, No. 72, § 3. 1935, No. 82, § 8. 

9825. Same; board's decision final. All such decisions of the board on appeal shall be final, provided that should the applicant's status as to property, income, resources or maintenance change subsequent to the decision so as to be materially less than when assistance was granted or denied then the applicant shall be entitled to a new hearing. 

1939, No. 72, § 3. 1935, No. 82, § 8. 

9826. Qualifications of beneficiary. Aid under this chapter shall not be granted to any person having equity in property in excess of $2,500.00 or, if married and not separated from his spouse, if the equity in their combined property exceeds $4,000.00. However, $1,000.00 in value of any house owned, used and occupied by applicant or by applicant and his spouse as his or their home shall not be considered in computing the value of his property. 

1945, No. 54, § 2. 1943, No. 48, § 1. 1935, No. 82, § 3. 

9827. Amount of assistance. The amount of assistance which any person shall receive shall be determined with due regard to the income, resources and maintenance available to the individual from whatever source derived, and with due regard to his necessary expenditures and the conditions existing in each case, but in no case shall the amount paid by the state including assistance from the United States government exceed a total of $45.00 per month or where a husband and wife living together are both applicants, which shall exceed for both $80.00 per month. 

1947, No. 193, § 2. 1945, No. 55, § 1. 1941, No. 57, § 1. 1939, No. 72, § 1. 1935, No. 82, § 4. 

9828. Payments; time. If assistance is allowed under this chapter, it shall be for the calendar month on which the application was approved by the commissioner.

Such assistance shall continue while the recipient is found qualified to receive the same. 

1945, No. 54, § 3. 1943, No. 48, § 3. 1935 S., No. 8, § 1. 1935, No. 82, § 9. 

9829. Same; warrants. The commissioner shall issue to each applicant to whom assistance is allowed a notice stating the amount of each monthly installment. On written order of the commissioner, the auditor of accounts shall issue his warrant on the state treasurer, who shall issue and forward check to such recipient in payment of each installment. The auditor of accounts and the state treasurer shall make and promulgate rules and regulations governing the issue of warrants for installments, and the issue of checks in payment of the same. 

1945, No. 54, § 3. 1943, No. 48, § 3. 1935 S., No. 8, § 1. 1935, No. 82, § 9. 

9830. Amounts, recovery of excess. The amount of the assistance may be changed at any time if the commissioner finds that the applicant's circumstances have changed. If at any time during the term of old age assistance the recipient or the recipient's spouse, if they are not separated, becomes possessed of additional income or property, the recipient of assistance shall immediately notify the commissioner of such change and the nature thereof, and the commissioner, on investigation, may either cancel or reduce the amount of assistance as the facts may then warrant. 

If, on the death of any person receiving assistance, it is found that the recipient was possessed of income or property in excess of the amount allowed by this chapter in respect of the amount of assistance granted, double the total amount of the assistance in excess of that to which the recipient was entitled may be recovered by the commissioner as a preferred claim from the estate of the recipient. In the event of such recovery an amount equivalent to the pro rata share to which the United States is equitably entitled shall be paid promptly to the designated agency of the federal government. 

1947, No. 202, § 10,256. 1946 S., No. 7, § 1. 1945, No. 54, § 4. 1935 S., No. 8, § 2. 

1935, No. 82, § 10. 

9831. Guardian. If, after investigation by the commissioner, the applicant for or recipient of assistance is found incapable of taking care of himself or his business affairs, the commissioner may direct the payment of the assistance to a guardian appointed by the probate court. 

1939, No. 72, § 4. 1935, No. 82, § 11. 

9832. Rights inalienable. All rights to old age assistance shall be inalienable by any assignment, transfer, attachment, trustee process, execution or otherwise. In the case of bankruptcy, the assistance shall not pass to or through any trustee or other persons acting on behalf of creditors. 

1935, No. 82, § 12. 

9833. Funeral expenses. If the funeral expenses do not exceed $150.00 and the estate of the deceased is insufficient to defray the same and there are no relatives legally liable to pay or assist in paying these expenses, reasonable funeral expenses for burial of a recipient of old age assistance shall be allowed and paid by the commissioner. 

1943, No. 48, § 4. 1937, No. 65, § 4. 1935, No. 82, § 13. 

9834. Cancellation. If at any time the commissioner has reason to believe that assistance has been improperly obtained, he shall cause a special investigation to be made, and may suspend payment of assistance pending the investigation. If on investigation it appears that the assistance was improperly obtained, the commissioner shall cancel the same and the person having so improperly obtained assistance shall not be eligible for reinstatement until his need has been established. 

1939, No. 72, § 5. 1935, No. 82, § 14. 

9835. Conviction of offense. If a person receiving assistance under this chapter is convicted of an offense and punished by imprisonment for one month or longer, the court imposing the sentence shall make report thereof forthwith to the

commissioner. During the time of such imprisonment, the person so imprisoned shall not be entitled to receive old age assistance and payments thereof shall be suspended for that period. If the person so imprisoned is married and living with his spouse, and if the spouse is eligible to receive assistance under this chapter, the commissioner shall award assistance to such spouse during the period of such suspension in accordance with the provisions of this chapter. 

1935, No. 82, § 15. 

9836. Penalty. A person who knowingly violates any provision of this chapter for which no penalty is specifically provided shall be fined not more than $100.00 or be imprisoned not more than sixty days, or both. If a person receiving assistance is convicted of an offense under this section, the commissioner shall cancel the assistance for a period not to exceed one year as determined by the commissioner. 

1947, No. 202, § 10,262. 1935, No. 82, § 16. 

9837. Fraud. A person shall not:

I. By means of a wilfully false statement or representation or by impersonation, or other fraudulent device obtain or attempt to obtain, or aid or abet any person to obtain assistance to which he is not entitled, or a larger amount of assistance than that to which he is justly entitled under this chapter; 

II. Dispose of property for the purpose or with the intent of obtaining assistance to which he is not entitled, or a larger amount of assistance than that to which he is entitled; or

III. Knowingly buy or aid or abet in buying, or dispose of, the property of a person receiving assistance without the consent of the commissioner. 

1935, No. 82, § 17. 

9838. Same; penalty. A person who violates a provision of the preceding section shall be fined not more than $500.00, or be imprisoned not more than one year, or both. 

1935, No. 82, § 17. 

9839. Amendment of law. Every assistance granted under the provisions of this chapter shall be deemed to be granted and shall be held subject to the provisions of any amendment or repealing act that may thereafter be passed. A recipient under this chapter shall not have any claim for compensation, or otherwise, by reason of his assistance being affected in any way by such amending or repealing act. 

1935, No. 82, § 18. 

9840. Report. The commissioner shall make to the governor and to the general assembly a report in detail of his acts and proceedings for the biennial period ending on June 30, next preceding. 

1937, No. 65, § 5. 1935, No. 82, § 19. 

9841. Report to federal agency. To obtain the benefits of any act passed by Congress, the commissioner shall submit to the social security board or the designated agency of the federal government such reports in such manner and at such times as such board or agency may require. 

1937, No. 65, § 6. 1935, No. 82, § 20. 

9842. Rate. If, at any time, the amounts received or to be received by this state from the United States for old age assistance shall be less than $20.00 per month for each person entitled to the full monthly compensation of $45.00 per month under this chapter, or shall be less than a like proportion of the amount of assistance to which those receiving less than the full allowance of $45.00 may be entitled, then the amount of the payment which the applicant would otherwise receive shall be reduced to the extent of such reduction in assistance from the United States. 

1945, No. 55, § 2. 1935, No. 82, § 21. 

9843. Lien. The amount paid as assistance to any aged person under this chapter shall constitute a lien on the property of the recipient which, upon his death,

shall be enforced against his estate; and of the amount realized from such lien an amount equivalent to the pro rata share to which the United States is equitably entitled shall be paid forthwith to the designated agency of the federal government. 

However, such a lien shall not be enforced against any real estate of the recipient while it is occupied as a home by the recipient's surviving spouse, if such marriage took place prior to April 11, 1935, and if the surviving spouse does not marry thereafter. Upon the granting of assistance by the commissioner, a notice of the statutory lien which attaches by reason of such grant shall be sent within thirty days for record to the town in which the property of the recipient is located, and the commissioner shall authorize the payment of fifty cents to the town clerk for the recording of such lien. The commissioner may discharge any such lien upon payment of the amount due, or whenever there is good cause therefor. 

1947, No. 202, § 10,270. 1946 S., No. 8, § 1. 1937, No. 65, § 7. 1935 S., No. 8, § 3. 

1935, No. 82, § 22. 

9844. Claim. On the death of a recipient of assistance, the state shall have a preferred claim against such person's estate for the amount of any assistance paid to him under the provisions of this chapter, and for the amount, if any, paid for funeral expenses, which shall be paid in priority to all other claims against such estate except probate fees and taxes. In the event of a recovery, an amount equivalent to the pro rata share to which the United States is equitably entitled shall be paid promptly to the designated agency of the federal government. 

1947, No. 202, § 10,269. 1946 S., No. 8, § 2. 1945, No. 54, § 5. 

935 S., No. 8, § 3. 1935, No. 82, § 22. 

1937, No. 65, § 7. 

9845. Lien for claims. If an applicant for assistance is married and not separated from his spouse, the commissioner shall obtain from such spouse a consent to the attachment of the liens provided for in the two preceding sections, to any property owned by such applicant and such spouse as tenants by the entireties before authorizing the payment to such applicant of any assistance under this chapter. Such lien shall thereupon become a charge upon such property, and such spouse shall take by right of survivorship subject to such liens. 

1947, No. 202, § 10,271. 1935, No. 82, § 22. 

9846. Allocation of funds. Should the funds available for old age assistance be insufficient to permit of assistance to all of those entitled to the same under the provisions of this chapter, the commissioner shall select, in his discretion, those in most urgent need of such assistance. 

1947, No. 202, § 10,272. 1943, No. 48, § 5. 1941, No. 58. 1937, No. 64. 1935 S., No. 8, § 4. 

1935, No. 82, § 23. 

9847. Construction. Nothing in this chapter shall be construed as repealing any other act or part thereof providing for the support of the poor except insofar as inconsistent therewith. The provisions of this chapter shall be construed liberally and as an additional method of supporting and providing for aged and deserving persons. If any provision of this chapter is held invalid, the validity of the remainder of the chapter shall not be affected thereby. 

1947, No. 202, § 10,273. 1935, No. 82, § 25.. 

CHARITIES. 

General Duties and Authority of Welfare Department. 

9848. Homes for children. The commissioner of social welfare or his agent shall take means to find suitable homes for all children under the care of the department of social welfare. In the case of children needing immediate relief, the commissioner or his agent shall arrange for temporary care in suitable homes which have been investigated and are approved by him, and such homes shall be paid a

reasonable amount for care and maintenance. When a suitable foster home cannot be found, such child may be placed in a licensed institution incorporated for receiving dependent or neglected children and such child's board shall be paid to such institution. 

1947, No. 202, § 10,095. 1943, No. 89, § 1. P. L. § 5415. 1933, No. 157, $.5212. 

G. L. § 7308. 1917, No. 244, § 12. 

9849. Same; visitation. Such commissioner or his agent shall visit a child, who has been placed in accordance with the preceding section, at least twice each year to ascertain the care, training and condition of the child. If the home in which the child is placed does not prove to be a suitable and proper place for such child, the commissioner may cancel the agreement with such home and take the child for placement in another home or in an institution. 

1947, No. 202, § 10,096. P. L. § 5416. 1933, No. 157, § 5113. G. L. § 7309. 1917, No. 244, § 13. 

9850. Medical and surgical treatment. If a child adjudged to be dependent, neglected or delinquent requires medical or surgical treatment, such child may be sent by the commissioner to some hospital, institution or home for such treatment. 

1947, No. 202, § 10,097. P. L. § 5417. 1933, No. 157, § 5114. G. L. § 7310. 1917, No. 244, § 14. 

1915, No. 92, § 6. 1912, No. 113, § 6. 

9851. Supervision; dependent, neglected or delinquent children. The department of social welfare shall have supervision and control of such dependent, neglected and delinquent children as it may take under its care and such as shall be committed to it by the juvenile courts, shall receive and disburse all funds which shall be given to it for charitable purposes and shall aid and assist in such charitable work as in the judgment of the commissioner of social welfare will best promote the general good of the state. The commissioner shall investigate and make report to the governor as to all public charities and in each even year shall make a report to the governor of the acts of the department. 

1947, No. 202, § 10,098. P. L. § 5418. 1933, No. 157, § 5115. G. L. § 7311. 1917, No. 244, § 7. 

9852. Same; physically defective or crippled children. If the department of social welfare finds that the parents or guardian of a physically defective or crippled child who needs medical or surgical treatment are willing to furnish such treatment but are financially unable to do so, it may provide such treatment. 

P. L. § 5419. 1933, No. 157, § 5116. 1921, No. 217, § 1. 

9853. Same; expenses; how met. The expense incurred by the department under the provisions of the preceding section shall be paid from the funds annually appropriated for the use of the department and from such funds as may be available therefor by private gift. 

1947, No. 202, § 10,100. P. L. § 5420. 1933, No. 157, § 5117. 1921, No. 217, § 2. 

9854. Power of visitation, generally. Such department shall have power of visitation in and over all institutions chartered by the state for the care of dependent classes which solicit public support for their work. 

P. L. § 5422. 1933, No. 157, § 5119. G. L. § 7313. 1917, No. 244, § 18. 

9855. Reports of overseers of poor to commissioner. The overseer of the poor in each town shall report to the commissioner of social welfare, on forms furnished by him, all cases of dependent, neglected or delinquent children coming to such overseer's attention and annually, during the month of February, shall make statistical reports to the commissioner on forms furnished by him showing such financial and other data as the commissioner may require. 

1947, No. 202, § 10,102. P. L. § 5423. 1933, No. 157, § 5120. 1921, No. 220. 1919, No. 205, § 1. 

G. L. § 7314. 1917, No. 244, § 15. 

9856. Placing children in homes. An overseer shall not place a child for more than twenty days in a home or institution or under the care of a person until such commissioner has approved such placement. However, the provisions of this

section shall not apply to the placing of such child in an incorporated orphans' home or home for dependent children in this state. 

1947, No. 202, § 10,103. P. L. § 5424. 1933, No. 157, § 5121. 1921, No. 220. 1919, No. 205, § 1. 

G. L. § 7314. 1917, No. 244,,§ 15. 

9857. Placing of children by orphans' home. When an orphans' home or home for dependent children places a child, such home shall notify the commissioner and furnish the name and address of such child. 

1947, No. 202, § 10,104. P. L. § 5425. 1933, No. 157, § 5122. 1921, No. 220. 1919, No. 205, § 1. 

G. L. § 7314. 1917, No. 244, § 15. 

9858. Keeping dependent or neglected child in town home. A dependent or neglected child shall not be kept in a town home, except a child less than two years old with its mother, and except in cases of emergency. However, in no case shall a child, except such child as aforesaid, be kept in a town home for a period exceeding ninety days. 

1947, No. 202, § 10,105. P. L. § 5426. 1933, No. 157, § 5123. 1921, No. 221. G. L. § 7315. 

1917, No. 244, § 16. 

9859. Investigation of poor relief and town homes. The commissioner of institutions and corrections or his agent shall investigate the administration of poor relief and the condition of the town homes in the state. He may, in his investigations and recommendations, request the cooperation of the state board of health when necessary or advisable. If any town home is found deficient in the care of its inmates, or if the building, equipment or appliances are unsanitary, defective or improper, or if any condition surrounding the care of the poor so warrants, the commissioner shall report and recommend such changes as he deems best for the correction of such deficiency or condition to the selectmen or directors of a town home association. 

1947, No. 202, § 10,106. P. L. § 5427. 1933, No. 157, § 5124. 1921, No. 222, § 1. 

1919, No. 205, § 2. G. L. § 7317. 1917, No. 244, § 20. 

9860. Same; failure of selectmen or directors to act; appeal. If, within a reasonable time, the selectmen or directors of a town home association fail to carry out the recommendations of such commissioner, he, at the expense of the town or town home association, may make such changes as he deems best for the correction of such deficiency or condition. However, the board of selectmen of a town or the directors of a town home association, within thirty days after the receipt of such recommendations, may appeal to the assistant judges of the county court, who shall hear such appeal and make such final order as they deem proper. 

1947, No. 202, § 10,107. P. L. § 5428. 1933, No. 157, § 5125. 1921, No. 222, § 1. 

1919, No. 205, § 2. G. L. § 7317. 1917, No. 244, § 20. 

9861. Same; formation of town home association, when. When, upon investigation of the town homes of certain towns or of a county, it appears to such commissioner that it will be for the benefit of the inmates of such town homes and will promote their welfare and enable proper separation into groups by organizing a town home association under the provisions of chapter 304, then the commissioner may call a meeting of the selectmen of such towns and present to them the facts and his recommendations. The selectmen of the several towns shall thereupon present the matter at the next annual town meeting for action in accordance with the provisions of such chapter. 

1947, No. 202, § 10,108. P. L. § 5429. 1933, No. 157, § 5126. 1921, No. 222, § 1. 

1919, No. 205, § 2. G. L. § 7317. 1917, No. 244, § 20. 

9862. Same; recovery of expense. An itemized statement for the expense incurred by the state in making the changes provided for in section 9860, in the case of a town, shall be furnished to its selectmen by the commissioner and they shall allow the same. In case of a town home association, the claim shall be furnished to the auditors of the respective towns composing the association and they shall equitably divide such expense and allow the same in proportion to the respective liability

of the several towns. The orders issued on such claims shall be payable to the state treasurer and on failure of the selectmen of a town to issue such order or the town treasurer to pay the same on demand, the state may recover the expense incurred in behalf of that town in an action of contract on this statute. 

1947, No. 202, § 10,109. P. L. § 5430. 1933, No. 157, § 5127. 

9863. Receiving trust funds. The department of social welfare may receive in trust, invest and expend in accordance with the provisions of such trusts, funds and property given, granted and bequeathed by persons for the support, care and education of dependent, neglected or delinquent children. It may receive and disburse funds given for the temporary needs of such children, and may receive such gifts to be used in carrying out the charitable work of the department in all branches. 

1935 S., No. 11, § 1. P. L. § 5431. 1933, No. 157, § 5128. G. L. § 7318. 1917, No. 244, § 21. 

Federal Assistance for Needy Children. 

9864. Support of child. If, upon investigation, in the case of a child under the age of sixteen, or under the age of eighteen, if regularly attending school, who has been deprived of parental support or care by reason of the death, continued absence from the home, or physical or mental incapacity of a parent, and who is living with his father, mother, grandfather, grandmother, brother, sister, stepfather, stepmother, stepbrother, stepsister, uncle, aunt, adoptive mother, adoptive father, grand father-in-law, grandmother-in-law, great grandmother, great grandfather, brother of half blood, adoptive brother, brother-in-law, sister of half blood, adoptive sister, sister-in-law, great uncle, uncle-in-law, aunt-in-law, great aunt, or adoptive grandparent if the child is a natural child of a parent who was adopted, in a place of residence maintained by one or more of such relatives as his or their own home, it is found that the child can remain with such relatives only if he or she is aided in its care, and if it appears desirable that the family be maintained and that such relative is a proper person to have the care of such child and that it would be for the benefit of such child that it should remain with such relative, then such department may pay a limited and reasonable amount to such relative for the maintenance of such child. 

1947, No. 202, § 10,111. 1945, No. 119, § 8. 1943, No. 89, § 2. 1941, No. 106, § 1. 

1939, No. 130, § 1. 1935 S., No. 11, § 2. 1935, No. 131. P. L. § 5421. 1933, No. 157, § 5118. 

1921, No. 218. G. L. § 7312. 1917, No. 244, § 17. 

9865. Same; assistance, determined how. The amount of assistance which any recipient shall receive shall be determined, in accordance with the rules and regulations of the state department, with due regard to the requirements and the conditions existing in the case and to the income and resources available from whatever source, and shall be sufficient, when added to the income and resources determined to be available to him, to provide him with a reasonable subsistence compatible with health and well-being. The department of social welfare shall be reimbursed monthly for one-eighth of each grant by the town in which the child is found on the date of application, but such expense paid by such town may be recovered by such town from the town liable for such child. All rights to assistance under this section shall be inalienable by any assignment, transfer, attachment, trustee process, execution or otherwise, and, in the case of bankruptcy, the assistance shall not pass to or through any trustee or other persons acting on behalf of creditors. 

1943, No. 89, § 2. 1941, No. 106, § 1. 

1946 S., No. 12, § 1. 1945, No. 119, § 8. 

1939, No. 130, § 1. 1935 S., No. 11, § 2. 1935, No. 131. P. L. § 5421. 1933, No. 157, § 5118. 

1921, No. 218. G. L. § 7312. 1917, No. 244, § 17. 

9866. Same. The department of social welfare, subject to the provisions of the three following sections, shall furnish financial assistance to needy children in accordance with the provisions of the two preceding sections.

9884. Definitions. The words hereinafter quoted, as used in this chapter, shall be construed as follows: 

"Delinquent child" shall include a child under sixteen years of age who violates a law of this state or a city or village ordinance; or who is incorrigible; or who is a persistent truant from school; or who associates with criminals, or reputed criminals, or vicious or immoral persons; or who is growing up in idleness or crime; or who wanders about the streets in the nighttime; or who frequents, visits or is found in a disorderly house, house of ill fame, saloon, barroom, or a place where intoxicating liquors are sold, exchanged or given away; or who patronizes, visits or is found in a gambling house or place where a gambling device is operated; or who uses vile, obscene, vulgar, profane or indecent language, or is guilty of immoral conduct.

"Dependent child" or "neglected child" shall mean a child under sixteen years of age who is dependent upon the public for support; or who is homeless, destitute or abandoned; or who has not proper parental care or guardianship; or who begs or receives alms; or who is found living in a house of ill fame or with a vicious or disreputable person; or whose home by reason of neglect, cruelty or depravity on the part of his parents, guardian or other person in whose care he may be, is an unfit place for such child; or whose environment is such as to warrant the state, in the interests of the child, in assuming his guardianship. STATE AID FOR THE BLIND. 

9921. State aid. Subject to the provisions of this chapter, blind persons residing in this state shall be entitled to aid from the state. 

1935 S., No. 12, § 1. 

9922. Qualifications. Aid may be granted to any blind person who: 

I. Has lost his eyesight while a resident of the state or has resided in the state for a period of two years during the nine years immediately preceding the filing of the application for assistance, the last year of which shall be continuous and immediately preceding such application; 

II. Is not an inmate of or being maintained by any public institution at the time of receiving assistance. An inmate of an institution may, however, make application for assistance but the assistance, if granted, shall not be given until he ceases to be an inmate;

III. Has not sufficient income or other resources to provide a reasonable subsistence compatible with decency and health;

IV. Has not made an assignment or transfer of property or income for the purpose of qualifying for assistance. 

1947, No. 202, § 10,228. 1946 S., No. 14, § 1. 1945, No. 114, § 1. 1941, No. 107, § 1. 

1935 S., No. 12, § 2. 

9923. Amount. The amount of assistance which any recipient shall receive shall be determined, in accordance with the rules and regulations of the state department, with due regard to the requirements and the conditions existing in the case and to the income and resources available from whatever source and shall be sufficient, when added to the income and resources determined to be available to him, to provide him with a reasonable subsistence compatible with decency and health. 

1946 S. No. 14, § 2. 1943, No. 90, § 1. 1941, No. 107, § 2. 1935 S., No. 12, § 3.1 

9924. Supervision; administration. The department of social welfare shall supervise the administration of assistance to the blind. 9954. Rights, duties, obligations. Upon the issuance of a final adoption decree the same rights, duties and obligations, and the same right of inheritance shall exist between the parties as though the person adopted had been the legitimate child of the person or persons making the adoption, except that the person adopted shall not be capable of taking property expressly limited to the heirs of the body of the persons making such adoption. The same right of inheritance shall exist between the person adopted and his issue on the one hand and natural or adopted children of the person or persons making the adoption and their issue on the other hand as though the person adopted had been the legitimate child of the person or persons making the adoption. However, there shall be no right of inheritance between the person adopted and his issue on the one hand and predecessors in line of descent and collateral kin of the person or persons making the adoption on the other hand. The natural parents of a minor shall be deprived, by the adoption, of all legal right to the control of such minor, and such minor shall be freed from all obligations of obedience and maintenance to them. A person adopted shall have

the same right of inheritance from and through his natural parents as though the adoption had not occurred, but the natural parents of a person adopted shall be deprived of all right of inheritance from or through such person. Notwithstanding the foregoing provisions of this section, when the adoption is made by a spouse of a natural parent, obligations of obedience and maintenance to, and rights of inheritance by and through the natural parent who has inter-married with the adopting parent shall not be affected.

Cruelty to Persons. 

8260. Abandonment or exposure of child. A person who abandons or exposes a child under the age of two years, whereby the life or health of such child is endangered, shall be imprisoned in the state prison not more than ten years or fined not more than $1,000.00, or both.

P. L. § 8395. G. L. § 6827. P. S. § 5722. 1896, No. 54, § 1. 

8261. Cruelty to children under ten by one over sixteen. A person over the age of sixteen years, having the custody, charge or care of a child under ten years of age, who wilfully assaults, ill treats, neglects or abandons or exposes such child, or causes or procures such child to be assaulted, illtreated, neglected, abandoned or exposed, in a manner to cause such child unnecessary suffering, or to endanger his health, shall be imprisoned in the state prison not more than two years or fined not more than $500.00, or both.

P. L. § 8396. G. L. § 6828. P. S. § 5723. 1896, No. 54, § 2. 

VAGRANTS. 

8444. Definition. A transient person, roving from place to place and living without visible means of support, who begs, or who rides or attempts to ride on a railroad freight train or engine without the consent of the person in charge thereof, or who enters or attempts to enter a dwelling house, barn or other building without the permission of the owners or occupants thereof, shall be deemed a vagrant. However, the act of applying by an indigent person to an overseer of the poor or to a police office for lodging or subsistence shall not be conclusive evidence that such person is a vagrant.

VOLUNTARY STERILIZATION. 

10,027. Construction. It shall be the policy of the state to prevent procreation of mentally defective and insane persons, when the public welfare and the welfare of such persons likely to procreate, can be improved by voluntary sterilization as provided in this chapter. 

P. L. § 5438. 1931, No. 174, § 1. 

10,028. Examination and certificate; operation. When two physicians and surgeons legally qualified to practice in the state examine a person resident of the state, and decide:

I. That such person is mentally defective or insane and likely to procreate mentally defective or insane persons if not sexually sterilized;

II. That the health and physical condition of such person will not be injured by the operation of vasectomy, if a male, or the operation of salpingectomy, if a female;

III. That the welfare of such person and the public welfare will be improved if such person is sterilized as aforesaid; and 

IV. Whether such person is or is not of sufficient intelligence to understand that he or she cannot procreate children after such operation is performed, and such examiners make and sign duplicate certificates setting forth those facts and make oath thereto before a justice of the peace or notary public, then it shall be lawful for any other physician and surgeon, legally qualified to practice in the state, when presented with such certificate, to perform such operation, provided: (a). He decides that the welfare of such person and the public welfare will be improved by such operation; (b). Such person has requested in writing, on such certificates, that such operation be performed, if the certificates show that such person is of sufficient intelligence to understand that he or she cannot procreate children after such operation is performed; or (c). The natural or legal guardian of such person has requested in writing on such certificates that such operation be performed, if the certificates show that such person is not of sufficient intelligence to understand that he or she cannot procreate children after such operation is performed; and (d). Such person voluntarily submits to such operation. 

P. L. §§ 5439, 5440. 1931, No. 174, § 2. 

10,029. Same; report. After performing such operation, the physician and surgeon shall endorse on each of the duplicate certificates when and where he performed such operation, keep one of such certificates and mail the other, postage prepaid, to the commissioner of institutions and corrections. 

P. L. § 5441. 1931, No. 174, § 2. 

10,030. Inmates of state institutions; fee. When such person is being supported by the state in an institution in the state, the commissioner of institutions and corrections is authorized to contract with two competent physicians and surgeons, not in the employment of the state at a price not exceeding $3.00 for each physician and surgeon to examine mentally defective or insane persons as he has reason to believe should be sterilized. If they make the certificates aforesaid, then the commissioner is authorized to contract with a competent physician and surgeon, not in the employment of the state, to perform such operation at a price not exceeding $20.00 for males and $30.00 for females and to contract with a hospital for such care and nursing of such person as is necessary. Such expenses shall be paid by the state and charged against the appropriation for the support of the institution supporting such person.

P. L. § 5442. 1933, No. 157, § 5136. 1931, No. 174, § 3.
