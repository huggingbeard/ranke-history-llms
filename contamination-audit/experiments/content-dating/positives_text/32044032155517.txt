At Home and Abroad 

The servicemen abroad cannot vote in the primaries. They can vote only for those candidates for public office who have been nominated on the home front. The women voters, who outnumber the men, will dominate the primaries. The Termite Ticket, therefore, is designed to mislead the women voters. The Termite Ticket is synonymous with 

Phony Slate. 2Who Pays for the Phony Slate?

Patronage and public office are his business and pleasure. The Republican party, in one hand and the Democratic party, in the other, are his livelihood. Among the men of Beacon Hill he is whisperingly called 

Nemo, silent partner of the Golden Dome.

He has favors to give, and influence to lend. Some public officials receive them gladly, and ask for more. Upon others he thrusts his unwelcome services, trusting to profit later from their practical gratitude. 

He employs those who are innocently ignorant of his schemes and strategems.

His capital investment is nominal, compared with the returns. He is the toast of stately banquets, recognized as powerful, and thus immune. 

So that he dreads nothing but therapeutic radium. 3The Termite Ticket 

Just as the termite that tunnels into sound timber is technically and decidedly not a true ant, so the Termite Ticket is not a true political slate. The true, or Straight Ticket, builds and defends party organization. The Termite Ticket destroys. It is among the campaign strategems of the profiteering political manipulator. 571653 [ 5 ]

A disturbingly large number of experienced voters do not comprehend the significance and vital importance of the Straight Ticket. That is a list of candidates who have received the official nomination, hence endorsement, of their party, in a primary, and are certified candidates for election for public office. Campaign literature of both the Republican and Democratic parties emphasize, at considerable expense, the importance of voting for the Straight party Ticket. "Carry this Slate to the Polls!" "Vote the Straight Ticket!"

The Straight Ticket cannot be used at any party primary: it can be used only at an election. Any mis-called "Regular Republican 

Ticket," that is presented to the voters at a primary, is a Not Straight 

Ticket, a Phony Slate, a fraud upon the voters.

Practical politicians learned in their kindergarten the theory and practice of tickets or slates. They respect the authority and power of the Straight Ticket. Many of them, as sometimes victim of the Phony 

Slate, know it at a glance as an obvious trick, an ancient and discredited campaign device; and they are not deceived by it. But the number of voters- -women more than men-who are today, as always before, misled by the Termite Ticket is nearly the whole voting list. 

The simplest things in life are the hardest to explain. So it is difficult to explain the Termite Ticket to the voter who is unsuspecting, or who is distracted by grave personal or business affairs around election time. The task might well be illustrated by the familiar and popular cartoon of the baseball fan who has brought his gay but bewildered girl friend to the big game of the series. It is like the task of a bookmaker at the racetrack to describe to a novice bettor the advantages or disadvantages of a parlay, or the chances of winning by following the advice of a doctored racing form, or a phony book on an eight-horse race, in which the entries, odds, percentages and risks have been scrambled by some arithmetical dopester. It would be like a new attendant in an institution for mental health giving instruction concerning the more subtle variations in the Einstein computations of the Fourth Dimension, by sign language, to a class of deaf and dumb. Neither Webster, nor Roget, nor even the Greek dictionary has a word for the task of explanation. It is too simple to be easy. 

And yet a Ticket or Slate is simply a list of candidates, prepared in advance,ake to the polls for guidance for convenience and to save time for the voter. For the woman voter, it might be compared with a shopping list, composed of items that have been recommended by reliable friends, guaranteed by quality labels, honestly and convincingly advertised, and the values of which the shopper has learned from her own experience. That would be the Straight Ticket. The Termite Ticket might best be compared in detail with a substitute, or phony, shopping list, presented to her at the entrance to a store.

The woman shopper could not conceivably be hornswoggled by a Termite Shopping List, with obviously phony brand names, handed to her through the mail, or on the street by a bearded stranger who loitered in front of the shop. Yet at the polling places it is quite different. 

Even the most intelligent and well-informed woman voter smilingly ac- [6]

cepts the Phony Slate from some Termite Ticket picket. Reading the patriotic slogan at the top, and recognizing among the names of candidates several who are respected leaders of her own political party, she confidently enters the voting booth and votes the Straight Termite Ticket! If this were business, and not politics, the woman voter could demand, and would receive back her ballot. But there is no Better 

Politics Bureau. In the canons of professional politics there is no ethical standard such as Truth in Advertising. There is no charge account at the polls and political goods cannot be exchanged for two or four years, a long, long while.

There are, incidentally, minor phony slates that hardly serve either party harmony or the cause of good government, yet are blindly tolerated by the party organizations. The Gang Phony, for instance, appears in a double or triple state Representative district, when two or three candidates for nomination to the House combine against the field, with a joint circular and ticket. This can be joined, but with greater risk of success, into a Senate-House phony. For city elections, particularly where the mis-called "non-partisan" system is in effect, the biggest vote-trapping phony slate is the one that is headed-for example-by a Democrat, whereas all the other names on the ticket are obviously Republican. 

This, logically, is mailed exclusively to enrolled Republican voters. It is the Democrat that profits. Still another form of Phony Slate is the 

Citizens Committee Ticket. That is one of the many ill-advised strategems adopted by hysterical dilettante reformers, who are responsible for the ephemeral character of all municipal reform movements. In the "University City," Cambridge, a sort of self-chosen Charter Committee, with ample funds, presented a slate to the voters that was a perplexing mixture of politics, race and religion. The voters were irritated but helpless. Within one month after the election, the Taxpayers Association was shouting for help: "There is real danger that the control of our city administration may go back into the hands of politicians." The city had already gone back. The Termite Ticket is simple and safe for the professional politician; but it should not be attempted by amateurs. 

The public seems unappreciative of the quixotic truth that there are many Republicans-and some Democrats-in public office who practice their conviction that now is not the time to display and shout their wares. They refuse to use, and silently repudiate campaign methods that are dishonest. Lacking the protection of official repudiation of the 

Phony Slate by their party organization, the clean campaigners are defenseless against that strategem. They are at the same time helpless against the whispered smears and doorway buttonholing that characteristically go with the Phony Slate. -4- 

The Catchvote Slogan 

An essential part of every Termite Ticket is the patriotic slogan. 

The dictionaries may be all wrong. The government of the United 

States may be neither a republic nor a democracy-nor a democracy in [7]

a republic-it may be a government by pressure groups and impressive slogans. Catchwords and phrases that hypnotize and entrap the voters. 

Promissory political notes. Dear today, but corny tomorrow. The best statesmen of both parties agree, however, that the vital All-American determination to win the war must not be used for partisan appeals. 

National Chairman Harrison E. Spangler, in a speech at the joint meeting of the national committee and the state chairmen, at Chicago, January 10th, declared that Republicans were united on the question of winning the war and that the winning of the war must be kept out of politics.

Spangler said: "The candidate for political office who attempts to make political capital out of the war will earn the scorn of patriots and repudiation of the voters. Today this country is Republican rather than Democratic. But our Army and Navy are neither Republican nor 

Democratic. They are Americans. 'Win the War' is a slogan that belongs to all of us.' 5The Middlesex Termite Ticket 

The Middlesex county Phony Slate was the most widespread and ambitious deception of the voters within memory, or secret records. It was the perfection of the Termite Ticket. It used a patriotic nameslogan to head an adulterated list of candidates, in order to defeat other candidates who had loyally supported the war effort. 

Five days, only, before the Massachusetts state primaries, on September 15, 1942, the unsuspecting Republican women voters of Middlesex county received by mail a postal card ticket or slate of candidates for public office.

The slate bore the heading, "Middlesex League in Support of the 

President's War Effort (its entire membership Republican)."

With one exception, it carried the names of candidates for all offices on the ballot. It omitted the name of U. S. Senator-now Maj. Henry 

Cabot Lodge, Jr.

It carried and displayed as window-dressing, or decoys, the honorable names of Governor Leverett Saltonstall, together with his Republican associates who were candidates for statewide office, and candidates for representative in congress. Their notable names were used without their knowledge or approval.

The name of the Republican party was likewise used, to deceive and mislead the women voters with the belief that this slate was an authorized appeal for loyal votes, made by a genuine Republican organization.

There are no records in the office of the secretary of the Commonwealth to show that such an organization ever existed. [8]

They Phony Slate of the "League" varied in the different electoral districts, in order to reach all candidates. Thus the Middlesex Termite 

Ticket increased or reduced the votes, and influenced the nomination or defeat of Republican candidates for governor, lieut-governor, secretary of state, state treasurer, state auditor, attorney general, senator in congress, representative in congress, executive councillor, state senator, representative in the General Court, district attorney, and county commissioner.

This Phony Slate was distributed also at the polls, by hired workers, among whom were Democrats. The ticket was eagerly accepted by voters who had no knowledge of the scheming forces behind it. They carried the slate into the voting booths and voted for the candidates it endorsed! 

Party Discord

The Middlesex county Termite Ticket had made a sneak attack, on the eve of the primary, upon candidates whose loyalty to the Republican party was an honored record. It had used the names of Governor 

Saltonstall, and his associates on the statewide ticket, to defeat their personal friends and party colleagues, and to cast out of office legislators who had faithfully supported the Saltonstall administration. It had used the name of Saltonstall against Lodge. Under the name Republican it had disrupted party harmony, setting brother against brother, party colleague against party colleague. This had been done without their previous knowledge, and without warning. Under the cloak of patriotism it had savagely attacked candidates who had actively supported the War Effort, and who had conspicuously cooperated with the American Legion, in legislative measures against subversive and un- American organizations and activities. Although addressed solely to women voters, it was aimed at the defeat of women candidates of the highest standing.

Inevitably, there was a widespread appeal made to party leaders, and to the Republican state committee, to publish a prompt repudiation of the Middlesex Phony Slate. There was no time to investigate the source of this eleventh-hour ticket.

The party leaders, veterans of political wars, who recognized the Termite Ticket as an ancient, if not honorable strategem, reasoned that since it was obvious trickery, it could safely be ignored. They so advised. 

With perhaps an excess of dignity and virtue, in the heat of the campaign, and in a crisis of cheap politics, they missed the clear warning that you can fool some of the voters all of the time, and all of the voters some of the time, but some time the women voters of Middlesex county may hold the Republican organization responsible for deception practiced, without repudiation, in the name of the Republican party. The leaders forgot their friends and colleagues. Politics has its own rules of good sportsmanship. While it is a fact that there is more "cheap politics" surrounding than within the profession, nevertheless there is need, in [9]

political campaigns, of the youthful spirit of the football gridiron, where a Harvard tackle good naturedly raises the Eli, whom he has just savagely thrown to the ground. Are collegians more wholesome than statesmen? 

The Republican state committee, as the logical body, was urged to publish a prompt official repudiation of the sham ticket. The decision of the state committee, after excited conferences, was that the time was too late for effective action. Repudiation, the committee feared, would further confuse the voters, stir up controversy, create unjustified criticism of the party leaders, and even jeopardize the success of the Republican party at the state election. 

That official silence has still been maintained, though nearly two years have passed. The disturbing mystery is that deception can be boldly practiced upon the Republican women voters on such a wide scale throughout Middlesex county-without public protest or repudiation. Someone or some group-must be immune. 

The "League" And The Law 

According to reliable legislative research, it is within the law, in every state of the Union, for an individual, or an organization, to use the names of political candidates, on a campaign slate or ticket, without their previous knowledge or consent. 

In Massachusetts, (Gen. Laws, Ter. Ed.) sec. 4, chap. 55, provides, under penalty, that a non-elected political committee shall file with the state secretary the names of its officers, and a statement of the purpose for which organized.

In the office of the secretary of the Commonwealth, there was no record of the names of officers, or purpose of organization, of the "Middlesex League In Support of the President's War Effort." There was, similarly, no subsequent report made by the “League" of campaign contributions or expenditures. 

Sec. 33A, of chap. 55, in brief, provides that "No organization, other than a political party, as defined by law, shall, in order to promote the success or defeat of a political party or principle or of a candidate in a public election, in any circular, advertisement or publication use in its organization name the name of such a political party, except with the written consent of the duly elected state committee representing such political party.”

It is superfluous to say that this "League" has not received from the Republican state committee written permission for the use of the word "Republican," as a part of its name.

The needle's eye for escape, in the statute, is the phrase "in its organization name." A political organization may have whatever name seems most likely to mislead or entrap the voters, and it is within the statutes to add, preferably within parentheses-"(its entire membership Republican)." It is not even required to prove the truth of that statement. Presumably, the entire membership, or the majority of the mem- [ 10 ]

bership, may be Democratic, Socialist, or Communist. The heading of the ticket has the designed effect, yet technically the word "Republican" is not a legal part of the "organization name. Such an organization may be actually sponsored by only one person, using the names of persons who are innocent of the scheme to sign the circular, slate or ticket. 8. 

Senate Bill 472 

The official spokesman for Governor Saltonstall suggested the advisability of legislation to prohibit the Termite Ticket. House bills 244 and 245 were accordingly drawn up and presented, early in the session of 1943, to the General Court. They prohibited, under penalty, the publication on tickets or slates of the names of candidates for public office, without their written permission.

While the General Court was debating these bills, a description of the method of the Phony Slate was published in the Boston Herald, by its well-informed and authoritative political columnist, William E. 

Mullins. Here it is: "The waning days of this reasonably peaceful legislative session have been enlivened in the Senate by a mild row among Republicans over the merits of a bill which seeks to end the ancient practice of manufacturing phony election slates. Politicians are familiar with the practice because many of them have been victims of it. "It is employed by obscure and sometimes disreputable candidates, particularly in a primary. The system is simple. A candidate with an easy conscience prepares a card on which is listed each of the offices to be filled in the election. For the important offices like Governor, Lieutenant-Governor and State Secretary he selects the most popular candidate. Into the list he smuggles his own name. "Thus he ends up with a full slate of candidates, including himself, attaches a meaningless indorsement to it, and distributes it in his own district, giving gullible voters the idea that he is running in collaboration with and indorsed by the candidates he has selected as his slate associates..

The two House bills were unanimously reported, in the form of Senate 472, by the joint legislative committee on election laws. That bill, under threat of filibuster by the opposition, was defeated by a rising vote in the 

Senate. Why?

The defeat of this bill to outlaw the Phony Slate, and to set up a standard for truth in political advertising, is evidence that the Republican legislators were not informed of all the facts. Even the astute and sound-reasoning "Bill" Mullins referred only to the use in a “district."

The Middlesex Phony Slate was distributed throughout the most important Republican stronghold in Massachusetts, and affected all the electoral districts, with but a few minor exceptions. For cheap windowdressing, it used the name of a statesman whom the future might choose for President of the United States! [11]

9The Pride of the Party 

Middlesex, with nearly half a million registered voters, is the greatest county in the State, and far and away the strongest Republican territory. 

It is proudly hailed as the Banner Republican County-not alone in Massachusetts, but perhaps in the nation. Campaign methods that are approved or tolerated in Middlesex are likely to be exemplary-and should be as Republican standards for state and nation. 

The Middlesex Club, for nearly four score years, has upheld the dignity and statesmanship of Republican policies. Has it congratulated the arrival, offered the hand of fellowship, or considered cooperation with the "Middlesex League (its entire membership Republican)"? Has the Middlesex Club been informed of any "War Effort" made by the "Middlesex League," other than to put on the cloak of a patriotic slogan, and hocus the women voters of Middlesex, with a Termite Ticket, in a 

Republican primary?

The question is with respect: "Which would have a greater practical effect upon the voters, a statesmanlike oration at the Lincoln Day dinner of the Middlesex Club, or a Phony Slate that is thrust into their hands at the door of a polling place, by some worker for the "Middlesex League"? 10 

Beyond Middlesex 

The Termite Ticket has possibilities that spread far beyond the Republican boundaries of Middlesex county, or the Commonwealth of Massachusetts. It is potentially as powerful, in every district of the Nation as the Straight Ticket of the party. Democrats have an envious though censorious memory of the Gerrymander, which has its name and picture in the dictionaries, and has brought unwanted fame to Essex county. Middlesex county can yet avert a similar notoriety, by repudiation, this Presidential year, of the Middlesex Phony Slate.

There are particular variations that are natural temptations to the political schemers of both national parties. The resounding message to 

Democrats, at the national convention in Chicago, in 1940, broadcast by the "Voice from the Sewers," had been planned to stampede the Presidential choice of the convention. It came from a hidden sourcebeneath the imposing platform from which statesmen shouted their loyalty to party policies.

Catchwords, slogans, organization name-slogans, adulterated lists of candidates! "Middlesex League in Support of the President's Domestic New Deal Policies and Fourth Term Effort (its entire membership Republican)"; "League in Support of the President's Postwar Lend

Lease and World Power Administration (its entire membership Republican)"; "League Opposed to the War Effort (its entire membership 

Republican)." Those are possibilities. [12]

An honest and patriotic slogan for the Republican party could be adapted from Civil War days: "This Government Cannot Endure Permanently Half New Deal and Half Free.' 

Consider thoughtfully this national possibility. Any subversive group, with a foreign-subsidy campaign fund, and having the determination to disrupt and destroy the American Two-Party system of democracy, could distribute, at the eleventh hour, a deliberately chaotic list of candidates for election, comprising all political parties, under the seductive and genuinely patriotic slogan "Make America Safe for Democracy!"

Smuggled into this list of candidates would be potential Quislings, for election to public office by the uninformed voters. 

This would be the logical ultraism of the Middlesex Phony Slate.
