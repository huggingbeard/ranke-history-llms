His life has become a symbol of interracial cooperation and understanding. Below is an abbreviated version of the parable: 

A certain man went into the forest and caught an eaglet which he brought home and raised with his chickens. Years later a passing naturalist upbraided the man for allowing an eagle to feed with his fowls. The man assured the naturalist that because the eagle had been raised as a chicken it had indeed become a chicken. The naturalist thought otherwise and offered to prove the bird an eagle. After several attempts to get the eagle to fly, the naturalist took it to a high mountain in the early morning. "Eagle," he said, "thou art an eagle. Thou dost belong to the sky and not to this earth; stretch forth thy wings and fly!"

Whereupon the eagle, trembling as if new life had come into it, looked straight into the sun, stretched its mighty wings and mounted higher and higher and never returned. It was an eagle though it had been kept and tamed as a chicken.

PURPOSE OF THE CONFERENCE 

The major objectives of the Conference are to broaden and deepen American understanding of the achievements and aspirations of Africa's people and to develop ideas which may be useful to the Commission in advising the U.S. Government on educational, scientific, and cultural aspects of American policies with respect to Africa. In pursuing these objectives the Conference will reassess Africa's educational needs and the best ways of meeting them, appraise the African programs already begun in the United States by a wide variety of educational, religious, philanthropic, civic, labor, business, and other groups; and relate to each other the rapidly increasing number of African interests and projects now underway in American institutions and other organizations. 

THE CONFERENCE PLAN 

The Eighth National Conference, which is being arranged in cooperation with Boston University, will be attended by more than 1,500 individuals and representatives of national organizations, including a substantial number of African leaders in the fields of education, science, and the arts. Admission will be by invitation from the U.S. National Commission for UNESCO. 

Participants will include specialists as well as citizens representing a wide range of interests. The program is designed to interest both.

Delegates will be addressed at plenary sessions and section meetings by leading personalities from Africa and the United States. The Conference will be divided into four sections: 

Education, Science, Culture, and Communication. Each section will hold one general meeting and then divide into panels. The 

Education Section will have five panels; Science, three panels; 

Culture, three panels; and Communication, one panel. The 12 panels will meet 5 times each on successive days. 

Each delegate is requested to select a panel of his choice. It is recommended that he remain with his panel throughout the Conference in order to obtain benefits from the continuity of the discussions. 1009578

In addition to the above sessions, delegates will have an opportunity to see numerous exhibits and films on Africa and to enjoy musical programs especially prepared and arranged for the Conference. 

To help participants obtain the maximum benefit from the Conference, a background book is in preparation which will be made available to participants before the Conference opens. 

It will contain three general background chapters on Africa and its people, government and politics, and African social organizations. These chapters will be followed by a background paper for each of the 12 panels, along with relevant maps and statistical information.

BACKGROUND OF THE CONFERENCE 

The United States National Commission for UNESCO is required under 

Public Law 565 (79th Congress, 2d Session) to "call general conferences for the discussion of matters relating to the activities of the Organization 

[United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization] to which conferences organized bodies actively interested in such matters shall be invited to send representatives 

Conferences have been held in Philadelphia, March 1947; Cleveland, March 1949; New York City, January 1952; Minneapolis, September 1953; 

Cincinnati, November 1955; San Francisco, November 1957; and Denver,

September 1959. 

For the first five National Conferences, the United States National Commission arranged programs devoted to a general consideration of all phases of the worldwide program of UNESCO. 

At the Sixth National Conference, the National Commission focused attention on one of the UNESCO major projects, “Mutual Appreciation of 

Eastern (Asian) and Western Cultural Values." 

The Seventh National Conference was devoted to furthering mutual understanding among peoples of the American Republics. The theme of the Conference was THE CULTURES OF THE AMERICAS: Achieve ments in Education, Science, and the Arts.

In planning the program of the Eighth National Conference, the Conference Committee has had the valuable help of a number of advisers to whom it wishes to express appreciation. These Conference advisers are:

ADMISSION 

Admission to the Conference sessions will be by ticket only. Tickets and badges will be handed to the delegates when they register. There is no registration fee. Participants will attend at their own expense. 

ACCOMMODATIONS 

Hotel reservations should be made directly with the Boston Convention and Tourist Bureau, 80 Federal Street, Boston, Mass., using the form provided with this program. The Bureau will handle all accommodations. 

Please do not request room reservations from the National Commission.

CONFERENCE HEADQUARTERS 

The Statler-Hilton Hotel in Boston has been designated as Headquarters Hotel. Conference activities will take place there and in other hotels and buildings in the immediate vicinity. 

NATIONAL COMMISSION MEETING 

The United States National Commission for UNESCO will hold its Twentieth Annual Meeting at Boston on October 21 and 22 at the Statler- Hilton Hotel immediately preceding the Conference. The National Commission consists of 100 members appointed by the Secretary of State. Of these, 60 represent national nongovernmental organizations having a total membership of over 30 million.
