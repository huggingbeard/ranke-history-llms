INISTER, macabre, and certainly startling, is this latest book of Gordon Sayre, in which a man and his wife are suddenly confronted with the imminent possibility of permanent dissolution. 

For fifteen years Pierce and Peggy were happily, though unconventionally, married.

Neither bad, at all times, been physically faithful, but neither had ever been unfaithful in their comradeship. 

Then Pierce lost his money and four men tried valiantly to take his wife away from him. But after six exciting months, Peggy discovers that it takes more than mere physical attraction or romantic affection to make a woman happy.

While it is true that every one must eventually pay the fiddler, the paradox is that everyone pays the fiddler exactly the same fee, whether they dance or not-so the author concludes one might as well get in as much dancing as possible.
