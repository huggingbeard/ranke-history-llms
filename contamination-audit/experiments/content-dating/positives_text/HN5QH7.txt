Chapter ELLY 

LLY sat in the shelter of the overhanging rock at the end of her hidden little beach-the end farthest away from the house-and stared out at the flashing gray water, trying to convince herself that it looked different from the ocean she had watched, fascinatedly and half-fearfully, all winter long. She thought-yes, she was almost sure—that its color had changed. It was no longer black, with yellowish foam, but the palest smoked crystal. The sky, too, was different, and behind the clouds was a light like the light of a moonstone. In another moment, it seemed, the sun would pour through, warming and lighting the whole world of water and land. "It is spring," Elly murmured. "It feels like it, even here, at the sea."

It was the first spring she had ever known at Lanesport, and the winter that was just over had been her first winter. 

She and her parents and occasionally Margot, her stepsister who was three years older than she, had spent the summers in the old New England fishing town ever since Elly was ten-six summers. ELLY stood at the top of the granite steps that led down to the beach.
