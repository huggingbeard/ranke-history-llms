THE PART OF YOUTH IN LAW-ENFORCEMENT. 

I am that

I am more than happy to greet the young men of the Hi-Y Clubs of America and to speak to you, I hope, as co-workers of the Federal Bureau of Investigation in what seems to me to be one of the most important jobs that any organization can accomplish of carrying on an intensive fight against youthful crime. constantly distressed in my work by the statistics which come to my desk showing that a great part of desperate crime today is committed by the younger members of society. However, I am heartened by the belief that this condition can be remedied and that the persons who can do the most to help bring our country to a law-abiding and honorable condition are young persons like yourselves with the vision to crusade toward a better life for all of youth. 

I am particularly interested in talking to you young men of the Hi-Y Clubs upon this very subject. The Y.M.C.A. always has been a crusading organization working for health and strength in mind, us morals, and body. It is one thing for a young man to go straight and 986.

It is a far better thing that this young man, in traveling the straight course, should extend the strong hand of fellowship to some weaker brother that he may guide him from the tangled jungles of criminality into the light of obedience to law and the tenets of clean living. Here also is a thought which I hope you will make a mental companion. If every young man of the 

Hi-Y Clubs of America each year could teach some other young man who is about to fall before the temptations of crime that it is a worthless game; that it can only end in disgrace and punishment and thus rescue him from the dangers which surround him, our problem of youthful criminality would come to an end within a comparatively short time. 

If the Hi-Y Clubs could adopt the slogan of "Save a Boy from Crime" and carry it through to a successful fulfillment, the Hi-Y would have the distinction of having saved America from a great part of the menace which now confronts it. 

This is a real menace. We all know, for instance, that the standing military and naval forces of the United States constitute about 250,000 armed men, but did you ever realize that the criminal standing army of America consists of twice that number -- a whole half-million of armed thugs, murderers, thieves, firebugs, assassins, robbers, and hold-up men? You, in your oath to the Hi-Y, pledge yourselves to allegiance to the flag of your country, feeling in your hearts that should a danger threaten America you would be willing to give your all, even to your lives to protect it. And, therefore, I want you to know that a danger does threaten America; that this

· 3 · country is being pillaged and plundered continuously by a vicious army which works from within, which enters our homes, which robs our families, and which, every year, commits a total of 1,500,000 major crimes. I do not mean mere infractions of city ordinances or even petty thefts or other minor violations of the law. I refer to crimes which constitute a tragedy in the families where they strike, such crimes as those of stealing the entire possessions of some person or of assault and battery, which leave a victim sprawled in his own blood after a heinous attack by some criminal foe. Of these 1,500,000 serious crimes, 12,000 are murders; 46,981 are felonious assaults; 

779,956 are larcenies, and 283,685 are burglaries. 

This is the type of crime to which I refer and this is the type of criminal against whom you should enlist yourself in battle. 

There is another thought that I hope will remain with you. 

It is possible that many of the criminals who offend against our laws might at some time have been turned into honest law-abiding citizens, if they had been properly encouraged. 

Perhaps, had they, as youths, enjoyed association with some strong-minded, strong-willed boy, determined to show his companions that a life of crime was a useless, dangerous and unromantic occupation, they might have been willing to take the better course. Every boy who is saved from crime is likewise saved from years of misery, and honest citizens of America are saved from the destruction and loss which a life of criminality brings about." 

Remember this whenever a boy talks to you about the joys of getting something for nothing. Here is a youth who is about to ruin his own future and to despoil the lives of others. And here is your chance,

instead of being tempted into becoming a violator of the law, to bring about a change in that boy so that he sees the decent life as the real life and criminality as a sordid existence in which there can only be one end disgrace and disillusionment and certain punishment. 4x4 There is no romance in crime and there is no romance in They criminals. We have passed through an era in which ill-advised persons and sentimental sob-sisters have attempted to paint the desperate law violators of America as men and women of romance. They are the absolute opposite. They are rats, vermin, vultures, regurgitating their filth to despoil the clean picture of American manhood and womanhood. sink deeper and deeper into a mire of viciousness which inevitably leads to filth in mind, filth in living, filth in morals, and in bodily health. They travel steadily downward until at last they are no more than craven beasts. Their rewards of plunder merely pass through their hands into those of rotten political renegades whom they must pay for protection. Their lives are constantly harried with no hope of possessions in the true sense of the word, no chance for a home, no chance for a position of respect or of standing in a community. 

I can liken them only to the scurrying of a horde of rats hunting their dusty holes before the approach of the exterminator. 

This, I must admit, is not the picture that may be given you in fiction or in the motion pictures, which is a land of makebelieve. I speak of actualities and I speak from experience, for I know criminality as it really exists; the slatternly, ugly life as it must be lived, the cheap pandering, the horribly dirty hideouts,

the prison-marked men and women who form the minions of crime. It is not at all a pleasant picture. 

Perhaps the picture of a criminal life best can be compared to the dust and dirt of an abandoned house where the windows are grimy and the panes knocked out, the floors rotten, the corners littered with old scatterings of aged newspapers, and the wind rattling the Such is the house of crime where sunlight does not enter and if a few faint rays do penetrate, they are serrated by broken shutters. prison bars.

You young men have a true job before you, that of teaching less discerning young fellows these facts about a life concerning which they have been misinformed. If they talk of John Dillinger as a heroic figure, you should remind them that John Dillinger was nothing but a beer-drinking plug-ugly, who bought his way from hideout to hideout, being brave only when he had a machine gun trained upon a victim and the victim at his mercy. When John Dillinger finally was brought to bay, he was not a hero he was not a Robin Hood, he was not a romantic motion picture figure but only a coward who did not know how to shoot, except from ambush. 

We remember the wonderful stories about the man who was "1 11 called the "Phantom of the Ozarks, the "King of Bank Robbers, the great hero of the Southwest, otherwise known as "Pretty Boy" Floyd.

Let us see how heroic he was. For years, he enjoyed the protection which he bought in various criminal hideouts throughout the Southwest

and when he had that protection, he was brave indeed. But after a time, the Federal Bureau of Investigation set upon his trail and it hunted him from one hideout to another, never allowing him to rest, never allowing him to build up an organization which would conceal, protect, feed, and harbor him. So at last, he was forced into the open and where then was this famous "Phantom of the Ozarks," this King of the Southwest outlaws, this lone bank robber, who had been touted and romanticized into a modern Robin Hood? He was a skulking, disheveled, dirty, ill-clothed hobo and, as such, he was hunted down to his end, as a hoodlum, an ego-inflated rat! These descriptions fit the story of every one of the so-called public enemies, who have managed to parade their way across the first pages of our newspapers. 

The silly and misguided boys throughout the United States who have tried to emulate them can look forward to no other end except such as came to these men. There is only one finish to a life of crime and it is far from a pleasant one. 

In the more or less spectacular captures which have been effected by the Federal Bureau of Investigation, such cases as those of Dillinger, "Pretty Boy" Floyd, "Baby Face" Nelson, Alvin Karpis, Harry Campbell, William Dainard, the kidnaper of the fine young boy,

George Weyerhaeuser of Tacoma, and others, it has been the result of efforts that should hold particular interest for members of the Hi-Y Clubs. I refer to team work. No single man in the Federal 

Bureau of Investigation brought about any one of these captures. 

They resulted from concerted effort by the entire organization Special Agents working diligently in the field, fingerprint experts

in the Division of Identification, scientists highly trained to the discovery of every clue as they labored in the crime laboratory; the executive force in Washington constantly on the alert day and night. 

All of these factors moved in unison, every man carrying his share of the load, every official and Special Agent and expert and scientist laboring in perfect cooperation to bring the case to a successful culmination. Our Bureau operates exactly like a football team, where the spectacular efforts of the men who carry the pigskin are dependent entirely upon the perfect offense and defense of the rest of the team. 

It is truly a "we" organization and not an "I" organization. I hope 

I can enlist you young men into the broadening of these efforts; into the knowledge that crime truly does not pay, and into a missionary effort by which you can spread this word wherever you go.

It has been particularly pleasing to me in the last few years to note that the psychology of American youth is greatly changing in its viewpoint toward criminality. More and more I note that young men in viewing their future lives are thinking seriously of lawenforcement as a career. This is highly encouraging. Law-enforcement is truly a profession. It should be provided for in the curricula of our schools and colleges to teach the fundamentals of law-enforcement. 

It is equally as important as the professions of law and medicine. 

A great part of the success of the Federal Bureau of Investigation has been due to the fact that 83% of its personnel are young men of college education, with degrees either in law or accounting, and whose personal background is one of unfaltering integrity. 

It is not enough that we merely have college men in law-enforcement.

Certainly, these are integrals of which any person can be proud and we of the FBI are indeed prideful that our organization is one in which every man is unswayed by the force of venal politics and is loyal to the FBI even to the sacrifice of life itself. 

Every young man and woman in this country should know They should know the story of something about law-enforcement. fingerprinting. They should know the romantic story of science as it applies to present-day law-enforcement. 

They should learn of the proper methods by which obedience for law can be brought about so that as future citizens they can become the guardians of their communities and demand that these communities have efficient methods in the protection of the locality in which they live. 

There are few homes which do not contain a medical cabinet and its first-aid remedies.

Show me the homes, however, in which there are first-aid kits for the protection against crime. Where, for instance, can we find communities which take the trouble to learn the causes behind crime, what should be done to bring about better conditions in our cities, the means and ways of forcing corrupt or renegade politicians to relinquish their hold upon police departments or the knowledge by which to prevent various forces of crime from gaining control in a community. Yet, where accident strikes only infrequently in a serious way, crime crushes a path into one home out of every 16 in America every year so that in a little more than every decade and a half, it marks every American family with the tragedy of robbery, hold-up, arson, assault, manslaughter, or murder.
