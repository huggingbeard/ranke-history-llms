INTRODUCTION 

Mankind since the beginning of time has sought a method or plan of living. Philosophers have sought for explanations of life and all its mysteries and in their search have given expression to their thoughts in codes, precepts and grams of wisdom. The search of American pioneers in thought, who wished a way of life led to the establishment of Brook Farm where man worked, thought, and saw the World in all its essence and substance through his own eyes and those of his associates.

This book was written originally as a research paper for an American Literature Class at the 

Teachers College of Connecticut where the Author majored in English. Full credit for the ideas expressed in the book are accorded to, O. B. Frothingham, author of Trancendentalism in New England; Swift, author of Brook Farm; Professor

Gohden, Transcendentalism; Riley Woodbridge, American Thought, and to Kant who defined the term "Transcendent."

My contribution is the representation of old facts in the light of present day conditions, interpreting the spirit of Brook Farm, and attempting to show the kinship of Brook Farm to the present. The various movements called Socialistic, Communistic, Utopian, Fascistic, are merely similar experiments. Americans preceded the Russian Revolution and Fascist 

States by many years. It has "Happened Here,"

The reader should examine the facts presented here and then try to interpret the contemporary scene. Much that is happening today is similar to past history. If you fail to understand the underlying philosophy, the personal qualities of the persons described herein will be of benefit to you in understanding individual reactions to "Utopia," when

America was young and emerging to the dismay of the Brook Farmers into a Materialistic Nation. We may have to try the Brook Farm method again but if we do it will comfort us to know that it is American in spirit.

1 Before viewing Transcendentalism in New England it might be well to trace briefly its source. 

Frothingham tells us that the "Transcendental Philosophy, so called, had a distinct origin in Immanuel Kant whose, Critique of Pure Reason, was published in 1781, and opened a new epoch in metaphysical thought." ¹ Kant merely interpreted the old issues of philosophy that had been discussed since the days of Aristotle. The attempts of Locke, who wrote the Essay on Human Understanding, were not satisfactory in the realm of philosophy which he abandoned, and as a result Kant felt obliged to reconsider Locke's attempts and do his work over again carefully. Kant devoted his life to philosophy and was a master in the sciences, proficient in languages, a man cultivated in literature, a severe student, of the German type, whose long, calm, peaceful years were spent in meditation, lecturing and writing. 

Kant held that the term "transcendent" was employed to designate qualities that lie outside of all "experience," that cannot be brought within the recognized formularies of thought, cannot be reached either by observation or reflection, or explained as the consequences of any discoverable antecedents.
