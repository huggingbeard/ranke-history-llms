PREFACE 

7. 

The author submitted the manuscript of the Ideas to a number of persons. Their comment encouraged him to believe that similar Ideas, incorporated in the future general plan for peace, might prove of value.

The Author's reason for writing the Ideas are:- 

He cannot remember of ever having read of a plan to use children, internationally, to solve problems of war and peace, except during the crusades, otherwise the effort of writing on this theme, for him, would not be intriguing. 

He desires to make a further contribution to the ideals so ably outlined before, during and after the United States participation in World War 1, by that great humanitarian Woodrow Wilson. He enlisted in that war, to sacrifice his life, if necessary, for one of those ideals. He would willingly do so again, but for physical disablement due to that preliminary world-wide struggle for the freedom of mankind. 

The author considers himself to be an uneducated person, and not very intelligent, therefore apology is made to anyone perusing this essay.

He has been intimately associated with the cardinal forces that make for peace and war. The forces have been outlined and analyzed by that other great humanitarian, Herbert Hoover, who with the collaborator Hugh Gibson wrote "The Problem of Lasting 

Peace". 

The author is indebted to these gentlemen for this timely (1942) book; it will prove of inestimable value to those who will plan a future world ordor, and to those who will attempt

The author wishes to thank that progressive educator, Robert M. Hutchins, for the many articles of clear thought written by him on diversified subjects, which has given greater understanding of the power of education to people. 

In January 1941 that courageous leader of men, Franklin Roosevelt, gave to our country ideals the furtherance of any one of which, would be worth, if necessary, the sacrifice of life. To quote:

INTRODUCTION 

15. 

Unconditional Surrender versus Negotiated Armistice The non-participants in the present conflict are the millions of innocent human beings within the United Nations and the Axis Powers' sphere of influence, and the inhabitants of the Neutral Nations. 

A mass of humanity representing over ninety-nine percent of the world's population, is helplessly awaiting their fate, which is in the hands of several million actual combatants guided by a few thousand political and military leaders and five chief executives. 

Of course, the belligerent nations' leadership tells the world the fighting will continue until the opposition surrenders. Any less statement would lessen the moral and fighting spirit of their people. It is certain that the leadership hopes the day of victory will be soon and come before their armies have destroyed so many lives and human values that reconstruction will be beyond their capacity even though victorious.

The vanquished will be even more helpless and certainly un-cooperative. 

It is possible that civilization could be destroyed before an unconditional surrender has been accomplished. 

We believe as Woodrow Wilson knew,-whenever the proper words are used in a formula to stop hostilities, then destruction will stop. This, of course, would result

16. 

in a negotiated armistice; peace to follow when and if certain armistice obligations have been consummated. 

The problem is to assemble the good words, that when considered by the belligerent nations will seem reasonable, and the project they outline practical, honorable and worth a trial with or without jeopardizing their existing military position or possible advantage.

An armistice decided upon; then after a reasonable time, from two to thirty years, has elapsed; it is then thought necessary to continue the slaughter and destruction, humanity will at least have had the benefit of the time interval. 

There hasn't been enough proper intelligence and understanding in the world in the past to prevent wars. 

The dynamic forces for war and peace have, because of the ignorance of the masses, throughout history been periodically on the side of War, so that warfare has beeh considered by leadership most of which is above the average intelligence, and all of which has been improperly educated- to be a necessary evil, or honorable or glorifying, or, that an occasional blood purge is purifying and advances civilization, The title of this essay is on Ideas for Peace; the theme is for the proper education of a mass of humanity, regardless of race, creed, color or place of abode. - for humanity

INITIATION 

19. 

In the City of Alpha, during the year 1943, the first meeting of representative educators, gathered together from all parts of the world, was adjourned. 

These educators were guests of the city whose citizens had underwritten the expense of an initial effort to start an experiment that they hoped would have far-reaching results. 

Earlier in the year a small group had carefully considered the possibilities of such an assembly, It was decided that prominent men, some of whom were in exile from their native lands, should be sent an invitation to attend. 

That with the invitation would be sent an outline of the aims and purposes of the meeting, and a request that, if the person invited could not attend, an available substitute be selected to represent him. 

Available present and past participants in the work of the League of Nations were also invited.

We, the undersigned, after due deliberation and discussion of ideas for peace, at an initial meeting in the city of Alpha, 1943, are hereby resolved:

GENERAL PLAN 

25. 

Outline 

Complying with paragraph five of the RESOLUTION. 

In general the plan is for educating one hundred million boys and girls to work for humanity and establish a better understanding among nations.

The plan is a guide for an experiment which will take several decades, be world-wide in scope, and when completed will have, at the very least, given to the world millions of citizens trained to think and act for the benefit of their fellow men. 

The experiment will take these children through educational training and experiences under the guidance of nations, such as have never been the fortune of children of any age. 

The children participating in the experiment will, at the expense of the nations, be the proteges of the world from the age of ten until they attain the age of twenty-four years. 

At the age of twenty-four, these proteges will return to their birthplaces with a degree of Bachelor of Humanity, given at twenty, and a degree of Bachelor of Laws, Science, Medicine, etc., given after four more years of successful study.

When the former student of this international, educational endeavor has in later life applied his knowledge, in the opinion of man, for humanity he could be given the Accolade of Chivalry of the new freedom for Humanity.

Assuming that the world population is two billion persons,

At the end of twenty-four years, or 1968, the last years class would graduate. Approximately five percent of the world population would then have received international training. 

Long before 1968 the reaction of nations attempting to cooperatively guide the training and attend to the welfare of this many children, would be known. To merely consider ways and means of starting this experiment, by several nations, would be beneficial.

Use of Children 

Making use of children of the world, to further an effort of humanity for better human relations, may seem fantastic and beyond consideration to persons whose children would be used in the experiment.

When the idea has been analysed and intelligently discussed, even with adverse family sentiment considered, we believe the reasoning and trial will prove the theory, that only by the use of children, in their formative years, can humanity accelerate its endeavor to bring peace to this earth. 

The reasons for using children are many and practical, Against their use, few and mostly sentimental. What are they?

Facilities Schools, Peace Training Matriculation 

The initial class in each facilities school, which would be two hundred students, would assemble at the facility nearest the home of the student. 

The students would reside at the facility for their first year's training.

Dispersal First Year 

After the first year's class, in each facility, has received its year of training, it is to be dispersed. The students are to be separated and receive their second year's training at schools located at two hundred different facilities. All students receiving peace training will be returned to their native homes once a year while their education is being completed. 

Second Year 

The facility for the students' second year of training is to be several hundred miles from his home, and/or the facility at which they attended school the first year. 

The second facility for the second year's training is to be within the students' native country. 

The students, at the completion of their second year's training, are to be separated again and enter two hundred different schools for their third year's training.

36. 

IDEAS FOR PE A CE Facilities Schools, Peace Training, Continued Third Year 

The third year's training is to be at the school of a facility located in a foreign country, adjacent to the students' own country. The third year class is to be separated as in previous years.

Fourth Year 

The students will enter schools in another foreign country adjacent to their own country. The fourth year class is to be separated as in previous years.

Summarization 

It will help the author if the reader will try to visualize from the preceding dispersal details that, up to this period of the students' training, they have individually come in contact with eight hundred of their class contemporaries. 

They have attended two schools in their own country, and a school in each of two countries adjacent to their own. They have not been able to make friendships of more than one year's duration.

They have seen more of the world, at the time of their fifteenth birthday, than over ninety-nine percent of the people living on earth in their era, will see in their life time. They will in their travels, have used land, air, and perhaps water transportation facilities. They have not lost contact with their homes or country and, of course, speak in their native dialect, or are literate in their nation's language, and the universal language used at all peace facilities.

Fifth to Ninth Year Inclusive. 

From fifth to ninth year inclusive, the students receive their training at a different facility each year. They have their home vacation periods. Their previous year's class is dispersed in the same manner as in former years to facilities in five other foreign countries. Their geographical progress, commencing at their home, will eventually take each of the students completely around the world and back to their homes, for the vacation period preceding the tenth year of training. 

Tenth Year 

For the tenth year's training the students return to the school of the facility in which they received their first year's work. The class of 1944 meets again in 1954 to exchange ideas and complete their studies for the degree of Bachelor of Humanity. This degree is for a perfected liberal arts education. 

Today's B.C. degree would be the nearest approach to the B.H. degree.

Trial will provide the answer. The foundation has been built. 

If the planning has been well done and the work has been executed with skill, energy, perseverance and cooperation by the nations' political representatives; if the craftsmen, artists, scientists, teachers and others involved in this early stage of the experiment, have been well selected; THEN, it is believed there will already have been something of tremendous value added to humanity's efforts for peace.

The American people are concentrating on the winning of the war for the second time in a generation. 

About seventy Federal agencies and several hundred organizations are working on plans for the future world order.

Ideas for ending the war and preserving the peace are being formulated by the million. Ideas for military apparatus and strategy for the combatants. Ideas for use of the leaders of nations who will eventually make a peace. It seems to be a tremendous task, and every person apparently wants to do what they can, to return the world to normalcy.

In the Ideas for Peace the author has attempted to show, without going into detail, that the war can be ended by the methods used by Woodrow Wilson. That the foundation for World order as built twenty-five years ago, can be re-established, using the same fundamental formula. 

That the idea of a League of Nations is still as true, though as yet untried to its fullest conception, method to bring into being a world with a paramount desire for international peace. The author also tries to show how education is a primary dynamic force for Peace and War and a method whereby this force can be used for the eventual establishment of peace.

The Author's Ideas are, in many respects, incomplete, incomprehensible. They are poorly edited and the subject as a whole seems to be beyond his capacity.
