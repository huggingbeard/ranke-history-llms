FOREWORD 

Sir Hugh Plat was born in 1552 and died in 1608. His life thus spanned the reigns of Mary Tudor and Queen Elizabeth and reached five years into that of James I of England. He was the first man in England to practise the trade of author, dealing with the things of everyday life. 

He was a prolific and popular writer, a man of means who maintained a gardener's paradise at Bishop Hall, Bethnal 

Green and at Kirby Castle. 

Plat was a Cambridge man and a lawyer but Wood in his famous annals of Oxford refers to him as "that great and learned observer." Gerard mentions him in connection with a bread made of parsnips "as my friend master Plat hath set foorth in his book of experimenes, which I have made no tryall of, nor mean to do." James I knighted Plat in the last year of Plat's life, nearly a score of years after he had become recognized as the greatest living authority on soils and manures. 

Plat's earliest writings were anonymous but in 1594 three books appeared with his name as author, each with a separate title page and all three bound together under the title of "The Jewel-house of Art and Nature." This facsimile is one of those books, one which is of particular interest for it is known to be based on one of the previous anonymous publications, a copy of which exists in the British Museum amid a huge collection of Plat's unpublished works. Both were the forerunners of "Delightes for Ladies" which appeared as a separate book in 1602 and was followed by at least eleven later editions, while as late as 1653 new editions of "The Jewel-house of Art and Nature" continued to be printed.
