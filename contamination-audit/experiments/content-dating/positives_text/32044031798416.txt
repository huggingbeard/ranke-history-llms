INTRODUCTION 

Adoption is the legal process which establishes the relationship of parent and child between persons not so related naturally. In brief, the adoption procedure involves: consent to adoption by one or both natural parents or other person or agency having legal custody of the child; placement of the child in the adoptive home; filing of a petition for the child in Probate Court by the adoptive parents; investigation of the petition as directed by the court; and disposition of the petition by the court. 

The data for this study are derived from two sources. 

The principal source is the "Report on Children for Whom Adoption Petitions Were Filed During the Year, 1954," submitted by the Division of Child Welfare Services, State Department of Social Welfare, Topeka, to the Children's Bureau of the Department of Health, Education and Welfare, Washington, D. C.; and additional information from the case cards on which this report was based. The second source of data was the Judicial Council Bulletins, 1940--1955. The only data from the latter source is that which is presented on page 5, "Total Number of 

Adoptions, 1940 to 1955."

The unit of measurement is the child for whom an adoption petition was filed. The 13 children for whom adoption petitions were withdrawn, denied, or dismissed during the 1954 calendar year are included in the data. Because the number of adoption petitions thus disposed of is small, this study uses the terms "adopted children" and "children for whom adoption'petitions were filed" interchangeably. 

Children for whom petitions were filed by relatives and non-relatives are included in this study. Information on step-parent adoptions was not available since the court is no longer required by law to report such information to the Division of Child Welfare Services. This study includes those children placed by child-placing agencies, public and private, by relatives of the child, and those placed "independently" by physicians, lawyers, ministers, friends, and others. 

Acknowledgments for invaluable aid in the study go to the State Department of Social Welfare, particularly to the Division of Child Welfare Services for the basic report and to the Division of Research and Statistics, for compilation of additional information. - 4 -
