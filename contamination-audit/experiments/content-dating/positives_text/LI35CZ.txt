In February, 1954, this Commission issued a report to the Governor and the Legislature recommending a construction and fiscal program for the improvement of the highways in the State of New York. That report reserved certain factors for further study and development. Among these were the possibility of the revision of federal aid to the states for highway construction and reconstruction; the effect of any reallocation of functions or revenues, either within the state or between the state and the federal governments, by reason of the recommendations resulting from the studies being conducted by the Federal Commission on Intergovernmental 

Relations and the Temporary Commission on the Fiscal 

Affairs of State Government; the question of whether the 

Thruway Authority would be authorized to complete all of its proposed sections and the traffic effect of the completed Thruway; the potential for self-sustaining toll rural controlled access expressways; a proposed effective date for the recommended motor fuel tax increase and the amount of State bonds to be authorized.

REALLOCATIONS OF FUNCTIONS AND REVENUES 

At present there are no recommendations reported by the Federal Commission on Intergovernmental Relations or the Temporary Commission on the Fiscal Affairs of State Government nor legislative acts based therson which affect this state's highway program or its recommended financing. 1

AUTHORIZATION OF COMPLETION OF ALL SECTIONS OF THE STATE THRUWAY AND THE TRAFFIC EFFECT OF THE COMPLETED THRUWAY 

In accordance with the recommendations of this Commission, the State Legislature, in its 1954 session, authorized the State Thruway Authority to complete the financing of all of the proposed sections of the State Thruway. At the present time, the Thruway has been opened to traffic from Buffalo to Harriman. In the short period of its operation, total traffic volume and revenues have exceeded estimates but it is impossible at this early date to fairly determine what portion of the traffic is generated traffic and what the ultimate effect will be upon paralleling state highways.

ADDITIONAL TOLL ROAD POTENTIAL 

Final reports on the possibility of converting planned rural controlled access expressways to selfsustaining toll roads have not as yet been completed and therefore this Commission is unable to take a position in connection therewith.

EFFECTIVE DATE OF PROPOSED MOTOR FUEL TAX INCREASE 

The actual construction of highways must always await the availability of funds for the purpose. A substantial portion of the funds required under the -5-

recommended fiscal program will come from the proceeds of a state bond issue and an increase of two cents per gallon on gasoline and three cents per gallon on diesel fuel. The proceeds of the increase in fuel taxes will be used for debt service on the bonds to be issued, with any excess, except in case of emergency, to be used In solely for highway construction or reconstruction, order to effectuate the program, this Commission recommends that the proposed increase in the motor fuels tax be enacted in the 1955 session of the Legislature, effective January 1, 1956, on condition that the proposed bond issue is approved by the electors of the state in the November, 1955 general election. This is in accordance with the schedule for financing a seven-year program of construction set forth in this Commission's previous report. (1954 Legislative Document # 38, Chart 25, Page 54) 

By so doing, the Legislature will have provided the means of servicing the debt authorized to be created by the constitutional amendment. If the people of the state so approve, the authority to borrow and the revenue necessary to service the debt will have a simultaneous beginning. Proposed legislation to effectuate these recommendations and the disposition of the additional motor fuel taxes are set forth in Appendix A. -6-

At the present time, nothing has been determined in connection with the reserved factors which leads this Commission to make any changes in its previously recommended fiscal program.

Future events may require a reconsideration of the program which is flexible enough to permit required adjustments. In the interim, the population of the state continues to increase, continue to increase.

Registrations of motor vehicles 

From a total registration in 1953 of 4,308,687 vehicles in the state, the figure has climbed to 4,530,000 in the year 1954, a 5.1% increase.

Traffic also continues to increase and the figures show a general overall traffic increase in 1954 of nearly 5% over 1953. 

Fortunately, construction costs have remained reasonably stable. This state's highway requirements continue to increase.

Although future changes in factors beyond control of the Governor and the Legislature of this state may require revision of the program already embarked upon, until such time, this Commission feels that the present program should be continued to be progressed. 

ALLEGED DIVERSION 

The Commission has noted that a few groups are claiming that diversion of highway user revenues exists 3 -7-

in the State of New York. The results of an exhaustive survey of the facts involved were fully stated in this Commission's previous report (Legislative Document # 38, 1954). We see no reason to change our previous determination in relation to so-called diversion. 

AMOUNT OF AUTHORIZED BOND ISSUE 

To start the program, the Legislature of this state in its 1954 session passes two sets of concurrent resolutions for the authorization of a state bond issue for highway purposes. After study of the concurrent resolutions and the factors herein discussed, this Commission recommends the repassage of the concurrent resolution of the Senate and Assembly (Assembly Numbers 

3406, 3800, Int. 3173; 1954 session) in the sum of $750,000,000 and submission of this proposed constitutional amendment to the people at the general election in November of 1955. The proposed concurrent resolution is set forth in Appendix B.

It is felt that this is the only safe way to assure the people of this state of the highway construction and reconstruction which is so needed and so desirable.

The adoption of this constitutional amendment will not automatically mean the expenditure of $750,000,000 for highways.

It will only authorize the Legislature to It will only authorize -8-

bond the state to that extent. 

Should legislation following the future report of the Clay Committee provide federal funds in such substantial amounts that the full $750,000,000 of state money would not be required, the Legislature of this state may see fit, in enabling legislation, to reduce the amount of bonds to be authorized. If, on the other hand, the funds are required as presently indicated, for highway construction, they will be available in 1956 and further delay will be avoided. They will likewise be available to provide matching funds to secure federal aid. Until the specifics are known concerning the recommendations of the Clay Committee and the congressional action thereon, this Commission will defer any recommendations as to enabling legislation in connection with the constitutional amendment.
