God has never ceased to warn His people or the nations about the inevitable result of neglect. The traitors on the inside were going into action, but their work was nothing compared to the dastardly act of the king. Without consulting his government or any of his people, Leopold called upon the 

Belgians to lay down their arms. Most of them did this thing and capitulated to the enemy.

This left the British and the French in a most critical position; it meant that they were cut off on all sides, and soon they were surrounded excepting for one passage to the sea, ending at Dunkirk. While the finest British forces fought a rearguard action which will stand out in the annals of the war as one of the greatest and most heroic, the others made their way through the corridor to the sea. It was a helpless and hopeless sight to see these hundreds of thousands of French and British soldiers on the sands and in the fields and in and around Dunkirk with no way of fighting back against the overpowering enemy. 

Hitler commanded Goering to send the entire air force into the action, and it was noticed that hospital ships in particular, plainly marked with a red cross, were attacked. The sand-beaches of Dunkirk made it impossible for boats of any size to come ashore, and the one harbor of Dunkirk was incapable of handling a great

disembarkation. On the field and at home it was felt that surely this was the end. However, God was awake and 

His eye was on the future outcome. His promises to Israel had to be kept; His prophecies regarding Tarshish and the young lions and the other nations must needs be fulfilled, and therefore He must move once more in the affairs of men.

The very finest German pilots were unable to make a hit upon the docks of Dunkirk from which many thousands made their escape. This mystery has never been explained. 

Emergency messages were sent to all nearby British ships, and soon war craft, merchant ships and pleasure craft were speeding across the Channel towards the flaming city of Dunkirk. Enemy ships were going in and out amongst them, airships were thick overhead, zooming down upon the helpless victims; it was a perfect picture of hell on earth. What men or ships could withstand the vicious attacks of the six thousand or more airplanes in the sky? Nevertheless, in this great maelstrom the fighting spirit of the British endured, and they made their way as close to the shores as was possible. 

The little pleasure launches and fishing boats could get close enough to take off many of the soldiers who had marched out shoulder-deep in long columns in perfect order. The perfect morale was a mark of valor.

How could three hundred and fifty thousand soldiers be rescued under such conditions as these? Only God knew, and He took the situation in hand. Fog is the worst enemy of the flier, and God in the days of Moses brought a cloud between the Israelites and their enemy. 

When the Israelites had left Egypt, they found the Red 

Sea before them and the armies of Pharaoh coming after them; it was a hopeless situation. God took the cloud which had been leading them and placed it behind them between their rear ranks and the spearhead of the enemy. The cloud was their rearward. 

This was almost duplicated at Dunkirk; as the people were praying throughout the British Empire as a result of the King's royal proclamation, clouds of fog filled the skies over the Channel and over the land. The entire 

German air force was grounded, and the ships large and small took three hundred and fifty thousand British and French off the sands of Dunkirk. When these warriors walked down the gangplanks to the shores of England, they raised their eyes heavenward in thankfulness for the rain and the fog which God had sent. Prime Minister Churchill declared that Dunkirk was truly a deliverance, and since he has said that, God has been 

England's Deliverer. What a marvelous Saviour is the 

Lord!

Nearly one thousand British ships plowed back and forth over that narrow twenty-mile strip of water be-

tween England and France under the protection of the cloud. It brought to mind Exodus 14:20, "And it came between the camp of the Egyptians and the camp of Israel; and it was a cloud and darkness."

America's greatest newspaper, The New York Times, said, "So long as the English language survives, the word 'Dunkirk' will be spoken with reverence." The 

Toronto Star Weekly said: "Thus have the thoughts of the British turned to God. The people are surely in the mood to render homage for great deliverance and to gird themselves spiritually to the old cry of God and the right, for the test of further battles to come."

Truly such deliverances as these should make the people humble themselves before God and be more eager to serve Him. However, all should remember the words of that great American, Abraham Lincoln, who said: "Let us not be so concerned about the Lord being on our side, but let us be sure that we are on the Lord's side." "Choose you this day whom ye will serve" is an admonition worthy of the respect of all because only righteousness exalteth a nation.

In the year 1941 on March 23, the darkness over Europe forced Britain once more to her knees. The great 

American magazines looked for the answer to the prayers of the British. The result was seen when Hitler's great Balkan advance was thwarted, and when four days

later Yugoslavia made her great decision to stand; and when the same week the British captured Cheren and Harar, and also gave the Italians such a naval trimming in the Mediterranean. God is still on the throne, and He will deliver Tarshish and the young lions until the very time their mission of befriending Israel is accomplished.

As long as the Church, the salt of the earth, remains in the earth, it shall be preserved from the gates of hell. Atheism does not believe in God, but this does not kill Him. The Word of God stands as the foundation for righteousness, and righteousness exalteth a nation.

God's grace is the Scriptures. This record is with us today, its prophecies have been and are being fulfilled.

God, that man shall be saved.
