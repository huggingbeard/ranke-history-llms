Author's Foreword 

In addition to the alarming spread of nudist cults in places where they might least be expected to bob up, the fads for wearing bathing suits, beach pajamas, riding habits, lounging robes, golf knickers and other abbreviated forms of costumes are increasing steadily.

Yet not every man wishes to bare his legs in the Florida sunlight and there are still many women who do not enjoy strutting about in blue satin kimonos.

It is for these, the sane minority of the respectable American social structure, that this volume has been compiled. 

Certain forms of recreation are herewith outlined for those who cling to dignity and the oldfashioned habit of appearing completely dressed. 

The following pages will outline numerous methods for getting a fair share of enjoyment out of life, all the while keeping your vest correctly buttoned or your tippet fetchingly thrown about your shoulders.

These diversions are described for those who

appreciate that long sleeves and long pants have played an outstanding part in the upbuilding of America, and for those who also, because of knockknees or scrawny shoulders, wish to remain inside their business suits or house dresses. 

Most of these pastimes may be enjoyed without wearing false-faces, although for some of the more precarious undertakings herein described, a good mask now and then might prove of great value.

Identity Mistaking

Many of the amusements to be indulged in while properly attired call for two or more players, but the interesting sport of identity mistaking can be enjoyed solo, or all by yourself, as the theme song writers say when they can find something to rhyme with it. 

To play this game, simply leave your abode on a balmy evening or a nice Sunday afternoon.

Stroll toward a street corner where human beings are likely to be passing. 

A citizen approaches. You approach him. You say, "Pardon me, but aren't you the late-ex-president Taft?" He's 

He will probably say, "Oh dear, no! dead." This counts five if you are going to keep a tally. So then you say, "Yes, yes, so he is," and walk on.

Now suppose there is a traffic officer standing out in the center of the street. You walk out to him and say, "Pardon my nearsightedness, but aren't you Commander Byrd?" 

Of course the officer may think that is a fly question, but then you must risk something, even 11

when you order hash. The chances are that the officer will say: 

1. (If you are a lady) "Oh, no, mam, I'm not even a sergeant" or 

2. (If you are the male of the species) "Just where the hell do you get that stuff, trying to kid somebody? My name is O'Flaherty and I got no use for poles," or the like. Remember that it is always best to have a soft answer ready, for unless you own a cafe, the law must be mollified.

Should the approaching gentleman be wearing a beard, you can rush up to him and say, "Well, if it isn't George Bernard Shaw!"

The man thus accosted will undoubtedly say, "Well, it isn't," and straightway begin to wonder if anybody else is going to mistake him for one of the Smith brothers.

Such an answer adds another ten to your score if you are keeping a tally, but few people keep tallies unless you have written the phone number of a snappy redhead on it and then you can never find it on your dresser after the bridge party. 

But should you be playing this contest in England, and the man you accost turn out to really be 

George Bernard Shaw, he will no doubt give you 12

a reply you can sell to one of the comic weeklies and thereby score yourself 100 in effort. 

The more courageous who attempt this little pastime may accost a gentleman walking with a lady by saying, "Well, well, Slippery Sam, and when did you leave Sing Sing?" There is a possibility that he may not reply but will instead punch you in the eye. If he does, you can display the darkened orb to your friends, saying, "Look what a fellow gave me when I asked him if he wasn't a former pickpocket!" Or you can, if you want to, practice a little deception and are a meek and mild old thing anyway, tell them that you ran into the bathroom door when you got up the other night for an aspirin. 

Should you be carrying an umbrella, you can rush up to a lady who may be standing on a corner waiting for a street car. If she has her back to you, you thwack her bruskly across the rear, saying at the same time, "Well, well, Aunt Emma, if seeing you here in (name your city) isn't a sight for sore eyes!" The apologies which follow may lead to a dinner partner, a romance, a courtship, a marriage, or better yet, a prison sentence.

For the lazy ones who object to walking, this game may also be played over the telephone. 13

Simply pick up your directory if you can lift it, and flick over to a certain page. Then play ticktack-toe until your pencil stops on a number. Ask the operator for it, and you may get it. A satisfied customer in Tweep, Ohio, writes that he did, and who knows, it may happen to you, too!

The most satisfying part about playing identity mistaking by phone is that it doesn't make the slightest bit of difference whether or not you get the right number. 

If a man answers, you say, "Hello, is this you, Joe?" Then before he can say anything, hang up the receiver quickly, muttering at the same time, "Wrong number!"

If the person replying happens to use a woman's voice, you may either ask, “Is this you, Gertrude?" or "What are you doing tonight, baby?" 

This latter opening remark may pay dividends. 

A circus manager in Fort Wayne, Indiana, once discovered a girl who weighed 650 pounds by this procedure.

And on the other hand, the grave danger of this phase of the pastime lies in the fact that most of them who answer back quickly, "Nothing—who is this?" generally look like the woman who has 14

charge of the wrinkling division of Potter's Prune

Works. 

If by chance the person replying should answer to the name of Joe or Gertrude, immediately ask if their last name happens to be Heppenstall or

Flounce and that generally silences them. In most cities there are very few Heppenstalls or Flounces in phone books. In the event they admit possession of one of the last names last mentioned, hang up the receiver quickly anyhow, and score yourself a thousand points. Then devote the rest of the evening or afternoon to some of the other forms of amusement outlined in other parts of the book. 15

Speakeasy Booes, or Frightening for Sport and Profit

This game is a holdover from the days when you had to know Mr. Sweeney to get a drink, but it works even better now that you can ask for 

Seagram's right out in the open.

Simply go alone, or with a party of friends, to one of the larger cafes. Select a table that is centrally located, or at least so that you have a good view of the main imbibing room. 

Watch for newcomers and as they settle down in their chairs and put back their means of identification, get ready. You will note that they order drinks all around. One of the party, the leading spirit, and the one who generally is permitted to grab the check, should be selected as your opponent. When the drinks arrive, his nervous fingers will soon twine themselves about his glass. 

He will begin to lift it and will be about to say, "Well, soaks, here's how" and that is when the game begins. You have been tiptoeing over toward him so that you are in back and a little to the left of him as he is about to raise his glass for the toast. 20

Just as he elevates it, in a loud voice, you yell "Booooo!" 

That frightens him and his hand shakes, causing all or most of the drink to spill. Twisting adeptly on one foot you ooze back to your table before he, in the throes of a nervous shock, has had a chance to identify you. His guests will laugh, and he may say something about knocking the block off that guy if he can find him, but general order soon reigns and a reorder of the drink is forthcoming.

The members of your party may participate in this contest by making wagers before you yell "Booooo!" They may bet, 1, that his drink falls on him; 2, that it falls on the table cloth, and 3, that it falls on the floor. One of the party makes book, never giving better than 2 to 1 odds, and a waiter can give you the result a moment after you have yelled. 

Then you and your party settle down and wait for another party coming in. More than three hands of this game may prove dangerous in that by the time you have frightened your third victim the other two may have had enough to drink so that they have been filled with false courage and may take a stick to you. In which case you may 21

need new bridgework or the services of a good physician, the name of whom you may obtain from the headwaiter at no additional cost.

So far frightening for fun has been discussed. 

Should you wish to do it for profit, simply arrange with the proprietor to make necessary the reordering of drinks of the person scared, and he will be glad the cafe proprietor, of course to give you a fifty per cent cut on all fresh drinks ordered because of your little deviltry.

This arrangement has been worked many times by the author who nest-egged a tidy sum because he did not choose as prospective victims men weighing over 135 pounds or standing more than five feet five. A surprising number of persons well within these limits inhabit swilling stations, so who knows, this very chapter may speed you on your way to fame and fortune or at least a few weeks' rest in a pleasant hospital! 22

Waxing Patriotic

Waxing patriotic is not nearly so tiring as waxing floors, and for those who like to appear pompous and important in the civic structure of the community it offers quite a diversity of appeals.

In its simplest form it consists of carrying an umbrella to a parade and when Old Glory is being carried past the corner you are standing on, remove your hat as all good citizens do, and gaze balefully about for some dumb bunny who still is wearing his derby or fedora. Rush up to him and smack at his headpiece, screaming, "Hats off, you dog, the flag is passing!" This will get you the praise of the multitude and no doubt a couple of black eyes if the near-sighted gent happens to be bigger than you are.

In its mildest form it consists of writing letters to the editor signed "Vox Populi" or if you cannot remember the spelling of that, sign them "Taxpayer for Thirty Years," raising hell with certain city officials who do not have the flag pole polished often enough, who allow dust to gather on the statue of General Washington or who are prone 23

to wait until three minutes past sundown by Ayers'

Almanac before taking down the flag in front of the city hall.

Another form is to attend Communist meetings and when the speaker is attacking the constitution or what there is left of it to attack, pull a small size American flag out of your vest and wave it frantically, the while yelling "Sic Semper Tyrannus" and "Long may she wave!" How long she may wave, or rather how long you may wave it, depends on how Red the Communistic followers are or see, and you may end up in the soldiers' and sailors' hospital with all bills paid by the government. 

If you have enough money you may journey to

Washington and filibuster or fill a mailbag or fill something with the petitions you have gathered after your neighbors have signed them calling upon Congress to do this, that or the other about some certain bill before Congress for consideration. But do not be too hard on congressmen because you must realize how little consideration you give most bills that you get mailed to you, even when they have this statement scrawled across them: "This is the last notice before placing in 24

the hands of our attorney," who no doubt has his hands full already.

By careful concentration and years of study even you, too, may learn all the words to "The 

Star Spangled Banner" and at the Flag Day exercises you may outshine the governor, the colonel commanding the state militia and the Forty-First Regimental Band by singing them to the wonder of all. By last count only seven people in the 

United States knew the words to the national anthem and one of them was a mute and he had to say them in sign language which is no help to a soprano trying to lead a group of numbskulls in community singing.

In heckling speakers a good feeling of patriotism helps because you are thus enabled to burst forth with, "But remember, sir, what Patrick 

Henry said" or "John Paul Jones" or any one of a number of fellows most of us read about in school and whose names only occur to us in such capacities these days when Mr. Eisenlohr or some other patriot names a cigar after them. 

But waxing patriotic is a great sport. It costs nothing and shows a great love for your country, especially if you have taken out your first citizenship papers. It might also be well to memorize 25

the names of the presidents from Washington to 

Hoover and to know what's in the president's cabinets except the usual few birds-eye-maple gnarls. Then, when the school board needs a speaker, they will unconsciously think of you and rightly so because waxing patriotic is a great sport and you are, after several essays into this field, a great waxer. 26

How to Speak Volumes 

There comes a time in the life of every married person when they would like to bust the hell out of their connubial partner, and this little pastime is outlined so that learned people, properly attired, may indulge in a sport heretofore limited to drunken beasts and brutal plumbers.

Suppose you are a wife (and many readers have often longed to be) and you are getting damned sick of some of the things your husband says to you. You secretly plan to smack him in the kisser with a flat iron, a rolling pin or a skillet of the weapons used by cartoonists in newspapers any but you realize that in heaving any of the aforementioned weapons you will be considered de trop. And you can't have that happen, not for anything. 

Lure your mate into the library. "You talk too much," you hurl at him and then hurl the dictionary at him. That should ordinarily be all the words for one evening. Should you miss him, heave the volume entitled "Second Thoughts."

If you do not have them, hit him with anything ever written by Elbert Hubbard. 27

And suppose you wish to throw him a curve select "Oliver Twist" and that ought to raise the 

Dickens. And if you wish to inflict serious damage, hit him with "Brass" by Charles G. Norris. If he comes home intoxicated and yaps, let "Main 

Street" rise up and smite him in your wrath, or, as we were saying, the library. That is accepted as a splendid place to sock a husband in. 

Suppose, however, you wish to use a book as a weapon when burglars are coming into your home. 

Simply toss "The Intruders" at them and if that doesn't hit them it will at least let them know you know who they are.

The works of Harold Bell Wright are being tossed about by thousands, particularly critics. 

And women who strike their husbands with books without wanting them to know just why they threw the tome, would do well to select as a weapon Sue's "Mysteries of Paris." 

And those with murder in their hearts might strike their opponent with a copy of the Saturday 

Evening Post of the twelve-foot-bookshelf.

A volume of biting sarcasm might be tossed at one's opponent in mild rebuke and "How Do They 

Get That Way?" serves as a question mark, a 28

mark that is sometimes carried by the human target for years. 

And any book of critics by William Lyons

Phelps is apt to be found deadly so that it should be chosen only after much deliberation. 

Husbands in hurling volumes at their wives will do well to choose books on the domestic sciences which, after they strike the wife on the head, may leave some slight impression and carry with them the mild rebuke that the wives would be better off if they paid more attention to their home duties and less to gadding about. 

Travel books imply the unspoken invitation to get the hell out of here and essays imply that the one struck had better right certain wrongs. 

Of course the gentlemanly husband will choose the volume he hurls at his better half with great skill and, if he is not a coward at heart, will only select light fiction. 29

Hymn-Stitching

Every member of the family can take part in this pastime and neighbors may be invited in, especially if you do not feel equal to inviting them in to repay them for the dinner they invited you to a week ago come Wednesday.

The rules are very simply outlined herein, and a child or a child mind should be able to grasp them firmly between two fingers.

Most everyone, unless he be a rank atheist or a fellow who stopped going to church in order to play golf, has a number of favorite hymns. And you have heard the deacons of the finest churches say time and again if you have ever listened to the deacons of the finest churches, "There is no real good collection of hymns in one volume." 

So if you are much of a hymn singer, you know the weighty wisdom of such a remark. Perhaps in the brown book there is "Old Hundred" but "Abide With Me" is in the green one your Uncle 

Cyrus gave you that Christmas you were expecting a twenty-dollar gold piece. "Rock of Ages" probably is found on page 56 of the black hymnal you took out of the pew when you left the last town 30

you moved away from. And so on down the line.

Even your neighbors, who frequently are your neighbors because of a latent inability to help themselves better their own conditions, have their favorites. So on your invitations to the hymnstitching party you are giving, you say, "Bring all your old hymn books."

Now if you and your neighbors have been singing hymns every Sunday for nigh on to twenty years, the pages and covers of even the best hymn books have become slightly torn, damaged and even, ripped.

The religious among your neighbors will come staggering in bearing armfuls of hymnals and the peekers across the street who have not been invited because you have heard nefarious cuss words floating out of their windows on long cool summer nights will no doubt think you are throwing a stag party or an old-fashion drinking bout. But your conscience is clear and that, for the uninvited. neighbors, is all that matters. 

Well, let's see now, we had the neighbors coming in with armfuls of hymnals. You then give them a thirty-minute talk, which may or may not be written out in advance as fancy (or your wife) dictates, on the fact that something must be done 31

to preserve the good old hymns. Rotarians in the crowd will cheer and there will be much applause to greet the end of your speech, as usual. 

You tell them that the time has come in your community when the good hymns we all love must be brought together under one cover and that they must be preserved for posterity or at least for your oldest boy who can reach a B flat in the tenor clef and is only (fill in his age here). 

You can tell them that rather than sit idly by and see the hymnals fall or be torn apart by consistent thumbing to pages 34, 56, 98, signals over or what, you think each person present should rip up his or her hymn books and stitch the favorite hymns together so that you can find them without having to put on your specs.

They will all agree and then everybody, after being passed a needle and a spool of Clark's No. 

70 black thread, can begin the pleasant pastime of hymn-stitching. 32

Baying at the Moon

We never would have known what quinine was if it hadn't been for Peruvian bark and the barks of many things, particularly dogs, have led us into no end of knowledge. 

You rarely hear of an asthmatic dog because it's owner generally has it chloroformed, and there are few canines suffering from shortening of the breath. No, sir, and the real reason is that just as soon as a full moon shows up they go out and bay at it. That, as Dr. Royal Copeland might have said if he had thought of it before the author (and might even say later if he perchance reads this book), is why dogs rarely have lung trouble.

But leave it to a dog to mix healthful exercise with pleasure. Anytime you grab the idea that a dog doesn't get a lot of sport out of yipping at postmen or barking at passing automobiles, you are nuts. 

If you have a dog you no doubt have been greatly annoyed along about ten o'clock on a bright moonlight night when Hector or Flossie (as some dogs are called) begins to bay at the moon. At first you think maybe the boresome Fletchers from 33

around the corner are going to come over and try to wheedle you into a bridge game on the night you have decided to rest your feet. Then if you are particularly nervous, it no doubt flashes across your mind that perhaps Aunt Hattie has come down from Bar Harbor, or Denver or Tia Juana or wherever Aunt Hattie comes down from.

So you hurriedly turn out the light, and, tiptoeing quietly, you peek through the drawn blind by first neatly punching a hole in it with a hot poker or a cheap lead pencil. 

And what do you see? 

Merely your good old dog Deuce Spot or Trey having himself a swell time testing his baritone at the moon. And you say, "Oh hell," or some other choice expression, go back to the light switch, sink back into your chair and in a bored way begin to finish any book by Harold Bell Wright. 

But a great restlessness steals over you. You fidget and begin to wonder if cod liver oil wouldn't be a great idea if they would only put it up in capsules you could swallow without thinking of prairie oysters or the like.

If you are a quitter, you damn the dog, lock the front and back porch doors and toddle upstairs to bed. If you are a Man in capital letters or a 34

Woman with the same size W, you get back to nature. 

You go upstairs but you do not undress for bed. 

You dress correctly, and in two-four time, go down the steps and open the front door. Your dog Eight Ball (if he is black) or Chip (if he is red or white) looks at you expectantly. 

He may imagine you are going to whip him for vocalizing. But you do not hit the dog under any circumstances, or under the chin. Instead you pat him and point his nose up at the moon. 

Presently he feels himself again, a habit most dogs have, and begins, a bit shamefacedly at first, to bay at that ol' davvil moon. Instead of saying "Shush, dammit!" you take the tenor (or soprano if you are a light, airy fairy Lillian sort of a lass, you know). Thus encouraged, the dog may attempt Schubert's Serenade, in which case you take the obligato if none of your neighbors is looking. 

Then sing the state song of Massachusetts since it is the bay state and before long you will find yourself in a bay state the like of which you have never before experienced.

After you have run over three or four numbers your dog may begin a meaningless series of "Aoww Wooooo!" bays, but these are bayside the 35

point or Bayside, Long Island, so you shake your head sadly, give him the correct pitch and start off singing Lu to the notes of "America" or some other patriotic song.

Then feeling a better man or woman or at least slightly hoarse, you return to the house on a dog trot and so to bed.

After this pastime is enjoyed it might be well to gargle with a light solution of hot water and bay leaves or even a couple of tablets of Bayer's Aspirin.

If you are baying solo, as do so many friendless. souls (who may not be friendless until they have tried this and then they are certain to be) bay your fool head off because the noise will never worry your neighbors who will turn over in the beds and damn the Schultz family next door for having such a noisy dog. They'd never suspect you, since they have seen you coming in out of the rain. countless times and feel that you are in your right mind most of the time. 

After years of moon baying your throat muscles will stand out like a corporal on a parade ground and you will need a collar two sizes larger. And even if you tear your vocal cords you will have had a ripping good time and that's something. 36

Information Bureau

Just as nearly every family has an old bureau. that hasn't got much in it, so most every city, town or hamlet (although we're not quite so sure about Hamlet, since that Bacon-Shakespeare controversy still exists) has an information bureau. It may be run by the Chamber of Commerce, by some wellmeaning railroad company or by Gnatsberg's Department Store.

Anyhow, it is generally to be found just below. a sign reading "Information Bureau" and they are supposed to tell you when the next train goes north, when the next train goes south, when the bank cashier is supposed to go south and a few other choice tidbits. But most of them are the bunk, as you learn to say in the navy.

The correctly attired sport dealing with information bureaus lies in finding out how near or how far away they are from their names. So for shortening, for which most housewives use lard, let us rechristen this game "Misnomer?" and we'll put the question mark there until proven otherwise 37

when it may be xx'd off with a stub pen, a typewriter eraser or a dab of Mother's blueing. 

You set about to ask the person or persons (since if there is a second party behind the wicket he will no doubt be drawn into the contest) a set of, let us say, twenty questions. All right, now say "Twenty questions." There, with that over with, we can proceed. You count each question as five and five times 20 makes a hundred or the stuff they taught us in arithmetic class at school was a lot of baloney unless you had a crush on your math teacher in which case it was erroneous book-learning. 

Very well, having written down your 20 questions (times 5 equals 100, don't forget, ask any certified public accountant). Then you are ready to waltz or fox trot up to the information bureau. 

Suppose it is a railroad information bureau. 

Pulling your pad out and brandishing your lead pencil, you snap at the man, woman, girl or child behind the counter, "Quick, tell me what time the 

2:54 train leaves." If the person replies "At six minutes of three, you damn' dumbbell," mark yourself minus 5 and secretly give the question answerer a B minus for common decency. If the answer is slow or indirect mark yourself up for 5. 38

That accomplished, you are ready to ask your second question. Suppose you next hit him with, "What per cent of all murders are committed by shooting?" This ought to stick them because the question in itself is perfectly killing. It's almost a damned cinch they can't tell you unless they read page 28 of Collier's for February 28, 1931, and the odds are still four to one in the morning line on your side because if people who worked in most information booths could read they would spend more time looking up under "Help Wanted" and less in trying to answer a lot of stickers. 

Question three might easily be, "What happens every time I breathe?" Of course you know the answer is that a Chinaman drops dead, but chances are your grandfather told you that when you were a little tot (if you were) and the odds against the person trying to give you the correct answer having had the same grandfather are 456 to 1. You can almost count on this one for five points. 

Your next might deal with music. "What does it take to make a good male quartette?" How in the hell should the alleged answerer even guess that the answer is five, for without an accompanist 39

it isn't a good male quartette. That's almost an automatic five points for you. 

But if you are big hearted you might be willing to give a sucker an even break, although this is foolish at any stage of the game. Then your ensuing question might be (and of course it might not, for anything can happen in these days of gang warfare and nickel hamburgers): "How many states does the Mississippi River flow through?”

Of course that ought to be easy but if the would be answerer fumblers, give yourself 5.

This might go on for pages and pages but you should begin to get a rough idea of how to play this game if you have any sense at all (something that no doubt your father used to doubt at times, especially when you broke the screen door down trying to get into the back of the house the night he left the front door unlocked for you).

Wrestling--With Problems 

Wrestling is a sport that is as old as the hills, in fact, it is older than the Joneses who have been living next door since years before anyone ever heard of the Hills. To some it is a gripping sport and the hold it has on the American public has never been explained although it has been frequently exposed. Instead of putting one's shoulder to the wheel as in most instances, the successful wrestler becomes famous by putting his opponent's shoulder to the mat.

Many people read about matches and wonder occasionally if some advertised contest is a good one. The best way to tell if a match is a good one is to strike it and if it lights, it is a good match. 

But enough of such flaming persiflage, for the manner in which wrestling may appeal to those interested in getting a mild kick out of life without removing their coats lies chiefly in instruction for the young.

The first thing that one notices about a wrestler except his opponent's arms is the fact that he grunts. Now grunting as done by some of our leading wrestlers is a fine art. For let it be recalled 53

that sometimes 12,000 people attend a wrestling match and to grunt so that your "ooof!" is heard by every cash customer is no easy task to accomplish. 

The annals of sport show us that there was never a successful wrestler who did not grunt. 

Therefore, many aspiring young wrestlers are beginning to realize that the first question likely to be asked them when they call on a promoter with a challenge or a subpoena is, “Can you grunt?" A knowledge of holds isn't nearly as necessary as when one is applying for job as a first mate on a freighter, but the promoters are great showmen, otherwise the customers would tumble instead of the wrestlers.

Those of us interested in helping his fellow man may well realize that a young wrestler can never get very far up the ladder of success unless he is able to grunt. So far, there have been no grunting schools for aspiring wrestlers and that is where the game we are about to have described for us comes in.

Scour your neighborhood (which no doubt could stand it, what with the city water department giving up the old-fashioned water sprinklers in many cities) and you will unearth a group of young fel- 54

lows who would like to take up wrestling. You may find some of them wrestling with packing cases and because they do not grunt correctly are suspected by their employers of being mere workmen.

Invite a group of these boys to your home some evening and then give them some instructions in the art of grunting. A pitch pipe will be found a great help here and if it doesn't work you can pitch it out the window.

Line the youths up so that they stand erect and at attention. To the count of four, have them grunt so that you may recognize championship timber at a glance. When you say three, that is to be the signal for their loudest grunt, in this fashion, "One, two, three-ugh-four!" They then inhale if they happen to be smoking cigarettes or if not, a deep breath never hurt anybody. Then have them grunt up and down the scale in C natural, which is the simplest key and the simpler things are, the more apt those hoping for careers as wrestlers are to master them.

In this fashion, you will find the youths coming to your home regularly and if your wife happens to have an unmarried sister who is ugly, romance 55

may take hold and things a turn for the better, especially if she is a big eater.

You will find that each of your students has his own particular problem to wrestle with and later on, he is given a human opponent. 

But, not, if pleading is of any avail, before you have taught him a certain grunt for use when his opponent has a toe hold on him, another for the flying mare (though this might well be a whinny) and yet another for a half Nelson. The grunt given when an opponent has a full Nelson should be a delight to the ears of all spectators, because a full Nelson generally means one's favorite is likely to take a fall out of somebody ere long. 

And then the wild, half-ape, half-human grunt which is executed in breaking out of a Nelson is a joy to the ear and one that is known by only the most expert of grunters. It is the battle cry used in battling Nelsons, and most everyone has heard of Battling Nelson unless born under Scorpio or a viaduct since 1915, in which case it generally does not matter. 

You can conduct your class somewhat in this manner: "For the toe hold, one two three-ugh-four! 

For the half Nelson, one two three-oomf-four! 56

For the flying mare, one two three-yow-four!

For a jiu jutsu hold, one two three-banzai-four!" (At any distance beyond the first ten rows of ringside seats "banzai" sounds like a swear word in Lithuanian and a lot of Liths go to wrestling matches). "For the headlock, one two threewoop-four!" and so on, far into the night.

When your pupils have distended, a few drops of digitalis may be dropped into them and when they learn their grunts backwards they are ready to make their debuts as wrestlers. In most of us, spinach hating lies inborn. Even the smallest child, his eyes on the stars and that jar of jam on the second shelf in the kitchen, feels the early rumblings of a deep ability for spinach hating.

It should be done openly and orally. We all eschew the secret drinker, that dour fellow who locks his office door and dips down into the lower drawer for a big swig when no one, even his stenographer, is nigh. There comes a time in the life of every one of us, all Pharisees at heart, when we long to look down on our fellow men. It is when you find yourself in just such a mood, especially after a long ride in a hot musty street car, that you play this game.

Mayonnaise Spilling 

One nice thing about the sport to be described is that it may cause consternation, dry cleaning bills, present servants to become former employes and a few other things.

Beginners may learn the fine points of the game in the privacy of their own kitchens. Suppose you have just made some mayonnaise. Or bought some. In order to play, one of these must be done unless you happen to be a provident sort of a soul and have a bottle of mayonnaise around. Well, you take your bottle or dish, as the case may be.

Whatever the case may be, the bottle or dish must contain at least two heaping tablespoonsful of mayonnaise.

The beginner will do well to grasp a piece of bread, upon which has previously been placed a leaf of lettuce still damp from being held under the hydrant or from being in one of those newfangled moisture pans in the higher priced electric refrigerators.

With bread in hand, which in this case is worth at least four or five in the bush, you open the bottle of mayonnaise or dip a spoon into the dish. 72

Then you spread the mayonnaise over the slippery leaf of lettuce. If you are playing solitaire you sneeze and this spills the mayonnaise. If two or more are playing you spell out the word "Constantinople" and on the final "e" joggle your partner's arm, causing the mayonnaise to swish off the lettuce leaf and fall to the floor, your lap, or the sink. A hearty laugh is enjoyed by yourself, if playing solitaire, or by all, if a jolly party is in progress. 

Having tested your spillability no less than 24 times, you are then ready to venture forth to some cheap restaurant. 

On arriving you brush the head waiter aside which will no doubt be news to him because head waiters are generally always brushing something aside if it is only the law or some bread crumbs left by a former occupant of the table you select, and then you prance gingerly to a table. 

With alert eye you gaze at the menu and select something likely to have mayonnaise on it. This you order and sit on your nerve's edge and one of the creaky chairs generally found in cheap restaurants until the waiter starts your way with the food covered with mayonnaise. As he is about to place it before you, you twitch suddenly, so that 73

your left forearm strikes his below, and a little in back of, his right wrist. This of course causes the mayonnaise to spill and the waiter to say "Sapristi!" or some word those foreign devils use. 

Then it is your cue to say, "Ooops! Sorry" and smile like you never had had any intention of spilling the nasty old mayonnaise anyhow. But you know better, you little devil, because you meant to spill it all the time. 

But this time the head waiter, if sober, will come dashing over with all the fire and dash an officious head waiter can command and say, "Did Madame (or Monsieur, as the aforementioned case may be) have a leetle hacssident?" And you grin, absolving the waiter for apparent carelessness and say, "Oh no, it was all my fault!" This saves the waiter his job and if you have stood in the breadline in front of an employment office for three days without even a Salvation Army doughnut in your purse, you know how important it is to save the waiter's job for him because he may have a family at home who don't give a good gosh darn about your little game of mayonnaise spilling.

So the waiter goes out and gets a replica of the original order and unless you are a truly fortitudious soul, you don't spill the replica. 74

Only enemies of progress spill replicas and instead of eating the thing which the waiter may have by this time dusted with a little potassium cyanide, you say, "Really I don't care for any of this (blank - see menu to fill in properly) and I believe I will have some of (again see menu for what you would really like to eat that doesn't come to over eighty-five cents with coffee and dessert.)" 

Everybody will grin and you give yourself 10 for a successful spill.

After you have tested your spillability and the temper of a half dozen waiters and a like number of headwaiters you are ready to tackle the cuisine at the Sherry Netherlands or the Roosevelt. If at the latter place it is always well to request Guy

When you spill mayonnaise at the Sherry Netherlands or the Roosevelt you have the comforting feeling of having spilled mayonnaise with the best of them and this is great pap indeed to inferiority complexes which are not as good as last year's 75

models. Then you have reached the championship class and you only get the world's championship by sneaking out into the kitchen of a Grade A hotel and nudging the salad chef's arm while he is mixing the dressing. He may then give you a dressing down which you can take down if you know shorthand or you may run screaming from the kitchen, crying "That man, he insulted me!" which will cause the head waiter to have consternation all over again and two consternations in one game is quite, quite enough.

The rules of this little game are very simple and so are you if you enjoy playing them. But by no means is the sport herein outlined to be confined to mayonnaise.

Villain Hissing

In keeping with the fact that there is nothing really new under the sun, but that it is spelled differently, the sport of villain hissing has probably been practised by your grandfather and his grandfather and so on, if he ever had enough money to go to a few shows before the undertaker began to work on him.

This is primarily a pastime that is begun by one person, though countless others may join in before the hissing is done with.

The best thing to do is to go to some theatre, if there still is one in your town, devoted to legitimate drama or, choosing the next best thing if there isn't, pick out one running a talking picture.

Of course for beginners, a melodrama is the best choice.

As soon as the villain flashes on the screen if a talkie- or flashes his searchlight (if the actors are appearing in person) you hiss. 

The actor playing the villain likes that because that tells him he has captured the author's idea of the villain to a nicety and if it is a talking picure the house manager will write "So realistic 77

that people in the audience hissed the villain" and

Variety will probably carry his letter in an ad for the picture at some later date. 

As soon as you start to hiss, others in the audience will turn and look at you. Some of them may recognize you and say, generally to their escorts or themselves if alone, "There's good old (fill in your name here) and he's hissing!" Little do they know that you have been studying radiators and have learned that this is the primitive way to let off steam.

If the audience is made up of know-nothings, some of them will begin to hiss you, which starts such a furore of hissing that the play or talking picture may soon be forgotten and the janitor will dash about frantically to see if the steam pipes have burst. 

In villain hissing, the player is warned not to become faint hearted. If at first you don't annoy the villain, the actors, the others who have paid up to $3.30 to get in, hiss, hiss again.

Dentists in the audience will tell their fair companions that the poor fellow no doubt has faulty bridgework and what swell plates they could make him if he would only drop in some time and in that way you are spreading joy because you pro- 78

vide a dentist with a chance to talk about himself when he generally talks about nothing but molars and cavities. 

But you go right on hissing. The villain may stop in his part and look audience-ward. This is known in show business as "breaking him up" and only experience ribbers such as Adam and Joe 

Frisco have ever been able to do this in a big way.

Presently the villain, having been hissed long and lustily by you and such other sportive folk in the house who have caught your infectious hiss and are hissing away for dear life, will slink off the stage and the heroine will be left with her pot of lentils which she can cook for her hero's supper if the gas pressure gets a little better. 

Before long the curtain will come down and perhaps a couple of ushers will also come down and, walking very erect, will ask you to step this way. 

If you are bow-legged or knock-kneed you can tell them you can't but will try your best which ought to be a belly laugh in any house except that theatre in Astoria which is colder than a Pittsburgh audience.

The ushers will then bring you before a guy who draws $50 a week as house manager because he knows Jake Shubert or is a near relation to the 79

Warner Brothers, whose tribe increased by leaps and Levinskys, and he will say, "What is the big idea?" 

You then reply, "Swell show," because the "S" in each word is a sibilant and permits of more and better hissing. He will then ask you to leave the theatre and you tell him no thanks and go back to your seat unless a policeman is barring the way. 

After you have again taken your seat, nudge your next-chair neighbor and say, "How about a little hissing from you this time, pal?" and unless he gets up and goes out, he will join you. As soon as the villain appears you and your newlyfound pal start hissing and ere long you will have made hiss-tory. Eventually you will be thrown out, but think of all the good hisses you have gotten in before you are finally ejected, which means put out in the best of families. 

Over the stewed squab, which can also be pronounced with slight hisses, at the club next day, you can tell the boys or girls you did some high class hissing at the (name the theatre) last night and one sour-faced member of the luncheon club will say "It is about time we were permitted to enjoy ourselves when we pay our good money to see a bad show." You can then suggest a hissing 80

club and take the entire house at 20 per cent off some Monday night when business is bad anyhow and go in for hissing on a large s-s-s-s-scale. 

Of course the game can be played better if there is no villain, in which case the hero may be hissed and people will immediately say, "There is another guy who don't like Rudy Vallee" and the house will choose sides, fifty per cent of them yelling "Hurray for the hisser" and the other half "Throw him out." But little you care for you have come to give the actors a little hiss and they can take it or leave it. 81

Crying Yourself To Sleep 

Every book has to have a last chapter else the printers would be drawing overtime with startling frequency when all that is needed is no more than six day's work and a union card, so what could better be chosen than a game to be played while courting Morpheus?

There is a little spot of sentiment in the hardest of us, so for those of you who enjoy a good bawl, here's a pastime that will wet more pillowslips than the Chinese laundry on the corner. It starts with an idea of your own and ends with snoring unless you sleep on your side. 

This game is called "Crying Yourself to Sleep" and should be played as solitaire for the most part because if you allow your wife to join you, she will no doubt cry herself into a new pair of I. Miller shoes or a mink wrap or at the worst, a rap on the jaw which no gentleman would give a lady except under mitigating circumstances. And unless the weather is cold enough, you will not be under these when retiring. 

The game must be played fully clothed because any person might think of the meat bill and cry 82

themselves to sleep if in pajamas or a night gown and there is no sentiment connected with a meat bill. They are the creation of butchers and this game calls for finesse or a Bermuda onion.

You lie down on the bed, your head uncomfortably gnarled up on a wrinkled pillow. You can start out by thinking of the girl you should have married and didn't. If that doesn't bring tears to your eyes think of her with one-half your brain and of your wife with the other half and if that combination doesn't work, send for a locksmith.

Or if you are a woman, think of all the people who asked you to marry them with one-half your cerebrum and of the mugg you chose with your cerebellum. 

If that doesn't work, you are probably happily married and have no damned business reading this anyhow. If all else fails think of the poor old lady you saw selling apples down street only she wasn't selling apples and you lacked the nerve to run over, shove a ten dollar bill into her wrinkled hand and tell her to go home and make herself some applesauce with her merchandise. If that still fails, think of the man you saw hanged at the Ohio State penitentiary and if that doesn't work, contemplate suicide. 83

Think of a number of ways to kill yourself and having selected one, in your fancy meditate upon what people would say when they realized good old (your name here) had gone and bumped himself or herself off. Feel sorry for your friends, your creditors, only do not dwell too deeply on the latter for you will start such a flow of tears that your neighbors will think there has been another Johnstown flood and that (your name here) is wetter than they thought. 

But anyhow, think up a good reason for tears.

A look through your old memory box may help and if all else fails to bring tears to your face, rush out to the nearest fruit stand and buy a Bermuda onion. eyes Having obtained the onion, hold it close to your for a few minutes and no end of sniffles will follow. Tears, idle tears will begin coursing down your cheeks, course they will, and then hide the Bermuda onion under your pillow for fifteen minutes and when the musical note is heard, if ever, throw it out the window so that it strikes a plod-. ding milk-wagon horse and makes him drive right past the Joneses, who will wonder in the morning who stole their milk.
