FOREWORD 

Any Musician travelling in Europe thrills when he finds himself following the footsteps of Great Musical Men and Women, and from that thrill gets an inspiration which is never forgotten. 

Some places stand out so prominently in Musical History that it hardly seems possible to overlook them and yet when Musicians meet after their travels and compare notes they find many interesting things have been overlooked. 

This little book has been written with the hopes of helping anyone wishing to see the places closely connected with Musical History. 

It is possible that some are omitted which will be considered important but the Author has sincerely tried to point to the outstanding. 

In some cases only the fact that certain composers were born, lived or died in a place is known, but wherever a guide can be found to definite addresses all the obtainable information is given. 

ELEANOR BRIGHAM.

AREZZO, Italy LIST OF PLACES 

The birthplace in 995 of Guido d'Arezzo, Founder of the four line staff.

BADEN-BADEN, Germany 

A tablet on the Little Theatre tells that Hector Berlioz composed his Opera "Beatrice et Bénédicte" for the opening of the charming baroque building in 1860, and that he conducted it himself. 

The landscapes for Weber's "Freischutz" are taken from the scenery about Baden-Baden. 

BAYREUTH, Germany 

In Maximilian Strasse is the Chapel of the Friedhof, where Franz Liszt is buried. On the corner of Wahnfried Strasse & Liszt 

Strasse is the house where Franz Liszt died in the midst of a Wagner Festival in 1886. Franz Liszt was Wagner's father-in-law. 

Richard Wagner lived in Bayreuth from 1872 to 1883. Here the first performance of the "Ring" (Der Ring des Nibelungen) took place in 1876 with the Emperor Wilhelm and King Ludwig both present. "Parsifal" first appeared in 1882. The Wagner Museum occupies the first floor of the Neues Schloss in Ludwig Strasse. In a garden on the right of Richard Wagner Strasse is the Villa Wahn-

fried, Wagner's house built in 1874. It bears the inscription — "Hier, wo mein Wähnen Friedenfand, Wahnfried sei dieses Haus von mir benannt' (I call my house Wahnfried because here my fancies first found peace). Above the house is a graffito by Krausse which depicts Wotan as a Wanderer. 

To the left of Wahnfried is the house of Mrs. Siegfried Wagner. 

Behind the Villa beneath an ivy clad tumulus covered by a granite slab Wagner lies buried. Siegfried Wagner is also buried here.

About fifteen minutes walk north of the Railway Station stands the Wagner theatre built for the performances of the works of Richard Wagner. This is where the Wagner Festivals are given. 

BERGAMO, Italy 

Gaetano Donizetti was born here in 1797 and died here in 1848. 

Composer of "Lucia di Lammermoor," "La Fille du Régiment" and many other operas. The Teatro Gaetano Donizetti is in the Piazza 

Donizetti and there is a monument of Donizetti seated upon a Greek exedra receiving inspiration from a Muse. In the Church of Santa 

Maria Maggiore in the right aisle is a monument to him by Vincenzo Vela.

BERGEN, Norway 

Ole Bull, the famous violinist, was born and died in Bergen,

1810. There is a statue to Ole Bull opposite the Hotel Norge. 

Edward Hagerup Grieg was born and died here, 1843-1907. He is considered as the greatest exponent of Scandinavian Music. In the By-parken there is a statue to his memory. 

BERLIN, Germany 

In the Dreifaltigkeit-Cemetery Felix Mendelssohn is buried to the right of the main path in the Central Section. 

Giacomo Meyerbeer was born in Berlin in 1791. Composer of "Robert le Diable,' 

"Les Huguenots," "L'Africaine," "Le

Prophète" and many other works. Founder of the Meyerbeer Scholarship at the Hochschule.

Ignaz Moscheles taught Felix Mendelssohn in Berlin in 1824. 

In Hildebrandt Strasse is a monument to Richard Wagner by Eberlein.

In the State Academy of Music, Hardenberg Strasse is the Royal 

Collection of Musical Instruments which includes J. S. Bach's Mani chord. The folding Harpischord of Frederick the Great, Pianos that belonged to C. M. von Weber, and Felix Mendelssohn and many other old instruments dating back to 1562.

BERNBURG, Germany 

Franz Abt, the song writer, was Kapellmeister here in 1841.

BIRMINGHAM, England 

Mendelssohn's Oratorio, "Elijah," was given its first performance in Birmingham in 1846.

BOLOGNA, Italy Francisco Antonio Pistocchi founded the First School of Singing in 1700. This was the first School of Music in the world in which vocal instruction was given systematically in several classes. 

Gioachimo Antonio Rossini, composer of "Il Barbiere di Sivig lia" (produced in 1816; hissed the first night and cheered the second); "Guilliaume Tell," and the best known "Stabat Mater," was born in Bologna. He lived there until 1855 in a house on the Via 

Mazzini which he built in 1825 and adorned with inscriptions from Cicero and Virgil. On the Piazza Rossini is the music school where 

Rossini studied in 1807-1810. It is called Liceo Rossini. 

The small Musee di San Petronio in the case 3, Room II, there is a collection of autographs of Orlando di Lasso, Palestrina, and other musicians.

BONN, Germany 

At number 20 Bonngasse is the house where Ludwig van Beethoven was born in 1770. It is now fitted up as a Museum. Portraits, piano, viola, instruments, ear trumpets and manuscript letters

of great interest fill the rooms. 

The garret where this great man was born remains unaltered. Behind the house in the little garden is a bronze bust by Aronson. On the West Side of the Munster Platz is a fine bronze statue of Beethoven, by Ernst Hähnel. Beethoven lived in Bonn until 1792.

Robert and Clara Schumann are buried in the Old Cemetery on the Endenicher Strasse. Robert Schumann died while in the Asylum at Bonn in 1856. There is a Monument to him in the Churchyard opposite the Sternenthor.

BOUGIVAL, France 

Georges Bizet died here in 1875.

BRUGES, Belgium 

The Traveller should under no circumstances miss a Carillon Concert played on the Chimes from the Belfry of Bruges. The Belfry was built in the latter part of the fourteenth century. 

BRUSSELS, Belgium 

There is a very interesting Collection of Musical Instruments dating from the 16th century in the wing of the Conservatoire Royal de Musique at the back of the building. Entrance at Rue aux Laines

11. This is considered one of the most important collections in 

Europe and was founded by V. Mahillon.

Academy of Music with a seated bronze figure of Liszt in the Facade.

CATANIA, Sicily 

Vincenzo Bellini was born in this place in 1801. In the Piazza 

Stesicoro is the monument to Bellini, erected in 1882; the seated figure of the Composer, with figures on the pedestal representing his chief operas, "Norma," "Il Pirata," "La Sonnambula," and "I Puritani." The statue was executed by Monteverdi of Rome. 

In the Piazza Bellini is the Teatro Bellini which was built in 1873-1890. Villa Bellini, a public garden, contains a bust of Bellini. 

In the San Nicolo Church is a special organ one of the finest in Europe. It has five keyboards, 72 stops, and 2916 pipes.

In the garden called Villa Pacini or Flora della Marina is a bust of G. Pacini, composer of operas, who was born in Catania in 1796 and died there in 1876.

CLEVE, Germany

COLOGNE, Germany

COPENHAGEN, Denmark Niels Wilhelm Gade, "The Founder of the Scandinavian School of Music," was born here in 1817. The Danish Government showed their appreciation of him by giving him a life pension in 1876. He died in 1890, a loved citizen of his natal place. 

Edward Hagerup Grieg founded the Copenhagen Concert Society, "Euterpe," for the production of works of young Norwegian Composers.

Jean Jacques Rousseau was born in 1812 on the Grand Rue No.

Felix Mendelssohn's career marked an epoch in the musical life of Leipzig. In 1843 he founded the Conservatorium of Music on 

Beethoven Strasse, which is one of the most famous in Europe. It was started by a legacy from Blümner to the King for the promotion of Art. In Mozart Strasse is the famous Gewandhaus, a music hall, where Mendelssohn conducted concerts for many years. There is a statue to his memory in front of this building. Mendelssohn died in Leipzig in 1847.

Ignaz Mocheles died here in 1870. 

Robert Schumann lived here with his Father-in-law, Friedrich Wieck, and taught in the Conservatorium under Mendelssohn in 1843 where there is a monument to his memory. In the Kaffeebaum, No. 4 

Kleine Fleischergasse, is a corner marked by the fact that Robert Schumann met his friends there and sat many hours at a special table where he wrote some of his most loved music. 

Richard Wagner was born in 1813 at Brühl Strasse 3, which adjoins the Richard Wagner Platz. Wagner had no natural bent for music and until he heard a Beethoven Symphony in 1827 had no desire to study music. He became a student at the Leipzig University,

and Cantor of the Thomasschule. A Wagner symphony was produced in Leipzig in 1833.

LEITOMISCHL, Czecho-Slovakia 

Friedrich Smetana, founder of the Czech School of Composers and composer of the Opera, "The Bartered Bride," and symphonic poems, was born here in 1824.

LE RONCOLE, near Busseto, Italy 

Guiseppe Verdi was born in this tiny village in 1813. His "Stabat Mater," "Il Trovatore," "Il Traviata," and other works so pleased the King of Italy that he gave the title, in 1893, of the Marchese di Busseto. Verdi wrote "Rigoletto" in forty days. 

LONDON, England 

Thomas Arne, composer of "Rule Britannia," is buried in St.

Paul's Churchyard on the West side of the Covent Garden Market. 

The entrance is on Bedford Street. A tablet to his memory has been placed in the Church. He was born in London in 1710. 

Michael William Balfe lived at 12 Seymour Street. He is buried in the Kensal Green Cemetery. There is a memorial to him in the 

North Choir Aisle of Westminster Abbey. He is well known for his 

Opera, "The Bohemian Girl," and the famous and loved song, "I 

Dreamt I Dwelt in Marble Halls.”

William Sterndale Bennett, a pianist of rare ability, who for his art was Knighted by Queen Victoria in 1871, is buried in Westminster Abbey. There is a floor memorial to him in the North Aisle. 

Orlando Gibbons was one of the early organists of the Royal Chapel. He is buried at Canterbury, but has a memorial bust in the 

North Aisle of Westminster Abbey.

Underground Station. 

Sir George Grove, the musical biographer, was born in London. 

Georg Friedrich Handel is buried in Westminster Abbey and the spot is marked by a monument by Roubillac. 

Buckingham Palace Library contains many original manuscripts which he presented to George III; 32 volumes of operas and 21 oratorios. Handel was music teacher to the Prince of Wales and the 

Princess Anne. He became a naturalized Englishman in 1759.

The Foundling Hospital has an organ in its Chapel presented by Handel and he bequeathed to this society a manuscript copy of his "Messiah." Here also is a portrait of Handel painted by Kneller. 

There is another organ said to have belonged to Handel in St. Anne's 

Church at Kew.

In Canons Park in St. Lawrence's Church is an organ on which 

Handel played many times when he was the Duke of Chandos' private choir master from 1718-1721. The Chandos Arms at Edgeware dating back to the fourteenth century, a picturesque house standing on the outskirts of London on the main road to the North of England, is now a Booking Office for Motor Coach Travellers. In this Inn 

Handel ate during the two years he lived at the Canons and here it is that he met the Village Blacksmith whom he immortalized. He also composed his Oratorio, "Esther," there. 

Handel lived at 25 Brook Street, and the house is marked with a tablet.

The Royal Society of Musicians, 12 Lisle Street, has many portraits and mementos of famous musicians. 

In the Royal College of Musicians in the basement is the Donaldson Museum of Musical Instruments (open during school sessions only). Among many other things are the Harmonica and Musical 

Glasses of Benjamin Franklin; a guitar belonging to Louis XV; a zither belonging to Titian; a clavicytherium or upright spinet of the

Quinault, and Scribe. In the first vestibule inside there are statues of Lully, Rameau, Gluck and Handel. The Musee de l'Opera is full

of most interesting things. Spontini's Inkstand. Cherubini's Snuff

Box, Paganini's Fiddle Bow, many portraits of musicians, a model of the Opera House at the Tuileries (1764-70) Gounod's guitar damaged at St. Could by a German Shell, Spontini's piano, Costumes for the stage, many autographs, Berlioz' Academic Uniform.

In the Conservatoire National de Musique, 14 Rue de Madrid, is a very rare collection of musical instruments. This collection is rather difficult to see as it is only open during the School Session. 

The former conductors of the Conservatoire were Cherubini, Auber, Ambroise-Thomas, and Theodore Dubois. Coquelin and Sarah Bernhardt studied elocution here. Violins, Pianos, Clavichords, Batons,

Portraits, Busts, and Manuscripts of many of the greatest musicians are found here, Chopin's Death Mask and the Cast of Paganini's hand.

PESARO, Italy 

Giochino Rossini, "The Swan of Pesaro" was born here in 1792.

He left his entire fortune to found a Musical School in this City. 

PETROGRAD, Russia 

Modist Petrovitch Mussorgski produced "Boris Godunoff" at the Imeprial Opera House, in 1874. Died here in 1881. 

In the foyer of the Conservatorium at Petrograd are statues of

Tschaikovsky and Rubenstein. Near the Imperial-Marie Theatre is a statue to Michail Ivanovitch Glinka the famous song writer. 

Peter Ilich Tschaikovsky died in Petrograd in 1893.

PODOLIA, Poland here.

Ignace Jan Paderewski and Anton Rubinstein were both born 

POTSDAM, Germany 

Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart's playing so impressed King Friedrich Wilhelm that he offered him the post of First Royal Kapellmeistre at 3,000 thaler ($2,250) a year but Mozart out of loyalty to his Emperor declined.

PRAGUE, Czecho-Slovakia 

In 1862 Antonin Dvorak became the conductor of the Bohemian Interims-Theatre. He also held a position as organist at St. Adalbert's Church. He was the first musician to be made a member of the Austrian House of Lords. He died in Prague suddenly in 1904. 

Ignaz Moscheles was born in Prague in 1794. He was a very prolific writer of pedagogic music. It was in this city that he first taught Felix Mendelssohn, taking him from there to Leipzig. 

Wolfgang Mozart wrote many of his most popular operas for

The production at Prague. "Don Juan" was his greatest success. 

Emperor made him "Chamber Composer." A festival opera "La

Clemenza de Tito" was written for the coronation of Emperor Leopold II and was performed the night before the ceremony in 1791. 

Carl Maria von Weber was Kapellmeister in 1813 of the National 

Theatre in Prague. 

REIDING, Hungary 

Franz Liszt was born here in 1811..

ROHRAU ON THE LEITHA, Lower Austria 

Franz Josef Haydn, composer of the "Austrian National Hymn," "The Seasons, "" "The Creation" and many orchestral works, was born here in 1732. There is a monument to him erected by Count 

Harrach. Haydn is truly called "The Father of Orchestral Music." 

ROME, Italy 

St. Cecilia, Christian Martyr and Patron Saint of Music is the supposed inventor of the organ. There is a lovely reclining figure of St. Cecilia at the entrance to the Catacombs. 

Muzio Clementi, the famous composer of the "Gradus ad Parnassum" was born in Rome in 1752.

Franz Liszt lived here for some years and became Abbé Liszt.

The Pope conferred the Order of the Golden Spur on Mozart in 1769 or 1770.

Giovanni Palestrina wrote Church Music with such great success that in 1564 he was made "Maestro" at St. Peters and appointed composer to the Pontificial Chapel. Pope Sixtus V made him "Maestro" of the Sistine Chapel.

In the Palace of the Conservatori, in the third room, is an excellent Collection of Musicians Portraits. 

SALZBURG, Austria 

Michael Haydn, composer of Church Music and brother of the famous Josef is buried in St. Peter's Cemetery. He has a monument in the Abbey Church. At the North entrance of the Cemetery is the 

Stifskeller which was a favorite resort of Haydn and his friends. 

Good wine is still served there.

Wolfgang Mozart was born here in 1756, at 9 Getreidegasse. 

The house now contains a Mozart Museum with many interesting portraits and Mozart's piano. His statue in bronze stands by the 

Schwanthaler in the Mozart Plaza. His airs are to be heard from the chimes of the Neubau (a government building) daily at 7, 11, and 6 o'clock. One should go a little before that time and get a seat in the nearby cafe. On the Markart Platz on the South side is the house of Mozart's Father. Around the corner of the North side is the

Little Marionette Theatre where among the delightful performances is often played the opera that Mozart wrote when he was twelve years old "Bastien & Bastienne." If one ascends the Route de 

Clavaire to the park one finds the little summer house where Mozart composed. There is a bronze statue by Hellmer in front of the house. 

In the Museum Carolin-Augusteum on the Franz Josef Kai there is a collection of Musical Instruments. 

At the Franciscan Monastery there is given daily at 10.30 a performance on the Pansymphonicum, an instrument invented by 

Peter Singer, one of the monks. 

SEESEN, Germany 

Heinrich Steinweg founder of the Steinway Pianoforte started his piano building here in 1820 at the age of 23. In 1839 he held his first exhibition showing one grand, one two-string square and one three-string square.

SOPRON, Austria 

In the Theatre Square is a bronze bust of Franz Liszt.

STAGYRA, Macedonia 

Aristotle, a writer of treatises concerning the system of music was born here in 384 B. C.

STOCKHOLM, Sweden 

Jenny Lind was born here in 1820.

TAVASTELUS, Finland 

Jean Sibelius, Composer of Symphonies, was born here in 1865.

TEUTENDORF, Germany 

Friedrich von Flotow, Composer of "Stradella" and "Martha,' was born here in 1812.

TOROPITZ, Russia 

The birthplace of Modist Petrovitch Mussorgski in 1839.

TISHVIN, Govt. of Novgorod, Russia 

Anton Arensky, Teacher in the Imperial Conservatory of Moscow, 1882; and Conductor of the Court Choir in 1885, was born here in 1862.

Nicholas Rimsky-Korsakow was born in Tishvin in 1844 and became the Director of the Free School of Music in Petrograd. He is best known for his Symphonic Compositions and his Opera "Le Coq d' Or."

TRAPANI, Cicily 

Alessandro Scarlatti, Founder of the "Neapolitan School of 

Music" was born here in 1659.

VESZPRIM, Hungary 

This is the birthplace of the famous violinist Leopold Auer.

VIENNA, Austria 

Ludwig van Beethoven decided to make Vienna his home soon after the death of his mother in 1792 and there he lived until his own death in 1827. Perhaps there is no Musician who has left a recollection of his personality in so many different places in one city. Beethoven lived in at least twenty houses as he moved frequently on account of his difficult disposition and slim pocketbook. 

In 1794 we find him living in Prince Lichnowsky's house as a guest, Alstergasse 45. Here he played many of his first compositions for the Prince's friends. In 1795 he was at Metastasiogasse 35. In the 

Tiefer Graben 24 he spent the years 1799-1800. This is the house described by Czerny. At No. 8 Grinzingerstrasse there is a memorial tablet on the house marking another dwelling place of the Great Musician. Another Beethoven house still standing is Heilingenstadt 

2. Just beyond the city in this suburb called Heiligenstadt is a walk known as the "Beethovengang." It leads to the Kahlenburg and a small park there is known as "Beethovenpark." A bust of Beethoven has been placed there. Another house well worth visiting is at 

Nussdorf, Pfarrplatz 2, an interesting old building dating from the cighteenth century. Another move took Beethoven to the corner of

Auerspergstrasse and Trautsohngasse. In the spring of 1823 he lived at the Villa Pronay at Hetzendorf, a charming old house well worth visiting. In 1825 the Master removed to Schwarzspanierhaus where he lived until his death. Beside the houses we have mentioned Beethoven spent much time in the various watering places near Vienna during the summers. Beethoven was buried in the Währing Church 

Yard but was later removed to the great Central Cemetery where a handsome monument marks the spot. There is hardly ever a day when there is not either a wreath of flowers or some tribute to his Art on his grave. In the Beethovenpark near the Vienna Konzerthaus there is a monument, a life sized figure. Another statue where he often rested on the Beethovengang. In the Heilingenstadter Park another, life sized, showing him with his hands crossed behind his back and bare-headed as he was wont to walk. In the 

Museums in Vienna one finds many of Beethoven's possessions, portraits, manuscripts.

Johannes Brahms lived for thirty years in Vienna in a house that has since been demolished. There is a memorial tablet on the wall of the Polytechnik which now occupies the site. His furniture has been stored with the hopes of sometime having a Brahms Museum where it would be placed exactly as he left it. Brahms died in 1897. 

He is buried in the Musicians Corner of the Central Cemetery. In

the Resselpark there is a fine statue of Brahms, seated in an arm chair looking towards the Musikverein where so many of his compositions had been performed. 

Anton Bruckner, Court Organist and "Master of the Symphony," lived many years in a house marked with a tablet telling the fact, in the Schottenring opposite the Weiner Bankverein. In 1875 be was given position of Lector of Music in the Vienna Conservatory where he remained until 1891. There is an interesting monument to his memory in the Stadtpark. 

Carl Cerzny, pupil of Beethoven and teacher of Liszt, was born in Vienna in 1791 and lived there all his life. He wrote over 1,000 works.

Christian Willibald Gluck, wrote two of his Operas, "Alceste" and "Orfeo," expressly for performance at Vienna. He married a

Viennese lady and lived on the Wiedner Haupstrasse No. 22 most of the time he was in Vienna. There is a simple piece of red marble at the entrance to this house bearing the words "Gluck's Wohnhaus." 

Josef Haydn was a chorister at St. Stephan's for eight years. 

On leaving this position he became 2nd Kapellmeister for Prince Paul Esterházy. In 1762 the Great Esterházy (Prince Nikolaus) succeeded his brother and became a real musical benefactor. Under his regime Haydn had to produce two weekly operatic performances and

two formal concerts, beside daily music. Haydn's genius expanded the Sonata form into the modern Symphony, of which he wrote 125 including overtures. A little house now situated at No. 84 Haydngasse is where Haydn lived the last years of his life and has now become the Haydn Museum. In this house was composed "The Creation" and the "Four Seasons," also the Famous Austrian National Anthem. Unusual honors were accorded to Haydn when he died in 

1809, Napoleon ordering a guard of honor placed about his house. 

Many high French Officers followed him to his grave. He was buried in the Vienna Churchyard but later moved to Eisenstadt where he rests beneath the choir in the little Church for which he wrote most of his masses. Haydn was known as a "National Favorite;" Prince Henry of Prussia gave him a gold medal, and King 

Friedrich Wilhelm II a diamond ring.

Joseph Lanner, the creator of the Viennese Waltz, was born here in 1801. He was buried in the Central Cemetery in the Musicians Corner in 1843.

Theodor Leschetizky lived and taught in Vienna from 1880 until his death. There is an interesting monument to him in the 

Turkenschanz Park.

Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart. In 1762 Mozart was presented to the Emperor Francis I. Mozart produced "Figaro" in Vienna in

and the "Magic Flute" in 1791. "Bastien and Bastienne" was privately performed in 1769. The house in which Mozart died in 1791 has been replaced by a modern building bearing the name of Mozarthof" and is at Hünnergasse 17. On a pedestal in the vestibule is a large bust of Mozart. There is written on the pedastal, "To the unequalled Master of the Art of Music who lived here until his death, the memorial is dedicated upon the rebuilding of this house by Pietro di Galvagni in 1849." Mozart was buried, after a funeral in the open air in front of St. Stephen's Cathedral, in a pauper's grave in St. Mark's Churchyard and the exact spot is not known. Over the place where the body may rest the celebrated singer, Anna Von Hasselt-Barth, caused a memorial to be set up in 1840 and the inscription reads "Early great, late recognized, never equalled." This granite block now constitutes the pedestal on the memorial in front of the little house in Kapuzinerberg where Mozart wrote the "Magic Flute." On the hundredth anniversary of Mozart's death the City of Vienna placed a memorial to Mozart in the St. Mark's Churchyard. It has since been removed to the Central 

Cemetery where it stands among the honorary graves of the great celebrities. There is a lovely statue to Mozart on the Albrechtsplatz behind the Opera House on the site of the Old Kärntnertor-theatre. 

One should also see the Mozartbrunnen on the Mozartplatz, with fig-

ures from the "Magic Flute," Tamino playing the Flute and Pamina leaning against him.

Franz Schubert was born in Vienna in 1797, in a simple one story building Nussdorferstrasse No. 54. It has been made a Museum. A bust of Schubert stands over the door. Schubert was a choir boy in the Jesuitenkollegium for a short time. In 1808 he was elected one of the three choir boys for the Imperial Royal Orchestra. 

His five years in this orchestra formed the greater part of his musical education. He is best known for his lovely songs. He died in 1828 and, as he had wished, was buried beside Beethoven in the Währing Churchyard. Later when Beethoven's body was moved to the Central Cemetery, Schubert's was also moved and placed beside his adored Musician. Schubert's gravestone bears the inscription which translated reads "Music here buried a great treasure but still greater hopes." There is a fine monument to Schubert in the Stadpark. 

Johann Strauss was born in Vienna in 1825, in a house still standing, No. 15 Lerchenfelderstrasse. At the age of fifteen he and

Lanner figure in one of the best orchestras for dance music in the world. The composition of the "Beautiful Blue Danube Waltz" 

gave him the name of the "Waltz King.' At Praterstrasse No. 54 there is a tablet telling that here it was that this Waltz was composed. He received $100 for the composition. There is also a 

---

country house he occupied in the suburb Salmannsdorf. He died in 

1899. In the house in which he died in Johann Straussgasse are many mementos of Strauss, among other things a wreath in silver laurel that was sent him from America. Frau Strausse showed them for many years.

There is a handsome statue to Strauss in the Stadtpark. There is also a statue to Strauss and Lanner together. 

Strauss is buried in the Central Cemetery.

The Museums in Vienna which should particularly interest the Musician are First of all the "Gesellschaft der Musikfreunde" which is housed in the top story of the Musikverein building. There are found very rare manuscripts, Beethoven's soup spoon and cane, Haydn's piano, Haydn's skull which was stolen from the museum in 1809 but was later returned, many plaster casts and over 400 musical instruments of the most various kinds from all parts of the earth. 

Second, a collection of musical instruments that is now contained in the so-called Corps-de-Logis tract of the Neue Hofburg, the "Estensische Sammlung." Among the instruments there is probably the most valuable violin in the world constructed entirely out of tortoise shell for the Empress Maria Theresia; there is also a very interesting instrument called "Orgelschrank," which belonged to the Crown Prince Rudolph. Last of all the Museum of the City of Vienna in the Rathaus building where Mozart's spinet, Lanner's "giraffe pi-

-- ano, many Beethoven mementos and other articles to interest the musician are found. The Haydn and Schubert Museums should not be overlooked.

VENICE, Italy 

Richard Wagner wrote the second act of "Tristan and Isolde" in a house where the Hotel Europe now stands. He died in Venice suddenly in 1883.

Ermanno Wolf-Ferrari, composer of the Oratorio, "Vita Nuova, was born here in 1876. 

In the Museo Civico, room 7, there is an excellent collection of 

Musical Instruments. 

WARSAW, Poland

Sigismund Noszkowski, inventor of a musical notation for the blind, was born in Warsaw in 1848.

WEIDENWANG, Germany 

Christian Willibald Gluck, composor of "Orpheus" and "Iphigenie en Tauride," was born here in 1714.

WEIMAR, Germany 

Johann Sebastian Bach played the violin in the Court Orchestra

for one year in 1703-1704. In 1714 he returned to Weimar and became the Concert Meister.

Karl Philipp Emanuel Bach, originator of the Sonata and Symphonic Forms, was born in Weimar in 1714. 

Franz Liszt lived and taught here for many years. From

Weimar he gave the world the new form of music, "The Symphonic 

Poem." He became Kapellmeister from 1849. Liszt helped Wagner bring out "Lohengrin" in 1850 at Weimar. He conducted the 

Beethoven Festival in 1870. In the Marien-Strasse is the Liszt-Museum in the house occupied by him from 1869 until his death in 1886. 

There is a statue to Liszt by Hahm a little South of the Tempelherrenhaus.

WIESBADEN, Germany 

Franz Abt died here in 1885.

ZELAZOWA, Poland 

In this little place, not far from Warsaw, Frederic Chopin was born in 1810.

ZWICKAU, Germany 

In 1889 there was unveiled a monument to Robert Schumann,

the leader of German Romantic Composers, who was born here in 1810. 

ZURICH, Switzerland 

Franz Abt was Kapellmeister here in 1841. He was one of the greatest song writers that has ever lived. On the Hohe Promenade and the Plaz Promenade are statues of some of the local musicians.
