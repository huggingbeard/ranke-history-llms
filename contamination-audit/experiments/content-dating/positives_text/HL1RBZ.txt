The results of our research indicate that significant savings in time and energy can be realized by amending current Virginia law to allow right turns on red at all locations except where prohibited by a sign.

On the safety side of the right turn on red controversy, we analyzed traffic accident reports at twenty intersections before and after right turn on red and found no significant increase in traffic crashes.

Arguments supporting right turn on red are that it increases intersection capacity, reduces delay and fuel usage, and does not result in increased accident experience.

It was found from the various studies that right turn on red accidents represent an insignificant percentage of all signalized intersection accidents.

For the generally permissive rule states, right turn on red accidents were found to be 0.61% of all signalized intersection accidents.

Right turn on red accidents tend to be less severe and result in fewer injuries than the average intersection accident. The results of the accident analysis support the claim that accidents resulting from the right turn on red are insignificant compared to all signalized intersection accidents and would be supportive of a generally permissive rule. 

The results of both field and computer simulation studies indicate that significant savings in delay accrues to motorists for nearly all conditions as a result of right turn on red.

The actual seconds saved per right turning vehicle were 4.5 seconds. 

Right turn on red reduces fuel consumption and auto emissions for most conditions. 

There could be an average fuel consumption savings of 2.6% for all vehicles in urban areas.

TURNING AGAINST A STEADY RED ARROW 

Under the Uniform Vehicle Code section, a driver facing any steady red signal may turn after stopping and yielding. Thus, if a driver faces a steady red arrow pointing to the right, a driver may turn right unless a sign prohibits the movement. This provision for turning in the direction of a red arrow was added to the Code in the belief that steady red lights should have the same basic meaning. It would be inconsistent to allow turns on circular red lights and ban them on red arrow lights.

PEDESTRIAN CROSSING ON RED 

If a driver is allowed to turn right on red, it is assumed that pedestrians will not be allowed to cross against the red light at the same time.

OBSERVATIONS 

1. This Commentary indicates that the states differ as to when drivers may turn on a steady red light. Some states continue to allow pedestrians to cross on red. Thus, the states are far from agreement on the meaning of a steady red light. Highway users cannot learn different rules to govern how they should proceed at red lights merely because they are walking or driving away from their home community. An effort to educate nonresident motorists and walkers would be wasteful and would be better directed to improving the knowledge of residents. It seems reasonable to suggest that state legislatures should adopt the same rules for red lights as soon as possible. 2. 

California adopted its present turn on red rule in 1947. 

It will take at least 30 years for all 50 states and the District of Columbia to adopt this rule. Hopefully, the spread of future innovations can be accomplished in less time. 

3. While the states have taken almost three decades to adopt turn on red laws, the number of states adopting the rule during the last few years has been impressive. Eighteen states adopted the 

UVC rule during the last two years. This fact would suggest that state legislatures do respond favorably and quickly when a need for action is documented. 4.

Some turn on red laws are poorly drafted and do not protect the rights of pedestrians. 

5. The author of this Commentary has seen signs in Maryland and New Jersey allowing drivers to turn on red without stopping.

This development bears watching. - 27
