AMCP 715-1 

FOREWORD 

The principal objectives of contract administration services are to: (1) Achieve uniformity in dealing with industry on the administration of Government contracts; (2) insure that the materials and services of the required quality are accepted; and (3) insure that materials and services are provided on time and at a reasonable cost to the purchasing activities, and that such additional contract administration tasks as may be requested by the purchasing activities and the program managers are performed. 

This pamphlet provides information concerning procedures for the coordination of actions between the U.S. Army Materiel Command contract purchasing offices and the Defense Contract Administration Services (DCAS), Defense Supply Agency, elements. 

This pamphlet will be revised from time to time to incorporate new information on improved techniques and procedures. Personnel at all levels in AMC are encouraged to submit through channels to the Commanding General, AMC, ATTN: AMCPP-S, suggested changes to this pamphlet based on their operating experience. 

PCO, as used in this pamphlet, denotes the procuring contracting officer, his installation or activity, and the staff available to him. ACO, as used in this pamphlet, denotes the administration contracting officer and his available staff.

1-1707 Value engineering change proposals. 

1-1707.1 References. a. ASPR, Section I, Part 17. 

b. DOD Handbook H-111. 

c. AR 11-26. d. AMCR's 11–12. 11–23, 11-26, and 715-55. e. DSAM 8105.1. f. DSAM 8300.1. 

1-1707.2 General. Value Engineering Change Proposals (VECP's) may be submitted under the provisions of either a value engineering incentive clause or a value engineering program requirement clause (see ASPR 1-1707). VECP's are processed in accordance with the procedure for Engineering 

Change Proposals (ECP's) prescribed by AMCR 11-26 and the additional requirements for expeditious handling set forth in AMCR 11-23. VECP's are evaluated as expeditiously as possible to achieve maximum cost reduction benefits from early implementation of acceptable proposals.

AMCP 715 

1-1707.3 Procedure. a. VECP's are forwarded to the PCO and ACO simultaneously. The ACO 

evaluation of the VECP and recommendations are forwarded to the PCO at the earliest practicable date after receipt of the copy of the VECP. b. The PCO first establishes that the VECP was developed and initiated by the contractor pursuant to the value engineering analysis clause. The PCO then notifies the value engineering component of the receipt of the VECP and forwards the VECP to the responsible engineering activity for a technical evaluation. The request for technical evaluation is made through the project manager if the item is project-managed. The project manager or the responsible engineering activity coordinates proposed change as appropriate. Engineering activity recommendation for approval (rejection) of VECP, or notification when final disposition is expected, is forwarded to the PCO within 15 calendar days from the date the VECP was received by the PCO. An engineering order when technical documentation is involved (or other appropriate documentation when technical documentation is not involved) and cost and savings estimates is also forwarded with the engineering activity's recommendation for VECP approval. Transmittal to the PCO is via the same channels through which request for evaluation was received. c. The PCO maintains a record of the status of the VECP at all times during the evaluation process and is the contact point for all inquiries from the ACO or the contractor. d. The PCO insures that sufficient funds are available to cover any increase in price on the instant contract before accepting a proposal providing for contractor sharing in collateral savings or in future acquisition savings based on the additional quantities stated in the contract. e. If approved, the contract is modified accordingly. If the VECP is rejected, the PCO notifies the ACO and the contractor of the rejection, and the reasons for rejection. f. The contracting officer, who negotiates the supplemental agreement, furnishes data ("before" and "after" item costs, quantities, offsetting costs, etc., considered in the negotiations) on savings to the project manager/requiring element for reporting purposes under the AMC Cost Reduction Program.

1-1800 Postaward orientation of contractors. 

1-1800.1 Reference. ASPR, section I, part 18. 

1-1800.2 General. A postaward orientation conference may be called by either the PCO or ACO. 

The contracting officer determining the need for this conference makes all the necessary arrangements, except that when the PCO initiates the request for a conference he may request the ACO to make the arrangements. 

1-1800.3 Procedure. he:

Procedure. When the PCO makes arrangements for a postaward orientation conference (1) Coordinates with the contractor, the ACO, and various elements of the purchasing office to determine names of participants and agenda items. 3-812 Review and evaluation. 

3-812.1 References. a. ASPR, section III, part 8. 

b. ASPR, section XV. 

c. AFP 70-1-3, Guide for Pricing (for interim use only, pending publication of ASPR Manual).

d. APP, section III, part 8. 

3-812.2 General. a. The PCO may request the ACO to provide a review and evaluation on proposed new or supplemental procurement. Generally, it is expected that this assignment will be used only in exceptional cases in connection with new procurements. The request is accompanied by a copy of the contractor's proposal or indicates that the contractor is to forward the proposal directly to the DCAS region, district, or plant office. Such request for review and evaluation is formally executed by the 

PCO official delegated such responsibility, and indicates the specific areas of interest requiring special attention. b. On postaward contract actions to be executed by the ACO, he is responsible for requesting the review and evaluation. The processing of this request is the same as for new procurement. If the 

ACO is not authorized to conduct the negotiations, he coordinates coverage and extent of review and evaluation with the concerned PCO. 

3-812.3 Procedure. a. Primary responsibility for requesting and coordinating review and evaluation to be performed by the DCAS financial services element is assigned to the pricing organization servicing a particular purchasing office, unless other specific delegation of the responsibility is made. b. Requests are formally prepared and expeditiously forwarded to the concerned ACO, and contain a copy of the contractor's proposal or indicate the manner by which a copy is to be delivered to the ACO. 

The request also indicates specific areas of interest or coverage to be undertaken by the ACO. Informal telephonic or other advance contacts with the ACO also are encouraged in order to expedite a report; however, the formalized request is also forwarded. Records of informal contacts are maintained. c. When procurement lead time is of the essence, the formal request makes note of this fact, and indicates the date by which the ACO's review and evaluation report is desired. Every assistance should be offered the ACO in order to meet a critical reporting deadline, consistent with the desires of the ACO; however, no interference with the ACO's responsibility is contemplated or permitted. d. Analysis and evaluation of postaward actions are the same as cited above. Negotiation and processing of contractual documents are in accordance with the authority delegated to the ACO. 11

3-960. Subcontract approval. 

3-960.1 References. a. ASPR, section III, Part 9. 

b. DSAM 8105.1. 

3-960.2 General. Prime contractors select the subcontractors. The PCO evaluates and agrees upon a contractor's proposed subcontracting in the negotiation of a fair and reasonable price for the prime contract and supplemental agreements thereto. However, formal subcontract consent is made by the ACO following award of the prime contract unless otherwise set forth in the contract make-or-buy program schedule. The ACO has the authority to consent to the placement of subcontracts as provided in the contract. 

3-960.3 Procedure. a. Where the work is complex, the dollar value is substantial, and there is not adequate price competition, the RFP states the requirement for submission of a contractor's plan for subcontracting DD Forms 633 (Contract Pricing Proposal) concurrent with any proposed make-or-buy program. b. In evaluating the contractor's proposal the PCO obtains information on proposed subcontracts from the financial element and ACO as a minimum and other procuring elements, DCAA, SBA, when deemed necessary. The recommendations received are included in the record of prenegotiation conference. c. Where consent to subcontracts has been definitely and finally agreed to during negotiations, the agreement reached is set forth in the resultant contract schedule and the appropriate subcontract clause (appropriately modified ASPR 3–903.1(b)) is a part of the General Provision. d. Where it is determined that closer surveillance of subcontracting is desirable (ASPR 3–903.1(c)), the PCO notifies the ACO of this requirement when assigning administration of the contract to ACO. e. After award of the prime contract, the ACO reviews and consents to subcontracting in accordance with the subcontracts clause. f. For the purpose of subcontract consent, it is not considered necessary to duplicate contractor and ACO records for retention in the procuring element PCO file. g. The PCO negotiates subcontract clause revisions/price adjustments and incorporates, prepares, and distributes supplemental agreements unless the PCO delegates this authority to ACO. 19

22-300 Variation in quantities. 

22-300.1 Reference. a. ASPR 1–325. 

b. ASPR 7-103.4 c. AR 37-21. 

22-300.2 Scope. This part sets forth suggested procedures for allowing and processing deliveries of quantities of supplies that either exceed or fall short of the quantities specified in fixed-price supply contracts. 

22-300.3 General. The variation in quantity clause (ASPR 7-103.4) is applicable only if, and to the extent, specified in the contract, and does not in itself authorize variations. The variation in quantity clause contained in ASPR 1–325 is included in the schedule of fixed-price supply contracts when consistent with general industry practice, it is desirable to permit variations in quantity allowable under 

Article 4 of the General Provisions, Standard Form 32 (ASPR 7-103.4). 

22-300.4 Procedure. a. Processing Variations Permitted by the Contract. Whenever the quantity delivered by a contractor varies within the limits and for the causes permitted by the contract, the deliveries are not accepted until it is determined by the ACO that the overrun or underrun was caused by one or more of the conditions permitted. The ACO immediately informs the PCO of any variation in the contract quantity. The PCO notifies the comptroller of the procuring activity when the variation is authorized. Additional shipping instructions (DD Form 1319, Change Order (or Administrative 

Change)), if required as a result of a variation are requested by the ACO from the procuring activity. b. Processing variations not permitted by the contract. (1) If the contractor produces an excess quantity under a contract which does not provide for any variation in quantity, or if the quantity exceeds the percentage permitted by the contract, the Government is under no obligation to accept the excess. Ordinarily the contractor is notified by the ACO that the Government does not consider itself obligated to accept the extra quantity. 

Where the contractor requests acceptance of the excess quantity, the matter is referred to the PCO. The PCO coordinates the matter with the appropriate segments of the Procuring 

Activity to determine if it is in the best interest of the Government to accept the excess. If the excess is not acceptable, the PCO advises the ACO of action to be taken. If a decision is made by the PCO to accept the excess, the PCO advises the ACO accordingly. The PCO, or 

ACO when delegated this responsibility, negotiates with the contractor for the additional amount and delivery date therefor and the contract is modified accordingly (DD Form 1319). (2) If the contractor requests the Government to accept quantities less than those permitted by the contract, the ACO considers the reasons for the lesser quantity and coordinate with the PCO as to acceptability. The PCO coordinates with the appropriate segments of the Procuring 

Activity and determines whether acceptance is in the best interest of the Government. The 

ACO is informed of the decision of the PCO. The PCO, or ACO when delegated this responsibility, negotiates with the contractor or authorizes the contract price to be appropriately decreased in price and quantity if the lesser quantity is acceptable. The contract is appropriately modified (DD Form 1319). 49

23-100 Contract disputes and appeals. 

23-100.1 References. a. ASPR 1–314. 

b. ASPR 7-103.12. c. ASPR, appendix A. d. APP 30, part 3. e. AMCR 715-12. 

23-100.2 General. a. Where differences arise with contractors during contract administration on questions of fact that cannot be resolved, the written findings and decision are prepared and signed by the ACO on matters for which he has authority to act without further review or approval by the PCO. 

In matters on which the PCO has retained authority and responsibility such as engineering determinations, the PCO issues the findings and decision. b. In either case the ACO or PCO utilizes the specialized skills of engineers, auditors, attorneys, etc., and the PCO or ACO, as appropriate, prior to issuance of the findings and decisions. A copy of the findings and decision of the ACO is forwarded to the PCO concurrently with transmittal to the contractor and, similarly, copies of decisions by PCO will be forwarded to the ACO. 

23-100.3 Procedure. a. Where the contractor appeals a findings and decision of the ACO, the ACO forwards the appeal to the Armed Services Board of Contract Appeals (ASBCA) and concurrently coordinates with the legal element of Defense Contract Administration Services Region (DCASR). 

The legal element promptly (1) assesses the Government's position and advises the ACO; (2) assembles all pertinent facts and documents; (3) assists the ACO in complying with appendix A, part 2, ASPR; and (4) prepares and conducts the Government's case at hearings before the ASBCA, or advises the 

ACO to negotiate a settlement. 52

AMCP 715 b. Where the contractor's appeal is against a findings and decision of the PCO, and the ACO receives a notice of appeal from the contractor, the ACO immediately forwards the notice to the PCO and concurrently coordinates with the legal element, DCASR. The legal element assists the ACO in assembling all available facts and documents required for forwarding to the PCO. c. When the contractor's appeal is forwarded to the PCO pursuant to the procedure in the above paragraph the PCO coordinates with the legal element of the purchasing office. The receipt of a notice of appeal from the decision of the contracting officer is essentially the commencement of an ordinary processing. 24-200 Anticipated or actual late delivery or performance. 

24-200.1 References. a. DSAM 8105.1, Contract Administration Manual for Contract Adminis tration Services. 

b. DSAM 8300.1, Production Procedures Manual. 

c. AMCR 11-15. 

24-200.2 General. The PCO, upon advice from the ACO, takes prompt action when there is an anticipated or actual late delivery or performance to insure that the right of the Government to terminate the contract for default is not jeopardized or lost. 

24-200.3 Procedure. a. Anticipated delays. Upon receipt of information from the ACO,"cure notice and contractor's reply," that the probability exists that the contractor will not make delivery or complete performance on or before the date(s) set forth in the contract, the PCO— (1) Notifies the NICP/PM and other interested activities of the anticipated delay. (2) Reviews the condition which is endangering performance and action being taken to correct same as presented by the contractor and review the ACO comments. (3) After coordination with the NICP/PM, etc., issues a "show cause" notice, extends the contract delivery schedule for an adequate consideration, or takes such other action as deemed appropriate. b. Actual delay. The PCO- (1) Reviews the facts and recommendations submitted by the ACO concerning the contractors delinquency in meeting the contract delivery schedule or performance requirements and notifies the NICP/PM and other interested activities of the delay. (2) After coordination with the NICP/PM, etc., advises the ACO of desired action with respect to issuance of a written notice to the contractor, calling attention to the delinquency. (3) Reviews the contractor's reply to the ACO notice and review the ACO report of the circumstances and recommendations. Advise the supply control point, etc., of the conditions. (4) After coordination with the supply control point, etc., issues a "show cause" notice, extends the contract delivery schedule for an adequate consideration, or takes such other action as deemed appropriate. 55
