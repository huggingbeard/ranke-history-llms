HONOLULU CORRISPONDINT 

Honolulu, Monday, Dec. 8, 1941.

BERTRAM K. HIGGINS MANAGING EDDYTORE, LIBERTY NEWS ASS'N NEW YORK CITY, N. Y. 

Dear Boss: 

Here I am on the job, like you wuz telling me to be, when I hired out by mail to you, to watch proceedings in the Melting Pot of the Pacific. But, Boss, the pot boiled over on me, come yesterday morning and I got kinda caught off base.

Here I was laying peaceful in bed sneering at the comics, when like the well known bolter from the blue out of the nowhere comes a lot of "boombooms" in the air. "What's that?", yelps the Little Woman, jumping up; "It sounds like a war at Pearl Harbor.' 

Ha Ha! you know how silly, woman are, hey 

Eddytore? Making moleskins outa mountain goats, as the saying goes. "Clam yourself, Sole Mia!". I urges, with my best belittling expression, "Whood the war be with? No one aint going to attack the good old U.S.A. Aint you been reading what Lindberg, Nye, Wheeler, et al (that "et al", Eddytore, is French, meaning "other dopes") has been saying at the America First meetings?"

"Americans first is good.", she sneers. "They will be the first Americans to start yelling for the Marines, when the shooting starts."

You see, Eddytore, all a long she's been a sorta 

Eliza trying to cross the Isolationists.

Just then the sound effects get a little louder, and there was a "rat-tat-tating" up in the air, that didn't sound so healthy. "I'm sure there is something wrong!", says the wife, "I'm going to get dressed and go over to my first aid unit." "Nuts", says I, elegantly, "Dont you suppose know a war, when I hear one? Have you forgot that 

I was 21 months in the A.E.F.?" "Goodness knows, My Hero," she replied, with wifely pride, "In the past 20 years, you have never given me an opportunity to forget it.'

Just then an announcer breaks into a church sermon, what was coming over the radio with, "Japanese planes are attacking Pearl Harbor! There are no further details at this time. Do not get excited.", and the radio swings back to the pulpit. "Ha Ha! That guy Orson Wells, sure gets around, doesn't he?" I peeps in one of them hollow-tile voices, as I calmingly arise and slip into my pants. "But he cant fool a Belknap. Keep collective, be calm like me." "If your so cool,", she withers, "What are you doing with your trousers on backwards?"

Just then there is an explosion up the rue (ever the 

French touch, Hey Eddytore?) and quicker that you can say "wela ka hao", the Menace dashes in. The same being my daughter. "Whoopee, Pops!", she yelps, "It's a war! Look what I got! I found it in the street." In her hand wrapped in a rag she's got a piece of bomb splinter. "Lemme see that!", I demands, reaching out and grabbing it, "Ouch! its white hot!". I drops it quick as a flash (I'm quick like that, Eddytore, your lucky you got the right man watching your interests here in this war). "What's the idea?", I demands in my stern father manner, "Bringing dangerous weepons, like that in the house, Where's your brother?" "He's off on his bike to report to his scout-master. 

He's a messenger in the defense force,", she gurgles, as she grabs her girl scout unyform and beats it out the door, "I'm off to my troop, 'bye now."

By this time the Little Woman has slipped on a nurses unyform and grabbing the helmet that was my buzzom companion in 1918, she gives me a fleeting kiss and is gone like the, you know what. "Hey, wait up, Jeanne of the Ark!", I yells after her, "When you coming home?” "Dont wait up for me, My Sweet." she sings out as she flags down a guy driving like a madcap and jumps in, "I'll be seeing you after the war.” 

Eddytore, how do ya like that? That's a helva war. 

What woulda Gen. Sherman have said about one,

where the women and children rush off to it, leaving the sterner sex to keep the home fires burning?

I kinda got off on the wrong foot on your corrispondink, Boss, but from nows-the-on, I'll be in there pitching for Liberty-there are 179,359 Americans in Honolulu, not counting the gobs, soldiers and gireens who are doing the same thing-And, Eddytore, I dont mean your news service either. 

Your Honolulu Corrispondint

WILLIAM JONES BELKNAP, SR. 

(Eddytore, "Sr." means my kid's name is the same as mine. Clever, Huh?) 

P.S. Boss, I forgot to tell you. The U. S. Navy is still the traffic cop at the Cross Roads of the Pacific. 

JAZZ HONOLULU NIGHTS B. K. HIGGINS, LIBERTY, NEWS Ass'n, Friend Boss: 

Thursday, Dec. 11, 1941 

Somewhere on Oahu, "Somewhere on Oahu', I'll bet none of these other smart alecky corrispondints have thought of that one, Hey Eddytore?. Attired in my best Richard-hardingdavis manner, a pair of field glasses and a sun-helmet, I been corrispondint all over the terraine, and Boss, I

got two swell scoops for you. The first one I got from another reporter, Harry Stroup, it is that Marshal Law has taken over the territory, the Marshal is a cousin of John Law. Good goin', Hey Boss? The other news dead-beat I got for you is that when the Civil

Governor checked out, the uncivil Governor closed the gin mills. But dont you worry about me, I got some cached away. 

We got here in Honolulu now, what they call blackouts, and Eddytore, they aint the kind that they used to have at Minski's. I been out every night, keeping my ear to the ground for Liberty. Tuesday night one of those fresh Provisional Police stepped on it. They call 'em Provisional on accounts of the way they eat up the provisions. 

Monday night, I sees a feminine form in the darkeness, while I'm creeping a long. "Aha!", thinks me, "A little romance in the dark, Say Le Gare". 

Approaching, I Sir-walter-raleighed, "Ah there, my little, Night Lark!" "Well, what do you want? Thing!", came the dulcet voice of my mother-in-law. Sherman was right in more ways than one, Hey Boss? 

For twenty years my wife has been wanting to know what I was always doing out all night so much. 

I got the answer now, I been rehearsing for this war blackout. If I get lost, all I gotta do is lay right down in the gutter and I know what part of town I'm in right away.

The other night when I was news-hawking for you, a guard yells out, "Halt! Who goes there?" "Friend!", I answers in a tail-wagging voice. "If your a friend, lend me five bucks." "Gee, I'd like to, Old Man,". I gulps, "but you see...' "Oh, just an enemy hey?" BANG! BANG!”

I ducks into the park and cuts across, only to fall into one of them trench bomb-shelters being dug. I lays down in the bottom with the breathe knocked outa me. An old gaffer with a beard about the length of jolly old Father Time's, sticks his head over the side and pipes, "Dont worry, Sonny, we'll try and get you outa the trenches by Christmas.' 

I crawl outa the trench and start for home, in front of an office building I sees a guy in the dark leaning against a pole. He had looked upon the wine when it was red-viz and to-wit, he was crocked. "Shay, chum," he lapels me, while I chin myself on his breathe, "I'm afraid them provosh poleesh will get me. Howsha bout helping me to my offish on the third floor?" 

Me, being of fine old Samaritan stock, Boss, I ups and starts helping him up the stairs. But on account of the blackout he goes out like a light and I gotta pack him all the way. On the third floor, using the touch system in the dark I finds a door that wuz un-

locked and heaved him in. When I gets down to the ground floor there wuz another fugitive from the 

Keeley Cure. 

"Hey, Brother!", he chirps, "I just dont wanna have the Provosh—” "Never mind,", I says, not the little nettled, "I know, come on." I grabs him, throws him over my shoulder, totes him to the third floor and dumps him in the room with the other Pilgrim. Eddytore, imagine my consternation, when I gets to the street I finds another drunk, hanging on to the pole. He was worse than the first two, his face was all battered up and his clothes wuz the somewhat-the-worse for wear. Without wasting anytime I starts out for him intending to add him to my collection on the third floor, when he sees me coming he gallops up the street in a staggering gait, yelling "Help! Help!". A Provisional cop runs out of a dark doorway, the drunk throws his arms around the guard like he was Hedda Lemarr and sobs. "Save me, Save Me! There's a fifth columnish back there. He's been carrying me upstairsh all night and throwing me down the elevator shaft!" 

I think that's all the corrispondinks, I got for you right now. I gotta do my nursing duties. My wife has got a sister, this sister has got a husband (P.S. 

His name is Elmer), and strange to relate they have begotten themselves an offspring, an Elmerite. They is working on defense and I draws the jolly little chore of singing him to sleep. Last night I tries "Hinky, Dinky, Parley Voo", on him, but no soap. He wants Mother Goose, like his doting mother mor-

pheouses him with. I dont know no Mother Geese. 

But like every son of a brother-in-law, he's adamant pest and insists. No Goose, no Sleep. So thinking back to my happy child-hood at the Gas Works, I get a piece of Goose in my memory. What I cant remem ber, I ad libs, and in my lush tenor I lullibies, "Sing a song of Singapore, Jungle full of "rats", Four and twenty Limeys, Play them with their gats, When the overture opened, The music was rubato, Wasn't that a dandy tune, To play for the Mikado?" 

WILLIAM (Just call me "Crooner") BELKNAP

P.S. And us Irish, thought we were the only ones who could fight at a Wake, Hey Eddytor?

Honolulu Press Club, Dec. 12, 1941

BERTRAM K. HIGGINS, LIBERTY NEWS ASS'N. 

Dear Bert:

I am writing this in the Honolulu Press Club. Gene 

Burns of the Associated Press who brought me here, told me it was and told me to make my self right at

home. I asked him how come the club had a sign over the door reading, ALOHA PANTS PRESSER? 

Gene puts his finger to his lips and hisses, "Sh!

That's to FOOL the enemy. If they was to ever hear that this was press headquarters, they would bomb it in a minute. Go right in, I'll get your membership card and be right back."

So I set up my portable on an ironing board and start pounding out this communikay. The attendent here, is a Chinese fellah and he is giving me some dirty looks and growling into a bunch of pants hanging up. He'll change his tune when Gene gets back with my membership card and he finds out who I am. 

Hey Eddytore?

Dont worry about the "affaire D'Kaneohe" as they say in France. Bill Hipple a newshawker from the 

Star-Bulletin, explained who I was and got me out. 

The head-keeper over there paid me a great complement, he told Bill, “That bird is a natural for a place like this."

Yesterday, thinking about conserving food, I Daniel-booned me a pigeon with Junior's air-rifle. It made a swell pie, but was my face red, when I bit into a piece of rolled up tissue paper and opened it to read, "send second Second Battalion to Kapu Point!" I guess I should not of cooked the feet, hey, Eddytore?

I was down to the Navel Censors, and a bird come up to me and stuck out his hand and says, "My names 

Yarbrough, I'm AP.” "Oh, a Protestant,", I sneers, "My granddad usta

knock the can off you APA's on Avenue A." But it turns out that he belongs to the Associated Press and 

I worms a hot tip for you, outa him.

He says that the way Churchill is going to attack Japan is to march troops over the Great Circle Route, down the Boston Post Road and up the Great White Way, through the Cumberland Gap, over the Mason 

Dixon Line and the Sante Fe Trail, through the

Golden Gate, past the Cross Roads of the Pacific, along the Road to Mandalay, up the Burma Road and sneak them through the Open Door of China and then WHAM! I guess there will be worse than "Dawn coming up like thunder cross the bay," then, hey Boss?

When I showed this dispatch to the blonde at the 

Navel Censors, she shakes her head and says, “Jazz, you are more to be pitied than censored.' Which shows I'm getting along all right at the censor's office, cause pity is akin to love, hey Boss? 

W. J. "Dumb" BELKNAP 

P.S. Boss, that "Dumb" is a little nickname I picked up, all the newshawkers call me that.

HOME FORTIFICATIONS BERTRAM K. HIGGINS 

Honolulu, Dec. 15, 1941

L. N. A.-NEW YORK CITY 

Dear Skipper;,

The U. S. Engineers have been publishing plans in the newspapers for bomb-shelters and urging all the civilians to dig them in their back yards. "Listen, my hero," chirped the Little Woman, "What about our bomb shelter. Am I going to have to dig it?" "You do me an injustice, My Pet," I replied, with my special injured expression, "all us Belknaps have been known as great diggers. Why, in 1918, I wuz known as "Digger" Belknap." "Yeah," chimed in the Young Menace, "And I heard that Aunt Kate was called "Golddigger" Belknap in 1920. "Quiet, daughter," I commanded, "I'll have you understand that my father helped dig the Panama Canal." "I suppose General Goethals' showed him where to dig, and Grandpa dug it singled-handed." she said with family pride, "The only thing you know about a spade, is that you always need one to fill a flush." "How come this child prattles of flushes?", I roared, "Where does she pick up such language?"

"What do you think she's doing while those rowdy friends of yours are taking you to the cleaners, every Friday night? Studying her Sunday School lesson?" "Well, getting back to digging," I remarked, none to quickly, "Great Uncle Ipswich Belknap dug goldbricks from the Comstock Lode." "Dug or sold?" twitted the Menace, "It MUST run in the family, Harry Hughes told me you were a "gold-brick" during the last war.-Mu-u-ther dont let him hit me with that chair.”

The next day there wuz a group of jolly little lads disporting themselves in childish play in front of our house and I rushed out all excited and yelling, "Gold! Gold! In an old trunk under the house, I found this map of buried treasure! (waving my crudely drawn plans for a bomb shelter) Pirate Gold buried in the back yard! We'll be partners, I'll furnish the map and the tools. Come on, Pirate Gold! 

Pieces of eight!"

With me leading the way, we rushes pell-mell, as the saying goes, into the back yard. I hastily marked out a huge "L" about two feet wide on the ground, and soon busy little hands was making the dirt fly.

All was going well for a half hour, when Junior strolls up eating an apple. "Darn swell of you guys," he says between bites, "To help Dad dig his bomb-shelter." "Bomb-shelter!" yelps one particularly vicious look-

Gold! Gold! Gold!

ing little ruffian, "He said it was treasure." Turning to me he demands, "Where's that gold?" "Oh didn't you hear?" coos the Menace from the kitchen window, "That was in the Comstock Lode." 

W. J. "Digger" BELKNAP 

P.S. Boss, I would have writ more, only it is getting dark and the Little Woman lost one of the lids off the kichen stove. I gotta sit on the hole to keep the flames from showing in the blackout. 

PURPLE HEARTED JAZZ 

Honolulu, Dec. 16, 1941

LIBERTY NEWS ASS'N: 

NEW YORK, N. Y. 

Dear Boss:

I am horse-de-combat as the French so cleverly put it and I should be purple hearted.

The other night when I got home in the dark, the neighbors told me there wuz a prowler around the neighborhood. "Rest at ease", I tells them, "Leave that bozo to me."

I goes in the house, gets off the shoes and sox, straps on the old equalizer and sits down by the open window with my feet propped up on the win-

dow sill. It was a beautiful moonlight night, Boss, with not a light showing in the blackout. I got drowsy and dropped off into a little nap. I woke up and there was two black hands reaching in the window. I took a quick shot-and blew two toes off my left foot on the sill. But what I cant understand, Eddytore, when I looked out in the moonlite yard, there wasnt anybody there.

Gene Burns and the other newshawkers sent me a bunch of vegetables, all tied up like flowers. A card read in "To our Hero-dont get toe-maine poison". 

Pretty nice of them, hey, Eddytore?, sending them vegetables, and just when we are threatened with a food shortage. 

W. JONES "Hero" BELKNAP

POETRY IT GIVES 

Honolulu T. H. Dec. 26, 1941 

· VERSIFIER MAGAZINE HOBOKEN, N. Y. 

Dear Mr. Verisifer:

A very earnest young United Press correspondink, Joe Custer was reading a poet I wrote and told me I wasting my time correspondinking and that I oughta spend all my time doing nothing but poeting. "In fact," says he, "You have a touch of Poe, at

least a touch of something. You oughta be with

John Greenleaf Whitter." 

I think he might be kinda kidding me, Mr. Versifier, who ever heard of a guy with a silly name like 

Greenleaf? But he gimme your address and told me you would pay big money for a Christmas poet which you will find enclosed. Please send the money to me care of Casey's Bar, on account I dont want the Little Woman to know I been argumenting my income.

Get set back in your chair, comfortable and hold your hat, because here it tish;

'Twas the night before Christmas, When all through the city, Not a highball was stirring, Oh my! What a pity!

The windows were black, And covered with care, Even your worst enemy, Was forbidden to glare. 

Children as quiet as 

A breeze from the south, The system is to 

Stuff a towel in their mouth.

The daddies were all home, And on Christmas Eve!

Which caused the fair women, To laugh up their sleeve.

When out on the street, There rose a great clatter, (No garbage men there) 

What could be the matter?

II 

Away to the window, I flew with my flash 

Tore open the shutters 

And threw up the sash, A block warden said, "Listen here, Egebert, Pull down that shade, This is an alert." 

I bravely led the way, (By forty-five feet) 

Guiding my clan, To the bomb-proof retreat.

III 

I jumped into the trench, Lay flat on my back, Awaiting the sounds, Of defending "Ack-Ack” "Up Taylor, Up Welch, You heroes of the air," 

I shouted aloud, In greatest despair, "Call out the navy 

And get some marines, Destroyers and aircraft, And big submarines!"

"Come now, my hero, It's time to go to bed, The alert is over," 

My doting wife said, "'Twas only a practice. 

Now 'tis Yuletide, dear 

Merry Christmas and 

A Braver New Year!"

WILLIAM (The Poet) BELKNAP

BITTER BATTLE FOUGHT IN HAWAII CHRISTMAS DAY; FRONTS EXTENDED By JAZZ BELKNAP (LIBERTY NEWS CORRESPONDENT) HONOLULU, T. H. Dec. 28 (Delayed).-In an of ficial communique issued by Gen. Satisfaction here today, it was reported that the forces on the home front held their own in the Christmas Day battle.

Following a war of nerves on the Turks for the past week, they were annihilated by dusk, being cut to pieces and having the stuffing knocked out of them. In last minute skirmishes the Turks rear was the last to be seized.

WINE, WOMEN AND WASSAIL! 

PFC. BILL BELKNAP, JR.

HEW NOA WARA SOUTH PACIFIC 

My Dear Son: 

Well, here is the well known Yuletide and you are off using up war bonds to beat all get out. Ah me, and lackaday, reminds of a Christmas when, me and "Spike" Hennessy and "Iron Jaw" Sullivan had just finished fighting the war to end all wars, just 25 years ago. Time marches on, but you'll find that old man Mars keeps pretty well in step with him. 

Stop me if you've heard this one. ("Who hasn't?", asks your mother, reading over my shoulder.) Me

and Spike and Iron Jaw had just got out of the brig long enough to take a little stroll from that la belle France up to the Rhine. Same being a river, which has watches floating on it, according to the song, but 

I never saw none.

We hiked up hill and down dales till hell wouldn't have it, and if you think that this letter is corny, you shoulda seen our feet. We picked 'em up and laid 'em down till we lands in a little burg called Cochem, laying alongside of the river as pretty as a picture, with a Castle on a hill sticking right up in the air. 

There was vineyards, wine cellars, wine casks and wine Stubens scattered from hell to breakfast and fairly shouting "Pistol Packing Poppa, lay that rifle down," Spike and Iron Jaw wasn't long taking advantage of the situation-and who was I to desert two comrades so far from home?

To add to the horrors of war, we was forced to sleep in beds with sheets. Iron Jaw and Spike and me was quartered in a big home which was lived in by a fella named Herr Öyle, who owned a jewelry store, a wife and three kids and the usual wine cellar. 

His wife was named Frau, in fact it is a very common name in Germany for the positive sex. 

I usta give the kids candy and what not and Mrs.

Frau, soap. Both being almost as scarce then in Germany as it is now. The result being that I was aces, likewise Big Casino with the Mrs. Comes Christmas, she ups and invites the three of us to the Christmas

Eve gathering of the clan. There was roast duck, apple kucken, cousins, wine, uncles, cheese, wine,

aunts, sausage, us, wine and all the delicacies of the season as the saying goes. Oh yes there was wine, too.

About 11 by the clock that night the small fry and what is laughingly called the weaker sex were banished to Morpheus and the statesmen gathered around the festive board for a little right and left handed bibbling.

All was well along the Moselle until along about sixteen o'clock as the navy so quaintly phrases it, when one of the candles on the Xmas tree burns down and the whole tree caught fire, causing no end of much ado. Leaping lightly to my feet ("You very likely had to crawl on your hands and knees," interrupts your mother), I grabbed a rug and doused the flames, all without benefit of clergy. Boy Howdy! 

Was old Herr Oyle grateful? He rushed down into the wine cellar and brought back a vintage that had top billing, 1863.

All HANDS toasted me for saving the house, so after that, about every 15 minutes, I would reach over and set fire to the tree and then jump up and save the house again, just a visiting fireman at heart.

The old man would then rush down three flights of stairs to the wine cellar and get another stoup of his liquid jewelry. Finally I suggested maybe we could save time, if we took the tree and the candles and adjourned to the cellar. "Oh, you Americans," he says, "With your wonderful genius for organization, your ideas on mass results, your capacity for efficiency. 

Come, Herr Spike and Herr Iron Jaw, I will take

Every minutes I set fire to the tree.

you to the cellar, we will drink of the fruit of the vine, and you, Herr Hero, will sample all of my poor cellar." And they did, and he did, and we did, and believe me Xantippe, I did.

I gotta go back and tell you what happened at midnight. At just twelve o'clock, the leader of the Yank band, got up in the old church tower which was built hundreds of years ago and played "Holy Night, Silent Night," on the cornet, the first snow of the season was falling and way up in the castle on the hill, where the General was living, he had put a big cross on top of the turret and the cross was all lit up with electric lights. Everybody went to the windows, and Bill, it was quite a scene; all those old-fashioned buildings and the Cross shining bright and clear through the gently falling snow, the silver notes of the cornet echoing in the night air. It kinda made your throat stop up. For about ten minutes, you really believed in "Peace on Earth, Good Will to 

Men," it almost existed, as time stopped in its flight.

But the cornetist in the tower, playing without a coat on, caught cold and died on New Years Day, and so did the spirit of peace. 

To make a long story longer, the next morning, Herr Oyle woke us up with another bottle of wine and said to step on it (not the wine) cause Mrs. Frau was waiting breakfast for us. She had enough food on the table to start a U.S.O. and after a light repast that lasted about an hour, we begged to be excused. 

Frauie told us she expected us back at noon for dinner. Well, we attended, but were weakening. For dinner we had a surprise, the Germans were a little

short on meat, so, we had horse. "Alright", I says, "But I hope I don't find no harness in the dressing". "Ha ha", chirps Oyle, "You droll Americans, that is good, come we drink to that". "Oh, a stirrup cup", cracks your father, "I have heard of them, but never had one before. Here's how." But he knew how.

That ended the day's festivities, except we hadda have Koffee Trincken at four, only there wasn't much coffee drunk. At seven o'clock, we hadda go to a dinner, what three American Signal Corps girls had fixed for us. It was suckling pig and roast turkey with trimmings. They said, "We know you boys are just starved for a good meal, so eat hearty”. 

Yessir, Bill, Sherman was right war is hell-on the digestion. 

Your father,

BILL SR.

DAD

Dear Abe:

You think that you had people stabbing your war effort in the back? You oughta see a guy we got now John L. Lewis, there's a bambino that Grant woulda court-martialed and shot, and even you, who was good at it, couldn't of thought up a reason for pardoning him.

Congress still kicks over the traces every once in a while. A lot of dumb-bunnies, lame wits and cheap politicians still make the Congressional Record the world's greatest joke book, while a few hard-workers in both houses strip down to their galluses and see that the wheels of Democracy go round.

That word Democracy is one that we use a lot and I don't believe any of us know just what is means. 

What the Hell did it mean in your day, Abe? 

I hope I don't paint too bad a picture of your country, Abe, there's not too many skunks in it and there is over a hundred million of em that are just as solid as Israel Putnam, John Paul Jones, Dan Boone and Sam Houston. You done just right, tho, Pres., We cant have them Vice Presidents

serving term after term, we havta have some change in the Administration now and then. Your idea of having a new Vice president every term is a good one. I hope you bring your sons up the same way.

When nobody knew where you was, I hear that they were forming a safari to search the Pentagon building for you, thinking that you might have gone to see the Secretary of War and gotten lost. They were already to start, only waiting to find some experienced guides that knew the building. And all the time you was right here in Honolulu. Aint science wonderful? 

I was your nearest neighbor at Waikiki. We lived just over that nice barbed wire fence they strung up. Right over in Kapiolani park by the monkey cages. It is kinda embarrassing sometimes to have the visitors throwing peanuts at us every time we come on the front porch and I'm trying to sue that fresh kid that yelled the other day, when I came outa the house, "Ooh, look, Mom, there's one with pants on." His mother didn't help none by saying, "Hush, Benny, maybe you'll make the other ones jealous." 

Sorry you didn't get to drop in and see us, but I know how nosey them secret service guys are. 

I guess you remember seeing me tho, I was the guy that waved at you as I was coming out of the 

Depot Pawn Shop as you went by. 

I think that Doug saw me, too, but he didn't let on. I'll bet that made him feel pretty important,

when he saw that trailer lunch wagon near your house at Waikiki with the sign on it MacArthur Hot Dog Stand. Between you and I, Franklin, I think that that trailer was headquarters for the start of a "Mac- 

Arthur for President" boom, but they just switched over to Hot Dawgs real quick when they heard you was coming to town.

Well, you are sure giving us our civil rights back to us as quick as you can. Getting the blackout dropped and lights on the cars has been OK. And the speed limit at night is back to 35 mile per hour.

You can see we are taking full advantage of it. Our traffic death rate jumped right up and our drunk driving arrest rate quickened right away. 

I see where the Board of Supervisors want to have the ten o'clock blackout lifted, but dont you pay no mind to them. There's an election coming up and they just dont wanta stop talking at 10 P.M. 

I see where you been having a little trouble with this guy Nelson, that's a good idea sending him to 

China, he'll behave when he comes back, look how good Hank Wallace was when he returned. Sending them to China shows that the New Deal has a kind heart. The Czar of Russia used to send them to Siberia and its cold there. 

Well, give my regards to your sons. Between them and the sons of that kin of Eleanor's, Teddy Roosevelt, they aint tarnishing the family name none. 

Your Waikiki Neighbor

Jazz

P.S. You were a nice quiet neighbor, and if you ever want a letter of recommendation along these lines let me know. You only got one bad habit and that letting them newspapermen clutter up the neighborhood on press conference days.

W. J. B.
