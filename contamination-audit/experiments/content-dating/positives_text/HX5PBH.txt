I DEDICATE THIS BOOK 

ΤΟ Those whom I once thought friends, but who, in the extremities of adversity and prosperity taught me the folly of most friendships.

PREFACE 

Don't read this book unless you have a sense of humor. You'll need it before you get through.

Although it was intended as strictly cynical observations, I find that some philosophy has crept into various of the paragraphs. 

For which I will not apologize-the reader can take it or leave it.

Anyhow, these writings are original, with exception of the scant half dozen or so quotations I have made in emphasizing some subject. 

The critic will aver that I wrote some of the stuff just to hear myself talk.

In fact a friend (?) who glanced at it at the start, told me that only a conceited jackass would write such piffle. ME conceited-why my dear girl-I own one of the world's worst inferiority complexes. What you mistake for conceit, is merely just a show of self confidence to avoid my forever bowing my head with shame at my utter worthlessness ineffectuality and uselessness.

Dear reader, the only reason I am continuing to live on this awful earth, is the wonderful kick I get out of trying to figure just what is going to happen to me next.

At least I hope now that I don't get thrown into jail for accepting money under false pretensesby some of the readers of this book who paid their good money expecting to read something interesting.

I may be cynical, and these writings are my own convictions—but at least I am sincere in my observations and I don't expect everybody, or in fact, any one to agree with me.

Every person is entitled to their own opinionsand I don't continually argue with every one to make them see my way of thinking. 

Live your own life-live your own way—just so you don't interfere with me-I'll not try to change you or reform you. In other words just mind your own business, and I will mind mine. 

P.S.--Just met the young lady who criticised me, first time I had seen her for a year. She says "I take back my criticism of your Book, I think I'll write one myself." Gosh-what is this? Must be the New Deal.

INGRATITUDE 

A wonderful saying, that:- "There is nothing sure in this life but Death and Taxes." 

May I add "and ingratitude."

THE OPTIMIST 

The antithesis of a cynic is the optimist-the cheerful idiot who is too dumb to realize what an ass the rest of mankind is making of him. 

THE PESSIMIST 

An optimist is one who thinks that everyone is honest and good until they have proved otherwise. 

A pessimist is one who considers everyone crooked until proof has been offered to the contrary.

The advantage of being a pessimist is, you get stung less often.

SOCIETY-WITH A CAPITAL S 

Society, after all, consists not so much in having that "refinement" which should be the main attribute of this supposedly higher social order; butin having just a little more nerve than the other person in crashing one's self into prominence and the public eye.

THE SOCIAL STRATUM 

There is only a thin hair line dividing the various social strata in this country. 

Formerly, about the only difference was, that the higher social order took more time for lunch. 

However, the past few years have changed this.

The ones now taking the most time for meals are the unemployed at one end of the bread line, and the social parasites at the other end. 

ANCESTOR DELUSION 

Nothing makes so much for conceit as pride of ancestry.

Some ancient ancestor may have made a lucky stroke, received the headlines of their day. Their descendants for centuries to come will bask in the reflected glory of it. 

It is a good thing that the dead cannot speak because if all were known of the past lives of our ancestors, we would probably wish to be buried alive for shame.

THE SNOB 

Pity the poor snobs. They suffer the illusion of pride of-what-they alone know. 10

Generally speaking-a snob is one who visions grandeur or achievement without the formality of working for it. 

THE PLEASURE OF DYING 

Present day civilization is more and more destroying idealism-real love-worth while ambitions. The race is to the swift. Only those attuned to daily slavery of mind and body can hope to succeed. 

And succeed to what? Merely that peace and quiet that comes only when you lie in your grave. 

Why is it?

THE MARTYR 

Take the man who is continually being of service-who does little things and big things for his family and friends, day in and day out.

Once in a while he will be unkind. 

Perhaps a cross to bear or burdens to carry, makes him thoughtless for a few moments. 

Alas! his past good works are forgotten. He is called all manner of names.

On the other hand, we have a man who is continually unkind-who does little if any good to any one. 11

Once in a while he will be in good humor.

He will make a concession-do a kind act. 

Alas! He is the salt of the earth-for weeks afterward his habitual unkindnesses are forgotten and everyone thinks what a fine fellow he "can be." 

Moral: The kindly chap must needs get his reward from his conscience-for gratitude is fickle and short lived.

THE DUMB RACE 

The world criticises a man or woman because, perhaps, they are silent or sad, or are presumed to have a grouch.

Often as not, they are suffering under some burden, and are too brave to whine. 

Which only goes to prove that, after all, the human race is too dumb to have understanding of the problems of their neighbors, and too selfish to care.

THE CHIVALROUS GENTLEMAN 

The chap who is the soul of chivalry, who jumps up and gives the lady his chair, who is known as a "fine gentleman," is most often the one who abuses his family in private; which goes to show that most of our life is a pose a sham; at least for the majority. 12

The poser gets credit for being a "wonderful" fellow; while the really good hearted chap, who works in a quiet way, his many duties to perform, etc., is overlooked, or more often, just cussed at. 

DELUSIONS 

Human nature always reverts to type sooner or later.

We spend weeks and months cultivating a real friendship, or a lover; praising them for their numerous good qualities.

Familiarity then creeps in. We exaggerate their occasional imperfections.

We wake up with a shock to find that, after all, they are not absolutely perfect.

We forget that there is no such animal as a "perfect" human being. However, we never seem to learn this lesson.

We are not philosophical enough to realize that people should be graded with tolerance, and if their virtues far outweigh their occasional faults, nothing more should be required or sought. 

THE STRANGER GETS THE CHOICEST MORSEL 

The ways of humans are, indeed, strange.

13

You are supposed to be, and probably brag about, your being kind hearted.

It is probably only a selfish pose, as exhibit:- 

You meet a perfect stranger, and invite him as a guest to your home. 

You pour out all your best smiles, your wit, your money on one who may little deserve it. In the background is forced one or two of our loved ones, who may need the kind words far more than the stranger.

We strive forever to make an impression upon strangers and friends, and neglect the ones we should really care for the most, being abrupt and unkind.

Our trouble is, that we do too little real thinking. 

It is a wonder that most of us haven't sense enough to give our loved ones the kind words, our kind help, and what is left over distribute to the friends and acquaintances. 

WHY LIVE? 

It is a great mystery to me why everybody on this earth is so intent to keep on living. 

They are fooled by fleeting pleasures, mostly shallow; the gossiping among friends (?); the oc- 14

casional praise or flattery, creating that warmish, genial feeling, exhiliarating but deceitful; the pleasures of the palate, of the flesh, of the eye and the ear. 

The only real compensation comes to the serious minded, and is in your own state of mind. The study or reading of some hobby or subject; or those of us who have music in our souls, and who get our greatest thrill out of listening to delightful melodies, but alas, these are few and far between. 

The deceit, the misunderstanding, the ill health, the ingratitude, the struggle of working like a slave incessantly. All this to obtain the meagre pleasures that occasionally come.

For my own part I am keeping up the farce of living because I am obsessed with an abnormal curiosity.

I get my greatest kick out of life, by trying to figure just what is going to happen next. 

MAKING OF A CYNIC 

Carlyle says: "No man lives without jostling and being jostled; in all ways he has to elbow himself through the world, giving and receiving offense." 

Which explains more aptly than I can put it why cynics are born and bred. 15

It takes an unusual optimist to go through life without being a confirmed grouch.

DEFINITION OF A CYNIC 

Oscar Wilde says: "The cynic is one who knows the price of everything, and the value of nothing." 

To which I of course must disagree.

My own definition of a cynic is "He whose experience has taught him the frailties, the weaknesses, the dumbness, and the selfishness of most of the human race." (Including myself, of course, if you say so I've a thick hide-so don't mind.)

RELIGION 

Religion, which above all, should be the most unselfish of all thoughts, has, in past centuries, proven the battleground of wars and hatreds. 

Religion as I understand it, is the faculty of living in such manner, that you go through life doing the most good to others.

Nevertheless, this country has divided itself into factions of Catholics, and scores of various Protestant denominations, not to mention many other sects.

They have all lost sight of the main goal of religion, and instead, spend most of their time in 16

selfish pleading to get as many converts as they can to their own particular creed.

The impossibility of ever getting the various adherents to agree on one common Church, is perhaps, the main reason why so many people join no congregation whatever.

THEY CALL THEMSELVES CHRISTIANS 

Consider the sulkers-the church is full of them. 

They listen with rapt attention to discussions on sin and service to humanity, and blindly think they are following the precepts of their God.

Yet when differences of opinion arise they are too selfish to realize that a compromise on ideas is the foundation of Christianity. Instead, they sulk, make themselves generally disagreeable and fail to give that cooperation and sacrifice in small things that is so necessary to uphold and carry on what should be the Church's main goal. That goal is:

Service and helpfulness to deserving humanity. one.

RELIGION 

Religion is a mysterious thing for nearly every The majority do not understand what it is all about, but they have a superstitious fear of what might or might not happen to them in the hereafter, and thus, like cowards, they follow 17

blindly the crowd, go to church once in a while, or at least profess sympathy or connection with some sect. 

BUYING RELIGION 

A good many men buy their religion the same as they buy their food.

They pay so much, and regularly listen to theological discussions for one hour on Sunday each week, and then forget it.

The only difference from food is, that they take food oftener, and never forget it. 

Also, they pay as little as they can for their religion, but no cost is too high for what they stuff in their stomachs.

HYPOCRITICAL CHURCHGOERS 

Men go to church and sing with fervor: "God be with you till we meet again."

That's just it-they wish the Lord to be with their neighbors until they meet them again next Sunday but they fail to take God and His policies with themselves in their business dealings during the week.

THE MINISTER'S PAY 

The salaries drawn by the average Minister of 18

the Gospel is another of our national disgraces. 

Excepting the few wealthier congregations-the average pastor is paid less than some unintelligent laborers.

Which probably goes to prove that the majority of us want our religion cheaper than any other necessity of life, and is a sad commentary on Christianity in general.

WHY GROW OLD? 

It is a sad fact that as we grow older, we grow more mellow, less beliggerent. It is a sure sign of approaching old age, not only in years, but in mind, when we start to compromise. 

YES I KNOW NOTHING 

No doubt the saying is true, that those who talk the most, generally know the least-hence, this book establishes me pretty solidly as a dumb-bell; for which I should no doubt give thanks, as I will not then be bothered with any one asking my opinion on asinine subjects.

JUSTICE?-TRY AND GET IT 

Justice?--Certainly, if you have money enough to hire just a little better lawyer than the other fellow. 19

THE ULTIMATE OF SATISFACTION 

To be a good sailor, to sit in the sunshine on the deck of an ocean liner on a perfect day in June, with the boat rolling and pitching in a heavy sea, and watching the majority of the passengers with their hands pressed to their stomachs, leaning over the rail.

DEFINITION OF A LAWYER 

A gentleman of intelligence, who believes the shortest distance between two given points, is to go around in a circle-and who also believes that to use 500 words (one-half of which have to be looked up in the dictionary) is the correct definition of a short sentence.

FOOLS FOREVER 

One trouble with the world and its continual unhappiness through faulty judgment, errors and sins committed, etc., is that the average person does not live long enough to derive much benefit from past experience. Even at that, the majority of us would still be making the same mistakes over and over again, even though we lived for a thousand years. 

As the saying goes: "There is no fool like an old fool." 20

HYPOCRITICAL MOURNERS 

Mourners at a funeral-a pathetic sight. 

The deceased will be eulogized and praised to the skies. 

He will be referred to as "A fine guy" etc. 

Chances are he probably died partly from a broken heart, or over grief from the way the world treated him during his life time. 

Weeds and dirt before death-flowers afterwards, seem to be the general course of life. 

LAUGH AND GROW FAT 

One of Addison's pithy sayings was "Man is the merriest species of the creation; all above or below him are serious.”

Quite true-humans are merry in their way, because they are mostly too stupid or nonsensical, or too lazy to give serious thought to important things. 

Which may all be for the best, else any serious contemplation of what they have to go through in life, would probably make them decide in early youth to drown themselves.

SELFISHNESS 

Selfishness is a trait that is so little discernible in ourselves, that it is hard to cure. 2 21

We entertain certain opinions or convictions, and immediately expect others to agree with us. 

We overlook the fact that no two human beings are alike, and that everyone has a state of mind of their own. Selfishness, to be precise, is in trying to make others give in to our whims, without being willing to compromise, or "live and let live”.

DON'T MIX FRIENDSHIP AND BUSINESS 

One of the most distressing tragedies of our daily life is due to the mistake of having business dealings with close friends.

Having the business acquaintance first, and then becoming friends, is ideal; but to start business relations with your friends, invariably results in losing both friends and business; due of course, to the natural trait of most of us, who are looking for something for nothing.

OBSERVATIONS OF A DEAF MAN 

That the ordinary intelligent man can do so much talking and listen to so many opinions and thoughts of other men, many successful, without absorbing considerable general knowledge that should assure him success in his own calling. But does He? Evidently not-else why are there so many failures. Which must prove that most men are just plain dumb asses. 22

That men in a conference or meeting, can speak so many thousands of words, yet go completely around in circles, taking an hour to get to a certain point, which should be arrived at in ten minutes. 

COMPANIONSHIP 

The country's multitudinous charities argue well for the kindliness of society in general. 

Nevertheless, it is mostly in the abstract. 

The blind and the deaf, have of course, their sympathizers, but leading the one, and putting forth the greater effort to talk to the other, is mostly a duty, and one that is shunned.

Society does not stop to realize that what the physically afflicted need, is NOT sympathy, but companionship.

This indictment of society in general, may be tempered by the acts of the FEW of us who DO give that real friendship to those mostly needing it. 

MORE INJUSTICE 

If you have "IT" you can get away with murder.

Take the man or woman who is habitually unpunctual, who never put themselves out for any one, who do nothing to help their fellows, if they have that unruffled agreeable disposition, their faults are 23

entirely overlooked or not even noticed. Take the average hard worker, the ones who sacrifice themselves to any cause, who are continually doing something for others. No one pays any attention to their good qualities-they are merely taken for granted. But let them have a lapse an occasional flare up, and they are then remembered only for their "temperament”.

INGRATITUDE AS USUAL 

There is nothing that so breeds a cynic as ingratitude, which of all sins I consider the worst. I know a true story of a man who made big money, who was generous to a fault. While in the flux of his prosperity, he loaned a relative the sum of One 

Thousand Dollars to assist him start a business.

Came a day when the generous giver or "loaner" was close to his last dollar. He wrote the relative asking a little assistance, feeling sure it would be forthcoming, as the borrower had just bought an expensive car, and was therefore evidently prospering. In a few days he received a reply, stating that they were only too happy to be of help, remembering his former assistance, etc., and inclosed was a check for Five Dollars.

THE SYCOPHANT 

Place in the same category as an ingrate-the 24

man who has done some one a favor and then forever after expects and looks for some return on his investment of good nature.

THE CONCEIT OF JURYMEN 

Nothing swells up the self importance of a man as he that has the luck to serve on a jury. 

It gives him an air of superiority from which he rarely recovers. Jumped from obscurity into a position covering a little power, it warps his judgment, flatters his ego, and makes him useless ever after even during the trial of cases. Notice the smugness, the self satisfaction, on the faces of twelve men on a jury. Nothing so adds to self conceit.

THE CONCEIT OF MANKIND 

Pin a badge on some obscure individual at a convention or exhibition or what not. 

Word it "Reception committee" or "Assistant 

Janitor" the title does not matter-it puffs the wearer up like a pouter pigeon, and he struts like a peacock. It flatters his ego out of all natural proportion-it shows up man the conceited animal he generally is.

LISTEN MY CHILDREN AND YOU SHALL HEAR 

When friends meet-the result is a constant chat- 25

ter each tries to outdo the other in telling them what is on their mind.

As a result both are bored to death, but each keeps up the constant search for people to talk to and with, in the hope that some day they will find some one who is a good "listener".

For such is human conceit. 

We actually believe that other people like to listen to us.

THE CHINESE VIEW POINT 

The Chinese may be a backward race, their ideas of happiness, of pleasure, may be far different from ours, but at least they lack that colossal conceit which is the most rampant character of citizens of other countries. 

Deprecating forever their good deeds, their hospitality-may be only a gesture, a pose-but they do not incessantly voice nor force their conceited opinions of themselves on others.

DEFINITION OF CONCEIT 

A sure conceited ass is the person who continually talks about what they are going to do-instead of waiting until the thing is done. 26

ANGER 

Much has been written of the uselessness, the danger of angry passions.

All of which may be true, but the benefit of anger in the system is that it is a spur to work, to achievement—a stirring up of our energies to some purpose to overcome the natural lassitude of most of our organs.

DISCONTENT 

Practically all of us are discontented. We are never satisfied with what we have, but must have something different.

Despite the fact that the only way to conquer discontent is to work hard, and do things we don't feel like doing, to achieve at least some modicum of happiness, most of us just keep on grumbling or whining and are too lazy mentally to get out of the rut in which we are driving.

LIFE AS IT IS 

This life is made up mostly of whining or laughing-but, sad to say, whining is most prevalent. 

THE SINS OF MANKIND 

George Bernard Shaw writes: "Do not waste your time on Social questions. 27

"What is the matter with the poor is poverty. 

What is the matter with the Rich, is Uselessness.”

Aptly put George! But there is the great middle class, and you should have added: "What is the matter with the majority of mankind is laziness, carelessness, ingratitude, and selfishness."

OUR CARDINAL SINS 

Mankind is guilty of many sins, but the worst is that of hurting other people. all 

Indifference, selfishness, thoughtlessness; these are the cardinal faults to which most of us are guilty.

NO EXCUSE BUT LAZINESS 

The difference between the successful man and the recipient of charity is that the one goes ahead and fights his natural inclination towards laziness, while the other just "wishes" for this and that without really doing anything to get it. 

LAZINESS 

Laziness-the greatest curse of mankind.

Whether it be physical, or only mental it is responsible for practically all of our troubles. Trace 28

to it% of the world's poverty, the need of Community funds and charity everywhere. Some of you may claim that you are hard working and always willing to do what should be done but the very organs that make up your body are such that you are continually doing things the easiest way.

Laziness is therefore mostly mental, and the person who loafs and neglects this and that is the person who is just too lazy mentally to combat the natural functions of his system. Laziness is consequently a lack of will power, a disinclination to do anything. The only just punishment for the habitual lazy individual is drowning or shooting, as they are a drag on the community. 

THE LAZY FEMALE 

List among Obnoxious individuals: "A girl or woman who has no friends or acquaintances; is too lazy to acquire any, yet, whose whole thought at times is, "What will the neighbors say?"

LAZINESS 

The heat of midsummer gives many of us a natural excuse for-laziness.

LAZINESS 

Misuse of common everyday English:

29

The woman who says she has no "Aptitude" for sewing. She will not admit it is laziness. 

The man who does not care for sports or walking. He will not admit it is laziness. 

The man who says his boss does not appreciate him. The employee is just too lazy to improve himself. 

The woman who doesn't like babies.

She is merely too lazy to raise them. 

The man or woman who does not care to mix around socially.

They are just too lazy mentally to take the trouble to make friends. 

But why continue!

FRIENDSHIP 

Few of us know the real meaning of friendship. 

The true friend is one who is unselfish, who goes out of themselves, and gives you the best that is in them. 

Despite this, most friendships are will-of-thewisps-formed lightly, with ulterior motives.

We like some one trait, or we admire some possession, or we are flattered by a little attention. 30

Then we are disillusioned, and wonder, after a few weeks what we ever saw in that person.

Acquaintances come and go, but that rare specimen of life-a perfect friendship is so few and far between that it's rarity is eloquent to the selfishness and self centered existence of all of us. 

THE VALUE OF ENEMIES 

The easiest way to move along the highway of life, is to take the path of least resistance. It is the way of the sloth, of the laggard, of the coward. 

And the world is full, too full of this species.

No man ever made a success, or did the world a good turn, without making enemies. Yet we strive forever, entirely too hard, to avoid making these enemies.

There are too few men, and women, with convictions strong enough to ignore making antagonistic contacts. 

Too easily do most of us, move along, like sheep, following the crowd, and saying "Yes-Yes".

ENEMIES 

Enemies should not be taken too seriously. For my own part I laugh at them, and get much sly amusement over letting my enemies fool themselves into thinking I am annoyed at them. 31

THE PASSIONS 

The person possessing passion of a kind is often unduly criticised.

It is far better to have all the known passions, and properly control them, than to be spineless, and to have an utter lack of feeling of any kind. 

The first are useful citizens of achievementthe last are utterly useless.

PASSION HAS ITS PLACE 

The passionate person is the one most often condemned. But where can you get in this life without passion of a kind.

The one who lacks passion is the one who is too lazy, too indifferent, to exert themselves to any noble effort.

THE INEFFICIENT SCHOOL SYSTEM 

The education of our youth is a disgrace to the intelligence of those directing it.

Most of the stuff that is crammed down their throats is utterly useless in future life.

The practical is but a small part of the routine, and the all important subject of human service is not even thought of, much less taught. 32

All of which accounts for the majority of the race being morons.

DOWN WITH BEAUTY 

At least no one can say that beauty has any allure for me. I have never, in a life time of hiring and firing stenographers, typists and clerks ever hired a real pretty girl. 

Woman Hater?—Not me-but the homlier a girl, the better worker she is. I am in business to make money-not to look at powder and paint. 

You are right Beatrice, I am a conceited ass for mentioning how virtuous this makes me.

LOVE ME-LOVE MY DOG 

Kindness to animals may be a minor trait, but the man who will abuse a dog or any other animal is not to be trusted. He will abuse his wife or any of his family or friends and is one of the lower species of humans.

WHERE IGNORANCE IS BLISS 

It is providential that some scientist has not, nor ever will invent something whereby we can read other's thoughts.

If we could read the selfishness, the indecency, 333

the avarice in other's minds, we would get no pleasure out of this life.

As it is, we are suckers all, and knowingly or not, the biggest sucker is generally the one who gets the greatest kick out of this fleeting life. 

THE FOOL HAS HIS PLACE 

Not every one can be a power. Were it not for the fools and jackasses who are used as tools, our successful men could not succeed.

OUR LOVE OF ARGUMENT 

A lot of us like to argue, but mostly it is only for the pleasure (?) of arguing.

It is a waste of time to argue with those who know more than we do, but alas, our lack of perception and our natural ego, make us fail to realize this.

On the other hand-where is our pride that we allow ourselves to get into arguments with those whom we know are our mental inferiors. 

WHY HAVE NERVES 

Lack of self control is one of our additional sins. 

You say "That gets on my nerves." 

Why admit you have nerves. 

It is bad enough 34

to have them-but worse still to be so weak as to try and evoke sympathy over your lack of will power.

WHY SMILE? 

It is said, that it takes over one hundred muscles of the face to register a frown, but only TEN to smile; therefore, we should always be smiling. 

Far better, I think it would be, to remain expressionless and work no muscles at all. 

Besides, if you wake up smiling, before ten minutes have passed, some one will have taken all the joy out of life by some dirty dig or criticism. 

LOVE STUFF 

"A night in June, Beneath the moon.' 

Two fools who will later wake up and realize what jackasses they have been.

DOCTORS OF TRADITION 

Anent Doctors-The Chinese have the right idea. 

They pay their doctors so much a year to keep them well; whereas, in the United States, all is vastly different.

They are enmeshed in a vicious system mostly of 35

their own making due to their supposedly high ethics.

They are never expected to give advice unsought or to call on or phone their erstwhile patients unsolicited.

As a result, the health of the nation is a disgrace. 

The Doctor is only called in when the patient is ill or dying, and is then stuffed with pills, herbs and drinks, most of which the Doctor himself would not take, as he knows well, that most of it is no good; but he has to do something to get the patient in the right "mental" mood.

The Doctor knows that nature only will effect a cure. Eventually, nature either kills or cures, and if the latter, of course the Doctor gets the credit. 

My principal criticism is, as to why the Doctors can't keep in closer touch with patients while they are well and remind them to observe nature's laws more closely, and thus keep well, instead of waiting until they are sick and giving advice after the damage has been done. 

But alas-you can never upset the ironclad, ages old rules of a class of men who dare not depart from tradition.

BE SURE AND GRAB FIRST 

Money no doubt, is the root of all evil. Life is 36

just one grab after another of trying to get something for nothing.

It is often the cause of breaking up of many otherwise firm friendships.

Unconsciously or not, self-preservation of our hard-earned money, comes first in our minds. Our sense of fairness and justice is swept aside when some of us are asked to cooperate in the finances of some social project. 

MEN AND WOMEN LIKE SHEEP 

Our citizens are fools. They follow blindly, like sheep, the whims and caprices of their rulers.

In times gone by, when the populace was not so reared in luxury, our citizens gave more time to thought, and injustices were discussed and fought. 

Now, however, the average person is so busy making ends meet, and figuring out new luxuries and pleasures, little thought is given to those who rule his every action.

Outrageous taxes, graft, curtailment of liberties undreamed of in years past, censorship and what not, are met with grumbling, but absorbed with a hopeless gesture. All because our citizens would rather have their pleasures than give time and thought to fighting. 37

Spineless imbeciles, all of us.

Likewise, we follow blindly like sheep, in our personal habits from day to day.

We buy things and run in debt just because some one else has them.

We wear the same kind of clothes, uncomfortable and hideous looking, because everybody else does.

The men wear hats and coats with the temperature at 95, because they are too dumb to defy fashion's edict.

The women wear high heels and bear torture just because they too, lack courage to act sensible. 

Spineless creatures-but common sense and intelligence in our daily lives is the rare exception. 

Moral: We probably deserve all the misfortunes and tragedies that fill our lives, because we are too lazy or indifferent to avoid them.

DON'T PITY THE WORKING GIRL 

The majority of us work only because we HAVE to. We have the foolish illusion that if we were rich and idle, we would have a far happier life.

It is only because we are inherently lazy that we cherish such illusions. 38

The unhappiest people I have ever met were those who had practically nothing to do, and worried themselves sick trying to do it. 

Which probably goes to prove that we are a restless race, and always want something different from what we have.

REACHING FOR THE MOON 

Speaking of the so-called weaker (?) sex, did you ever see a perfectly contented woman? They are never satisfied with what they have, but are forever craving after something they cannot get. They pass up many good things in life, just because they want something better.

If, after making sacrifices, they happen to get what they want-alas, then, they find they want something else.

A NATION OF LIARS 

The reason I never believe a person is telling the truth is because of the inclination of every one of us to exaggerate.

We exaggerate our ages, up or down, our accomplishment, our wealth-not to mention the exaggerations of the eye and the ear.

This is one of the natural traits of mankind that 

I have never been able to figure out. 39

DISTORTION OF VALUES 

One of the many failings of the human race is the false value put on objects.

We lavish more attention and care on our automobiles, our dogs and cats, and our pigs, than we do on our own family.

THE GOLDEN RULE SELDOM WORKS 

Preaching the Golden Rule in business sounds very noble and inspiring, but the trouble is, that after all, self preservation is the first law of nature, and this rule will not work when it conflicts with nature.

YOU GET IT COMING OR GOING 

If you make money and are successful, it is because in some manner you are a robber or a crook, or both; or so the world thinks.

I know a merchant who has a large turnover and averages 15% profit on goods out of which must come all his overhead. Yet, because he sells a luxury people say he charges outrageous prices, despite the fact that his income tax is nothing to brag about.

If a man charges low prices, makes a failure, goes into bankruptcy-loses his all-he is still to the world, a crook—a repudiator of his obligations. 40

Any way you look at it-he gets it in the neck.

SUCCESS 

We are all familiar with the hokum handed out by successful men as to how they achieved it. 

Boiled down however, there is one all important ingredient. It is this: "Forever and ever doing things you don't feel like doing." 

Every failure every one in a rut, owes their position to mental laziness and taking the path of least resistance.

CARELESSNESS 

One of the greatest of all crimes-Carlessness.

THE ROAD HOG 

I passed an automobile wreck the other day, lying in the ditch-and breathed a fervent prayer, hoping it was the road hog who had tried to run me off the road several hours previously. 

THEY NEVER STAY SHREWD 

Why is it that men will go from poverty to comfort by years of hard labor and shrewd thinking, then try and get from comfort to luxury by gambl- 41

ing in stocks and what not, thereby losing all sense of shrewdness and balance.

THE STOCK GAMBLER 

Stock speculation is nothing more or less than legalized gambling on a big scale. 

The stock market has its uses of course, but without the speculation and gambling and suckers, it 

I would be flat indeed. "Once a sucker, always a sucker," was aptly proved in October and November 1929. 

Those who are now broke, and "off the market for life," will prove the nucleus of big suckers playing the market in future years.

One of the largest brokers in the country remarked after the crash:-"Everybody lost money except liars and beggars."

Pretty close to it, but not altogether true as I know quite a few who had sense enough to sell at fictitious values and buy when prices of some stocks were below real value, and suckers were being forced out; which proves that you can only make money in the market consistently once in 6 or 8 years.

But alas, how few there are, who can stand prosperity and let the market alone year after year. 42

PHILOSOPHY 

The happiest man is the one with the broadest sense of humor. 

Reverses and discomforts are to him "funny," and pleasures enjoyed all the more.

RADIO NUISANCES 

The playing of jazz bands, which is not music, but merely discordant noises, all with much the same tune.

The monologists, men, women and children, who have nothing to say, but say it, Oh! so self-importantly.

The political wind-bag, who discourses on supposedly important subjects, who uses thousands of words, all of which could be condensed in one hundred words, to tell us what we already know, which we found out long ago, and about which he himself knows absolutely nothing.

However, the Radio has to cater mostly to morons and imbeciles, thus must the few intelligent and intellectual suffer thereby.

THE POOR DOCTOR 

When we get really ill, we yell for the Doctor.

43

We get impatient if he delays a few minutes in arriving. 

Finally, we get better. Later we get well. Still later, we get his bill, and Oh-so much later, do we bother about paying it.

It is a great wonder that our Doctors are not all confirmed cynics.

THE POOR WIFE 

As has been noted before, the only difference between a servant and a wife, is that the servant gets paid good wages, while the wife works for nothing. However, it is to be noted that the servant is generally treated with some respect, in order to keep her satisfied with her work, whereby the wife has to take any kind of talk or treatment that is handed her, knowing that there is nothing she can do about it.

THE DELUDED POSER 

Of all the pests, the poser is one of the worst. 

The person, whose every action is to impress certain false ideas or opinions on their friends or acquaintances. Generally they fool mosty themselves, and are laughed at behind their backs. Lacking a discerning mind, their judgment faulty, their 44

sense of values abnormal, they are more to be pitied than censured.

OUR POOR STOMACHS 

Nothwithstanding the fact that everyone knows there are more people killed by overeating than by starvation, we go on day by day stuffing ourselves with every imaginable dish. We suffer; are cautious for a while, then when well, forget the lesson we should have learned and commence all over again to abuse our stomachs.

DO IT TOMORROW 

You often see the motto "Do it now" in business men's offices.

But how often is this creed carried out? Very seldom, as mankind is too inherently lazy to do anything today that can be put off until tomorrow. 

COMPETITION 

The only successful people in this life are those who fight the hardest.

If you are absolutely down and out, organized charity will help you, but once you get on your feet, the organized world will do its damndest to try and prevent you from getting anywhere. 45

Competition may be the life of trade, as the saying goes, but the true spirit of competition is in trying to prevent the other fellow from getting what you want yourself.

CIVILIZED? 

Any traveler will confirm the fact that there is far less morality in the so-called civilized United States, than there is in the supposedly uncivilized country of Borneo and its Dyaks. 

New definition for Webster's next issue: 

"Civilization (in brief): 

Laxity of morals."

THE TRUE TEST 

If you wish to test the ruggedness of a person's soul, take them on a camping trip.

Be alone with them in the woods or by the stream for days or weeks. 

Here you will find out the truth about them; whether they be shallow creatures, or noble ones.

PARASITES 

Every time one man, by dint of hard labor and never ceasing thinking and planning, makes a big success of something, there will be anywhere from 46

ten to one hundred persons who will eventually bask in the reflected glory of his achievement, share the laurels, and cling like leeches to his tenacles. 

DON'T PITY THE POOR 

Some one wrote:-"The Poor are more to be pitied than censured." 

This is all wrong.

It should be: The Poor are mostly always to be censured, instead of pitied. 

Pity the poor, and you encourage laziness and lack of systematic saving.

When poverty is not the result of physical laziness, it is generally the result of mental lazinessa too early-unthinking marriage-a disinclination to conserve physical and financial resources when able.

WASTE SYMPATHY 

It may be fine to sympathize with your friend or neighbor over their troubles, but your sympathy frequently does more harm than good. Said friend may need punishment instead. 

Why? Because most all the woes of your friends and neighbors are self-inflicted. 47

They get sick, because they abuse their bodies, despite warnings.

They lose their jobs and fail to get work, because they are too lazy to try hard enough to do something else.

They are broke and dependent upon charity, because in prosperous times, they were too happy-golucky to save anything. But why go on?

The fact remains that ninety-nine per cent of a person's troubles are their own fault; and a great 

Ideal of these troubles are a result of the world's quick sympathy with the unfortunate, without pausing to think of the real merits of the case. In other words, we condone and excuse too easily the shortcomings and sins of mankind.

SAVING 

A good part of the world's ills is due to poverty or lack of sufficient funds to meet the necessary expenses of one's daily life.

There is and always will be a small proportion of our population who are mentally unable to make enough to get out of the rut of living from hand to mouth even by the most rigid economy; but on the other hand, the great majority of our people make enough to save a small portion of their income if they put their minds to it. 48

Enormous Savings Bank Deposits as well as good investments elsewhere sound well when viewed from the whole-but statistics prove they come from but a small proportion of the population. 

Even before the days of our present luxuries, Autos, Radios, etc.-fools, both men and women were loath to save for the future; but now, with a luxury mad populace demanding automobiles at so much down and so much the rest of your life, or until you are broke, what chance do they have to save.

Far be it from me to knock the installment business, which no doubt has done much to increase production in this country, and make for prosperous conditions.

The country's prosperity formerly helped my own-but I must confess that it's right hard to work up sympathy for a lot of charities to which I am asked to give, when causes of need of the majority can be traced back to the neglect of that age old adage, "Save when you can for the future."

MISDIRECTED CHARITY 

Poverty is invariably the victim's own fault. 

How many scores of times have I, in the past few years had men and girls come to me for work -pleading dire necessity. 49

I have then called on them later, to be met with: "I don't care to do that kind of work." Hence my antipathy towards appeals of various charitable organizations. 

Why should I be called upon to "give" them something when they refuse to do any labor or earn it?

AS BETWEEN MAN AND WOMAN 

This volume is perfectly neutral as regards the faults of man or woman, and makes no pretense of condemning one any more than the other. 

However, who can explain the "complex" that all women have that of exaggerating the importance of small things, an unwillingness to change a certain routine in her life. 

She will, for instance, receive a sudden invitation to a party, or to some event-but no-she is sorry -she cannot go as she was planning to have her hair washed, or she was planning to write a letter, or she was planning to bake a cake, et cetra et cetra. 

Mind in a rut?-perhaps.

Then take the man-he was planning to do this, or planning to do that-but does he do it? Most assuredly not-he is a will of the wisp, and will 50

jump on a minute's notice to whatever strikes his fancy.

Mind topsy-turvy-most assuredly. 

So between the two sexes, I guess we are all just a little bit "crazy".

THE MYSTERY OF "TEARS" 

A woman will go to church and see a wedding. 

She will shed tears-for what? God alone knows.

Let us hope she is welling up and showing her tender feelings. 

But alas! Ten minutes later she will revert to her "cattish" instincts and the tenderness is all gone.

WOMEN AS PRUDES 

Webster's definition of a prude should be:- "An evil minded person who overdoes "goodness" in public."

Ingratitude, again. 

Question: Why do they (the Mothers) still populate the world?

The child marries the same cycle all over again. 

LAXITY OF PARENTS 

Another of the world's ills, and perhaps one of the greatest:

The carlessness of parents in bringing up boys and girls.

A laxity of restrictions; a blindness in thinking their morals, and their thoughts will eventually take proper care of them.

INGRATITUDE OF CHILDREN 

Ingratitude takes many forms, but I know of no worse than the ingratitude of children towards their parents. 52

Countless parents deserve some of this treatment by their offspring due to their laxity or laziness of methods in bringing up but for a child to have lived some 15 or 20 years, getting their education, their living and what not at the expense and often at the great sacrifice of their parents, and then to abandon them when they marry, to criticise them, to ignore their desires—well, no punishment is too great for such thoughtless children, but alas, as is usual with most high crimes it is overlooked, and repeated in generation after generation. 

WE ARE FOOLS ALL 

We must all have been born with an excess amount of optimism in us. Else how could the average man or woman go through the rebuffs and vicissitudes common to all of us, and retain the semblance of ordinary courtesy that society demands.

Often, after a series of three or four rebuffs, examples of ingratitude, et cetra, we have sworn never to do a gracious act for any one again, never to give any one a smile, but alas, in a few hours-all is forgotten, showing perhaps that we are all fools of a sort, to so easily forgive and forget.

TOO EASILY PARDONED 

The greatest evil of society today is the laxity 53

of punishment for our sins, crimes and omissions.

The habitual criminal will get but a light sentence, if not get off altogether.

The gold digger, the philanderer, the sponger, the loafer, the ungrateful, the ungracious, the unchristian, are all received with open arms by their fellow citizens their sins and omissions entirely overlooked if they have a ready smile, or a "way" with them.

MAKING THEM LIKE IT 

We have all heard that hokum about the easiest way to succeed is to give the people what they want. 

This is all wrong. The best way to get the most coin in these times is to make the people think and crave for what you are giving them, because, in the first place they are too stupid to have any intelligent thought of their own.

Perhaps the reason we do not seek expert advice on financial matters, is the sad 54

BUSINESS IN GOVERNMENT 

The business of governing our Country, States and Cities, constitutes the world's largest industry.

The sole reason is, that whereas private enterprise budgets its expenditures to fit its income, Government spends first, regardless, and then tries to find the income afterwards.

THE POLITICAL MESS 

Slamming politics is a stale pastime; yet there lives the hope that thereby purification of the taint of the system may sometime be made.

I say, "Taint of the System," and that is the whole root of the evil of present day government. 56

Take the average aspirant for office.

Of course, he is actuated by lust for power, personal aggrandizement and so forth; yet, cynic though I am, I do believe there are a few candidates for office who are the soul of honesty, and who are actuated by the best of motives. 

Some of these honest candidates are eventually elected. But what happens?

Do they reform the laws-make their government a business-like institution-reduce taxes and so forth? 

Assuredly not! And why?

Because, in the process of getting elected, they had to mix with hundreds and thousands of socalled friends or well wishers.

They had to do it-else, how could they have been elected?

After election, what becomes of our reform candidate's resolutions?

Forgotten - alas - because he now feels under moral obligation to help and assist his many so called friends. "Taint of system." That's it exactly.

A system, whereby it is impossible to be elected 57
