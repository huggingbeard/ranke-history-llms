SPECTROPHOTOMETRIC DETERMINATION OF COCAINE, PROCAINE AND TETRACAINE 

By M. J. Pro, R. A. Nelson, W. P. Butler, and A. P. Mathers 

(Alcohol and Tobacco Tax Division Laboratory, Internal Revenue Service, 

Washington, D. D.)

In narcotic enforcement work, cocaine samples are sometimes obtained with procaine and tetracaine as diluents. The chemical methods now employed are tedious and require larger samples than are usually available for the quantitative determination of these materials. Since the three compounds exhibit absorption in the ultraviolet and infrared regions of the spectrum, spectrophotometry offers a means of identification and quantitative determination of these drugs. In this paper a procedure is presented for the simultaneous determination of these compounds by ultraviolet spectrophotometry and infrared data is shown to be a valuable adjunct for identification. Mixtures can be identified by comparing their infrared absorption spectra with spectra prepared from the pure compounds.

EXPERIMENTAL 

Absorbance curves prepared from equal molar concentrations of cocaine, procaine, and tetracaine in 0.1 N hydrochloric acid showed absorption maxima between 227 and 233 mu; also procaine and tetracaine gave maxima at 280 and 310 mu respectively. Due to their common absorption regions at the shorter wavelengths and the intensity of the tetracaine absorption at the procaine maxima, acid solution was found to be unsuitable for their simultaneous determinations. On the other hand after alkaline hydrolysis, there was considerably less interference in the measurement of the individual absorbances. 

Absorption data for cocaine, procaine, and tetracaine in 0.03 N sodium hydroxide are presented in Fig. 1. These curves show a single maximum at 226, 264 and 280 mu for cocaine, procaine and tetracaine respectively. 

Both procaine and tetracaine absorb slightly at 226 mu while the absorption of cocaine at 264 and 280 mu is negligible. Although procaine and tetracaine absorb appreciably at each other's maximum, neither offers serious absorption interference with the other.

Qualitative Identification 

The infra red spectra of the hydrochlorides of cocaine, procaine, tetracaine and mixtures of these drugs are presented in Figs. 3 and 4. 

The curves were made from films of material deposited on sodium chloride discs. To prepare the films, the samples were dissolved in anhydrous methanol and aliquots containing approximately 5 mg. of material were evaporated to dryness on sodium chloride discs in a vacuum dessicator at room temperature. The data presented in Fig. 5 show the relative intensities of the principal absorption bands of the pure drugs and some typical mixtures.

DISCUSSION 

Although procaine and tetracaine are the more common cocaine diluents, experience with many enforcement samples has shown that other substances may be incorporated. Cocaine in the form of thick cakes often contains ecgonine. Other less common diluents are milk sugar,

- 6starch, mannitol, and magnesium sulfate. No interference was found for ecgonine and the latter diluents since they show no absorption in the ultraviolet region in concentrations up to 100 p.p.m. 

In this infra red work the film-from-solution technique was selected because the hydrochlorides of these drugs are very soluble in anhydrous methanol. A further advantage of this solvent is the insolubility of the less common diluents such as milk sugar, starch, mannitol and magnesium sulfate. However, it was recognized that solvent entrapment and crystal orientation are disadvantages of this technique. For this reason the pressed bromide method may be preferable for pure mixtures of these drugs. 

Since the mull technique requires the removal of diluents, it was less desirable for this work. Similarly the insolubility of these compounds in carbon disulfide and other common infrared solvents limited the applicability of the solvent method. 

Ultraviolet spectrophotometry for the determination of these drugs is preferred because it has the advantages of speed, accuracy, and ease of manipulation. Infrared analysis offers an additional rapid method for their identification.
