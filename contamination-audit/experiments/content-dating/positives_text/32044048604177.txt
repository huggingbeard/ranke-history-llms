UNION NOW 

-FOR PEACE OR WAR? 

Che Danger in the Plan of Clarence Streit

CRITICALLY sick world is groping frantically for a life-saving medicine. Most of the trusted doctors are of the homeopathic school. Cure war with war. The chief prescriptions so far are isolation and collective security, the romanticized expression for collective insecurity. Both remedies, however, are so fantastically expensive that their cost threatens to strip the patients bare and even take the bread out of their children's mouths.

But even the astronomical size of their war-preventives fails to give a sense of security. People see countries disappear from the map of the world like caved-in geological formations during cosmic catastrophes. They watch tens of millions of human beings sucked into the whirlpool. Petrified by fear of a similar fate, despairing of any life-saving medicine, the nations are swallowing the quack concoctions of their unimaginative old doctors. 

Into this scene came Clarence K. Streit offering his book Union 

Now as the panacea for the world's ills. A new doctor with a prescription not only free of charge but guaranteed to cure the patient and to provide greater well-being than ever before enjoyed. 

Yes, union now would be a panacea, and union is the only guarantee to rid the world of war, of economic, political and social injustice. Yet there is something wrong with Mr. Streit's prescription.

All through the ages, but most intensively since 1914, independent thinkers have drafted innumerable plans for a world community. There accumulated also through the ages an immense literature of abstract arguments as to the advantages of peace over war. -[1

Mr. Streit has rendered us a great service by combining the age-old idea of world organization with the arguments for its need, and particularly by pointing out the urgency of its need. He has made a most important contribution to the literature of world organization. But there is a grave danger inherent in his plan of procedure to achieve World Union.

The first four chapters of Union Now are devoted to arguments. 

They analyze clearly the inadequacy of Leagues and Alliances and show why the world has to come into a Federal Union, and why Now. They are certain to convince everyone that there is a warless alternative for saving mankind from that self-destruction toward which isolation or collective military actions are driving it. 

In these chapters Mr. Streit presents a lucid explanation of the 

League of Nations' failure as a war-preventive and universal peacecreating machinery. The League put no restriction on national sovereignty and tried to coerce nations as a whole. Federal Union authorities would act not on nations but directly on individuals in the constitutionally established fields of action. For instance, not 

Germany but Hitler, not Russia but Stalin, not Italy but Mussolini and their colleagues in other dictatorships would be treated as menaces to world peace before a world forum. 

But the most important chapter-V-telling how World Union could be achieved now is in great part straight refutation of Mr. 

Streit's own arguments.

Mr. Streit is the victim of a disastrously dangerous illusion which makes him believe that World Union Now must begin with an appeal not for immediate World Union but for merely a Union of the North Atlantic democracies. He does not seem to realize that to start with a power group of nations, labelled Union of Democracies, would provoke a Union of the rest of the world in hostile opposition. If practical attempts at world organization along Mr. 

Streit's propositions were made today, we would have to abandon hope of an all-inclusive World Union. We would face the most savage clash between the group of Mr. Streit's so-called democracies and the rest of the nations. The present gigantic wrestling match between the Umbrella governments and the Axis Führers to draw more and more nations into their orbits, illustrates conclusively the fallacy of Mr. Streit's basic contention. The historic monstrosity of 2]—

Hitler and Stalin, after discarding all pretences of idealogical differences of their regimes, joining hands, each thumbing his nose at the Anti-Comintern and the Anti-Fascist groups, both of which have exerted themselves to get these two detested powers into an alliance, should be a warning to Mr. Streit that his "nucleus method" would not result in World Union but in world catastrophe. 

With undoubted sincerity Mr. Streit claims an all-inclusive union of all nations as his ultimate aim. But his terminology, not to say his proposal itself, is confusing. On the very jacket of his book there are three different designations for the democracies he would unionize : 

1. "the World's Democracies" 

2. "the Leading Democracies" 

3. "the United States and the European Democracies"

In addition we find in the book itself the following terms: 

1. On the title page the sub-title: "Federal Union of the De mocracies of the North Atlantic"; also on pages 2, 6, and elsewhere, and as the sub-title of Mr. Streit's Bulletin. 

2. "Atlantic Democracies" (P. 4)

On the pamphlet "For Union Now", a reprint of the first chapter of the book to serve as an “appetizer",—the title page has: "Federal Union of the Democracies". 

On the folder "What we can do Now" still another title: "World 

Federal Union".

Each of these designations means something different, and none of them covers the ultimate suggestion that the ratification of the 

World Constitution by "say, three great powers" would be sufficient for the Constitution "to go into effect" (P. 108). Only toward the end of the book (P. 251), in his Illustrative Constitution, does Mr. 

Streit reveal the identity of the three great powers. Mr. Streit's plan would be more clearly defined if his book were sub-titled: "Federal Union of France, the United Kingdom and the United

States". On the last page of the folder "10 Facts" it is suggested that "the three with the most people (the United States, the United Kingdom and France) shall be sufficient to get the Union started". -[3

Mr. Streit can hardly mean the United Kingdom which includes only 

England, Wales, Scotland and North Ireland. This is not a combination to be counted among those that have "most people". 

That Mr. Streit believes the united government of these three great powers is sufficient and the most promising immediate step toward World Union, is manifest also in his apology for the inclusion. of a few small democracies in the exclusive group for the drafting of the Union Constitution. In Chapter V we find these derogatory statements about the small democracies: "to hold together solidly three great stones it often helps to take small stones to fill the holes. . . . If the small European and overseas democracies did not exist it would almost be necessary to invent them for the purpose of bringing the Americans, British and French to swallow false pride in a constitutional convention, compromise and adjust their differences, and agree on a definite detailed constitution. It is not, however, essential that the nucleus include every one of these small democracies. 

If a few of them balk at coming in, the nucleus could be formed without them So the small democracies, Holland, Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Finland, Switzerland, etc. are needed only as cement to hold the Big 

Three together. It even does not matter whether all the cement is available. Can these little democracies, who have contributed immeasurably to the culture and civilization of the world be expected to play the role Mr. Streit's plan offers them? 

No more tragic mistake could be made than to exclude any nation on earth from the initial invitation to form a World Government. Those excluded at the start, even though told they would be admitted later, would be marked as second class, inferior nations. 

The mistake of the League of Nations in distinguishing between classes of nations would be repeated in another form. It is also more than questionable whether the "three great stones" could afford to risk the initial omission of nations for whose close cooperation they are now struggling.

Could the United States consent to the omission from the initial invitation of Central and South America? 

America? Could England and

France leave out Soviet Russia, or Turkey, Poland and the little nations they have so recently coaxed into their orbit? 4]-

Only by utter disregard of reality can one believe that any government sincerely desiring peace would participate in a constitutional conference to which only a hand-picked group of ten to twenty nations, arbitrarily labelled "democracies", was invited. The reluctance of the Scandinavian and other nations to line up with either the Umbrella powers or the Axis governments is sufficient illustration of this fact. 

Mr. Streit's plan maintains the power principle and the employment of organized military force as a constitutional requisite. This makes his plan a straight successor to Mr. Taft's defunct League to 

Enforce Peace and a companion of Lord Davies' and Winston

Churchill's New Commonwealth campaign, which, under the camouflage of serving world peace, is clearly endeavoring to create a "world police force" for the protection of the British Empire. While not suspecting Mr. Streit of deliberately serving such a camouflaged purpose, one cannot but regret that he provokes distrust in his honest endeavor.

A plan to bring about a new warless world order cannot be based on power policies. As long as we continue to consider organized force a possible means of achieving security and protection, we are keeping up the traditional war system. We are perpetuating the insane race in war equipment and permitting fear, suspicion, hate, and other psychological impulses to dehumanize mankind from his very cradle. 

Mr. Streit's "Union of Democracies" is planned most emphatically as a power organization. In the subdivision of Chapter V entitled "The Overwhelming Power of the Fifteen" (democracies), Mr. Streit marshalls twelve pages of facts and charts to boast how "relatively feeble any other conceivable combination (for Union) would be" (P. 94), compared with the "decisive world power in the hands of the fifteen democracies" (P. 98). No Hitler or Mussolini could assert more effectively than Mr. Streit that the "fifteen democracies together practically own this earth" (P. 104); and they certainly would agree with him that if the fifteen were organized into a Union of Democracies they would be "almighty on this planet" (P. 104). Mr. Streit leaves no doubt that his limited Union would be overwhelming in "war power" (P. 101), through the material possessions in "war essentials" (table 2), and “armed power" (table 5), and that it would rely, as all the nations have done since organized -[5

war became an instrument of national policy everywhere, on force, on power, on might. 

Such arrogant boasting by a Union of Democracies would be a Godsend to the autocracies. What fury could Hitler, Mussolini,

Stalin and other dictators inflame if they lashed their people into fighting the Democratic Union in "self-defense, for essential raw materials and for room to live in"!

With much apparent justification the dictators could accuse the Democratic Union of being bent on crushing their countries. 

We get a taste of this in the incendiary speeches of Hitler and Mussolini against the encirclement policies of the democracies, and in the Axis powers' furious attempts to coax or coerce nations, also wooed by the Umbrella governments, into cohesion with their power combination. But they could not incense their peoples thus into aggressive wars and annexations after all nations had been included in an initial invitation to form a World Union. 

Nobody can expect the initially excluded small democracies and other small or large non-totalitarian nations to trust a Federal Union of Democracies, chiefly, if not exclusively, based on the Great Democracies which have so recently betrayed a number of nations they had pledged to protect. Their offended pride or their sense of desperate helplessness would deliver them to the Axis powers. 

Mr. Streit's plan to limit the nucleus of World Union to ten or twenty "democracies", of which he names the fifteen of his choice, gives them also the accolade of being the "peace loving nations". In this he shares the fallacy of all those who urge peace-loving nations to destroy the war-loving nations. But there are no war-loving nations. All nations are peace-loving. No people on earth want war. The German people want peace, as they have shown when the infamous Munich pact kept them out of a war they thought was going to start next day. The Italian people do not want to fight the 

Ethiopians, the Spanish or any other people. We know of the 

Japanese people who do not feel at home in Manchuria or in the stretches of China that their murderous war lords claim as conquered territory. The Russian people have learned the grim lesson of their bloody revolution. Ghandi's people want freedom for India but they do not want to gain it by physical violence. The Jews want Great 6]-

Britain and the United States to live up to their guarantees and promises in Palestine without resort to violence. The tribes of 

Waziristan on the Northwest border of British India do not fight Great Britain but try to protect themselves against the air bombing of British military forces. 

I repeat, all nations are peace-loving. There are only—and they exist in every nation-war-loving individuals and tiny minority groups who favor war for the profit they make out of it, or for lust of power.

Mussolini, Hitler and the Japanese war lords excited their people with the cry for world markets, raw materials and space for their over-population. Franco fought in defense of the Catholic Church, but he used Catholic Italians, Mohammedan Moors and Protestant Germans to destroy Catholic Spaniards and part of Catholic Spain. 

To wrest the power from the fists of the Führers and the nontotalitarian militarist individuals and groups as well, we must prove from the start our confidence in the peoples of the world. The right of equality within the community of nations can only be established by extending to every nation on our globe the invitation to create the world union with a practical, modern world government. 

If a dictator or government whose nation had been invited to participate in the World Constitutional Conference, as an equal in the community of nations, were to refuse representation, we who are working for world government would then be free to act for the prevention of aggression and the spread of totalitarianism until the world government was ready to function. By using every means of communication to keep in continuous touch with the unrepresented peoples so as to inform them of the measures proposed and discussed in the Conference, they would realize that no military action was contemplated against them. And they would be urged not to be misled into aggression because a frame-work of safety and justice for all was in process of erection.

Non-cooperating governments would be exposed to their own peoples as the enemies of their interests and they would thus be deprived of the only effective argument to drive their citizens to aggressive war. People of unrepresented nations could be invited to send unofficial representatives to the World Constitutional Con- -[7

ference, or, if that were impossible, to take part in its discussion by correspondence or other possible ways.

The Campaign for World Government, now in its second year of action, has carefully drafted detailed plans for the preparation of a World Constitutional Conference to draw up the Constitution of an all-inclusive, non-military, democratically constructed Federation of Nations. The Campaign devotes special efforts to prepare plans for direct informative contact with the peoples of the totalitarian states.

When Mr. Streit proposes to invite at least twelve, preferably fifteen and at most twenty "democracies" to form an "almighty power organization", he apparently does not realize that exclusion from the initial invitation, relegation to a secondary status, would crush the peace-loving, and peace-longing elements within the totalitarian nations. It would deprive people who are smarting under the cruellest oppression ever practiced, of an instrument with which to defeat their oppressors, to liberate themselves and turn from the darkness of their tortured existence into the light of a new era. 

Without participation in the construction of a sane world order, no nation can hope to save itself from military domination. Unimaginative anti-fascists on the contrary, expect self-liberation of totalitarian victim peoples to occur only after war and revolution. 

This is the militaristic reasoning par excellence: wage war against your enemies in order to make them peaceful!

Among the numerous fallacies in Mr. Streit's plan is the arbitrary bestowal of the title "democracy" on the fifteen countries he picked out: Australia, Belgium, Canada, Denmark, Finland, France, Ireland, the Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway, Sweden, Switzerland, the 

Union of South Africa, the United Kingdom and the United States of America.

The meaning of democracy needs a thorough reinterpretation. 

The builders of a new world order must create also a new wordorder. To illustrate the incorrectness of applying arbitrarily the qualification of democracy I need only point to the appalling metamorphosis of England from a Democracy into an almost dictatorially-ruled nation,—parliamentary government notwithstanding. 8]-

A loyal English Liberal, the novelist, J. B. Priestley, an imperialist and not opposed to the use of military force, stated in 

Harpers Magazine of May, 1939: 

"....not only does the House of Commons fail to represent public opinion, but now the Government itself does not really represent the House of Commons. We are no longer even governed by a Cabinet. We are governed by an inner Cabinet. Our policy is being decided, not by public opinion, not by the House, not even by the whole Cabinet, but by Chamberlain, Simon, Hoare, and Lord Halifax. We voters can make our opinion felt, within the limits of a very bad system, at the next General Election; but until that time comes we have about as much to say as to what Britain will do as one of Hitler's Germans has as to what the Reich will do. The ordinary German or Italian citizen has no idea what Hitler or Mussolini will spring upon him next, but then neither have I, who am supposed to be a member of a democracy, the least idea what Chamberlain and his friends are going to do next. (And some of these friends, who go about with mysterious authority, men like Montagu Norman and Sir Horace Wilson, are not even elected persons.) 

Thus when Chamberlain went to visit Mussolini in January he was not delegated to go by the British people, who did not understand at all why he went. Indeed, I am certain that if there had been a referendum on the question of that visit, there would have been a decided majority against it.'..

Among other prominent English men and women denouncing the destruction of democracy in England we have Sir Gilbert Murray in a May issue of the London Times, Sylvia Pankhurst in her New 

Times and Ethiopia News and George Bernard Shaw in Liberty for 

September 10, 1938. Shaw says: "The opponents of Mussolini, Hitler and Stalin usually call themselves democrats, and in Britain they labor under the delusion that ours is a democratic state. The fact is that Britain is a plutocratic oligarchy supported by democratic snobbery." 

Several of Mr. Streit's selected "democracies" exclude half of their population from the right to vote or to be elected to their legislatures. Women have no political rights in France and are still subject to the Code Napoleon, one of the most humiliating legal systems for women based on the Napoleonic principle that "nature made women slaves of their husbands". They have no vote in 

Switzerland which in other respects has the most perfect constitution. 

Belgian women have no vote. In the Union of South Africa, to the greater glory of democracy, not only the females among the two million white population, but all the five million black people have no -[9.

political rights whatever. Mr. Hitler may point with pride to the fact that whatever poltical rights nominally exist in his Reich, they are at least equal for men and women. For instance, both have the "vote" at the age of twenty. The Canadian Democracy includes the province of Quebec, of "Padlock Law" fame, of which the June 10 issue of Propaganda Analysis has this to tell: "Last February, Mayor Camilien Houde of Montreal, delivered an outburst against the British. 'If war comes', he said, 'and if Italy is on one side and England on the other, the sympathy of the French Canadians in Quebec will be on the side of Italy. Remember that the great majority of French Canadians are Roman Catholics, and that the Pope is in Rome. We French Canadians are Normans, not Latins, but we have become Latinized over a period of years. The French Canadians are Fascists by blood, but not by name. The Latins have always been in favor of dictators.'''

Mr. Streit expects a good deal of uncritical acceptance of assertions that do not bear analysis. Under the sub-title "The Close 

Cohesion of the Fifteen", for instance, Mr. Streit asks: "What other nucleus of fifteen has such natural bonds to unite such power as ours?" (P. 90).

There is tragic humor in Mr. Streit's claim that "Geographically, culturally, commercially, financially, politically, historically, our fifteen provide a most cohesive nucleus". (P. 94). It would take a geographical tour de force to put Switzerland, South Africa, Australia, or New Zealand on the border of the North Atlantic.

It is equally difficult to recognize the close cohesion of the Canadian Catholic and the Dublin Protestant, of the Swedish free people and the dictatorially ruled blacks of the Union of South Africa. "The culture of our fifteen is inextricably interconnected. 

The civilization of these democracies has reached broadly the same level", (P. 90),-Mr. Streit asserts. I challenge the reader to find proofs of close cohesion between the superior Fifteen. "Not last are the Fifteen bound together by the peaceful, good neighborly relations they enjoy with each other and desire to enjoy with all the world" (P. 91), is another assertion hard to swallow.

An idyllic picture presents itself of Iceland and Switzerland, Holland and Australia spending a neighborly Sunday peacefully together in South Africa, hoping that some of the other neighbors, like New Zealand or France will drop in. Politically powerless white women 10]--

and all the native blacks would be surprised to learn that in Mr. 

Streit's fifteen democracies government is "based on the individual as equal unit". (P. 91).

Mr. Streit utterly ignores India, China, South America and all other nations, calling them, including Russia, “immature democracies". 

With the Kiplingesque arrogance of the white man towards his black brother, he fails to notice the fact that since the betrayal of Ethiopia the black population of the world is drawing closer to a union than ever before. He ignores also the findings by anthropologists of ordered communal living among aborigines that white civilizations may well emulate. 

Mr. Streit in his introductory chapters puts proper emphasis on the immediate need for World Union. But he fails to see that the hurricane spread of totalitarianism with its dehumanized practices · must be urgently halted. The spread of totalitarianism is driving mankind towards a cataclysm which cannot be averted by the frenzied war preparations now bankrupting the world economically and morally. But the first active public step towards the creation of an all-inclusive World Union and the knowledge of this step would relieve the world immediately of its agonized fear of world dictatorship and stop the militarization of the human family from the cradle to the grave.

A Union limited to a hand-picked group led by discredited, untrustworthy "Great Democracies" could not bring about such relief. On the contrary, it would pour oil on the fire. 

It would be a supreme challenge to the numerically overwhelming excluded nations to unite their power to wrest the ostentatiously boasted "possession of the earth" from its twelve to twenty owners.

The titanic clash would bring world peace. The peace of the cemetery, the peace of the ashes of our civilization. 

Mr. Streit also fails to realize that the barbaric formal and informal wars now waged in several countries,—in China, in Ethiopia, in Waziristan, in Palestine, must be stopped. 

Chinese, Ethiopians, Jews, Arabs and the Indian tribes could be persuaded to stop armed resistance and endure foreign rule or other injustices temporarily if they were assured that an all-inclusive World Federation was in the process of organization—a Federation within which the oppressed nations could regain what was justly -[11

theirs and adjust their differences before a Worid Forum, and become legitimate members of the family of nations. 

The great danger in Mr. Streit's prescription for healing mankind's critical illness lies in the inclusion of ingredients that worldhistory has proved poisonous and in the exclusion of tested safe ingredients that the science of statecraft offers us today. 

The most important combination-exclusion of military coercion (of a state) and initial invitations to all then existing states-was offered by the United States one hundred and fifty years ago when the world was given an unprecedented example of what the political genius of man can create. In the face of almost universal disbelief in the possibility of its creation, a handful of inspired men united thirteen quarreling, jealous, parochial-minded states into a Union. 

The thirteen original states retained sovereignty, delegating only an agreed part of it to the Federal Government. In their sublime wisdom the creators of the United States Constitution excluded the possibility of one state's arming against another and provided a forum where the disputes between states could and were settled by judicial process.

Professor William I. Hull of Swarthmore points out in his excellent brochure entitled Imperialism, Armaments, War and Our American Solution that in the course of living under Federal government the Supreme Court of the United States has settled ninety inter-state disputes. No military force backed the Supreme Court's decisions.

There was and there is no provision to compel the states to abide by the Supreme Court's decisions. Yet they were respected in every instance. If the states had been free to fight one another with their own armed forces the history of the forty-eight federally united states would have been as blood-drenched as the history of the nations in the rest of the world that consider armies and navies the only guarantee of national security.

The creation of the League of Nations in 1919 was an attempt to apply the system of federal living to the whole world. But the victory-drunk Versailles dictators, panting for punishment of the defeated nations, overpowered President Wilson and made of the 12]-

covenant an instrument doomed to failure for many structural reasons, but chiefly because it maintained the coercive military system. 

The mile-stones on the death march of the League of Nations are Manchuria, Ethiopia, China, Austria, Czechoslovakia, Spain,

Albania, Memel, world rearmament on an astronomical scale, and millions of stateless men, women and children roaming the world in search of shelter and food. The lesson of the tragic failure of the 

League should teach us what to avoid in the structure of a World Constitution which must be created as the only means to save mankind.

As new hitherto unimagined problems stagger international life, individuals, organizations, the Pope and church groups clamor for the calling of all kinds of international conferences to patch up one or another of the multitude of issues which now keep the world in a state of fear-created insanity.

Only the organization of a new world order based on a constitution containing the best features of all the tried and proved constitutions of the world, such as those of the United States, Switzerland, Canada and other federal unions can provide a peaceful solution for all the problems plaguing mankind.

And only in such real World Union can we demonstrate that we consider all human beings God's children or evolved from the same primordial stuff. In his brochure, Professor Hull summarizes the characteristics of the unity of the human family thus: "The fundamental and universal needs of food, clothing, shelter, family life, and the love of children, beauty, justice and joy, are common to all humanity, and they far outweigh all superficial differences put together, and it is precisely these fundamental needs which war tramples under foot."

No time must be lost in preparing and calling a World Constitutional Conference. Rather than frustrate our world-saving aims by following Mr. Streit's prescription of limitation at the start, we must ardently hope that he will join with the Campaign for World

Government in uniting all those who have recently published plans for a World Constitution, coordinating the plans, and starting concerted and practical action. Among the American authors of such plans are: Lola Maverick Lloyd, Professor William I. Hull, -[13

Crichton Clarke, Carleton Washburn, Vergil V. Phelps, Benjamin 

A. Javits, Professor W. F. Gerhardt, Roswell P. Barnes and John 

D. Rockefeller, Jr.

The immense value of Mr. Streit's book is that it has given the idea of federal union of the world the greatest publicity it has had since the world campaign for the League of Nations. He could render mankind that service in which Woodrow Wilson and Henry Ford failed if in analytical review of his thesis, he acted as the scientist who changes his formula even if he has spent a life-time of research on it when a better scientific solution of his problem occurs to him or is found by fellow scientists.

We, the biggest, We, the white skinned, We, the practicing democracies,

We, the bearers of culture and civilization, Yes, we, the arrogant masters of this planet!

Reaching at random into the treasure trove of mankind's past and present, I ask you to look at this handful of gems of democracy, culture and civilization that have no place in the blue-prints of Mr. 

Streit's plan for World Union Now.

India: 

"There is no denying the fact that England is administering India for England's benefit and not India's. It is hard for me to say this, because until I went to India my sympathies were all on the English side. My early education was much in England and I have many dear personal friends there. But what I am saying now is the truth, and the truth must be told....The obvious fact stares us in the face that there is at no time in no year, any shortage of foodstuffs in India. The trouble is that the taxes imposed by the British Government being fifty per cent of produce, the Indian starves that England's annual revenue may not be diminished by a dollar. Eighty percent of the whole population has been thrown back upon the soil because England's discriminating duties have ruined practically every branch of native manufacture; and these tillers of the soil, when they have sold themselves for the last time to the money lender, when they have over and over again mortgaged their crops and their bit of land, are sold by the tax-collector to wander about until they drop of starvation.... We send shiploads of grain to

India, but there is plenty of grain in India. The trouble is the people have been ground down until they are too poor to buy it. Famine is chronic there now, though the same shipments of foodstuffs are made annually to England, the same drainage of millions of dollars goes on every year.. (Rev. Dr. Charles Cuthbert Hall, late President of the
