Kosher dishes are usually kept separated in every kitchen where kosher laws are observed. The very religious woman who, by mistake, uses a knife which is usually kept to cut meat for cheese or butter consults a rabbi in such a case. The rabbi's direction would be to clean the knife by a method of sticking it into hard soil ten times in succession.

There are likewise often private affairs presented to the rabbi, who sometimes makes some suggestion in solving some problems for the family. It once happened that a woman who could not afford to leave her kitchen and go to consult the rabbi sent her twelve year old son. She instructed the boy to ask two questions. She was very much annoyed by a certain collector whom she owed for furniture, and she wanted the rabbi to advise her what to do. The second question was that by mistake she cut cheese with a knife which she had formerly used for meat, and she wanted to know what to do with the knife.

NOTE: Religious Jews do not eat dairy products immediately after meals in which meat is served. They usually wait six hours.

NOTE: In the Bible are given certain rules how to select the animals, birds, and fishes fit for human consumption. A story is told in the Talmud that Rabbi 

Broka met Elijah, the prophet, in the market place, where a goodly gathering of natives had assembled.

In the course of the conversation, Rabbi Broka asked Elijah to point out those in the crowd who he thought would, above others, share in the bliss of the Olam Habba-

The story is told to emphasize the fact that even the editors of the Talmud recognized the value of a humorous story.
