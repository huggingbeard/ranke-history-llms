The Washington Bureau of Governmental Research, Inc., is a fact-finding organization designed assist legislators, state officers, and citizens in solving problems of government. It is a citizen-supporte non-partisan, non-political research and fact-finding civic organization. It will not deal in propaganda, ta part in any political campaign, nor endorse or oppose any candidates for public offices. 

The primary purpose of the Bureau is to assist citizens and officials in obtaining effective st government at a reasonable cost. The plan is to search out facts about government and to render unbia interpretations of them. Its aim is to cooperate with public officials and to be helpful rather than critical. 

The Bureau will make detailed studies and investigations of matters relating to public revenue, penditures, indebtedness, governmental organization and administration, and taxation, of this state and local taxing districts, and make public the results thereof. The results of its studies will be available to citizens, and generally to all groups having an interest in government.

COMPARISON OF APPROPRIATIONS AVAILABLE FOR 1947-1949 BIENNIUM 

with

ACTUAL EXPENDITURES FOR 1943-1945 AND 1945-1947 BIENNIUMS 

When the Thirtieth Session of the Washington State Legislature adjourned in March 1947 it had approved appropriation bills, from all funds, in excess of 645 million dollars. The Governor vetoed items amounting to 25 million dollars, leaving a total enacted into law of 620 million dollars. 

This is an all-time high and compares with 549 million dollars appropriated by the 1945 Session and 328 million dollars by the 1943 Session.

After making the usual adjustments covering (1) deficiency appropriations, $16 million; (2) difference between amounts shown as reappropriations in the Session Laws and the actual amount unexpended and reappropriated, $6 million; and (3) non-appropriation transfers budgeted, $13 million, we have a total adopted 

Budget for the 1947-1949 Biennium of $610,717,593.30 from all funds in the State Treasury. 

By eliminating Emergencies, Contingencies, Transfers, Refunds and Non-recurring and Non-comparable items from the total appropriations of $610,717,593.30, we arrive at total appropriations for all state purposes and state aid of $566,429,446.56 for the 1947-1949 Biennium. This is an increase of $189,603,324.62 or 50% over the amounts actually expended for like purposes in the 1945-1947 Biennium and an increase of $318,598,- 290.51 or 129% over actual expenditures during the 1943-1945 Biennium. 

Under the broad heading of State Aid and Assistance actual expenditures increased from $181 million in 1943-1945 to $272 million in 1945-1947, an increase of 51%. Appropriations for 1947-1949 total $353 million. an increase of $81 million or 30% over actual for 1945-1947 and an increase of $172 million or 95% over actual for the 1943-1945 Biennium. 

The Regular Operating Expenses of State Government, not including Capital Outlays or Major Repairs, increased from $65 million in the 1943-1945 Biennium to $92 million in the 1945-1947 Biennium, an increase of 42%. Appropriations for the 1947-1949 Biennium total $164 million, an increase of $71 million or 77% over actual for 1945-1947 and an increase of $99 million or 151% over actual for 1943-1945. 

Since the adjournment of the Legislature conflicting statements have been made regarding the sufficiency of appropriations for certain functions of government. 

In some cases departmental heads submitted requests greatly in excess of the appropriations under which they operated during the 1945-1947 Biennium. The Legislature, while not granting such requests in full, did ap propriate more than was appropriated for the previous biennium; yet some contend that the "cut" in funds wil require a reduction in staff or services rendered. In some cases appropriations made by the Legislature were les than the amounts appropriated or expended in the previous biennium. 

It is not our purpose here to in any way express an opinion as to the merits of either side of the contro versy, but rather to present a statement of facts, prepared on a comparable basis, so that those interested may become informed and reach their own conclusions. With this objective in mind the Bureau of Governmenta 

Research has conducted this study from the official records and reports of the offices of Budget Director, Stat Auditor, and State Treasurer and the appropriation acts for the various periods covered. 

The Statement submitted herewith covers three biennial fiscal periods, viz.: 

April 1, 1943 to March 31, 1945, April 1, 1945 to March 31, 1947, and 

April 1, 1947 to March 31, 1949, 1

Ex- The figures shown for the first two periods are actual expenditures made relating to such periods. penditures from deficiency appropriations are included in the fiscal period during which the expenditure was incurred rather than for the period when paid. For example, the 1947 Legislature made 35 deficiency appropriations to 29 departments covering delayed bills, that is, claims received too late to be included in the 1943-1945 biennial accounts. These claims were actually paid by the State Auditor in April 1947, in the 1945-1947 biennial accounts. However, in this statement the expenditures are included under the 1943-1945 biennium, the period when actually incurred. This gives a true picture of the actual cost of operating the various departments and functions of government, from funds in the State Treasury, for these two biennial periods. 

The Statement also shows the percentage of increase, or decrease, in actual expenditures for the 19451947 Biennium as compared with the 1943-1945 Biennium. 

For the 1947-1949 Biennium the statement shows the amounts appropriated as set up on the books of the State Auditor together with the amount and percentage of increase or decrease in appropriations compared with actual costs for the two prior bienniums. 

In this connection a word of caution is perhaps desirable. We are here making a comparison of two different things, appropriations for one period with actual expenditures for two prior periods. It does not necessarily follow that all appropriations will be fully expended. Certain appropriations such as for claims and awards under the Industrial Insurance laws, pensions for Teachers' Retirement System, gasoline tax refunds, and others where the expenditures are fixed or controlled by statute, are generally made in amounts somewhat in excess of the normal rate of expenditure in order to provide for unusual demands that might occur. The reader will understand that in such cases reversions of unexpended balances can be expected. The comparison is of value, however, in the cases of most appropriations for salaries and wages and for operations of the various departments and functions of government, particularly so in determining whether the legislature has provided sufficient appropriation for the operation of the regular state offices, departments and institutions. This determination can best be made from a study of the detailed schedules of the report rather than from summary totals. 

The data submitted are arranged in three statements: 

(1) RECAPITULATION 

page 6 This is a summary statement of the over-all expenditures from the State Treasury broken down by the major purposes of expenditure, grouped under the following headings: (a) Regular Operating Expenses. Under this heading are arranged the comparable costs of State 

Government. It includes the salaries and operating costs of all elective and appointive State Officers; 

Boards and Committees; State Institutions; University and Colleges; State Highways; and miscellaneous functions of State Government. Also on a separate line is shown the aggregate amount for Capital Outlays, Major Repairs and Betterments for the foregoing state agencies. This grouping represented approximately 25% of the actual expenditures from the State Treasury during the 1943-1945 and 1945-1947 Bienniums. (b) State Aid and Assistance. Under this heading are arranged the state support to Elementary and High Schools and Junior Colleges; aid to Counties, Cities, Towns and Other Taxing Districts, including funds distributed as well as payment for services rendered to or on account of such municipalities; and Pensions, Awards and Assistance, including old age and other categories of public assistance, teachers' and various state employees retirement funds, industrial insurance benefits and assistance to veterans and other handicapped persons. This grouping represented approximately 73% of the actual expenditures during the two previous bienniums. (c) Other. Under this heading are included provisions for Emergencies and Contingencies; Transfers between funds (which do not constitute expenditures); Refunds of revenues; and Non-recurring and 

Non-comparable Items. This latter classification includes war-time and other activities that are now discontinued or not included in the 1947-1949 appropriations. 2

(2) DETAILED STATEMENTS BY DEPARTMENTS 

pages 7 to 29

This statement carries the same information as above only broken down by each department or function of Government and segregated by salaries, operations and other activities. 

The information shown in this statement is on a comparable basis, arranged according to major objects. as listed in the Recapitulation, regardless of the fund from which expended. 

Appropriations for a department may appear under different headings; for instance, administrative appropriations for the Department of Health appear under "Administrative Code and Appointive Departments"; assistance programs such as maternity and infant care, treatment for venereal diseases, cerebral palsy, etc., appear under "Pensions, Awards and Assistance"; public health work and tuberculosis hospitalization are listed under "Counties, Cities and Other Taxing Districts," while certain war emergency allotments are listed under "Non-Recurring and Non-Comparable Items.” 

A study of the data included in this section of the report will show the extent that expenditures for  and for Operating and other expenses have or have not been increased during the biennium 1945-1947 and whether or not the appropriations made by the 1947 Legislature will permit further increases or require some retrenchment.

In conclusion, it is again pointed out that the comparison being made is between appropriations for the 1947-1949 Biennium with actual expenditures for the two prior periods. 

The appropriations have been made and can be expended. Whether they are to be fully expended or substantial amounts reverted depends on several factors, including: (1) Continued increases in the cost of commodities and equipment purchased by the State Departments. (2) Increases in salaries and wages paid either through employment of additional employees, higher salary rates, or both. (3) The actual demand made upon those appropriations where the amount of payments are controlled by statute. (4) Capital Outlay Program. Availability of materials and labor will have a bearing on the number of buildings that can be completed during the fiscal biennium. (5) Failure in Revenue. In a number of appropriations, expenditures are limited to revenues received. (6) The extent that the Governor exercises the power vested in him to control expenditures under the Pre-Audit Law and other provisions of law relating to the allotment of funds by the Governor.
