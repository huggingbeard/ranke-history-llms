The School Library 

What did the children find out about a library?

All the children in grade two liked the library hour. They never wanted to miss it. 

One afternoon each week they went to the library. The librarian helped them find the books they wanted to read.

Sometimes during the library hour the children showed their books or pictures to one another. They often told about stories they had read. 29

One day Fred wanted to look at a picture in Bob's book. Fred did not close his book. He put it on the table with its face down. "Please do not put the book down on its face, Fred," said the librarian. "Close the book before you put it down. I know you want to help take care of our books. Then they will last a long time." 

Fred picked up the book and closed it. He thanked the librarian for telling him how to take care of it. 30

One day Jean did not finish her book during the library hour. She wanted to take it home. She asked the librarian if she could take it. "Our grade is going to have a book party," said Jean. "I want to finish the Halloween story in this book. I am going to tell it to the children at the party."

The librarian opened the book at the back. She took out a white card.

Jean wrote her name on the card. The librarian put the date on it. She closed the book and gave it to Jean. "Please do not keep the book more than one week, Jean. Other children may want to read it then," said the librarian. 31

The children in grade two had a book party in the library. They invited the first grade children.

Some of the children in grade two told stories. Other children made up a play about one of their stories. 

They played the story of Goldilocks and the Three Bears. "That was a fine book party," said a boy in grade one. "Next year we will be able to read stories. We will invite you to our book party.” 32

He wore boots and mittens made of sealskin. He put dried moss inside his boots. When it was very cold, he put moss inside his mittens, too. 233

With moss in the mittens his hands were warm even on the coldest days. 

The Eskimos drove their dog sleds over the snow and ice very fast.

When they reached the sea where they were to hunt, they unpacked the sleds. They made houses called igloos from blocks of snow. Snow benches, covered with blankets of sealskins and reindeer skins, were the beds. 

Soon smoke curled from the oil lamps through the tops of the igloos. 

Tingook, wrapped in a blanket of sealskin, curled up on one of the moss pillows on a snow bench. "This snow igloo is warmer than a skin tent," he said. "It is almost as warm as our winter house." 234

Each morning the men looked for walrus. Many days passed before any walrus were seen. At last Tingook's father saw several walrus swimming near by. He did not make a sound as he crawled out on the smooth ice. 

One big walrus wondered what was crawling on the ice. It swam slowly by and raised up to look. Tingook's father was able to get the walrus. 235

Tingook helped his father skin the walrus and cut up the meat. They packed it and took it to their igloo. 

That night everyone met in the igloo. There was plenty of food for all of them. The meat was cooked in stone bowls over an oil lamp. Pieces were taken out of the bowls with the fingers and passed around. 

After dinner everyone enjoyed songs and stories. They were happy because there were plenty of walrus near. 

There would be frozen meat to take home. During the winter there would be plenty of food for all. 236

The Polar Cubs 

What did Tingook think of the cubs? 

Tingook knew many ways to enjoy the long winter. One of the things he liked best was to skate about making marks on the hard snow. 

One morning when Tingook became tired, he crawled behind some ice to rest. While he was resting, he saw a shadow move near some cakes of ice. 

He could not hear a sound. He kept quiet and watched closely. 237

There was a polar bear with two polar cubs. The mother bear left the cubs behind the cakes of ice. She went toward a deep pool of water to hunt for seal. 

She had not gone far when the cubs started to follow her. The mother bear boxed their ears and sent them back to hide behind the ice. 

Tingook wanted to laugh, but he did not make a sound. He did not want the polar bear to know he was there. 

When the mother bear had gone, one of the cubs ran toward the ice where Tingook was resting. It looked so little and playful that Tingook thought he might have some fun with it. He crawled toward it. 238

He had almost reached it when he heard a queer noise. He turned quickly. The big polar bear was coming straight toward him. Tingook started for home as fast as his skates could carry him. "She might get me instead of a seal," he thought. "I wish she had stayed away a little while longer. I wanted to play with those cubs, but I knew better than to try it when the mother bear was near." 239

Eskimo Games 

Which of these games would you enjoy? 

Tingook and his friends enjoy many games. In one game a large piece of bone is used. In the bone are rows of holes. A player throws the bone straight into the air. As it comes down, he catches it upon a stick. 

The one who catches the bone ten times wins. Then he has another turn. 

This time he must catch the bone on the holes in the center row. the best players can do this.

In summer both boys and girls play ball. A soft ball is passed from one to another. The players try to keep the ball in motion. They jump about, hitting the ball. Each player seems to be trying to shout louder than the others. 

If the ball drops to the ground, everyone rests for a while. Then at it the players go again, making more noise than before. 241

In winter sometimes the boys have a rolling race. Each boy puts his head between his legs and wraps his arms around them. Then over and over he rolls, down the hill, like a big fur ball. 

One day Tingook went to the hill where some boys were rolling. "Come on, Tingook," shouted Metek. "See who can roll to the bottom of the hill first." 

Tingook and Metek rolled up like balls. At the word "go" they started down the hill. "Go on, Tingook, go on," shouted the boys. "You are ahead of Metek." 

Tingook wanted to see how far ahead he was. He let go his legs. 242

This made him stop rolling. As he looked up, a big fur ball rolled by. "There goes Metek!" called the boys. "He will win the race." 

When Metek got to the bottom of the hill, he looked back at Tingook and laughed. "I would always win if you did that," he called. "The next time I will keep on going until I win the race," said Tingook. 243
