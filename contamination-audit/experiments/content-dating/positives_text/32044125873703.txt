REPUBLIC OF CHINA LEADER OF DRUG RING SENTENCED TO DEATH, OTHERS GET LIFE IMPRISONMENT [Article: "Big Drug Pushing Case Decided, Ch'en Chin-sheng (7115 6651 3932) 

Sentenced to Death, Wu Sen-teng (0702 2773 5671) and Twenty Others Get Heavy 

Sentences"; Taipei, Lien-ho Pao, Chinese, 1 October 1971, p 31

The Kao-hsiung district criminal court announced its decision yesterday (30 September) on the big drug pushing case of Kao-nan [7559 0589] district which was broken by the narcotics squad of the investigation bureau in July of this year. Of the 22 defendants, the professional drug pusher Ch'en 

Chin-sheng was sentenced to death, the four professional drug pushers, Wu Sen-teng, Tai Cheng-chi [2071 2398 0679], K'o Tsai-i [2688 0375 4135], and Lu T'ien-liang [4151 1131 5328], were sentenced to life imprisonment, all were stripped of their civil rights for life, and the 17 other defendants each got heavy sentences.

The decision stated that the defendant Ch'en Chin-sheng (54, nicknamed "Big Belly," in custody) and the fugitive Ts'ai Ch'ang-ch'ing [5591 7022 1987] (nicknamed Chungking [pun: characters could also mean heavy Ch'ing] or Bearded 

Ch'ing), were the two main drug pushers in the Kao-nan district. After buying their drugs in the Pei-pu district, they resold the drugs for a profit in the region of Kao-hsiung and T'ai-nan. They both had their own resalers but they also kept in contact with each other and supplied each other with drugs. 

The defendant Ch'en Chin-sheng, has been dealing in drugs since the restoration of Taiwan and he is an addict himself. When selling drugs, since 

Ch'en Chin-sheng was a big dealer, he seldom traded directly with the drug users but sold his drugs through middiemen such as the defendants Wu Sen-teng (30, resident of Kao-hsiung municipality, in custody), Tai Cheng-chi (32, resident of Kao-hsiung hsien, in custody), and K'o Tsai-i (47, resident of

T'ai-nan hsien, in custody). The fugitive Ts'ai Ch'ang-ch'ing's drugs were resold by the defendant Lu T'ien-liang (35, resident of Kao-hsiung municipality, in custody).

Ch'en Chin-sheng and the other defendants used a roundabout way of delivering their drugs from the middlemen to the small dealers and then to - 1 -

the addicts.

On each drug transaction, one or more of them would take a taxi to a prearranged spot and meet a prearranged person, but instead of turning over the goods right then, would inform the other party where to go next to pick up the goods, and would set the time to go there. In 1970, they began dealing in large quantities of drugs. Since they kept their selling methods secret, they were not detected by the units concerned, and their drug dealing operations went smoothly, they grew all the more haughty.

In March and April of this year, after receiving a tip, the narcotics squad of the investigation bureau started an investigation. After more than three months, the whole case was finally broken. After interrogation by the 

Kao-hsiung district inspector's office, Ch'en Chin-sheng and the 21 others were indicted in late August.

After a month's trial, the Kao-hsiung district criminal court yesterday announced its decision.

According to the main sentence of the decision, Ch'en Chin-sheng as a habitual criminal in processing drugs was sentenced to 7 years and also as a habitual criminal in selling drugs was sentenced to death and deprivation of all civil rights. The death sentence is to be executed. Five large packages, eight medium size packages, and four small packages of morphine, three cans of heroin, and all his property worth 36,590 Taiwan dollars were confiscated.

COLOMBIA SMUGGLING OF MARIHUANA TO U. S. INCREASES [Article; Cali, Occidente, Spanish, 20 October 1971, p 10] 

The Customs Investigating Committee, appointed by the Senate, declared that smuggling has taken another unsuspected turn, which is threatening to provoke the suspension of the shipment of bananas to the 

United States.

As it found out, in the Turbo region, the banana-producing zone, marihuana traffic is increasing due to the fact that the plant is exported to the United States under bunches of bananas. 

A pound is quoted at 150 pesos, and in the United States they sell it at 250 U.S. dollars. There is no secret service in Turbo and there are only 14 policemen. On the other hand, the Customs guard does not have at its disposal sufficient means for control either in order to repress the smuggling or in order to promote investigations, due to which the majority of cases go unpunished.

The Senate committee, which is composed of legislators Armando Zabarain and Enrique Millan, the delegate from the national attorney general's office, and court clerk Felipe Abisambra, went to Turbo where it had the opportunity to find out about the previously reported facts. 

According to the statements of the investigators, the United States has notified the Colombian Government that if that illegal traffic continues, the entrance of bananas into the United States could be prohibited.

Another factor that helps to make the Turbo zone a paradise for smugglers is the fact that several yachts that bring contraband from Panama are anchored at the port. These small ships are protected by the flags of the United States and Panama and operate with a permit 

From National Customs, - 9-

The network of smugglers that operates on the coast of Choco and in Turbo is vast. It is pointed out that in the Choco territory there are no fewer than 20 equipped underground airports that operate in the middle of the jungle. There are also several underground airports in 

Cordoba. The smugglers also rely on a fleet of small aircraft. The directors of the illegal trade are individuals who have made large fortunes. A spokesman for the Senate committee stated that in all that vast northwestern region of the nation there are no appropriate highways, which makes combating smuggling by current methods even more difficult. 11,438

COLOMBIA INTERNATIONAL DRUG TRAFFICKER ARRESTED [Article; Medellin, El Colombiano, Spanish, 21 October 1971, p 4] 

Yesterday morning at the Las Playas Airport forces of the Administrative Department of Security (DAS) carried out an important capture of a woman coming from the United States after having established that they were dealing with an International trafficker in marihuana. 

Accoridng to reports in that secret corps, she was identified as Luz Myriam Gomez or Cifuentes Gomez and has her residence at 24th Street and Highway 75. The arrest occurred minutes after she had peacefully passed through the customs offices without having her contraband plus several thousands of dollars confiscated. 

In the initial investigation that the DAS made, it was verified that the woman was returning from the United States where she had taken a large quantity of marihuana without it being known how she transported it.

In the investigation the agents found 3,600 dollars in cash on her, the product of her sales of the marihuana abroad, as well as a large quantity of contraband valued at approximately 100,000 pesos.

Her Passport 

The woman was taken to the offices of the DAS for examination. 

Upon inspection of her passport, it was further established that it was falsified and that she has been traveling with it for several months. 

In addition, it was discovered that she is the same person that robbed the Caldas Bank in this city several days ago, or so it is said in this secret bureau. Marihuana

After executing several duties in these offices, several agents went to the residence of Luz Myriam Gomez for the purpose of making an exhaustive search. Several arrobas [1 arroba equals 25 pounds] of CAD 11

marihuana, ready to be sent abroad, were found inside a cupboard, camouflaged in some merchandise. Authorities conclude that the woman was prepared to remain in this city for a period of 2 days before traveling again to the United States, taking with her the marihuana that was confiscated in her house. 

Yesterday afternoon she was turned over to the jurisdiction of the Customs judge of this city for a rigorous investigation, as well as to concretely clarify several facts so that she could enlarge upon the statement that she initially made concerning the way that she had entered with the contraband and money. 11,438

COLOMBIA RECOVERED ADDICTS CAMPAIGN AGAINST DRUG USE [Article; Bogota, El Siglo, Spanish, 13 October 1971, p 3] 

A group of young people belonging to the Drug Addicts Anonymous movement organized a demonstration in the downtown streets of Bogota yesterday in order to warn the citizenry, especially young people and parents, of the dangers of heroin and related drugs, narcotics, stupefacients, and marihuana. 

Drug Addicts Anonymous chose this date because it is significant and symbolic in that it marks the celebration of Latin Race Day. 

The young demonstrators proceeded along 7th Road from Bolivar Square to Jimenez Avenue, urging the citizenry to combat the consumption of marihuane, alcohol, and the hallucinogenic drugs.

The demonstration won popular support, as the people who saw the young people were surprised and gratified to see that young people formerly in the clutches of hard drugs have now, thanks to their own will and efforts, recovered. 

A group of these young people, including two girls, Magnolia and Amparo, and their comrades in the Drug Addicts (Recovered) Anonymous association, visited the editorial offices of this newspaper. 

One of the girls spoke of her own recovery as follows: "I feel entirely recovered, both physically and mentally. 

I have begun my studies again, and I would urgently invite the young people who are suffering from the scourge of marihuana or drug addiction to join this movement of ours, which has done so much for us in this connection.' -13.

In answer to questions, one of the other young recovered addicts said: "I am 15 years old. I began to smoke marihuana at 13, in the hippie environment of the 60's. I let my hair grow and completely abandoned my home and studies. I wandered throughout the city. I hitchhiked to Medellin and Cali. I ended up in prison, charged with selling marihuana. They sent me back to my home. My family told me to join Drug Addicts Anonymous. I attended the meetings, but my recovery was difficult. 

Little by little I fought free of the influence of drugs, and today I am devoted to this movement which saved me. I make a fervent appeal to all young people who suffer from what I suffered from, to join this movement. They can come to No 9-33 13th Street, Room 606, where they will be warmly welcomed. The meetings there are on 

Monday, Wednesday, Thursday, and Friday, beginning at 7 p.m.

Absolutely no money is needed to join this movement, nor are there any requirements as to age or social status. The only thing of interest to us is that those who come suffer from the problem and really want to recover." 

Another recovered addict spoke as follows:

"At 11 years of age I was led into trying marihuana by a friend. At 13 I left home to join a group of boys who smoked marihuana. I even reached the point where marihuana no longer gave me the desired effect. Then I sought other drugs, encouraged by those comrades whom I thought marvelous. abandoned my home completely, and pursued the most depressing wandering life. I walked the streets begging, not not to buy food but marihuana. I went to Venezuela, partly on foot and partly traveling in vehicles which would pick me up. I was jailed on the charge of peddling marihuana. I reached bottom. Then one day, a comrade with whom I had also smoked marihuana suggested that we go to Drug Addicts Anonymous, where I began to see something new. I was struck by the examples of recovery. I saw and I experienced a different world -- healthy, comfortable. 

I stopped smoking. This was more than 7 months ago now. I feel like a real man, rather than the mistaken notion that I was a man because I smoked marihuana. Today I am with my family again, and I am studying. I owe my whole recovery to Drug 

Addicts Anonymous. 

Another former addict said: "I will be 20 years old on 11 December. I began to smoke marihuana at 15. But then I went on to drugs. I spent 

7 months in the clinic in La Paz. I was also in Sibate. For 

8 days now I have been attending Drug Addicts Anonymous meetings, where I feel that my recovery is progressing. I want to

VENEZUELA INTERMINISTERIAL COMMISSION WORK PLAN TO DEAL WITH DRUGS READY 

Article; Caracas, La Verdad, Spanish, 28 September 1971, p b-87

A work plan to be progressively applied has been prepared by the Interministerial Commission on the Illicit Use of Drugs, headed by the Attorney General of the Republic, Dr Cesar Naranjo Ostty. 

It is divided into four main sections: 1) prevention, including education and publicity, to be handled by the Ministry of 

Education; 2) the rehabilitation of drug addicts, who will be sent to special centers for treatment. This treatment will be entrusted to the Ministries of Justice and Public Works, which will have the necessary legal powers; 3) the repression of drug dealers and growers, of persons illegally producing drugs in laboratories, of persons in possession of drugs; 4) the investigation and conduct of competent organizations, to wit, the Ministries of Finance, Domestic 

Relations, Defense, and the Judicial Technical Police. 

President Nery Mago stated that the commission had completed a report to be presented to the Chamber at the end of October. It includes the following recommendations, among others: reform of the penal code; reform of the pharmacy law; re form of the narcotics law; and reform of the law concerning vagrants and wrongdoers. 

Deputy Nery Mago also said that the commission has worked out a narcotics bill to be presented before the Chamber. He said that in accordance with recommendations made by the commission, consumption of barbiturates will drop 60 percent.

The interministerial commission is made up of the Ministries of Justice, Education, Defense, Domestic Relations, Health, and CORDIPLAN Office of Coordination and Planning/.
