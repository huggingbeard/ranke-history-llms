Evelyn Armitage had not been at all eager to make the short trip, but when she found herself in the car half an hour later, she would have admitted that there were redeeming features in the situation. In the first place, the drive from Sunset Harbor across Caribou Island was one that never lost its attraction for her. 

In the second, Harold being on the front seat with the chauffeur, she was left to herself, and to be alone in the social season of mid-July was a rare luxury. 

The motion prevented her from thinking too hard, though there was a certain crucial matter which would have to get itself thought out sometime soon but from which she was glad to escape for the present. If she had stayed at home on the porch of Surfside, she could hardly have escaped from herself. But here beauty came rushing at her. The road looped around the curves of bay after bay, the wooded shores, dark as indigo, being caught on the mirroring ripples and pierced with lances of silver.
