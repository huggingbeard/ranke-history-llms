DECEMBER 1, 1948. 

RESOLVE OF AUTHORIZATION. 

CHAPTER 89. 

To the Honorable Senate and House of Representatives. 

The special unpaid Commission, established for the purpose of making an investigation of the conduct of the Reformatory for Women at Framingham, hereby submits the following report.

RESOLVE PROVIDING FOR AN INVESTIGATION BY AN UNPAID SPECIAL COMMISSION OF THE CONDUCT OF THE REFORMATORY FOR WOMEN AT FRAMINGHAM. 

Resolved, That an unpaid special commission, to consist of three members of the senate to be designated by the president thereof, five members of the house of representatives to be designated by the speaker thereof, and three persons to be appointed by the governor, is hereby established to make an immediate and thorough investigation of the conduct of the reformatory for women at Framingham to determine whether said institution is being operated properly and in accordance with the laws relating thereto, and with a view to making such changes in the law as may seem advisable for improving conditions at said institution. The commission shall be provided with quarters in the state house or elsewhere, may hold public hearings, and shall have the power to summon witnesses and to require the production of books, records, contracts and papers of said reformatory

Said commission shall report to the general court the results of its investigation and study, and its recommendations, if any, together with drafts of legislation necessary to carry its recommendations into effect, by filing the same with the clerk of the senate on or before the first Wednesday of December in the current year. 

Approved, June 16, 1948.

The Commission met and organized on July 15, 1948. 

Senator J. Elmer Callahan was elected chairman, and 

Representative Allan Roy Kingston, vice chairman.

A number of meetings were held in executive session, the Commission held one public hearing, and a trip was made to the Reformatory for Women at Framingham.

From all the information before the Commission it appears that changes both in laws pertaining to women committed to the Reformatory and in rules pertaining to administration of the Reformatory and its inmates are vitally necessary.

The Commission recommends that a new Commission be appointed, with sufficient funds to thoroughly investigate our state institution and those of other States. 

All records including transcription of evidence taken at the public hearing are to be turned over to the new Commission.

J. ELMER CALLAHAN, ALLAN ROY KINGSTON, Chairman.

SUPPLEMENTARY REPORT OF SENATOR MICHAEL LOPRESTI, MEMBER OF OF THE RECESS COMMISSION INVESTIGATING CONDITIONS AT THE MASSACHUSETTS WOMEN'S REFORMATORY AT FRAMING- HAM. 

I agree with the majority members of the Recess Commission in voting to recommend that a new recess commission be appointed by the Massachusetts General Court to continue the uncompleted investigation of the conditions at the Reformatory for Women at Framingham.

This Recess Commission was controlled by members who were partisan, in favor of the authorities at the Reformatory and dilatory in their duty to the General Court. This group failed to live up to the mandate of the General Court.

I hereby inform the members of the General Court that definite evidence has been gathered that warrants the appointment of another commission to investigate thoroughly, as you so instructed in your resolve. It is imperative to the welfare of inmates at the Reformatory. 

The so-called Dwyer Report, evidence that I have in my possession plus evidence in the possession of Attorney 

Paul A. Carbone and Edmund O'Callaghan, counsel for the Di Benedetto family, will justify your appointment of another commission which, no doubt, will follow your instructions.

The General Court passed your resolve in June, 1948. 

Records will show that the first committee meeting of this recess commission was held by a minority group who formulated plans at their initial meeting to hamper the investigation. The record will show that no clerk was to be hired, that no secretary was to be employed. 

Correspondence will show that no public hearings were to be held. Minutes of the meeting will show you the names of those who participated at the initial meeting.

The moral obligations of members of the Commission can be dealt with as you see fit, but let me warn you of a more serious offence, and that is of the pressure groups in back of the persons responsible for blocking this Commission from performing its duties. Despite the fact that they are held to be our leading citizens in the field of penology, psychology, psychiatry, social welfare and friends of prisoners, and have had the run of the reformatory at Framingham for these past seventeen years, not one of them has every publicly stated that he found anything wrong at the reformatory. Yet I stood ready at the first opportunity given to me at a public or private hearing to produce more than a score of former inmates who would reveal conditions unheard of in this day and age. Now as to the historical facts of the case: 

Last January Mr. and Mrs. Enrico Di Benedetto, constituents of mine, and parents of Antoinette Di Benedetto, accompanied by their counsel, Attorney Paul A. Carbone, conferred with me and requested that I assist them to learn the details in connection with the death of their daughter.

At that time counsel and parents revealed through a series of letters now in the possession of Mr. Carbone the frame of mind of their daughter who was serving an unexpired term as a stubborn child in the Reformatory for Women at Framingham. 

I am sorry I cannot express the frame of mind in psychiatric terms so that this record could be easily analyzed by social welfare groups and scientific and medical authorities, with whom it has been my misfortune to come in contact within the past eleven months.
