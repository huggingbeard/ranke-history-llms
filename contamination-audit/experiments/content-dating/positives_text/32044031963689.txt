REPORT ON REVIEW OF MORTGAGE SERVICING OPERA- TIONS, FEDERAL NATIONAL MORTGAGE ASSOCIATION, HOUSING AND HOME FINANCE AGENCY, OCTOBER 1957 

The General Accounting Office has made a review of the Mortgage 

Servicing Operations of the FEDERAL NATIONAL MORTGAGE ASSOCIA- TION, Housing and Home Finance Agency, as of October 1957, pursuant to the Government Corporation Control Act (31 U. S. C. 

841) and the Budget and Accounting Act, 1921 (31 U. S. C. 53). 

SCOPE OF REVIEW 

Our review, which was conducted at the Atlanta and Dallas agency offices and the Washington central office, included consideration and evaluation of the Federal National Mortgage Association's policies, procedures, and administrative controls to evaluate their effectiveness and a selective examination of case files and records to determine actual operating practices. 

SUMMARY OF FINDINGS 

Noted during our review were the following principal deficiencies: 

1. FNMA employees entered into an agreement with a defaulted mortgagor before consulting agency legal counsel. As a result, FNMA was prevented from recovering rents which were collected by the mortgagor after default. (See pp. 3 to 5.) 

2. FNMA began foreclosure proceedings against a mortgagor of rental properties without consulting agency legal counsel about the recovery of rents collected during the period of foreclosure. 

As a result, the mortgagor was able to retain these rents. (See pp. 5 and 6.) 

3. FNMA's reviews of (a) defaulted mortgagor's operations and (b) servicer's reports and inspections were inadequate. (See pp. 7 and 8.) 

4. FNMA has not made any annual general inspections of properties securing mortgages that were acquired from RFC in 1954. Such inspections are intended to determine whether there is evidence of waste or lack of repairs to the mortgage collateral. (See pp. 8 and 9.) 

5. FNMA did not determine the physical condition of certain multifamily housing projects before it approved the deferment of mortgage principal payments. (See pp. 9 and 10.) 

6. FNMA did not take aggressive action to retard deterioration of a multifamily project. (See p. 11.) 

7. FNMA had not audited the records of multifamily project mortgagors that were required to remit excess cash receipts. (See pp. 12 to 14.) 1 20011-58-

GENERAL Federal National Mortgage Association (FNMA) is a constituent agency of the Housing and Home Finance Agency. FNMA buys, services, and sells eligible mortgages insured or guaranteed by the 

Veterans Administration (VA) or insured by the Federal Housing Administration (FHA) to provide (1) supplementary assistance to the secondary market for home mortgages and (2) special assistance for the financing of certain home mortgages. FNMA is also required to manage and liquidate in an orderly manner its October 31, 1954, mortgage portfolio with a minimum of adverse effect upon the home mortgage market and at a minimum of loss to the Federal Government. 

The general policies which govern FNMA's operations are determined by a 5-man board of directors of which the Housing and Home 

Finance Administrator is chairman. The President of FNMA is the chief executive officer.

FNMA maintains a Washington, D. C., central office organization which directs operations. Mortgages are purchased, serviced, and sold through five agency (field) offices located in Atlanta, Georgia, Chicago, Illinois, Dallas, Texas, Los Angeles, California, and Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. In addition, a sales office is maintained in 

New York City. 

FNMA mortgages secured by 1to 4-family dwellings are serviced by private organizations. Fees paid to the servicers by FNMA are based on a rate of one half of 1 percent per annum of the unmatured principal balance of each mortgage serviced. Servicing consists of (1) collecting monthly installments and remitting principal and interest, less service fees, to FNMA, (2) paying taxes and insurance on the mortgaged properties, (3) maintaining accounting records and rendering reports to FNMA, (4) inspecting mortgaged properties, (5) attempting to cure mortgagors' delinquencies, and (6) recommending action to be taken by FNMA on delinquent mortgages. 

FNMA mortgages secured by multifamily housing projects are serviced by FNMA agency offices. In addition to its FHA-insured project mortgage holdings, FNMA also owns certain mortgages which were acquired from the Reconstruction Finance Corporation (RFC) on July 1, 1954, and which are neither FHA-insured nor VA-guaranteed. The mortgages acquired from RFC are serviced by the FNMA 

Washington central office.

If a mortgagor does not make a payment due under the mortgage terms, FNMA may (1) assist the mortgagor in curing the default by entering into a forbearance agreement or a modification agreement, (2) initiate action to obtain title to the property by foreclosure or by acceptance of a deed to the property from the mortgagor, or (3) in the case of an FHA-insured mortgage secured by a multifamily housing project, assign the mortgage to FHA. 

For an individual mortgage secured by a 1to 4-family dwelling a forbearance agreement permits the mortgagor to defer principa payments for a specified period of time after which the principal payments are increased until the default is cured. For a mortgag on a multifamily housing project, or for mortgages on a group of 10 or more 1to 4-family dwellings owned by 1 mortgagor, a forbearanc agreement or a modification agreement permits the mortgagors t defer principal payments for a specified period of time during which

they are required to remit monthly to FNMA (or its servicer) cash receipts in excess of stipulated disbursements. Under the latter type of forbearance agreement, a portion of the mortgage interest payments also is deferred. 

FINDINGS AND RECOMMENDATIONS 

Agency managers or other FNMA officials are aware of the deficiencies that are discussed in the following pages of this report. In certain instances no corrective action can be taken by FNMA as far as the specific cases cited in our report are concerned. We believe, however, that, in those cases where no corrective action can be taken, the deficiencies we cite illustrate weaknesses in certain of FNMA's administrative procedures and that our recommendations, if carried out, will help to prevent similar deficiencies in the future. 

FNMA PREVENTED FROM RECOVERING RENTS COLLECTED BY MORTGAGOR AFTER DEFAULT 

During our review of the Atlanta agency office, we noted two instances where rents were collected by a mortgagor in default but were not assigned to FNMA. 

Mealing Heights, Inc., North Augusta, South Carolina

Mealing Heights, Inc., did not make the January 1956 and subsequent monthly payments for forty 1-family rental properties on which it was the mortgagor.

In a letter dated April 5, 1956, the Atlanta office, before consulting its legal counsel, advised the mortgagor, who was in default and had requested forbearance, that: * * * we are not in position to recommend that any forbearance be granted on Mealing Heights; however, we would be willing to withhold legal action provided the following stipulations are met: (1) Current payments on the 40 units will be made, starting with the 

April 1956 payment, for a period of six months. (2) We are to be furnished operating statements each month, and at any time the net operating return exceeds the current monthly payment on the 40 units, a check will be sent to our Servicer for the overage. 

The mortgagor indicated his acceptance of the FNMA proposal by signing a copy of the FNMA letter. The letter agreement did not provide that expenses would be approved by FNMA only if they were necessary and reasonable, nor did it provide that all rents would be assigned to FNMA. Such provisions, however, are included in formal 

Forbearance Agreements (FNMA Form 234). 

The mortgagor submitted operating statements to FNMA for each of the 6 months from April to September 1956. These statements showed that during this 6-month period gross receipts were $12,159, Operating disbursements were $3,961, and net receipts were $8,198. 

During the period the mortgagor remitted six mortgage payments totaling $11,874, or $3,676 more than the total reported net receipts. 

On October 30, 1956, the Atlanta office extended the letter agreement for an additional 6 months, but the mortgagor did not make the Octo- "ber 1956 and subsequent payments or submit monthly operating statements. tg ניך wh On December 21, 1956, the Atlanta office advised the mortgagor that it would accept a voluntary deed to the 40 properties in lieu of

foreclosing on the mortgages if the mortgagor would assign and remit to FNMA the rents collected for the months of January and February 1957. The mortgagor, however, was not willing to assign and remit the rents and, on February 21, 1957, FNMA instituted foreclosure proceedings. Rents due March 1, 1957, were collected by a receiver appointed by the court. An examination of the mortgagor's records by the FNMA auditors in May 1957 showed that during January and February 1957 the mortgagor collected rents of $4,198 and paid expenses of $955.

FNMA attorneys believe that, in the absence of an agreement providing for the assignment of rents, rents collected by the mortgagor from the date of default to the date the receiver was appointed could not be recovered by FNMA. 

In a memorandum dated September 23, 1957, the Atlanta agency manager informed us that:

Exception was taken to our entering into a forbearance arrangement with the mortgagor-corporation under a letter agreement rather than under 

Forbearance Agreement, FNMA Form 234, and also our failure to capture approximately $4,000 in rental income. While there is a provision in our regulations for entering into formal forbearance with project-mortgagors under FNMA Form 234, where ten or more living units are involved, there is no prohibitation against our entering into other arrangements. *** the action taken was based on an administrative decision. In the instant case the forbearance arrangements worked to our advantage rather than disadvantage, for we were successful in collecting six full regular monthly installments rather than the six reduced payments which would have covered only the net income, or the basic minimum payment of escrow deposits and interest.

Under the Forbearance Agreement, Form 234, the principal portion of the installments would have been waived, in the event income was not sufficient to meet such payment. During the six-month period it was necessary for the mortgagor to supplement funds to meet the full regular monthly installments. 

The Atlanta agency manager's comments indicate that FNMA's purpose in entering into the letter agreement was to require payment of at least one full mortgage installment each month during the forbearance period. We agree that this requirement is desirable, and because of it the letter agreement worked to the Government's advantage during the first 6 months when the mortgagor complied with the agreement. We believe, however, that the letter agreement should have contained an assignment of rents provision such as is included in Forbearance Agreements (FNMA Form 234). Had the letter agreement contained an assignment of rents provision, FNMA might have been enabled to recover by legal action the rents collected by the mortgagor during the 5-month period before the receiver was appointed, when the mortgagor did not comply with the agreement. 

We believe also that a method used by the Dallas agency office offers greater assurance to FNMA that mortgagors will remit one full mort gage installment each month during forbearance periods. In our review of the FNMA Dallas office, we noted that FNMA entered into Forbearance Agreements (FNMA Form 234) with the S & S Realty Company, Benton, Arkansas, and that the company C principals were required to personally guarantee that the amounts to a be remitted to FNMA each month during forbearance periods would not be less than one full mortgage installment.

Recommendation to the President of FNMA 

FNMA should revise its procedures to require that, for those cases where the payment of at least one full mortgage installment each month during a forbearance period appears to be feasible, FNMA agency offices enter into a Forbearance Agreement (FNMA Form 234) and require the mortgagor's principals to personally guarantee the minimum payment.

In a letter to us dated April 15, 1958, the President of FNMA stated that:

It is not always practicable to have the mortgagor's principals personally guarantee the minimum payment, but wherever possible, we shall endeavor to obtain this guarantee. 

On April 18, 1958, by letter, the President of FNMA instructed each agency office to obtain the guarantee wherever possible.

In a memorandum to us dated September 23, 1957, the Atlanta agency manager stated, in part, that: was

We acknowledge that the collection of rental income in these cases poorly handled and we are subject to criticism; however, our procedures have been improved and we do not believe there will be a recurrence. 

Section 2312 of the FNMA Manual deals with the collection of rent during periods when payments on mortgages securing rental property are delinquent. The section requires that specific steps be taken by the servicer and FNMA, including a requirement that the FNMA loan service supervisor consult with agency counsel as to the action which may be taken by FNMA to collect the rental income. 

FNMA files indicate that the Atlanta office did not consult with its agency counsel, as required by section 2312, before entering into the letter agreement with Mealing Heights, Inc., or before instituting foreclosure proceedings on the King Development Company mortgages.

Recommendation to the President of FNMA To give greater assurance that FNMA will be able to collect rental income from mortgagors in default, the legal action available to FNMA under the applicable state laws should be reviewed by the 

Atlanta agency office counsel. The FNMA Washington central office should direct its Atlanta office employees to consult promptly with its agency counsel, as required by section 2312 of the FNMA Manual, on legal matters concerning mortgage defaults involving rental property before entering into agreements with mortgagors or before taking other legal action.

In a letter to us dated April 15, 1958, the President of FNMA stated that:

As you have noted, present instructions require operating personnel to consult with agency counsel before concluding any agreement or contrad which has legal implications. Our personnel are fully aware of this require ment, and in the agency concerned the importance of complying with thi requirement has been impressed upon the appropriate personnel. 

On April 18, 1958, by letter, the President of FNMA called th matter to the attention of each agency office and instructed them to comply with the requirement.

MULTIFAMILY HOUSING PROJECTS NOT INSPECTED BEFORE APPROVING DEFERMENTS OF MORTGAGE PRINCIPAL PAYMENTS 

In our review of the Atlanta and Dallas offices, we noted that the FNMA Washington office approved requests of two multifamily project mortgagors for deferments of mortgage principal payments without first requiring inspections of the collateral properties. The mortgagors had experienced difficulty in meeting full monthly mortgage payments.

Likens-Foster Olathe Corporation, Olathe, Kansas 

(FNMA loan No. 15-105718) 

Effective February 1, 1957, FNMA approved the mortgagor's request for deferral of mortgage principal payments and waiver of replacement reserve payments. On July 24, 1957, the FNMA Dallas office recommended to its Washington central office that the mortgagor's request for a 6-month extension of the deferment and waiver arrangements be denied. On August 2, 1957, however, the FNMA 

Washington central office authorized the extension for another 6 nonths, effective August 1, 1957. At the time the extension was authorized, FNMA had not inspected the property collateral to the nortgage in 16 months (since April 2, 1956) to determine its physical condition. On September 6 and 7, 1957, however, FNMA did inspect he property and observed that the general condition of the property vas poor and that numerous repairs were required.

In a letter to us dated October 9, 1957, the Dallas agency manager stated: *** we did apparently fail to secure an inspection report on this project prior to our consideration of a modification agreement proposed by the mortgagor. However, I would again like to emphasize*** that as a policy we believe it advisable to always have an inspection made of any project which evidences difficulty in meeting their monthly installment payments, and also as a policy we would require this inspection before entering into any terms of forbearance or modification. 

In this instance there was some question actually as to whether we would consider an extension of the modification agreement, and in fact, we have recommended to our Washington office that their request for forbearance be denied and that we institute liquidation proceedings if the loans were not brought current. However, after a review of all the factors our Washington office felt that a further extension of the agreement would be to the best interest of both the mortgagor and this Association * * *.

Magnolia Springs Apartments, Green Cove Springs, 

Florida (FNMA loan No. 09-83588)

During the 18-month period beginning March 1, 1956, FNMA approved the mortgagor's requests for deferral of mortgage principal payments and waiver of replacement reserve payments. On August 

6, 1957, the FNMA Atlanta office recommended to its Washington central office against a 6-month extension of the deferment and waiver arrangements. The Atlanta office believed that FNMA should assign the mortgage to FHA. On August 27, 1957, however, the FNMA 

Washington central office authorized the extension for another 6 months, effective September 1, 1957. The last FNMA inspection report in the Atlanta office files at that time was dated August 29, 1956, and was a year old. This report showed that the property collateral to the mortgage generally was in good condition. 

Recommendation to the President of FNMA We believe that the FNMA Washington central office should have required the Atlanta and Dallas offices to inspect the properties and should have considered the physical condition of the properties before authorizing the deferment and waiver of mortgage payments. We recommend, therefore, that FNMA revise its procedures to require that, on receipt of a multifamily project mortgagor's request for deferment of mortgage principal payments, the agency office make an inspection of the collateral property and submit a copy of the inspection report to the FNMA Washington office for review.

In a letter to us dated April 15, 1958, the President of FNMA stated that:

One property in each of our Atlanta and Dallas Agencies was not inspected before the extension of forbearance agreements. Our procedures in this respect are being reviewed to make certain that inspections take place under such circumstances.

Our review at the Atlanta and Dallas agency offices included review of all deferments of mortgage principal payments of multi family project mortgagors where the deferment period had not expired At the time of our review, however, only one multifamily projec mortgagor at each office was operating under such a deferment, and the same deficiency was noted in both cases. 

On April 18, 1958, by letter, the President of FNMA instructed agency offices to inspect all multifamily housing projects befor approving mortgage deferments.

AGGRESSIVE ACTION NOT TAKEN TO RETARD DETERIORATION OF A MULTIFAMILY PROJECT 

A FNMA inspection of the Council Heights project (FNMA loan 

No. 35-36155) at Lawton, Oklahoma, in April 1956 disclosed the need for exterior painting of all buildings. The mortgagor is experiencing financial difficulty and invariably remits the mortgage payment about a month late. The FNMA Dallas office has not pressed the mortgagor to perform the required painting because of his financial difficulty. 

On February 7, 1957, the FHA Oklahoma City insuring office advised the FNMA Dallas office that:

It is our opinion that * * * the exterior wood trim of all buildings should be painted this year in order to protect the surface from deterioration. 

On June 13, 1957, the mortgagor advised the FNMA Dallas office that:

The outside trim on the buildings needs to be painted now, in order to forestall serious damage to the wood and save the property from suffering loss in the months ahead.

In view of your letter that requests arrangements be made to take care of the future installments on or before the first day in which they fall due, there is a question in our minds whether to continue trying to keep the property well maintained or neglect it entirely in an effort to get ahead financially.

An FNMA field representative inspected the property on July 25, 1957, and reported that: *** [the exteriors of] all structures should be painted. The desirability of it being painted was pointed out in my previous inspection of April 27, 1956. The need is naturally becoming increasingly more urgent.

The cost of this outside painting for all the units, which we believe should be done, would probably run in to some thousands of dollars which, obviously, the mortgagor does not have. AUDITS NOT MADE OF THE RECORDS OF MULTIFAMILY PROJECT MORTGAGORS REQUIRED TO REMIT EXCESS CASH RECEIPTS 

In our review we noted that FNMA had not made a site audit of the operations of two multifamily project mortgagors who, since February 1955 and March 1956, were required to remit residual receipts to FNMA monthly. The mortgagors are required to submit monthly reports of income and expenses to FNMA, and FNMA auditors have made office reviews of some of these reports. 

Dunbar House Inc., Washington, D. C. 

(FNMA loan No. 600050)

The original loan of $750,000 on this project was made by RFC on 

October 13, 1947, and was to have been repaid in full by October 13, 1957. The mortgage, which is not FHA-insured or VA-guaranteed, was acquired by FNMA from RFC on July 1, 1954. At November 30, 1957, the unpaid mortgage principal balance was $284,721 and, of the $465,279 repaid, $431,176 was paid as consideration for the release of portions of the mortgage security. The first monthly mortgage principal payment, due in May 1948, was deferred until July 1951, and thereafter relatively few monthly payments were made. As of November 30, 1957, (1) no mortgage principal payment had been made since January 1955 and this payment covered the monthly installment due September 1954 and (2) no mortgage interest payment had been made since May 1957 and this payment covered interest through 

November 1955. Since March 1955, FNMA has advanced the mortgagor $20,244 to pay real estate taxes and to modernize the elevators. 

The mortgagor has repaid $13,278 of these advances, leaving an unpaid balance of $6,966 at November 30, 1957.

The mortgage (deed of trust) provides that advances to the borrower for protection of security shall be repaid with interest at the rate of 6 percent per annum. In our review we noted that such advances are being repaid with interest at the mortgage interest rate of 42 percent per annum. An FNMA official informed us that, on advances made by FNMA for the protection of mortgage security, FNMA has charged interest at the mortgage interest rate. This policy, however, has never been officially adopted in writing by the agency. We believe that FNMA should have a written policy on this matter. Further, unless an official administrative decision has been made to reduce the interest rate, we believe that the interest charged on advances should not be at less than the rate provided for in the mortgage agreement. 

At the insistence of FNMA, the mortgagor contracted with an independent firm in February 1955 to (1) manage the property, (2) submit to FNMA monthly reports of income and expenses, and (3) remit monthly to FNMA any excess of cash receipts over disburse ments. The monthly reports of the managing agent are reviewed by 

FNMA auditors. 

In January 1956 the FNMA loan manager requested the director of examination and audit to make an audit of the mortgagor in the near future. The auditors, however, have not made a site audit of the operations of the mortgagor or its managing agent. Considering the history of defaults on this mortgage, a comprehensive site audit of the records and operations of the mortgagor by FNMA would be desirable.

In a letter to us dated April 15, 1958, the President of FNMA stated that:

The Director of Examination and Audit has personally visited the hotel and reviewed its [accounting system] records and procedures in 1956, 1957 and 1958. He has also discussed the operations with the representatives of the Managing Agent and the hotel auditor, Mr. S. W. Kessee, who performs a continuous audit of the records and operations of the hotel. 

The comments of the hotel auditor on the extent of his examination and the records and procedures maintained are set forth on the attached memorandum. A daily control and supervision of operations is exercised by a representative of The Carey Winston Company, the Managing Agent, which has an excellent reputation in this field. 

In addition, members of the Loan Manager's staff have visited the hotel and held conferences in our offices to discuss the operations with the Man-aging Agent and the mortgagor. 

The default in principal payments is the result of declining room occupancy caused by the movement of transient patronage to downtown hotels, the general decline in all hotel revenue in Washington, and the need for renovation of the hotel's furnishings and equipment. The Managing Agent is pursuing an aggressive campaign to increase occupancy and there has. recently been some improvement. The mortgagor is attempting to secure additional capital with which to replace present furnishings and equipment. 

In view of the records and procedures maintained by the hotel, the nature of the supervision and control exercised by the Managing Agent, the review of operations and procedures maintained by our Loan Manager's staff and the Director of Examination and Audit, we believe the operations of the hotel are properly controlled, and that our present knowledge of these operations is sufficient for us to make the necessary decisions. 

It is doubtful if a comprehensive site audit at present would produce sufficient additional knowledge of the operations, to warrant the cost of such an audit.

Since, at the time of our audit of FNMA, no reports had been prepared by FNMA employees on their reviews of the mortgagor's operations and procedures, we are unable to evaluate the adequacy of these reviews. Further, our review of the memorandum containing the comments of the hotel auditor, referred to above, indicates that the auditor is performing primarily a bookkeeping function and not an independent audit function.

Recommendation to the President of FNMA 

FNMA should evaluate the reviews it has made of the mortgagor's operations and procedures to determine whether they were adequate to protect the interests of FNMA. If they were inadequate, we ecommend that the director of examination and audit be instructed o review the operations and to audit the records of Dunbar House, nc. 

Magnolia Springs Apartments Inc., Green Cove Springs, Florida 

FNMA loan No. 09-83588)

The unpaid principal balance of the FHA-insured mortgage on his project was $2,549,169 on March 1, 1956. Commencing on that ate, principal payments were deferred for four 6-month periods, or For a total of 24 months. During this time the mortgagor was reuired to submit reports of income and expenses and to remit any xcess of cash receipts over disbursements to FNMA each month. 

On completion of our field work at the FNMA Atlanta office in eptember 1957, FNMA auditors had reviewed some of the monthly ports submitted by the mortgagor but had not made a site audit of he mortgagor's records and operations. We informed the Atlanta rency manager that FNMA had need for a site audit of the mort-

gagor in order to determine the accuracy and validity of reported income and expenses. In a memorandum to us dated September 23, 1957, the Atlanta agency manager stated that:

There is no provision made for FNMA to verify the operating statements. 

While this may be considered advantageous under certain circumstances, the expense must be considered and if such expense were chargeable to the mortgagor it would serve only to reduce the net income from the properties. 

On October 18, 1957, however, he requested the chief of examination and audit in Atlanta to examine the mortgagor's records. On December 27, 1957, in a report to the Atlanta agency manager concerning the examination of the mortgagor's records, the chief of examination and audit commented, in part, that:

During the past fiscal year ended 10-31-57, the mortgagor, through its public accountant, has followed a policy of preparing statements for submission to FNMA which are neither strictly on a cash or accrual basis, but a combination of both, with many other inconsistencies, which in the light of examination makes these statements worthless for any accurate determination of liability by the mortgagor under the modification agreement. 

Recommendation to the President of FNMA 

We believe that, when a mortgagor repeatedly requests deferment of mortgage principal payments, FNMA should determine the ac curacy and validity of monthly reports previously submitted to FNMA by the mortgagor. We recommend, therefore, that FNMA require multifamily project mortgagors to submit, on expiration of each deferment, a statement of cash receipts and disbursements for the preceding deferment period properly certified by an independent public ac countant. These certified statements would, in our opinion, alleviate the need for audits by FNMA.

In a letter to us dated April 15, 1958, the President of FNMA stated that:

We agree with this recommendation and shall revise our procedures to require it.

On April 18, 1958, by letter, the President of FNMA instructed each agency office to carry out this recommendation.
