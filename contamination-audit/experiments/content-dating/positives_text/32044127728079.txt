THE CENTRAL PEAK 

ONE's religion may be called a boat then his ideas and beliefs about 

God are its rudder.

I& 

Are we not all sailing on what we call the sea of life? The shores to be reached are those of heaven and hell. 

Any man or woman, boy or girl, can steer his boat or ship on this sea of life toward his choice of these two shores. 

And his rudder is his ideas and beliefs about God. 

Pause one moment now for reflection.

Pluck here from the tree of implied truth a certain trend of thought worthy to be the apple of one's eye:—

The rudder in hand implies thrilling privilege. The privilege in turn implies sobering responsibility. The responsibility implies the alternatives of loyalty and disloyalty to itself. Loyalty implies prize, disloyalty penalty. 

All these implications are self-evident.

The ideas and beliefs that any man has about God are the most important of all the thoughts that his mind contains. This is so true that it is true without reservations or qualifications. 

One's ideas and beliefs about God may be so false as to make the man his own worst enemy. They may be so true as to make the man his own best friend.

No church creed is truer than the truth of what it both says and implies about the nature of God.

True ideas and beliefs about God, to the extent of their dominance in the religious thinking of any man, or in the theological and doctrinal teachings of any church denomination, or in the faiths of any of the pagan religions of the earth, cause them to be luminous with spiritual truth.

The reverse of this is also true: false ideas and beliefs about God, to the extent of their dominance in the religious thinking of any man, or in the theological and doctrinal teachings of any church denomination, or in the faiths of any of the pagan religions of the earth, cause them to be beclouded, more or less, even to the blackness of total spiritual blindness or to the destructive negations of atheism. 

At Best All Ideas of Men Are Limited 

All true ideas and beliefs about God that any of us may have or yet

acquire, whether as boys and girls, or as men and women, are true only in a limited way. They will never be complete or perfect. They will always be growing and improving. This is because we humans are finite whereas God is infinite.

The permanency of knowledge and belief does not necessarily imply changes that are reversals of beliefs. They may and should be only changes of expansion. For example: is it not self-evident that any knowledge which changes except to expand was not knowledge in the first instance? 

Anybody who can think can see for himself that a limited man cannot and never can comprehend all that constitutes the unlimited. 

This implies the self-evident fact that no man can comprehend who or what God is as He exists in Himself. And yet!-any man, and even any child of thinking age, can have ideas and beliefs about God that are true in a limited way.

To be true in a limited way means to be essentially true. This is the same as being true as relating to one's eternal happiness and noblest usefulness.

If one's ideas and beliefs about God are essentially true, true in a limited way, they are then qualified to function as the rudder by which one may direct his course to the paradisiacal landings. 

True ideas and beliefs of this kind about God may even carry varying quantities of alloys of what is untrue. These alloys gradually disappear, however, in the course of one's regeneration—in the course of the ship's progress according to compass. 2

GOD'S REVELATION OF HIMSELF 

LL true ideas and beliefs about God, limited and incomplete as they Amay be, are given us by God Himself by means of ampevelation of Himself.

We call this Revelation the Written Word of God. 

The Written Word of God constitutes the greater part of what we call the Bible. Several of the books of the Bible are not portions of that "Word which was in the beginning with God, and was God, and became flesh and dwelt among us."-(John 1.)

This fact will be explained in 24. 

The greater part of the Bible, however, does actually constitute the veritable Word of God.

The Word of God contains within its unique literature-within its literal or natural sense-the revelation of all that both men and angels can now or ever know and believe truly about God. 

Of course this truth is inconceivable to those who know nothing of the essential difference between God's Word and all other books. This essential difference will be explained as we proceed. 

God's written Word is again unique in the realm of books in that It alone performs the miracle of imparting to every human being, by either direct or reflected light or both, such ideas and beliefs about God as his mind is able to receive to its eternal betterment-and to impart to him no more and no other than such ideas and beliefs. 

This miracle will gradually come to light as we proceed. 

The Word in Literature 

"The Word that was in the beginning with God and was God and became flesh," as stated in the first chapter of John's Gospel, also became literature. 

The Word not only became flesh in the Person of Jesus but it also became literature in the written Word of God. 

The very term word implies truth, either spoken, written, or exemplified, or all three.

Thus, the Word-meaning Divine Truth as It exists in God and is God -is related to Its literal sense, or, to Its assumed language of men sense, somewhat as It was to the assumed body of flesh of Jesus. 

This is why Jesus could truthfully say that He had come to fulfill the 3

Law and the Prophets-even to "every jot and tittle." His words follow. "Think not that I am come to destroy the Law and the Prophets: I am not come to destroy, but to fulfill. For verily I say unto you, till heaven and earth pass, one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law, till all be fulfilled.”—(Matt. 5:17, 18.)

And this is why He explained, on the very day of His resurrection, to the two disciples on way to Emmaus, that Moses, and the prophets and all the Scriptures dealt with things concerning Himself. "On the first day of the week," following the crucifixion and resurrection, "two of the disciples were on the way to a village called Emmaus. 

And it came to pass that Jesus Himself drew near, and went with them. 

But their eyes were holden that they should not know Him."—(Luke 

24:13). In response to their conversation concerning the news of the resurrection He said unto them, “O fools, and slow of heart to believe all that the prophets have spoken: ought not Christ to have suffered these things, and to enter into His glory? and beginning at Moses and all the prophets, He expounded unto them in all the Scriptures the things concerning Himself.”—(Luke 24:27)

An Analogy in Nature The relations of electricity to a battery, or, of high voltage to the mechanisms for reducing it to low voltage are imperfect materialistic analogies giving some illustration of the relations of Divine Truth to either the natural wording of the written Word, or, to the finite and imperfect inheritance taken on in the Person of Jesus from the virgin mother.

Hence, the Divine Truth sense of the Word is related to Its literature sense to Its language of men sense-somewhat as a man's soul is related to its physical body.

A real analogy on the plane of nature is the relation of water to the well that contains it. This is illustrated by the spiritual implications of the remarks of Jesus to the woman of Samaria at Jacob's well. 

The well itself corresponds to the worldly terminology of the enclosing or surrounding literal sense of the Word. The water in the well corresponds to the spiritual content of Truth within the Word. 

What else could be implied by “living water which, when drunk, becomes in one a well of water springing up into everlasting life"?

This well of water is in the one who drinks it in the same sense, and as really, as that the kingdom of heaven is within one. "The Kingdom of heaven is within you."-Jesus. "My Kingdom is not of this world."-Jesus.

DIVINE TRUTH IS ONE WORD 

IVINE Truth, meaning "the Word that was with God and was God," Dwhether whether It be God in God (as It exists in Itself); or, God in the assumed body of the man Jesus; or, God in the assumed language of man as it forms the written Word, is exactly the same Thing because It is one Thing.

God in God, and God in Jesus, and God in the Word do not subdivide into three Gods. They are one God in three situations or aspects. 

Divine Truth existing as God in God is above our finite or limited comprehension, as already pointed out. But, as It exists both as God in Jesus, and as God in the literal sense of the written Word, It is such that we can acquire from It true ideas and beliefs about It. 

Hence, our quest in making the acquaintance of Jesus, or, in reading the Word, is primarily to procure true ideas and beliefs about God. All other good things flow from these. 

Any and all true ideas and beliefs about Jesus Christ are true ideas and beliefs about God.

And the one original Fountain of true information about Jesus Christ is the written Word. 

Hence it is that the written Word, in giving us true ideas and beliefs about God, does it by the method of giving us true ideas and beliefs about Jesus Christ.

This is just simply because all that anybody can truly know and believe about God is what he comes to truly know and believe about Jesus Christ.

And this applies to angels as well as to men. This will be explained later.

There are many mistaken and false ideas and beliefs about Jesus Christ. 

To that extent mistaken and false ideas about God prevail.

One of the Simplest of all True Ideas About God 

One of the truest, simplest and most fundamental of all ideas about God imparted by the written Word is one that even a child can both see and believe. Jesus is described in the Word as a Man. Hence God is a Man.

The question of what KIND of a Man Jesus was leads on to and merges

into a never ending succession and accumulation of true ideas and beliefs that are expansive in their nature. 

Any child, or any adult in his or her spiritual childhood, who wants to be as sure as possible that he is steering with his rudder in the right direction, should begin with the idea and belief that God is a Man in the very human form of Jesus Christ.

The simple true idea of God as the Lord Jesus, or, of God as the very Person that Jesus was, is a seed idea. 

And a more marvelous seed does not exist because when it finds lodgement in a receptive mind it soon begins to sprout. It then grows into the tree of life described in Genesis, the first Book of the Word, as existing in the Garden of Eden, and, in the Apocalypse, the last Book of the Word, as existing in the New Jerusalem. 

The Personality of God, which mystifies many, may be seen to be as simple and as true as that God is a Man. One needs only to recall that the Word everywhere implies the Personality of Jesus. The Personality of Jesus is the Personality of God.

But bear in mind that the simple true idea of the Personality of God is as expansive as is the seed idea of God as a Man.

HE WRITTEN Word has one purpose supreme over all others.

THE R 

Before stating this supreme purpose its clearness will be enhanced if one thing in particular is pointed out which is not Its supreme purpose. 

It is not the supreme purpose of God's Word to substitute for college and university professors in teaching the natural sciences and the natural philosophies and secular human history. 

The supreme purpose of the written Word is to impart or teach true ideas and beliefs about God. 

This one supreme purpose has many ends in view. Indeed ends that are innumerable. Several of the chief of these will be spoken of later. 

To repeat, and with some amplification, the supreme purpose of the written Word is to impart to boys and girls, and to men and women, and to all angels of the universal heavens, such true ideas and beliefs about God as their minds respectively are able to receive in freedom of thought and with rational understanding. 

Yes, as just stated, the written Word of God exists with the angels of heaven as really as with men of this world.

If the Word of Truth which was with God and is God is the interior Content of our written Word, it stands to reason that It must likewise be the interior Content of the written Word in heaven. This is because there exists but One Infinite and Divine Truth. And this is because there is but one God.

Angels, indeed, may be qualified to receive from the Word a thousand true ideas and beliefs about God to our one. But this is due to the start they have over us in the acquirement of superior abilities for receiving them. Divine Truth, however, is the one Fountain of truth for both men and angels.

The only essential difference between the Word in heaven and the Word on earth is that they are written in two different languages. The 

Truth in each one is the same identical Truth. 

The essential difference between the language in which the Word in heaven is written and the Word on earth is written will be both described and explained further on.

Word, they would look into the letter of Its meanings in the light of the radiating halo of spiritual truth that crowns the head of Its Infant Occupant. 

By continuing this lead in Scriptural study it would soon be discovered that the spiritual halo from the Babe in the manger gradually increases and becomes as the veritable light from the sun of heaven enlightening the rational understanding in an ever-expanding knowledge of God. 

Such is the function of the Word. 

Yes, there is a veritable sun in heaven as described in 21. How it differs from ours is also told. 

The conceptions of the interior grandeur and luminosity of what lies concealed within the exterior literature aspect of the Word of God, as these conceptions are now held by most christians, are pathetically inadequate for the dire needs of our Christian civilization in this hour of crisis and transition.

The Christian Church is now agonizing for an adequate theology. An adequate theology will be the spiritual body of its resurrection. This adequate theology may now be found by sighting the halo rays from Jesus.

The Nature of the Growth of Jesus 

Superficial thinking will immediately raise the question at this point, Does God grow? We speak of the growth of Jesus from infancy to His final glorification. And we speak of Jesus as God. 

Does this not imply the growth of God?

The fact is that some finite things attaching to Jesus and to which He was closely related did grow as will now be shown. 

The growth of these finite things gave an appearance, according to our

natural way of looking at things, that Jesus grew. But the interior continuous conscious Soul of Jesus did not grow. This in Him was Infinite and Eternal.

As intimated above, it was "things with Jesus" that changed because He never did actually change and for the reason that God changeth not. 

The things with Him that gave the appearance of His growth from birth to resurrection were the finite things taken on by the virgin birth. 

There was a growth of the finite things which attached to Jesus, but did not as things in themselves constitute Jesus, and the growth of these things is what we call the growth of Jesus because that is the way it appears.

We must necessarily speak according to appearances until we know enough to distinguish appearances from genuine truth. This may be illustrated by the one time necessity for speaking of the sunrise which never did rise.

God as Jesus, or Jesus as God, for both expressions mean one thing, had taken on or attached to Himself some things that were finite and imperfectly human.

These had been "assumed" from the virgin mother. 

These finite attachments, as things in themselves, were not He. 

They were the very quintessence, and the all in epitome, of the evils that afflict the human race. But these evils did not cause Jesus to be evil. 

The presence of these assumed evils attaching in this way to the Person of Jesus is what caused "Him to be tempted in all things as we are."

These assumed evils, however, and finite things were to have but a temporary stay or abode with Him.

This is because they in themselves were no part of what constitutes the eternally continuing consciousness of God.

Hence, the changes, which constituted the true appearances of growth in the Person of Jesus, consisted chiefly of the gradual elimination of the evils and finite things inherited or "assumed" from the virgin birth. 

The elimination of evils was accomplished chiefly by means of temptation combats admitted into Himself, and hence experienced by Him, in which temptations, which were far more numerous and indescribably severer than those experienced by us, He was always completely victorious. 

The agony of the cross marked the finality of His temptation combat victories.

His victories in these temptation combats were of such a nature that they reduced the hells to order and brought them under control. 

Thereby the perfect equilibrium was forever preserved by which all men would be enabled to choose in freedom according to reason between spiritual life and death.

Thus He was our Saviour-the Redeemer of the World.

IN RELIEF OF DOUBT MANUEL SWEDENBORG says many things about heaven and hell. 

E How can we know that he is not an impostor?

And how can we know that he, even if intentionally truthful, was not self-deceived?

There is a way to know. 

These two questions are superlatively pertinent. 

This is because impostors have been the cause of a continuous tragedy through the ages which is accountable for untold millions of spiritual casualties. 

The Answer 

During some years after I had begun reading Swedenborg, the thought, like a persisting and disturbing visitor, would appear and raise the question: How can I be sure that what Swedenborg says is true?

The hopes inspired by his writings for the continuance of life after death, with its promises of never-ending activities of unselfish usefulness with indescribable happiness, were thrilling. I wanted to believe them. 

Everything said in his writings seemed plausible and reasonable. 

And I did believe.

However, my believing was with an ever present cloudy element of doubt more or less disturbing.

I had not yet found the criterion for complete and undoubting proof, such, for example, as I have for my knowledge of my own personal consciousness-or, as the chemist has for his belief that a million years ago, and that a million years hence, chemical relations between material substances did and will exist. 

These are examples of a type of belief that is based on a criterion that dissipates all doubt.

On one occasion I was reading Swedenborg's interpretation of some passage from the Word according to its inner or angelic sense—the sense which, as shown in both preceding and succeeding numbers, brings to the sight of one's rational understanding the divinity of the Lord's Humanity.

The genuine truth within the passage, as shown by his interpretation of it, was not indicated in the slightest way by its literal interpretation. But so sublime was the truth and so clearly did I see it that I was both surprised and thrilled. 12

It was a passage from the Apocalypse Revealed as I now remember. 

I paused in my reading. I wanted to dwell on the significance and the implications of this unusual and striking experience. 

I made mental note the second time, and with more deliberation, of this pregnant fact, namely, that I had actually seen a genuine heavenly truth with the eyes of my rational understanding as clearly and as certainly as I ever saw a worldly object with my natural eyes. 

Then I gave approval to the thought that even though the rest of mankind should remain blind to it, I could never be able not to believe it, because, with the eyes of my rational understanding, I had seen it as a veritable spiritual truth.

Since that time I have seen hundreds of such genuine spiritual truths brought into clearness of rational understanding by Swedenborg's method of interpreting the Scriptures. 

Later, when my unwelcome visitor returned once more to raise the doubt as to the certainty of Swedenborg's veracity in what he says about heaven and hell, it was given me to think of the correct answer. 

The idea suddenly dawned on me that these hundreds of truths, from the interior or spiritual sense of the Word, which had been made as visible to the eyes of my rational understanding as objects are to eyesight by Swedenborg's interpretations of them—that these attest, with the certainty of Divine Revelation Itself, to the truth of what Swedenborg says about heaven and hell.

This is necessarily so for several reasons: 

First, these stars of spiritual truth from the interior sense of the Word are truths about which no doubt can exist with those who have seen them. At the age of twelve I saw Pikes Peak. Since then I have never had a doubt of its existence.

Second, they never could have been seen except by means such as were provided by the Lord through Swedenborg, and these means proceed by a certain method of Scriptural interpretation by which the divinity of the Lord's Humanity is brought to the clear vision of a man's rational understanding.

Hence this type of belief is based, not on hearsay or authority, but on actual spiritual sight.

Third, these means and methods constitute a key for unlocking the Word, thereby opening the doors of its literal sense into its divine and infinite interiors wherein the Word that was with God and was 

God resides and radiates.

An indispensable part of this key are the things said by Swedenborg about heaven and hell. 13

Therefore, every truth brought to the light of one's rational understanding by means of this key attests to the unquestionable character of the things that make the key.

The arbitrament of war is the finality of all arguments between nations as pertaining to certain ends. There is such a thing as a finality of the arguments of reason for determining complete and perfect proof. And it is as determining as is the finality of the arbitrament of war. 

Such finality for perfect proof of Swedenborg's veracity as to what he says about heaven and hell is the implied sponsorship of it by the entire interior truths of the Word of God. 

The foregoing implies, of course, that no one can be capable of availing himself of the full value of this proof before he himself has experienced personally the vision of at least some of the distinctively spiritual truths within the natural sense of the written Word. 14

SELF EXISTENT REALITY 

ELF Existent Reality was never created.

SELE 

It is One Thing. It is the Infinite Thing. 

Innumerable, yes, infinite things exist from Self Existent Reality. 

Whatever the Self Existent Reality may be, It is the I AM, bespoken by Jehovah to Moses at the burning bush that was not consumed. It always was and is and always will be. 

It never came to be. It will never cease to be. It is. It is the I AM. 

It would naturally seem that Nothing instead of Something exists. 

One of the greatest of all surprises is to find oneself alive. 

Indeed the most surprising thing of all things to me has been my perfectly certain knowledge of my own personal consciousness which declares, with evidence that I cannot doubt, that reality and not nothingness exists.

The knowledge of this fact has, in times of quiet meditation, overwhelmed me with an amazement so intense as to hurt.

How wonderful that an Original Something should exist from which something not original could be derived!

All things other than the Self Existent Reality have been somehow brought forth or created by It from Itself. 

Hence all other things are derivations from the Self Existent. All derivations are things other than the Self Existent. 

The difference between what is Self Existent and what is derivative is so distinct and manifest that nearly anybody, it would seem, should be able to see it.

The importance of seeing this difference is a primal matter in any advanced thinking in religion.

The difference is that between the Creator and the created. 

It helps one to see and comprehend the fundamental difference between a finite man and the infinite God. Thereby it gives expansion to simple true ideas and beliefs about God. 

A derivative is always a finite thing and exists in a state of dependence. 

Hence man is always dependent on God his Creator. He is dependent momentarily and perpetually for his preservation.

Preservation of this kind is continuous creation. 

Jesus as the Word that was with God and was God is shown to classify as Self Existent by many passages of Scripture as, for example, "As the 17

Father hath life in Himself so hath He given to the Son to have life in 

Himself." (John 5:26) "The Jews sought the more to kill Jesus because

He said God was His Father making Himself equal with God." (John 5:18).

A Simple True Philosophic Idea 

Whatever or Whoever constitutes Self Existent Reality, That It is which is God. This is a philosophic conception that, self evidently, is true. 

The mind unenlightened spiritually can grasp this idea as easily as one spiritually enlightened. 

This implies that persons who rationally confirm themselves in the belief that the material universe, with its appearances of infinite space, infinite distances, infinite time, infinite number of stars perhaps, and even its infinitessimal things within the atom, and the like, constitutes Self Existent Reality, are persons believing that nature is God. 

Such is the central idea of pantheism. 

It happens that Self Existent Reality has taken the initiative for our enlightenment on this subject by providing for us a Revelation of all that we are now or ever will be capable of knowing and believing truly about Itself.

One of the first and simplest and most fundamental of all the things It reveals about Itself by means of It's written Word is that “It” is not "it," but that "It" is a Man.

Self Existent Reality is not nature. It is not the material universe. 

It is a Man. 

It is also Man.

That God is Man is an expansion of the idea of God as a Man. 

But do not mistake. The idea of God as a Man never becomes an outgrown idea. It remains as an elemental and never changing part of the expanded idea of God as Man.

Expansion does not imply expelling that which is expanded. In the case here both are true ideas with one simply more expanded than the other. The lesser is a necessary part of the greater. 

Self Existent Reality as Man is God. 

Hence God is not inanimate matter. 

Man is Mind. 

Mind is Life. 

Which constitutes Self Existent Reality, Mind or matter? Life or death? "Jesus is the Life." This answers the question. 

The material universe (which in itself is dead) is not God. On the contrary it has been created by God-who is very Life Itself. 18

The material universe is a derivative. It is not Self Existent. 

The Lord Jesus Christ Who is God is the only Self Existent Reality. 

Can Reality Be Derived?

The Law of Cause and Effect 

This simple true idea of corespondences becomes also a simple true idea of the universal law of cause and effect. 

The bigness of the law of cause and effect may be sensed or glimpsed in the idea that the material universe, inclusive of the infinite things which constitute it, is but an effect from a spiritual cause. 

The relations of this material effect with its spiritual cause is that of spiritual correspondences.

The manner in which any law functions is more or less affected by the conditions under which it acts. Gravitation, for instance, under certain conditions even helps an airplane in its upward flight. It helps a submerged cork to rise to the top of the water.

SOMETIM A HELP TO BELIEF 

OMETIMES the mental status of a man is such that agnosticism is inevitable from sheer inability to see with rational clearness the mere possibility, to say nothing of the probability or actuality, of the truth of some religious verity.

For example: The idea of the Divine Providence implies that Providence is actually causing everything that happens in the world to contribute to the ultimate best happiness of the righteous, and to the ultimate least or mildest suffering possible of the wicked, and, hence to that ultimate best good that is possible in the nature of things of both the righteous and the wicked.

This claim is deeply involved in complexities of detail. Therefore as a help to seeing the possibility of such a sublime reality the following illustration is presented: 

People these days are familiar with automatic mechanism. An automatic machine is composed of many parts. If one of them should be taken. apart and all the parts should be scattered around as so many units of metal, wood, rubber, chemicals and the like; and then, if these separated parts should be observed and studied by an unenlightened person, what would be the outcome of his studies? The information gained would be both meagre and superficial.

Now, one of the big generalities of christian truth is that the material universe, in the hands of Providence is something like what an automatic machine is in the hands of a man.

Therefore, when men learn furthermore from Divine Revelation what the Lord's chief end or purpose was in designing, creating, and preserving the material universe, then, in studying the innumerable "parts," or the things of the universe, they study them in their relations to this end or purpose.

Now, as regarding the Lord's chief end or purpose in designing, creating and preserving the material universe, we find it to be that of providing Himself with necessary means and instrumental agency for creating and preserving the human race; and, for contributing thereafter continuously and unendingly toward such ultimate best good of every human being as may be potentially possible in the nature of things. 

No other meaning can attach to the Lord's infinite Love. The Lord invariably employs adequate means and methods and thereby operates in 60

accordance with Divine law and order. Nothing of the nature of whim or caprice attaches to the nature of the Lord. 

Let us continue the illustration. Envisage a man of average normal intelligence living in some remote and isolated region of the earth in complete ignorance of the value of power transportation and of the existence of the automobile. Suppose he should find an automobile stalled on his premises. Naturally he would be surprised. He would look at it. He would study it. In studying it he would naturally look for its values to him. To discover values is the inspiration even in the study of science. In his studies of this automobile he would make efforts to take it apart. For some of its parts he would find meagre uses. But to the most of its parts no discoverable values would attach. 

Can you not see that this man's predicament, in his task of finding and utilizing the values in that automobile, is the predicament of the scientist in his task of learning the essential truths about material phenomena or appearances? It is likewise the predicament of the social reformer in his studies of the essential values that may attach to proposed theories of reform.

But suppose now that some one should come along who could tell this man things about the automobile from the point of view of its chief end or purpose? He would explain that the car as one unit or as a whole, served the marvelous purpose of rapid and power transportation. Furthermore, in order that the car as a whole should serve this end it was necessary that every one of its thousand parts should serve the same end; and, not only so, but that the highest value attaching to any one of the parts came into existence from its qualification to co-operate with the others in serving this same end.

Think of it! A mechanism composed of a thousand parts, each of which functions in its own way, and which, by so doing enjoys its own individual exercise or sport, and which also operates automatically in such a way as to serve, with a hundred per cent efficiency, the same identical end being served by every other part, which end is rapid and power transportation, subject to the will and intelligence of the one man in control! 

Questioning thoughts would linger. How could it be that a thousand individual parts, all differing one from another and each playing its own game, having its own fun, doing its work, acting at all times according to its distinctive nature, enjoying unrestricted and full self-expression, be overruled in such manner and so secretly controlled as to serve with automatic precision and certitude and with a hundred per cent efficiency the same purpose of giving to man rapid and power transportation? The answer is that the designer or inventor of the automaton had rapid and 61

power transportation in mind as the end in view or chief objective, and that, therefore, in designing and building it he provided from the beginning that every part would at all times and under all circumstances (practically speaking) co-operate automatically with all the other parts to the common end. And, to repeat, this common end or chief purpose of God in creating the material universe is to provide Himself with instrumental means for creating the human race and then for making all things work toward the ultimate best possible good for every man. 

Does not this illustration make clear, then, the possibility, at least, that even the hairs of one's head are numbered, and that not even a sparrow falls without Providential observance, and that the Will and the Wisdom of God are operating in even the minutest of realities as really as in the larger things? Not a screw or a wire in the automobile but serves the common end. The inter-relationships of all things, including the smallest as well as the largest of things, due to God's original design for them, work perfectly co-operatively and as it were automatically, toward the success of the Lord's chief end or purpose. 62

The Difference Between Knowledge and Belief 

Most people mistake knowledge for belief. These are not synonymous terms. They are two very different things. 

All sense impressions of earliest childhood somehow register, in the memory then forming, as facts of knowledge. They nourish the child mind somewhat as milk nourishes the body. Before any belief comes into existence innumerable sense impressions must have been fed into the child's mind from which it has grown. 

Beliefs do not and cannot appear before rationality begins to take form. 

But knowledges do.

This is because a belief is the outcome of such use as one's rationality has made of his knowledges. 

It should also be noted here that at the age when spiritual freedom takes form rationality acts in conjunction with spiritual freedom in the formation of opinions and beliefs.

The acquirement of knowledge is, of course, continuous with life itself, but the beliefs formed from them by rationality in spiritual freedom are distinctive. 69

Due to the part taken by spirtual freedom the rational belief may be either essentially true or essentially untrue. 

All animals have knowledges but no animal has ideas or beliefs. 

True rational beliefs occupy a far higher position in any human mentality or intelligence than do memory knowledges of facts. 

This is because simple facts not assimilated by rationality are, as things in themselves, nothing other than inexplicable phenomena. 

They are made explicable by rationality. Knowledges with adults are but as food for rationality in spiritual freedom in the formation of opinions, judgments, ideas, beliefs and the like. These, always, may be either essentially true or essentially untrue and yet rational. 

Several examples of both knowledge and belief will indicate the essential difference between the two.

I know of my own consciousness. I believe in the personal consciousness of other people. To repeat, I know of my own consciousness; but I do not know, I only believe, that another person has consciousness. 

This belief is arrived at by a process of reasoning. That another man has consciousness is a rational conclusion which is a belief. This one belief is the flowering of many knowledges coordinated by reason. 

This belief represents a type of beliefs which cannot be doubted more than knowledges can.

Now, spiritually speaking, I know of my personal experiences. I know them as both good and evil. I know what the Word says to me about them. From such things beliefs begin. I believe in both heaven and hell. 

I believe in my own immortality. 

Another example. A chemist does not know, he can only believe, that a million years ago oxygen and hydrogen made water. 

This belief of the chemist about the permanency of chemical relations is as indubitable as any knowledge. 

Again, spiritually speaking, I know of my own conscience. I also know of the conflict between my good and evil because of my conscience. Beliefs take form from these about regeneration of character and about God and the purpose for which I was made. 

One belief may require the rational support of a thousand or more knowledges.

A belief is an organism of rationally correlated facts of knowledge. 

Any true christian belief, therefore, as distinguished from a parrot's talk about its belief, has a background of growth and must arise from and rest upon a grouping of relevant facts of knowledge that is adequate. 

It can never be an instantaneous creation of persuasion, enthusiasm or excitement independently of a background of adequate preparation. The 70

substance of belief which is knowledge must precede any belief that is to be more rational and enduring than parrot's talk.

Third, an inward urge or purpose to find and believe, in freedom, the truth, rather than untruth, with intentions to do the truth, whatever the requirements may be in terms of such personal sacrifices as may be necessary.

Any one having gained new information of a satisfactory sort from what precedes in this booklet will naturally be interested in knowing some things about Swedenborg and his writings. Space puts strict limitations on the presentation of such information. A few general facts, however, with directions as to how such information may be easily obtained would seem to be in order.

Swedenborg, at the age of 56, after having become one of the most, if not the most, distinguished of all scientists up to his time, began an intensive study of the written Word of God. 

He had been ennobled by the Queen of Sweden and made a life member of the Swedish Diet which membership he continued to retain to his death at the age of 84.

His intensive study of the Word was begun with a mind as well disciplined by the methods and exactions of science as any human mind could well be. Hence his attitude toward the spiritual investigations he was starting upon was exactly opposite to that of the dogmatist. His attitude was that of finding the truth for truth's sake, believing, with true science, in the general principle that truth leads only to new values whereas untruth leads only to loss.

During the last 28 years of his life, between the ages of 56 and 84, Swedenborg wrote thirty large volumes, all of which deal with Divine Revelations from within the written Word of God. 

These thirty volumes do, indeed, deal so effectively with the obscuring 73

clouds of the literal sense of the Word, by way of modifying and dispersing them, that heaven's sunlight, direct from the Lord's Divine

Humanity which is encompassed by the sun of heaven, as already shown, reveals Jesus Christ in His power and great glory. It is by means of these direct rays from Himself as the Sun of heaven, shining upon and through the clouds of the literal sense of His written Word, that He has already made and is now making His second coming. 

This opening of the interior sense of the Word, enabling the Lord's Divinity to be seen and rationally comprehended, constitutes the second coming. This second coming is to be a permanent stay. He is not to depart as He did before. The increase of His kingdom on earth as likewise in heaven is insured by the operations of His Divine Love and Divine Wisdom in the personal lives of those who will be His agencies in the world for both shunning evils and doing good. 

The Arcana Coelestia, his most voluminous book, is in 12 volumes by one publisher and in 19 volumes by another. This book gives the spiritual interpretation, according to the language of spiritual correspondences, described in a preceding number, of Genesis and Exodus. It gives the spiritual meaning of these two Books of the Word, sentence by sentence, verse by verse, chapter by chapter, from the beginning of Genesis to the ending of Exodus. Together with these interpretations are hundreds of quotations from other Books of the Word, which, too, are either interpreted or used for confirmation of interpretations. 

The Apocalypse Revealed, in 2 volumes, and, The Apocalypse Explained, in 6 volumes, are two books devoted to the interior or spiritual interpretation of every sentence and verse of the Apocalypse or Book of Revelation. The Apocalypse Revealed was written after The Apocalypse 

Explained, and, presumably, as a more condensed treatment of the subject. Both seem to be equally luminous. 

Heaven and Its Wonders and Hell is the most widely read and most popular of all his works. In this book is given a vast fund of information about the conditions of life hereafter which is indispensable to a knowledge of the language of spiritual correspondences by means of which alone can the spiritual sense of the Word be studied with any appreciable degree of known accuracy.

The Divine Love and The Divine Wisdom is the book which, more perhaps than all his others combined, has given him his reputation as a spiritual philosopher. Its style is spiritually philosophic. His other books stand primarily for spiritual seership. 

Divine Providence is second as to circulation and popularity. It shows in 382 pages of profound and luminous writing that Providence is the 74

government of the Divine Love and the Divine Wisdom of the Lord. 

The Four Doctrines divides between the doctrine of the Lord, the doctrine of the Sacred Scriptures, the doctrine of Faith and the doctrine of 

Charity. It is a book of 321 pages. 

Conjugial Love, (published also under the title Marriage Love), shows, in a masterly and profound manner, and, strictly from the Scriptural or spiritual point of view, the origin of sex, the continuance of the sexes as they exist in both heaven and hell, and, more than all else, how one's conditions of life hereafter are to be affected for either good or bad by one's attitude toward and conduct in matters of sex while in this world.

It is undoubtedly the profoundest and sanest book ever written on this general subject. He was 81 years of age when he wrote this book. 

The True Christian Religion, 1098 pages, in two volumes, was his final work written at the age of 83, and is, in a way, a summary with additions of all his other books.
