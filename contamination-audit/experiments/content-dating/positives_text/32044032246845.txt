6/22/55 FO ORD The many favorable reports emanating from the so-called "coordinating councils" of California and the favorable attention which this development in community organization has attracted throughout the country prompted the National Probation Association to send its field director, Mr. Francis H. 

Hiller, to California in September 1935 to make a brief study of this development. The report herewith briefly summarizes his observations. It is not intended as a detailed or critical report, the study being brief and preliminary in character. We are continuing the study and are securing more information as to methods and results obtained by these and other similar councils working for the coordination of agencies in the field of prevention and treatment of juvenile delinquency. 

In order to secure information as to promising developments in this field from all parts of the country, the Association has employed Mr. Kenneth S. Beam, organizer and director of the coordinating councils of Los Angeles County, for a period of six months beginning December 1, 1935. Through the cooperation of the Los Angeles County Probation Department, Mr. Beam has been given a leave of absence from his work in Los 

Angeles As a result of the work which he has undertaken we hope to prepare more complete reports and to be in a position to offer advisory service to communities seeking to establish or perfect this promising communi ty development. 

December 28, 1935

CHARLES L. CHUTE Executive Director National Probation Association

A month spent in visiting the coordinating councils of Los Angeles County, San Francisco, Berkeley and Oakland, attending meetings, interviewing leaders, conferring with participating organization members and reading reports, is an interesting and instructive experience. Everywhere 

I went I found almost unanimous approval of the council movement, although, of course, there were differences of opinion as to the details of organization. Different forms of organization were found in various communities and, although in each community the sponsors of the movement are confident that they have the best plan yet, it is everywhere insisted upon that none of them should be slavishly copied. It is felt that the councils are still in an exploratory stage and that they will continue to evolve as to form and method, although probably not as to fundamental purpose、. 2 A coordinating council may be described as an association of public and private welfare agencies and civic bodies to coordinate their work, to discover and estimate more accurately than heretofore the demoralizing influences in the ir community and neighborhood, and to plan and act jointly for the betterment of conditions, most frequently with special reference to the prevention of juvenile delinquency.
