THE GARNISHEE LAW OF INDIANA, 1937 The General Assembly of 1937, by the amendment of several statutes, has provided a method of levying executions against income or profits and enforcing the same. This procedure is applicable only to judgments rendered after July 1, 1937. While referred to as the 

Garnishee Law, the proceeding is really a proceeding supplementary to execution and should be so denominated. 

The method, in general, adopted by the general assembly was, first, to amend the exemption statute to provide that incomes or profits shall be exempt only to the extent of fifteen dollars per week. 

Where the income or profits exceeds that amount per week, then ninety per cent of the income or profits remains exempt and ten per cent becomes subject to execution. 

The provisions of the Civil Code, were next amended giving jurisdiction to justices of the peace and providing certain changes in the procedure.

THE EXEMPTION STATUTE 

This statute as amended is chapter 296, Acts of 1937, page 1348, and will appear as Burns' Annotated Statutes 1933, § 2-3501. It reads as follows: 

From and after the taking effect of this act, the following property of any resident householder of the state of Indiana shall not be liable for levy or sale on execution or any other final process from a court, for any debt growing out of or founded upon a contract express or implied: (a) Real estate, or estates or rights therein or thereto of the value of not more than seven hundred dollars. (b) Tangible personal property to the value of six hundred dollars. (c) Intangible personal property, including choses in action, profits, debts owing, and income owing, to the value of fifteen dollars. (d) Income or profits of whatever nature of not more than fifteen dollars per week. If such income and profits shall exceed the sum of fifteen dollars per week, then ninety percentum of such income and profits in excess of said sum of fifteen dollars per week shall be exempt and ten percentum thereof shall be subject to execution; provided, however, That none

of the foregoing provisions of this act shall apply to any judgment obtained prior to July 1, 1937.

In no event shall the total of all exempted property exclusive of income or profits under the provisions of this act exceed in value the sum of one thousand dollars. 

Under this statute, the debtor is entitled to hold his wages not exceeding fifteen dollars per week exempt from execution. If the wages amount to more than fifteen dollars in any one week, then ten per cent of the amount over fifteen dollars may be applied, each week upon the judgment. Thus when the wage earned amounts to twenty dollars per week, ten per cent of five dollars may be taken weekly, and where the wage is thirty dollars per week, one dollar and a half may be applied weekly, but not more than ten per cent of the excess over fifteen dollars per week may be taken, no matter how many claimants there may be for the excess, as only that part subject to execution, may be applied upon the judgment. As most of the cases arising under this statute, with which justices of the peace will be concerned, will arise under subdivision (d), it is unnecessary to note what might appear as a contradiction between that subdivision and (c) next preceding. But this contradiction is undoubtedly more apparent than real, when it is considered that subdivision (d) undoubtedly was intended to apply particularly to cases where the earnings were upon a continuing basis. Wages and commissions computed upon a weekly basis, whether so paid or not, are squarely within the contemplation of subdivision (d) of this statute. 

THE PROCEDURE 

General outline of proceedings. There are three separate and entirely distinct proceedings available under the new act making these amendments to the Civil Code with reference to supplementary proceedings. 

1. To bring the defendant alone into court for examination only, after execution has been returned. 

2. To bring the defendant alone into court for examination concerning his property and for the application thereof upon an outstanding execution. 

3. To bring in other persons along with the defendant who are indebted, or will from time to time be indebted to defendant, for examination concerning such property and for the application thereof upon the judgment either while execution is outstanding or has been returned.

1. When examination only of judgment debtor is desired

Section 1 of chapter 84 of the Act of 1937, page 440, amends § 592 

of the Civil Code. It is Burns' Annotated Statutes, §2-4401, and, as amended, is as follows:

When an execution against the property of the judgment debtor or any of several debtors in the same judgment, issued to an officer authorized to serve the same at the place where such judgment debtor resides, or, if he do not reside in the state, to the officer of the court rendering such judgment who is authorized to serve such execution, is returned unsatisfied, in whole or in part, the judgment creditor, after such return is made, shall be entitled to an order, to be issued by any circuit, superior, municipal or city court or by any justice of the peace in the jurisdiction to which such execution issued, or by the clerk thereof in vacation, requiring the judgment debtor to appear forthwith before the court, if in session, or, if the court is not in session, then before the judge thereof, at a time and place to be specified therein, or on the first day of the next term of the court, to answer concerning his property or income or profits within the county to which the execution was issued; and a transcript of a judgment from a justice of the peace filed and docketed in the circuit court shall, for the purposes of this act, have all the effect of a judgment rendered originally in such court. 

This section provides only one remedy. The judgment debtor may be brought into court and examined under oath concerning his property, income or profits within the county to which execution was issued. When this examination has been obtained, the proceeding, under this statute, is at an end. No order may be made by the court except for the appearance of the judgment debtor and his examination under oath.

When judgment creditor entitled to examination. Under this particular section of the statutes, it is only where execution has issued and has been returned unsatisfied, in whole or in part, that the judgment debtor may be ordered to appear and submit to examination as to his property. No affidavit is required, but a complaint should be filed showing the facts necessary to justify the justice in issuing the order.

2. When examination only of judgment debtor and application and of his property upon execution is desired In general. This proceeding is to be instituted under section 2 of chapter 84, Acts 1937, Burns' Annotated Statutes 1933, § 2-4402. The purpose of this proceeding is to secure the application of property held by the judgment debtor to an outstanding execution. No third parties may be brought in under this provision of the statutes. This section is as follows: 

If, after the issuing of an execution against property, the execution plaintiff, or other person in his behalf, shall make and file an affidavit with the clerk of any court of record of any city, county or township to the effect that any judgment debtor, residing in the territorial jurisdiction of such court, has property or income or profits, describing the same, which he unjustly refuses to apply toward the satisfaction of the judgment, the court, if in session, or the judge or clerk thereof in vacation, shall issue a subpoena requiring the judgment debtor to appear forthwith before the court, if in session, or, if not, at the next term of the court, or before the judge thereof, at a time and place to be specified therein, to answer concerning the same; and such proceedings may, thereafter, be had for the application of the property or income or profits of the judgment debtor toward the satisfaction of the judgment as provided upon the return of an execution.

Procedure to obtain order and subsequent proceedings. In this instance an affidavit is required, which may be made either by the party or by any person in his behalf.

3. When examination of third persons is desired and for application of property in their hands belonging to judgment debtor Section 3 of chapter 84, Acts of 1937, page 440, furnishes the authority for this proceeding. This section is as follows: 

After the issuing or return of an execution against the property of the judgment debtor or any one of the several debtors in the same judgment, and upon an affidavit that any person, corporation, municipal or otherwise, the state or any subdivision or agency thereof has property of such judgment debtor, or is or will be from time to time indebted to him in any amount, although the amount shall be determined from time to time as it becomes due and payable, which, together with other property claimed by him as exempt from execution, shall exceed the amount of property so exempt by law, such person, corporation, or any member thereof, or the auditor of state or auditing officer of the municipal corporations, subdivisions or agencies of the state, may be required to appear and answer concerning the same, as above provided: Provided, That the governmental officers above named shall not be required to appear personally in court, but interrogatories shall be submitted by the parties and a copy thereof, with blanks for answer, and a copy of the order of the court ordering the same answered shall be transmitted to the officer required to answer the same by the clerk of the court, by registered mail, and on receipt of such interrogatories and such order, such officer shall answer such interrogatories and return the same to such clerk by registered mail, and such court shall have full power to compel answers thereto.¹ 

An entirely new feature of this section is the provision for reaching debts of the judgment debtor in the hands of state, county or municipal officers.2

They are not required to appear in person, as they might be called upon frequently to do so and in widely scattered portions of the state. Special provision is therefore made for the submission of interrogatories to them and for answer thereof by such officers. 

Under this provision, the State of Indiana may itself be sued as well as any subdivision thereof where it is indebted to the judgment debtor. 1 Burns 1933, § 2-4403.

Where state or other public officers are named as third parties' defendant. Personal appearance of the auditor of state, or the auditing officer of any municipal corporation or of any political subdivision of the state is specifically dispensed with by section 3 of chapter 84.12 

Where any of such officers is named as a third party defendant then the examination of such officer is to be taken by interrogatories.13 

The statute has been set out heretofore 14 and it will be noted that it provides that interrogatories "shall be submitted by the parties."

These must be submitted in duplicate and should be so drafted that a copy with spaces for answers can be transmitted to the officer. 

Quite clearly this statute contemplates the submission of interrogatories by both the judgment creditor and the judgment debtor, in order that the examination may conform as nearly as practicable to the method of oral examination. 

The plaintiff should prepare and file his interrogations with the complaint. The judgment debtor may then file his cross interrogatories in the same form.

An order should then be made upon the third party defendant to answer both sets, and a copy of this order with both sets of interrogatories should be sent by registered mail to the officer whose examination is required.

The court is given full power to require the answering of such interrogatories and to compel the return thereof by registered mail to the court.

The trial in such cases would then proceed in the usual manner, the interrogatories and the answers thereto being deemed to take the place of the oral examination of the third party defendant. 

In all such cases, it would be well that a certified copy of the judgment entry be sent, as soon as entered, to the governmental officer.

Cases to which proceedings are not applicable. Section 5 of chapter 

84, Acts of 1937, page 440, is as follows:

Except in such cases where the denial of this remedy would be a violation of the Constitution of the State of Indiana or the United States of America, no execution shall issue under this act upon any judgment upon a debt secured by mortgage upon real property, nor upon any judgment upon any contract of the sale of personal property unless the title to the said property passes at the time of said sale:1 .15

Under this statute, two classes of cases can not be made the basis of a supplementary proceeding. 

1. Where the judgment has been obtained upon a debt secured by mortgage upon real estate. 

2. Contract for sale of personal property where title does not pass at time of sale. 

15 Burns 1933, § 2-4406a.

No supplementary proceeding may therefore be begun upon a judgment obtained in any suit founded upon a debt secured by mortgage upon real estate. Deficiency judgments were evidently intended to be covered by this provision. Conditional sales contracts are excluded by the other clause of this section. Of course it would be a matter of defense to show that the proceeding is in fact, founded upon. a judgment of the prohibited kind.
