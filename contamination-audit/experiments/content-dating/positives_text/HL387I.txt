ALL DIVISIONS OF THE COURT OF COMMON PLEAS REPORT PROGRESS IN THE 1970-'71 COURT YEAR 

The Court of Common Pleas continues to move ahead in its efforts to serve the 1,605,000 people who live in Allegheny 

County, Pennsylvania.

The Criminal Division disposed of a total of 7,728 indictments containing 9,185 charges during the 12 months ended 

June 30, 1971. The average age of disposed cases was 9.1 months.

The Civil Division disposed of 5,814 cases during the year, 972 cases more than in 1969-70. The best parameter for assessing the efficiency of a court is the average age of disposed cases. In the year 1970-71, the average age of all disposed civil cases was 16.2, compared with 18.1 months in the previous year.

This dramatic reduction in the Civil Division shows that the Court is continuing to move ahead in its efforts to be current.

The fact that our Court has maintained for another year its preeminent position in civil litigation ranks it as one of the most progressive metropolitan courts in the nation. The 

Institute of Judicial Administration of New York University, the recognized clearing house for nation-wide court statistics, has reported that the time-lag in the disposition of civil cases in our Court again is among the lowest of all metropolitan courts in counties with populations exceeding 750,000. No other court in a county comparable in population with Allegheny County has reported a shorter time lapse between date of issue and trial of civil cases. were:

Among major developments during the last fiscal year Planning for the expansion of the arbitration program based upon the General Assembly's enabling act increasing the maximum limit from $3,000 to $10,000. 

Conversion to computer of statistical records for the civil trial list and the criminal caseload. -6-

Design development of the conceptual systems and computer operation in all four divisions of the Court. 

Submission to the Governor's Justice Commission of a plan to establish an Allegheny County Bail 

Agency and drastic revamping of present bail procedures. 

Design of a plan to be submitted to the Governor's Justice Commission to provide counsel for indigent defendants in criminal cases at preliminary hearings in order to comply with a ruling of the U. S. Supreme Court.

Formation of a committee to begin the planning for the proposed Hall of Justice to be built on a plot purchased by the County of Allegheny at Fourth and 

Grant Streets.

The expansion of the limit for arbitration from $3,000 to $10,000 was authorized by the Pennsylvania legislature after an urgent appeal by President Judge D. Donald Jamieson, of the Philadelphia Court of Common Pleas, and President Judge 

Henry Ellenbogen, of the Court of Common Pleas of Allegheny

County. One of the most significant changes in laws affecting the operation of the Court, the higher limit will permit the expansion of the Court's already successful arbitration program. 

It also will relieve some of the pressure on the civil trial list.

Although the record of the Court--due in no small measure to the dedication and continued hard work of the judges --is enviable when compared to other courts of similar size, the Court's operation has been greatly hampered by two problems. 

These are the lack of adequate judicial manpower and sufficient courtrooms and court facilities. 

The most critical problem confronting the Court of Common Pleas of Allegheny County is our growing caseload, especially in criminal cases. We simply do not have sufficient 

Common Pleas Court Judges to process speedily and efficiently the caseload that confronts us. -7-

The State Court Administrator, after careful analysis, has advised the Legislature of Pennsylvania that our Court needs an additional 12 judges. An increase in judicial strength is necessary to enable us to make real progress and to better serve the public.

A new Hall of Justice, embracing all divisions and agencies of the Court, will bring about increased efficiency and will result in such savings as the operation of one central jury pool instead of the two now required. CIVIL 

DIVISION 

Although hampered by a reduction in the number of judges due to the assignment of additional judges to the Criminal Division, the Civil Division compiled an enviable record during the report year. 

The number of pending cases at the end of this fiscal year has been reduced to below 5,000 cases, viz., 4,949 cases. The average age of pending cases was 12.7 months, compared to 12.9 months at the same time last year. This parameter has dropped steadily each year since 1966.

This record was accomplished despite the fact that a total of 3,667 cases, 198 more than last year, were placed at issue for trial. The Division disposed of a record number of 

5,814 cases on the civil trial list, 1,068 more than last year.

The number of dispositions was the highest in the history of the Court.

One of the most accurate measurements of the time lapse of litigation is the average age of disposed cases. In the present fiscal year, the average age of all 5,814 civil cases disposed of was 16.2 months. This compares with the average age of all disposed cases in the previous year of 18.1 months. Another indicator of progress is the fact that 44.2 percent of the civil dispositions occurred within a year or less of the date of issue, a gain of 4.8 percent over the previous year's 39.4 percent.

In the coming year, the Civil Division expects to increase drastically its compulsory arbitration program. The 

Pennsylvania General Assembly enacted a bill increasing the arbitration limit from $3,000 to $10,000 and the Court early in July, 1971, authorized this change for the Civil Division. -8-

The thrust of the Civil Division in the new Court Year will be to further reduce the number of pending cases and the waiting time for those cases. 

CRIMINAL DIVISION 

The Criminal Division of the Court has been under increasing pressure in recent years because of the sharply rising crime rate and the multiple hearings generally required in each case to comply with recent United States Supreme Court rulings.

The Division disposed of 9,185 charges in the court year. There were 3,618 active indictments pending as of . This was a small reduction of 159 cases from the 3,777 pending active indictments on June 30, 1970. The average age for all disposed cases was 9.1 months. This would have been even lower had not the Court made a special effort to rid the dockets of old pending cases which had been continued for reasons no longer deemed valid.

Again this summer, the Criminal Division maintained a full jury trial schedule in July. Traditionally, jury trials have not been held in the summer months. The Criminal Division courtrooms have been renovated and air-conditioning installed. 

The renovation also included the installation of carpeting in the courtrooms, improved lighting, etc. Two new experimental courtrooms were built, patterned after designs awarded prizes by the American Judicature Society. One is designed "in the round" and the other courtroom places the center of activity in a corner of the room. The purpose is to determine from actual experience whether or not any concept other than the traditional one is more efficient.

Judges regularly assigned to the Criminal Division were assisted by 24 visiting and senior judges from other counties who accepted assignments for 440 judicial days during the year. 

FAMILY DIVISION 

The Family Division continued during the year to grow in responsibility and in the amount of work it performed. The increasing work mirrored the social stresses of the community. The Adult Section of the Family Division assumed responsibility for the criminal cases between family members, cases formerly heard in the Criminal 

Division. 

-9-

There was a sharp increase in the number of divorce actions filed, numbering 4,916 for the fiscal year as compared with 4,364 in the year 1969-70. A total of 3,977 divorce decrees were entered, 923 more than in the year 1969-70. A total of 150 divorce cases were pending at the end of the Court 

Year. 

The number of cases handled by the Collecting and Disbursing Office, the agency responsible for receiving and paying out support payments, increased in the 1970-71 year to 

15,296, or 2,931 more cases than in 1969-70. At the same time the money handled by the office increased by $505,038, to a total of $7,853,510. This money, collected primarily from fathers, was distributed by court order to persons entitled to support by family members.

In the Juvenile Section, the assignment of a second judge for a full year made it possible to spend more time on individual problems. The number of informations, petitions and summons filed with the Court dropped by 373 from the prior year's total. The number of partial and final hearings dropped from 

7,903 to 6,056.

The past year was one of innovation for the Juvenile Section. Nova House, a new concept in the providing of a homelike atmosphere for older girls, was put into full operation. 

As the year ended, the Court established the first of the contemplated community-based probation centers to be established throughout Allegheny County. The first center, situated on the 

North Side, is a pilot program for the project which is designed to permit probation officers and case workers to work with children on probation more frequently and within the community in which they live and go to school.

During the year the Leech Farm site was selected and purchased by the County, in cooperation with the Court, for the construction of a new Juvenile Detention Center. The new 

Detention Center will replace the present overcrowded detention facility in the Juvenile Section building in the Oakland area. -10-

ORPHANS COURT DIVISION 

This Division continues to function effectively despite the additional burdens placed upon it since October 1, 1969, when all cases involving the Mental Health and Mental Retardation Act were assigned to it. This includes the responsibility for the civil commitments to state mental hospitals. In nine months of the previous report period, the Court handled a total of 180 petitions for civil commitments. In the 12 months ending , the 

Division handled a total of 305 petitions, held 308 hearings and promulgated 414 other orders.

This additional work necessitated the reassignment of part of the staff and various changes in procedures. 

The judges and the staff were equal to the challenge, however, even though there was no lessening in the flow of the customary matters requiring the Court's attention. 

The Division conducted 3,202 audit hearings on accounts of executors, administrators, trustees and guardians. 

In addition, 678 estates of $5,000 or less were processed without audit. Decrees of distribution were entered for 3,880 estates, an increase of 79 over the previous year.

There were 129 hearings on citations, an increase from 110 in the 1969-70 report year. These include appeals from decrees of the Register of Wills, proceedings against fiduciaries, sales of real estate and miscellaneous hearings. 

AUXILIARY SERVICES The Behavior Clinic and the Adult Probation Office, both in the Criminal Division, perform important auxiliary functions for the Court of Common Pleas. 

The Behavior Clinic conducts psychiatric exami nations of defendants on the order of the Court. 

It completed 572 psychiatric examinations during the report year. During the coming Court Year, the staff of the Clinic will be bolstered by the addition of two full-time psychiatrists to assist the director. This will permit a decrease in the use of fee-paid, part-time consultants. 

The Behavior Clinic, one of the first in the nation when it was established in 1937, has the responsibility of making psychiatric, medical, psychological and social studies of defendants ordered examined by the Court. -11-

Defendants in sex, arson and homicide cases are regularly examined. Examinations are made in other types of cases when specifically ordered by a judge. A report of the findings is made to the hearing judge in a particular case. 

The Probation Office supervises defendants placed on probation and conducts pre-sentence investigations. 

The caseload of the Adult Probation Office con tinues to increase, rising to 3,877 on . This is 

159 more cases than on the same date in 1970. Although the recommended caseload per officer is 40 cases, no additional probation officers were appointed. The additional cases were distributed among the field officers who already are handling in excess of 100 cases each.

The Probation Office completed 505 investigations, including 335 detailed pre-sentence investigations and reports during the year. -12-

THE ARBITRATION PROGRAM IN THE CIVIL DIVISION 

During the fiscal report period all civil cases where the damage claim was less than $3,000.00 were referred for compulsory arbitration. The awards may be appealed, in which event the cases are set for conciliation and trial before judges. 

The hearings in these cases were held before Boards of Arbitration composed of three attorneys per board. 

In the year ending June 30, 1971, disposition of 2,611 cases was made by arbitration hearings, compared to 1,923 cases in the previous year, an increase of 688 cases. 

As of June 30, 1971, there was an inventory of 1,960 pending arbitration cases listed for trial. This compares with an inventory of 1,627 pending cases on June 30, 1970.

The 1,960 cases listed as pending are those cases which have been assigned hearing dates. The inventory showed 5,804 open cases on the docket as of June 30, 1971, but these include many cases which have been continued because the litigants did not wish to press them. The Court intends during the coming year to make an effort to close these inactive cases and to remove them from the docket.

ADULT SECTION 

 COLLECTING AND DISBURSING OFFICE: 

FAMILY DIVISION 

The Collecting and Disbursing Office of the Family Division receives and disburses all funds ordered paid by the Court in alimony, nonsupport, and criminal cases where enforcement is made by the Director of the Adult (Domestic Relations) Section. 

From July 1, 1970, to July 1, 1971, this office handled 15,296 cases, an increase of 2,931 over the number handled the prior court year. Of these 15,296 cases, 2,204 were new. There were 407 more new cases in 1970-71 than in 1969-70. Of the new orders, 81 were in connection with alimony petitions. Since February 1, 1971, 146 new fornication and bastardy orders have been filed with the Collecting and Disbursing Office.

Since its founding in 1913, this office has handled 74,684 files.

The 64 District Justices of the Peace in Allegheny County handled 51,953 cases in the 12-month period from July 1, 1970, to 

June 30, 1971. There were 32,705 motor vehicle cases, 15,251 criminal cases, and 3,997 civil cases.

This is the first full year of operation for the 64 new salaried Justices of the Peace. The Court's last annual report showed the 

Justices of the Peace handled 16,810 cases in the six-month period from the inauguration of the new judiciary system on January 5, 1970, through June 30, 1970, the end of the previous court year.
