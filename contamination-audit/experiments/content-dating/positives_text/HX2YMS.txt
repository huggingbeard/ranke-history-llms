Dear Reader: 

This book was written for entertainment purposes only. I could not hope to reform our costly and humorless idealists-who would force us to live as we should, perhaps, but as we won't and never will. 

Once upon a time, I was very young and callow, and full of beautiful ideas. So I proceeded to make some beautiful mistakes and eventually discovered that our world of low visibility is not ideal, but filled with people like myself who are imperfect and sometimes human, with weaknesses ranging all the way from religious prejudices to fallen arches. 

However, the few self-admittedly perfect people I have met were not very interesting. They never make mistakes, of course, but I always feel embarrassed in the presence of perfect people, like one of those nude dreams where you flit helplessly about exposed to God and everybody, with not even a wisp of fog to wrap around your personality. 

I might even tell you what Randolph Whitney 

Finks, critic on The New York Blast, said about my book-but, then, you may like it. One never knows, does one? Anyway, if you get as much fun from the reading as I did from the writing, it's an even break. 

Wishing you the same, I remain 

Sincerely yours,

The somber weathered doors of the church swung wide and old Jasper Casley, the sexton, silhouetted in pantomime against the shadowed interior of the vestibule, moved silently but hurriedly to fasten them, so that the congregation which soon would be flowing outwards could have clear passage.

This was the largest congregation in the town; and the church edifice, standing in the quiet dignity of its venerable age, dominated the street corner removed but one city block from the principal business thoroughfare, where it had stood like a faithful and patient sentinel for nearly a century, ever observant of the many changes slowly taking place in the complacent and prosaic life of this small, yet typically American community.
