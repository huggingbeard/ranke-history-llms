Retailers Excise Taxes On Certain Fuels 

If you deliver any of the fuels described below into a fuel supply tank of a vehicle or aircraft when you sell the fuels, or if you acquire fuels tax-free that you later put to a taxable use, you are liable for the tax. You must file a return on Form 720 and pay the tax as discussed above.

Motor Fuels 

A tax of 4¢ a gallon is imposed on diesel fuel and certain special motor fuels whether sold to an owner, lessee, or other operator and delivered into the fuel supply tank of a highway motor vehicle or acquired in any manner other than through a taxable sale and used as fuel for a highway motor vehicle. 

In addition to diesel fuel, the tax attaches to such products as benzol, benzene, naptha, liquid petroleum gas, casinghead and natural gasoline, and any other liquid (except gasoline, kerosene, gas oil, and fuel oil) when sold or used for such purpose. 

However, if the fuel is used in a motor vehicle not required to be registered for highway use under the laws of any state or foreign country or was used before July 1, 1970, as aircraft fuel, a 2¢ a gallon tax rate applies.

The term "motor vehicle" includes all types of vehicles propelled by motor and designed for carrying loads from one place to another, regardless of the type of load and material carried and whether or not the vehicle is required to be registered for highway use. 

It does not include farm tractors, trench diggers, power shovels, bulldozers, road graders or rollers, and similar equipment that does not carry a load; nor does it include any vehicle that moves exclusively on rails. 

Nonhighway use. If special fuels are sold for use or are used as fuel in motorboats, or motor vehicles other than highway vehicles required to be registered for highway use or used before July 1, 1970, as aircraft fuel, the tax is 2¢ a gallon rather than 44 a gallon.

The 24 a gallon tax also applies to special motor fuels sold for use in highway vehicles owned by the United 

States and not used on the highways.

The tax is payable, ordinarily, by the person making the sale of diesel fuel or special motor fuels. 

Exceptions. If a dealer delivers fuel tax free to your storage tank for your use as a heating fuel, and you later use the fuel in a registered highway vehicle, you must pay the tax of 4¢ a gallon to the Internal Revenue Service. 

If you buy fuel for use in a vehicle not required to be registered for highway use, on which the dealer pays tax at the 2¢ rate, and you later use the fuel in a registered highway vehicle, you must pay the additional 2¢ a gallon tax even though the vehicle is seldom or never used on the highway. 

Or, if you acquire fuel in any manner other than a taxable sale and use it in a motor vehicle or motorboat, you must pay the tax at the rate that applies to your use of the fuel.

Aviation Fuels 

A tax applies to aviation fuels that are sold after June 30, 1970, to an owner, lessee, or other operator for use as fuel for an aircraft in noncommercial aviation or that are acquired for that use after June 30, 1970, in any manner other than through a taxable sale. The rates at which the tax applies are as follows: 

1) 7¢ a gallon on special fuels (including jet fuels); and 

2) 3¢ a gallon for gasoline (in addition to the regular 4¢ a gallon manufacturers tax on gasoline). 

Additional tax will be due if a lesser tax was paid on purchase of fuels later used in noncommercial aviation. 

Noncommercial Aviation (i.e., fuels taxes apply) means use of an aircraft other than in a business of transporting persons or property for compensation or hire by air. Also, noncommercial aviation is defined to include the following two uses even though they are in a business of transporting persons or property for compensation or hire by air: a) transportation by an aircraft having maximum certificated takeoff weight (maximum weight in type or airworthiness certificate) of 6,000 pounds or less and not operated on an established line; and b) certain transportation that is furnished by one member to another member of an affiliated group of corporations.

Manufacturers Excise Taxes 

If you are a manufacturer, producer, or importer, and you sell, lease, or use an article upon which the manufacturers excise tax is imposed, you are liable for the tax.

Lease considered sale. The lease of an article (including any renewal or extension of the lease) is generally considered a taxable sale. 

Determination of sale price. In arriving at the amount on which the tax is based, the price for which an article is sold includes any charge for coverings or containers regardless of their nature, and any charge incident to placing the article in a condition packed ready for shipment.

You should exclude the manufacturers excise tax from the taxable sale price, whether or not it is stated as a separate charge.

The cost of transportation, delivery, insurance, installation, and other charges you incur in placing the article in the hands of the purchaser may also be excluded from the selling price. 

However, the exclusion is contingent on your being able to substantiate the charges by your records. 

Discounts, rebates, and similar allowances actually granted to the purchaser may also result in adjustment of the taxable sale price. 

Local advertising charges. A charge that is made separately when the article is sold and that qualifies as a charge for "local advertising" may, within certain limitations, be excluded in the determination of sale price. For further information contact your Internal Revenue office.

Constructive sales price. If an article is sold by the manufacturer, producer, or importer at retail, on consignment, or is sold (other than through an arms-length transaction) at less than the fair market price, the tax shall be based on the price for which such articles are sold in the ordinary course of trade by manufacturers and producers of such articles. 

This basis for tax, where required to be 5

made, is determined by the Commissioner. 

Special rule. If as a manufacturer, producer, or importer, you sell an article to a retailer, or at retail, the tax will be based on the lower (1) the price at which the article is sold, or (2) the highest price at which you sell such articles to wholesale distributors, provided the following conditions are met: 

1) You must regularly sell such articles at retail, or to retailers. 

2) You must regularly sell such articles to one or more wholesale distributors in arms-length transactions and establish that the wholesale prices are determined without regard to any tax benefit under this provision. 

3) The transaction must be at armslength. 

If you are a manufacturer of automobiles and related items (except for parts and accessories), to use the Special Rule, it will also be necessary, to establish that the normal method of sales for such articles within the industry is not to sell them at retail, or to retailers. 

Intercompany sales. If a manufacturer, producer, or importer regularly sells an article to an affiliated distributor that regularly sells the article to independent retailers (and not to wholesale distributors), the fair market price (the tax base) of the article is 90% of the lowest price for which the affiliated distributor sells the article to independent retailers, in arm's-length transactions.

If a manufacturer, producer, or importer of an article regularly sells (except for tax-free sales) only to an affiliated distributor that regularly sells (except for tax-free sales) only to retailers in an industry in which the normal method is to sell such articles in arm'slength transactions to distributors, the fair market price for the article is the distributor's regular selling price to retailers reduced by a percentage of that selling price based on the markup on sales of similar articles by independent wholesale distributors in that industry. 

Bonus goods. If the manufacturer gives "bonus" or "free goods" with the purchase of a certain quantity of other merchandise, the sales price must be allocated between the "free goods" and merchandise sold when determining the excise tax. This allocation is especially important when only one of the items is subject to excise tax or if the items are subject to different tax rates. The tax is imposed only on the sale price attributable to the taxable articles. 

Example. A manufacturer sells a quantity of taxable articles and "gives" the purchaser certain nontaxable articles as a bonus. The sales price of the shipment is $15. The normal sales price is $20 of which $15 is attributable to the taxable articles and $5 to the nontaxable articles. Since the taxable items represent 3/4 of the normal sales price, the tax is based on 3/4 of the actual sales price, or $11.25 (3/4 of $15). The remaining $3.75 is allocated to the nontaxable articles.

When tax attaches is dependent upon the terms of the sales contract. Generally, the tax attaches when title passes from the seller to the purchaser or, when a title has not passed, upon delivery of the article to the customer for his use.

Partial payments. When taxable articles are leased by you, sold conditionally, sold on installment with chattel mortgage, or sold on installment with title to pass in the future notwithstanding partial payments made, the tax that must be paid for each partial payment will be a percentage of each partial payment equal to the rate of tax in effect on the date the partial payment is due. 

Uncollectible accounts. You may not take credit or claim a refund for the tax you have paid on articles sold on open accounts that become uncollectible. 

Sales exempt from tax. Sales to state and local governments, certain sales for further manufacture, sales for export, sales of certain supplies for vessels and airplanes, sales for the exclusive use of certain nonprofit educational organizations, and sales of articles manufactured or produced by Indians, are ordinarily exempt from the manufacturers tax. 

For requirements as to registration or certification of sellers and purchasers and other information and instructions for applying these exemptions, as well as other transactions that may be exempt from tax, contact your Internal 

Revenue office. 

Further manufacture. You may sell and purchase articles tax-free for further manufacture of certain articles. If you purchase articles tax-free and resell or use them other than in the manufacture of another article, you are liable for the tax on their resale or use just as if you had manufactured and sold them. 

Contact your Internal Revenue office for information on how to register to sell and purchase articles tax-free.

Inner tubes for tires, 10¢ a pound. 

Tread rubber, 5¢ a pound.

Determination of weight. Weight shall be based on total weight exclusive of metal rims or rim bases in the case of tires. 

Manufacturer's retail stores. In the case of manufacturer-owned or importer-owned retail stores, the excise tax on tires and inner tubes is imposed at the time these articles are delivered to the stores, rather than at the time of sale.

Bicycle tires and tubes sold for use, or used, in the manufacture of new bicycles. 

Tire carcasses not suitable for commercial use. 

Tread rubber exempt in certain cases. 

The tax on tread rubber does not apply when sold to any person for his use other than in the recapping or retreading of tires of the type used on highway vehicles. 

Gasoline and lubricating oils. Every person subject to tax on gasoline or lubricating oils must register with his

District Director before he enters into a transaction for which any tax liability will be incurred. 

Tax rate on gasoline. A tax of 4¢ a gallon is applicable on gasoline sold by any producer or importer.

Gasoline means all products commonly or commercially known or sold as gasoline that are suitable for use as a motor fuel. This definition does not include casinghead and natural gasoline unless they are blended with other compounds to make gasoline used in motors. 

A producer includes a refiner, compounder, or blender, a wholesale distributor, and a dealer selling gasoline exclusively to producers of gasoline, as well as producers. 

Tax rate on lubricating oil. A tax of 

6¢ a gallon is applicable on the sale of lubricating oils (other than cutting oils) sold by manufacturers and producers. 

Lubricating oil may be sold tax-free by the manufacturer if it has been determined by the Commissioner of Internal

Revenue to be seldom used as a lubricant, but used almost exclusively for nonlubricating purposes. 

Cutting oils include oils sold for use in cutting and machining operations on metals, such as in drawing, rolling, shearing, punching, stamping, and certain forging operations.

Fishing Equipment 

A tax of 10% of the sales price is imposed on fishing rods, creels, reels, artificial lures, baits, and flies, sold by manufacturers, producers, or importers, including any parts or accessories sold on or in connection therewith. Firearms

A tax of 10% is imposed on the sales price of pistols and revolvers sold by manufacturers, producers, or importers.

A tax of 11% is imposed on the sales price of shells, cartridges and firearms, other than pistols and revolvers. A manufacturer of firearms, shells and cartridges is also liable for the tax on shells and cartridges he uses in testing his own firearms or sells to other manufacturers of firearms for such testing purposes.

Transportation 

Persons by air. Amounts paid for taxable transportation of persons by air that begins after June 30, 1970, including amounts paid for related seating or sleeping accommodations, are subject to a tax of 8%. "Transportation" includes layover time and movement of aircraft in deadhead service. The tax applies to amounts paid outside the United States but only for taxable transportation that begins and ends in the United States. Certain transportation that takes place entirely outside the United States (and the 225-mile zone), and that meets specified tests, is not subject to the tax.

The person paying for taxable transportation is liable for the tax; and, ordinarily, the person receiving the payment must collect the tax and file returns. However, the tax must be collected by the person furnishing the initial transportation provided for under a prepaid order, exchange order, etc., paid for outside the United States. 

Taxable transportation is transportation by air: (1) that begins either in the

United States or in any portion of 

Canada and Mexico that is not more than 225 miles from the nearest point in the continental United States (225-mile zone) and ends in the United States or in the 225-mile zone; and (2) if not included in (1) above, to the extent of the portion that is directly or indirectly from one port or station to another in the United States (if not a part of uninterrupted international air transportation).

Policies A tax is imposed on the issuance of policies of insurance by foreign insurers. The rate of tax is applied on each dollar, or fraction thereof, of the premium paid and is as follows: 

1) Four Cents; casualty insurance and indemnity bonds.

Example. Upon a premium payment of $10.10, the tax amounts to 44 cents. 

2) One Cent; life, sickness, and accident insurance, annuity contract and reinsurance policies.

Example. Upon a premium payment of $10.10, the tax amounts to 11 cents. 

Meaning of premium. The term premium for this purpose means the agreed price or consideration for assuming and carrying the risk or obligation, and includes any additional charge assessment that may be payable under the contract, whether in one sum or by installments. or 

When liability attaches. The liability for this tax attaches at the time the premium payment is transferred to the foreign insurer (including transfers to any bank, trust fund or similar recipient, designated by the foreign insurer), or to any nonresident agent, solicitor, or broker. A person may pay the tax before the liability attaches if he keeps records consistent with that practice. 

Duty to remit tax. The tax must be remitted by the person who makes the payment of the premium to the foreign insurer or his agent. This means the resident person who actually transfers the money or its equivalent to the insurer or agent.

Records. The person required to remit this tax must keep accurate records that identify each policy or instrument subject to tax. These records must clearly establish the type of policy or instrument, the gross premium paid, the identity of the insured and insurer, the total premium charged and, if the premium is to be paid in installments, the amount and anniversary date of each installment.

The records must be retained at the place of business or other convenient location for at least three years from the later of (1) the date any part of the tax became due; or (2) the date any part of the tax was paid. During this period the records must be readily accessible to authorized Internal 

Revenue officers or employees.

The person having control or possession of a policy or instrument subject to this tax must retain the policy for at least three years from the date any part of the tax on it was paid.
