FOREWORD 

You can imagine how persuasive and appealing the pages of the Journal were to me. I own I am not sure I can separate their appeal from the place and the person that made them. The way they keep haunting me after a week's time may be a sign that they would ring as beautifully in a stranger's ear as they did in mine.

FALL TERM 

September 22. A noisy State highway runs through a quiet little reservation on its streaming way from one great city to another. At least it looks like a quiet spot, with far-reaching level lawns, and multitudes of shady trees and evergreens, that hold the dazzling snow in winter. 

But, perhaps, if the traveler could detach himself, mentally and physically, and pause, instead of hurrying by, he might feel rather than see, that his first impression was erroneous. A quiet reservation bathed in green, outwardly, yes! but inwardly a seething life, passing through phase after phase of changewhole change, and half change, and infinitesimal change, seen not with the naked eye. 3

To be or not to be? Ah! that is the question for a school!

This bright warm day in late summer, and the highway, always crowded, becomes almost impassable. For every taxi that can be procured is bringing boys to school, some returning and others for the first time. 

Outwardly again, perhaps, they look the same, but inwardly, Oh! what a difference! Old boys run gaily up to the familiar Houses, and establish themselves in the twinkling of an eye-but new boys pause, and wonder, and ultimately ask where they must go. So many Houses, so much alike, at first. At first! please notice, for after a few weeks the seeming sameness disappears. At least so the boys will tell you, and they ought to know.

For the House is merely a transparent cloak for the group it covers, for its president, its officers, and those they rule, and for its House Master, who in turn.

rules them. Though very often a House disports itself so graciously that no ruling is in evidence at all.

I said the old boys find their places easily; they do, although there is one 

House where the approach is made more slowly and with circumspection. It is the senior House, and those who have gathered painfully together the necessary number of "points" for college take up their abode there, instead of returning to the Houses they had left in spring. Taking possession of their rooms in this House is a somewhat deliberate affair. 

It must be done with a mixture of dignity and carelessness. The first entry may mean much in days that follow on. 

So we have come to school with new and old,—and now?

Each House is crowded to the doors, and hurry and confusion reign supreme. 

Some rooms are good, and others very bad. At first, again at first, things are, 5

moreover, complicated by anxious parents, who have not known till now just what it means to "leave a boy in school!"

The "Doctor" must be seen,-for must not... be watched quite carefully, -so different, in many ways, from other boys. "The food," "a sunny room,' 

He understands, of course! Trunks must be emptied and disposed of, details seen to, anything, in fact, to distance the farewell. But it must come, and does. "Good-bye, good-bye," "So hard to see our parents go, but if they must, well then, they must." 

And here we are, alone! Despite the crowd, alone! A few tears sting the bright eyes for a moment, but almost at once the bell clangs out, and groups fall into place, for lunch or dinner, as the case may be. A lightning speed-and that is over too. "How can we eat, with so much still to do?"

But this year, I remember, varied in its course, for Sunday was the first whole day of term, and gave more time for toilets than of yore.-Though all was not as peaceful as it seemed. For singers must be singled out to fill the choir's ranks on this first Sunday, and how to know a singer at a glance and fit him to a cassock after that! But it was done, 'twas done. And after the excitement came the lull. The slow march up the aisle to organ music, and fluttering hearts resumed their natural beat. For music has a means of soothing, we are told! 

The Head took charge, and gave at last the "watchword for the year." "Courage!" Learned by him in summer months, while dwelling on the courage of a boy. We knew the boy, and loved him. What a word to pass to us!

May we keep it as this year, and all the years go on. After the Word, the Hymn. 

May you, too, some day hear it sung!

For a moment it was hard to pass out in the sunlight once again. But now the year has started, and we go into the nid-

The days move slowly by; the Teain is out, and many minor teams cover the playing-fields on every side, the coaches working bravely, and the boys. Play after play is run, and run again.

October 6. (Late). Our first real game was won. After some anxious moments, and grave fears.

On this same evening came the opening banquet for The Form, an hour full of interest for us who watch, and wait, and listen, all the time.

The tables filled to overflowing, and yet a quiet party for a while. For they are not yet sure, quite sure of whom their

friends will be, whether the old, or new who mingled with us at this feast to bring "The Form" together for the year. "The Form," a strange name for a crowd. Yet not so strange, because, you see, they form "A Form" before the year is done.

While courses passed the Head was gathering jokes on those before him, and on those who later took their turn with speeches following dinner. Their icecream stood, neglected; such a price they gladly paid for glory on this opening night. "Shall we lead the School up higher?

In former years, have we done all we might?" We feel their answer to the boy they trust, the boy who is, this year, their choice to lead the School, to hold the newformed Form to what is right. 

An enviable boy we think, facing his

Form full square, their chosen leader for the days to come.

Or seem to fill their places in the School! 

New guests slip in now every day and mix together in a genial crowd. So many points of view, so much to say, to argue out, comment on, and explain. We talk of teams in school or college, those we know, of books and plays and politics (this year!) Touch lightly on religion, careful there; we are so different, yet so much alike. One of our former leaders visited us lately, noting changes with an anxious eye. For almost any change is dreaded, even those acknowledged to be good, so jealous are they of their School, their 

School. 

When asked to stand in "Conference," he did, quite simply, looking down upon his old domain, but told us afterwards in confidence that it had been hard, quite hard. Though not so very long ago he did it every day without a qualm. 

We wait to read the novel he is writing, and wish him all success. He is our own, as all the others are, our own in a peculiar way. His joy is ours, and we hold him in our heart. 14

October 12. We smile today because a friend is with us. One of our two young masters who have been a year away from school. One to regain his health, the other to pursue more closely for a time his literary work. We have a strange schoolconsciousness. We know our friends. 

Oh yes! We know our friends.

On Sundays anyone, from all four quarters of the globe, may easily come.

Gathered about the campus now the sun shines on great, rusty piles of autumn leaves. We work and play to music of the leaves. 

On Monday nights we entertain boy presidents of Houses in the School, on whom quite heavy burdens rest, and to whom numerous matters, dealing with the boys may be referred, and thereby free the Head.

In four days more the Prom will be upon us and to the boys long seem the days before! (Written during the Prom-November 24 and 25.)

Our girl-guests occupy our largest House, the boys who live there move, for the short time that they are with us.

Our willing staff, with fresh white jackets on, carry the bags upstairs, after the greetings to the hostesses are done. 

At last the pretty butterflies drift down, and finding eager partners, wend their way off to the Gym. There music waits to catch them in its whirl, beneath the many-colored draperies.
