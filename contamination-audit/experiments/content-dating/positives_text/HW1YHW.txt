FOREWORD 

BOOKS dealing with prophetic subjects are of increasing interest in these dark and distressful days. 

Many people are giving heed to the "more sure word of prophecy... as unto a light that shineth in a dark place" as never before. 

A book entitled "The Morning Cometh" will command attention on the part of the reading public, especially when it brings the witness of a fresh pen into the realm of prophetic literature.

This book is the first of its kind from the mind of its author, who is a Christian layman, and who has for a long time given spoken testimony to prophetic themes. Its pages reveal an earnest love for God's Word, a spiritual devotion and a desire to comfort God's Israel. 

Parts II and III should stimulate and provoke serious thought in the direction of fulfilled prophecy, and awaken lively interest in the promised advent of the King of Kings. 

The author has given considerable time and done much research work to dig out Messianic predictions from the Scriptures and to discover undeniable facts of history to prove that "prophecy is the mold of history." 

Part I will be of much value to the student, because it contains an immense compilation of facts from the pens of able expositors, whose voices on prophetic themes are regarded as acknowledged authorities. 

I do not agree with some of the conclusions of the book, but am in hearty accord with the purpose of its writer, and gladly express the hope that others will catch the contagion of his enthusiasm and be numbered among the countless hosts who "love His appearing."

May the message turn the tides of thinking into the happy channels of "that blessed hope, and the glorious appearing of the great God and our Saviour Jesus Christ."

W. H. ROGERS, D. D. 

New York City.

A BUSINESS MAN SPEAKS 

PIRITUAL wisdom is not always to be found in the Seminary. Too often the thing which Festus feared might have happened to Paul, does truly happen to the University professor-"Much learning doth make thee mad!"

Here is a business man who has taken to heart the statement of the Lord Jesus Christ, "Whosoever shall not receive the Kingdom of God as a little child shall not enter," 

Luke 18:17. In that earnest childlike simplicity, this humble servant of God has approached God's revelation to mankind with the first elementary stipulation, as given in 

Proverbs 1:7, "The fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge." And now from his pen has come forth a book written by a layman for laymen, a book which it has been my privilege to see in the making, and even to urge in the publishing of it. 

May the Lord abundantly bless the testimony of this book, and may it not only warm the hearts and strengthen the faith of thousands of the Lord's people everywhere, but may it also be used as a means to bring unsaved men to the foot of the Cross, and to cry out, "I believe! Help thou my unbelief."

JOSEPH HOFFMAN COHN. 

Brooklyn, N. Y.

PREFACE 

"Midnight—strange mystic hourwhen the veil between the frail present and the eternal future grows thin." -Harriet Beecher Stowe.

CHE author has tried to give credit in this book for information taken from the published works of sound expositors, and sincerely hopes that no such credit has been overlooked. The most careful checking sometimes fails to prevent small errors from creeping into a work of this character. The author will be grateful for having such errors called to his attention.

These findings and conclusions, the result of a research undertaken as a matter of personal interest, are now being presented in book form at the instance of interested friends. 

To none of these does the author feel more greatly indebted for encouragement than to Captain Anna M. Smith of the Volunteers of America, whose staunch faith, translated into practical works, was her stout staff through the Valley of the Shadow and a rod of strength on her voyage into what some are pleased to call the Unknown.

This research, prompted by the outbreak of the World War, finds its written record in process of publication as the truce, or armistice, of 1918 is ended, with the combatants, having had time to lick their wounds, engaged in a resumption of hostilities. So the world goes into its macabre dance in celebration of having reached the peak of civilization.

For years writers on prophetic subjects have been united in interpreting Ezekiel 38 as the prediction of an alliance to take place between Russia and Germany, prior to their joint foray with Turkey and other allies toward the end of this age, against Palestine for the chemical wealth of the Dead Sea.

This threat, according to the same prophet, will be met by protests from England "and the young lions thereof" which may include the United States. The inference is that, to be effective, such protest must come from Powers of military superiority or parity. In other words, from a vic-

torious England, or more probably by a peace formula yet to be devised which will save some face for all concerned; or, possibly a stalemate may bring about another truce or armistice.

Until the recent rapprochement between Russia and Germany upset the calculations of European statesmen, such a denouement in the relations between Stalin and Hitler seemed most improbable and even those accepting Ezekiel's prediction as gospel truth put it forward as an eventuality probable under subsequent rulers. Its surprising accomplishment in our day has most arresting implications for those who find corroborative end-age timing in a multitude of other prophecies. One thing becomes clear by the 

Russian-German alliance and that is that Hitler did not persecute the Jews because they were Communists, as he said, but simply because they were Jews. 

If Italy should elect to cast her lot in with the Allies, Mussolini would be in a good position to drive a hard bargain. In addition to offering the force of his dynamic personality he could conceivably, as the friend of Islam, unite the Moslem adherents of Allah in a holy war against the hordes of the Northern Godless. Surprises as great have already arisen out of this conflict of unpredictable duration, involvement and terms for lasting peace. 

If his price should include the Mandate for Palestine; if the ten countries of the Eastern and Western divisions of the Old Roman Empire, the ten toes and ten horns of prophecy, should group themselves into an opposing Southern Federation; if these countries should delegate their power for a limited time to the head of the Italian State, any or all of these developments would constitute a revelation of that person as the Roman Prince who is to come, the coming War Lord at whose step the earth will tremble. 

Any of these prophesied steps will presage the wrath to come, and if any of them should emerge from the present conflict as the prophesied German-Russian alliance has already done, or should they eventuate during this generation, as is most probable, inasmuch as that would be a post- 

Rapture event, I hope that you, my dear reader, will not then be here. 

September 25, 1939 

Minneapolis, Minnesota. -H. M. B.

CHAPTER I The Challenge of Prophecy 

"The fool hath said in his heart there is no God.". - Psalms 14:1The desire to know what the future may hold in store, an inclination which runs the gamut from the next day's weather to the hereafter, is elemental and deeply ingrained in human nature. As the cauldron of world affairs comes to a boil, destroying old norms and sweeping away old moorings, the intensity of this desire to storm the portals of the future increases.

This book review of the World's Best Seller, undertaken in the light of this widespread desire, being but the conclusions of a layman, is not advanced in defense of any particular school of religious thought. We rest the moral side of our case upon the all-inclusive "whosoever will", leaving the shades and nuances of salvation and condemnation to theological argument.

No answer to present forebodings nor any key to the future can be found by consulting evil spirits who "peep and mutter" (Isaiah 8:19); or spirits who "kept not their first estate, but left their own habitation" (Jude 6) and who feared "torment before the time" (Matthew 8:29).

Nor does any lasting value accrue from visiting fortune tellers, clairvoyants, palmists or astrologers.

The inquirer soon loses interest in following the uninspired, disconnected divinations of Michel Nostradamus, Johann Adam Muller, Mother Shipton, et al., whose reputations for occasional prescience may, or may not, have arisen from the reading of the inspired prophets of Israel. Their predictions, with their percentage of fallibility, and lacking the stamp of divine authenticity, merely serve to throw

into relief the connected story of the inspired prophets of the Bible, whose writings cover the period we call time and reach backward and forward into the incomprehensible illimitable which they call eternity.

Separated though these writers may have been by centuries, nevertheless, as touching upon the same subjects, they are found in complete accord. Infallibly they have charted our past, illumined our present and disclosed our future.

This Book of books not only provides a pattern for living and a philosophy of dying but charts the entire course of human affairs from a bad beginning to a most glorious finish.

God through the Hebrew prophets has woven a beautiful tapestry in threads of crimson and gold and bound it in royal purple. Worked in the center are the figures of a Babe in a 

Manger, a Man on a Cross, a King on a Throne. The background, lighted with promising colors, is sprinkled with the rosemaries of remembrance and the lilies of hope. 

The pattern in this work of matchless precision is not coincidental but shows a design of prophecy. Starting at the bottom in straight, formal lines, relieved by figures of typical lambs, the pattern changes at the picture of a Cross. 

Then appear the free colors of scarlet and gold. 

Near the top the figure 666 is worked into and across the pattern in deep black. Just beneath it the threads of gold are broken off, ending, in an upward spiral. 

From the black dominant number to the end of the body of the tapestry, the colors grow increasingly somber except for being sprinkled throughout by short golden threads, the ends of which are turned up, like the previous group of the same color.

The tapestry has two parallel matched borders, one a completed design in prophecy, the other, its historical complement, still remaining unfinished on the loom of time.

At the top center the finished border and the pattern meet at a golden Crown, which imparts all the hues of the rainbow to the top edge and fringe.

The eye of the observer is arrested by the figure of a man in deep blue, having a prominent nose and wearing a round cap. The figure, repeated between the close parallel lines of the borders, has a staff in his hand, and his back is bent beneath a heavy pack. 

In every case the figure is so woven in as always to show his left foot placed upon one border, his right upon the other, which is to say, "Behold the Jew and believe The Jew", for one proof of prophetic inspiration is written in our midst on tablets of flesh in the scars on the tragic Jew. 

They do well who turn the pages of this Book searching for understanding of present portents with their probable fulfilment, for the expression "Heaven knows when" is more true than idle. Such search may result in finding the hope of Titus 2:13: "Looking for that blessed hope, and the glorious appearing of the great God and our Saviour 

Jesus Christ." and securing for them as well the comfort of I Thessalonians 4:16-18: "For the Lord Himself shall descend from heaven with a shout..... and the dead in Christ. shall rise first: Then we which are alive and remain, shall be caught up together with them in clouds (i. e. swarms) to meet the Lord in the air: and so shall we ever be with the Lord. Wherefore comfort one another (see Isaiah 61:2) with these words."

Such as seek and find this fulfilled desire may view with equanimity the perplexing present and the forbidding future.

The words of prophecy found in "the law and the testimony" (Isaiah 8:20) carry a blessing to the reader and a challenge to the imitator. "Blessed be the name of God for ever and ever; for wisdom and might are His: and He changeth the times and the seasons: He removeth kings and setteth up kings:... He revealeth the deep and secret things: He knoweth what is in the darkness, and the light dwelleth with Him." (Daniel 2:20-22). "We have also a more sure word of prophecy: whereunto ye do well that ye take heed, as unto a light that shineth in a dark place, until the day dawn, and the Day Star arise in your hearts. For the prophecy came not in old time by the will of man; but holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost." (2 Peter 1:19, 21). "And now I have told you before it come to pass that when it is come to pass, ye might believe." (John 14:29). "Remember the former things of old; for I am God, and there is none else: I am God and there is none like me, declaring the end from the beginning, and from ancient times the things that are not yet done, saying, My counsel shall stand, and I will do all my pleasure:" (Isaiah 46:9-10). "The Lord of hosts hath sworn, saying, Surely as I have thought, so shall it come to pass; and as 

I have purposed, so shall it stand." (Isaiah 14:24). "Blessed he that readeth, and they that hear the words of this prophecy and keep those things which are written therein." (Revelation 1:3).

"I will put enmity... between thy seed and her (the woman's) seed."

Genesis 3:15 - 

The promised establishment of the Kingdom rejected two thousand years ago has been delayed by the conflict between the seed of the serpent and the seed of the woman (Genesis 3:15, Jeremiah 31:22). This conflict has for its objective, on the Heavenly side, the circumvention of Satan's prideful aspirations to usurp for himself the worship due to God alone and is waged for the redemption of the creation he despoiled. 

This original spiritual rebel gained some Heavenly converts before he fell "as lightning from Heaven" (Luke 

10:18), taking with him those angels "who kept not their first estate" (Jude 6), causing the chaos in nature described in Genesis 1:2.

From Heaven Satan fell through the stratosphere to his present atmospheric base from which he rules as the "Prince of the Power of the Air" (Ephesians 2:2). In that sphere of operations Gabriel was delayed by one of 

Satan's lieutenants for twenty-one days when speeding his way down through the constellations to meet Daniel: "But the prince of the kingdom of Persia withstood me one and twenty days: but lo, Michael, one of the chief princes, came to help me." (Daniel 10:13).

Not human factors, but this same spiritual rebellion, prompted the apostle Paul to say in Ephesians 6:12: "For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the

rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places."

CHAPTER III This Age of Ours 

"To proclaim the acceptable year of the Lord, and the day of vengeance of our God:"-Isaiah 61:2 

In the preceding chapters we have shown the purpose of prophecy and the background for the course of the ages. 

Inasmuch as in our times we are at the point of convergence of all lines of end-age prophecy, whether chronological, typical, Jewish or Church, it is imperative that we take a definite stand in religious matters. 

To do otherwise would, under present circumstances, contribute to the dereliction of true faith in all walks of life, already burdening the shoulders of the coming generation. In taking this position we must be extremely careful to avoid distorting the context in the least respect. 

There is no other answer adequate to meet the present world wide dread and deep and poignant misery. 

At last we have reached the point in human history where intelligent men no longer boast of the progress of civilization as they see most of the achievements of science wrongly applied. There are few takers for the affirmative side of "Resolved: That the world is growing better."

The exiled Einstein, our most publicized scientific thinker, claims solution of the greatest mystery of science by discovery of a single law governing all the phenomena of the material universe. This he calls his "unified field theory".

As Bailey Millard points out, such a theory of unification under a single law of the all pervasive operations of gravitation, magnetism and electricity approximates admission of divine control.

Tennyson, unhampered by scientific reluctance, admitted spiritual law to be the governing force in a "unified field theory" when he wrote the following in his last stanza of "In Memoriam": "One God, one law, one element, And one far-off divine event 

To which the whole creation moves.”

It is our purpose in this book to consider whether there be a "balm in Gilead," for one cannot help but sense a great

Hand back of the otherwise purposeless shifting of the scenery on the stage of world affairs. We propose to show by the Scriptures that there is a revealed design back of past and present major events working toward predicted future objectives, which cannot, by human or Satanic means, be frustrated.

As the stoutest hearts quail before the fearful implications of an unpredictable future, there is no research of greater present moment than the one to which we aspire in this work. It is therefore timely and urgent, as we near impalement upon the sharp horns of humanity's greatest dilemma, to attempt to prove that a way of escape is provided as evidenced in prototype by Enoch. 

Biblically speaking the age in which we live is divided into two parts. The first we call this year of grace, and the second, Doomsday, or as popularly envisioned "the end of the world." This is the day of battle and vengeance which writes finis to our age. There is a multiplicity of prophecies and parables concerning the first part and a great many referring to the second, or final day, to which Isaiah alone, for instance, refers a hundred times. 

Twenty-six hundred years ago the great prophet was moved by the Holy Spirit to write these words: "The Spirit of the Lord God is upon me; because the Lord hath appointed me to preach good

tidings unto the meek: He hath sent me to bind up the broken hearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives and the opening of the prison to them that are bound; To proclaim the acceptable year of the 

Lord, and the day of vengeance of our God; to comfort all that mourn; To appoint unto them that mourn in Israel, to give them beauty for ashes, the oil of joy for mourning, the garment of praise for the spirit of heaviness; that they might be called trees of righteousness, the planting of the Lord that He might be glorified. And they shall build the old wastes, they shall raise up the former desolations, and shall repair the waste cities, the desolations of many generations." (Isaiah 61:1-4).

Here we find prophesied the coming of the Messiah, the 

Rapture, Armageddon, the Second Coming, the Redemption of Israel and the Millennium. 

Seven hundred and twenty-nine years later the Lord

Jesus Christ stood in the Synagogue and quoted from this prophecy. He took in His prophesied coming which would substitute grace for law and included the promised Rapture in His quotation, but omitted, as being yet future, the words relating to the day of God's vengeance, His Return on that day to redeem Israel, and the Millennium.

After expressing sufficiency and finality by closing the Book and sitting down He placed His official seal upon the proclamation by stating, "This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears" (Luke 4:21).

In thus officially opening our part of this age, Jesus named it after Himself, A.D., Anno Domini, the year of the Lord. We call it "this year of grace"; He called it "the acceptable year". During this dispensation His limitless grace marks the Father's acceptance of all who receive 

His son.

Our age, which was thus termed a year and a day and which includes both A.D. and the day of vengeance, opened with the coming of the Messiah and will close with His return as King. A.D., opened by this announcement, will close the evening preceding the day of vengeance. Opened on earth it will be closed in Heaven. He who opened A.D. here will close it there. According to Revelation 16:16, this official closing immediately follows the mobilization of the world's armies at Armageddon for the last battle of the age in the day of God's vengeance in the Valley of Jehoshaphat. Prior thereto in verse 15 is given the prophesied Rapture escape. 

Just as God warned in Genesis 6:3 that His Spirit would not always strive with man so now we find, as the rulers gather their armies to fight God and the daughter of His people Israel, that grace is finally withdrawn and the acceptable year closed: "There should be time no longer: but in the days of the voice of the seventh angel, when he shall begin to sound, the mystery of God should be finished. And the seventh angel poured out his vial into the air; and there came a great voice out of the temple of heaven, from the throne, saying, 'It is done.'" (Revelation 10:6; 16:17).

With words of confusion, collapse and chaos being more freely used, we see a world in alarming political flux, dancing around a bonfire of precedents and precepts, breaking across its knees the treaties and pledges of changing expediency, while it wonders what the outcome will be. 

In these days of instantaneous communication there is immediate world wide reaction to major developments in the European theater of current affairs where threats of war and hopes of peace seem to be continually hanging in the balance. Such is the present state of world psychol-

ogy that we weigh most carefully the utterances of Europe's leading figures to see if, between the lines, we may find hidden their future intents and plans.

Those bestriding Colossi of the god of force, Mussolini, Hitler and Stalin are regarded with fear and dread by all free peoples.

If we read prophecy aright we learn that the countries of the Old Roman Empire, including England, Belgium, France, Spain and other Southern European countries are to pool their power under the present, or a succeeding, head of Italy. The countries of Germany, Russia and Turkey are to form an opposing federation. Which coalition will form first, and why, is not disclosed.

Current News Digest says: 

"Bankers tremble, newspapers croak, professors prophesy, farmers cry, business slackens, credit shrinks, politicians shirk and even criminals are discouraged."

Stephen Leacock writes in the Banking Journal: 

"We are all afraid together. We have lost courage. Everyday's news brings us a scare of apprehension of approaching war, of imminent disaster or deepening depression. We get to expect it, to ask it, like martyrs asking for suffering. "What is there that we can do about it? Nothing that I know of."

From plain Scriptures it would appear that our trying times not only have come to stay but are predicted to grow more oppressive.

The words of news commentators nowadays almost plagiarize the prophets' writings, and their tales of atrocities committed against the Jews are outstanding in a world that is dazed with the sufferings of humanity. Our com-

prehension would be staggered by such accounts were not our sensibilities dulled by repetition of them. 

Improbabilities, currently considered, merge into certainties before the eternal fiat, "God hath said". To such as read their newspapers in the light of their Bibles the stirring events of this generation fit into the pattern of the ancient mosaic of prophecy and thus lighten our souls with hope instead of darkening them with despair.

Never was there such need for Christian militancy as in these days not only of unprecedented assaults from without, but of renegade weakening within. 

The Bishop of Winchester, in his presidential address before the Church Congress on October 8th, 1935, said: "The changes of our time have brought upon Christianity the most critical conflicts and the most searching ordeals which it has experienced since its foundation. Neither in the days of the persecution by the Roman Empire, nor when Arianism struck at the heart of the Christian faith, nor when Mohammedanism threatened to overwhelm

Christendom, has the Church been exposed to dangers so great and attacks so fierce as those which menace it today." 

General Pershing has this to say as the shades of the watchman's night of Isaiah 21:11-12 are falling fast, with the shapes they assume fully as frightful as their prophetic descriptions, "Prospects for peace do not look promising.

If another war takes place, Western civilization, as we know it, cannot survive.” 

Herbert Hoover describes the present fear of a threatened debacle as follows: 

"Fear by nations of one another, fear by governments of their citizens, fear by citizens of their

governments and the vague fear of people everywhere that general war is upon them again. Fear of the promised massacre of civil populations from the air. Fear everywhere."

However, there is hope to be found in the face of hopelessness. There is an "out". As Keith L. Brooks says, "The darker the outlook, the brighter the uplook."

For centuries the church has believed profoundly in the prophecies of the first coming of God's Son to earth, in humiliation, to die as a sacrifice for sin. But these prophecies are rare in comparison with the number of those that foretell a different coming-in majesty and power. In fact, the ratio between the two is as one to twenty. In the 

Old Testament, beginning at Moses, through the Psalms and all the prophets, there are more than fifteen hundred allusions to the second coming in glory. If the prophecies of the first coming have been so marvelously fulfilled, we certainly have abundant grounds for believing that those of the second will be also, and in an equally literal way. When the disciples doubted the rare prophecies of His death, Jesus said, "O fools, and slow of heart to believe all that the prophets have spoken" (Luke 24:25). How much more would He have reason to rebuke the "slow of heart” in this age, who doubt the more numerous prophecies of His glorious return?

In the New Testament, 318 passages are occupied with instruction about the appearing, coming, day, and reign of the Lord. They are found in every book, in the ratio of one verse in every twenty-five. The angel's annunciation to Mary included both comings: "thou shalt conceive in thy womb, and bring forth a son, and shalt call his name

JESUS. He shall be great, and shall be called the Son of the 

Highest." This much referred to the first coming, and has already taken place. But the angel continued: “and the

Here a future coming is clearly in view. There is no warrant for interpreting the first part of this announcement literally, and not the second part. John the Baptist preached of the second coming. The Lord Jesus Himself spoke of it constantly, in parable, exhortation and sermon, at the end of His life before His Judges, and as a last message to His followers. All the apostles wrote on the same theme, in connection with every fundamental doctrine-resurrection, sonship, self-denial, service-in fact this coming is preeminent above any other fact or doctrine of Scripture. Thus, because it is so bound up with every phase of the Christian's life, we ought to give it serious study.

The second coming of our Lord is a two-fold event. Unless we realize this, the varying descriptions of it appear directly contradictory. Jesus says on the one hand, "I am ... the bright and morning star" (Revelation 22:16). On the other, it is said that He is "the Sun of Righteousness" (Malachi 4:2). He states in one place that His coming will be in secret, like "a thief in the night" (Matthew 24:42, 43), and in another: "as the lightning cometh out of the east, and shineth even unto the west; so shall also the coming of the Son of man be" (Matthew 24:27). It is written that we will meet the Lord "in the air" (I Thessalonians 4:17), and also that "his feet shall stand in that day upon the mount of Olives" (Zechariah 14:4).

The explanation is that there are two parts to the return of our Lord, (1) the coming for His own, which is secret, invisible to the world, "in the air", and is referred to by the Greek word "parousia", "presence"; and (2) the

visible glorious appearance, for judgment, which is public, on the earth, and is referred to by the Greek word "ephiphaneia", "manifestation". There is a passage which mentions both aspects, and reveals their relationship: "The Lord Jesus shall be revealed from heaven with his mighty angels, in flaming fire taking vengeance on them that know not God, and that obey not the gospel of our Lord Jesus Christ; who shall be punished with everlasting destruction from the presence of the Lord, and from the glory of his power; when he shall (have) come to be glorified in his saints" (II Thessalonians 1:7-10). 

The "shall come" in our Authorized and Revised Versions of the Scriptures is a mistranslation of the Greek subjunctive aorist tense of the verb, and should read, instead of the above, "shall have come". Thus we find that the 

Lord will come for His own, before He will come with them in judgment. We shall consider in this chapter only the first aspect, the parousia for the saints, their sudden, secret removal from out of the earth to their Lord. 

When Paul spoke of this aspect he said, "Behold, I shew you a mystery; We shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed in a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trump: for the trumpet shall sound, and the dead shall be raised incorruptible, and we shall be changed. For this corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality" (I Corinthians 15:51-53).

He called it a "mystery" because up till then, this part of the second coming had been a truth hidden from the Old Testament prophets. All that they had seen was the 

Messiah's later manifestation in glory. And yet, even in the Old Testament, there are a few references to it, which must have seemed cryptic until further light was shed on them from the New Testament. For example, in Psalms 

17:15, David says "As for me, I will behold thy face in

righteousness: I shall be satisfied, when I awake, with thy likeness."

As certain as the Divine promise which brought Jesus, the Messiah, to Bethlehem, just so certain is fulfillment of the even more abundant promise of His return for His own, and, seven years later, with His own. First, as the 

Messiah, the Savior, second, as the Bridegroom, the Bright and Morning Star, last, as King of Kings, the Sun of

Righteousness with healing in His Wings for the nations.

This will be the Morning following the night of Tribulation to which the watchman referred in Isaiah 21:11-12, the Golden Age of song, poem and story, concerning which the Bible abounds in superlative description, the Utopia of humanity's dreams.

Not man alone, but all creation groans for this promised redemption (Romans 8:22-23) by the Desire of Nations. 

How shall we who thrill to the tread of returning heroes, describe such a return, a return in majesty, power and glory unexcelled, viewed by every eye from one end of the world to the other.

In contemplating this vindicative glory from afar the prophets complained of language's paucity until we see 

Isaiah metaphorically throw up his hands to exclaim: "For since the beginning of the world, men have not heard, nor perceived by the ear, neither hath the eye seen, O God, beside thee, what He hath prepared for him that waiteth for Him." (Isaiah 64:4).

The former glory, which we but faintly glimpse, once put aside as of no reputation (Philippians 2:7), is now reinforced by the accompanying fruits and riches of the supreme sacrifice of love, not for friends alone, great as we know such love to be, but for all creation and all mankind. (Revelation 5:5-10, 13). 

Not only will "that" generation praise the Lord, but:- "Let the heavens rejoice, and let the earth be glad; let the sea roar, and the fullness thereof.

Let the field be joyful, and all that is therein; then shall all the trees of the wood rejoice before the Lord: for He cometh, for He cometh to judge the earth: He shall judge the world with righteousness, and the people with His truth." (Psalms 96:11-13).

Rabinowitz says, "do you know what questioning and controversy the Jews have kept up over Zechariah 12:10: "They shall look upon Me whom they have pierced." They will not admit it is Jehovah whom they have pierced. Hence the dispute about the whom: but do you notice that this word is simply the first and last letters of the Hebrew alphabet-Aleph, Taw?

Do you wonder, then that I was filled with awe and astonishment when I opened at Revelation 1:7-8, and read these words of Zechariah quoted by John: "Behold, He cometh with clouds; and every eye shall see Him, and they also which pierced Him;" and then heard the glorified 

Lord saying, "I am the Alpha and Omega"?

As W. F. McMillen, D. D., points out, Jesus is to return as King of Israel, being the son of David. As such He shall rule Palestine "from sea to sea" (Psalms 72:8). As the son of Abraham He shall rule the world "from the river (Euphrates) unto the ends of the earth". (Psalms 72:8).

He who was born King of the Jews and died King of the Jews without sceptre or crown, now comes to take up His long delayed rule, not only as King of the Jews, but King of all nations. Queen Victoria, with both Cross and 

Crown in mind, expressed the hope that she might be the first ruler to lay an earthly crown at His feet. "And the voice of weeping shall no more be heard in her (Jerusalem, Palestine) nor the voice crying (at the Wailing Wall). "And the rebuke of His people (Israel) shall He take away from off all the earth". (Isaiah 25:8).

The bush shall burn no more. 

It is said that an elderly Jewish gentleman, residing in 

Jerusalem, walks each day to the Mount of Olives expecting that the Messiah, for whom he looks, will first come to that spot in fulfillment of Zechariah 14:4, "And His feet shall stand in that day upon the Mount of Olives."

In so doing he does not know that such an event will also fulfill the prophecy of Acts 1:11, made at the time of the Ascension, nor perhaps, consider the Old Testament prophesies of intervening trouble for Jacob concentrated upon the very place of his present residence. 

To the scene of lowliness and rejection, now comes the

Messiah, as King, the heir apparent to David's throne, His by right of ascension through Divine promise, by right of succession through Mary, and by legal right through adoption by Joseph.

This glorious return, which will cause a nation to be born in a day and change the topography of Palestine (Zechariah 14:4, 5, 10) will register, at last, complete justice for all the inequities of this world. 

The glory of such coming lighted by the glory of the 

Father, is beyond human comprehension. Words, though written across the expanse of heaven in flaming stars, were useless to even initial it. The sum of all the victorious returns of earthly conquering heroes, with all their pomp and plaudits, pales before the glory, majesty and power of this return.

This King of Kings is accompanied by the archangels and "ten thousand times ten thousand and thousands of thousands" of angels (Revelation 5:11). Also, "ten thousands of His saints" (Jude 14) and countless little children. 

Amongst the latter, it is possible Armageddon soldiers may

see resurrected and translated children of their own color, nationality, acquaintance and family. 

The saints coming in this return, including the Tribulation converts, are the last now made first (Matthew

Thus the time for which time was made has come, when "He shall shew, who is the blessed and only Potentate, the

Daniel 7:28 - 

For years I've prayed, and yet I see no change.

The mountain stands exactly where it stood; 

The shadows that it casts are just as deep; 

The pathway to its summit e'en more steep. 

Shall I pray on? 

Shall I pray on with ne'er a hopeful sign?

Not only does the mountain still remain 

But, while I watch to see it disappear,

Becomes the more appalling year by year. 

Shall I pray on? 

I will pray on. Though distant it may seem, The answer may be almost at my door,

Or just around the corner on its way, But whether near or far, yes, I shall pray- 

I will pray on.
