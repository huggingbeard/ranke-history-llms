N' EARLY June sun warmed the Atlantic's balmy breezes, and an exquisite pattern of lace tipped the swells of the sea with breath-taking beauty. A plane soared and dipped like a giant gull overhead, before circling gracefully toward the mainland. 

Marilou Sherman stood on the high Point of Windsor Island engrossed in the matchless view stretching before her, till a low growl from the forest behind startled her. Had she been too bold in venturing to this rocky ledge alone? The growls came nearer and suddenly a large shepherd dog emerged from the thicket, followed by a man. Instantly Marilou saw that the man was blind; his magnificent dog in harness was leading him. "I'm only an intruder - not an enemy!" the girl exclaimed, and the dog barked. "Good!" retorted the man, silencing his companion with, "Quiet, Trusty!" "Your dog is a beauty, but he acts as if he might be dangerous." "Trusty is a female, as are most Seeing Eye dogs. She serves as my eyes, guard, companion and friend. You surprised her, for visitors seldom come out here." "I've been promising myself this excursion for months. I had to come. And now I'm fascinated by the splendid view; but I didn't expect to meet anyone."

The man tapped with his hickory cane upon several rocks. 

When he found one to his liking, he smiled and said, "Welcome!

Have a seat. It's the most comfortable I have to offer, under the shade of a fine old elm. I'm always glad to have visitors, for now 

I can sit awhile and talk with you, whereas otherwise I have to dream alone when I find the time."

Altogether, it proved to be a happy and exciting day, in spite of the signs of destruction that gave cause for solemn considerations. 

The next morning Marilou had an early call from Miss Tinson. "Judge Garrison wants to see you," she said.
