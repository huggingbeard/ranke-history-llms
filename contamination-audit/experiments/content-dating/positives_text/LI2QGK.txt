PREFACE 

This volume contains 48 maps and charts pertaining to the characteristics and distributional patterns of population and housing in Philadelphia and its immediate environs. Originally prepared by the Commission for use in its own work, these maps have now been brought together and published in a single volume in response to a constantly recurring demand for such information by other agencies and individuals who likewise are concerned with planning for or servicing the same area. 

Although most of these items are based upon data from the 1940 Census or other sources of approximately the same date, they still represent the most reliable information that can be obtained on a strictly comparable basis to give a fairly complete and comprehensive picture of the area as a whole. It is believed that they may serve many useful purposes in current research before they can be replaced by corresponding data from the 1950 Census, and that their utility may continue as a basis for comparing data of 1940 and 1950 to determine the changes occurring during the current decade.

Because the significance of these maps to any particular agency will depend primarily upon the specific interests of that agency, no attempt has been made to supplement them with any interpretive text - which could not possibly be designed to meet the needs of all. In general, however, they provide a background of basic information which should be useful in almost any study requiring a clear picture of the spatial patterns and ecological relationships of population and housing throughout the City or within its component communities and neighborhoods. Because these maps are of uniform scale and comparable design, they show at a glance what might be less apparent in tabular data. 

This series of maps was developed by Harlin G. Loomer, Sociological 

Planning Analyst of the Planning Analysis Division, who was assisted in the preparation of the report by other members of the Division. 

The Commission wishes to encourage the use of its materials by other agencies of kindred interests and offers, in addition to its publications, the facilities of its library and data in its general files to any concerned with the well-being of the Philadelphia community. Permission to reproduce these maps for display or publication may be obtained by application to the City Planning Commission. 1
