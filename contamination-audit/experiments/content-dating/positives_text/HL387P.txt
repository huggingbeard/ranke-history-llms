The following pages comprise the FOURTEENTH ANNUAL REPORT of the Court of Common Pleas of Allegheny County, Fifth Judicial District of 

Pennsylvania, for the calendar year 1977. 

Statistics contained herein demonstrate that the Court has managed to keep its lists of pending cases from rising appreciably despite increases in cases filed and a reduction in judicial manpower resulting from unfilled vacancies. 

During the year, judges and court administrators from many other jurisdictions throughout the country have visited our Court to observe innovations in procedures which have become commonplace in Pittsburgh. 

Still other advancements, including the conversion to a One Day/ One Trial Jury System, should lead to further efficiency in the coming year.

THE COURT OF COMMON PLEAS OF ALLEGHENY COUNTY ANNUAL REPORT 

71 1977 One of the obligations of the Court is to inform its judges, its staff and the general public as to the status of its operations. Each year the Court seeks to do this by publishing an Annual Report. This FOURTEENTH ANNUAL REPORT covers the 

Court's activities in the calendar year 1977. 

The work of the Court in Pittsburgh has come increasingly into prominence among judicial circles throughout the nation because of its reputation for instituting innovative practices and procedures without a significant increase in either judicial personnel or staff.

Indeed, during 1977, the National Center for State Courts sent a task force to our Court for a two-week period to observe new procedures used here as part of the Center's Project to Reduce Delay in the Courts. Pittsburgh was one of only a few cities chosen for such site visits. Additionally, there have been visits to Pittsburgh by judges and court administrators from jurisdictions in Michigan, Maryland, Ohio and others.

Each year the administrative staff of the Court has worked to improve the record keeping abilities, including the accuracy of data supplied not only in the following pages but on a more frequent basis to the judges of the Court to aid in the Court's management throughout the year. 

The increased accuracy of the data will bear fruit further in the Court's ability to analyze its own operations in the future. 

CIVIL DIVISION 

The number of general trial cases placed at issue in the Civil Division during 1977 rose for the fourth consecutive year, numbering 4,644 as compared to 4,444 during the previous year. Similarly, the number of cases added to the trial list from the arbitration program rose from 554 in 1976 to 849 in 1977. Thus, even though the Civil Division disposed of 272 more cases in 1977 (4,106) than the previous year, the inventory of pending cases increased to 6,240, or 563 more than at the end of 1976.

This is reflected in the fact that the average age of all cases disposed of in 

1977 was 16.5 months, an increase of 1.8 months over the average age in 1976. Still, the 16.5 month average age is one of the lowest of any metropolitan court in the nation. Furthermore, changes in assignments and procedures to be used during 1978 in the handling of the caseload in the Civil Division are expected to meet the slight increases noted and, indeed, may well reduce those measurements by the end of the year. -9-

CRIMINAL DIVISION 

The Criminal Division managed during 1977 to further reduce its pending caseload. On December 31, 1977, there were only 2,760 defendants pending trial or sentence, 11.6 per cent less than the same date the previous year.

Although the number of cases in which complaints were filed dropped appreciably over the previous year, 537 inactive cases were reactivated from the inactive file for varying reasons. Court officials are pleased that the eliminination of the grand jury did not result in an increase in cases filed as had been predicted by some people.

Meanwhile, the high level of dispositions remains at about the same level as the previous year, resulting in the lower pending caseload at the end of the year. 

An effective measure of the overall efficiency of the Division can best be determined by examining the average time span from filing of the complaint to disposition. In 1977, the average age of all disposed cases was 115 days, three less days than the average in 1976. The average was well within the mandate that all cases must be disposed of within 180 days. 

ORPHANS' COURT DIVISION 

For the second straight year, virtually every category of cases in the Orphans' Court Division increased over the previous year. 

The total number of decrees of distribution dropped slightly from 4,047 in 1976 to 3,856 in 1977. But in the same period, the number of hearings of citations, the number of petitions filed, the number of arguments and the number of hearings on incompetents' estates increased.

The most significant increase came in the number of civil commitments. The number of such petitions presented rose sharply from 851 in 1976 to 1,553 in 1977. 

The number of hearings held in such cases rose to 1,370 in 1977, a total of 222 more than the previous year.

These increases are a result of full operation of the new Mental Health Procedures Act governing civil commitments. One aspect of this Act is the necessity for certifications and recommendations from the Court-appointed Mental Health Review Officer based upon on-site hearings held in each case. These recommendations totaled 

1,018 in 1977, the first full year of this operation. Only 56 recommendations were made in 1976.

Possibly indicative of changing social mores, the number of adoption decrees entered fell although the total of orders of court in this area continued to increase. -10-

FAMILY DIVISION 

The Family Division has responsibility for five general areas of litigation Support cases, Custody cases, Equity cases, Divorce cases and Juvenile cases. The first four categories are handled in the Adult Section of the Division while the other is assigned to the Juvenile Section. 

During 1977, the Adult Section disposed of 7,014 support cases, 5,046 divorce cases, 595 custody actions, 286 protection from abuse cases and 110 equity matters. 

Thus, as of December 31, 1977, 1,162 support cases were pending, and 61 equity matters were awaiting court action. 

As of the end of the year, the scheduling interval between new cases and conference and/or court hearing was two weeks, the interval between filing of new petitions and court hearing was only eight weeks and the interval for pending custody cases was 10 months from filing date.

The amount of money collected and disbursed by the Division in support payments continues to rise, the total being $17,828,895, compared to $16,510,636 in 1976.

During 1977, the Juvenile Section disposed of a total of 2,181 delinquent cases at the intake/probation level and 4,101 at final court hearing. The section also disposed of 870 cases involving deprived children at intake and 704 at final hearing. The latter two categories represent an increase of 345 cases over 1976. 

What must be understood is that each case may require several court hearings and, of course, frequent contacts with court personnel in addition to the judges. 

The section managed, however, to keep abreast of its caseload. A total of 

901 cases were pending at the end of 1977, compared to 1,042 at the end of the previous year. -11-
