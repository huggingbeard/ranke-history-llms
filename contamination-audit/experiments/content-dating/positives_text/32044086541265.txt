PHUNOI AND AKHA TONES By Robert Shafer 

Although the Sino-Tibetan group of languages is best known as tonal, relatively little is actually known about its tones and tonal laws. Within the large group, the tones have been extensively recorded in only two sub-groups, the Sinitic and the Taic. The only languages in the third great sub-group, the Tibeto-Burman, in which the tones of a considerable number of words have been reasonably well recorded, are the Burmish-Loloish. Since of late years tonal languages have been found to occur in other parts of the world, a study of the tonal laws of some of the languages within this best known tonal language group should be of general linguistic interest.

Moreover, Burmese is of importance for a comparative study of the Sino-Tibetan languages because of its slight phonetic degeneration from proto-Tibetan. And Loloish languages supply the key to such degeneration as has occurred in Burmese. 

Consequently the connecting links between Burmese and Loloish are of unusual interest.

Where no tone is indicated, as in words with surd stop finals, the tone may be either mid-level or highfalling (vide L.S.I., v. I. pt. II, p. 29).
