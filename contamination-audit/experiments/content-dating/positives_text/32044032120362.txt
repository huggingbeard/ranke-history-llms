PART ONE THE LANGUAGE OF THE CONSTITUTION 

I. 

IMPORTANCE 

(1) Constitutional law is merely interpretation of the Constitution. 

The first consideration is the exact language of the Constitution. What is not interpretation of specific language in the Constitution, is not constitutional law. For instance, a statement that the statute is unconstitutional because it is discriminatory, without picking the constitutional prohibition against discrimination which is violated, is not constitutional law. The word "discrimination" is not in the Constitution. There are many prohibitions against discrimination. The prohibition involved in this case must be identified. The broadest prohibition against discrimination is in the Fourteenth Amendment, "nor (shall any State) deny to any person within its jurisdiction the equal protection of the laws." One of the most important prohibitions against discrimination is implied from the grant to the Federal Government of the power to regulate interstate commerce. The implication is that no State may discriminate against interstate commerce. The provision in Article IV, Section 

2, that the citizens of each State shall be entitled to all privileges and immunities of citizens in the several States is in substance a prohibition of discrimination against citizens of other States. The provision in Article I, Section 8, that "all duties, imposts and excises shall be uniform throughout the United States" is a prohibition against Federal tax discrimination. The two due process of law clauses contain some prohibition against clearly unreasonable discriminations. These are the principal examples. No two of these prohibitions are construed identically. Therefore, the result and all discussion in a given case depend on which prohibition is involved. 

Again, an attack on constitutionality of a statute on ground of its injustice must be based on a specific clause. Probably this attack is an application of "due process.

Not until the clause is picked can the discussion attain the dignity of "constitutional law."

PART TWO BASIC RULES AND INTERPRETATIONS GENERAL OUTLINE 

(4) The value of any text depends on the order in which ideas are discussed. The key to this presentation is mastery of the basic rules and interpretations next presented, prior to study of the specific clauses in Part Three. This discussion of the particular clauses presupposes more than acquaintance with these preliminary basic rules. 

I. POWERS OF THE UNITED STATES 

(5) Powers Must be Granted. The United States has only the powers granted in the Constitution. All its powers must be derived from the 

Constitution itself. The Tenth Amendment states, "The powers not delegated to the United States by the Constitution, nor prohibited by it to the States, are reserved to the States respectively, or to the people.” 

This is the basic rule, although it is sometimes half hidden by the breadth of the war power in war times and the present broad interpretation of the interstate commerce power and the Federal power to tax. (6) Sovereign Powers. A corollary is that the United States does not have "all sovereign powers." In general, the United States cannot regulate local business. A Federal divorce law for the whole country would be unconstitutional. A Federal statute governing conveyances of realty for the whole country would be unconstitutional. Distinguish, however, a Federal tax on conveyances of realty in the whole country which is constitutional. 

A Federal statute prohibiting horse racing in the whole country in peace time would be unconstitutional. The ground for unconstitutionality in each above case is lack of Federal power (usurpation of State power). (7) Implied Powers: But powers may be implied. 

Section 8, clause 18, says,

Article I, 

"Congress shall have the power to make all laws which shall be necessary and proper for carrying into execution the foregoing powers, and all other powers vested by this Constitution in the government of the United States, or in any department or officer thereof."

The United States power to regulate interstate commerce implies the power to regulate acts outside of interstate commerce which vitally affect interstate commerce. So, a United States statute regulating hours and wages of manufacturers whose goods importantly affect interstate commerce is constitutional, although the manufacture is not part of interstate commerce but a preliminary local act.¹ So, again, the United States Interstate Commerce Commission may constitutionally regulate intrastate rates which are in competition with interstate rates, although its powers are derived entirely from Congress and from the Federal power to regulate interstate commerce.2

(8) Theory of Implication. There may be implied power to exercise an express power by means which are not indispensably necessary. Two express Federal powers, (1) "to borrow money" and (2) "to regulate the currency," imply the power to issue paper money as legal tender. This line of decisions (Legal Tender Cases) shows that Congress can exercise a given power in any reasonably appropriate way. Paper money was one reasonable means of borrowing money. It was not the most obvious means, and the most obvious means-bonds, etc.-would have been effective. Power to regulate interstate commerce implies the power to create a railroad corporation to engage in interstate commerce, although obviously the power to regulate could have been exercised effectively without creating such a corporation. The same power to regulate interstate commerce authorizes the United States to build a bridge over a navigable river. There is an implied power in Congress to investigate in any field reasonably calculated to aid in framing legislation. This power to investigate is much broader than the power to regulate. (9) Regulation for General Welfare. From the power to tax "for the *** * general welfare of the United States" the power to spend the revenue "for the *** general welfare". may be implied, but there is no implied power to control and regulate "for the *** general welfare." The argument that a regulatory Federal statute is constitutional because it is "for the *** general welfare" is, therefore, completely unsound. To be constitutional, the regulation must be within powers expressly or impliedly granted to the United States, such as "interstate commerce" or "taxation." So, the United States has no power to control the school system in a State directly, but it has power to contribute money to the State school systems and to make these contributions depend on certain conditions in the State schools. (See Paragraph 38.) Whether there is any limit on this type of indirect

Federal practical control over local matters has not been decided. (10) Resulting Powers. Certain United States powers are implied by broad deduction from the whole Constitution, rather than specific clauses. The implication is neither direct nor obvious. The clearest example is in the field of foreign relations. The Constitution as a whole shows intent that all power to control foreign relations be in the United States, whereas domestic power is divided between the United States and States. Internationally, the United States is a nation among nations: Domestically, it shares sovereignty. Therefore, the United States does have all sovereign powers as to foreign matters. This is a limitation on section (6) above. 

The statements made in section (6) are absolutely true as to domestic powers. The broad power in regard to foreign relations is called by 

Willoughby a resulting power. So, the United States power to make it a crime to counterfeit foreign securities cannot be directly implied from any one or two specific powers granted. It exists because the United States has all sovereign powers in regard to foreign relations. These powers implied by general deduction are sometimes called resulting powers. 5. (11) Police Power. In the District of Columbia, Maryland ceded its State sovereignty to the United States. The Federal Government may punish local crimes and enforce the Federal Bankruptcy Act. So, a Federal statute regulating all wages and hours in all employments in the District of Columbia is constitutional.

II. POWERS OF STATES 

(13) States have all inherent powers of sovereignty except those denied them by the United States or the State Constitutions. Any State statute applicable within that State is prima facie within the State's powers. It is not necessary to find a grant of power in the State Constitution or the Federal Constitution. The only Federal question will be, does this statute violate some limitation on the State power, such as the due process clause or the equal protection clause? A Federal statute is not prima facie within the scope of Federal powers. The first question presented by any 

Federal statute is, what power of the United States includes this statute.

IV. THREE DEPARTMENTS OF GOVERNMENT 

(16) Separation of Departments. There are three separate and distinct departments of the United States Government: (1) Legislative, (2)

Executive, and (3) Judicial. Each department is given certain checks on the other two, but outside these express checks, no department may interfere with the other two. This separation is not expressly stated in the Constitution. It is implied from the first words of Articles I, II, and III, respectively, which words "vest" the legislative power in Congress, the executive power in a President, and the judicial power in the courts. V. ARTICLE I, SECTION 9, CONTROLS UNITED STATES ONLY 

(21) Some clauses in the Constitution are worded so generally as to seem to apply to both United States and the States, but actually control only one of our two sovereignties. The language of Article I, Section 9, clause 

2, is, "The privilege of the writ of habeas corpus shall not be suspended, unless when in cases of rebellion or invasion the public safety may require it."

Taken out of its setting, this would seem to control both the United States and the States. The context shows clearly that it was intended to limit the 

United States only. Section 9 limits the United States only. Section 10 limits the States only. The grounds are clear: (1) No provisions in Section 

9 expressly limit the States. (2) Many of the provisions of Section 9 could not apply to the States. (3) Several provisions of Section 9 expressly limit the United States only. (4) All the provisions of Section 10 expressly limit the States only. (5) Section 9 says, "No bill of attainder or ex post facto law shall be passed." Section 10 states expressly that no State shall pass any bill of attainder or ex post facto law.

The repetition of the prohibition, this time expressly directed to the States, shows that the States were not controlled by this Section 9 prohibition. 

Therefore, where other provisions in Section 9 are not specifically applied to the States in Section 10, the States are not affected. The suspension of habeas corpus clause does not apply to the States. The only constitutional limit. on the State suspending habeas corpus is that the suspension must not take liberty without due process of law.

VI. THE BILL OF RIGHTS (22) Controls United States Only. The first eight Amendments, the 

Bill of Rights (often described as the first ten Amendments), limit the United States only. In many cases the unqualified language would indicate that the provision applied to both the United States and the States, but actually the States are not limited because historically the sole intent in adopting these Amendments was protection against the new sovereignty recently created: There was no intent to restrain the State Governments. 

For instance, the Fourth Amendment as to illegal searches and seizures would literally prohibit such acts by either State or Federal officers, but it is construed not to apply to State officers. A search of all the houses in a block by a State policeman without a warrant, based on general suspicion of an illegal act in that vicinity, would not violate the Constitution.16 (23) Civil Liberties. The express protections of specific civil liberties are almost all in the Bill of Rights. These are: (1) Prohibition of laws respecting establishment of religion or prohibiting exercise of religion, or abridging freedom of speech or press or right to assemble and petition government, (2) prohibition of infringement of right to keep and bear arms, (3) limitation on quartering of soldiers, (4) prohibition of unreasonable

VII. RELATION OF BILL OF RIGHTS AND DUE PROCESS 

(24) Due Process Often Protects Same Rights. However, much of the protection against the United States specifically embodied in the Bill of Rights is applied to the States under the provision in the Fourteenth Amendment, "nor shall any State deprive any person of life, liberty or property without due process of law." 

(25) Freedom of Speech and Press Under Due Process. For example, the only provision in the Constitution expressly referring to freedom of speech or press is the First Amendment. This applies to the United States only, but unreasonable interferences with freedom of speech or press by a State are unconstitutional because they violate due process. The word "liberty" in the due process clause clearly includes freedom of speech and press. The protection is only against deprivations of liberty without due process of law. The interferences must be quite unreasonable to violate the 

Fourteenth Amendment. Probably there are borderline interferences with freedom of speech or press which, if done by the United States, would be unconstitutional, but if done by a State, would be constitutional because the specific prohibition in the First Amendment is fuller protection than the general prohibition of denial of due process. (26) Double Jeopardy Under Due Process. The only double jeopardy clause in the Constitution is in the Fifth Amendment and applies to the United States, not to the States. However, any provision for unreasonable double jeopardy violates due process. A State statute providing for an appeal from an acquittal because of errors of law by the court and retrial of the accused, is constitutional.17 A State statute providing for a retrial after acquittal at the option of the district attorney would be unconstitutional, whether Federal or State. The unconsitutionality of this practice in the Federal court is obvious as it is double jeopardy, and even in the State court such double jeopardy would be sufficiently unreasonable to violate due process and to deny equal protection. We could not assume that the State's attorney would always exercise his discretion in exactly the same manner, based entirely on the facts of the specific case, because he is not a judicial officer and his obligation does not extend that far. 

(27) Impartial Jury Under Due Process. The specific requirement of 

"an impartial jury" in criminal cases applies only in the Federal courts. 

However, due process connotes a jury that is not clearly partial. A conviction. by such a jury in a State court would be unconstitutional. It probably requires 

17 Palko vs. Connecticut, 302 US 319, 82 L Ed 288.

clearer partiality of a juror to violate due process in the State court than to violate the specific impartial jury requirement in a Federal court. 

(28) Notice of Accusation Under Due Process. Due process requires that the defendant in a criminal case be advised of the nature and cause of the accusation. This specific requirement applied to the United States by the Bill of Rights is about the same as the requirement in the State court under the due process clause. (29) Confrontation Under Due Process. In the Federal courts the defendant has the express protection "*** the accused shall enjoy the right *** to be confronted with the witnesses against him." Generally. it would be unconstitutional to deny this confrontation in the State courts because it would violate due process, but reasonable exceptions could be justified in the State courts more easily than in the Federal courts. 

(30) Right to Counsel Under Due Process. In the Federal courts the defendant has the express protection of the right "to have the assistance of counsel for his defense." In a State court denial of counsel for defense would violate due process. The protection in the Federal court is presumably greater. For instance, it has been held that appointment of a defense attorney three days before the murder trial in a State court in a case in which all events and all witnesses were in the immediate vicinity, satisfied due process.1 18 Such an appointment only three days in advance of trial should not satisfy the specific protection in the Federal courts. 

(31) Rights Not Under Due Process. Many protections in the Bill of Rights are never included in due process. These protections do not exist against the State Governments. There is no protection against State interference with the right to keep and bear arms. The State may try any criminal case on an information alone. A presentment or indictment in a 

State prosecution is never required by the Federal Constitution. Trial by jury is not necessary to constitute due process. In general, the prohibition against self-incrimination has no application in the State courts. 

VIII. HISTORICAL MEANING OF PHRASES 

(32) Most of the significant words and phrases in the Constitution, especially in the Bill of Rights, were phrases entirely familiar in England and with a definite interpretation and usage in English legal history. Many of them were part of the unwritten English Constitution. These phrases were placed in the Constitution, subject to the well-known exceptions and limitations which had been developed in English history. Here are some examples: (1) Freedom of speech and press does not include freedom to publish libels, blasphemies or indecencies, nor to yell "Fire" in a crowded building, nor to counsel sedition. (2) The direct prohibition of double jeopardy does not prevent retrial in case of disagreed jury, or a nolle pros. before the jury is sworn, or a conviction reversed on appeal and retrial ordered, or mistrial due to death of juryman or judge. In other words, under the English concept of double jeopardy, jeopardy did not attach until the jury was sworn, and was not 18 

Avery vs. Alabama, 308 US 444, 84 L Ed 377.

complete if the trial ended with a disagreed jury, or death of judge or juror, or if a conviction was followed by reversal on appeal. (3) In determining what is procedural due process of law, the fact that the procedure now in question was customary in England means that it was due process. However, the reverse of this is not true. The absence of a precedent for a given procedure is not conclusive to show that that procedure violates due process. If the due process clause barred change in procedure, it would bar all advance. (4) Trial by jury means jury of twelve because it meant that in England. Provision for trial by jury in civil cases is not violated by a directed verdict if a reasonable man could come to but one conclusion on the evidence. Literally, the directed verdict denies trial by jury. The justification for the directed verdict is English practice. Every Englishman was entitled to trial by jury, but this included the practice of directed verdict. (5) The right to an impartial jury in a criminal case does not bar United States employees as jurors. Prima facie, an employee of the opposing party in litigation is partial, but the protection of an impartial jury had been recognized in England for centuries, and yet it had been invariably held that a servant of the Crown was a proper juror in a criminal case. 

IX. RESULT OF UNCONSTITUTIONALITY 

(33) A statute unconstitutional as a whole is a nullity from the beginning. An example is a Federal Statute fixing grounds for divorce throughout the country. This is beyond the Federal powers. It is void even before it is declared unconstitutional by the courts. If a statute is declared unconstitutional because of the facts involved in a particular application of the statute, the statute may be constitutional otherwise. An example is under an assessment for street improvements on the adjoining land at the rate of $2.00 per front foot. This was held unconstitutional as applied to a street railway company which held a long strip of adjoining land only thirty feet wide for its tracks. The ground of the decision was the unreasonableness of the assessment on the street railway as compared to the average abutting owner with full lot depth of 150 feet. This decision did not make the statute unconstitutional as a whole. Any statute which is partially unconstitutional raises the question whether the legislature intended it to remain in effect with the unconstitutional clauses or applications eliminated. Only a person directly affected may attack the constitutionality of a statute. A taxpayer does not have sufficient interest in Federal funds to attack the constitutionality of an appropriation act.

PART THREE SPECIFIC CLAUSES I. THE PREAMBLE 

(34) No power in the Federal Government is derived from the Preamble. The name "Preamble" indicates its introductory and explanatory purpose. It states the purposes for which the Constitution was established. 

Among its purposes were "to insure domestic tranquility and promote the general welfare." This statement of purposes is not a grant of powers. 

The United States can never justify a Federal statute by contending that it 'insures tranquility or promotes the general welfare,' except as discussed hereafter under the Federal power to tax when the phrase 'general welfare' is repeated. (See Paragraph 38.) 

II. DELEGATION OF LEGISLATIVE POWERS (35) Fact-Finding Authority. Section 1 of Article I says, "All legislative powers herein granted shall be vested in a Congress * The word "vested" is construed to mean that Congress cannot delegate legislative powers to others. Congress may provide that certain legislation becomes effective when certain events happen, and may delegate authority to given persons to determine and declare when those events have happened. 

This is not a delegation of legislative power, but merely delegation of factfinding authority. Under this principle some discretion may be delegated, provided Congress exercises the basic discretion. The Interstate Commerce 

Act, providing that railroads shall charge reasonable rates, and delegating to the Interstate Commerce Commission authority to decide what are reasonable rates, is constitutional. (36) Administrative Rules. Also, Congress may delegate power to establish administrative rules and regulations. This is an inevitable limitation on the rule against delegation. If Congress were forced to decide all details, it could not exercise its broad powers. (37) Local Option Laws. Even the delegation of some local decision back to the local community has been attacked as improper delegation of authority. However, such local option laws, giving each community the choice for itself, are constitutional. Local self-government is such an integral part of Anglo Saxon history that this practice constitutes an implied exception to the rule against delegation. To this extent our basic provisions for representative (republican) government may be violated by provisions for literally democratic government (direct government by the people).

(51) In General. The last stated power of Congress is "to make all laws which shall be necessary and proper" for the execution of all powers. vested in the United States. An example is the power to make laws to enforce treaties. Before the Migratory Bird Treaty with Canada, the

(52) Eminent Domain. The important Federal power of Eminent 

Domain is implied from this clause. Eminent Domain is clearly "necessary" and "proper". It is implied with reasonable limitations. These limitations are: (1) The taking must be for a public use. (53) Taking Under Eminent Domain and Police Power Compared. 

When property is taken under the police power there is no compensation to the owner. The nature of the taking is quite different from eminent domain. The typical taking under Eminent Domain passes title to the 

United States or its assignee, such as a railroad obtaining its right of way.

(65) Contract Subject to Exceptions. Contracts are subject from creation to various exceptions, express or implied. The corporation charter is a contract with the State, but is subject to an existing statute providing for amendment. All property and contracts are from inception subject to reasonable exercise of the police power. All rate and price contracts are subject to the State police power to control rates and prices if the business is imbued with a public interest. So, A contracts with B to supply B with electricity at a certain price for ten years. During that period a Public Utility Commission is established to fix rates and it fixes a different rate. The new rate applies. The change in rates is not unconstitutional as an impairment of contract because the contract was always subject to such rate control. If there is a contract between lawyer and client for the prosecution of the client's claim against the State and a provision for 20 percent contingent fee to the lawyer, a subsequent State statute appropriating money to pay the claim and providing that no attorney shall receive more than 10 percent for the prosecution of this claim, is constitutional. From the beginning, the lawyer's contract for a contingent fee was subject to the inherent power of the sovereign to refuse to pay a claim, and the corollary power to pay it on any condition, reasonable or not. The condition on this payment is the 10 percent limit on fee to attorney.

XIV. TREATY POWER (67) Under Article II, Section 2, clause 2, the President has the power to make treaties "by and with the advice and consent of the Senate." The basic power is not vested in the Senate but in the President. The President has the exclusive power to conduct negotiations. Such advance consent is clearly constitutional. The power of the Senate is not legislative power which cannot be delegated. So, this objection to advance consent fails. XVII. FIRST PRIVILEGES AND IMMUNITIES CLAUSE 

(73) Definition and Exceptions. There are two "privileges and immunities" clauses. The first one, Section 2 of Article IV, states "citizens of each State shall be entitled to all privileges and immunities of the citizens of the several States." So, a State cannot require from citizens of other States a license fee to engage in business as a real estate agent, higher than is paid by local citizens. There are four well recognized exceptions to the ruling: (1) Political privileges, such as voting. (2) Quasi-political privileges, such as practicing law. 

(3) Property rights of the State. 

(4) Reasonable procedural discriminations.

The first two exceptions are obvious. A State may provide that only citizens may vote or practice law. The third exception means that if the privilege involved is the use of State property, the exclusion of outsiders from the use of State property, is a normal incident of ownership. The "privileges and immunities" clause was not intended to restrict the State as a proprietor, but only as a sovereign. A citizen of Ohio owning land in Ohio could exclude citizens of other States from that land. When the State of Ohio owns land it has this same power to exclude citizens of other States. Constitution controls the State as a sovereign interfering with our affairs: it does not control the State as an owner of property, enjoying that ownership. So, 55

54 Williams vs. North Carolina, 317 US 287. 87 L Ed 279.

Similar charges for hunting and fishing privileges have been sustained, based on the State's quasi-property in wild animals and fish. An example of the fourth exception, reasonable procedural discriminations, is the common requirement that if a non-resident sues he must give the defendant security for the costs of the suit. (74) Corporations. Corporations and aliens are not protected under the privileges and immunities clause. They are not citizens. The word "citizen" in the Constitution does not include corporations except in the "diversity of citizenship" clause. A State may exclude any foreign corporation from doing business in the State unless the business is interstate commerce. XX. GUARANTEE OF REPUBLICAN GOVERNMENT 

(77) The guarantee to each State of a republican form of government raises a political question, not a judicial one. The guarantee is enforced by Congress, not by the courts. In general, the republican form of government is a representative government as distinguished from a democratic government. That is, the people do not rule directly, but rule indirectly by electing those who rule. It has been contended that State provisions for a referendum, therefore, violate the guarantee of republican government in giving the people this direct power. The Supreme Court refused to dispose of the point, on the ground that Congress had final say on this political question.

XXI. SUPREME LAW OF LAND 

(78) The provision that the Constitution, the United States statutes and treaties shall be the supreme law of the land means (1) that a Federal statute or treaty nullifies an inconsistent State statute either prior or subsequent, and (2) that between inconsistent Federal statute and treaty, the later in time, whether statute or treaty, nullifies the prior. There are two limitations on the rule that a treaty is of equal dignity with a statute as law: (1) A treaty providing that the United States pay money requires an appropriation of the money by Congress to authorize the payment. This is based on the provision in the Constitution that "all bills for raising revenue shall originate in the House of Representatives." (2) The treaty must be within the proper field of treaties. There is practically no authority on this, but clearly a treaty between the United States and Canada that provided the grounds for divorce in the two countries in all cases would be unconstitutional.

XXII. FREEDOM OF SPEECH AND PRESS 

(79) In the First Amendment, freedom of speech and press "does not permit publication of libels, blasphemies or indecent articles or other publications injurious to public morals or private reputation." A Federal statute punishing interference with the draft in war time involved conflict between the war power and freedom of speech. To the extent necessary to the exercise of the war power, freedom of speech may be controlled.

(88) The next provision is against double jeopardy. It does not mean that a man can never be tried twice in the Federal courts. The exceptions and limitations developed in English law are adopted as part of the phrase.

XXIX. DUE PROCESS (92) The Two Due Process Clauses. The Fifth Amendment provides that the United States cannot deprive a person of life, liberty or property without due process of law. The Fourteenth Amendment places the same restriction on the States. The interpretation of the two clauses is identical, so the two due process clauses are discussed here together. The due process clauses protect all persons. This includes aliens and corporations. (93) Life, Liberty or Property. The first element in a violation of the due process clause is the "life, liberty or property" of which one is deprived. 

These protected elements must be defined. "Life" requires no definition. "Liberty" includes not only freedom from imprisonment or other physical restraint, but also includes freedom of speech and press, religious liberty, liberty to contract and enter gainful occupations, to meet and to parade (but the type of protection in the various fields of liberty is different). "Property" is liberally construed. A contract is property of the contracting parties. So, while the provision against laws impairing the obligation of contract applies only to the States, the due process clause protects contracts almost as broadly against the Federal Government. The State cannot "impair the obligation." The United States cannot "deprive" a contracting party of his property in that contract. So, the repeal of Federal laws relating to veterans' insurance was unconstitutional as applied to outstanding policies.69 The statement, often made, that the United States may impair contracts is not accurate, because the contract is property and is protected by the due process clause. Distinguish the point that although the repeal of the veteran's insurance law was unconstitutional, the United States cannot be sued without its consent, and a statute allowing a suit against the United States is not a term of any contract, but merely the grant of a revocable privilege. The courts could not aid the veteran if the statute did not give him. power to sue the United States. (94) The Deprivation. The second element to be considered under the due process clause is the depriving. If one's life, liberty or property is unreasonably affected, but not to the extent of depriving one of life, liberty or property, the due process clause is not violated. So, reducing a statute of limitations period for suit on a right is not depriving the plaintiff of that right if a reasonable period is left for suit.70 A statute reducing the period for suit from six years to three years, but allowing suit for at least one year from date in all cases, is constitutional. Such a statute passed in June 1940, without the one-year proviso, would be clearly unconstitutional as to a claim which arose in May 1937, or even in July 1937. As to the July 1937 claim, it would leave such a short time for suit that it would practically 'deprive the plaintiff of his claim. A procedural change may deprive one of his property or claim, as where it bars or practically bars recovery, but many procedural changes do not in any sense destroy the claim or property. These may be applied retroactively. A change in the rules of evidence may deprive one of the parties of property although not applied retroactively. So, a

(97) Exercising Powers to Which Property is Subject. If contract or property is subject to a power from inception, the subsequent exercise of that power does not violate the due process clause. All contracts and other property are, from the beginning, subject to police power. Therefore, the subsequent exercise of the police power by the State is constitutional, although there is a prima facie taking of the property. All contracts and property are also subject to certain Federal powers, such as, (1) control of money, (2) bankruptcy, (3) control of interstate commerce, and (4) the inherent power of sovereignties to refuse to pay their lawful obligations. Here are examples in order of each of these points: (1) Federal statute reducing the gold content of the dollar is constitutional, although it reduces the true commodity value of a prior private bond or annuity contract.75 (2) A mortgages his farm to B. A United States bankruptcy statute during depression allows 

B to redeem and escape further obligation on payment of the true value of the farm, although less than the debt. This is constitutional.76 (3) A railroad settles claim of one injured, by a contract for life pass on railroad to injured party. Later Federal statute prohibiting passes to those not employed by railroads, is constitutional.77 (4) A lawyer contracts to prosecute a claim against the United States on a 20 percent contingent fee agreement, and the United States appropriates money to pay the claim with a provision that no attorney shall receive more than 10 percent. This is constitutional.78 

In each case the contract was subject to the prospective statute, and the subsequent statute did not take any property. (98) Due Process in Procedure. The next question is the definition. of due process. It is a most elastic phrase. As to procedure, it requires an opportunity for hearing, reasonable notice, actual or constructive, of hearing and its purpose, and conformity to settled usages and procedure. Due process does not require trial by jury. So, Federal disbarment or contempt proceedings without a jury, are constitutional, although the act of the defendant may also amount to a Federal crime, the direct trial for which must be by jury. Such disbarment or contempt proceedings would not be within "criminal prosecutions" under the Sixth Amendment.

Due process requires that a court have jurisdiction. A personal judgment must be based on personal service on party or his agent appointed to be served or voluntary appearance, with the following exceptions: (1) A State may provide that its own citizens, while out of the State, may be served constructively. (2) A statute may provide that a citizen of another State driving his automobile on State roads impliedly appoints a certain local officer as the owner's agent to receive service in any suit based on the operation of that automobile in this State.79 Judgment in rem based on jurisdiction over that res, with only constructive service on the party, is due process.

Due process requires an impartial, but not necessarily a learned, court. 

The Constitution does not attempt to guarantee any standards of intelligence or education in State judges, but it does prohibit such conditions as the

The phrase "due process" suggests that the clause is a protection against improper procedure only. 

This is not true, as many cases already discussed illustrate. Where the procedure is quite proper, but the substance involves an improper deprivation, the statute is unconstitutional. The situation commonly raised when a 

State statute is attacked as a violation of the due process clause in its substance, is that the attacker relies on the due process clause, the opponent relies on the police power of the State to which the property and liberty was always subject, and the attacker answers with the contention that this is an unreasonable exercise of that police power. A State fixing rates so low that property is confiscated, violates due process. So, a State Rate Commission's decisions are subject to this partial review to see that certain limitations have not been exceeded, but not to consider the Commission's exercise of discretion within the limitations of reasonableness. (100) Reasonableness.. The last element in due process to be considered is reasonableness. Due process prohibits unreasonableness. Also, the principal answer to due process, police power, is limited by reasonableness.

XXXI. TRIAL BY JURY 

(105) Definition. The requirement of jury trial means a jury of twelve persons. This requirement in the Sixth Amendment, stated to be "in all criminal prosecutions" is construed not to apply to prosecutions for breaches of minor municipal ordinances. So, the Constitution does not require jury trial in the prosecution for breach of a parking regulation in the District of Columbia. The requirement of an impartial jury in criminal cases is not violated by jurors who are employed by the United States.88 

Of course, an employee of one's opponent in litigation, in general, is not impartial, but in early English criminal law, although the requirement of an impartial jury was recognized, servants of the Crown were competent members of criminal juries. The phrase "impartial jury" was borrowed from 

English legal history with its accepted limitations. (106) Waiver. Clearly, trial by jury may be waived in all cases except capital offenses.89 It is contended that trial by jury is an integral part of the framework of a criminal trial, as much as a judge on the bench. The opposition admits that a defendant could not waive 'a judge,' but contends that a jury is only a protection to the defendant. It is held to be not an essential in the framework, but merely a protection to defendant. The right to trial by jury may be waived in whole or in part. So, a waiver may make valid a verdict by eleven jurors. Probably trial by jury cannot be waived in capital offenses by analogy to the rule that a defendant cannot plead guilty to first degree murder.

XXXVIII. EQUAL PROTECTION 

(113) Who is Protected. The equal protection clause is the principal prohibition against State discrimination. It protects "any person within its jurisdiction." This means that a foreign corporation is not entitled to equal protection until it is properly doing business within the State. The clause prohibits unreasonable discriminations. (114) The Discrimination. The equal protection clause does not attack all types of injustices: It only attacks inequalities. One well treated by a statute may complain because a few are treated. even better, although the vast majority are treated worse than the objector. So, a State realty tax provision that all coal land should be assessed at $100 per acre is attacked by the owner of coal land worth thousands of dollars per acre, on the sole ground that there are some coal fields even more valuable than his which obtain an advantage over him from this low flat assessment. His contention is sound. The statute is unconstitutional as to this coal land owner, although he is better treated than the vast majority of land owners. He is denied equal protection as compared with the few. If an appeal of man convicted. and in jail is suppressed by jailors, due process is not violated because it does not include an appeal, but there is a denial of equal protection as compared with a man out on bond who could press his appeal.95 (115) Discrimination in Administration. Although the wording of a statute creates no discrimination, if the administration of it shows intentional discrimination against some class without reasonable grounds, there is a denial of equal protection. A city ordinance requires those operating laundries in frame houses to obtain consent of certain authorities. These authorities, in giving and denying consent, clearly and intentionally discriminate against the Chinese. The Supreme Court found a denial of equal protection.96 The discrimination produced by different enforcements of laws by different officials, involving no intent to discriminate, is not a violation of equal protection. Two judges sitting at different times in the same court may be, respectively, severe and mild in sentences imposed There is a clear discrimination against the one sentenced by the severe judge, but the sentence is constitutional. This injustice is inherent in any human machinery of government. Similarly, two policemen may have different policies as to automobile speeders. One may arrest every violator, the other may arrest only flagrant violators. There is no violation of the 

Constitution. Distinguish the situation in which police act together to be severe on all Negro violators. Here there is a denial of equal protection. 

(116) Definition of Protection. The next question is: What is this protection which must be equal? Excluding Negroes from the only State law school has been held to be unconstitutional as a denial of equal protection.97 This makes the privilege of studying law not a mere privilege, but an essential to one's protection. (121) The Fifteenth Amendment does not guarantee the right to vote to the Negroes. PART FOUR CONCLUSION XLI. Common Sense Interpretation of Constitution 

(123) Throughout Constitutional Law the approach to every question is common sense, non-technical and non-mathematical. An engineering degree is a handicap to the student of Constitutional Law. A difference in degree often makes a difference in kind. In Constitutional Law most situations presented to a court are neither black nor white. They are some shade or tint of gray in between. The line of demarcation between the valid and the invalid cannot be exact. Literally two concepts contradict each other but both are accepted as valid. The power to exclude a foreign corporation is not completely exhausted when the corporation is once licensed to do business in the State. But the corporation once in the State is entitled to equal protection. Mathematically this means (1) the State can discriminate against this corporation but (2) the State cannot discriminate against this corporation. 

Nevertheless these antagonistic principles are accepted. The result is a compromise. A reasonable increase in the license fee for the foreign corporation is constitutional. Here are three applications of common sense reasoning. (1) The due process clause does not protect interests that are not vested in the owners. For instance an interest to run from the grantor's death subject to power of revocation in the grantor, will not be protected against a future inheritance tax law passed before the grantor dies. Nevertheless an ordinary contingent remainder in real property is sufficiently vested under the due process clause to be protected. The technical real property distinction between vested and contingent is ignored.110 (2) The privileges and immunities clause prevents discrimination against citizens of other States. 

Corporations are not citizens. Therefore a foreign corporation may be charged a higher license fee than local corporations. An ordinary partnership is not a separate entity: legally it is the two or three citizens who are members of the partnership. So, the foreign partnership is protected against such higher license fee. The common law trust is between the partnership and the corporation. From a technical viewpoint it is more like a partnership: it has no separate entity. From the commonsense viewpoint it is more like the corporation. In number of members the common law trust is similar to a corporation. The common law trust and the corporation serve the same practical purpose, providing a form in which many people may own and operate a business together. The Supreme Court held that the common law trust was not entitled to privileges and immunities.111 (3) Tangible property is taxable at its physical situs. Intangible property is always taxable at the domicile of the owner and is also taxable in other States with color of jurisdiction. X domiciled in New York has paper money in a safe deposit box in Pennsylvania. The question is which State may tax. 

If the paper money is tangible property only Pennsylvania may tax. If it is intangible property clearly New York may tax. Any textbook on economics will make paper money tangible property, a chose in action. Practically we do not use it as a chose in action, a claim against the government. We use it as a 110 111

XLII THE COMMONEST ERRORS 

(124) This is a negative chapter, a recital of a few of the common ridiculous errors. The statements in red are the inexcusable mistakes.

Error No. 1. A State statute providing for a jury of six is constitutional because a jury of six satisfies the constitutional requirement of trial by jury. 

Comment: Two errors-1. The word "jury" in the Constitution means jury of twelve. 2. "Trial by Jury" provision is in Bill of Rights and controls

United States only. States are not bound. Correct result reached by two errors which offset each other.

Error No. 2. A United States Statute taxing all mortgages which are to run more than five years is unconstitutional because it denies equal protection to long term mortgages, which is unreasonable discrimination. 

Comment: Three errors-1. Equal protection clause controls States only. 

2. Omission of due process clause as a reason. The statute is probably unconstitutional as taking property without due process. The only direct decision is that such a State statute is unconstitutional as denial of equal protection. 3. Omission of direct tax clause as a reason. This is apparently an annual tax. If so it is a direct tax which obviously will not be apportioned among the States in accordance with population and is therefore unconstitutional. If the tax is applied once only to the execution of the mortgage, this direct tax objection is avoided. The tax is then an excise tax on the act and not a direct tax. 

Error No. 3. A State statute providing that "any newspaper may be suppressed for publication of a libel of a State official unless the defamatory statement is true and published for a proper purpose," is unconstitutional because it violates the "freedom of speech and press" clause. 

Comment: Two errors-1. The "freedom of speech and press" clause does not restrict the States. This clause is in the First Amendment. The 

Bill of Rights does not apply to the States. 2. Omission of due process clause as a reason. The due process clause in the Fourteenth Amendment provides that "liberty" cannot be taken without due process. "Liberty" includes freedom of speech and press. This interference is clearly so unreasonable that it violates due process. 

Error No. 4. A corporation is not entitled to "privileges and immunities" under Article IV, Section 2 because the corporation is not a person under this clause. 

Comment: Two errors-1. The reference to "person". The clause says "citizens" are entitled. Therefore the only question is whether a corporation is a citizen. A corporation is not a citizen under any clause in the Constitution except 'diversity of citizenship' clause. 2. A corporation is a person.

If the word person had been used in this clause, corporations would have been included. A corporation is a person. generally. 

112 Silberman vs. Blodgett, 277 US 1, 72 L Ed 749.

Error No. 5. A Federal statute destroying existing contractual retirement rights of United States employees is unconstitutional because it impairs the obligation of contract.

Comment: Two errors-1. Only the States are prohibited from passing laws which impair the obligation of contract. The clause does not apply to the United States. 2.. There is no mention of the due process clause which actually applies and makes this statute unconstitutional. 

A contract is property under the due process clause. 

Error No. 6. A State statute providing that a judge may charge the jury to consider defendant's failure to take the stand in a criminal case, when applied to a crime committed prior to statute, is unconstitutional because it violates the constitutional immunity from self-incrimination. 

Comment: Two errors-1. The self-incrimination clause is in the Bill of 

Rights and does not control the States. 2. The ex post facto clause is not mentioned. This clause is violated by this retroactive procedural change which makes it easier to convict. 

Error No. 7. State statute providing that defendant in a criminal case, after an acquittal may be retried, is unconstitutional because it violates the double jeopardy clause.

Comment: Two errors-1. The double jeopardy clause applies to United 

States only. 2. Omission of due process clause as a reason. This double jeopardy is so unreasonable that it clearly violates due process and is unconstitutional.
