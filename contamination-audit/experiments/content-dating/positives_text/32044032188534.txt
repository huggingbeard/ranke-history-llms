A FACTUAL STUDY OF THE OFFICE OF THE CLERK OF THE UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT FOR THE DISTRICT OF NEW JERSEY ENERAL IV. Composition of the Office of the Clerk 

The office of the Clerk of the U. S. District Court for he District of New Jersey is located in the U. S. Post Office nd Federal Court Building in downtown Trenton. The building s approximately twenty-five years old and is in satisfactory epair. The office of the Clerk, the chambers of the Judge, he courtrooms, including one used for Bankruptcy hearings and he Referee's office are located on the third floor. The hambers of the Judge, staff offices and office of the Referee n Bankruptcy are well furnished and present a dignified tmosphere. The office of the Clerk, while sufficiently large or current operations, leaves much to be desired with respect o equipment and furniture.

This office is organizationally two separate operations. he Clerk of the Court as managing director for the entire istrict maintains his office in the space provided. This also erves as the office for fiscal operations for the entire district nd as records depository for completed and closed cases for the istrict, including records of closed proceedings of the U. S. ommissioners. his office.

Personnel records are also centrally located in All statistical reports are submitted through the Office of he Clerk, where consolidation, arrangement and other appropriate ctions are performed. The Clerk and Financial Deputy make up hat might be termed the Headquarters of the Office of the Clerk f the U. S. District Court for the District of New Jersey. -4-

PERSONNEL 

Personnel records and related personnel management activities are performed within the Headquarters. A thorough examination was not made of the individual personnel files, but the Clerk indicated in an informal conversation that all essential personnel documents such as oaths of office, position authorizations, notifications of changes in grade and salary, etc., were maintained. These files were not maintained according to accepted personnel file practices, that of separating and filing permanent personnel records on the right-hand side of the folder, chronologically under an Acco Fastener and temporary records in like manner on the left-hand side.

Position descriptions indicated too much emphasis on routine and clerical duties and little or no stress on the responsibilities that make a position difficult or complex. 

Examples of duties performed were noticeably lacking. No reference was made to the relationships between the position assignment and the over-all responsibilities of the office of the Clerk. No reference was made to relationships and daily contacts between incumbents and the public, press, the Court, lawyers, litigants and representatives of other Governmental agencies.

MORALE 

In the interest of brevity, the evaluation of morale is consolidated into this simple statement for the entire District. 

The attitude of each and every member of the staff of the Clerk: was friendly, enthusiastic and cooperative. All personnel exhibited a desire to share responsibilities and to perform duties beyond their appointed assignments. There was an obvious reflection of excellent leadership and supervision throughout the entire organization.

PRELIMINARY CRIMINAL PROCEDURES 

Prior to indictment there are many preliminary criminal oceedures. Most of these apply to apprehension and appearance efore a United States Commissioner for preliminary examination. 

1. Individuals arrested upon a complaint of any person are presented without delay before the nearest

U. S. Commissioner or other officer empowered to commit persons charged with offenses against laws of the United States. Persons arrested without warrant are presented without delay before a commissioner or other officer, where a complaint is filed. 

2. The commissioner informs the defendant of the complaint against him, of his rights to retain counsel and have a preliminary hearing. The defendant is also informed that he is not required to make a statement as it might be used against him. The defendant must be allowed reasonable time and opportunity to consult counsel and post bail. 

3. The defendant is not called upon to enter a plea. 

The defendant is held over to answer in the district court when a preliminary examination is waived. When the defendant does not waive examination the commissioner shall hear the evidence within a reasonable time.

Defendant may cross-examine witnesses against him and may introduce evidence in his own behalf. The commissioner from the evidence presented may hold the defendant to answer in district court or discharge him. The commissioner sets bail for defendants held over to answer in district court in accordance with the Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure. After the proceedings, the commissioner transmits all papers related thereto and any bail taken by him to the Clerk of the district court.

ADVANTAGES OF CENTRALIZED FINANCIAL CONTROL 

The Financial Deputy works directly for the Clerk of Court at Trenton. She is a grade GS-9. Her duties include accounting, auditing, certain disbursing duties and the preparation of financial reports. 

This is a full-time responsibility. Centralization thus relieves the deputies-in-charge of each vicinag of fiscal responsibilities and results in more accurate, timely and effective accounting as well as ease of preparation of financial reports. This seem like an economical operation for New Jersey or other organizations of comparable size and structure. 

Another advantage of central control is the facility for preparing consolidated financial reports require by the Administrative Office of the United States Courts and those required by the Immigration and 

Naturalization Service of the Department of Justice.

PASSPORT PROCEDURES 

At each of the vicinages the clerk's office processes passport applications. This is not a full-time responsibility and varies with the seasonal travel requirements. In New Jersey the Newark vicinage has the largest number of applications. During the heavier periods as many as 50 to 60 passport applications are processed daily. 

1. Passports are issued on application to persons who are citizens or who owe allegiance to the United States. 

2. Applications are filed with the office of the clerk of the court. 

3. Applicants must furnish proof of citizenship or allegiance. Several forms of such proof are acceptable. 

4. Passport clerk is required to review citizenship evidence and authenticate documents submitted. 

5. Certain special requirements govern the issuance of passports to women citizens married to aliens. In such instances the clerk must review and authenticate the supporting documents. 

6. Photographs conforming to certain specific specifications must be submitted with the application. 

7. The identity of the applicant must be established by one of several means set forth in passport instructions. 

8. Applicants whose business is in connection with the performance of a contract agreement with an agency of the U. S. Government must submit a letter indicating the organization by which employed, position occupied, destination, purpose of travel and proposed length of stay abroad. -43-

RECORDS AND PROCEDURES 

1. 

Pre-sentence investigations 

Pre-sentence investigations are made by the probation service in criminal matters prior to the imposition of sentence or the granting of probation. Investigations are not made until the defendant has pleaded guilty or has been found guilty. Based upon these conditions and at the order of the court, the Probation Service: a. Interviews the defendant and obtains personal history information by either having defendant prepare local Form No. 1 or by the Probation Officer preparing it upon information obtained during the interview. b. Subsequent to the interview the Probation Officer handling the case makes personal reference checks, visiting and interviewing members of the defendant's family, friends, past and present employers, educators, clergy, and other persons acquainted with the defendant. c. Arranges the pertinent details in form of a report to the Court handling the case. Such reports are made without recommendations but do indicate whether the Probation Officer feels that the defendant "would" or "would not" be a potential probationer. -53-

2. 

Sentence or Judgment a. In instances where the defendant is interned, one copy of the pre-sentence investigation is sent to the 

Bureau of Prisons, Washington, D. C., and another to the institution of correction in which the defendant is incarcerated. b. Defendants granted probation in lieu of internment report regularly to an assigned Probation Officer, who follows the personal progress of the probationer. c. The Probation Office in cooperation with the U.S. 

Attorney, assists in collecting fines and restitution payments assessed as a part of the original judgment. When necessary, arrangements are made with the Court to review judgments and make appropriate adjustments. d. Collections of fines are submitted daily to Trenton. 

Collection of restitution payments are mailed directly to the person, organization or agency to which the Court order directed it to be paid. 

Checks or money orders must be made payable to the cler or other designated recipient as fines and restitution payments are not accepted by the Probation Office.

Parolees a. The institution in which defendants have been interned reports to the Probation Office respecting the progress of the prisoner and date of release. b. Split Sentences 

By order of the Court, sentences may be split requiring the defendant to serve part of his sentence in prison and other part under the supervision of the Probation 

Office. 

Military Cases 

The Probation Office in cooperation with the military services supervises military parolees and reports to the releasing service as to the progress of the parolee. 

Prior to releasing a military prisoner the military service furnishes the receiving Probation Office with a pre-release report. This report provides basic personal history information on the prisoner, including offense, term and condition of parolee and address of next of kin or address of relative to which the parolee intends to return.

Juvenile Delinquency Act 

In administering the provisions of this act, considerable discretion must be exercised by the Probation Officer as a defendant in the 17-year range may be subject to both this act and the Youth Correction Act. Generally, the provisions of the act most suitable to crime involved are applied. -55-

Records and Reports a. After initial reference of cases by the office of the Clerk of Court, complete records are prepared and maintained by the office of the Referee until the cases are closed. Closed files are sent to the office of the Clerk of Court in Trenton. In the Referee's office complete dockets are prepared for each case and are filed in filing trays. 

Index cards are also prepared and maintained by the surname of the Bankrupt or the corporate or partnership names. The clerk of court also maintained a docket on each bankruptcy case. 

This docket shows the initial filing, reference, all proceedings on review or on appeal, the termination, and the receipt and disbursement of all monies for the Referees' Salary and Expense Funds, the Clerk's fees and Trustee's fees. The cross-index cards maintained by the Clerk's office show the court, number of the case and the name of the Referee to whom the case is referred. The practice of keeping duplicate bankruptcy files within the office of the Clerk with its resultant workload of filing original petitions, schedules and other related papers is a matter for future consideration, bearing in mind that organizationally the chief clerk and clerks of the Referee's office should be under the supervision of the Clerk of the Court. b. All docket entries at Newark and Trenton were typed.

In Camden, however, a streamlined procedure was introduced using rubber stamps with standardized entries. This seems to be a time-saving method for simplifying docketing. Unlike the docketing procedures in the office of the Clerk, the Referee's office used a numerical control for each docket entry. This seems to be an effective control device for insuring that all documents are entered and accounted for in any review of a case file. -57-

The multitude of forms in use in bankruptcy matters is staggering. While an examination of more than

Workload 

The hearing of bankruptcy matters in all three Referees' offices seemed to be most expeditious. The average number of cases heard varied from 15 to 30 per day, largely no-asset cases. New Jersey was noticeably effective in the trial of bankruptcy matters and in the administrative management of related proceedings preceding the actual hearings.

XI. OFFICE OF THE UNITED STATES MARSHAL 

A. 

Disbursing activities only

Since the office of the United States Marshal is under the jurisdiction of the Department of Justice the only activities performed by that office for the courts which relate to this study involve disbursing activities. As shown under Fiscal 

Procedures, the Marshal receives an allocation of funds from the Budget Officer of the Administrative Office of the U. s. 

Courts and within the limitations of this allocation disburses funds accordingly. The range of these disbursements involves pay of salaries and expenses, including travel and subsistence, of Judges, their staffs, Referees, the Clerk, Reporters, Probation Officers and other supporting personnel of the courts. 

Disbursements are also made from appropriated funds for emergency services, repairs, etc. In addition the fees and allowances of jurors are also paid. 

While the efficacy of disbursing by an agency outside of the jurisdiction of the Administrative Office of the U.S. Courts may be questioned, this survey revealed a smooth-working relationship between the Marshal's Office and the Clerk's Office His fiscal clerk consumed only part of her time to accomplish her disbursing duties. Payments were made promptly. Essential reports were prepared. Adequate financial controls were kept. 

No apparent advantages could be ascertained from any change in present arrangements. On the whole the Marshal's office did a commendable job. -60-

II. Functions and Objectives of the Office of the Clerk of Court: 

The office of the Clerk of the Court serves as the administrative and financial center for the district court and associated or supporting activities. The diversity of responsibilities and duties of the Clerk's office are wide and varied and affects the lives of many of our citizens and prospective citizens. 

Accuracy and promptness in performing the several responsibilities of the Office of the Clerk is fundamental to the prompt and proper administration of justice.

To illustrate it might be noted, that by rules the Office of the Clerk is required to be open at all times for the receipt of filings which might as the result of delay adversely affect a litigant in his substantive rights to a just, impartial and speedy hearing. In fulfilling this requirement the Clerk or his designated representative must be available and able to accept filings, appraise such filings and arrange, when necessary, to deliver such papers to a Judge for decision or action. Orders so issued must be properly recorded and urgent action required thereby instituted.

In urgent as well as routine filings the Office of the Clerk of the Court institutes original process by issuing subpoenas, summonses, attachments, monitions, citations, warrants, etc.

In the issuance of process, accuracy in the selection of the instrument and promptness of service is mandatory. Failure on the part of members of the Office of the Clerk to meet the above conditions or requirements could result in the imposition of a judgment against the Clerk for damages equal to that sought by the injured litigant.
