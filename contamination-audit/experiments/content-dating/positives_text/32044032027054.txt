INTRODUCTION a. The Nature of Government Contracts. 

A contract made between the Government and a private person has characteristics which distinguish it from a contract made between two private persons. First, the Government contract must stem from, and meet requirements established by statute; second, the procedures established for the enforcement of the contractual liability of the Government are limited; third, the nature of the law of contract applicable differs, for the courts in construing Government contracts are not bound by the law of the jurisdiction in which the contract is made or in which it is to be completed, but are guided by the Federal Common Law respecting Government 1/ contracts; fourth, a person in contracting with the Government is dealing with a dual personality, both contractor and sovereign.. 

Nevertheless, the legal principles applicable to Government contracts are substantially the same as the principles applicable to contracts between private persons. In an equity suit, for instance, the Government is subjected to the same pre- 2/ sumptions of law and fact as an individual. 

When the Government enters the domain of commerce, however, it cannot be held liable for an obstruction to the performance of its contracts resulting from its public and general acts

A contractor may seek relief directly in the courts if the contracting officer fails or refuses to render a decision 23/ or if the department head or his under the disputes article, duly authorized representative merely affirms the contracting officer's decision without making an independent review. e. Procedure before the Board of Contract Appeals. 

Taking an appeal to the Armed Services Board of Contract Appeals is a relatively simple matter. If the contractor is not satisfied with the written decision of the contracting officer, he may direct his appeal to the Secretary. All the contractor need say is that he appeals from a decision of the contracting officer who denied his claim and to contend that he is wrong. 

After the appeal is filed, the contracting officer is required to gather together into a file all the documents and papers relating to the dispute, consisting of a complete copy of the contract, a copy of the notice of appeal, a copy of the contracting officer's decision, and copies of correspondence between the parties bearing on the matter in dispute. The file is forwarded to the A.S.B.C.A. by way of the legal staff of the department.

The Decision Making Procedure of the A.S.B.C.A. 

The charter establishing the Armed Services Board of Contract Appeals specifies that the Board shall be composed of three panels, that is, an Army, Navy, and Air Force panel, each consisting of members trained in the law. Each panel will be responsible to the Secretary of the Department for which it is authorized to act. The member of the Board or panel who presides at a hearing is assigned the task of writing a decision in accordance with his judgment. His proposed decision is then submitted to the other members of the panel who may adopt the decision as written or suggest revisions. 

Once the decision is agreed to by any one panel, it is submitted to the chairmen of the other two panels. If the chairmen, after a study of the decision, conclude that it is correct, they indicate that a review by the full board is not required. The decision then becomes the final decision of the Armed Services Board of Contract Appeals. In the event that either or both of the other two panel chairmen do not concur in the proposed decision, the decision is submitted to every member of the Board for review. If the Board cannot agree, a split decision results and the majority will prevail. After a decision 14
