FOREWORD 

Sectarianism must die that religion may live. It must be destroyed that man may have a more abundant religious life. Every sect-Christianity, Judaism, Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism, Shinto, and the rest-must be merged in or replaced by a great world religion. 

Humanity, as it scales the heights of the centuries, needs to look to the ladders by which it climbs. Incompleteness, flaw, decay, obsolescence, always threaten. 

No institution is free from them. They elude the indifferent, the careless, the uncritical, as well as the ignorant. They are more elusive in nonmaterial than in material fields. They are most elusive of all in religion. Yet it is in religion that people are least critical.

Religion, in spite of its kindly attitude and stupendous power, is but somewhat beneficent. Waste of religion's potential beneficence is humanity's greatest and most inexcusable loss. Religion is by its very nature the most helpful cultural area. Given half a chance, its potential helpfulness would become actual. 

Indoctrination in childhood prevents religious people from detecting adulteration and blight. Evils are ascribed to the depravity of man rather than to foreign elements and disease within religion itself. 

Sectarianism is the outstanding characteristic of that which goes by the name "religion." It produces and maintains great numbers of sects, self-centeredness, astounding range of quality even within a sect, slavery to the past, intersect jealousy and conflict, and impairment of the reputation of religion. In the atomic age, these things are deadly beyond expression.

Religion is being eaten away and disfigured by the deadliest of all diseases-sectarianism. This cancerous disease so numbs the mind and befuddles its victims 3

that they do not realize how deathly sick they are. 

Sectarians actually believe that they are religious, but in the long run are even more destructive than nonreligious and antireligious environmental forces. Organized religion is rotting at its core, and the rot has begun eating into the real essence of religion. Religion's first great task is to set its own house in order by casting out or transforming sectarianism. 

Sectarianism parasitically consumes time and wealth given by good people in the name of religion. Its ethics include low and degrading content from primitive and barbarous days. It restricts intelligence and retards the search for new truth and new ways of life.

Sectarianism divides people into mutually exclusive groups and rigidly holds them there. It hamstrings the development of wholesome world relations as it stresses localism so strongly that the world remains split into a large number of competitive and hostile sectarian groups. Each group refuses to accept the global point of view or be congenial to the organization of a new religion which would do so. 

People are going to be religious. In a civilized country they have a right to expect a religion relatively free from superstitious ignorance. Sectarianism is so rampant in even civilized countries that superstition is greater in this field than in any other. The very conceit of civilization makes it harder for people in civilized countries to realize that in religion they are still primitive and barbaric. If superstition were isolated, its consequences would not be so tragic. Its prevalence, pervasiveness, and prestige enable it to spatter other institutions grievously with its murky mire. 

Purification of religion-getting rid of manifest and manifold adulteration and blight-would be of incalculable benefit to both the religious and the nonreli-

gious. Religion would then be geared to its greatest task-the achievement of all-pervasive, worldwide brotherhood.

If sects were teachable, they would provide a productive beginning of a much more wholesome individual and world religious life. Their destruction is inevitable unless they show greater willingness to transform themselves into, or allow themselves to be transformed into, a thoroughly wholesome religion, worldwide in scope.

The decision to destroy, or even to merge, one's own sect is a very hard one. Most religious people have a fondness for and a fear originating in their sect. 

Fondness and fear beget a strong desire to keep sectarianism in spite of its faults and its folly. 

Destruction of sectarianism is but the beginning of the religious task. The truly religious elements in each sect must be united in a really universal religion. A positive, forward-looking, sound, universal religion must take root even as sectarianism is uprooted. It is the greatest need of our time. Cultural advancement has proceeded far enough to make such a religion possible.

World Brotherhood is the great religious need, yet religion moves but slowly toward brotherhood. A house divided against itself finds it even harder to go forward than to stand. Religion is so thoroughly divided between pursuit of brotherhood and claims of theological institutionalism that the former finds the latter a ball and chain about its leg at every step. 

Each church and church-related institution goes on the uncritical assumption that its soil and climate are peculiarly favorable to brotherhood. Even when a church believes strongly in brotherhood, it feels that the best route to brotherhood lies through its institutional channels.

Altruistic as well as selfish churches spend vast amounts of time and energy in activities designed to promote their own sectarian characteristics. It is perfectly evident to the outsider that a vast proportion of these activities do not promote brotherliness. Many of them are in effect downright unbrotherly. Any informed person who listens to religious programs on the radio for a single week knows that much of this religious propaganda promotes institutional ignorance and superstition far more than it promotes brotherhood. come. 

Most religious people still look upon religion as primarily a getting rather than a giving. Some think primarily of the benefits of this life-consolation and protection from the thousand ills that flesh is heir to. 

Some think primarily of a better status in the life to Some think strongly of the benefits both here and hereafter. The majority are not moved primarily by organized thoughts at all-they are controlled by feeling. They are comforted by or enjoy personal contacts, music, ceremony, ritual, sacraments—in short, the old familiar atmosphere of their church. It seems like home to them.

Sometimes the self-centered attitude embraces an individual only. Frequently it takes in the family, and, perhaps, a few other people. Basically, in any case, it is religion for what "I" or "we" can get out of it rather than a broad sympathetic earnestness to serve men. 

Even when organized religion urges its members to do a good turn for Samaritan strangers, it suggests that they will get an extra star or two for their crown. 

So long as organized religion caters to the "get" rather than to the "give" psychology, it will remain unable to transform either itself or its adherents into genuine brothers of one another, much less into brothers-atheart of all mankind.

Transition from sectarianism to real religion is one of the greatest of all privileges. People fail to realize this because their religious personalities are so starved and cramped by sectarianism that they fail to see beyond, much less want to get out of, their sectarian pens and styes. The better elements of their natures and the civilized parts of their cultures rebel against the narrowness and imbecility of their sectarian world. 

Confirmed sectarians, unaided, seldom break their chains. Neither human nature nor civilization is sufficiently strong and vigorous to break habits which have been formed in childhood and hardened through the years. Unfree themselves, sectarians damn their children and society to a life of intellectual slavery, emotional warp, and superstitious ignorance. Genuinely religious people are free intellectually and emotionally to explore the whole of human culture. They may accept from any source. They live, and train their children to live, fully. They broaden the horizon and elevate the tone of society. 

Religion is in essence the fairest and the finest of all things-brotherhood. Brotherhood is the core of the new religion. It is an element in all mature re ligions. Transition from sectarianism to Brotherhood should not be unduly difficult save for the very ignorant or the very selfish.

Transition from sectarianism to religion is one of the very highest duties. Few if any duties outrank it in rightness or service. Sectarianism is so evil by nature and so pernicious in practice that its destruction is the great aim of all enlightened men who love their fellows.

Morality is human and humane so cannot tolerate the nonhumanity and inhumanity inseparable from sect. Morality shares with religion the obligation to

reveal sectarianism in its true colors. It is in duty bound to do what it can to destroy sectarianism that real religion may live, grow, and work effectively with 

God in the service of man.

All religious people-organized and unorganizedare invited to cooperate in getting the new World Brotherhood movement organized and under way. 

Those who cooperate are constructive builders in time and in eternity.

GLOBAL RELIGION 

VS. 

SECTARIAN SELF-CENTEREDNESS 

Humanity is of one blood. All men belong to but one human family.

Humanity is of many cultures-economic, political, social, and religious. 

Cultural diversity has blinded men to their unity and led them to destroy one another by war, persecution, starvation, and every other means. Until culture is redirected to reinforce rather than disrupt unity, men will continue to slay one another and to justifyeven glorify the various cultural loyalties out of which murderous hatreds and conflicts arise. Until the inhabitants of each oasis of culture come to value the broad field of humanity more than they value their own oasis, each oasis will still make war on other oases. 

As global contacts multiply, only unity can keep destructive conflicts from multiplying.

The basis of global unity must be psychic. Unless varied peoples of the world begin to feel as one-that they are much alike and have much in common-stable global political or social organization can never be a reality. Organizations will fail because they are not -psychically-together. The United Nations will not be really united.

Religion by its very nature is better suited than other cultures to unite men. Religious sects, nevertheless, are perpetuating deeper and more permanent divisions among men than any other culture, not excepting nationalism. Religion is man's best friend.

Sectarianism is his deadliest enemy. The most urgent task is to transform sect into-or supplant it by global Brotherhood. 

The vital need of global unity is recognized on every hand, but the relation of sect and religion to this unity is ignored. Failure to bring sect and religion into line with global unity is a particularly great world menace. 

It is an incessant menace so long as religion remains out of line with global unity. 

Religion Should Promote Global Unity 

Religion which is worthy of the name will unite men. 

In the present world the unity will be global. 

Global unity which all thoughtful, right-minded people desire is essentially religious in nature. The unity presupposes common interests and common needs. It presupposes common effort to serve the interests and meet the needs. It presupposes a common sympathy and mutual good will. It depends on harmony, order, oneness.

Global unity being essentially religious, one would expect religion to have a permanent place in the plans of those who are in charge of global strategy. Contrary to expectation, religion has been neglected. In seeking bonds to bind men into global unity it is the forgotten tie.

When leaders do refer to religion in connection with global unity, reference is generally adjusted to sectarianism. This is true of political and social as well as of religious leaders. Not that the leaders always take sides. Far from it. Frequently the reference is supposed to be unsectarian in that it treats all sects alike. 

The tendency, nevertheless, is to strengthen sect. 

When the British Prime Minister praised intersect cooperation, then urged each sect to be true to its own fundamental tenets, he tried to ride horses going in 10

opposite directions. By far the stronger horse was the sectarian one. Cooperation between sects is adjusted to fundamental tenets rather than the other way around. The center of gravity remains self. 

When the President of the United States spoke affectionately of "Holy Writ" in one of his fireside chats he may have been totally oblivious to the sectarian nature of the term. To Christians, the Bible is holy. 

To nonChristians, on the contrary, the New Testament is a sentence to eternal damnation. Its central thought is that hundreds of millions of Chinese, Indians, Arabians, and other nonChristian people are hopelessly sinful here and without hope hereafter. 

There is not a chance to convince the hundreds of millions of "heathen" that the Bible is a holy book. 

Acceptance of such a doctrine is too high a price to pay for global unity. "The heathen" will not pay it. The term "Holy Writ" tends to deepen sectarianism among Christians and nonChristian sects. 

Nearly every nonChristian sect has its own "Holy Writ" which provides the best if not the exclusive religious way. Leaders in each sect tend to polarize religion around its Holy Writ. In doing so, they deepen sectarianism. In this way, leadership in various lands uses religion to defeat rather than to promote global unity. 

To say that leadership is too narrow, too easy-going, or too hypocritical to accept a global in place of a sectarian point of view is not to tell the whole story. 

Even politically wise and conscientious leaders are at sea. They regard the global religious question as too hot to handle. The best they can conceive of is cooperating sects-with each sect determining the extent of its cooperation. 11

Excessive sectarianism now is comparable to exces sive nationalism at the close of World War I. Leaders knew that excessive nationalism divided the world and that it ought to be curbed. But the issue was so hot that they organized the world without any real attempt to control excessive nationalism. As a result of this side-stepping of the hot issue, so much nationalistic heat was inclosed within the League of Nations that the League was consumed by it. 

Leaders of the United Nations publicly acknowledge that nationalism must be curbed or World War II will soon be followed by a third. Leadership is now acknowledging publicly what intelligent leadership knew all along that excessive nationalism and world unity cannot dwell together. 

Intelligent leadership knows today that excessive sectarianism and global unity cannot exist together. 

Yet that leadership is too timid to try to curb sectarianism. It is following the path of least resistance today and ignoring the day of reckoning. Already there is angling to line up certain sects on one side or other of the political fence, thus strengthening the bargaining position of sectarian leaders. Planners-even global planners—are trying to tie sects as sects into the plans, thus ignorantly or mischievously defeating the very idea of global unity. Religion, because it is dominated by and catered to as sectarianism, fails in its most essential task-to promote unity. 

Failure of religion to shake off sectarianism and to promote global unity is particularly deplorable in view of the nature of religion-real religion-in character. 

Religion is, by its very nature, universal. It centers in man's relation to the universe-the whole of it. Its outlook is broad. It must be congenial to man's adjustment to the universe everywhere. It is broad 12

enough to include all, yet considerate enough to find a place for each.

Religion is closely bound up with ethics and morals. 

The three are social in nature. Like ethics and morals, religion is as wide in extent as social relationships. 

Many of these relationships are global. Although harder to identify and appreciate than local relationships, global relationships are nowise less religious. 

There is no reason biological or, in the long run, cultural, for great multiplicity of sects throughout the world.

Beneath varied cultures of different peoples, there is an innate nature which is very much alike from people to people. The white, black, yellow, or brown baby is not at birth predisposed to any sect. The white child will accept Confucianism or Buddhism as readily as the yellow child. The yellow child will accept Christianity or Judaism as readily as the white. The identification of race and religion has been accidental. There is no biological reason why any race should continue the religion which history happened to leave upon its doorstep.

Religion, to be effective, must be such that it can adjust itself especially in the long run-to fundamental innate needs. Innate needs are nearly or quite universal, so that the religion which fits those needs will tend to be universal. Such a religion will necessarily promote global unity by being, in its essential nature, very much the same throughout the world. 

Religion and the general culture of a people are so closely inter-related that it is sometimes supposed that each great cultural group has the religion best suited to its needs. Religion and general culture, in reality, reinforce each other so that a group with a biased general culture would be better off if it had a religion 13

which tended to counteract rather than to reinforce its general culture.

There is no great people today which would not gain much if it could trade some features of its own religion for those of other religions. In this way it could build up a more desirable general culture as well as a more desirable religion. In some cases the general cultural pattern of a people needs changing, and such changing would indicate religious changes are needed to counterbalance the general culture. 

Humanity is tending steadily, though slowly and unevenly, toward a common culture. This fact proves the desirability of a tendency toward a common religion. 

With essentially the same biological inheritance, reinforced by increasing cultural likeness, men of all lands will be able to worship as one.

Biological and cultural differences within each people, rather than among peoples, do require some differences in religion. Uncultured people of low intelligence and cultured people of high intelligence do have in part a different approach to religion. The difference, however, is one of degree. Both groups may be served within the framework of the same religion. 

Great multiplicity of sects is bad for everyone, including religion itself. It is bad in the local community, on the world front, and for personality.

The presence of many sects in a local community tends to fix attention on sectarian differences. The fundamental religious attitudes and activities common to all excite little comment. They are the responsibility of all in general and of no one in particular. Sectarian differences, on the contrary, invite comparison and comment. These are the things which tend to come to mind when the public or members think in terms of organized religion. 14

Half a dozen or more weak churches struggle along in a small community, using up most of their energy in just keeping alive. They have so small a supply of energy left that they can do but little to create a better community. If all the energy were concentrated in a single church, keeping alive would be no task at all. 

Most of the energy could be used to bring about varied and significant improvements. Religion would then be the real community leader shaping will, thought, acts, and institutions along spiritual lines. Community rather than sect would be the horizon. 

On the world front, sectarianism is worse even than in local communities. In many parts of the world one religion-sometimes two closely related religions-has the field pretty much to itself. Most adherents of the prevalent religion tend to identify their one sect with religion. Other sects are but a poor imitation of religion if indeed they are not dangerously irreligious. 

To many sectarians-particularly the devout onesother religions are but wolf-devils masquerading in the clothes of religious sheep. The sectarian Christian damns nonChristians with the one word "pagan" and is sure he has spoken the final word. In the meantime, the nonChristian damns Christians with a similar epithet. Even when the strict sectarian is not frank enough to say so openly, he condemns in his heart. 

A broad world culture is impossible so long as narrow sectarianism maintains its present power. Unless world culture is cramped into sectarian molds and bears sectarian imprint, sectarians prefer their sectionalism. 

In the final analysis, sect is placed over against humanity, and sectionalism over against the world. The strict sectarian always chooses sectarian nationalism or sectionalism and uses the rest of the world to serve its purposes. 15

Personality shows clearly the food on which it feeds. 

It requires variety as well as good quality. Much of the food of fine personality is found in the religious field. Where a society is cut into numerous sects the religious food in each is likely to be skimpy. If intersectarian relations are bad, even such religious food as exists is likely to be scorched by conflict. 

Adherents of each sect avoid the good as well as the bad in competing sects. Having imputed inferiority to certain others, the entire pattern of religious life of the others must be distrusted. It may shield hidden as well as evident dangers. In such an atmosphere, well-rounded personality cannot grow. Each personality is shut and stunted within its own prison walls. 

Personality is culturally created. Different sects tend to create different types of personality. Once created, the different types interpret life differently. 

They, therefore, misinterpret one another. Partly because of different natures and partly because of their attitude toward one another, different types of personality find it very difficult to harmonize sufficiently to promote world order.

To a shrinking world, potential clash of personality has extremely serious implications. Personal contacts of leaders and other inhabitants throughout the world are becoming very common. If personalities are too dissimilar, contacts are likely to breed distrust rather than good will. Nothing is so appropriate a creator of worldwide desirable personality elements as a religion of brotherhood. If a world personality is to emerge, its core should be brotherhood.

Religion-real religion-recognizes type of life. It does not recognize race, class, or nationality. It, therefore, is suited to an extension as broad as humanity itself. It abhors exploitation of the weak-be it na- 16

tion, culture, or individual-by the strong. Imperialistic exploitation, which has done so much to dwarf culture and degrade personality, is none the less antireligious when practiced by Christian nations. Religion demands that every man shall have his opportunity to achieve whatever the world has to offer. 

Religion-if genuine is guided by the principles of justice and mercy. These are essentially global rather than local.

Righteousness, too, tends to be global in nature, yet most sects interpret it in such a way as to fit their particular theology or way of life. In giving righteousness a meaning based on localism, sects hinder the religious promotion of global unity as well as of righteousness.

Religion tends to promote stability. International relations have been highly unstable. If the stability natural to and characteristic of religion were extended to the international field, this extension would be an important contribution to global unity. 

Religion is interpersonal. It is concerned with the relation of every person to every other person. No person is or at least ought to be-beyond the religious tie which binds. 

Religion is by nature strongly human. It has, or should have, the human touch. Basically there is a great deal of likeness in human beings the world around. Religion, if it followed its human impulse, would tend to permeate every corner of the globe. Since human beings have so much in common, religion ought to bind them together cordially. Sects are now trying to prevent this permeation and unity unless through sectarian channels. Religion ought to burst sectarian bonds to become global. 17

Sectarianism Dominates and Deadens 

Mankind is being crucified on the cross of sectarianism.

Sectarianism tends to organize as many new sects as possible. The dividing line between a few sectarians and others may be ever so inconsequential but the few insist on setting up a separate group and going their own way-sweet or bitter as the case may be. Once organized as a separate group, with a set of straightlaced leaders, every control known to man is used to keep members inside the organization. This is the reason the world has so many sects that it is impossible to count them.

There is a tendency for adherents of each sect to continue to teach that it is the best religion-in many cases the only true religion. There are two very grave objections to this point of view. 

The basis on which the assumption of each group of adherents rests is false. The real reason why most adherents belong to a certain religion is accident of birth or association. A person tends to belong to the religion to which his parents belonged or to which he became attached by attending its services in early youth. 

There was no critical choice of one religion as against another. There is no reason for the members of each group, therefore, to believe that its religion has more validity than other religions. 

One of the most prevalent as well as dangerous fallacies in the field of religion is the view that there is virtue in one believing one's religion is superior to others. The real test of the quality of a person's religion is the willingness eagerness to get the best religion rather than to hold on unquestioningly and tenaciously to that which one accepted uncritically in earlier years. 18

A second grave objection to the adherents of each religion teaching that it is the only real religion is the tendency of this teaching to divide men. Since religion is such a serious matter, there is a strong tendency to feel drawn toward those in one's own religious circle. 

As the members of each religious group are drawn closely together, they are made sectarian in sympathy. 

One of the greatest tragedies of modern life is the fact that religions-even those which give a permanent place to the concepts "love" and "fellowship"-awaken distrust in people of various religions for one another. 

While each religion unites its own members, it-often inadvertently-turns its members away from, and even conditions them against, members of other religions. 

Society is cut into segments among which there is isolation or antagonism. A great and well-nigh impassable gulf is created.

Worse still is the tendency of the members of one sect to regard themselves as superior because they look upon their religion as superior. An antisocial superiority complex is created. If this sense of superiority were confined to religious matters, the effect would not be so regrettable. The superiority complex carries over into other fields of culture. Since it is a feeling rather than a judgment, it has great driving power. 

It is hard to combat. 

There is a tendency for a sect that considers itself superior-particularly if the sense of superiority amounts to exclusiveness-to trust to its religious leaders for leadership in nonreligious as well as in religious fields. The more zealous or even fanatical a religious leader, the more highly he is valued by sectarians who have been thoroughly conditioned.

Intolerance by individual members or by group attitudes may intensify division and distrust, but the real 19

trouble is more fundamental. It arises from the fact that a group which has a sense of superiority is not hospitable to unity-even psychic unity-with other groups. Intensity of love without breadth of horizon draws groups of men away from one another. Each group may be a veritable love nest of its own members for one another, but even a large number of love nests do not make for unity beyond the confines of each nest. 

Sometimes the very exclusive intensity of love within each nest may make one even more sensitive to real or supposed errors in, or wrongs by, other nests.

Pernicious effects of sectarianism are heightened by the geographic, cultural, racial, and national division of religions. It is much easier to look down upon "the heathen" if they are of a different place, culture, race, or nationality. Nationalism eagerly seizes upon religious zeal and intolerance to promote its own ends. 

Even though religious attitudes are not consciously promoted, harnessed and used by nationalist leaders, sectarianism firmly if subtly binds sectarians to the chariot of nationalism. The world remains divided into areas within each of which people impute superiority to themselves. Mutual resentment results. World unity is obstructed. Worldwide good neighborliness and friendship cannot grow.

The superiority complex of Christianity is immeasureably increased by the fact that it is the prevailing religion of the powerful nations of the West. It claims and gets credit for much of the highly complex civili zation of Europe and the Western Hemisphere. Strange to say, it is not blamed for the faults of that same civilization.

Islam, Shinto, Hinduism, Confucianism, or other religion likewise tends to reflect and to reinforce the attitude of the cultural and national groups where it is prevalent. 20

If the membership of various religions were thoroughly intermingled geographically, culturally, racially, and nationally, the overlapping would tend to hold the various religious groups together in spite of the dispersive effect of religious differences. 

Sectarians dominate religion in every land. Everywhere religious people are organized on the basis of a certain body of dogma, sometimes of very exact and detailed dogma. In the eyes of strict sectarians, everything lying beyond the circle of the sect is either antireligious, nonreligious, or a very low grade of religious. 

Seldom if ever is the extra-sectarian accorded equal standing with the sectarian.

Public worship and sectarian literature include moral platitudes and admonitions to love and be kindly, but these are taken for granted by religious people. They hardly increase the breadth of, or fervor for, brotherliness. The admonitions result in channeling charity through sectarian agencies. There is serious doubt that they result in raising the general level of moral conduct or in bringing about an over-all increase in the sum total of charitable relief.

Rare indeed is the church or other sectarian agency that concentrates on brotherliness to the exclusion of other things. Few, if any, follow up their advice by a vigorously intensive campaign to see that the agencies of community power incorporate brotherliness into social, economic, and political relations. 

The world does not have today, nor has it ever had, a great broadly religious organization. Large religious groupings have been of single sects, of confederation of sects, or for specific and limited religious objectives. 

The religious, as such, has never organized broadly on a significant scale. 21

Because religion has been organized by sects rather than by the broadly religious, people everywhere have been forced to choose between sectarianism and isolation. Such choice has been neither pleasant for individuals nor good for society. 

People dislike, especially in such an important matter as religion, to be isolated. Rather than experience religious isolation, countless millions have participated in sectarianism so narrow as to grieve as well as warp them. They have chosen-or under pressure have accepted-what seemed the lesser of two evils. 

Other countless millions, who accepted isolation rather than cramp their souls into narrow sectarianism, have suffered double loss. They have been pained by the isolation itself. They have suffered because they have gone without the very great help which membership and attendant fellowship in organized religion can give.

The enforced choice has been as bad for society as for individuals. Those who have accepted sectarianism have used much if not most of their religious energy for narrowly sectarian rather than broadly and deeply social ends. Those who have accepted isolation have withheld from society the contribution they might have made to the power of organized religion. 

In either case, society has been a very great loser. 

In quite a significant sense, the religion of each country has been what the sects and sectarians there have said it was. One had to play on sectarian teams and by sectarian rules or stay off the team of organized religion.

In no country has religion-free and unsectarian religion-had an opportunity to show what it could do. 

The time to give it that opportunity has come. 22

The quality of sectarianism varies sharply from sect to sect, from country to country within each sect, and from social class to social class in the same country. 

Sects and sectarians tend to have in common, nevertheless, some very distinctive and pronounced characteristics.

Sects are so very highly self-centered that they put self first-tend to measure everything in terms of self. 

This is true even of sects which try to be, and think they are, very broad and altruistic. 

Each sect, believing its religious system the most important element in the religious life of man, is so strongly subjective and biased that objective measurement is impossible. Each sect is actually ignorant of the fact that it is a prisoner within its own shell. 

Blinded by its own self-centeredness, it can see nothing better than itself. It has little incentive therefore to seek to improve itself by learning from outside sources or from its own experiences.

Like-minded sectarians, associating with one another, deepen and strengthen their peculiar attitudes and beliefs. They reinforce the faith of one another in their own system. That faith is made well-nigh indelible by the warm sympathy generated by association. This sympathy is generally far more potent than intelligent analysis or judgment in winning and holding allegiance.

Sects and sectarians are backward looking-oriented to the past rather than to the present and future. Because their origin was in the very distant past, much of their culture would normally tend to be out of date. 

This tendency has been aggravated by the sense of sacredness attached to religious culture. Belief in the integrity if not the infallibility of early as well as later sectarian things causes sectarians to hang on to even 23

that which is obviously antiquated. The tendency to preserve sectarian tradition is strongly reinforced by love and fear-love for what they believe sectarian influences have done for them and fear for the fearful consequences of tampering with the sacred ark of tradition.

The hold of religious tradition is so strong that numerous people who have given up sectarianism believe such tradition is necessary to strong religious conviction. Many nonsectarians as well as sectarians fear that the rooting up of sectarian tradition would destroy the prospect of vigorous religion, organized or unorganized.

This point of view is the erroneous and unfortunate result of sectarian training and sectarian social psychology. It fails to take account of the readiness of people to respond in considerable degree to intelligent and socialized appeals. It stresses fear and other compulsive elements in human behavior unduly. 

Worship of the past and of its authority is inseparable from sectarianism. Escape from the dead hand of the past is impossible for those who owe allegiance to a sect that has been accumulating tradition through the centuries. Intellectually they may be able to do so. 

Emotionally they cannot. When people are ready to adjust their affective nature, their intellectual nature, and their outward behavior solely in the light of the present, sectarianism has vanished. One may sit in the old pew and keep one's name on the church roll for sake of family tradition, but these are but the fossil reminders of a sectarian life that has vanished. 

One cannot be both sectarian and modern, for past and present points of view cannot be harmonized effectively. Those who try to be both, suffer from divided, discomforting, and ineffective allegiance. 24

Sectarianism, having arisen in prescientific days, is full of unscientific elements. Fettered by tradition, it but tardily, timidly, and incompletely attempts to separate the unscientific from the nonscientific. By insisting on speaking in the realm of scientifically demonstrable phenomena where it contradicts proven truth, sectarians discredit religion in whose name they speak.

Much sectarianism which arose in pre-philosophic days and is decidedly primitive still lingers to plague the faithful and disgust outsiders-even the not-toowell-informed or not-too-intelligent outsiders.

Sectarianism, in spite of modern sectarianism's lip service to tolerance, is fundamentally intolerant and cannot be otherwise. It has an exalted opinion of its own importance and is missionary in spirit. It is a light giver rather than a light seeker. Its chief business is to make the other fellow see the light. The essential thing is to make sure that the other fellow gets the light-method is of secondary importance. 

Rising levels of social conscience and propriety no longer permit such harsh and brutal methods as formerly, but in his heart of hearts the strict sectarian is as intolerant as in the days of the rack and the faggot. 

He is as sure as his ancestor a thousand years ago that the heretic is headed for hell. There is danger lest he, consciously or unconsciously, encourage the use of the atom bomb on “heathen” nations.

Only the person who denies the universality of his religion by saying one religion is as good as another can be genuinely tolerant. All great religions today look upon themselves as essentially universal. It is their duty to humanity-and to God if they include belief in God-to root out the other fellow's religion that they may replace it with their own. Recent tech- 25

nique is to graft one's own on to the other fellow's to achieve a quicker growth. The newness is chiefly in method. It does not arise out of basic faith in what the other fellow believes and practices. 

Sectarianism violates the most elemental conception of justice. Some sects look upon themselves as chosen people, the elect, the especially favored. They occupy a special position because of who they are rather than because of what they are. Most sects believe their relationship to a central figure or their practice of a certain ritual is the essence of religion-the source of their superiority. They "stand in" with the Judge of the universe or have bribed Him with their special merit and salvation formulas.

In many sectarian systems there is little connection between ethical conduct and their systems of merit or salvation. Some sects even disparage morality as an aid to salvation in order to stress the saving power of their belief or practice. The idea of the sacrifice of the good to atone for the sins of the wicked is still held among civilized people in spite of the fact that it is wholly unethical and too degrading a system to appeal to fair-minded men, to say nothing of a just and holy God.

Sectarianism creates much of the fear it gets credit for relieving. Each sect defines sin in terms of blind tradition or its own peculiar system. Sins of one sect are often virtues of another. Having set up its own list of sins, each sect threatens those who commit them, thus creating fear in the hearts of sinners and would-be sinners. By offering a means of escape from these sins-sect-created as well as real ones-the sect gains the gratitude of those who regard themselves as saved.

Bases of a thousand fears-mostly groundless-are 26

laid in early childhood. Organized religion is kept busy in later years giving temporary relief to fears which had been so thoroughly instilled in childhood as to become chronic. Every time a child is made to believe a certain thing is sinful a job is created for professional sin removers or, at least, professional teachers of how to avoid getting punished. The concept of total depravity of man which has played so fundamental a part in western religious thought is but a long-drawn-out self-created need for relief plus gratitude to the Reliever. 

Children reared in murky sectarian pools have no more opportunity to know pure religion than tadpoles in the barnyard frog pond have to know clean water. 

They think that to crawl out of sectarian mud is to leave religion.

Sects are seldom thoroughly self-critical, so are slow to detect-much less correct-their own faults. Reluctance to criticise self-save for lack of effective propagation-springs from two sources. There is fear of causing doubt of the validity of one's own system in the minds of adherents. There is reluctance to provide ammunition to competing sects. Weaknesses are passed over lightly while strong points are stressed in intrasect and intersect discussions. Prudence as well as sentiment opposes a thorough-going examination with view to genuine housecleaning.

Sectarianism is the canker-worm eating at the vitals of religion.

Sectarianism divides society into groups which are more or less mutually exclusive. Each sect tends to isolate its membership. The isolation is deceptive because it exists in spite of physical intermingling. As the mind of each sectarian is indelibly saturated with the particularity of sect it is closed to alien and, in many cases, supplementary points of view. That which 27

seems to threaten sect, no matter how true or helpful in itself, is avoided as though it were the plague. 

Sectarianism is fascist by nature-never democratic. 

It does not and cannot believe in equality of sects. 

Convinced of its own superiority, each sect strives for a preferred position. Its program is far more important than the program of other sects. It must be protected from harm even though the personalities and opportunities of its members are destroyed in the process. The more unreasonable and unethical a sect, the more the urge to fascist controls and the less reluctant it is in stooping to use them. 

Sectarians, having become accustomed to fascist behavior in religion, tend toward fascism in other fields of culture. The mind that has been indoctrinated in religion becomes an easy prey to the indoctrinated schoolman and politician. The religious lockstep prepares its victims for lockstep in other cultural fields. 

The roadway between political fascism and religious fascism is not a one-way street. Sectarianism is now the greatest enemy of democracy in personality, state, and society. The danger is far greater where sectarianism is aggravated by the fascist teaching and practice of a sect which is itself highly fascist. 

Sectarianism is so tragically in conflict with accumulating knowledge and pervasive truth that very many sectarians are opposing accumulation of knowledge and spread of truth. When accumulating knowledge negates or seems to negate, sectarian points of view, sectarians disparage knowledge. They fight it lest it destroy their faith, or at least, lest it lead neophytes astray.

Intense sectarianism discourages the truth-first movement. If various sects would put truth first, they would draw nearer one another as they make progress 28

toward the common goal. Sectarianism keeps sects from doing this. It puts its own tradition first. It assumes that everything which contradicts that tradition is untrue. Alien points of view never get a hearing. With each sect feeding on its own tradition there is not variety enough to nourish a broad, deep, vital religion. Sectarian paths lead nowhere in particular because they are built in line with narrow subjective points of view rather than to achieve objective or broadly subjective goals. 

Sectarianism closes the mind. Because it is essentially propagative rather than evaluative it refuses to listen to those who would discuss the possibility of substituting a better religion. Any questioning of its fundamentals by its membership is considered traitorous.

Sectarianism defends itself against attack from any quarter-especially when sectarian weaknesses are being revealed. This defense determines in large measure its activity. Pressing neighborhood problems and broad social goals are neglected while sectarianism beats off critics of itself. Very much religious energy -sometimes far the greater part-is consumed in meaningless sectarian struggles. Energy wasted is so badly needed for the achievement of really religious objectives that its loss is one of the most harmful results of sectarianism.

This wastage of energy on nonessentials is not accidental. The very nature of sectarianism makes the giving of exaggerated importance to nonessentials inevitable and incessant. All modern sects find some place in their system for brotherhood. Since all hold this in common, no sect feels that the achievement of brotherhood is its primary duty. Each sect's primary duty is to save itself pure and unspotted from pollution 29

by the world-including well-meaning but misguided sects. That is, all sects but itself. 

Mutual dumbness is widespread because of sectarian insistence on resisting inquiry. Each sect desires seclusion so strongly that it is willing to pay any price to get it. That price is the withholding of criticism of other sects. "I will not talk about you if you will not talk about me" is the well-nigh universal motto. 

The result is a blind spot-religion-in civilization. 

No other area enjoys quite so great immunity from the light. Under cover of religious and social darkness, each sect peddles and portions unlabelled sectarian patent medicine to its members and to the tiny tots who toddle unsuspectingly into infected sectarian pens. 

The worse the sect, the more it gains by this conspiracy of silence. A sect whose religious culture is high enough to be pretty well abreast of the best elements of civilization would have little to lose from free and frank intersect inquiry and criticism. A sect which is indescribably primitive in its religious life has everything to fear. Even the sect that is most intolerant in a country where it is in a position to prevent investigation and inquiry of itself is the loudest clamorer for "tolerance" in a country where other sects are influential enough to threaten mutual examination.

Religious tolerance, so-called, is known as an expression of religious liberty, but the net result is the enslavement of the minds of children to sectarianism. 

Sectarian control of immature minds is so effective that unilateral religious action is nearly as effective as dictatorial government in preventing religious freedom. If indoctrination has been severe, freedom of choice will be impossible throughout life.

The sectarian never believes in or practices religious freedom. He never wants adherents and prospective 30

adherents to have good opportunity to come in contact with the doctrines and practices of other sects. He never wants opposing views to be presented under as favorable circumstances and in as favorable a light as his own.

Fear, with accompanying pain and feeling of insecurity, is inseparable from and increased by sectarianism. Religions date from the age of fear and anxiety.

In those days man's fears arose in large measure from manifestations of nature which he could neither understand, control, nor guard against. Fear was a strong element in every part of early culture, including religion. 

Rising levels of culture have brought better understanding, control, and methods of guarding against fearful aspects of nature, but religion clings to its fear psychology with blind and cruel tenacity. The more primitive the religion, the more emphasis it places on fear. The more religious and sensitive the sectarian, the deeper the sorrow and pain of seeing loved ones and others bound for the perdition proclaimed by sectarians as the inevitable lot of sinners.

Religion ought to be the greatest of all burden bearers as it lightens for men the burdens inseparable from life. Sectarianism has added far more to than it has subtracted from the fears of life.

Sectarianism obstructs up-to-date, effective methods of social amelioration and social reform. Instead of asking, What is the most effective way to act? sectarians ask, What would Jesus do? How would Confucius, Buddha, or Mohammed handle the situation?

Pacifism in the United States during the two decades following World War I, which disarmed and damned the country to World War II, had its chief origin and richest nourishment in sectarian loyalty. Genuine re- 31

ligion dictated preparation adequate to defend, and the will to defend, human values. Sectarianism dictated adherence to a leader who, centuries ago, had prated of peace through nonresistance. Sectarianism, blind to modern requirements, set the war trap which caught the nation in 1941. It has set and continues to set the stage for countless other misfortunes and calamities. 

Sectarianism will not let men live intelligently and wholesomely. Better by far that it should die than that it should continue to shackle society. 

Sectarianism Abhors Global Unity 

The center of gravity of each sect is sect. This is true of a Christian, Jewish, Islamic, Buddhist, Hindu, Confucian, or any other sect. The general outlook of a sect may be humanitarian but each sect looks upon itself as a peculiarly effective agency in bringing religion to men. The highway of global religious wellbeing is identical with or but an extension of its sectarian path. The effect of this belief of each sect in the supreme importance of itself is to place self ahead of humanity.

Each sect is unique. The center of gravity of each is, therefore, different. As each seeks its own center, it draws away from other sects. The religious life of the world, and even of individual countries, is segmented. Humanity is divided into sections which do not readily understand, love, or cooperate with one another. 

Sectarianism carries a particular procedure. If a sect's procedure were abandoned, its reason to be would Institutionalism dictates, therefore, that the procedure shall be preserved. . It is preserved at all costs-even the cost of religion itself. cease.

Sectarianism deliberately generates a feeling of su- 32

periority. Leaders of each sect are usually successful in instilling a feeling of superiority in the rank and file of membership. This feeling of superiority often extends to nonreligious as well as to religious items. 

It retards unity as the members of each sect hesitate to accept even less to seek-unity with those whom they consider inferior.

All statesmen as well as politicians know that politics and sectarianism are badly mixed. Political unification of India has been blocked indefinitely by conflict between Hindus and Moslems. From the viewpoint of world politics, Turkey's control of Islam is as important as her control of the Dardanelles. Palestine bleeds because sectarian fanaticism and nationalistic politics are jumbled. Communist Russia's ambitions in the 

Near East are promoted by Moslem-Christian conflict. 

Moslem leaders have threatened to start a "holy war" against Western influence of Christian nations and call on Russia for help. Christian leaders are seldom wholeheartedly critical of their country's aggression in non- Christian lands-blessings of Christian culture are thought to outweigh wrongs of aggression. Shintoism is but an accentuated form of sectarian-national tie-up which is widespread, if not universal. International sectarians play the international political game with all the skill of old veterans, and all the success of gamblers with marked cards. 

Sectarians fear the effects of association or even contact with other sects. Association or other contacts may cause members-particularly the impressionable young-of one sect to leave the faith of their fathers and go a whoring after other gods. To guard against such apostasy, each sect builds walls between itself and the world-even religious world-outside. Other sects are taboo. Most sects have ceased to teach that all 33

others are devil worshippers, but strong traces of this point of view remain to plague man's attempt to be religious.

Sectarian walls are particularly effective and permanent because they are composed of emotion and sentiment rather than of intelligence and reason. When one touches them to test their quality, they generate so much emotionalism that intelligent appraisal becomes impossible. Sectarians simply cling blindly, fervently, and proudly to that which they have been taught is the best religion. They will not look over the wall to see what lies beyond.

Sectarianism lags particularly far behind global thought and action because it is rooted in and looks toward the past. No amount of facelifting does away with its tendency to reflect the outlook of yesterday rather than of today or tomorrow. It fails to comprehend, much less to guide, the new global psychology. 

Sectarian reinforcement of the tendency of each people to idealize and rationalize its own culture deepens and widens the chasms which divide men. Sect, which always reflects a people's culture, gives a kind of religious sanction to that culture. The culture comes to be regarded as a chosen culture in the same sense that the people or sect is a chosen people or agency with a divine mission.

Sects try to convert one another. This creates intersect suspicion and dislike. Where sects are identified with different races, nationalities, or cultures, the suspicion and dislike tend to become racial, national, or cultural.

Sect cramps and dwarfs outlook. It is partial and incomplete. Each sect may not include much of the religious life of other sects. Each neglects such of the religious life of mankind as it cannot catch and retain 34

within its own sectarian walls. A sect's understanding is not deep enough and its vision is not broad enough to include the whole of global life. 

Members of each sect see the world, including other sects, through sectarian glasses. Everything is colored by them. Sectarian divisions-with resultant hostility -are a denial of the very principles to which modern religion professes allegiance. 

Sect polarizes leadership. A sectarian leader is expected to give the greater part of time and energy to sectarian matters. Sectarian pressure is stronger where the leader is an employee of the sect. Such leader is under obligation to serve sect because salary is provided by it. Habit is as powerful in this field as in any other. The person who has been employed by a sect tends to be more and more fully transformed into its likeness. It becomes increasingly difficult to be global.

Chained to an authoritative if not infallible source, each so-called universal religion has tended to lag behind other world movements in expansive outlook. As the tempo of expansion has quickened in some secular fields, religious lag has become more and more obstructive. The lag's hindrance to global unity is now greater than ever.

Men cannot find one another because they are separated by sectarian walls. Even their love for one another is expected to flow along sectarian channels. 

Sect and global life simply do not go together. They cannot be harmonized. The conflict is inherent. 

Proof of the inappropriateness of any of the great religions to become universal is both external and internal.

The very fact that each religion is now making very few inroads into other religions is a strong external 35

proof. Proximity of Christianity and Judaism for centuries with scant transfer of membership is a case in point. With these two kindred religious cultures impinging on each other constantly, one would expect that there would be a considerable transfer of membership. As a matter of fact, very few Jews become 

Christians and very few Christians embrace Judaism. 

The same persistence of each religious faith continues, with few exceptions, irrespective of the prevalence of religious contacts and irrespective of the religions which are in contact. Since the progress of even the most prevalent religion toward worldwide acceptance is very slow, the presumption from this external evidence is that none of the prevalent religions is really appropriate for a global religion.

Internal evidence strengthens the belief that no religion now prevalent is appropriate for a global religion. 

Each religion is saturated with local color and tradition.

Christianity is largely a product of Judaism, Greek philosophy, and Roman practice. It is still so thoroughly a product of these elements that it reflects its cultural origins in every part. It clings so tenaciously to elements from its local origins that it refuses to put. on universality. It expects the world to adapt itself to historic Christianity. It shows little interest in accepting from other sources those elements which would change it into a world religion. Fundamentally it stresses self rather than society. People conditioned to a different culture do not take to Christianity readily.

Islam is so thoroughly saturated with Jewish and Arabic elements that it is not readily acceptable to people of other cultures. In spite of many converts in 36

India, Islam in that country is weak compared to Hinduism which preceded it.

Hinduism is so thoroughly Oriental that it has made little progress in the West. There is no indication that its prospects of making more rapid progress are improving.

Buddhism, Confucianism, and Taoism are better adapted than Christianity and Islam, as the latter two are conceived by their orthodox members, for universality, but they have made little progress beyond the Orient. Changes in each of these religions has made it, with the possible exception of Islam, less welladapted now than in the days of its founder for universality.

Shinto is so intensely racial and national that it is not likely to make much progress outside of Japan and 

Japanese colonies.

The conclusion from internal evidence is that all these religions are so deeply rooted in local culture and chained by local tradition that they are not likely, even under pressure of urgent need, to slough off localism and clothe themselves with universality.

Existence of a central figure as the dominant feature of most authoritative revealed religions probably militates strongly against universality. Ideas such as

Brotherhood of Man and Fatherhood of God connote universality. A Jesus or a Mohammed is a concrete person about whom details cluster. Details which attract people of one culture may repel those of another. 

A person appropriate to a certain culture group may appeal strongly to the people of that group. Culture varies far too much from group to group for religion or any other element of culture to find a personality which appeals strongly to members of all cultural groups. Brotherhood is universal, but a person who 37

would qualify as a "brother" in one group might fail to so qualify in another. Members of each group will insist on cutting their own patterns of brotherhood. 

Unreasonableness of many doctrines and practices of various authoritative revealed religions precludes the probability-if not possibility-of their ever becoming global religions. Few educated-or even intelligent uneducated-adults can be induced to believe the miracles which are an integral part of most current religions. Those educated or intelligent adults who do believe them were indoctrinated long before they were old enough to judge their truth or falsity. A religion or other cultural pattern which can be perpetuated only if it gets hold of children and indoctrinates them does not have substance enough to constitute a religion appropriate for civilized people. So long as there are civilized areas in the world, such a religion can never be appropriate for a world religion. 

Evidence, external and internal, is overwhelming that no prevalent religion today is appropriate for a world religion. Not one is taking on global traits fast enough to indicate it will become appropriate. 

Sects must either become global, or a new global religion must be organized. There is no third route to global unity. If sects would become global, tremendous amounts of time and energy would be saved. The vast religious power now organized by sects would be wellnigh irresistible if organized globally and led dynamically. 

Sects, unfortunately and tragically, show no inclination to become global. Each stays within its own shell and tries to pull the rest of the world in after it. 

The narrower the shell, the more fanatical the attempt to drag the entire world into it—and the more zealous 38

the sniping at that part of the world which remains outside.

There are many reasons why sects refuse to become global: 

Localism has so thoroughly saturated various religions that a sect is seldom able to distinguish its localism from its more fundamental religious features. 

Religions generally originate with a founder. Attention is focussed on the founder and his immediate surroundings. Everything connected with him and his environment takes on religious significance. The striking receives great emphasis whether it is relevant or irrelevant religiously. The local becomes so thoroughly mixed with the universal that adherents fail to distinguish the two. To the less discerning members, the local is likely to be accepted as the peculiarly precious feature of the sect—that which marks it off from other sects.

Time idealizes the local features and integrates them more fully with the general features of the founder. 

They become an integral part of the institutionalism of the sect. Even the trivial becomes sacred. Local features are more colorful than general religious features, so become the nucleus of sectarian sentiment. 

Local sectarian features carry a large part of the load of propagation. Because other sects reject and even ridicule the alleged religious significance of the local features of each sect, great ingenuity is used to build up a defense for them. Sectarians are indoctriated with this defense even as they are shielded from the attacks.

Sects do not have their roots in soil deep and rich enough to produce a global flower. Originating in a mediocre if not meager culture, and failing to absorb readily the expanding culture of modern times, sects 39

have neither grown so rapidly as some other cultural fields nor enriched their soil sufficiently to grow to global proportions.

Many sects do not appreciate, much less promote, global culture. On the contrary, they impede global culture both intellectually and emotionally. Intellectually they fail to provide a foundation strong and substantial enough to support a global structure. Emotionally they organize effective sentiments about too limited culture areas to be congenial to global culture. 

Form of worship, which varies greatly from sect to sect, bulks very large in the religion of numerous people. With many people it is the really dynamic feature. When these are engaged in a certain form of worship they feel more religious and believe they are more religious than at other times. When they leave their accustomed form of worship they pass psychologically from the sacred to the secular. Because the form of worship to which one has become conditioned is so satisfying, it is accepted as indisputable proof of the validity of the sect which practices that form. 

Varying forms of worship, therefore, when identified with various sects, intensify sectarian loyalty, thus hindering global unity. Sec- Childhood indoctrination is a very great-probably the very greatest-means of sectarian control. tarian leaders conceive their chief task to be the inculcation of sectarian doctrines and ways of life. The younger the person to be indoctrinated, the surer the indoctrination. This is particularly true if the doctrine or way of life is such that a mature mind cannot accept it. Knowing this, sectarian leaders wrap children in sectarian blankets as soon after birth as possible. 

Cradle-rolls are not purposeless fads. They are as meaningful to sectarian leaders as they are meaning- 40

less to the babies. So are the host of other rites for small children.

Controls build so strong a sectarian fence around children before the children are able to evaluate religiously that they are never able to escape. Most adults who have been thoroughly indoctrinated as children seldom evince any real interest to escape. Sects controlled by such closed minds have no interest in global religion-save to fight it.

Sectarian schools and sectarian youth organizations which strengthen and perpetuate sectarianism put off the day of global religion. These schools and youth organizations vary all the way from those with a slight sectarian bent to those that are rabidly partisan. The latter put sectarianism above everything else and turn out whole droves of young fanatics. Fanaticism is held somewhat in check in a country which professes religious toleration, but is a strong factor for the intensification of sectarian limitations. Global religion is as much anathema as irreligion to these zealous sectarians. In a religious community it is more anathema to the intensely sectarian, for it gives stiffer competition. 

Sectarian leaders are slow to change from sectarian to global organization lest they lose control. Those in power know that any serious unsettlement, reorientation, or reorganization may curtail their power. The safest course, if they are to retain power, is to maintain sectarian organizations. Sectarian leaders with vision, courage, and initiative enough to try to lead followers from the narrow folds of sectarianism to the broader fields of global religion are rare indeed. The most adventuresome generally do little more than leave a bar or two down in the hope that a few members of the flock may wander out and nibble for awhile on the fresh global grass. 41

Leadership of global proportion is annoyed if not disgusted outright by sectarian restrictions. There is a tendency therefore for leadership of such proportion to prefer the secular because it offers greater freedom. 

Many leaders of global proportion avoid sectarian positions as they fight shy of sectarian-sponsored undertakings or limit their activities to those undertakings in which the sectarian flavor is less pronounced. Because sects are deprived of such leadership, they lose one of the most dynamic of all potentially global forces. 

Religion as a whole suffers.

Orthodox sectarians of each sect, as well as fanatics, believe the only religious hope for the world is that sect. They swell the ranks of those who believe that concentration in sectarian channels is the wise course. 

They thicken sectarian walls and make them more adamantin. They support sectarian leadership and think in sectarian terms. To them the unsectarian, if it tries to be religious, is false.

In backward communities and among sheltered people, orthodoxy is strong. Global religion has too broad a horizon to appeal to narrow orthodoxy. Social control tends to keep all but the more independent, adventuresome, and courageous in line. Each sectarian is comforted by the close proximity of the rest of the flock. 

Sectarians are more likely than others to bring strong pressure on leadership. Believing that any straying from sectarian paths is leaving the religious way, sectarians are quick to detect and rebuke any straying from those paths. Liberals, on the contrary, note such straying without alarm. Sectarians therefore are constantly exerting more vigorous and sustained pressure on leadership than is exerted by liberals. While those who are obsessed with sectarianism 42

are moving heaven and hell-frequently without knowing the difference between the two-to preserve sectarianism, those who are more congenial to global points of view are largely silent. The result of sectarian pressure is to bring zealous sectarians into positions of leadership, thus intensifying and perpetuating sect. 

Intense intrasect cooperation and intense intersect competition generate much emotionalism. Each sectarian tends to accept emotional satisfaction as evidence of proof of truth. The more emotionalism generated in a drab society the stronger the proof. Zealous sectarianism therefore commends itself strongly to those in need of emotional excitement or relief. Because intelligent appraisal has far less driving power than emotional urge, global religion is now at a big disadvantage compared to sectarianism. In time, global religion may generate sufficient emotion to satisfy those who require the emotional test. In the meantime, it is at very great disadvantage in winning the masses.

Each sect, irrespective of its quality or appropriateness, is heir to man's felt need of religion. Feeling the religious need, each person generally participates through the sect which is most socially convenient. 

The sect may be a poor excuse for religion, but if that is the most available one it is chosen. Once one becomes identified with a sect, habit and inertia are strong forces binding one to it or to a closely related sect. 

Membership in any sect, no matter how narrow, brings a sense of religious belonging. Most people have a kind of blind hope that the sect to which they belong is as good as any other-or, at least, is good enough to pass muster as religion. Having satisfied their conscience by membership in one sect, people seldom 43

bother about seeking one of finer quality. Global religion will have to be taken to sectarians; they will not seek it.

Each sect reaps the benefit of loyalty to religion. 

Today nearly all sects are using much of this loyalty to bind religious men and women more securely with sectarian bonds. Global religion invites religious loyalty to wider horizons and a fuller life.

Each sect fears it would lose its identity in a new unity. The weaker the case for a sect the more it fears loss of identity. Sectarian leaders realize that a new unity would be selective. It would tend to reject nonreligious and irreligious features of each sect. To avoid rejection or modification, sect avoids unity. The greater the inherent weakness of a sect the more vehemently it rejects proposals of unity. The greater the weakness the more it dreads a critical comparison and evaluation to find a basis of unity. 

Religion, to save itself, must become global. Yet that is exactly what the various sects refuse to do. 

Sectarian tares choke out religious grain.

Zealous sects and sectarians not only refuse to become global. They fight those who would do so. They fear that the breadth and richness of global religion would in a fair competition quickly win against the narrowness and poverty of their own sectarianism. 

They are alert to suppress global religion before it can get under way. The lukewarm and easy-going offer little resistance. The zealous set the direction and the pace.

Evidence abounds that sects continue to abhor global religion.

How Sects Perpetuate Themselves 

Sectarianism is so very destructive, gives rise to so many evils, that its persistence is inexcusable. It would 44

have been drastically reduced long ago but for its selfperpetuating nature. Knowledge of the means used to perpetuate self goes a long way toward reducing the sectarian's confidence in his own sect. He soon comes to see that sectarian sagacity, if not craftiness, has played a much bigger part than the hand of God in that perpetuation.

Identification of sect with religion in the popular mind is a main cause of persistence of sect. People think of religion in terms of the sects with which they are acquainted. They seldom consider looking for a better religion or creating a better one by independent action. They, because they are religious, support sects in spite of disapproval of many sectarian points of view and activities. To reject sect seems nearly synonymous with rejecting religion—or, at least, with rejecting organized religion.

The religious urge is so great that numerous people prefer even the most primitive type of religion to none at all. So long as a sect or group of sects can keep people believing that the choice is between sectarian religion and none at all, sects can perpetuate themselves. 

Perpetuation is easier because skilful sectarian leadership is all things to all men. Every sect today contains high as well as low cultural elements. Sectarian leaders stress the high-intelligently or ethically- to those persons who are sufficiently far advanced to be disgusted with the low. Members and prospective members on low levels are given those sectarian elements which are more to their liking. Sectarian leadership even today does not dare let its membership read and ponder all parts of its sacred literature. Holy 

Writ even infallible Holy Writ-is so unholy in spots that those spots are carefully avoided. The ethical and 45

timely parts are played up, the unethical and antiquated are avoided or made palatable by ingenious explanations or biased interpretations. 

Long history of sects and the institutional wisdom gained during it are mighty factors in self-perpetuation. A long history carries with it a considerable amount of prestige, irrespective of the quality of the history. An institution which has lasted a long time and is still going strong is likely to continue to last a long time. People prefer to be connected with a going institution even though the going may not be on such a high plane as that of other but less popular institutions. "Wrong with the majority rather than right with the minority" is the unspoken preference of countless millions of people. 

During its long history a sect spreads its roots widely and sinks them deeply into many phases of culture. In numerous if not most fields of culture there is no strict dividing line between religious and nonreligious cultural threads. To take out the religious threads would be to disrupt the cultural patterns. A noncritical public comes to believe that to take out sectarian threads would spoil as well as disrupt cultural patterns. Social habit and inertia preserve sectarian elements along with the rest. Rather than try to separate the truly religious from the sectarian and discard the latter, the tendency is to plod, or be dragged along, in the same old sectarian rut.

A tremendous amount of institutional wisdom, sagacity, and craftiness are gained by a sect during its long history. It learns much about human nature and the way to appeal to it. It learns the fundamental aspirations, immediate desires, and fears of people. 

It notes human weaknesses and how these can be used to institutional advantage. 46

A sect learns, too, how it can use the family and other institutions as propagandic agencies. Much of the load of propagation and perpetuation is shifted to the shoulders of various agencies.

Sects perpetuate themselves more effectively because they are looked upon as guardians of morality, of social solidarity, of cultural values. The general public is induced to believe that since sectarianism is synonymous with religion its destruction would undermine the moral, social, and cultural foundations of society. 

Many of those who reject the theology of sects would preserve them as a protection against moral and social disintegration.

The public fails to see that much of the moral and social system is sect-created. Having taught the public to value their own type of morals and social behavior, sects get credit for being contributors to the good life when they uphold their own creation— good or bad.

Sects have a good name and capitalize on it handsomely. Their charitable activity and their ethical teaching commend them strongly to the general public as well as to their own membership. Their charity may be unwisely administered and their ethics antiquated, but in the eyes of the public they at least mean well. The poor relief given by a sect may be paid for in part by nonsectarians or even by those who make no profession of religion, but those receiving it are grateful to the sectarian agency which provides for them. If poor relief is adequate, recipients ask no theological questions.

individual or family through a trying time is not soon forgotten. Those who have been helped have a sentimental attachment which precludes close analysis or accurate judgment of the broad sectarian program of the helper. Where fear is present in crises or at other times, its easing increases gratitude and promotes noncritical attitudes.

Pervasiveness of sectarianism and the resultant public sentiment and public opinion are big factors in strengthening and perpetuating the hold of sect. Appealing to the feelings, sectarianism passes from person to person until the whole community comes to have a favorable attitude. Public sentiment is transformed into public opinion until the judgments of individuals and groups support sectarian activity. In numerous communities, sects come to be regarded as public institutions. Sectarian leaders come to feel they have semi-compulsory authority over the public as well as over membership. 

Loyalty to family is one of the strongest, if irrational, props to sectarianism. Even educated members of the family who have lost faith in the uniqueness of their sect, are reluctant to break with it. They are likely to retain nominal allegiance and send their children to the same institution in spite of the fact that they have ceased to believe the fundamental things it teaches. The children come under the instruction of strict sectarian leaders who indoctrinate them in dogmas that the parents have long since ceased to believe. 

As these children become older, those who are liberally educated will follow in the footsteps of their parents by giving up sectarian points of view. But they are likely to send their own children to the same institution to begin the same process of indoctrination as 48

their parents and grandparents had undergone. The process seems endless.

Family loyalty is generally conceived and practiced as loyalty to sectarian tradition rather than as loyalty to truth. Even when parents know the truth, they frequently encourage their children to go where sectarian tradition is rampant. They would rather have their children in Sunday school learning that which the parents know is untrue than to have them isolated. 

Sectarian professional leaders tend to be drawn from narrow and bigoted parts of sects. In the first place, the more ignorant and less well educated a person, the more likely he is to be a strict sectarian. There is therefore a very close connection between ignorance, little education other than indoctrination, and sectarianism.

In the second place, members of independent mind are generally kept out of dominant sectarian positions. 

A person who is too independent to submit to indoctrination is eased out of pre-theological or theological school as unfit for ordination. When a person of independent mind succeeds in passing the rigid sectarian tests to achieve ordination, sectarians hound his footsteps. There is a tendency for the abler and more independent spirits, who could do much to save their sects from antiquated behavior, to be hounded out. 

Nonconformists who are hounded out lose the power and influence flowing from organized religious leadership. They go on their way with vanishing influence while conformists, who control sectarian machinery and speak in the name of the sect, make children and other plastic personalities into conformists like themselves. Nourished from within and casting out nonconformists, sects change little and learn less save 49

means of intensifying their own sectarian self-centeredness.

Education-in school, church, and elsewhere-ranks high as a means of sectarian preference and self-perpetuation. Even public schools are instruments of sectarian propaganda. In a thousand ways, conscious and unconscious, public schools in the United States favor the Christian-Jewish tradition. Public schools in Islamic, Confucian, Buddhist, Shinto, or other lands -in so far as there are public schools there-favor the dominant sect. The same is true of private unsectarian schools.

Sectarian schools are the chief mainstay of sectarianism in education. Some schools maintained by various sects are scarcely more sectarian than public schools. At the other extreme are sectarian schools so biased and bigoted that their chief purpose is to create sectarians. They are often amazingly efficient in cramping the minds of children and youth to the narrowest kind of sectarianism.

Many sects choose sectarian teachers and use sectarian observances in school. Some even publish their own textbooks and supplementary reading material. 

Everything is seen from and subject to sectarian points of view. Children are led to believe that their sect is the most important of all institutions. They must give it their first allegiance and preserve it with a devotion as blind as that of the most zealous Nazis or Japanese nationalists.

Children trained over long periods in the strictest sectarian schools are seldom able to readjust themselves to balanced points of view. A society that looks with complaisance on such in doctrination and accepts it as meeting the requirements of compulsory educa- 50

tion, is flirting with suicide. Sectarian schools cannot stand the tests of modern education.

Sectarian education in church, synagogue, and other religious agencies is far more legitimate than in weekday schools. Here a sect has the right to inculcate its principles and its system. This right does not, however, change the quality of the teaching or indoctrination. Some sects, even in their most seclusive agencies, are much concerned with personal and social helpfulness. Others are chiefly concerned with dyeing the minds of their membership an even deeper sectarian hue. They give a thousand times more time and effort to self-perpetuation than to self-improvement. 

The easy-going attitude of the general public makes it a ready prey to zealous sectarians. The great majority of people seldom take the trouble to examine their religious beliefs and practices carefully. They take their religious outfit with far less scrutiny than they give to their millinery or haberdashery. They seem to be much more concerned about quality of clothing than quality of religion. Even those who demand the most up-to-date and whole clothes accept without question a religion centuries behind the times and moth-eaten with mythology and superstition. 

Emotional nature of sectarianism is one key to its hold on nearly all types of people. Even intelligent people often yield quite readily to emotional and sentimental appeals of a low order. Because sectarian connections are so highly charged with emotion, even people who use intelligence in other fields frequently neglect or refuse to do so in the face of sectarian appeals. Skilful sectarian leaders begin to build emotional reactions early in the life of children that these may block an intelligent appraisal of sectarian systems. 51

Sentiments built in this way are likely to outlast rational judgments.

Music is used with telling effect by churches and other sectarian agencies. Religious music makes such a strong appeal that it endears the source of the music to those who hear it. In a Buddhist temple, Buddhists would be drawn closer to Buddhism by the same music by which Christians in a Christian church would be drawn closer to Christianity, or Jews in a synagogue be drawn closer to Judaism. 

Sectarians generally hear music in their own sectarian agencies, so members of each sect feel drawn closer to it. The person who finds music in his sectarian place of worship joyful or soothing is likely to feel that his sect means a very great deal to him. 

Music is the sugar-coating for sectarian dogmas or practices that may be too bitter or even downright beastly for anyone to swallow in the raw.

So with various elements of ritual and other symbols -especially those which from long habit have acquired a sentimental appeal. Worshippers may appreciate the religious content or only the symbol itself-but their sect gets credit for whatever good the worshippers receive. 

People are reluctant to change their minds in so important a matter as religion. As they get older, learning becomes increasingly difficult and painful. So they hang on to the old sect with even more persistence than they hang on to the old political party or the old neighborhood gang. The very inertia of people-especially people reared in a conservative sectarian society is one of the greatest conservators of sectarianism and enemies to religious improvement. 

Sectarians get much encouragement from government. In those countries with a state church or in 52

those dominated by a single sect, aid to the favored sect is direct and bountiful. In those countries where sects are numerous, government encouragement is distributed more evenly. Even there, government encouragement is usually put to sectarian as well as to religious ends.

Lack of aggressiveness on the part of nonsectarians results in the strengthening and perpetuation of sectarianism. Sectarian myths and pretentions are generally so vulnerable that confidence in them could be easily undermined by those who know how flimsy and false they are. Instead of telling sectarians and the general public the truth, nonsectarians generally are silent.

Every week the radio brings in the most earnest sectarian talks but not once is there an antisectarian talk. Great gobs of superstition and unethical sectarianism are proclaimed with impunity, but one doubts very much that private or public agencies which control radio would permit an evaluative discussion of the views of any sect by those who disbelieve in it. "Intolerance" and "religious strife" would be the charge against those who should try to save the general public from sectarian indoctrination.

Die-hard fanatical sectarians put more energy into a single campaign than nonsectarians expend in an entire year. They use the contagious kind of emotional and sentimental appeal. It works.

Intelligent and informed persons are not sectarians unless they have been indelibly indoctrinated in childhood, have been deeply swayed by emotional and sentimental appeals, or are using sect as an agency to promote selfish ends.

Sectarianism is used by numerous leaders to promote selfish ends. Politicians cater to a sect or group of 53

sects with numerous members to get votes. Conservatives, because they are afraid to change, tend to favor any sect which stands for the status quo. Wealthy people know that present-day sects generally support private property and private enterprise. "Thou shalt not steal" has a property as well as a moral value. 

Religion is generally on the side of law and order. 

Those who are afraid of lawlessness and disorder are in favor of religion. In a sectarian society, they are in favor of sects on social rather than on theological grounds because sectarian religion is the only religion they know. Business men frequently count on co-sectarians to prefer their stores to stores owned by members of other sects or by nonsectarians. 

Prevalence and persistence of a sect are but slightly related to its truth or to its plausibility. In fact, sects are so contradictory that they tend to cancel one another out. Each sect has little or nothing that is true save what it has in common with other sects. 

Sects achieve prevalence and persistence because of organized power. A religion of Brotherhood, without the superstitions and mythologies of sectarianism, would sweep the countries of the world if it had onetenth the organized power of the great sects in each country.

Various sects-Christianity, Judaism, Islam, Bud dhism, and the rest-cling so tenaciously to their sectarianism that they will have to be eradicated unless they can be induced to show greater willingness to discard sectarianism. 

If leaders of various sects were willing to give up sectarianism, they could doubtless transform the sects and merge them in a great world-wide brotherhood. 

This ought to happen. It would be the orderly, progressive way of replacing lower forms of religion with 54

higher forms. There are few signs, however, that the leadership of organized sectarianism is willing to lead the rank and file of membership onto higher ground. 

It would appear that sects as well as sectarianism will have to be destroyed.

Sectarianism Is Being Undermined 

Sectarianism, and the sects which breed it, are on the way out. Progress toward eliminating them, though, is very, very slow. Religious people who ought to be clearing out sectarian tares that religious wheat may grow are the most zealous protectors and cultivators of the tares.

Undermining of sectarianism, though largely unplanned and unintentional, is taking place from many causes and on many fronts.

Religious origins are better understood. This better understanding is beginning to percolate among educated laymen. More and more people are learning that the various sects arose out of a gradual accumulation and integration of many cultures rather than through special revelation and miraculous manifestations. Each sect is a composite of the many culture traits current at the time of its origin, and numerous additions from the cultural contacts of later generations. Students of religious history have come to see that each sect is thoroughly human rather than divine in origin. 

As belief in the divine origin of sects disintegrates, each sect tends to receive evaluative scrutiny rather than blind and reverent acceptance. Such scrutiny shows up the absurdities and flaws of each sect. It undermines the confidence of adherents. 

The study of comparative religion is doing much to undermine confidence in the exclusiveness of each sect, therefore in sectarianism itself. Various sects have too 55

much in common for any one to claim predominance in virtue or truth. They have evidently been manufactured in the same mill-that of human experience. 

They are all products of humanity's striving to solve the same problems. They are too much alike for one to have had a divine and the others a human origin. 

To the careful observer, each has a good deal of the good and a good deal of the bad. 

Confidence in basic religion may be enhanced by the study of comparative religion; confidence in the outstanding superiority of any one sect vanishes. Each sect takes its place along with the rest as one room in the religious cathedral which man has been building through the ages. Each room suits those who live in it chiefly because they have lived in it, not because it is in itself more desirable than other rooms. 

Knowledge of the origin and development of moral systems has a share in proving the monstrous pretentiousness of sectarian claims. So soon as people learn that the various moral codes which form a part of sacred scriptures are of human rather than divine origin they cease to bolster their belief in the divine origin of a religious system by reference to the fineness of its moral code.

Codes once accepted blindly as perfect revelations from God are now criticized freely and evaluated according to their intrinsic value. As the criticism of a code reveals many bad moral features, people have less faith in the sect which proclaims the code as divinely -even infallibly-given. 

Increase of intelligence and spread of information among a larger and larger proportion of people are undermining sectarianism with irresistible power in spite of the slowness of the process. Evolution, science, and increasing amount and improving quality of higher 56

education are sharpening the wits and broadening the minds of men. They are doing this in spite of the fact that many sectarian schools of higher learning are far more concerned with cramping the minds of students to sectarian proportions than with teaching them to think or to get a knowledge of the universe. 

There was a trend away from indoctrination during the latter years of the nineteenth and the early years of the twentieth century. This trend was in general culture as well as in the schools. It made sectarian indoctrination less complete in spite of the attempt of sectarian leaders to counteract it. With the rise of communism and fascism, the trend was slowed down if not reversed by the combined efforts of sectarianism, communism, and fascism. Although despising one another, and differing in method, the type of mind of the three is basically the same.

Increased emphasis on freedom and democracy has begun to destroy the foundation of sectarianism. Ignorance and superstition can be as effective as prison walls or dictatorial laws in barring the way to freedom and robbing people of democratic opportunity. Few things do so much as sectarianism to imprison the mind. Few things are so undemocratic as sectarianism which speaks with even more authority and finality than the worst of political dictators.

Neither freedom nor democracy is safe in a sectarian society. Intelligent friends of freedom and democracy are aware of the urgent need to curb now, and ultimately destroy, sectarianism. If sects continue to insist on clinging to their sectarianism, the major social conflict will be between sectarians and friends of freedom. 

A fuller knowledge of the power of propaganda is cutting the ground from under sectarian faith. People 3 57

have learned that strong and unopposed propaganda can make people believe anything. Sects have enjoyed a golden opportunity to impress their systems on the minds of their membership, and, to a considerable degree, on the minds of the general public. 

Propaganda is far more powerful than plausibility. 

Thoughtful people now know that their sectarian preferences have been created by propaganda. A knowledge of this fact is bound to make intelligent people recognize the true source of their sectarianism. 

Psychology of religion and sociology of religion are doing much to weaken sectarianism. These show that the sense of sin, of being forgiven, of divine presence, and similar manifestations of religious experience are of social origin. The warm sympathetic atmosphere which fills the church or synagogue where earnest worshippers gather and feel the presence of one another is easily mistaken for the divine presence induced by faith, sacrifice, or ritual.

In spite of the fact that members of all sects, each using different symbols, have the same reaction, each sect ascribes the results to the correctness of its own system. Knowledge of the psychology and sociology of religious experience dissolves sectarianism like sun dissolves the morning mist.

Superiority of the secular over the sectarian in many fields is breaking down faith in the latter. Intelligent observers are asking how an institution claiming divine guidance can be so far behind secular institutions in achievement of desirable social ends. In education, child training, morals, understanding of modern life, political activity, child labor, property, literature, respect for personality, and other fields, sectarian organizations often lag miles behind secular agencies. 

Increasing contact and intermingling of cultures 58

between East and West are threatening the exclusive psychology of all great world sects. When Buddhists, Christians, Mohammedans, Confucians, and members of other sects meet and mingle in business, diplomacy, military activities, education, and other fields, each group finds it hard to believe that the others are but inferior "heathen" bound for a bottomless hell. When people see with their own eyes that heathendom is not so hellish after all, they are sure to question the exclusiveness of their own means of salvation-here and hereafter.

There is no real reason for sectarians to continue to believe in their own sect. As soon as one is freed from the effects of early indoctrination, one realizes this. 

As the truth spreads, sectarianism tends to disintegrate. Disintegration of sectarianism will mean drastic transformation or disintegration of the various sects. 

As an ever-increasing number of people reject sectarianism, some very drastic possibilities arise:

Enlightened people may get out of organized religion, leaving only the most ignorant and bigoted to carry on. Sectarianism would then become more and more degenerate. Only the dregs of religion would compose the sects. Sectarianism so constituted would sink lower and lower until it would be a stench in the nostrils of civilized society. 

Antireligion may be the result of the decline of sectarianism unless an unsectarian religion is organized to take over. Sectarianism has been so successful in getting people to accept blindly rather than to evaluate religiously that many of them fail to distinguish sectarianism from genuine religion. A good many of these are going to turn against religion in general when they turn against sectarianism, since most of the religion they know is sectarian. When they fail to dis- 59

tinguish, they think people must be saved from religion as well as from sectarianism. In their fight to destroy sectarianism and its evils, they are likely to set the cause of real religion back sharply. 

Irreligion is now far more prevalent than antireligion. It is due in considerable degree to the disintegration of sectarianism. The great majority of those who lose faith in their sect are filled with regret rather than with bitterness.

Sectarianism discourages attempts to become well acquainted with real religion. When one's sectarianism is shattered, one is cast adrift. One frequently feels there is no middle ground between sectarianism and nonreligion.

Nonexistence of an unsectarian religious organization to which to turn, and the shock of disillusionment, cause many former sectarians to try to get along without anything that they would define as religion. 

As disintegration of sectarianism and the sects speeds up, the number of nonreligious-at least, organized nonreligious-people will increase rapidly unless something better is prepared for them. None but unsectarian religion will be acceptable.

Humanism-centered in and drawing inspiration from man-is increasing and becoming organized. If sectarian organization of religion with its God-consciousness is destroyed before an unsectarian God-conscious organization has been created, atheistic and agnostic humanism are likely to get much of the energy now flowing into sectarian organizations. Such humanism is free to use recent advances in knowledge and up-to-date methods, but lacks the strength which springs from a strong conviction of a theistic universe. 

This attempt to salvage social values from the wreckage of religion avoids the weakness of the "Let God 60

do it" attitude. It lacks the inspiration, driving power, fellowship, and sense of deep eternal values of theistic humanism.

The major interreligious struggle of the future will be between theistic and nontheistic humanism. Sectarianism is ignorantly tipping the scales in favor of the latter. Christianity, Judaism, Islam, Buddhism, and other great sects are unwittingly digging the grave of God-conscious religion. They have so besprinkled and bespattered God with their sectarianism that intelligent and decent men turn away in disgust. The answer to the need of those who are disgusted with sectarianism and to the need of countless others is a great unsectarian Brotherhood under the Fatherhood of God.

Necessity and Nature of Global Religion 

The world, divided against itself, cannot stand. This is particularly true when the division is in so fundamental a thing as religion.

Consequences of religious disunity have been and doubtless will continue to be tragic so long as this disunity is rampant. Religious adaptation, toleration, and appreciation do much to relieve the tragic consequences, but they do not get to the roots of the evil which bears such tragic fruit.

Psychic unity must keep step with interdependence or man will continue to rise up against his fellow man for whom he feels no fellowship and slay him. Religious unity is the surest foundation-the only really sure foundation-on which dependable worldwide psychic unity can be built. 

Global unity is a necessity. Religion should promote it. Sects are not global and will not become so. The possibility of a global religion lies in the creation of 61

a new of a global-religion. Only such a religion is actually going to promote global unity. 

The great merit of global religion-a truly global religion-is that it would unite men the world around rather than divide them. Religion is one of the strongest of all group ties. A worldwide religion would tend to make the people of various lands feel as one. To be sure, there might be a good many nonreligious and irreligious individuals, but these would be scattered among religious people. There would be no such geographic, cultural, racial, or national grouping as Christian versus pagan. Politicians and propagandists would not find it so easy to stir up or intensify widespread and well-nigh total intolerance and bigotry.

Worldwide unity and integration are the only kinds which fit the concepts "Brotherhood of Man" and "Fatherhood of God". "Worldwide" is the only concept broad enough to be called "human". It is the only one which is broad enough to give every human being his proper perspective. It is the only one to which all men could rightly pledge allegiance.

Global religion is necessary because no other factor or combination of factors can achieve global unity. No other factor has both the sympathy and breadth to achieve it. Family, neighborhood, or similar local group generates enough sympathy to unite its members. But no culture save religion will extend this sympathy globally so that it may unite all mankind. 

Global religion would focus variety in so far as it does not go even further and bring about integration. 

The focus will be brotherhood or other central religious value. Variety will be chiefly in instrumental rather than in intrinsic value. Under global religion, instru- 62

mental value will continue to be great in spite of widespread discarding of pseudo-instrumental values. 

Such items as language, social system, industrial technique, and political organization are but the skeleton on which a world unity may grow. They are but the framework of unity, not unity itself. 

Any part of this framework may be used to convey destructive forces which are inimical to global unity. They do not guarantee do not, in fact, necessarily promote-unity. 

Impact of various parts of the world on one another is stronger than ever before. It is, in reality, becoming increasingly terrific. The atom bomb is but one in a long series of terrifying forces. Unless a religion broad enough to include all parts and soften impact is created, worldwide destructiveness is sure to continue. 

Such destructiveness would probably increase. Technology, which has made worldwide conflict possible and inevitable, is congenial to a global religion-the only kind which is likely to ease rather than intensify global collisions. With increase of worldwide interdependence and conflict, an effective global religion is the crying need of our time.

The world has come to expect religion to be a leader in, rather than a drag on, progress. It expects religion to reflect the best rather than the poor and the mediocre in modern culture. A sect which fails to measure up, brings religion as well as itself into disrepute. Because sects are failing to measure up-save in the eyes of the indoctrinated-religion is now in considerable disrepute. Global religion is necessary that it may shake sectarian shackles off the religious life of the world and prove that religion can be uniformly high in quality.

Nonexistence of any religion-any well-known religion, at least suitable for a world religion need not 63

preclude the possibility of such a religion. The religions of the world have been, to a considerable degree, historic accidents, colored by localism or regionalism. 

Because the world has consisted of isolated regions, religions have been very many and very different. 

Most of them have grown up locally out of local conditions or to serve local needs. The very nature of their origin makes it unlikely that they would be worldwide.

Shortcomings of past and present religions as world religions prove little about a religion designed as a world religion. They do not indicate that a world religion is unlikely. Since various religions have been the product of local conditions, local training, and local happenings, presumably a worldwide culture and worldwide economy would induce a world religion rather than a local one. The world religion itself would become an important agency-perhaps the most important of all agencies-in building and maintaining a worldwide culture.

If there are world religious needs, that fact suggests the possibility, if not probability, of a real world religion. So many present religious or pseudo-religious needs appear to be induced by various forms of preconditioning that the detection of non-conditioned world religious needs might be very difficult. Such needs would be innate in all human beings or germane to various societies.

Brotherhood as a world religious need arises on the psychic side, from the gregarious nature of man. A person feels better when he is conscious of being bound to other people by ties of love and friendship. On the economic side, brotherhood is required to reduce the hazards of poverty and to relieve the hardships incident thereto. Security, either psychic or economic, 64

can hardly exist for the great masses of men unless there is fellowship.

Fellowship seems to be an essential religious need for all mankind. The lowest savage and the highest civilized man share this need. So do all races, all nationalities, all geographic groups, and cultural patterns. 

A religion which provides this ingredient in theory and in practice has laid a firm foundation for a global religion.

People in various parts of the world require a second religious essential-God. The universally essential need for God is probably expressed most accurately in the concept "fatherhood". That concept and the need it implies are sufficiently universal and basically enough alike the world over to suggest Fatherhood of God as the second essential of a world religion. 

This second concept-Fatherhood of God is indispensable now, whatever may be the case at some indefinite future time. The concept "God" is so thoroughly a part of religious feeling and thought that a global religion without God would not satisfy the great body of religious people in various lands. 

God does supply a very strongly felt need. The need does not appear to be induced by training. The specific purpose which God serves and the specific manner in which He achieves that purpose may be the product of training, but the felt need and the belief in the need of God are so widespread that they probably arise, or would arise, independently of training.

Global religion is one wth a minimum of localism. 

Localism can be detected by its uniqueness. When a trait-the refusal of the Hindus to kill vermin and venomous snakes, for instance-is a part of one sect but absent in others, one may suspect that it is localism. If a religious trait is closely akin to other traits 65

in a culture, but contrary to the traits of other cultures, one may suspect that it is local-mutual to the local religion and to the local secular culture-rather than universal. Localism may be detected because it is contrary to anything which intelligent people would believe except for early indoctrination. A thing so unreasonable as to be rejected by intelligent people is local-can never become universal so long as there is free intelligence in the world. 

Global religion, like a constitution, would have to be broad in outline. It might include little beside the foundation stones on which religious practices would be built. It would have, nevertheless, to contain something of the universal and of the particular. At the present time-perhaps perpetually-many local variations of belief and practice would be desirable as well as inevitable. Such variation, brotherhood centered, would be far superior to current drastic religious pluralism.

The essence of global religion would be a central core which would be desirable and could be made desired the world around. The core would be theistic humanism. The concepts which composed the core would be broad enough to be congenial to the great majority of religious people. "Brotherhood of Man" might be broad enough to qualify for the humanist concept and "Fatherhood of God" broad enough to qualify for the theistic concept. 

On first thought, these concepts may appear too broad to serve any practical purpose. In reality they have very great motivating force. Humanity-once attention has been directed to it and sentiments woven about it has very great psychic attraction. Although inhumanity seems so rampant as to put humanity to shame, and people use comparatively little humanitari- 66

anism, it is at least possible that the concept "humanity" would become the strongest of all concepts in those who have been educated properly. 

Cohesive power of the concept "humanity" is great enough, indeed, to promote the belief that a community of religious interests could be built about it. If people were properly trained, it might well prove far stronger than race, nationality, language, or other forces which now unite groups. If this concept "humanity" has great cohesive power, a world religion is the only kind that would suit it. A world religion is the only one that would be coterminous with humanity. 

The concept "Fatherhood of God" recognizes the need of a divine supplement to humanism. It is general enough to avoid offending those who wish to avoid theology, but serves as a starting point for those who wish to include it. It has more of God than Confucianism whose dearth of God brought the new version of 

Buddhism to China.

Various sub-groups within a global religion need not quarrel with one another unless one group should claim exclusive right to room within its fold or should act contrary to the principles of brotherhood or fatherhood. Within the circle of brotherhood and beneath the canopy of fatherhood, various individuals and groups could devise any means or use any ceremonies which would enable them to realize brotherliness and recognize the Father.

Global religion will be ameliorative and cohesive. It will ease the lot of all men as it draws all together. It will have concern for the individual as well as for the world. There will be no forgotten men. - Global religion will be high enough ethically to have the respect of people. There is sufficient ethical ad- 67

vancement in certain circles in various lands to reject any religion with low ethics. Such religion might make progress among certain classes but would not become really global.

Global religion will be sufficiently free from irrational elements to be acceptable to intelligent people. People who are aware of the highly irrational elements in the doctrines and practices of most sects may be inclined to doubt that irrationality in a religion impedes its diffusion. This point of view overlooks two facts. 

Irrationalities now gain acceptance because of indoctrination in childhood. Even such acceptance as they appear to receive among intelligent people is more apparent than real. Intelligent people accept sects in spite of irrational elements. Global religion must be of such high quality that it will be acceptable to the more intelligent classes in various countries. 

Global religion will be organized about some principle or principles of universal significance. Such principle will have meaning for all men irrespective of race, nationality, geographic location, or degree of cultural advancement. It will claim the loyalty and entice the sentimental attachment of all. It will preserve global character in spite of local interpretation and application.

Brotherhood of Man meets the requirement of global in that it includes all mankind. The concept "brotherhood" meets the requirement of sympathy and humaneness which are essential to religion. It permits each group to define brotherhood and seek it in its own way. It suggests mutual obligations but permits each local group to choose the content of the obligation. It meets the requirement of an intelligent religion because the benefits flowing from the practice of Brotherhood are continuously evident. 68

Global religion will not be bound blindly and rigidly to the past. It will not permit itself to be fastened to an authoritative hitching post-prophet, scripture, or any other. Instead of being a slave to the past, it will use the past as its servant in achieving a better present and future. It will insist on the right to remain free. 

Global religion will not be simply a synthesis of various sects. It will, however, draw freely from the experience of all. It will draw freely from secular sources. 

It will be continuously and zealously seeking means to bring brotherhood to men-to all men. 

Global religion, if it is to remain global, will be adaptable, progressive, teachable. It will be willing to change its ways when more efficient ways of achieving its ideal of brotherhood are found or created. It will have faith in the universe to the extent of believing better ways are possible. It will have faith in its ability to find or create them. It will have faith in its own willingness to accept and conform to those better ways. It will learn in the same spirit that other teachable areas of modern culture learn. 

Much of the new ethical, intelligent, brotherly global religion is implicit in old religions. The new, in fact, continues the long-term trend of religious evolution. 

Just as the widespread sects of today have tended to displace sects of even narrower vision and lower standards, global religion will displace current sects. And for the same reason. Global religion is more in line than the sects with the essential nature of the universe and its religious implications. 69

II BUILDING BROTHERHOOD 

Individuals throughout the world need the same kind of religion. Individuals in all lands are born with the same type of traits, the same fundamental needs, and the same relation to the universe. Cultural differences call for local variations, and variety of social change necessitates different modifications, but similar inborn nature requires, in the long run, the same type of religion for all men everywhere. 

Individuals and the world need the same kind of religion. World culture exists to meet the needs of human nature which is common to all men. It must merge and incarnate the traits of that nature. It must provide the atmosphere in which human nature may develop fully and function freely. A culture which fails to generate this atmosphere is not and can never become a world culture in any meaningful sense. Only the culture which is congenial to the traits common to human nature can provide such atmosphere. This fact applies to religion as completely and forcefully as to other forms of culture.

Individuals throughout the world need the same religion. The same in name and organization as well as in center of gravity and fundamental content. Membership in the same organization gives a greater sense of unity than membership in separate-even identical -organizations. Worldwide unified organization promotes extensive interstimulation. Global structure incites and energizes global thought and global action. 

A satisfactory religion will be appropriate to the world and to individuals in all parts of the world. The requirements of both must be met adequately. Although world economy arises out of and must contin- 70

uously adapt and readapt itself to human nature, it has requirements of its own. Religion must help the world meet those requirements. It must do this without diminishing its service to individuals. 

The best religion is the one centered in the crucial area where individual economy and world economy meet most vitally and coincide most completely. Intrinsically unified itself and touching the two economies in the area most fundamental to both, the best religion serves a dual purpose-individual and global. 

The most vital, fundamental, and meaningful area common to individuals and the world is brotherhood with its many ramifications. The best religion therefore is Brotherhood, the new world religion. 

Nature of Brotherhood

Brotherhood is a religion with a twofold focus-God and man.

Brotherhood assumes a God worthy of worship by man. It assumes that men are bound to God and to one another by ethical ties. 

Brotherhood recognizes frankly and honestly that these are assumptions. It does not, like sectarianism, try to prove them by citing unproven-often irrational and contradictory-revelations from the past or alleged miraculous manifestations of the present. It does believe that reasonable and fair examination and interpretation of the universe tend to confirm the assumptions.

The deeper things of life-the ultimate values, the things which give meaning to life are not capable of scientific proof. One must in relation to these high values make assumptions to guide or must drift blindly and aimlessly over the sea of life. A worthy God and 71

ethical obligations among men are as rational as any primary assumptions. They are more beneficent than others.

Having assumed that men are in ethical relationship to God and to one another, it follows that religion is the field of this relationship. Brotherhood, unlike sectarianism, avoids beclouding and bedeviling this relationship with accumulation of myriad superstitions and irrational practices handed down authoritatively and reverently from the primitive and barbaric days of the human race. It combines and uses the best features of the old religions, and such new inventions as are desirable, to build a new religion of greatest benefit to mankind. It recognizes no revelation save what can be learned by studying nature, including man, and culture.

Brotherhood believes that in religion, as in other fields, knowledge and wisdom are cumulative. Men today are wiser than men of earlier ages. Because religious knowledge and wisdom are cumulative, and religion itself progressive, the wise religious man today is very much wiser than Confucius, Buddha, Jesus, Mohammed, and other wise men of old.

God, worthy in himself and bound by ethical ties to man, gives the universe a meaning, a tone which a Godless universe could never have. He imparts a sense of worth, integrity, direction, and security. Spirituality, sacredness, truth, sympathy, and goodness have a wider and deeper meaning. They come with greater -with compelling-authority. Righteousness is on a firmer foundation. 

God, as father of mankind and bound to all men by ethical ties, enhances the dignity of man. Fellowship and brotherhood have a richer, fuller, more satisfying meaning than they could ever have in an atheistic 72

world-even the most humanistic. A theistic world inspires men and gives them a positive, productive, satisfying attitude toward God and toward man. 

Brothers rejoice in the sense of belonging to the Father. The feeling of unity is ever-present although it is often deeper and stronger during crises or group worship. It may be intensified by symbols associated with worship. Brotherhood encourages the fullest possible personal relationship of the individual to God. 

Man, the second focus of Brotherhood, brings the nature of man, his place in the universe, his culture, and all his relationships within the field of religion. 

Everything that man is or does has religious significance for it is related to his well-being. Man is the center of religious planning and religious activity. 

Service to man is the worship of God. 

Brotherhood takes it for granted that God is far more interested in what men do to one another than in what they say about Him. The chief aim of organized religion is to make thoroughgoing, worldwide brotherhood a reality. Brotherhood thinks that the most effective means of doing this is the organization and development of a great unsectarian religion built around the concept "brotherhood".

Brotherhood is humane as well as human. It is sympathetic, compassionate, kind. It earnestly seeks opportunities to be of service. It has the human touch -is interested in all men everywhere. It respects the personality of each. It strengthens the personality of the strong. It recreates and rebuilds wrecked personalities. Its approach is through love and fellowship rather than through fear and dictatorship.

Brotherhood is as free as humanism to adjust its program and its methods to fit modern conditions. It does not, like sectarianism, have to fit its thinking and 73

its activity into a framework centuries old. It has no authoritative founder, no holy writ, or other sacred relics to confuse thought and limit action. 

Open-minded, willing to learn, despising sectarian indoctrination, Brotherhood can keep abreast of the times. Instead of trying, like sectarianism, to distill all wisdom out of its own system, Brotherhood is alert and eager to learn from any source. Its object is to find out and practice the best means of creating brotherhood rather than to perpetuate a particular sectarian system.

Brotherhood uses all tradition for the distillation of truth. It refuses to confine itself to the tradition of a single sect. It uses secular as well as religious tradition in seeking more effective means of promoting brotherhood. It studies the past as well as present culture of all lands to see what help they give. Acknowledging special allegiance to no sectarian or other stream of culture, it avoids domination by, or entangling connections with, any. It plays no favorites. 

Revelation is what wisdom can discern from every source. Brotherhood assumes that the sayings of religious men are attempts to put into words the feelings, aspirations, and beliefs of those who are trying to understand God and man and to improve the relationship of men to God and to one another. As the truth, value, and authority of revelation depends upon its intrinsic qualities, authorship is unimportant. 

God has created a universe of order and has given men intelligence to progressively understand and control it. No man, book, or institution has been given special wisdom to understand and reveal the universe. 

None has been given special authority to control it. 

In religion, every man has the same privilege as every other man to learn from God and nature directly 74

rather than through institutions claiming a unique origin or relationship to God.

Brotherhood can adapt and readapt itself any number of times. It has no elaborate system of dogma to shackle it. In a rapidly changing world the ability to make frequent and sometimes drastic changes is a great asset. When Brotherhood finds that a certain approach or policy is not achieving adequately, there are no sacred traditions to require its continuance. 

Brotherhood is truly universal and pervasive, as a sect can never be. Brotherhood is desirable in all lands, among all people, for all cultures, and at all times. 

It is appropriate for all classes and conditions of men. 

It is as valuable among nations as among individuals and domestic institutions. It should flow from the individual outward toward the locality, the community, and the world. It should flow from each of these and other institutions toward the individual and toward one another. Unlike sectarianism, it has no antipathies save unbrotherliness.

Brotherhood lends itself to almost infinite variety. 

Different types of inborn and different types of acquired nature find it congenial as recipients of its benefits and as agents in its promotion. Brotherhood can be incorporated into the policies and activities of all kinds of institutions. Any political, social, educational, business, or recreational as well as religious organization can find a place for it in its program. Brotherhood provides a substantial basis for, and fits in nicely with, varied progressive, forward-looking programs. It is congenial to the greatest multiplicity in type of approach and method.

Brotherhood has room for wholesome conflicting points of view. People differ widely as to the best ways of achieving it. Instead of a dogmatic, crystal- 75

ized program, Brotherhood encourages free discussion, initiative, variety, and experimentation. It gives each individual and each group an opportunity to present and to carry forward in his or its own way so far as that way is religious-that is, brotherhoodward. 

Brotherhood is a perpetual challenge. It is an incentive to call forth the best that men and institutions can do. It is an invitation to organize new institutions, new policies, new programs, and other social inventions. It makes plain that the most zealous efforts will be far more than repaid by their social usefulness. 

The end-brotherhood-is so desirable that it challenges all religious people to do their level best to achieve it.

Brotherhood is not inquisitorial as to the beliefs of its members. If they have caught the spirit of brotherhood they will function in the right direction. So long as their conduct is all right their beliefs will not bar them from fellowship in the new organization. If their conduct is not in the right direction, they will not feel at home. Quality of life is the test of brotherhood. It is a test by which each person may judge himself.

Membership in Brotherhood will be broader than those who believe in, and only in, the bare assumptions of an ethical God and ethical men in mutual relationships. Some conservatives with at least vestiges of sectarianism still clinging to them will prefer Brotherhood to sectarian organizations. At the other extreme, some atheistic humanists will doubtless be so strongly attracted by Brotherhood's aggressive humanist program that they will desire the human side of the fellowship and wish to contribute their effort to the common good. Traces of all theological opinion, or none at all, may be found within the new religion. All those 76

who are willing to work for Brotherhood among men will be welcome irrespective of the nature or meagerness of their theology. Any one whose chief interest is this Brotherhood will not seem greatly out of place. 

Brotherhood is truth-seeking, is thoroughly in sympathy with the truth-first movement. As it has nothing to defend but brotherliness and nothing to hide, it welcomes the truth from any quarter. It believes that in the long run religion must be built on the truth. 

It is sure that sectarianism which always puts self ahead of truth is shortsighted from the viewpoint of genuine religion. It realizes that only by suppression and distortion of truth can sectarianism hope to survive indefinitely. It is glad at any time to sit around the conference table with any or all sects for exchange of points of view.

Brotherhood is a practical religion, a religion of action. It seeks to translate its sentiment into vigorous, comprehensive action. It is congenial to all the joy, comfort, and security each individual receives from association and fellowship. But it maintains that religion must bear a large fruitage of brotherliness. 

Creeds and escapist rituals are not adequate substitutes for practical programs and energetic action which fruit brotherhood. 

Need for Brotherhood

Brotherhood preserves the essentials of religion for individuals-for all individuals. It seeks to unite all religious people. It does away with localism as a religious essential. It increases quantity of religion even as it improves quality.

The need for religion is powerful and permanent whether the problem is considered from the viewpoint of the individual or society. Since old-fashioned re- 77

vealed religions are on the way out-out, at least, of all circles where there is any considerable degree of intelligence-humanity is to have either no organized religion, atheistic humanism, or Brotherhood. 

Brotherhood is needed to keep alive God-conscious religion. This kind of religion is badly needed, for there are aspects of religion which are deeper than manthan each man taken singly or all men collectively. 

From numerous points of view, Brotherhood is a haven for those who have rejected sectarianism yet wish to be religious.

Brotherhood saves the intellect from the superstitions of sectarianism. It never requires Brothers to believe or practice that which they fear is wrong or degrading. This lifts a heavy burden off the shoulders of those who think, and a considerable burden off those who have been indoctrinated beyond the point where they do think, in religion. Sectarians have long led a double religious life-their intelligence rebelling against many of the very things their faith requires them to believe and do.

Intelligent sectarians often feel a sense of guilt because they do not feel a sense of guilt at the things their sectarianism says is wrong. Brotherhood does these sectarians a double service. It frees them from the hardship of avoiding numerous harmless things. It frees them from the sense of guilt for omitting to carry out many directions of sectarian leaders. 

Brotherhood saves from fear due to sectarian hellfire theology and kindred teachings. It stresses the delights of love and satisfaction of fellowship rather than the vengeance of a wrathful God and the cruelties inflicted by sectarians in His name. Sectarianism, during its long and tragic history, has thought up or borrowed from secularism many extremely terrifying 78

threats. When enlightenment advances far enough to challenge sectarianism, sectarian leadership resorts more and more zealously to such threats to scare people into their sectarian systems. Brotherhood believes there is a more efficient way, and it knows there is a far more agreeable way, to get people to be religious. 

Brotherhood saves personality from sectarian stultification. In order to extol their system of salvation and intensify by contrast the blessedness of the blessed, sects generally depict man as rotten by nature and fit only to be damned. Either he is going to have to remain in this miserable state here and get thoroughgoing damnation later or he must creep into the sectarian sheepfold for a thorough delousing if not complete regeneration.

The total depravity of man doctrine is worse than unwholesome-it goes the limit in stressing negative control. If the rank and file of men should take this doctrine as seriously as their sectarian teachers, life would soon be a real hell. Only because man is by nature very much better than the sectarian dogma that describes him, has he been able to survive that dogma with some decency and self-respect. But man has not escaped unscathed. The widespread inferiority complex-especially in relation to the church and its teaching is traceable in large measure to dogmatic defamation of human nature and of its unsectarian efforts to be decent.

Brotherhood is far more positive than the old theologies toward personality. It recognizes that men may be men and fine ones too-without submitting to sectarian techniques. It opens new and enlarged avenues of personal development. It stresses the fact that men may make their way upward if they have a mind to do so. 79

Brotherhood would reduce the menace of sectarian schools. As it does not have irrational dogmas which must be inculcated before the child gets old enough to think for himself, Brotherhood schools would not be a necessity. If religious schools were established, they would be broadly religious and tend to integrate religious thinking and practice with community life. They would avoid the present practice of sectarian schools which puts sectarianism first in education as in other things.

Man's inborn nature requires brotherhood. Man is social by nature. He longs for association with his fellows. He is congenial to sympathy. He begins life bound to his fellows by ties which promote mutual sentiments. He needs an emotional tonic which only right relationship to his fellows can ever give. That right relationship is mutual brotherliness. Brotherhood religion is able to provide much of the brotherliness which man requires.

This sense of unity and social nearness which Brothers feel for one another lightens the burdens of life. 

Brothers tend to have a breadth of sympathy which sectarians find very difficult. This is an inclusive sympathy which is contrary to the very principle of sectarianism. It is based on brotherly ties rather than on the rigid, unyielding, often cruel, dogmas that hold sectarians together.

Adequacy of Brotherhood

Brotherhood is as adequate as truth. At this point 

Brotherhood takes issue with those sectarian leaders who say that truth is less than adequate. 84

A clergyman was chatting with a friend on the street when one of his less-educated parishioners approached and asked about the advisability of visiting a shrine in a nearby city to get rid of a chronic illness. The clergyman encouraged the visit. When the parishioner had gone on his way rejoicing at the prospect of being cured, the clergyman remarked apologetically that educated members of his sect did not believe in such cures but that they encouraged the uneducated to believe in order to hold them.

That clergyman is typical of countless sectarian leaders who maintain that if truth is not adequate to keep members in sectarian pens, resort should be had to untruth.

Brotherhood would include all the religious knowledge there is-it goes as far as known truth goes. 

It does not pretend to have special reservoirs of inspired knowledge which are known to it alone. 

Brotherhood has no secret formulas for cleansing sin, no authoritative keys to the kingdom. It can promise all that any religion can promise legitimately. 

It refuses to compete with religious quacks-old or new, big or little-who ignorantly or hypocritically promise what neither they nor anyone else can deliver.

If religion were so degenerate by nature as to require huge masses of ignorance, superstition, unethical dogmas, conscienceless dictatorship, and antisocial maneuvers to keep it going, sectarianism might be necessary. 

Since religion is by nature wholesome enough to be promoted by wholesome methods alone, Brotherhood is adequate. It is adequate logically and psychologically. It is adequate as personal religion and as social religion.

The God of Brotherhood is far more nearly adequate than the God of sectarianism for religion that is ethic- 85

ally mature. religion that is not ethically mature. The God of Brotherhood is higher and finer than the best that men have been able to achieve. He is a constant incentive to mankind to rise higher and higher. 

Modern civilization has no need for a The God of sectarianism retains so many of the characteristics of the unethical gods of primitive, barbaric, and early civilized times that He at best is but an incentive to a divided religious personality. Neither 

He nor any of His man-made satellites is really fit for civilized man to worship. 

The man of Brotherhood is far more nearly adequate than the man of sectarianism. Better by nature, he can adapt himself more readily to a religion of high ethics and good morals. The man of Brotherhood may more easily be a worthy companion for those who desire fellowship. He is better suited than the worthless worm of sectarian theology to be a brother to other men. He is, or can become, a child of God without the cleaning techniques-wet or dry-of sectarianism. 

Brotherhood is in line with the current trend toward human values. This trend is much in evidence in secular culture. It is also in evidence in the most progressive religious sects. The measure of the real worth of a person or an institution is service or disservice to man. As mutual service is the essence of brotherhood, the new religion ranks very high in promoting human values.

Brotherhood is in line with the current trend toward sharing the good things of life. In the new religion, all share opportunities and share on equal terms. Every effort is made to break down age-old as well as new prejudices which exclude groups from equal opportunity. Religion adjusted to human values rather than 86

to salvation techniques is a great encouragement toward worldwide as well as community sharing. 

Brotherhood does more than share objectively. It creates and fosters a sense of sharing. This sense of sharing deepens and strengthens human sympathy. 

It enriches brotherliness. It banishes the feeling of isolation whether the source of the feeling is social estrangement or sectarianism's picture of the sinner shuddering alone in the presence of an angry God, a gleeful Devil, or both, as they compete for the miserable wretch.

Brotherhood is easily understandable-far more so than the complicated and confused systems of various sects. This is a big asset toward the essential work of religion. The rank and file of men and women can see some connection between the nature of their religion and the results desired. 

Being more easily understood than sectarianism, Brotherhood does not require the elaborate systems of clergy and ritual characteristic of some of the sects. 

In Brotherhood there is not the same temptation as in sectarianism to build up from generation to generation great gobs of hocus-pocus to bewilder and hold the laity.

Intelligent behavior is always at a premium in Brotherhood. In much sectarian theory and practice, intelligence is taboo. If sectarian laity really understood the elaborate systems in which they live and with which they worship, they would insist on so thorough housecleaning that little would be left.

Brotherhood supplies a perpetual goal-more brotherhood. No matter how fine the quality and how much the quantity of brotherliness achieved in any place or time, there is always room for improvement. 

There is no prospect of achieving a perfect brother- 87

hood. Possibility of improvement is almost infinite. 

Prospect of much improvement is bright. Possibility and prospect provide strong incentives to religious people to keep steadfastly at the job of being religious. 

The strong incentive-giving goals are both personal and social. 

Brotherhood is a strong personal religion:

Brotherhood provides as great a sense of certainty -as much psychic security-as is wholesome. It does not attempt to compete with those predestinarians who think they know that their salvation was guaranteed from the foundation of the world and that no power in heaven would and no power in hell could damn them at this late date. Nor does it compete with those sanctified people who are entirely sure of their sinlessness and of a straight, if somewhat thorny, road to heaven. 

The absolute sense of certainty of the predestinarian and of the sanctified may be a great relief to them; it is hardly wholesome for society. 

Brotherhood has access to and accepts for their intrinsic worth all intimations or proofs of God, immortality, and other religious values. For the intelligent, these are the only sources of a sense of certainty. 

Groundless claims of esoteric sources of certainty, and indoctrination in them, are far too high a price to pay for the gullibility which passes among sectarians for certainty. 

Brotherhood motivates particularly deeply and strongly. It is tied to and draws its motivation from all humanity. The motivation moves on the high and strong plane of brotherliness and fellowship rather than across the great sectarian chasm which separates the saved from the damned-the sons of God from the children of the devil. Brotherhood, picturing those who are, or are trying to be, worthy Brothers, has a 88

stronger appeal than sectarianism, picturing the totally depraved creatures who have never been able to recover from Adam's mistake.

The God-conscious unity of Brotherhood is twofold. 

The individual feels akin to God, close to Him personally. The creation of brotherliness among men is the cooperative task of God and man. Each Brother is bound to God by personal ties and by the joint action of God and religious men in promoting brotherliness. 

This two-fold unity is of immeasurable value to those who have sufficient insight to see and appraise it fully. 

It is the source of great comfort, satisfaction, and joy. 

It more than compensates for any sacrifices made in the interest of Brotherhood.

This new religion does not, like most sects, impose a long list of characteristics of God. Through the ages, men have guessed at the characteristics of God. As sectarian leaders, usually long after they have been dead, achieved the status of authority, their guesses were taken as statements-often infallible of fact. 

To be in really good standing, one had to believe in the accuracy of the guesses. Even some of the largest and reputedly most enlightened sects have thought that to doubt the accuracy of these guesses has been to keep one from getting on the salvation boat. "Doubt and be damned" was, and still is, the serious judgment of sectarians in even civilized lands. 

Brotherhood avoids vexing and discouraging with a long creed. The long creeds of various sects contradict themselves and one another so frequently and fundamentally that they are not to be taken seriously as statements of truth. They must be taken very seriously as burdens imposed upon membership and upon society. A goodly proportion of the activity of sects is the result of, and to keep people in line with, these 89

creeds. Time and energy needed to build a better world are frittered away in sectarianism on creeds that are more likely to prove stumbling blocks than stepping stones in the religious life of individuals and communities. By avoiding a long creed, Brotherhood is able to concentrate its full time and energy on building a better world. In addition, Brotherhood saves its own self-respect and preserves its intellectual honesty by refusing to accept, promulgate, and promote a long list of doubtful dogmas.

Brotherhood is a strong social religion: 

The center of gravity in Brotherhood is the wellbeing of all men everywhere. The well-being is dependent on types and quality of culture, and of institutions as well as on types and quality of individuals. 

Brotherhood seeks out and tries to remove all social ills. It tries to get rid of the harsh and cruel features which sear men. It assists in building more helpfully creative social institutions and conditions. 

Social motivation and social direction are united in Brotherhood far more completely and harmoniously than is generally true in sectarianism, or even in religion itself. Motivation in large measure has its origin in men-in the realization of unity as well as in the ethical obligation. Movement to implement the motivation is back toward men, the source of the motivation. 

Motivation tends to support and be as strong as the movement itself. Reinforcing each other, motivation and movement are strengthened by their common source.

Brotherhood, by its very nature, is a criterion of value for institutions and other culture. A good successful institution or other item of culture is one which furthers progress toward brotherhood. An institution 90

or cultural element which hinders this progress is a bad institution, a religious failure.

In this field of evaluation, Brotherhood is far superior to the sects. The latter evaluate according to conformity to sectarian institutionalism. Sectarianism is measuring its membership and nonmembership by the degree to which they follow in the footsteps of Mohammed, Buddha, Moses, Jesus, or more recent sectarians who speak in the name of the founders.

No matter how unbrotherly or unwise an element of a sectarian system may be, the conformist gets a high grade, but the more brotherly or wiser nonconformist a low one. Brotherhood, feeding upon brotherliness, tends to produce more of and to grow into the likeness of its own kind. Sectarianism, feeding upon itself, tends to accentuate its own kind, irrespective of its quality.

Brotherhood is appropriate to various types of appeal. It lends itself to emotional stimuli. Reference to humanity tends to call forth strong but orderly emotion. Sentiments woven about humanity are wholesome in nature. They may become permanent and very strong.

Intellect approves Brotherhood because religious energy is expended for a desirable objective and one that is practical. Some elements of brotherliness have long been found in the best appeals used by family, religion, government, and other institutions. Numerous institutions have prepared the way for the thoroughly brotherliness-centered appeal of Brotherhood.

Brotherhood is congenial to the use of the most varied symbolism. Its inclusiveness-both personal and cultural-affords numberless points for symbolic origination and symbolic appeal. Innumerable aspects of brotherliness can be symbolized by multiple symbols. 91

Symbols may be varied according to age, intellectual level, sentimental attachment, current happenings, general cultural background, and other conditioning factors. Whatever will help get across the message of brotherliness is appropriate.

Symbols may be changed any number of times to adjust them to changing cultural trends. Instead of holding back cultural trends by the use of symbols of yesterday, Brotherhood can reinterpret brotherliness continuously in terms of changing culture. In this way, Brotherhood can precede and to some extent guide rather than follow and hold back a culture. By prompt and progressive adjustment of symbols, Brotherhood is able to promote higher cultural levels. 

Many, probably most, symbols now in use by various sects could be reinterpreted to carry the message of brotherliness. For former sectarians who had conditioned their religious sentiments to these symbols a certain advantage might be found in their use in spite of the danger of backsliding into sectarianism. In so far as current symbols prove inadequate, form as well as meaning could be modified, and new symbols invented. It

Brotherhood bridges more nearly adequately than sectarianism the great chasms which divide class from class and group from group. It is more successful in holding divergent groups together. It appeals more strongly to groups on both sides of the chasms. puts humanity above class. It gives each group its full share in the benefits arising out of, as well as obligations connected with, brotherhood. By measuring all classes and all groups by the same standard, Brotherhood avoids the complaint continuously made against sectarianism-it plays favorites. 

Brotherhood offers each person all that he or she 92

can use. It is adequate for the requirements of the most intelligent. For those who approach religion from the affective rather than the intellectual side, it offers the warmest kind of sympathy and sentiments. To the great undifferentiated mass of common men it offers more satisfactory actual living conditions as well as hope for and encouragement toward a still better life. Keeping its eye on brotherliness, the new religion encourages each person to use the approach that is most nearly adequate for each.

The business and political life of every nation is sadly deficient in brotherliness. Sectarianism has failed miserably to get much of even such brotherliness as it teaches into business and politics. No religious system may be able to get into these secular fields all the brotherliness they need, but Brotherhood is by nature far better able than sectarianism to socialize these and other secular fields.

Brotherhood is in line with the socially-useful features of historic religion. It would make use of these in so far as they prove the best agencies in building the new and better religion into the structure of society.

Brotherhood is congenial to, so can use, much of the literature and sentiment of other religions. It has a certain advantage in that it is free to borrow from all others. Sects hesitate to borrow from one another lest members stray from one sect into another. Brotherhood has no such hesitancy. It believes that expressions of brotherliness and aids toward its achievement belong to all mankind. A sect does not have the right to exclusive use of a religious achievement even though the religious men who made it happened to belong to that sect.

Brotherhood-free from sectarian localism and other 93

forms of narrowness-is a particularly rich field for the creation of literature and sentiment. Its theme is noble, yet it has the common touch throughout. Angles from which the central theme may be approached and methods of treatment are numberless. Brotherhood is full of interest for all classes of people. It is, in fact, the greatest human interest story in history. 

In time it will have the most nearly adequate literature and sentiment of any religion. 

No other religion can even approach the adequacy of Brotherhood as a world religion. Inclusive, cohesive, adaptable, free, in fellowship with God and brotherly toward men, Brotherhood meets the needs of all nations and of all classes within each nation. 

Will, Thought, and Action

Even without organization, Brotherhood has much to offer each individual. Personal relationship to God and to men is invaluable. Like-minded people may have cordial contact and warm fellowship without organization.

Even unorganized Brothers may be productive, sometimes very productive. Each Brother, unorganized as well as organized, may and should be a radiant point of the will, thought, and action which are in the hearts, minds, and activities of those who are moved by the spirit of brotherliness. Unorganized and organized will, thought, and action should reinforce each other. 

Leadership on both sides should recognize the need of the closest coordination and cooperation between the two. They are workers in the same vineyard. 

The individual Brother is one of the key factors in the growth of Brotherhood. Realizing how very much 

Brotherhood does for the individual and society, each Brother has a desire to share his religious life with 94

others. Brothers should yield to this desire for their own satisfaction and as a service to others. In this way each becomes an active radiant point. In conversation and correspondence with friends and acquaintances, one may speak of the meaning and helpfulness of the new religion. Joy in the fellowship which is the very heart of Brotherhood makes the teaching as well as the living of it a genuine joy.

The family, likewise, is a key factor in the growth of Brotherhood. There is no more appropriate place for brotherhood than the family. Parents who are 

Brothers can bring their children up in an intelligent religion, even though the prevailing religion of the community is very superstitious. They can bring up their children to be religious in a community where there is a dearth of religion. The decline of the family as a center of religious instruction and life has been brought about by the quality of prevailing religion. 

Parents who could not conscientiously and wholeheartedly subscribe to authoritative revealed religion got out of the habit of emphasizing religion in the home. Brotherhood makes it agreeable for even the most intelligent parents to teach and practice religion in the home.

Organized Brotherhood is better than unorganized for the individual Brother. It is far better for society. 

Organization produces more brotherliness among Brothers and increases its joy. It can carry the Brotherhood message effectively to all mankind-at home and abroad. Organization gives power to destroy destructive forces and to cooperate with constructive ones. 

Organized Brotherhood is better than unorganized because it is a more effective instrument for increasing brotherliness of many kinds, in many places, at all times. 95

Organized will, thought, action, and institutions are included. They are closely interrelated. Will to achieve any part of Brotherhood excites thought and action. As thought suggests routes to achievement, will is encouraged to take action. As action results in achievement, both will and thought are encouraged to put forth still greater effort. 

Each institution is a unified objective expression of will, thought, and action. It is their cause as well as effect for it increases and uses them to create more Brotherhood. Wisely and zealously used, the four are effective agencies in increasing brotherliness among men and in strengthening the organized movement itself.

Will to Brotherhood is strengthened when individuals who desire brotherhood find one another. The more numerous and earnest the contacts the stronger the will to Brotherhood. When people find that the attitude of many others is similar to their own, this knowledge encourages by giving confidence. There are many individuals who desire Brotherhood, so the finding and keeping in contact with one another are important factors in organizing and promoting it. 

People love to talk. They enjoy giving their opinion whether it is considerate or just a snap judgment. One need not fear therefore that inquiries concerning attitude toward Brotherhood will offend unduly. A fanatic here and there may take offense, but the friendlier attitude of the many far outweighs the hostility of the few.

When discussing Brotherhood, even to strangers, one need not be colorless. Those who will it should say so freely and forcefully. Expressions of will tend to be contagious. A person who discusses Brotherhood in this way does more than find others who believe in it. 96

He makes converts, so increases the number of people who believe as he does.

Finding and making of those who will Brotherhood are achieved by the same or closely related methodsincreasing the number and friendliness of contacts and presenting the cause of Brotherhood in as attractive a light as possible. Even though the finding is but the simple process of locating, it is certain to result in considerable interstimulation. 

Making of those who will Brotherhood out of those who are neutral or hostile is highly creative. It is the goal toward which appreciative Brothers strive. In this as in other fields, practice gives confidence and tends to make perfect. 

Contacts may be face to face or by correspondence.

The person who believes in Brotherhood should use his daily contacts in social life, religion, education, political life and, to some extent, even in business. Contacts with family, friends, acquaintances and strangers should be used to broach the topic.

The more contacts one makes the more proficient one becomes in making them. As society becomes more accustomed to discussions of Brotherhood, such discussions will seem the most natural thing in the world. 

Brotherhood should be the topic of many letters to editors of daily papers, especially to editors who regularly print letters from readers. It should be the topic in letters to columnists, radio commentators, public officials, educators, religious leaders, authors, and others who make public opinion.

The relationship between the individual will and the social will is strongly reciprocal. When many individual wills are vigorously expressing the same point of view, the social will has a strong tendency to fall in line. When the social will is vigorous in a given direc- 97

tion, it modifies many individual wills into line with itself.

The most effective will to Brotherhood would be the combined will of individuals and society. Society is so thoroughly saturated with sectarianism, however, that the social will to Brotherhood has scarcely been born. That will can be generated by the will of individuals. As individual Brothers find one another and make new converts they tend to transform the social will into the likeness of their own.

Firm and harmonious integration of individual wills with the social will is the best organization of the will to Brotherhood. Each Brother can play a part in bringing about this integration. 

Intimations of the will to Brotherhood are very numerous, but they indicate that the will is now very weak in the great majority of cases. They occur in the personal, social, political, educational, religious, and economic fields. The keen observer uses intimations to locate the will so that he may multiply and strengthen it.

Different intimations point to different elements in 

Brotherhood. One intimation may show a longing for brotherliness. Another, a dissatisfaction with sectarianism or with humanism. Another, an open and inquiring mind in search of religious truth. These and many other attitudes indicate the beginnings of the will to Brotherhood or a conscious need which only 

Brotherhood can fill adequately.

The conscious need is generally only partial. The person is conscious of needing something without realizing that something is Brotherhood. The promoter of Brotherhood broadens the conscious need until it 98

includes all elements of Brotherhood. He deepens and strengthens the consciousness.

In addition to creating the elements not already in existence and strengthening all elements, the process includes unification of existing elements and focalization of all elements in Brotherhood. 

Organized thought includes the thoughts of the rank and file along with those of leadership. Inclusion of the thoughts as well as the will and action of the rank and file is particularly important in a movement so thoroughly saturated with humanity as Brotherhood.

The range of thought is extremely wide. 

At one extreme are philosophers who speculate on the nature of God, the universe, and man. At the other are those practically-minded people of detail who try to find more efficient ways of adjusting Brotherhood to the everyday needs of the people. Every aspect of the movement should receive careful, constructive, persistent thought.

The thought-unorganized or organized-is never dictatorial or authoritative. Every person is free at all times to question any thought and to rethink any part or the whole. People are encouraged to do this. 

Fluidity of thought tends to eliminate the faulty or the out-of-date and to bring into the movement those thoughts that are most helpful at a given time and place.

The starting point and plan of people's thinking vary very greatly from person to person. One may begin with the system of one or more of the sects. Another may begin with those secular agencies which have been most successful in practicing brotherliness. Another may begin with an analysis of the nature of brotherliness and its relation to social forces which are, or 99

can be made, available. Still others may begin from even more greatly varying points of view. 

Provision should be made for stimulating, exchanging, sifting, and recording thought. Only in this way can facilities for productive thinking and its results be fully utilized.

Thought is stimulated by attempts to deal with real situations. Brothers should be keenly and analytically alive to what is going on about them, particularly the brotherly and unbrotherly aspects of community life. Calling attention to community evils and suggesting something be done about them stimulate thinking to devise ways and means of effective action. 

Calling attention to brotherly aspects in some parts leads to mental exploration of means to bring brotherliness to other parts.

Discussion, informally by individuals or more formally in institutions, stimulates, exchanges, and sifts thought. As many and as convenient facilities for discussion, oral and written, as possible should be provided. Discussions should be adjusted to local conditions, including the interests and capacities of participants.

National and international as well as local discussion leaders should be developed. Printed outlines, reference readings, and other discussion aids should be developed and distributed as rapidly as possible. 

Brotherhood periodicals should take the lead in keeping before the membership and others the most pertinent topics for discussion. They should give relevant recent information to aid in making each discussion as helpful as possible. Radio programs should deal with the livelier issues.

As books appear on various phases of the movement they will record in permanent form the more solid 100

material produced by it. As many books appear, Brotherhood libraries will be built up. 

In time attempts at thoroughgoing organization and systemization of Brotherhood will be made. Authority, sacredness, rigidity, and ossification-the besetting sins of sectarianism-will attempt to gain control of the movement as they have of other religions, even those which set out as reform and progressive movements.

Every effort must be made to keep the dead hand of tradition from throttling organized and unorganized Brotherhood. The best hope of success lies in keeping the attention of leadership and rank and file on current items. Solving of current problems-sensing needs and meeting them-will keep the movement alive and growing.

Spontaneous action and sporadic action are helpful, but organized, systematic, continuous action will bear a greater part of the load of promotion. To the latter, the greater part of the thought of the movement must be given. Into the latter, the greater part of the effort in behalf of Brotherhood will be put at present and in the future.

Informal organization may be very helpful. A small number of people bound together by the spirit of Brotherhood can be a creative force in any community. 

They can win new members on the one hand and spread the spirit of brotherliness on the other. There may be no regular meetings of the group, no officers-just men and women bound by their loyalty to a cause in which they believe and for which they are willing to work.

Informal gatherings of neighbors, young people, and others at the homes of members, would give opportunity to call attention to the movement. If special 101

attention were paid to those who are not members of any sect, sectarian jealousy would be kept to a minimum. 

Informal organization may exist among members of any place of business, social organization, political club, school, college, or other institution. Brothers are in close touch with fellow members of their respective institutions, so are able to add a personal touch and appeal to the message of Brotherhood. In addition to forming a nucleus of Brotherhood in each institution, the organization would encourage each individual member to promote the cause when away from, as well as within, his own institution.

Committees of correspondence should be formed to keep centers in touch with one another. Letters passing between centers would give information, encouragement, and guidance.

Individuals as well as committees may have organized correspondence. Widely scattered leaders would find it very helpful to keep in regular touch with one another. This would be a quick way of letting one another know of successful action in any part of the country or world.

Leaders should be ready to visit neighboring and even distant communities to carry forward the work. Many of these would be volunteers who serve without pay. 

Some would be in the employ of Brotherhood organizations or depend on contribution from listeners for such sums as might be given.

Provision should be made for outdoor as well as for indoor meetings. The great crowds who throng the busiest sections of our large cities are a varied soil into which the seeds of Brotherhood should be sown broadcast. A soap box on which to stand, plenty of social courage, and, where required, a police permit are all 102

that are needed to let the crowds know what the new movement is and intends to do. 

Smaller cities, towns, and even villages offer good opportunities for outdoor meetings. The people there are less restless than those of the great cities. Competition from places of amusement, recreation, and business pressure is less keen. People are more inclined to stop to listen. Greater unity and neighborliness in the smaller places are favorable factors if the seed of the new religion take root.

Music, crayon sketching, posters, and leaflets draw crowds in city or smaller centers of population. Even people who linger but a few seconds may get some idea of the general nature of Brotherhood; enough, perhaps, to stimulate discussion.

Many forms of advertising should be used. Newspapers, periodicals, radio, billboards, handbills, pamphlets, buttons and banners, as well as speakers, should be used to the greatest extent that financial and human resources will permit. Postal cards containing a brief notice of Brotherhood should be distributed by the millions to college students and other groups of young people. Mailing lists might include leaders in all walks of life. As the movement develops, emblems should be adopted and used as widely as possible. 

Unorganized Brothers and others interested in the new religion should be invited to send their names and addresses to a central agency. Appropriate literature should be sent to all who do so.

An especially urgent appeal should be made to the unchurched and to the dissatisfied churched. They should be shown that they may have organized religion without sectarianism.

Every effort should be made to gear the vitality and enthusiasm of youth to Brotherhood. 103

Institutions of Brotherhood No matter how valuable unorganized and informally organized Brotherhood may be, they need to be supplemented by formal, permanent institutions. Such institutions will be invaluable in promoting will, thought and action. They should be very numerous. 

Local conditions are, in most cases, the chief determiner of the appropriate types of institutions for each community. As conditions change, types of institution may require modification. Results should be noted constantly and adjustments made in the light of these.

Brotherhood Churches would provide opportunity for work and worship. Recognition of the opportunity and obligation to work at Brotherhood may attract many who have been looking upon churches as places of worship chiefly.

Brotherhood Churches are particularly important for countries where the church is now the chief local religious unit. Sectarians are already familiar with this type of organization. Religious leaders have been trained in it, and this training would be helpful in getting the new churches organized and under way. 

The local church, in spite of its sectarianism, has a good name in the community. This reputation would be a factor in giving the new religion a favorable reputation.

Brotherhood Churches must not feel constrained to follow closely the type of worship or other activity of sectarian churches. They need to borrow much with which former sectarians are familiar, but they need to modify and invent. The chief objective-brotherliness-should take precedence over sectarian practice or community habits. 104

People conditioned to various sects are bound to feel differently in the same type of worship or church activity. In one community, people may be accustomed to and prefer very little ritual. In another community, people may be accustomed to and prefer very elaborate ritual. Brotherhood should adjust its program to suit the tastes of people in each community. 

This adjustment refers to short-run program only. 

Presumably the difference in taste is due chiefly to conditioning. There is no valid reason why this difference should continue generation after generation. If it should be found that meager ritual or elaborate ritual is better suited by nature to achieve the purposes of Brotherhood, children should be conditioned to the more effective type. 

Building Brotherhood Churches must be experimental. This refers to type of administration, worship, community service, and other factors. As Brotherhood makes no claim that its form of organization is divinely and authoritatively determined, its members should feel particularly free to experiment. Devices to serve as criteria should be worked out so that each church may measure its progress absolutely and against that of other churches.

The process of organizing a Brotherhood Church will vary from place to place:

Where the majority of members of a local church have come to accept Brotherhood, the church may vote to leave the sect to which it belongs and go over to Brotherhood. The church may, on the contrary, prefer to remain in the original sect and work as a Brotherhood nucleus there. If numerous churches so remain, many sects may become strongly Brotherhood in fact, though not in name. This would be in line with the modern trend, for very large minorities- 105

perhaps majorities-in many churches have departed very far from the historical position of their sect. At the present time there are numerous members in various sects who are much closer to Brotherhood than to the historic position of their own sect. The Brotherhood movement encourages a clarification of opinion. 

Whole sects may evolve into Brotherhood without change of name.

Where a minority of the members of a local church are Brothers in belief, they may remain in the original sect and exchange views with fellow members until there is a crystallization of views. If the membership discusses religious fundamentals freely and frankly, there should be a rapid shift among the more intelligent from traditional religion to Brotherhood. 

The minority in one church may prefer to withdraw and organize a Brotherhood Church. The minority in a number of churches may wish to withdraw and unite to form a Brotherhood Church.

Where Brothers are not members of any church they are free, of course, to organize a church at once. 

If all opportunities are followed, Brotherhood Churches should spring up rapidly. 

Many Brotherhood Churches are likely to be small and their equipment meager during their early life. 

Where no permanent building is available, meetings may be held in homes of members or in buildings which may be rented or borrowed. Fellowship among members may be as great without a permanent building as with one. The church may be concerned about and interested in community improvement even though it has no "home" save in the hearts of its members.

Hymnals, literature, and other equipment used by various Christian, Jewish, Buddhist, Hindu, Islamic, 106

Taoist, Confucian, and other sects, may be used freely in so far as they are appropriate. 

Worship may be similar to that of any wholesome worship of other sects, or may be any new forms which may be invented.

It is well to bear in mind that the religious heritage of the human race belongs to the whole human race rather than to the sect which happened to be in favor at the time various parts of that heritage were created. 

A Brotherhood Church should be active in teaching and practicing the improvement of society to promote Brotherhood. Even as it recreates its members and as many others as possible to make them more brotherly, it will be studying its community and society to see opportunities for recreating them. A Brotherhood 

Church ought to be the most practical of churches as its hands are not tied by tradition. It will serve its own members, but it will also serve society, both near and so far as resources permit-far. 

The spirit of the church is particularly important. 

It should promote cooperation with other religious groups, but this cooperation ought not to prevent mutual examination and criticism. In religion, as in other fields, there should be a survival of the fit. The test of fitness is service to mankind.

Methods of organizing the first Brotherhood Churches, as well as later developments, must be experimental. There is no authoritative approach. Experience is the best teacher in the long run. If each group of 

Brothers will use its best judgment when it plans and organizes its church, comparative efficiency of various methods will become apparent. 

Two periods in the organization of Brotherhood Churches are likely to occur, though with much overlapping. During the early days of the movement, 107

churches will probably be organized by individuals rather than by sectarian churches swinging over as units. Like-minded individuals in contact with one another will organize. These individuals who recognize in one another kindred spirits may have met rather informally in the homes of members to confer, plan, or organize. At first, the tendency will be for individuals of highest quality to provide leadership and a large proportion of membership.

The second period will see local churches-probably large numbers of them-swing over as units. As the global Brotherhood idea permeates the religious life of a community, more and more people will become restless in their sectarian pens. With Brotherhoodminded majorities insisting that their churches unite with the new movement, there may be mass evacuation of sectarian pens.

Until this second period is reached, clergymen will be in a particularly difficult position. Most of them are employed by sects. If they sever connection with established sects they may be giving up positions with little prospect of finding ones in Brotherhood. Clergymen in unsectarian schools are in the best position to unite with the new movement. They are not required to accept sectarianism as a condition of employment. 

When time for formal, legal organization of churches arrives, organization under state law as nonprofit associations may be advisable. When an independent local sectarian church swings over as a unit, no new legal adjustment may be necessary except change of name.

Where local church property is in the name of church officials rather than in the name of the church itself, legal steps should be taken to require surrender of legal title to the church property. The practice of 108

some autocratic hierarchs of keeping legal title to church property in their own names may not prevent the local membership from recovering the property they have given.

Before a Brotherhood Church is organized, a very careful survey should be made of the numerous factors involved. A church too weak to take root and grow is likely to give the new movement a bad name. Such an unfavorable impression should be avoided.

The survey should include number and type of prospective charter members, other prospective members, quality of leadership, property available, location of nearest Brotherhood Churches, and sectarian agencies. 

It will probably be advisable in many cases to organize legally some time before opening for formal worship. Much ground work must be done before a new church can hope to compete with established sectarian churches. In acquiring property and in organizing preliminary campaigns, legal standing will prove very convenient.

Before beginning formal and regular worship, equipment should be gathered and plans made for months or years in advance. Literature and songs should have been selected. Ritual requirements-meager or elaborate should have been met as far as possible. Leadership should have received some preliminary training. 

Everything is eclectic and experimental, but it should be planned as carefully and fully as possible.

Brotherhood Clubs can be invaluable agents of promotion and growth. They are united by very strong psychic bonds-fatherhood and brotherhood. They provide opportunity for continuous fellowship among Brothers. They offer perennial opportunity for interpersonal stimulation and sympathy. They give a sense of security. They bring the joy which comes from 109

realization of a joint and successful endeavor of a high order.

Brotherhood Clubs improve religion itself. They get religion out into the open where the accuracy and worth of sectarian points of view can be examined. 

In a kindly critical way, Brothers can investigate, discuss, and evaluate various views and practices-their own included. By this method they can detect many untrue things and numerous unwise practices. Superstitions are more likely to be recognized as such. 

Brotherhood Clubs can be very active agencies in promoting Brotherhood in school, neighborhood, and community. They can be particularly alert to detect evils and do what they can to remove them. They can further the finer features in their surroundings. They can raise the general level of life about them. They can preach and practice the social gospel continuously. 

The first task for those who wish to organize a Brotherhood Club is to locate one another. Any person who is doubtful about or dissatisfied with his religion, or with religion in general, may broach the topic of religion to friends and acquaintances. He is likely to find others who are like-minded. Some of them are likely to agree that Brotherhood is very desirable. 

Those who accept Brotherhood become the nucleus of an informal group whose members exchange opinions among themselves and with others. The discussions may be casual ones as members happen to meet. They may take place in the homes of various members or other convenient places.

If Brothers desire to organize a formal group, they should arrange for a permanent place of meeting, officers, committees, and other features. They may provide a brief constitution, by-laws, and emblem. If so, parliamentary rules should be observed. Brotherhood 110

Clubs may be very useful in social, as well as in religious, training if members are willing to work. 

Topics appropriate for discussion in Brotherhood Clubs are legion. They arise out of the sociology, psychology, philosophy, history, and application of religion. Both personal and community aspects are included. Truth in religion, scriptures and history of various religions, contemporary religious practices, relation of science to religion, reason and religion, mental health, the nature of man, worship, the church, freedom, liberalism, democracy, unity, universality, opportunity, inequalities, ideas of the good, ethics, institutionalism, religious development of children, and social pathology are but a few of many appropriate topics. Any topic, in fact, which is vitally related to 

Fatherhood of God or Brotherhood of Man is appropriate.

Brotherhood Clubs should promote intersectarian and unsectarian discussion groups as well as Brotherhood groups. Emphasis should be placed on "truth first". No person with love for truth can rightly object to such discussion. No person with a solid appreciation of the truth of his sect or personal views will object to it. These discussion groups inject light into the dark places of religion where superstition and unethical and unwise practices have lingered. 

Brotherhood Clubs should locate unbrotherly aspects of neighborhood and community. Brotherhood Clubs in schools should locate unbrotherly aspects of schools. 

In nearly all localities there are aspects which can be readily recognized as unbrotherly. 

Brotherhood Clubs should initiate, organize, and execute campaigns to reduce unbrotherliness whereever it exists. To do this with a will requires the development of sympathy for various items on the part 111

of Brothers. Only when Brothers come to feel the brotherhood they preach will they be able to practice it vigorously and induce others to practice it. 

Brotherhood Clubs should give considerable attention to public relations. They should prepare notices about the Brotherhood movement and its activities. 

They should use the newspaper, the radio, the platform, and other media to let the public know what it is doing.

Brotherhood Clubs should collect appropriate literature, music, works of art, and other means to create and refresh the Brotherhood spirit. Brotherhood points of view are to be found in many places. Various countries throughout the entire world can make a contribution.

Brotherhood Clubs should promote the creation of literature, music, art, and other forms of culture. With attention centered on and energy organized about the concepts "fatherhood" and "brotherhood", much very fine creative work should be achieved. Humanity in its long climb upward has never conceived or used finer themes. 

Brotherhood Clubs should correspond with one another. They can give one another many helpful suggestions and a great deal of encouragement. During the early years of a great movement, such guidance and encouragement are likely to prove two of the most helpful of all contributions to growth. Since Brotherhood stresses light rather than tradition, it is eager to get and use all the wisdom it can.

Brotherhood Clubs should correspond with nonBrotherhood as well as with Brotherhood organizations. 

Their outgoing letters may carry many helpful suggestions and generate good will. Incoming letters may bring helpful information and provide guidance. 112

Brotherhood Clubs should cultivate friendly and harmonious relations with other well-meaning agencies in so far as this can be done without adulterating the quality of their work or restricting the aggressiveness of their activity. A new movement, particularly, is likely to be misunderstood. As other neighborhood and community agencies come to appreciate the high motive and helpful activity of Brothers, they will cooperate more and more fully.

Brotherhood Clubs may be neighborhood wide, community wide, or even more extensive. Some may be by correspondence. There may be women's clubs, men's clubs, girls' clubs, boys' clubs, and clubs without distinction of age or sex. Clubs may be organized about special interests including sports, summer camps, reading, study, citizenship, current events, debating, social work, poor relief, and travel. Clubs should be in public and private high schools and colleges. They should be among organized and unorganized laborers, farmers, business men, and professional people. They may be organized by place of business. 

By organizing large numbers of clubs in various fields, Brotherhood can extend its influence very rapidly. As the public becomes acquainted with its purpose, they will welcome its entrance into many fields in addition to religion in the narrow sense. 

Brotherhood Centers would yield great satisfaction, enhance prestige, and win new members. Some centers should be rooms for reading and relaxation where the public as well as members might gather. Some should be in the mountains, or at the seashore where vacationists could stay and conventions gather. Some should be along crowded highways where tourists and others might stop and linger. Each center would be an experiment in brotherliness. 113

Brotherhood Schools should be organized experimentally from the nursery school to the graduate school and professional school. They would try to do more effectively what public and the best private schools are already trying to do-socialize young people. They would be freer than the public schools to experiment in new ways of teaching young people to be brotherly. Unlike sectarian schools which put sectarianism first to divide society, Brotherhood Schools would attempt to unite society more closely and integrate its thought and activity on the high plane of brotherliness. They would be a much-needed addition to the educational systems of various countries. 

Children and youth trained in Brotherhood Schools would be an important leaven in society. All parts of society-social, political, economic, educational, religious, and international-would feel the effect of those who have been trained to put brotherliness first. 

A comprehensive promotion of Brotherhood is going to cost much money. The prospect of the new movement raising much money in the near future is not bright. Brotherhood faces one very serious initial disadvantage financially compared to many of the sects. It cannot promise its radio listeners salvation from hell-fire or cure from incurable disease if they will send in enough dollars to keep the wave lengths saturated with sectarianism. Nor can it tell the parents that the road to heaven runs much closer to sectarian than to public schools. Its appeal for funds must be on a higher level. 

The basic financial appeal of Brotherhood, like its other appeals, must be positive-based on love. It must never make fear basic. It acknowledges man's gratitude to God. It recognizes man's obligation to his fellow-men. Unless it can get financial aid on this 114

basis it cannot get it at all without denying its own nature.

Fundamentally, the task of raising funds is twofold. 

Brotherhood must increase love of fellow-men to the giving point. It must convince membership and the public that Brotherhood is ready and able to achieve its proposed purpose. If it can do these two things successfully, money will be forthcoming. 

Brotherhood, like the Red Cross, has one great advantage all funds are for the central purpose of human well-being. Unlike sectarianism, it does not waste much of its income keeping alive hoary superstitions or operating a cumbrous salvation machine. 

As the public comes to appreciate this superiority, the tendency will be for a greater proportion of religious funds to flow into Brotherhood.

Methods of raising funds will doubtless follow reasonably closely the methods now in use by various religious and charitable agencies. Since the public is acquainted with these methods, a considerable advantage lies in using them. Quantity of income will depend largely on quality of institutions which make use of them. 

Brothers will draw inspiration from their cooperative fellowship as they seek to use the most efficient methods to achieve the well-being of men. To experience the Fatherhood of God more fully and to achieve the Brotherhood of Man more completely, Brotherhood Churches, Clubs, and Centers should be organized in every community of every country. 

High Time To Begin 

Now is the time to organize Brotherhood and to promote it as vigorously as possible. Brotherhood is timeless by nature, but the proper timing of the or- 115

ganization and promotion of the religion of Brotherhood is very important. Both the broad cultural background and the current conditions are ripe for this new and better religion.

A considerable degree of cultural and intellectual advancement is necessary to penetrate the mass of accumulated superstition in which mankind in general and sectarians in particular have been floundering. 

Without such advancement the light of the new religion might be washed out by the ignorance of the masses or deliberately snuffed out by designing sectarian leadership. The required degree of advancement has been achieved if cultural areas are free to function. 

The chief danger now is from sectarians who are still doing all they can to dilute cultural channels with sectarianism.

Civil and military contacts are broadening. Mobility of population is high. Workers who flocked into centers of wartime industry are still readjusting their lives. Mobility has tended to reduce sectarianism in two ways. Moving out of the old environment has meant lessening of various controls, including sect. 

Contacts, often very intense, with people of various sects, have tended to break down localism and produce elements of cosmopolitanism. Contacts, by inviting comparison, tend to reduce the religion of various sects to the common denominator.

The strong global sentiment that pervades the atmosphere in various countries would promote world brotherhood. When people create or even talk about world currency, banking, economics, police force, politics, capital, and other global features, they are conditioning themselves to global points of view. Acceptance of global in any field tends to promote the idea of global in other fields. 116

The crisis of World War II tended to let religion flow across sectarian lines on the one hand at the very time that the need of religion induced more fervent participation in even the disgustingly low sectarian practices on the other.

Men in the military forces of various nations were thrown together irrespective of sect. Military men are inclined to value one another according to what each is as a man rather than according to sectarian membership. Comradeship in arms tended to wear away prejudices -including religious prejudices which divided men. Men who lived together in fox holes are not so easily segregated into sectarian pens. 

Many veterans refuse to allow themselves to be cramped into narrow sectarianism like so many sardines in a can.

This situation creates a golden opportunity to approach those from whom sectarianism was largely burnt out by the fires of war. Men of various faiths who fought shoulder to shoulder through hellish years of war have much more respect for one another's God and much less faith in the exclusiveness of their own religious system. 

Postwar restlessness gives a special and intense urgency to the friendliness, stability, and security provided by Brotherhood. Veterans and nonveterans alike feel a great need for psychic rest. To the more enlightened, restlessness is increased rather than diminished by attempts to enclose them in sectarian pens and doctor them up with sectarian nostrums.

Many ties were broken by the war-home ties and larger social ties. There is still a feeling of isolation, of lonesomeness. New ties must be formed to replace those which were broken. The new should be deep and broad enough to be adequate. Even though the 117

old narrow sectarian ties seemed broad enough in prewar days, they are no longer so. A new force is needed to heal, a new tie to bind. Brotherhood is the only religion deep and broad enough to provide adequate ties in the postwar world.

Even as the feeling of isolation increases, rapid and prevalent communication eats away the fact of physical isolation. The coincidence of the two isolations is not accidental. As winds from the four corners of the globe blow through sectarian pens, inmates begin to feel very lonesome as they look out upon the worldwide cultural pastures in which nonsectarians feed. The more intelligent pen-mates are especially disturbed for they begin to recognize the intellectual and ethical poverty in which they have been clothed. 

On the home front, the people of the United States are becoming more keenly conscious of their minorities, frictions, and repulsions. They are beginning to be ashamed of the fact that men and women are discriminated against in employment, politics, place of residence, and a thousand other things because of creed or color. And the sectarians are as active as the nonreligious in keeping the minorities down. Brotherhood may be in their written creeds; it is not in their overt acts. 

Religious people have known that they have needed something, but have not known what they have needed or how to go about finding out. They have become increasingly aware of the failure of sectarianism to remove social injustice and unify society. The awareness is awakening a critical attitude toward sectarian systems.

On the world front, Brotherhood tends to coincide in extent with expanding culture, including institutions. Much of that culture is disruptive and badly 118

in need of a cohesive cultural trait to hold it together. 

Brotherhood is best adapted for this cohesive function because it tends to permeate the whole and reduce harshness even as it gets various elements to harmonize differences and accept the common goal. 

The United Nations must have more than a common political purpose to hold it together permanently and securely. If special interests are permitted to go their irresponsible way unchecked, they will gradually eat away the base on which the world union has been built. 

Sectarianism is as deep and may become as dangerous a special interest as any other. Unless Brotherhood replaces sectarianism there is much reason to fear that "religion" will be a major factor in bringing about a third world war.

World economic and social conflict is as bitter as, though less bloody than, world political conflict. It, too, requires oil on its troubled waters. Brotherhood is that oil.

Whether a person looks at the world from the political, economic, or social point of view, one conclusion is inescapable—it must have a common composition as well as a common constitution. Religion-not sectarianism is an indispensable part of any strong composition.

The youth of the nation and of the world are aroused. They want, and are beginning to demand, something better in every field. That something better will not just happen. If it comes at all, it will arise out of a fundamental philosophy of life in harmony with an inclusive betterment. 

Secular cultures fall short of the new goal in breadth, depth, and power. Sectarianism is far too narrow and 119

contradictory to achieve it. An unsectarian worldwide religion is required. Brotherhood meets that requirement.

Man, at last, has found a religion which frees him from the superstitions which have clung so tenaciously since primitive times. This religion will, in addition, bind the entire world in a cooperative fellowship. It will tend to elevate humanity and ennoble men. Viewed from any angle, the time for the organization and promotion of Brotherhood is more than ripe. It is very, very urgent. 120

A FINAL WORD 

The culture of a people gives birth to a religion congenial to that culture. Emergent world culture would give birth to a world religion. Builders of 

Brotherhood are striving to stay the hand of sectarian abortionists who would murder the new religion before it can get a firm grip on life. 

The idea of a superior world religion appeals to the most enlightened members of various groups. It appeals also to those young members who have not been intellectually deadened by super-indoctrination. It would appeal to very many who now consider themselves nonreligious or antireligious.

Intellectually many leaders, including a considerable proportion of the better-educated clergy, are ready for a superior world religion. Many religious leaders and a considerable number of laymen in the West who call themselves Christians do so because Christianity is the prevailing religion there. They do not mean to suggest that they are more Christian than they are Jewish, Islamic, Buddhist, or Confucian. They mean only that they are religious. 

Educated leaders subscribe to the ethical teachings common to all religions plus such sympathetic attachments as brotherhood and fatherhood. They belong psychologically to a world religion rather than to the various sects to which most of them belong. Psychologically many, if not most, of them would be happier in a world religion. Indefiniteness of the word "Christian" is so great that it may signify anything from the most conservative theology of fundamentalists to the most liberal view of modernists. Other religions, too, are nearly or quite as indefinite as Christianity. 

The term "world religion" would free liberals from restrictions of local theologies and local institutional- 121

ism. The practical value of the adherence of liberal leaders would be immense. They have contact with great numbers of religious people and institutions which would be immensely valuable in organizing the new religion.

The creation of worldwide sympathy between various members of a world religion might be difficult, but would be less difficult than the present task of creating worldwide sympathy between members of different sectarian groups. Each great sect now gets along with little sympathy between members of its various sects in many countries. As to relations among the great sects, they have frequently been characterized by the bitterest hostility rather than by sympathy and good will.

Innate natures of men in various countries, as well as within each country, have enough in common to promote a good deal of similarity in religious practice. 

Intellectual level of activity in connection with worship and other procedures would vary, but the level between communities or even world geographic areas need not be greater than that between cultural groups within each community-particularly in the long run. 

Ceremonial and other appeals would have to be adjusted to fit needs of specific groups, but all could be integrated to the central themes of brotherhood and fatherhood. These themes are simple enough for the cradle, yet profound enough for the most mature minds. 

Brotherhood bears in its own bosom the seeds of its own success if soil can be found and cultivated. 

Volunteers are needed to plant and cultivate. Organized power of religion is still opposed to organizing the essence of religion-brotherhood. 

Until such time as Brotherhood takes root, those 122

who walk that way may walk alone, but the pathway will be upward. Those who prefer to walk upward, even though alone, point the way to better things for the timid.

Each individual must now choose between sect and religion, between the way of the majority and the way of truth. It is a matter of individual responsibility, of personal integrity. In final analysis, each person must do his own choosing, but the nature of the choice will go a long way toward determining the mature character of those who make the choice. 

Throughout countless ages, men have hungered and thirsted for one another. Throughout countless ages, obstacles have divided man from man. Across the wall of obstacles men have sought but seldom found deep and extensive brotherliness in one another. The ages have been wearing away the obstacles which divide men, but haphazard erosion has been very slow. The time has come to organize and push brotherliness zealously and persistently. 

Today the call is for those who prefer to pioneer in the broad and promising field of Brotherhood rather than to continue to try to get religious sustenance from the stunted and dried grasses in the narrow and barren pens of sectarianism.

The chief but inexcusable obstacle to the creation of World Brotherhood is sectarianism with its vested interests. Sects suck up nearly all the energy contributed to organized religion. As the religious impulse seeks fruitage in organization, it is seized by sectarians and turned into sectarian channels. 

Sectarianism is always active in transforming religious interest and energy into itself. In order to protect its virtual monopoly of the religious field-of the organized religious field, certainly-sectarianism is 123

alert to detect and destroy embryonic global Brotherhood. Because global Brotherhood neglects or rejects the localisms of various sects, the sects treat it as an enemy-the narrow sects as one of their deadliest enemies. Global Brotherhood, therefore, must fight two battles simultaneously-one against indifference and irreligion and one against sectarianism. 

The restraining power of sects is particularly great because various other agencies cooperate with them. 

Sects-identified in the popular mind with religionhave a great deal of prestige and power. Any evaluation of sects which is unfavorable is likely to be taken as an example of religious intolerance. Means of communication frown on or refuse to transmit such evaluation. Social agencies generally go further and condemn those who make the evaluations, no matter how accurate. A new religion therefore has to face the opposition of many secular agencies which cooperate with the established sects because the new seems to be trying to undermine that which prevailing community sentiment looks upon as religion. 

This obstacle of sectarianism, to be sure, is being reduced by the rising level of intelligence of the population, but its reduction in that way is painfully slow. 

Direct attempt to remove sectarianism as an obstacle to World Brotherhood is urgently needed.

The direct attempt to remove sectarianism should be one of evolution rather than one of revolution. Each religious sect contains very fine religious elements. 

Each has intimations of brotherhood. Any real religion-World Brotherhood or other-would do better to make use of these fine elements and intimations in building a new religion. It is much better to do this than to attempt to wipe out sects quickly by destruc- 124

tive criticism, then try to build the new religion on the ruins of the old.

So soon as people generally appreciate fully the central truth that the belief of each in a particular sect is a product of early indoctrination, they will be more willing to modify their beliefs. It is far better for those who are becoming skeptical of sectarianism, as well as for World Brotherhood, to have their attention called to the new movement so that they will have something new in which they can believe as they modify or discard the old.

In times of crises people change more readily than during normal times. A crisis and the forces which produce it combine to shake people out of old ruts. At such times people even change fundamental points of view-a rare occurrence during normal times. 

The depression of the 1930s and World War II pro duced a great crisis or series of crises in the lives of nations and people the world over. The atom bomb raised fear to unprecedented heights. Many people lost faith in the old foundations-economic, social, political, and religious. They became more receptive to the new than they had ever been. Religion, as well as other fields of culture, had and still has a golden opportunity. Now is the time of times to forge the bond of global religion to unite all men. 

The increasing number of people who emphasize humanity and its needs are becoming ever more impatient with theological niceties which divide sects. 

As greater and greater stress is laid on human wellbeing and kindly attitudes which accompany it, there is a tendency of kindly people of various culturesincluding religion to draw more closely together. 

Those who strive to banish want, fear, and other evils are more interested in what other people are doing to 125

assist than in what the others believe. Religious security tends to merge with social security. The religious test is the test of service. That test can be appreciated globally; sectarian tests cannot.

Competition from a high class world religion would be good for the various sects. It would force them to reëxamine their sectarianism to ascertain to what extent it is valid. It would cause sects to reduce localism, moderate unethical teachings, and discard irrational features. It would make sects preach and practice a high class religion or lose all those who are intelligent enough to know and religious enough to care. 

Once well under way, World Brotherhood should make rapid progress. Sectarianism is now so thoroughly honeycombed with skepticism that it would melt away rapidly in spite of frantic efforts of vested interests, if a world religion with prestige should ap- Millions of people now identified with various sects would welcome the opportunity to get their feet on something more inviting and substantial than the questionable sectarian block on which each has been standing or trying to stand.

Faith is as necessary in Brotherhood as in sectarianism, but it is a different kind of faith. Not faith that the Supernatural will intervene at the right moment to achieve what men have been able but have neglected to achieve. It is faith in the possibility of achieving brotherhood throughout the world. Faith that men as brothers, recognizing the Fatherhood of God, will devote themselves to mutual and cooperative building of a better way of life. 

With religious accumulations to date as a foundation, the new religion will be able to push forward with its own inventions and adjustments the more rapidly. It 126

should be the wisest of the religions, as well as the most effective, for it has the light shed during the religious history of the human race to guide it as it tries experimentally to build the best possible way of life.

A most promising feature is the fact that people throughout the world are much more alike than the sects to which they belong. Given half a chance by their various religions and religious systems, they would be even more alike than they are at present. 

Christianity, Buddhism, Islam, Confucianism, and other great sects originated as reforms. The founder of each stood on a higher ethical plane and was of broader vision than the sectarians around him. Not one of them believed that he had spoken the ultimate in religion. Each was a seeker after a better way of life than the world had known. Each expected his followers to seek a higher way-a better religionthan he had found.

Christians, Buddhists, Islamites, Confucianists, and members of other great sects ought, in the spirit of their founders, to be congenial to religious reform. 

Those who are true followers of the great founders will seek and find a religion better suited to the twentieth century than the founders of earlier centuries could create. They will build a much better religion than the sects which have permitted sectarianism to encrust and cramp the way of life of the founders. 

The better religion is World Brotherhood-the religious brotherhood of the twentieth century and the centuries which lie ahead. Each religiously mature person will accord Brotherhood first place. The hard, thick walls of sectarianism can be battered down.
