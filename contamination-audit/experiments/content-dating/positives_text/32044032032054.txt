USDA SUMMARY OF REGISTERED AGRICULTURAL PESTICIDE CHEMICAL USES 

(3rd Edition) This Summary has been compiled as a service to State and Federal regulatory officials having responsibility for the proper use of pesticides. Agricultural research agencies, agricultural advisors, food processors, and registrants may find it a useful reference. 

THIS SUMMARY IS NOT A LIST OF RECOMMENDATIONS, nor does it modify or supplant any recommendation made by this Department or any of the various States.

Primarily, this Summary is a compilation of pesticide uses which directly or indirectly involve raw agricultural commodities, that is, food or feed in its raw or natural state. It also includes other crops not used as food and sites which would be of interest to agriculturalists. The following is a brief description of the types of pesticide use patterns which are entered in the Summary and the use patterns which are excluded. 

I. Patterns of use which are entered in the Summary 1. 2. 

Treatments to crops which will be used as food or feed, or the produce thereof used for food or feed. 

Treatments to tobacco and other agricultural crops which are not used as food or feed. 

3. Treatments to crops which are grown only for seed to be used for planting. 

4. Seed treatments to seed used for agricultural purposes. 5.

Postharvest treatments to raw agricultural commodities in the field or in storage. 

6. Soil treatments to land which is or will be planted to crops. 

7. Treatments to animals which are used for the production of meat, milk, eggs, or other raw agricultural commodities. 

8. Treatments to agricultural premises that could result in the contamination of raw agricultural commodities. 9.

Treatments to aquatic sites including ponds, lakes, impoundments, ditches, and other water areas on agricultural lands. ii

The absence from the Summary of the use patterns which come under type II above in no way affects the acceptability of these uses for registration under the Act.

The pagination adopted for this edition is necessary to allow for expansion of the volume. A theoretical page number would be 

I-B-01-00.01. The first number (roman numeral) indicates the volume in which the page belongs. The letter indicates the alphabetical group in which the chemical has been placed. For instance, the page for bromacil would bear the letter "B". The first two digits indicate the page number assigned to the chemical within the alphabetical group. The two numbers after the decimal point indicate the page numbers within the chemical, while the middle two digits are available for the insertion of new chemicals added to the volume after publication. The actual page numbers are reduced for simplicity by eliminating the ciphers where possible. 

Thus typical examples of page numbers are I-B-1 or I-C-2.1. 

The charts show tolerances and exemptions from the requirement of a tolerance which have been established by the Food and Drug Administration, Department of Health, Education, and Welfare, under provisions of the Pesticide Chemicals Amendment to the Federal Food, Drug, and

Cosmetic Act. The charts also show those pesticides which have been formally classified "safe chemicals" under that statute, and the patterns of use considered to be "nonfood uses", thus not requiring the establishment of a tolerance. Nonfood uses are determined by the Pesticides Regulation Division, USDA, based upon a total consideration of the pattern of use, the nature of the chemical, and the extent to which the chemical is absorbed or translocated by the plant.

Nonfood uses include certain uses on crops grown for seed to be used for planting only, or certain treatments of seeds which are not intended to be used for food, feed, or oil purposes. Also, some uses for which it can be concluded that there is no reasonable expectation of any residue on the food may be considered as nonfood uses, and registered in the absence of a finite tolerance. iii

When two or more pesticidal chemicals are mixed, the limitations applicable to the ingredient having the most restricted registration should be followed.

As new chemicals and new patterns of use are registered by the Department, they will be added to present listings. 

A pattern of use now listed will be revised or deleted should new information support such action. 

If you use pesticides, apply them only when needed and handle them with care. Follow the directions and heed all precautions on the container label. If pesticides are handled or applied improperly, or if unused portions are disposed of improperly, they may be injurious to humans, domestic animals, desirable plants, honey bees and other pollinating insects, fish, and wildlife and may contaminate water supplies.

EXPLANATION OF CHARTS 

Charts are set up for individual pesticide chemicals, which are identified by the acceptable well-known common names as well as by the chemical designations. In many instances trade or proprietary names, and other common names not necessarily accepted by the Pesticides Regulation Division, may appear at the bottom of the page.

These are used for the purpose of cross-reference only for the convenience of the reader. They are not to be construed as sanctioned or endorsed in any manner by the Division or the U.S. 

Department of Agriculture.

EXPLANATION OF CHARTS (Con.) 

The tabulations on the charts are divided into four columns under the following headings: 

USE TOLERANCE DOSAGE 

In this column are listed the crops, animals, or sites of application to which the pesticide is applied. 11 In this column is shown the tolerance, the exemption from the requirement of a tolerance, or a declaration as a "safe chemical, if one has been established by the Food and Drug 

Administration. Patterns of use which cannot reasonably be expected to result in residues in or on food or feed may not require a tolerance and may be designated as nonfood uses. These are indicated by the letters "NF." 

The word "Extended" appears where action is being taken to aquire data to petition for a tolerance.

This column gives the maximum quantity of the actual chemical that may be applied per single application. It is shown in pounds per acre, percentage concentration, or other specified form.

LIMITATIONS In this column are listed specific limitations on the application of the pesticide to the given use, applicable under current registrations.

These are usually given in the number of days required between last application and harvest. 

Brief directions for application are also listed in some cases.

All acceptable uses (dosage rates and related limitations) cannot be shown in the charts. The single dosages and the specific limitations given represent the maximum for safe use. In interpreting the charts, any use involving the dosage given with a longer time limit, or a lower dosage with the same time limits, would be equally acceptable from the standpoint of residues. A label submitted in connection with registration which lists a higher dosage or more lenient limitations would require data to support such use before the product could be registered.
