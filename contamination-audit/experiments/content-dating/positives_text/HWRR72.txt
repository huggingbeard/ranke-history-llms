V. THE GREAT SEA MONSTER 

The account of Jonah and the whale has been made a subject of ridicule by many uninformed persons. They say that the throat of a whale is so small that it could not accomodate a man. Such a statement reveals an ignorance that is two-fold. 

In the first place there are many species of whales. 

In the next place the Bible nowhere asserts that it was a whale which swallowed Jonah.

In studying the Bible we should remember that we are reading a "translation". While it is quite reliable there are often shades of meaning conveyed by the word in the original, which are not carried over into the translation. An illustration of this is found in Matthew 12:40, "Jonah was three days and three nights in the whale's belly." The Greek word translated "whale" is "ketos," which means literally a sea monster. The Hebrew counterpart of that Greek word is "dag". The whale was called "tannin" in the Hebrew. Thus it will be seen that a proper translation of the original words eliminates at once one of the objections of the critics —yes, the most common objection.

2. Jonah's Experience Was Supernaturally Pos sible.

We are not among those who reject everything in the Bible which does not appear to be scientifically possible. WE BELIEVE THAT EVERY- 

THING IS SUPERNATURALLY POSSIBLE. THE GOD WHO CREATED THE UNIVERSE CAN CON- TROL THE UNIVERSE. THE GOD WHO MADE THE LAWS GOVERNING THE UNIVERSE CAN SET ASIDE THOSE LAWS OR OVERCOME THEM BY HIGHER LAWS. 

Were there no scientific evidence proving the possibility of a man being swallowed by a whale and surviving the experience, we would conclude either that science had not yet caught up with the (22)

Bible, or that God had performed a great miracle. 

We are by no means trying to eliminate the supernatural from this incident but we are impressed with the fact that God often works in the realm of the natural to accomplish his purpose. When all the facts are known the supposed "scientific impossibilities' vanish into thin air. 

God "prepared" or "appointed" this fish to swallow Jonah. Had God desired He could have fitted up a modern six-room apartment inside the whale and given Jonah every possible convenience. 

Nothing is impossible with God. We do not believe that God did this because Jonah said the sea weeds were wrapped about his head. Jonah survived his experience in the whale. It is said that gastric juices will not digest living tissue. If they would, one would digest his own stomach. This may explain why Jonah remained alive and was undigested.

No doubt his body bore marks of his experience which proved to the inhabitants of Nineveh that his testimony was genuine.

I. JONAH'S PREACHING 

"And the word of the Lord came unto Jonah the second time" (Jonah 3:1). In the Scriptures the realm of the flesh is always first and the realm of the Spirit second. It is first the natural, then the spiritual. Jonah's first response to God's call was in the realm of the flesh. "Jonah rose up to flee" (Jonah 1:3). The flesh is always running away from God. This has been true since the first act of disobedience by our first parents. The second time God calls it is the spiritual nature of 

Jonah which dominates his action.

There is another great truth which is marvelously evident. It was AFTER Jonah's resurrection experience that Jonah followed the Lord. The true believer has passed from death unto life and it is in this resurrection life that he goes forth to proclaim the message of God in the power of the Spirit. 

God said, "Go unto Nineveh .. and preach unto it THE PREACHING THAT I BID THEE" 

(Jonah 3:2). Surely there is food for thought in that statement: "The preaching that I bid thee.”

I wonder how many preachers are preaching what the Lord bids them? These are solemn words. God (27)

will honor only that preaching which is in accordance with His will. "So Jonah arose, and went unto Nineveh, according to the word of the Lord" (Jonah 3:3). How blessed are those words in comparison with the words of the first chapter, "Jonah rose up to flee"!)

Jonah's message was a message of judgment, "Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be thrown" (Jonah 3:4). God must always judge sin. 

2. NINEVEH'S REPENTANCE 

over- Jonah came from the jaws of death with a message from God. Nineveh realized that he was telling the truth. This was no ordinary messenger and no ordinary message. "The people of Nineveh believed God" (Jonah 3:5). This is the only reason 

Nineveh was spared. They proclaimed a fast. "From the greatest of them even to the least of them," believed. The revival spread to the king's palace. The king himself laid aside his royal robe, donned a garment of sackcloth and sat in ashes. 

He issued a proclamation that everyone should 

"CRY MIGHTILY UNTO GOD" and "TURN EVERYONE FROM HIS EVIL WAY" (Jonah 3:8). 

Here is a great lesson for our rulers today. If they would issue such a proclamation no doubt many people would respond to its message and cry mightily unto God. (28)

3. NINEVEH'S PRESERVATION 

God spared Nineveh because "THE PEOPLE

OF NINEVEH BELIEVED GOD." Faith stays 

God's judgment. Unbelief brings judgment and condemnation. Nineveh turned to God and lived. 

This is also true today. "He that heareth my word, and BELIEVETH on him that sent me, HATH 

EVERLASTING LIFE, and SHALL NOT COME 

INTO CONDEMNATION; but is passed from death unto life" (John 5:24). This is God's plan of salvation.

Our Lord not only confirmed the truth of Jonah's experience in the whale, but also of Nineveh's great revival. "The men of Nineveh shall rise in judgment with this generation, and shall condemn it: because they repented at the preaching of Jonah; and, behold, a greater than Jonah is here" (Matt. 12:41).

CHAPTER IV. 

GOD'S GRACE AND JONAH'S DISGRACE 

One would think that after such a demonstration of the power of God as Jonah witnessed, he would be rejoicing. Yet this is not the case. The goodness of God is the cause of Jonah's complaint. 

1. GOD'S MATCHLESS GRACE 

Jonah here tells why he fled to Tarshish. He was afraid God would spare Nineveh. "For I knew that thou art a gracious God, and merciful, slow (29)

to anger, and of great kindness, and repentest thee of the evil" (Jonah 4:2). Nineveh was an enemy of Israel. Jonah was afraid that the preservation of Nineveh would mean the destruction of Israel. 

As we study the Bible we are impressed with the fact of God's mercy and loving kindness.

God's grace bridges the gap between His perfection and man's frailty. If man will only turn to God and believe he will become the object of 

God's mercy. God spared that great city. 

2. JONAH'S ANGER 

"But it displeased Jonah exceedingly, and he was very angry" (Jonah 4:1). Had the revival been in Israel it would have pleased Jonah mightily. Because it was a revival among another people Jonah was very much displeased. There are many Jonahs today. They have a tremendous zeal and passion for souls so long as it is in behalf of their own church or organization. But when a rival church has a great revival, when the crowds go there and that work prospers they act just as Jonah did when Nineveh repented. It is amazing what a beautiful prayer someone can make in behalf of souls coming to God in a revival that pertains to his own work. It is just as amazing to observe the displeasure of the same person when the 

Lord honors the preaching of another. Jonah prayed that God would permit him to die. The (30)

grace of God was so great that He did not even grant Jonah this request.

Jonah reminds us of the man who was going to hang himself but tied the knot so he could change his mind. Jonah prays to die, yet he “went out of the city" a safe distance so he would not be destroyed with the city should God's judgment strike. God showed His grace by preparing a gourd to shelter Jonah from the heat of the sun. God next prepared a worm to destroy the gourd. Then 

He prepared "a vehement east wind" and the gourd withered away. God was teaching Jonah a lesson. Jonah had pity upon the gourd but had no pity upon Nineveh. "And should I not spare Nineveh, that great city, wherein are more than sixscore thousand persons that cannot discern bebetween their right hand and their left hand; and also much cattle?" (Jonah 4:11).

Lost sinner, the Book of Jonah teaches us that by faith in the Lord we may escape God's condemnation His grace is sufficient to reach the vilest sinner. Flee to Him for refuge today. 

Christian, remember that only in the pathway of obedience is there joy and blessing and fruitfulness in the Lord's service. May we walk close to 

Him day by day.
