INTRODUCTION SINCE 1900 THERE HAS BEEN AN ABSOLUTE AND RELATIVE INCREASE IN THE NUMBER OF PENNSYLVANIANS LIVING 65 YEARS OR MORE. THERE HAS BEEN A 6.9% INCREASE IN THE POPULATION OF THE STATE DURING THE PAST 10 YEARS BUT THAT WITH THIS INCREASE THERE HAS BEEN A SHIFTING OF POPULATION FROM RURAL AND DEPRESSED AREAS TO URBAN AREAS. OF THE 67 COUNTIES IN THE STATE, 26 SHOW A LOSS IN POPULATION RANGING FROM 0.6% TO 15.9%. OF THE COUNTIES WHICH INCREASED IN POPULATION, 20 INCREASED FROM 1.2% TO 10%; 16 FROM 10% TO 20% AND ONE, BUCKS COUNTY, BECAUSE OF ITS EXPANDING INDUSTRIAL DEVELOPMENT, IN- 

CREASED 112.

THE FIVE COUNTIES SHOWING THE GREATEST INCREASE ARE ALL IN THE SOUTHEASTERN PART OF THE STATE. 

THE NUMBER OF PENNSYLVANIANS AGED OVER 65 HAVE INCREASED FROM A RATE OF 8.4 IN 1950 TO 9.3% IN 1957, BASED ON BUREAU OF CENSUS ESTIMATES. THE 1960 ESTIMATE FOR THE UNITED STATES AS A WHOLE INDICATES THAT THE RATE FOR 

THOSE 65 AND OVER is 8.8%.

IT IS RECOGNIZED THAT PERSONS WITH SPECIFIC CHRONIC DISEASE OFTEN REGARD THEMSELVES AS BEING IN GOOD HEALTH BECAUSE THEY CAN ENTLY AND SATISFYINGLY ENGAGE IN THEIR USUAL DAILY LIVING ACTIVITIES.

THERE IS DEFINITE NEED FOR THE DEVELOPMENT OF LOCAL RESPONSIBILITY FOR THE OLDER AGE GROUP. SUCH ACTION SHOULD NOT REQUIRE THE ESTABLISHMENT OF ANY ADDI TIONAL AGENCIES BUT CAN BE EFFECTED BY COORDINATION OF EFFORTS OF EXISTING AGENCIES.
