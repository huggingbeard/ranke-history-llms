INTRODUCTION Article I, Section 9 of the Constitution of Ohio provides: 

"All persons shall be bailable by sufficient sureties except for capital offenses where the proof is evident or the presumption great. Excessive bail shall not be required, nor excessive fines imposed. 

From time to time it has been advisable to make a report on the system used by the City of Cleveland in accepting bail bonds for the release of prisoners, in order to check the administrative efficiency and to discover any existing abuses present in the carrying out of the multitude of details involved. 

From the acceptance of the bond to its discharge, or in case of forfeiture, to its being certified to the office of the Chief Justice of Municipal Court, and to the collection of judgment by the Law Department, careful records must be kept. These records deal with who is surety on the bond, the equity in real property pledged as security, the handling of cash bonds, the discharge of bonds on the defendant's avpearance in court (and refund of such cash bond payments), bond forfeitures, notifying surety of 20-day period, transmitting the forfeited bond for collection, recording of the transmitted forfeited bond, filing suit for collection, and obtaining judgment. 

The system is further complicated and made vulnerable by demands for favoritism, protests as to the amount of bond set, complaints from those who desire to write bonds professionally without conforming to professional bond-writing requirements, pressure applied on the Law Department to hold off filing suit and unmerited requests for mitigation of judgments rendered.

A REPORT ON BAIL BONDS IN THE CITY OF CLEVELAND 

When a person is arrested and put in jail, especially for the commission of a lesser offense (a misdemeanor), the first question entering his mind is how to get out. 

In practically every case, the accused is allowed the facility of a telephone call to a relative, friend or lawyer to arrange for his release. 

This necessitates securing one of four types of bonds. 

If the accused is confined in a precinct police station, the person contacted must appear in the Clerk of Court's office in Central Police Station where bond is arranged. When one of the four types of bail bonds has been arranged for the release of the prisoner, the friend, relative or attorney must then deliver the bond to the precinct station to secure the prisoner's release, (The Clerk's office has no facilities to bring an ordinary prisoner to Central Station to complete the bond transaction, so the bond must be delivered to the precinct station.)

Of course arrangements for a prisoner's release can be more quickly and easily completed where the prisoner is confined in Central Police Station. 

In the case of an out-of-town driver, arrested for a traffic violation, and it is shown that he knows no one in Cleveland to intercede for him to secure his release on bond, the Clerk will make arrangements for him to post a cash bail bond so long as he has the cash on his person. Many of these bonds are forfeited where it is not practical nor convenient for the accused to return to Cleveland to stand trial.

Of course, if the prisoner does not have available sufficient funds to put up the bond, he is confined in jail over night and stands trial the next day. - 1 -

Types of Bail Bonds 

There are four types of bail bonds accepted by the Clerk of Municipal Court which, under certain conditions, secure the release of a prisoner until his hearing in Municipal Court. 

Personal Bond 

A personal bond is one in which the accused, having a legal residence in Cuyahoga County, signs as his own surety to secure his release from jail until he stands trial. Some friend or relative must first intercede on behalf of the accused before he is given an opportunity to sign the bond. 

These bonds are issued only on minor violations of ordinances, such as simple intoxication and traffic violations. If there is property damage or personal injury as a result of the accused's intoxication or traffic violation, no personal bond is permitted.

About 90% of the personal bonds written are by those accused of simple intoxication. These are generally pedestrians so intoxicated as to be traffic hazards or drunks found sleeping in doorways. Property Bonds 

These bonds are often written by a relative or a friend of the prisoner, pledging real property in Cuyahoga County. If, however, a relative or friend is not available, the services of a professional bondsmen may be obtained.

Some quasi-professional bondsmen operate with this type of bond in pledging their property for a prisoner's release, usually with a commission of 5% of the bond's face value, with a minimum cost of $15 per bond. This practice is discouraged by the Clerk's office and when they determine that one person in a short period of time has written a number of bonds, the Clerk's office terminates his activities in this respect, and demands he qualify as a professional bondsman.

The property pledged must be located in Cuyahoga County with an equity value double the amount of the bond to be issued. On bonds up to $500 where property is pledged, the bondsman signs an affidavit certifying that the equity in the property is adequate, as well as showing his receipt for taxes paid, which states the assessed valuation on the auditor's books. In property pledged for bonds over $500, the Clerk now demands a recent Abstract of Title to determine that the equity in the property is adequate. 

Upon accepting a property bond, the Clerk certifies a copy to the County Recorder, who places a lien on the property pledged. When the bond is discharged, the Recorder is notified and the lien on the property is cancelled. 

If the bond is forfeited, the lien remains on the property at an interest rate of 6% per year. - 5 -

Cash Bonds 

Cash bonds are usually for $25 or $50, for violations of a lesser nature, ordinarily given by traffic or intoxication offenders, who, in many instances reside outside Cuyahoga County. The cash is handed over to the Clerk at the time the bond is issued and he keeps it in a special bank account. 

Before the present Clerk of Court took office in January 1948, the money pledged on these cash bonds was kept in the Clerk's office, in a safe. 

Often there were thousands of dollars kept in the safe, which was rarely locked and inadequately protected. To correct this old-fashioned method of doing business, the cash pledged is now placed in a separate checking account. 

When the accused appears for trial, the bond is discharged and the money is refunded to the bondsman. If the bond is forfeited, after a judgment is taken by the city on the bond, the money is sent to the Civil Branch of the Clerk's office, for payment of costs and distribution to the Law Department of the City of Cleveland where it is placed in the city's general operating fund.

Professional Bondsman 

Professional bondsmen write bonds for a fee. To do so, they must register with the Clerk of Court by furnishing a recent Abstract of Title proving a minimum equity in real property of $25,000 before they can write bonds pledging that property as security.

Forfeiture of Bonds 

When the defendant on bond fails to appear at the time of his hearing, Ohio General Code 13435-18 makes it compulsory that the Court declare the bond forfeited and issues a capias for the arrest of the defendant. The Clerk then serves on the surety a notice of the failure of the defendant to appear and that the surety has 20 days in which to produce the body of the defendant. 

Should the surety produce the defendant within the 20 days, the order of the forfeiture is revoked, the defendant stands trial and the full amount of the bond is discharged. 

In the event the defendant does not appear after the 20-day waiting period, the Clerk of Municipal Court sends a notice of the bond forfeiture to the Chief Justice of the Municipal Court. As a practical matter the Clerk waits for several days to transmit a group of bond forfeitures to the Chief Justice rather than sending one or more every day. The Chief Justice then makes an entry for his records of receipt of the bond and forwards it to the city Law Department for collection.

Other Cuyahoga County Municipalities Collection Methods 

Court clerks of other municipalities in Cuyahoga County usually collect a forfeited bond (practically always cash) by setting up a forfeited bond account, and turning the money over to the municipality once each month.

City of Cleveland Law Department 

It is one of the functions of the Law Department of the City of Cleveland, upon receipt of a forfeited bond from the Chief Justice of the Municipal Court, to go through the formality of filing a form petition and securing an automatic judgment on the bond.

In many instances in the period under study--August 1946 through February 1949--forfeited bonds gathered dust in the Law Department until someone there found time to prepare petitions to secure judgment so that the bonds might be collected.

Surety Bonds 

Of the two surety bonds forfeited to the City of Cleveland during this period, one was sent to the Law Departrent May 3, 1947 and suit was brought and judgment of $100 and costs rendered. A motion for mitigation was filed, and on April 8, 1948 this judgment was reduced to $1.00 and costs. 

The other was sent to the Law Department February 23, 1948, and suit was brought and judgment of $200 and costs granted June 11, 1948. This judgment was paid in full the next day.

As can be seen in the case of the first bond, nearly three months elapsed before the city brought suit. In the second instance, over 3-1/2 months elapsed before suit was brought. 

Although a surety company is considered "as good as gold" in paying judgment, there appears to be little reason for the delay, especially where the petition to be drawn is merely a form.
