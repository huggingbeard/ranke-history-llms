N 

"AND PEOPLE CALL ME 

THE PIED PIPER" ew Haven's in Connecticut 

By the river Quinnipiac;

A state we look upon with pride, And one that might all others guide: 

We boast it left unratified

The law, of which the lack Of it might breed respect for laws; 

But when fools legislate, applause 

Becomes demoniac. Gin!

II 

We make it now in baths wherein 

We dump in alcohol by gallons; 

No matter if porcelain or tin,

Or whose mucky paws or dirty talons 

Sat soaking in soap, or peeled-off skin; 

Mixing all up we laugh or grin,

All eagerness, till we can begin 

To start in and drink it, And joyously think it 

The Devil himself might call it a sin!

1 2 

III 

With Prohibition simply making

A sort of extra sin of drinking We've made a cult of cocktail-shaking, For man from sin is far from shrinking. 

The net result of education We have found, with much elation, Is that the rulers of the nation

With all their Leagues and solemn plots, Are mostly ignorant idiots.

Some fit their seats like bolts in sockets, Some fly up like fizzing rockets, All, no doubt, fill up their pockets; 

Making, with much elocution,

Amendments to the constitution. 

IV 

The Wickersham Committee 

Found Prohibition a failure; 

But dared not, more's the pity, Inform the legislature. 

They've got the documents, of course, Revealing failure to enforce, And lies from every tainted source,

From those whose jobs it is to see 

Crime flourishing, and he or she 

Can profit from it openly. 

Some men live to persecute:

The Inquisition's sad repute 

Might even hell itself pollute;

But since in this, our year of grace, Religion's cranks have little place 

And drivellers of a pater noster, This Prohibition seems to foster

Those spies and liars whose words transmute Life-lovers into sot and brute- 

The Inquisition's substitute. 

"Get rid of drink," so we were told, "A finer race we then can mold." 

Saloons, we all agreed, were worse 

Than God's edict and Adam's curse; 

For all our love of Bible-teaching, God cursing man seems idle preaching:

For man himself, since early breeching, Has cursed and sworn with yells and screeching: 

His brothers and himself impeaching;

At cursing even God out-reaching.

Thus no one needs to wonder much

At man forbidding such and such, That this and that he must not touch:

The God of all his universe 

First taught him how and what to curse.

VI 

The sponsers of the motion said, This drinks the Devil and all he bred;

Get rid of it and we shall see 

The closing up of every slum, The coming of millenium, A thing we crave for speedily. 

Each glass's rim that holds a drink 

Simply represents the brink

Of Hell, whose fashioning our Maker Takes the part of a master-baker. 

But not to sink to such a level 

He deputized an angel: Devil; 

And he it was invented drink, To make a crime-infested mob, And thus insure himself his job. 

This soul-snatcher, damned freebooter 

Is cute all right, but we'll be cuter,

Let him simmer, melancholic

On his ovens diabolic, All his planning we can spoil 

By making drink non-alcoholic; 

And he can rave and stew and boil

On the lack of sizzling sinners; 

Thus his machinations foil 

By drinking water with our dinners!

The law was passed with acclamation By half the self-deluded nation. 

VII 

Whereat, it seems, the Devil smiled; 

If he didn't, he could have done.

Of all the sin that ignorance piled Since earth began, there has been none 

So much together domiciled,

Or prisons filled with violaters, The while self-blinded legislators 

Of lawlessness are propagators.

For ere that law went in effect One seemed to know what to expect; 

With the noise of bottle-smashing, With the sound of mirrors crashing, Came the sight of pistols flashing;

Great men, small men, lean men, brawny men, Brown men, black men, grey men, tawny men, Men from Maine, Florida crackers,

In town and hamlet, city and village, Dealt in liquor, became hi-jackers, From dusk till dawn went out to pillage 

Anything bottled, none sought to choose 

Providing the stuff they got was booze,

Over the roads, the streets and highways, From Canada's border down the by-ways 

Came trucks of scotch and drivers frisky

With loaded guns, quite grim and ready, Protecting with their lives the whisky 

For which a nation madly thirsted,

Trusting that the boys were steady 

And federal agents quickly worsted. 

For who are these who would deny us? 

From our pleasures seek to pry us?

Hypocrites, the snivelling pious 

Of the tribe of Ananias! 

All their actions go to show it,

In their heart of hearts they know it. 

The joy of life's to them, denial;

All its pleasures but a trial; 

Psycopaths, who once were whipt 

When their elders found them playing,

In cupboards put when they were stript 

To end their fulsome, tiresome braying.

Their whining, pining is their dining, Love and luxury and wining, In putrid pruriency swining.

As elders, now, they'll do confining 

When they see us gaily chaffing, With our playmates loudly laughing, Or from a wine-gass gladly quaffing.

VIII 

You have heard the boasters telling

Of the federal agents shelling 

A Novia Scotian shipping master, And of the "I'm Alone" disaster.

For in this matter of wanton killing The government still is far ahead, For every agent that has bled The citizens count many dead, With all the jails and prisons filling.

IX In war one can excuse a spy, When caught he pays a price: to die. 

But what are we to say of fellows 

Who greet you pleasantly with "Hellos”, Joining in, with laughter hearty,

Some steadily maturing party, Shake all by the hands and meet you, Like an old friend come to greet you; 

Then slyly, with a knowing wink, Suggests we have a spot to drink, Not colored cider, nor red ink, Or anything approaching pink; 

But something where the ice can clink,

Himself produce the missing link 

To go with ginger ale and ice; 

You start to drink...and in a trice 

A whistle blows you're caught in vice: 

When next you meet him all unsought, He comes as witness, for the court!

Windward racing, or to leeward.

Up and down the country's seaboard,

Coastguards from their gun-boats' base 

Any ship at all can chase, Stop a captain, send a bullet 

Across his bows or down his gullet.

Find a fishing smack and shell it, If its cargo's rum-why tell it?

They take it back to shore and sell it. 

If lawlessness e'er wanted law 

Prohibition fits its paw.

XI 

"Now," say its sponsors, "it's all to the good!" 

What if we spill a little blood?

It costs no more than a civil war, And as for blood, what else is it for? 

The victory is ours, if we slave hard,

Even to filling every graveyard." 

XII 

So now we have the rattle of guns 

Coming from Chicago, New York and San Francisco, And sparing none, from farmers' sons

To social scions, heirs of wealth,

And the scourings of Europe. 

All out for looting by pistolling, shooting,

Rooting and tooting, the Government hooting, Gangsters all peltering, in armored cars sheltering, With bullets, and bodies in pools of blood weltering, In a reign of anarchy the country is sweltering. 

Fortunes are made by stealth. 

Gangsters have their Palm Beach homes,

Bathing tanks with marble domes, Debaucheries out-classing Rome's Thrill them and fill them with touches of power, Of American civilization the flower.

XIII 

Did anyone know, or anyone dream 

The price of the law was this bloody stream? 

Or anyone even stop to think, Or if they did, to curdle or shrink 

At the price that men might pay for a drink? 

What Piper piping merrily 

Blinded them so they could not see

Laws against liberty all will break, And wretched laws but wretches make?

Life means love, and following it, laughter,

Nor will our beliefs reverse the hereafter; 

Whatever it be, if heaven or hell, Or total extinction, or back to a cell,

Nor christian, nor pagan, nor infidel, Can ever know more, or the truth of it tell. 

But if we grant that no one saw The workings of the present law 

The future may some lessons draw.

Man may out-wit his natural taste, But speed in this means lesser haste. 

Ignorance was the piper when 

Prohibition sang to men.

When Knowledge pipes will those who hear 

Be also the men who live in fear

Of what Life is? No? Let us be 

Free from witches riding brooms

The Inquisition's galaxy 

Of spies and ghastly torture rooms. 

One lesson we've learnt in all our schools:

When Alcohol kills it kills but fools!

For only the fool by drink is sunk, And bigger the fool when he is drunk.

But nothing one says can give much joy 

To one whose sourness is his toy. 

He lives and moves in present fear

The Devil's at hand and will appear 

The moment he drinks a glass of beer.

The world's a place of tremors and shocks, At every sense of security mocks,

The place for such is a madhouse but 

There aren't enough for them all to be put. 

Too many are mad, as you and I know, Who listen when silly, fool pipers blow: 

Singing of safety by nullification 

Of anything savoring of exhileration; 

Trembling in fear and heart palpitation, Knowing their madness will let itself out

The minute they cannot know what they're about. 

XIV 

Alas, alas for New Haven,

Where drinking was never so much of a sin, Had no fear of beer, of whiskey or gin, Nor dreaded some madness or weakness within;

✈ 

And Connecticut never was craven,

Nor trembles about some dithering soul That is half afraid it is scarcely whole, However it be with Nevada, Nebraska, Maine or Florida and Tennessee, Kansas, Iowa and Mississippi, The two Dakotas and far Alaska.

The truth of it is: it's a lost endeavor, For while men live they will forever 

Drink and drunkenly get and never 

Will ever the law obey, No matter how they yap and bicker, Whether it make one glad or sick, or

Whether the government poison liquor, And maiden spinsters utter dismay 

At man's inordinate desire 

To set his veins and brain on fire..

There never seems to have been a time 

When Prohibition brought not crime, When, to forbid a natural pleasure, Carried as a war-time measure,

As man returned to peace again

He'd fight again for liberty, Regardless of the moral pain

It caused the ranks of prudery. 

We know what that committee found,

Though in their words we may be drowned; 

When a law is rotten each one breaks it, And if man wants a drink he takes it, If he can't buy one, then he makes it. 

In Pennsylvania there's a tribe 

That makes good beer; professors gibe

At those who claim there's harm in it, But who can teach those lacking wit? 

For blatant ignorance can't be cured;

It's patent, for we have endured, For longer than we care to say,

The puritan landed here to stay, Do witches ride his conscience? Yea!

XVI 

Oh, would that we could all be wipers 

Out of Prohibition's pipers! 

But pipe they as frantically as fanatics choose, They never can pipe us all free from booze.
