SIX MONTHS IN PERSPECTIVE – AN INTRODUCTIONS NEE 

Section 10(g) of the Military Selective Service Act (MSSA) requires that the Director of Selective Service report to the Congress twice a year on the activities of the System. Accordingly, I am pleased to submit this report on the Selective Service for the period of April 1, 1982 to September 30, 1982. The report includes our progress toward contributing to the national preparedness, a discussion of the modern Selective Service as we continue revitalization, and an update on our standby field system to include involvement in the New Federalism initiatives and steps to insure fairness in any future draft.

As we closed the previous reporting period, Selective Service was just beginning to issue statistics reflecting the success of President Reagan's grace period in encouraging the young men of America to add their names to the registrant file. As the weeks.c progressed and registrations continued to flow in, the totals reached 8 then 8.5 million, and, by the end of this reporting period, had passed 8.7 million. Registrations accounted for approximately 94% of the eligible men who should have registered since mid-1980. 

The Agency implemented an active compliance program aimed at identification and follow-up of potential non-registrants. In addition, through interaction with the 

Department of Justice, the first of the knowing and willful non-registrants were indicted. Of the eleven men indicted, two had come to trial, and both were convicted of failing to register.

Over the summer, more than 85% of our volunteer Local Board members (8,980 men and women) participated in two days of intensive training conducted at more than 350 locations nationwide. Also during this period we embarked on a program, supported by almost every State Secretary of State and many members of Congress, which will add a voter registration message to correspondence between Selective Service and the young men of America. These were but two examples of New Federalism initiatives developed by this agency.

These six months have been busy and challenging. Selective Service has returned from "deep standby" and stands ready to perform its mobilization mission. 2 

AIDING NATIONAL PREPAREDNESS - THE REGISTRATION PROGRAF INFORMING THE CITIZENRY – THE PUBLIC AWARENESS CAMPAIGNÉE 

Publicizing the registration program continues to be of paramount importance to Selective Service since we believe the majority of those who have not registered are simply unaware of the requirement. Those who oppose registration contend that the six percent who have not yet registered are active resisters; however, previous experience indicates otherwise. For example, at the height of the anti-war and anti-draft protests during the Vietnam era, most young men registered. The registration rate did not drop until the induction authority had expired, and many men apparently believed the registration requirement had also expired. An intensive publicity campaign brought the e registration rates back up. Increased public awareness is essential as the government's primary goal is registration, not prosecution. 1

RECOGNIZING THEIR RESPONSIBILITY – REGISTRATION Registration Update 

At the close of the last reporting period we were continuing to process the registrations received during the two month grace period granted by the President following his announcement in January that registration would continue. The overwhelming response during the grace period, during which we received 800,080 registrations, indicated that most young men recognized their responsibility and that most of those who had not registered were unaware of or misinformed about the registration requirement. Over 100,000 registrations from the 1960 through 1963 yearof-birth groups have continued to be received since the last report. The registration rate for the 1964 year group stands at 84.6%. This is much improved over the 77% rate achieved last year for the equivalent time period. The rate nearly equals the 88.0% experienced in 1973, and the 87.5% experienced in 1974, when the agency had a full field structure of Area Offices and State Headquarters. In both of those periods, a compliance rate of 90% was attained by February of the following year. Even though continuous registration is improved over last year and is comparable to historical experience, our goal is to register all eligible men. We must continue to strive to improve registration compliance through vigorous efforts to increase public awareness, and the implementation of an active enforcement program. 2

Program Support by U.S. Postal Service 

The favorable working association between the U.S. Postal Service and Selective 

Service which began with the reinstitution of registration in July 1980, continued during the period of this report.

Distribution of a revised Registration Form to the two Postal Service Supply Centers and all classified post offices was completed on April 5, 1982. Resupply is now accomplished within normal Postal Service operating supply procedures. 

With the cooperation of Postal Service Headquarters, two articles about the registration program have been published in the Postal Bulletin which goes to each Postal Service facility. One article dealt with the distribution and resupply of the revised 

Registration Form and all other Selective Service forms used by the Postal Service; the other was a complete restatement of procedures associated with the registration program. The impact of the latter article was favorable, and produced increased compliance with requirements associated with the Registration Report Program, particularly submission of negative reports.

The Registration Report Program is a control process whereby Selective Service can determine if a given Postal Service facility has forwarded Registration Forms on a specified date. It also permits Selective Service to verify the accuracy of the count of forms reported as sent. The Program provides a visible reminder to the Postal Service facility that completed registrations are to be forwarded to Selective Service. 

Overall, the registration program, as supported by the Postal Service, is working well, but occasional changes in postal personnel at the local level cause some inconvenience. However, when a problem is identified, the Postal Service management takes prompt corrective action.

REGISTRATION FOLLOW-UP - IDENTIFYING THE NON-REGISTRANT 

In January of this year, the President authorized a grace period to permit men to register late. The grace period added 800,080 registrants to the Selective Service master file. However, the Presidential order also informed the nation that Selective Service would initiate an aggressive compliance program to identify, and take legal action against those men who knowingly and willfully refused to register in accordance with the Military Selective Service Act. The aggressive program would supplement the passive program which is based primarily on actions related to self-identified nonregistrants and those identified by neighbors, parents, etc.

Federal Data Sources 

Analysis concluded that the best source of identifying information would be other federal government records, and that the files of the Social Security Administration and the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) would be most productive. Interagency agreements were arranged. The Social Security Account Number (SSAN) was to be the key to the processing. Use of the SSAN by Selective Service had previously been authorized by The Congress.

In June, the Social Security Administration provided a computer tape of men born from 1960 through 1963. After preliminary processing, a tape of men born in 1963 was matched with Selective Service data to delete all those who had registered. This tape was then matched with tapes provided by the Department of Defense and the U.S. Coast 

Guard to delete active duty service members.

From the remaining tape of potential nonregistrants, the first batch of 75,000 names was forwarded to the IRS to be matched against their file of taxpayers to obtain the most current address. The IRS successfully matched 33,000 names and, in August, sent each person a letter advising that they had been identified as possible nonregistrants and requesting that they register immediately by return mail. By the end of this reporting period, about 52% of those who received letters had responded to Selective Service. More than 61% had included a completed registration form. Selective Service is continuing to followup to determine if the remainder are required to register. Names of men ultimately identified as having knowingly and willfully refused to register will be referred to the Department of Justice.

A second batch of approximately 250,000 names will be forwarded to IRS in October for address matching. Persons identified via this process will subsequently receive letters from IRS.

Non-Federal Sources 

While federal data sources provide the best data for identifying potential nonregistrants, it was concluded that selected non-federal sources could also be used. 

The best source was found to be State driver's license records since they most often included men in the age groups obligated to register, and because most States maintain automated file systems somewhat compatible with Selective Service records.

In August, requests were made to each State Department of Motor Vehicles that lists of registration eligible men be made available to Selective Service in the form of automated data tapes which would be matched against the Selective Service registrant file. 

By the end of the period all but six States had agreed to provide the data, some gratis, some on a cost reimbursible basis. Data tapes from 20 States had been received. 

Those persons remaining on these lists after preliminary processing and matching against Selective Service files will receive a letter requesting that they register. Again, those who knowingly and willfully refuse to register will be referred to the Department of Justice.

The success of any agency is dependent on the quality of the people who comprise its workforce. The Selective Service System has always been fortunate in its ability to recruit and retain personnel of the highest caliber. ENHANCING THE PROCESS - REFINING THE REGISTRANT PROCESSING SYSTEM 

We continue to improve our procedures for registrant processing. As reported previously, the Selective Service Regulations were published in final form in the Federal Register on February 1, 1982, and ecame effective upon publication (47 FR 4640). Since then, we have been updating procedures to reflect regulatory changes, and developing the Operations Manual to incorporate these changes. 

The previous report discussed at length the provisions of the regulations as they relate to the Selective Service mission to ensure that the fundamental concepts of uniformity, consistency, fairness and equity prevail. In addition, it identified the important distinctions between postponements, deferments and exemptions. We are currently engaged in transforming those regulatory provisions into simple, easily executed procedures in our Operations Manual. The necessary forms, instructional letters and management reports that match the new procedures are also being developed or revised. It is essential that the operational guidance contained in the manuals and other instructional material be easy to understand, since the persons who will use this material during a mobilization will not have the benefit of extensive prior training. 

SELECTIVE SERVICE IN THE COURTS – CIVIL LITIGATION AND COMPLIANCE 

Litigation

On July 8, 1982, the decision of the United States District Court in the District of Columbia in Wolman v. United States, 542 F. Supp. 84 (1982) terminated litigation which had been pending over two years relative to the authority of the Selective Service System to require registrants to furnish their Social Security Account Number. The 

Court noted that an amendment to the Military Selective Service Act by Public Law 9786, approved December 1, 1981, authorized Selective Service by regulation to require registrants to furnish their Social Security Account Numbers. The Court also held that the regulations pertaining to registration which became effective July 18, 1980, met the statutory requirements.

Compliance Since the last report to the Congress, the Department of Justice has begun the prosecution of those men who willfully refuse to register pursuant to the Military 

Selective Service Act. 

As of September 30, 1982, the Selective Service System has referred to the Department of Justice for investigation and possible prosecution the names of 341 persons who have been identified and alleged to be willful non-registrants. The names were usually obtained from anonymous informants and, at times, voluntarily, from alleged non-registrants themselves.

The Department of Justice has assigned these cases to respective U.S. Attorneys throughout the United States. There have been eleven indictments. Of these, two have been tried and both have resulted in convictions. The first man convicted was Enten 

Eller of Bridgewater, Virginia. No significant legal defense was offered in that case. 

Mr. Eller's defense was based entirely upon conscientious objection. The second man convicted was Benjamin H. Sasway of Vista, California. A number of legal defenses were offered by Mr. Sasway. Among them, selective prosecution and invalidity of the 

Selective Service regulations concerning registration. The defenses were rejected by the 10

AUTOMATION – THE COMPUTER NETWORK 

In January 1980, when Selective Service revitalization began, the agency owned two small computers which were used to process accounting and payroll but could not be expanded to support mass registration or to maintain registrant files on as many as 16 million men. Further, both the computer hardware and applications software were incapable of supplying the data processing capacity needed to implement an emergency classification, induction, or area office support program as required to meet DoD mobilization requirements. Pre-revitalization estimates that it would take the agency 

110 days to deliver the first inductee in a post-mobilization registration (80 days longer than the 30-day DoD limit) resulted chiefly from the lack of automatic data processing (ADP) capacity.

During 1980, we began to build our own in-house capability to support the registrant processing and administrative needs of sss. First, we constructed a permanent Joint Computer Center (JCC) in Great Lakes, Illinois. This facility, which became operational in September 1981, is owned and operated jointly by SSS and the 

Military Enlistment Processing Command (MEPCOM). The JCC is equipped with an 

IBM 370-165-II computer and will handle continuous registration as well as our application development requirements for the Registration Information Management System. Second, we built the SSS owned and operated Data Management Center (DMC). 

The DMC is located in the same building as the JCC and provides the operations support personnel required to process continuous registration. This support includes data entry, error correction, mail and logistics and computer operations. The DMC became operational in September 1981. Third, the Agency has acquired a Prime 550-II minicomputer which will provide support for all administrative applications in the future. Fourth, we have installed NBI word processing systems in all secretarial locations within the Agency. In addition to word processing, these systems have communications capability and will serve as the primary terminal for the administrative applications run on the Prime 550-. Fifth, we have an agreement with DoD that upon mobilization, pre-designated Armed Forces Recruiting Offices will become SSS Area Offices. These Offices will possess a United States Army Recruiting Command (USAREC) JOIN terminal system. This terminal will provide the main data entry source for claims and appeal data processed by the Area Offices. The generic configuration for this terminal is a small, microprocessor based business system capable of data entry, local editing, local off-line storage, word processing, low-speed printing and low-speed dial-up communications with the JCC. 11

MOBILIZATION CONCEPT OF OPERATION 

Using the Registrant Information Management System (RIMS) and the RIMS data base, SSS is prepared to issue induction orders on M+3. The RIMS data base will be developed and maintained by Selective Service at the JCC and will contain all active registrants.

RIMS will select registrants for induction, produce Mailgrams to notify the registrants and schedule the registrants for processing at the Military Entrance Processing Stations (MEPS). Initially, ordered registrants will be directed to report for a physical examination at the MEPS 10 days after the order is issued. 

As ordered registrants appear at the MEPS, the results of their processing will be consolidated and the results of all MEPS processing will be provided to the Selective 

Service RIMS system. 

As ordered registrants file claims at the Selective Service Area Offices, the Area Office personnel will enter the claim/appeal information into the Area Office terminal system. The data will be locally edited and stored for later transmission to the JCC. If required, a letter will also be produced by the terminal system and sent to the registrant. At the end of the day, the Area Office will dial up the JCC and transmit the accumulated transactions to RIMS. The Area Office Terminals will be installed early next year. If the system is activated before then the claims appeal information will be mailed to the DMC for processing.

After the MEPS processing transactions and all the Area Office transactions are received, RIMS will perform a daily update of the registrant data base. Following the data base daily update, the new daily quota will be established and new induction orders will be produced.

Alternative Service Program 

This subsystem provides for the placement of conscientious objectors in appropriate civilian work in lieu of induction. This will be accomplished by maintaining a bank of job openings which contribute to the national health, safety or interest and matching these openings with participating conscientious objectors by considering each individual's interests, aptitudes and abilities. Once a conscientious objector is placed in a job opening, RIMS will continue to monitor changes in his status to insure that he is satisfactorily meeting the objectives of the Alternative Service Program. The Program is currently in the requirements analysis stage. 

Violator Identification

RIMS will be used in conjunction with other data bases to identify men who have allegedly violated the Military Selective Service Act.

THE STANDBY SYSTEM – OUR FIELD ORGANIZATION NEW FEDERALISM - INVOLVING THE STATES 

This past January, President Reagan announced his New Federalism concept designed to return control of various programs to the States. The Selective Service 

System is not involved in grant-in-aid and other dollar programs which fall under the aegis of the federalism initiative. Rather, our contribution to New Federalism reflects a return to involvement of the States and members of the general public in the processes for management and operation of Selective Service should the nation ever find it necessary to return to a draft. 

In the past year, the role of the State Directors of Selective Service, who are nominated by their Governors and appointed by the President to their part-time positions, has been significantly expanded. At time of mobilization, the State Director's will assume operational control of Selective Service activities within their States. They will be directly responsible for operation of Area Offices processing registrant claims for deferment, postponement, or exemption from service; will monitor operation of all Local 

Boards in their State; will conduct liaison and public affairs programs with the Governors and others, and will provide support for the Alternative Service process which will place conscientious objectors into public service jobs.

State Directors control the program to recruit, screen and interview applicants for about 10,600 volunteer Local Board member positions. Local Board members are recommended by the State Directors to their Governors, then nominated by the Governors for appointment by the Director of Selective Service in the name of the President. State Directors have a continuing responsibility to insure recruitment of replacement for positions which become vacant. The State Director must endeavor to insure that Local Board membership reflects, to the maximum extent practicable, the ethnic and racial composition of the registrants in the area served by the Board. 14

As part of our effort to insure compliance with the registration requirement, we have recently sought cooperation of the States in making available their automated driver's license files for computerized matching with the Selective Service registrant file. The object is to identify men who have not registered so that they may be encouraged to register. Almost every State has agreed to cooperate in this program. 

Due to conceptual similarities between registration with Selective Service and registering to vote, the upcoming elections prompted Secretaries of State in the various states to ask us to encourage men who register with us to also register to vote. In response, we will, as of October, include a voter registration reminder as part of the letter we forward to each man acknowledging his Selective Service registration. By way of reciprocation, one Secretary of State suggested that we might provide Selective Service registration information to be distributed by voter registration officials. Several 

States are now providing this assistance. 

Finally, our recently initiated Registration Improvement Program will shift a great Ideal of the responsibility for contacting the public and encouraging registration compliance from the National Headquarters to the Regions and the State Directors. In a sense, Selective Service will return to a "grass roots" program taking maximum advantage of our field network to inform the public of the registration requirement and encourage registrations. Our 10,565 Local Board members will have the opportunity to assist on a volunteer basis.

Our New Federalism efforts reflect the role and responsibility of the citizenry in national preparedness. We have received welcome support for these programs.

This program to train our citizen board members in the applicable laws, regulations and procedures began in April, 1982. The program was the first attempt ever undertaken by Selective Service to provide uniform training to board members on a nationwide basis.

FAIRNESS FOR ALL - ALTERNATIVE SERVICE 

Conscientious objectors who are exempted from military service must, in lieu of induction, fulfill their obligations under the Military Selective Service Act by performing "such civilian work contributing to the maintenance of the national health, safety or interest, as the Director may deem appropriate." The Director of Selective Service is specifically charged by the Congress, in the MSSA, with the responsibility "for the placement of such persons (conscientious objectors) in appropriate civilian work." 

In order to make contingency plans to meet this requirement in the event of a full scale mobilization, an Alternative Service Division was created in the National Headquarters.

During this report period, the contingency planning process advanced with a final decision being made on the selection of sites for the 48 Alternative Service Offices around the country that would administer Alternative Service. The proposed Alternative 

Service regulations were published for public comment in the Federal Register on June 7, 1982. Following analysis of all comments received, the proposed regulations were revised and published again for public comment on September 30, 1982. Several documents reached the advanced draft stage during the six month period including the procedures, the handbooks for employers and Alternative Service Workers, and the mobilization plan for Alternative Service Offices. 

With the regulations nearing final form, the various elements of the Alternative Service Program are being put into place. The principal tasks remaining are to prepare the system for computerization, to complete agreements with a number of prospective employers of Alternative Service Workers, and to prepare training programs for the persons who would staff the Alternative Service Program if the draft were reinstated. 16

HOW HAVE WE DONE? – SUMMARY AND THE FUTURE 

As Selective Service closes this reporting period, the agency continues to update and add to its registration data base, enhancing its ability to respond to Department of Defense mobilization manpower requirements. Our registrant data file, consisting of the records of more than 8.7 million young men for a compliance rate nearly of 94%, is a reflection of the work accomplished by Selective Service, and the success of the registration process. The public awareness program to improve knowledge of the registration requirement, and our aggressive compliance program implemented with the assistance of the Social Security Administration, the Internal Revenue Service, the 

Department of Justice, and almost every state government, will continue. The program is dedicated to insuring, in an emergency, that Selective Service will be prepared to meet its mission requirements with an equitable program which insures that the rights of every young man are upheld.

Looking to the future, Selective Service must continue its efforts to register all eligible young men. To assist in achieving this goal, a registration improvement task force has been assembled to examine alternate methods of increasing registration compliance. The task force will elicit input from our State and Region Directors, Reserve Force members, and the National Headquarters staff. Conclusions and recommendations will be presented at a conference to be held in October in Fort Worth, Texas.

Selective Service participation in exercise PROUD SABER 83/REX 82 BRAVO in the fall as part of the continuing revitalization program, will be used to identify areas of success or continuing problems in the agency's plans, policies, procedures and systems used to select, process, induct, and deliver manpower to the armed forces. 

Our program to select, nominate and appoint approximately 500 additional citizens and community leaders to staff the 95 District Appeal Boards will be completed by next Spring. When this has been accomplished, the claims structure will be essentially complete.

The Director of Selective Service must, within the requirements of the Military Selective Service Act, make the decision of "who serves when not all serve". The 

Selective Service System is prepared to perform this mission should the world situation require the nation to activate the induction process and mobilize its manpower. 17

CONCLUSION 

During the past year more than 2.4 million men have registered bringing the total of names on our registration data base to more than 8.7 million. The Agency has escalated its move from deep standby toward full mission capability.omg 

Selective Service, with a permanent staff of only 243 and a fiscal year 1982. budget of $19.7 million, has achieved a compliance rate of nearly 94%. The Agency is proud of this accomplishment, perhaps the most successful of all federal compliance programs However, we will continue our efforts to reach those young men who have not yet registered. In accepting the appointment as Director of Selective Service, I set the following goals: (1) To improve the service which the System renders to the Nation, (2) To strive for conspicuous efficiency and economy in the expenditure of public funds, (3) To increase publie confidence in the peacetime operations and wartime capabilities of the System, (4) To assure a completely fair system for furnishing manpower to the armed forces should Congress ever decide to institute an involuntary military training and/or service program, and (5) To respect the conscientious convictions and religious scruples of every citizen, acting always within the authority and limitations of such laws as the 

Congress shall enact. 

I wish to acknowledge with gratitude the assistance the Congress has provided to the Selective Service System in meeting these goals. With your continued support, I am confident that the Selective Service System will serve the nation well in the years ahead. 18
