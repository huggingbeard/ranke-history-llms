I CRY, MORMON

CHAPTER I 

At morning, a boy knelt in the woods to pray: "Oh God, Omnipotent, I seek Thy shrine. 

Who, in this darkness, claiming Thy light, walketh 

With Thee?" 

Strange torments, of heaven or of hell, Seized Joseph, son of Joseph, and bound his tongue. 

Darkness poured over day: and Joseph cried 

To God his fear and begged deliverance.

Transplendent light blazed, above the brightness 

Even of the sun, falling as suns might fall,

Where Joseph knelt, and freed his tongue. 

And God? 

Did God without intent bind Joseph's tongue 

And throw a torch diffused to loose the bond? 

Light pierced light, blinding Joseph's eyes 

And entering his soul, where God put sight; 

And he beheld two beings, light within light. 

A voice spoke: "This is My Beloved Son, Hear Him." And Joseph cried: "Is there a church 

Of God, oh, Son of God, bright in brightness, Darkening the light about Thee?" Christ answered anguish : "Within their churches all men's lips are mine,

But not their hearts. They teach for doctrines, men's 

Commandments, having a form of Godliness 

But they deny the power thereof. Deny them!

Dwell with Me and I will know thy love." 

Lashed to the strengthless ground, Joseph watched 

The light, dwindling . . . a moon . . . a star... a darkness. 

Joseph turned to the world to give his joy,

To bring to God all men with one accord. 

He preached the God in him, shouting his joy! 

Pastor, parishioner, laughing, turned away.

APPEARANCE OF MORONI 

Three years men mocked. Joseph cried to God,

Lying wakeful on his bed in darkness: "Oh, Lord, none will believe that Thou, My God, Hast given me of thyself and of thy Son.

I stand abused, my father's dwelling grown 

Miserable from the richness of Thy gift." 

Silver as a winter moon, light flaked

The night within the room, grew and was God; 

Beside the bed of Joseph, a fair image, Of flesh yet not of flesh, of light yet not 

Of light, of lightning countenance, stood robed 

In white, white as sun's white heat. And spoke: "Joseph, son of Joseph, I am Moroni, Messenger of God. Rejoicing in thee, God, by thy hand brings forth from its long darkness,

A book transcribed on gold, wherein is written The history of a people. Listen well, Joseph: In Jerusalem, long ago,

Lived Lehi with his sons, Laman, Lemual, Sam, Nephi, and his wife Sariah. 

God Said: 'Blessed art thou, Lehi, because of the things 10

Which thou hast done; and because thou hast been faithful

And hast declared unto this people the things 

Which I commanded thee, behold, they seek 

To take away thy life. Wherefore I say,

Renounce the land of thine inheritance, And go into the wilderness. Take only 

Thy family and tents.' 

Three days Lehi,

The sons of Lehi, and his wife, Sariah, Journeyed in the wilderness. Laman 

And Lemual rebuked Lehi: ‘Oh, thou 

Visionary man! Who but a fool 

Denounces wealth to bite his bread with dust.'

But Lehi always turned slow eyes upon them, Seeing their folly without bitterness.

Lehi spoke, turning in joy to Nephi : "The Lord commanded me that thou and thy brothers

Shouldst go to Jerusalem, to the house of Laban, Who, of thy fathers and the Jews, hath records, Graven on plates of brass. Bring that brass hither!'

Before Jerusalem, under the walls, The four brothers counciled and cast lots. 

The lot fell upon Laman, and he went 

To Laban's house and asked him for the brass. 

Laban, anger widening his eyes, Bellowed "Thou art a robber and I will slay thee.' "This he shouted at me and I fled,'

Laman told his brothers at Jerusalem's walls, 'We are on a fool's errand.' Nephi said: 'Let us go down to the land of our father's inheritance,

To the gold in the coffers, the rings and the riches he left there, 11

Let us offer these to Laban, surely Laban 

Will give us then the plates of worthless brass.'

But when they came laden, hate shone in Laban's 

Small eyes: 'Go! Take thy gold!' 

But in the streets,

Knives and dark faces flashed the greed of Laban, And the four fled from the terror of their gold, To the bare shelter of the wilderness. 

Laman and Lemual, fearless in anger, cursed

Nephi and all his plans, spat and struck him. 

An angel stayed them with a lifted hand: 'Why do ye smite your brother with a rod? 

Know ye not that the Lord has chosen him 

To be a ruler over you, and this 

Because of your iniquities? Behold, Ye shall go up to Jerusalem again, And the Lord will deliver Laban into your hands.' 

The angel voice died like the wind's hummings;

The image faded. Laman dropped the rod, Sullen with fear: 'How is it possible 

That the Lord will deliver Laban into our hands?

He is a mighty man, and he can command 

Fifty, yea, even, he can slay fifty; 

They why not us?' But Nephi answered him: 'Let us be faithful in keeping God's commandments; 

For behold He is mightier than all the earth, Then why not mightier than Laban's fifty,

Yea, or even than his tens of thousands.' 

That night they hid themselves in the moon's darkness

Under the watched walls of Jerusalem. 

Nephi, creeping in the narrow shadows, 12

Deceived the city's gate and walked in the streets. 

Near the moonblacked door of Laban's house,

A man, felled as by wine, slept on the ground. 

The dark face and sneer were Laban's; his sword 

Gold hilted, steel bladed, cold in the moon,

Lay at his side. 'Slay him!' A spirit spoke. 

Nephi recoiled from the murder in his hand. 'Slay him! 

It is better that one man 

Perish than that a nation should dwindle and perish 

In unbelief.' Nephi took Laban's head

By the harsh hair and smote it off with the sword; 

Clothed in Laban's armor, Nephi hastened 

Into the treasury, placed the brass treasure

In the servant Zoram's arms and led the way. 

Nephi's brothers, seeing Laban, fled; 

He threw away his cloak, calling after them, And seized Zoram lest Jerusalem follow. 

Trembling in Nephi's grasp, Zoram heard God,

And went with him into the waste to Lehi. 

God said: 'It is not meet that thou, Lehi. 

Shouldst take thy family to the wilderness 

Alone; thy sons should take daughters to wife, That they may raise up seed unto the Lord 

In the land of promise.' Therefore the four brothers

Returned to Jerusalem 

To bring down Ishmael and his family 

To the wilderness; and Ishmael followed them. 

Eight years, God-led, they wandered, to a land 

Of fruit and honey, Bountiful; to a sea, Irriantum. 'Nephi,' God said, "Thou shalt construct

A ship, after the manner which I shall show thee, 13

That I may carry thy people across these waters.'

Nephi's brothers, seeing he built a ship, Laughed. 'Our brother is a fool for he thinketh 

That he can build a ship; yea, and he also

Thinketh that he can cross these great waters.' "Thou art like our father, led away

By the foolish imaginations of his heart. 

Years we have wandered the barrens, and our women Have toiled and have borne children and suffered all things 

Save it were death; years we have suffered the wilderness!'

Nephi spoke of Israel in bondage, And of Moses, whom God led from the Egyptians; 

Of manna sent from heaven, of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and the serpent's bite: 'Why is it ye can be so hard in your hearts? 

I fear lest ye shall be cast off Forever.' 

They plunged at Nephi. 'In the name of Almighty God!' 

He cried out, 'I command that ye touch me not,

For I am filled with the power of God, even Unto the consuming of my flesh; 

And who shall lay his hands upon me shall wither

Even as a dried reed; and he shall be 

As nought before the power of God, for God 

Shall smite him!' Their lifted anger fell; their strength

Sickened; they lived fearful of the Lord. 'Stretch forth thy hand again unto thy brethren, And they shall not wither before thee, but I

Will shock them, saith the Lord, and this will I do, That they may know that I am the Lord, their God.'

Nor did they wither, but the Lord shook them, As if with violence of fear and fever, 14

And they fell, worshipping Nephi. Nephi said: 'I am thy brother, yea, even thy younger 

Brother; wherefore, worship the Lord thy God, And honor thy father and thy mother, that thy days 

May be long upon the land, which the Lord thy God 

Giveth thee.' 

God blessed their promised land; 

There ox, ass, goat and wild goat roamed; there gold, Silver and iron abounded. God commanded: 'Nephi, thou shalt make plates of ore; engrave

Upon them records of thy people, all 

Thy journeyings and prophesies and those 

Of thy beloved father, that thy people

After thee may know these things, and I, The Lord possess them.' 

In his strengthless age,

Wasting toward death, Lehi said to his sons: 'Rebel no more against your brother Nephi, Whose views have been glorious. God is with him. 

Hearken to his voice and ye shall not perish.' 

But Laman and Lemual forgot his wisdom: 'Our younger brother thinks to rule over us;

And we have had much trial because of him; 

Wherefore, now let us slay him, that we may 

Be not afflicted more because of his words.'

Fleeing the murder of Laman and Lemual, Nephi and all who heard him, led by the Lord, Journeyed into the darkness and the north. 

And God cursed Lemual and Laman; turned

Dark the fairness of their skins; and all 

Who followed them were cursed; babes were born dark. 15

In the land "Nephi," Nephi wrote of war 

And prophesy; and after Nephi, Jacob, And after Jacob, Enos. A thousand years,

Kings and prophets wrote of the children of God 

Turning from righteousness: 'Woe unto him 

That shall deny commandments of the Lord.' 

Ammaron trembled for their wickedness

And wrote their wickedness and hid the plates Which had been handed down from generations. 

To the last prophet, the child Mormon, he said: 'When ye are twenty and four go to the land 

Antum, unto a hill called Shim. God's word,

There have I deposited to the Lord, A record of this people. Ye shall take 

The plates of Nephi and engrave upon them The desolation that ye shall observe.' 

Mormon wrote the voice of God: 'Vengeance 

Is mine, and I will repay; and because this people

Repented not after I had delivered them, Behold, they shall be cut off from the earth.' 

Years favored Nephite victories until 

Pride, greed, hate, confidence, launching their armies 

Wearied the mercy of God; the Nephite strength

Waned on the battlement. Their general, Mormon, the prophet, with the plates from Shim, Led the Nephites to the land of Cumorah, To pitch their tents around the hill Cumorah. 

Broad ax, bow, arrow, sword, the terror moved 

Upon them; then the soft earth sucked their blood.

Into the country south but twenty-four 

Hoped for their lives; hunted they fell. Only 16

Moroni, son of Mormon,-I—remained, Alone, last white man on the continent.

I lived to seal the record in Cumorah, 'Where none can have the power to bring it to light 

Save it be given him of God;-God's word- 

And blessed be he that can bring this thing to light;

It shall be brought out of darkness into light; 

And it shall be done by the power of God.'" Then Joseph 

Saw upon the brightness etched, Cumorah: 

Light on light. Flowers bloomed; grass slept upon it; "Here, Joseph, lie the plates of Mormon. Here, With them are two round stones in silver bows, Urim and Thummim, fastened to a breastplate. ... Behold the day cometh that shall burn as an oven, And all the proud, yea, all that do wickedly, Shall burn as stubble; for they that come shall burn them,

Saith the Lord of hosts, that it shall leave them Neither root nor branch." He quoted ancient 

Prophesies about to be fulfilled. 

And the light began to gather to his person; 

A bright conduit from the firmament, Embraced the glory where Moroni stood, And Joseph watched the feet that walked on air, Step by step, ascending, till they vanished. 

Infinity had touched and tortured Joseph. 

His was joy like sorrow, faith like doubt. 

His mind asked: "Was Moroni God's or sleep's?" 

The light, that light, a winter moonrise, grew 

In rich crecendi, thunderings of suns. "Joseph, son of Joseph, I am Moroni . . ."

He spoke the self-same wonders and forewarned 17

Of judgments; famine, sword, and pestilence. 

Glory effaced the glory where he stood. 

Again the light: moonlight, and sun on sun, And the voice speaking, and the light withdrawn.

FOURTH APPEARANCE OF MORONI 

Weary in the still fields the boy labored, Until fatigue like illness made him cry: "Father, Let me go home. I am tired, the world staggers, It turns and blurs." "My son, go home and rest." 

But in the second field, he fell, struck down 

By a strong voice: "Joseph, son of Joseph, I am Moroni . . . "The bright angel stood

Repeating all he had said; when he had told 

The last judgments of God Moroni said: "Joseph, tell thy father of these things." 

Joseph, in the strength of those words' life, Ran to his father, and his father knew 

By his own joy God's word and blessed his son. 

At high Cumorah Joseph dug God's treasure, "Oh God, my eyes see, my hands will touch

The gold plates! The breastplates! Two round stones 

In silver bows!" Eager, he reached. "The time 

Is not arrived." Moroni shone and spoke, "Return to thy father's house. After a year 

Come and I will meet thee. Come each year 

Until thy God devotes thee to His work."

JOSEPH'S TEMPTATION 

Joseph listened to the voice of Satan: "Thy father is poor, thy mother weeps. Perhaps 18

Thou couldst enrich thyself: a piece of gold, Or some small thing-" 

And Joseph climbed Cumorah: "Why do I tremble so. Yes! Yes! 

The stone! The box is here!" Under the lid 

His searching hand touched lightning! And again

Reaching he felt the violence of God; 

A third time Joseph reached and was struck numb. "Who tortures me? What power denies me?" "Look!" 

Moroni spoke, "The glory of God!" Then flashed 

A radiance, but on that dawn blackness 

Seeped. The prince of darkness and his followers 

Leaped yelping. Joseph cried: "Drive them away!" "Behold the glory of God, the curse of evil 

That thou mayst know hereafter Satan's power. 

What leads to good is God's; all else is his 

Who feeds destruction. Holiness is peace." 

Joseph cried: "Forgive me! Forgive me, Lord!" 19

CHAPTER II. 

"Professor Anton, I am Martin Harris." "Mr. Harris, what can I do for you?" "Your fine reputation brings me here, sir,

With a document, translation of a part 

Of old gold plates found in the hill Cumorah. 

Will you examine it?" "Yes, certainly." 

Lamplight flashed over the out-thrust paper. "Yes, this is accurate, more so than any 

I have seen translated from the Egyptian." "And these transcriptions are not yet translated." 

The intellectual hand stretched out: "Yes, yes, These characters . . . Assyrian . . . Arabic .. 

Egyptian... Chaldaic... interesting indeed." "Professor could you certify that these 

Are true characters?" "Certainly. If you wish

I will write out a statement to that effect . . . 

Now may I ask how you came to possess 

These documents?" "They are the property 

Of a Joseph Smith, who claims they are a record

Of people on this continent many hundreds 

Of years ago." "A record? Made by whom?" "By many kings and prophets. The last, Mormon,

Abridged some of the more ancient records 

And made as well accounts of his own time." "Under what circumstances were these plates found?" 20

"They were revealed to Smith by an angel of God." "Did I hear you say an angel, sir?" "Yes." "And who translates them?" "Joseph Smith." "Give me that certificate again." 

He crumpled it: "Angels are of sick minds, I will translate the plates if you will bring them." "The plates are sealed." "I cannot read a book 

That is sealed." "Professor..." "Good day, Mr. Harris."

MORONI CALLS FOR THE PLATES 

Ending his labor of the golden plates, Joseph watched the high moon stilled upon 

The summer sky. "Joseph, thou hast done well." 

Moroni stood, bright in the room's moon glow. 

He touched the plates, their gold caught golden fire. 

He lifted them and walking on flame, vanished.

So Nehi prophesied: "The book shall be 

Hid from the eyes of the world, that the eyes of none 

Behold it save it be that three to whom 

The book shall be delivered.'

THE THREE WITNESSES 

Joseph Smith 

And God's three witnesses, Oliver Cowdery, David Whitmer, and Martin Harris knelt. 

Brightness descended to the woods: an angel

Bearing the shining book, and all about him, Cast upon the air, the sacred things, The Breastplate, the miraculous directors, Urim and Thummim, and the sword of Laban. 

Slowly the God-hand turned the glowing pages. 21

A voice said, "God reveals!" 

God's brightness faded. "When ye have seen it, testify the truth, So saith the Lord." 

Keeping the commandment,

They preached of God: God's word: The book of Mormon. 

And humble folk cherished, and men of wealth; 

Against them flowed the hate of miscreance. 

MOB VIOLENCE IN HIRAM 

Emma Smith trundled a wailing child, Sick with child's sickness: A child not born

To her: a twin, of two she had found and loved, When she had mourned her own: their mother, dead 

From bearing them. Her humming muted voices 

Out in the chill March darkness, and footsteps

Quick over frost, till the door panels cracked 

And clattered down: the mob came roaring in. 

Joseph rose from where he slept. A fist 

Crashed on his mouth; his head fell on the arms 

Impelling him down stairs and into night. 

A vial of acid broke against his teeth,

Over his body, hot, thick, stinking tar 

Clotted; the smell of feathers sickened him: "God will avenge me!" The tar closed his mouth, Blows numbed him . . . footsteps faded. ... 

The frosty moon, Fingering the bits of whiteness, woke him there, And led him home. His wife wept, cried: "My dear, What have they done to you? What have they done 

To us?" She clutched the child, "His fever is high, 22

They took him from his bed." "They did that too?" "They have no mercy." 

And through the dawn and the day, And through three other dawns she watched the child 

Gasping the useless air until it died. 

And she wept for bitterness of death and said: "Now what more, Joseph? Is there peace in Zion? 

One child dies here. What use to spare the others? 

Our enemies will kill them and us all." "If God wills that we die, we die. I do 

Not fear our enemies, I fear our friends.

Apostacy, dissention, unrighteousness, These things are sadder far than martydom." 

FOUNDING Of Far West 

And the promised land was terror: mounted mobs, Big in the burning fields' shadow, harried, Till saints at whips and gun-mouths fled before them 

Into the prairies. There their wandering fires

Were home and school and church. They drifted West, Where none inhabited, founded Far West. "Build! Build! so saith the Lord, and consecrate 

A temple unto Me!"

BATTLE OF CROOKED RIVER 

"Come on, you, open up!" Sudden fists clubbed 

Nathan Pinkham's door. "Yes, gentlemen?" 

The crowded path shouted: a mob's yell, Bogart cried: "Look for guns!" "Captain Bogart, There are two more men in here." "Tie them all up!

The fewer god damned Mormons there are the better." 23

Pinkham and two Elders, hands rope-bitten 

At their backs, stumbled: shoved through the door. They were mounted,

Horses wound to the front and sprang into night. 

A guard, out of alarmed Far West, rode close. 

A scout reined up: "They have not crossed the ford, sir." "Then they are somewhere in this vicinity." "Dismount!" Thin shadows on a hill, they marched, Clear in dawn to the enemy below. 

A shot! O'Banian slumped. "Charge!" Horses, men, Swashed down the hill, crashing beneath; shot cracked; "God and Liberty!" Steel greyed the air. "They run!" Backing, turning, the mob tumbled 

Fell through the ford. "They run!" "They run!" Stray shot 

Sang. A lone man, couched behind a tree, Fired: Patten, captain of the guard, hunched forward.

THE HAWN'S MILL MASSACRE 

News cried in the streets! 

Hate bellowed: "Exterminate 

The Mormons!" Galloping, from the low horizon, Men, armed, horses, hundreds, advanced full speed.

The guard at Hawn's Mill looked doomward in the late sun. 

The ground leapt into dust! Death's horseman slid 

To a stopped turbulence! The guard cried: "Peace!" 

Sharp shot sputtered: behind him, the Mormon men 

Leapt to the answer, fired, while the crazed women 

Flung themselves at the crackling menace, shrieking: "Murder!" "Murder!" and panted to the woods. 24

In the bloody ruins of an old blacksmith shop, The last Mormon sank. The wounded lay with death. "Help us, O Lord!" Thomas McBride, bleeding, Dragged himself before the coming hate, But found no sanctuary. "Gimme yer gun, You dog!" Wounds that ached were kicked and spat on. "Take it." Smiling ironic gratitude, Surly Rogers stooped: "Good gun," he said, And fired; then with his corn knife's eager claw,

He hacked at the old man, face, hands, and limbs, Mingling his great guffaws with his companions', Revelling, murdering, stripping the dead. "What's this?" 

Reynolds dragged, from under the bellows, a boy, Sardius Smith, small in fright, and shot, Laughing: "Nits make lice. He'd have been a Mormon !" "Body of God! The wagons ain't been touched."

Turning from blood, they pounced on wagons and tents, Plundered the few houses: "There's nothing here!" "These damned Mormons must have hid their gold." "Let's go!" Hoofs scuffled, throbbed, and faded. 

Nothing human stirred except in pain.

In the veiled dusk before the dawn, women 

Began to tip-toe from the woods. Slowly

They piled their dead in an old discarded well. 

THE SIEGE OF FAR WEST 

Meanwhile the blooded mob drew near Far West. 

Two thousand there hallooed a fierce welcome. 

To the joined mobs' orders to surrender, Captain Morey cried: "But why? Who are you?" "We are sent by Governor Boggs, to exterminate 25

You 'Saints' and burn the place." 

Far West's protector, Hinkle, the traitor, rode

To confer with the enemy general, Samuel Lucas, And returned to Joseph Smith with messages: "General Lucas wishes an interview 

With you, sir, and if possible, Brothers Rigdon, Pratt, Wight, and Robinson." 

As they rode out,

Lucas and many riders closed them in. 

Colonel Hinkle, coming beside them said: "General, these are the prisoners I agreed 

To deliver up." 

Hinkle returned to march

The militia from Far West; the mob, let loose, Swarmed on the panicked Saints to flog and rape. 

Whipped bent backs writhed; women's sobs died with them 

In the streets; survivors straggled out. So fell 

Far West.

BETRAYAL OF THE PROPHET 

In Nauvoo: built in fear and faith 

In Illinois, fermented jealousy, Malevolence and resentment. Hearts were warped,

Even the strongest, perilously close 

To Joseph Smith. Of this the Prophet spoke: "I am exposed to far greater danger

From traitors than from enemies without; 

All enemies upon the earth may roar

But they accomplish nothing, unless some Who are among us in our councils wish 

By falsehood and deceit, to bring united 26

Vengeance upon our heads. Our Christ was kissed 

By one who was his own: 'Whomever I 

Shall kiss, he is the man.' Our Savior died. 

My friends, we have a Judas in our midst." "What does he mean?" "Surely none would betray 

Brother Joseph Smith!" But close looks questioned. 

And in one secret house, treachery murmured: "A fallen prophet!" "He must die!" "Brother, Will you take the oath?" A candidate stood forward. "Do you solemnly swear before your God and all

The holy angels and these brothers here, That you will give your life, your liberty,

Your influence, your all, for the destruction 

Of Joseph Smith, so help you God?" "I do." "Next!" Robert Scott replied: "I cannot take 

This oath. I am too young to understand." "Come, come, now, take the oath, you'll have no part 

In the affair." "I cannot." "No, nor I." 

Young Harris, son of Elmer Harris spoke: "He has done nothing to me." They tried to pass. "You'll take the oath or die!" Faces and knives 

Crowded them. "Speak: accept the oath!" "No! No!" 

Many merciless hands seized them. "One moment." 

Cowles said: "Their parents know their whereabouts. 

The boys must be released." "You are right! You, Scott 

And Harris, if you breathe a word of what 

You saw and heard, you will be killed. Now, go." 

Joseph Smith stood watching the moving water

Folding the sun in its slow ripples. Scott's brother 

Beside him, touched his arm: "They're here, at last." 27

"Well, Brother Scott, Brother Harris, what news?" "They plan your death." "Who leads them?" "Chauncey L. 

Higbee, William and Wilson Law, Joseph 

Jackson and Robert Foster, Sir." "I see.”

Lies, accusations against the innocent. 

Ripened Missouri's hate. The Nauvoo Expositor 

Procured by the conspirators, denounced 

The Mormons: thieves, villains, God's enemies. 

"REPEAL THE NAUVOO CHARTER!" blacked its pages. "Abate those lies!" The city council ordered:

Their deputies, the Marshall and his men, Pied type, burned papers, carried out the press. 

The traitors, violent from the Law's assault, In secret fired the building. "They burned the press 

And drove us from our houses!" "They seek our lives!" 

They whined in Carthage. They charged Joseph with riot! "Come to Nauvoo," Joseph begged the Governor : "See for yourself." From Carthage, Governor Ford 

Condemned him. Joseph must know Carthage law! 

Hopeless of justice, Joseph and Hyrum crossed 

The river to seek their safety in the west. 

Messages followed them, pleading: "Come back!" "Come back!" "Do not desert us!" "Return to fight the cause 

Of righteousness!" "What shall I do? What shall 

We do, Brother Hyrum?" cried Joseph, sadly. "Let us go back." "There we shall be butchered!" 28

"We must put our trust in God!" 

Before them lay 

Carthage, the desperate city. Joseph said: "I am going like a lamb to the slaughter, but I

Am calm as a summer's morning. I have a conscience 

Void of offense toward God and toward all men. 

If they take my life I shall die an innocent man, And my blood shall cry from the ground for vengeance; and it 

Shall be said of me, 'He was murdered in cold blood.'"

Sixty riders with their Captain, Dunn, Surrounded Joseph's men: "Deliver up 

The Nauvoo Legion's arms!" "We are unarmed; 

The Legion's arms are in Nauvoo." "Turn back."

They rode back to Nauvoo and left it late. 

Carthage Grays knotted the Carthage square.

Dunn's men with their two prisoners rode in. "Stand off you McDonough boys and let us shoot." "God damn you, Joe, we've got you now!" Blasphemy 

Jailed them. Blasphemy guarded two days, two nights.

THE MARTYRDOM 

"You, sir," Dan Jones, the Prophet's friend, questioned 

A Carthage Gray, "Why do you men shoot here 

So near the jail?" "Old Joe's in there ain't he?

We've had too much trouble to bring him here 

To let him get away alive. Unless 

You want to die with him, you'd better leave 

Before sundown." "Why before sundown?" "Well, Because I'm a prophet, see? I say, that neither 

He nor his brother nor anyone who remains 29

With them will see sunset today." 

Dan Jones 

Protested to the Governor : "Sir, your unruly 

Troops plan to kill Joseph and Hyrum Smith 

Before sundown today." "Calm yourself, sir, You are unnecessarily alarmed." "Indeed I am not, sir. On my way here, To tell you of a personal encounter, I heard an officer, speaking to the troops,

I quote his very words, 'Our troops will be 

Discharged this morning in obedience

To orders, and for a sham we will leave town; 

But when the Governor and the McDonough troops 

Have left this afternoon, we will return 

And kill these men, if we have to tear the jail down.' 

Surely, sir, you must see the need for action." "You are mistaken. The people are not cruel." "The Messrs. Smith are American citizens, And have surrendered to your Excellency, Upon your pledging your honor for their safety;

They are also Master Masons, and as such
