All of the things in this book are made of pasteboard boxes except those in the last chapter which are made mostly of wooden boxes. 

Begin collecting all sorts of little boxes right away. 

Get a large pasteboard box or carton, such as comes to the grocery store containing many packages or cans, and put all of the small boxes in the large box. By collecting all of the little boxes that are discarded about the house, you will have quite a collection in a surprisingly short time; but if this process seems slow, you can get an armful of small empty boxes at almost any store for the asking.

Collect the other things you will need in a mediumsized box. Most of them you can find about the house -bits of brightly colored paper and pasteboard, empty spools, lollypop sticks, skewers, pieces of wire, and little odds and ends of things that look as if they might be handy for making things. Of course, you will not need all of these things for making any one article, and often if you do not have the materials mentioned for making [1]

4. DECORATED BOXES 

You can turn the common ugly pasteboard boxes that once held groceries into beautiful boxes that may be put on the dresser, in the drawers, and on the shelves to keep things in.

Pick out strong boxes with lids that will come off. 

Spread an old newspaper over the table and get the scissors, paste, and glue. The paste is for paper and the glue for cloth. Get out any piece of wallpaper, cretonne, or crêpe paper with printed designs that you may happen to have. An old rag is always handy when you are gluing, for patting the paper or cloth in place and for wiping your fingers.

The oblong box at the top of the page has wall paper pasted all over it. Any scraps of wall paper or pieces from a sample book may be used. All four sides of the [16]

box are covered by a single piece that reaches across the bottom, turns up at each side, and is cut at the corners. The upper edges of the piece lap over the upper edge of the box and are pasted down inside. The lid is covered with a single piece of paper in the same way, with the edges turned inside and pasted. 

The tall round box once held rolled oats. It is covered with cloth instead of paper. The kind of cloth used was cretonne with a bright pattern in colors printed on it. 

Paste will not hold cloth very well; hence glue or rubber cement should be used. There is no hemming or sewing to be done; the glue will keep the edges of the cloth from raveling.

Cut a round piece of cloth to fit the top and another to fit the bottom of the box. You can stand the box on the cloth and mark around it to get the size right. Cut a straight piece to go around the sides of the box and a narrow strip for the edge of the lid. Lay a piece of the cloth face down on a piece of an old newspaper and spread glue along the edges of the cloth. Then lay the cloth on the box and pat it down with a cloth to make it stick. You must be careful to put only enough glue on the cloth to make it stick, for if you put too much glue on the cloth, it will run through and make the cloth look messy and soiled. If your glue happens to be "runny," put some of it in a shallow dish and let it partly dry till it becomes thick and very sticky. Rubber [ 17 ]

5. CHRISTMAS BOXES 

There are all sorts of ways of making common pasteboard boxes into jolly Christmas boxes. Red and green paper, either crêpe paper or smooth paper, is the most necessary material. There are many ways of using either.

To make a box like that shown at a, you will need smooth red and green paper. If the box has any advertising printed on it, cover it first with plain paper, white, tan or light gray. Cut out red birds from the red paper and paste one on each side of the box, turning two of them one way, two the other way, and placing them in different positions. Cut out a handful of green holly leaves and place them on the sides of the box, trying them first one way and then another. The stems may be cut from brown paper or they may be drawn with ink. The eyes are drawn and the bills are made by cutting bits of yellow paper and pasting them in place.

8. LANTERN SHADES 

From round boxes that held rolled oats or other groceries you can make lantern-shaped shades for hanging electric lights or for candles.

Figure 1 shows an especially pretty shade made from a rolled-oats box. To make it, discard the cover and turn the box upside down. Cut a round hole in the bottom that is now used for the top, making it just large enough to fit tightly over the end of an electric light socket that holds the bulb.

Now for the pattern of the design. Cut a piece of [ 28 ]

paper that will just fit around the sides of the box and draw on it the design shown in Figure 4. You will not find this design so hard to draw as you might think at first, for it will be pretty, even though you make many of the curves different. When you have it drawn, cut out all of the light parts, leaving only that which shows, black in the picture. Now lay this pattern around the rolled-oats box and paste the four corners a bit to hold it while you mark on the box around all of the cut-out parts of the pattern. Take the pattern off next, and cut out all of the light parts of the design from the box, cutting the birds out separately. A knife and sharppointed scissors will both help to trim out the design. 

When you have all of the edges trimmed neatly and the five birds cut out, paint the outside of the box and of the bird shapes with gold paint or with enamel. Then cut a piece of yellow or peach-colored tissue paper to fit the sides of the box, spread paste on the inside of the box, not missing any of the trimmed design, and stick the tissue paper to the inside of the box for the light to shine through. Paste the five birds in their places on the tissue paper. To put the shade over a drop light, screw the bulb out of the socket, put the end of the socket in the hole in the shade, and screw the bulb back in.

A TURNTABLE 

When the train comes to the end of the line, a turntable is needed to get the engine turned around and headed back the other way. The cars do not need to be turned around; they can face in one direction as well as another.

To make the turntable, you will need a wide round box such as the largest-size rolled-oats box or a larger box, a skewer, a short spool, two tacks, and an elastic band. One of those very short spools that buttonhole twist comes on will be the best kind. Cut the box down till it is quite shallow, just deep enough for the spool to turn in easily. Cut a piece of skewer not quite as long [84]

as the spool is high. Fasten the skewer to the exact center of the bottom of the box by driving a tack through the bottom of the box into it. Then put the spool on the skewer.

Make a small hole in one side of the box and put an elastic band around the spool, through the hole, and around the remainder of the skewer on the outside. If it is hard to do this, you can cut away a piece from the bottom of the box. The elastic band should fit quite tightly.

Now cut the oblong piece on the turntable that the engine stands on. It is the bottom of a box with narrow strips of two long sides left on it. Measure the width. of your engine and make it large enough for the engine to stand on. Cut a hole in the top of the round box not quite as large as the spool and drive two tiny tacks through the center of the oblong piece into the spool. 

Now, if the rubber is drawn tight, the oblong part will turn as you turn the skewer on the outside of the round box and will turn the engine in any position.

4. FURNITURE FOR THE CAMP OR CLUBROOM 

If you have any sort of old shack for a camp, or if there is a spot in the basement or the attic that you can use for a clubroom, you can make it both comfortable and cozy with furniture constructed from old boxes and barrels. You can even fit out an attic bedroom in this way. All of the furniture needed except a stove-even including comfortable beds-can be made of this material.

For a bedroom or a clubroom, cheap calico or percale will be needed for curtains around the boxes, but for a camp, no curtains are necessary. No one room will need all of the furniture described here, but you can choose the pieces most suited to your needs.

One of the simplest yet most useful kinds of furniture may be made by nailing cleats (strips of wood) to the two sides of the box on the inside and laying boards across the cleats for shelves. Such shelved boxes may serve as chairs, cupboards, bookcases, or dressers. The curtained seat by the one-legged table is made of two boxes nailed together. There is only one shelf in each box. Any sort of thing may be kept inside.

There may be a curtain tacked across the open side or the whole thing may be covered. To make it both comfortable and pretty, spread clean straw or several [178]

thicknesses of an old rug over the top and tack a piece of calico over them. Then gather a curtain to go around as many sides as are exposed, sew it to a double strip of cloth two inches wide and tack it around the top. 

If the seat is to be kept in the corner of the room, only two sides need to be curtained. 

The one-legged table is hinged to the wall and the leg is hinged to the table so that the table may be folded up against the wall and the leg folded against the underside of the table. A piece of old linoleum or two thicknesses of building paper tacked or glued to the top will give a smooth surface for writing. Shelves in the boxes will be handy for books and papers, if the table is to be used for writing or studying. If it is [182]

A barrel may be used to make a big armchair. Cut it one-third of the way through a little below the middle; then trim it in the shape shown. Fasten the hoops securely on the part that is to be used. Now make a seat that will rest on the front edge of the barrel. Use as many straight boards as necessary and fasten them together with cleats on the underside. Nail three little blocks to the back and sides of the barrel on the inside for the seat to rest on. The space under the seat may be used to store many things. If the barrel is made of hardwood, it will look well if smoothed and painted; but if it is made of very splintery wood, it will be more comfortable as well as better-looking if it is covered. Pieces of old rugs or canvas may be tacked over it, or it may be padded with a worn-out quilt and calico or cretonne tacked over it. Two cushions dropped in the chair will add greatly to its appearance and its comfort. 

A table may be made from a barrel by cutting off about one-third of the barrel from the top and putting a round or square table top on the barrel. The cleats that hold the boards of the top together should just fit inside the barrel so that the top will not wabble nor be pushed from side to side. A keg of the right size will make a good seat if a flat top is put on it. The covered seat by the dresser was made from a keg by making a [184]

round top somewhat larger than the top of the keg.
