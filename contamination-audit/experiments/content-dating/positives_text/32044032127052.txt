​OAHU PRISON INMATES A Sociological Analysis of 261 Admissions 

Criminals, felons, jailbirds, crooks, or to use the lingo of the prison, "cons" or paahaos or x-men: what odious associations such names evoke in the minds of most of us! For some strange reason we are prone to forget the humanness of men who have been so human as to err. The average citizen at first thought would deny that he knew any criminals. His conception of the man in prison (or even the man who is on parole) would be a man to be feared or hated, suspected or avoided. He would not think of him as a son or as a father, as a man with a home or a homeless youngster. He would assume that he was chronically in the toils of the law or at least an old acquaintance of the Juvenile Court or the Boys' Industrial School. He would probably think of him as protesting his innocence, though a ne'erdo-well and addicted to drink. He might associate criminal acts with clever, unprincipled, educated men, or with stupid fellows who knew no better. Would he realize that if he ran over and killed a child he might be one of these very "criminals”? Would he assume that most of those who become entangled with the law were aliens or would he single out one racial group and charge it with major responsibility? 

The fact is that few people, even those dealing most intimately with him, have a clear idea of the law breaker, in so far as his age, race, citizenship, family life and background, education, economic status, personal habits, club membership or criminal record, are concerned.

It was to clarify this picture that this study of the records of the men in Oahu Prison was made. 

All existing records of the 464 inmates of the Prison as of July 1, 1936 were carefully studied and all data dealing with 52 different types of information were tabulated. This information was then coded and transferred to a Hollerith punch card. Through the courtesy of the Honolulu Police Department the cards were then tabulated on its tabulating machine. 3

Further analysis revealed that the information available on inmates admitted prior to Jan. 1, 1935 was sufficiently incomplete to warrant discarding the earlier group entirely. Consequently the present study is confined to the 261 men admitted as Territorial prisoners between Jan. 1, 1935 and Sept. 1, 1936.

The information contained in the headings which in general cover the personal histories, habits, and backgrounds of the prisoners is based on the statements of the men themselves given in personal interviews to the prison social worker. Much of this information has never been verified nor would it be possible in most cases to verify it. Exceptions to this are the paragraphs covering relief status and military service as verification of the information contained in these paragraphs as well as all that contained under the heading criminal record had been obtained from the proper authorities. In every case the status of the prisoner was considered as of the time of the crime for which he was incarcerated between the above mentioned dates, rather than his status at the time of any previous crime.

Nationality was determined by the usual census procedure, in general, those of non-mixed parentage being given the nationality of their parents, those who were part-Hawaiian being classed as Caucasian-Hawaiian, or Asiatic-Hawaiian according to the nationality of the non-Hawaiian parent, and those who were of colored mixed parentage being given the nationality of the father. The figures on nationality revealed that 28.8 percent of the men were Filipinos, and 27.9 percent were Hawaiians or part-Hawaiians. As each of those two nationalities constituted such a large percentage of the whole group it was thought desirable to analyze each of these subgroups separately so that they could be compared with each other or with the group as a whole. Henceforth when the word Hawaiian is used it includes all reported part-Hawaiians as well. 

SECTION I 

Section I deals with the entire group of 261 prisoners, Section II with the Filipino and Hawaiian groups, numbering 

75 and 73, respectively.

Group as a Whole. 

Age, Race, Citizenship 

Analysis showed that 50.0 percent of the men were under 

25 years of age at the time the crime was committed, and that only 9.6 percent were over 42. The ages reported most frequently were 18 and 19. 

57.8 percent were born in the Hawaiian Islands and 26.4 percent in the Philippines.

The Filipinos constituted the largest racial group with 28.8 percent although according to the Board of Health estimates they constitute only 13.6 percent of the general population in 

1936. The next largest group was the Hawaiian with 27.9 percent as compared to an estimated 15.0 percent of the population. Third in percentage were the Japanese with 10.7 percent as compared however to 38.2 percent in the general population. 

The Puerto Ricans claimed the distinction of having the highest percentage in the prison population, 8.4 percent, as compared to their percentage in the general population, 1.9 percent. To summarize briefly the Filipinos, Hawaiians, Portuguese, Puerto Ricans and Koreans had a higher percentage in the prison population than they are estimated to have in the population as a whole whereas in the case of the Japanese, other Caucasians, and the Chinese the opposite was true. 

56.7 percent had lived in the Territory all their lives. Of the others, those who had been in the Territory 10-14 years showed the next highest percent of commitments with 11.9 percent.

Those who had been here 5-9 years followed with 10.7 percent. 

67.8 percent of the prison group were citizens, 26.4 percent were Filipino nationals, and 5.0 percent were aliens, as against 77.4 percent, 10.0 percent and 12.6 percent respectively in the general population. 

Family Life and Background 

63.6 percent of the men were single, 23.4 percent were members of broken families as they had been divorced, deserted, separated, widowed or had common law wives. 13.0 percent were married and living with their wives. 

50.3 percent were living with either their parents, parent,

foster parents, wife, or near relative. 25.7 percent were living with other men either in rooming houses or labor camps, and 

20.3 percent were living alone. 

75.8 percent had no children and 70.2 percent had no dependants. 18.0 percent had 1 to 3 children, 6.2 percent had 4 to 9 children. 

71.6 percent came from families broken either by death, separation, or re-marriage of parents. In 25.7 percent of the cases both parents were dead, in 34.5 percent one parent was dead. Thus 60.2 percent had lost one parent through death. In only 28.4 percent of the cases were both parents alive and still married at the time of the crime. 

Only 6.9 percent were without at least one living brother or sister. Three was the most frequently reported number of living siblings.

Education 

27.6 percent of the men were either illiterate or had had no education beyond the second grade. 29.2 percent stopped their education between the third and sixth grades. 24.2 percent left school between the seventh and ninth grades, and 12.6 percent entered high school.

Fifteen years was the most frequently reported age for leaving school, 19.6 percent, with fourteen years as a close second, 17.6 percent. 14.6 percent had either less than one year of school or none at all, all but a few of these being Filipinos.

Economic Status 

73.6 percent were classed as unskilled laborers, including 

25.3 percent whose principal occupation had been agricultural work. 15.0 percent were semi-skilled laborers, 6.9 percent were skilled, and 3.1 percent had held clerical jobs. 

55.6 percent were steadily employed at the time they committed the crime, i.e., they had full time jobs which they had held for at least one week. 11.1 percent had intermittent, i.e., steady but not full time, or casual employment. 33.3 percent were unemployed. 

50.6 percent were earning their living. 23.0 percent were

unemployed and possessed neither money, property nor insurance with which to maintain themselves. 13.4 percent possessed savings, real estate, insurance or automobile, though generally these possessions had so little cash value that they might well be ignored as a resource for self support. In 13.0 percent of the cases there was no information in regard to the above points. 

80.1 percent were not receiving any form of relief, nor were their parents. 10.0 percent were on work relief or W.P.A. projects when the crime was committed. In 5.0 percent of the cases parents were receiving either direct or work relief. 

Personal Habits 

In 39.4 percent of the cases there was no record of the men's physical condition. Of the men on whom information was available 91.7 percent were in fair or good health, and 8.2 percent were physically handicapped, through loss of limbs, etc., or in poor health. 

63.2 percent of the men used liquor, 31.8 percent moderately, and 27.9 percent intemperately. 36.8 percent never drank, and 

28.0 percent neither drank nor gambled. 14.0 percent gambled and 24.2 went to prostitutes. 41.7 percent admitted they were promiscuous, having had relations either with prostitutes or women other than their wives. 28.4 percent denied all sex experience, 19.2 claimed relations with their wives only, and 3.4 percent were classed as perverted because they were convicted of sodomy, although not all of these were of the aggressive type.

Group Participation 

80.1 percent did not belong to any racial, fraternal, character building, or labor organization at the time of the crime. 

47.9 percent had never had military service, 10.7 percent had had peace time service and 6.2 percent had had war time service. In 35.2 percent of the cases, however, there was no information available on the subject of military training. 

62.1 percent were Roman Catholics by up bringing, 21.5 percent were Protestants, 8.4 percent were Buddhists and 5.7 percent claimed to have had no religious training at all. Very

few of the men were regular church attendants at the time of the crime.

Criminal Record 

57.5 percent had never been in a reformatory or penal institution. 30.2 percent had previously been in a jail or penitentiary, including Oahu Prison, and 19.5 percent had been in the 

Boys' Industrial School at Waialee. 

50.2 percent committed crimes against property, the leading crimes in this category being first and second degree burglary, 29.1 percent, and malicious conversion, 6.9 percent. 28.8 percent committed a crime of a sex nature, sex offenses against girls under 16 being the charge in 18.4 percent of the cases. 21.1 percent had committed crimes against the person, 4.2 percent having been committed for first and second degree murder. 

75.9 percent committed the crime on Oahu and 61.3 percent committed it in Honolulu. 

75.4 percent committed the crime without accomplices. 

It was impossible to determine motives with any great degree of accuracy, but it may be noted that in 39.8 percent of the cases the crime was a premeditated attempt to gain property, and in 23.4 percent of the cases it was a premeditated attempt to satisfy sex desires. 

56.4 percent had not taken alcoholic liquor within 3 hours before committing the crime. 14.5 percent admitted they had had several drinks within 3 hours but were aware of their actions, and 7.3 percent said they were dead drunk and unaware of what they were doing. There was no information available in 21.8 percent of the cases. 

86.9 percent pled guilty to the crime charged and were sentenced without jury trial by the judges before whom they appeared. The remainder had jury trials. 

59.0 percent were admitted to the prison in the calendar year 1935, and 41.0 percent were admitted between January 1 and September 1, 1936. 

65.5 percent had had previous contacts with or investigations by the police at one time or another in their careers. 27.6 percent had had 2-4 investigations and 18.0 percent had had 

15.7 percent had had 5-9 such investigations. one.

77.percent had never been in juvenile court whereas 22.2 percent had been in juvenile court one or more times. 

80.9 percent had never been committed to the Boys' Industrial School. Of those who were, 6.7 percent were committed before the age of 14. 

50.6 percent had never committed a police court offense. 

46.7 percent had committed one or more such offences, 9.9 percent having committed at least five. 18.6 percent had spent time in the County jail. 

75.9 percent had had no previous felony conviction, 19.6 percent had had one and 4.6 percent had had 2-4 previous felony convictions. Of these men who had been convicted previously 89 percent had committed a crime against property and nearly 10 percent a crime of sex. 

85.4 percent had never previously been in Oahu Prison. 

6.5 percent had been in the prison less than 2 years.

Of the 37 in the whole group of 261 who were paroled after a previous conviction, 45.8 percent served one year, and 32.4 percent served two years before violating parole. Of the parole violators, 78.7 percent were violators once, 8.2 percent (or 3) were violators 3-4 times. 

90.0 percent of the whole group had never been on probation. 9.6 percent had violated probation once.

It is possible to summarize the above information to some extent by pointing out information characteristic of 75 percent and again of 50 percent of the men.

Seventy-five percent of the men were under 35 years of age, had one or more living brothers or sisters and had no children. They were in fair or good health and neither they nor their parents were receiving relief. They did not belong to any organization. They had no accomplices and pled guilty to the crime charged. They had never been in juvenile court, nor committed to jail or the Boys' Industrial School, nor had they previously been convicted of a felony. 

Fifty percent of the men studied were under 25 years of age. They were healthy, unmarried citizens born in the Hawaiian Islands, and living with their surviving parent, or relatives. 

They had from four to nine living brothers and sisters but no children or dependants. Unskilled laborers, they were steadily

employed earning their living and were not receiving relief for themselves nor was their surviving parent. They did not belong to any organization nor had they had any military service. 

Although in the habit of drinking liquor, they had not had any in the 3 hours preceding the crime. They had had one or more contacts with, or investigations by, the police, but had never been in juvenile court, the Boys' Industrial School, or the City and County jail. They had never committed a police court offense or been convicted of a felony. All alone, they committed a crime against property in Honolulu. Pleading guilty they were incarcerated in 1935. 

SECTION II 

It is perhaps only a coincidence that a practically identical number of Filipinos and Hawaiians were admitted to the prison during the period under study. The fact however, provides an unusual opportunity for comparison of the social background and behavior of these two important groups. Each group, it will be remembered, had a larger proportion in the prison population than in the general population. The Hawaiian and part- 

Hawaiian group, numbering 73, represented 15 percent of the total population of the Islands and 27.9 percent of the recent prison inmates. The Filipino group with 75, represented but 

13.6 percent of the total population and 28.8 percent of the recent prison inmates.

The contrasts in the two groups are of course striking. One group contains the blood in greater or less purity of the native Polynesian, obliged in the brief span of a century and a half to adjust himself, in his native land, to the culture of the modern American civilization. The other, of Malayan origin, recently transplanted from a simple agricultural life under primitive conditions, seldom speaking English, living for the most part on plantations without the possibility of marriage with one of his race, and usually ignorant of the fine points of American criminal law. It is natural that their adjustment to the temptations inherent in the complex civilized life of today should be dissimilar. Again it is pointed out that no attempt has been made to interpret the significance of the facts which are revealed. 10

As in the first section covering the entire group, a composite picture of the Filipino inmates as contrasted with Hawaiian inmates, is attempted by presenting these characteristics which were found to be true for 50 percent of the men in question. 

Instead of presenting them in paragraph form the parallel column method is used below in Table I. The characteristics which are printed in italics are true of 75 percent of the men in question.

Filipino and Hawaiian Groups Age, Race, Citizenship 

49.4 percent of the Filipinos were under twenty-seven years of age, 34.7 percent being under twenty-five and 2.7 percent being over forty-two. The mode or most frequently reported age was twenty-four. The Hawaiians on the other hand showed a younger age grouping, 52.0 percent being under twenty-two, and 71.1 percent being under twenty-five. 5.6 percent were over forty-two. Their modal age was nineteen. 

92.0 percent of the Filipinos were born in the Philippines and 8.0 percent were born in the Hawaiian Islands, whereas 

98.6 percent of the Hawaiians were born in the Islands, 53.4 percent having been born on Oahu and 19.2 on Hawaii.

As regards racial stock, the Pure Hawaiians constituted 45.2 percent of the total in this group, Caucasian and Asiatic 

Hawaiians making up 34.2 percent and 20.6 respectively. 

29.3 percent of the Filipinos had been in the Islands 10-14 years, 28.0 percent 5-9 years, 12.0 percent 15-19 years. 8.0 percent had lived here all their lives. 98.6 percent of the 

Hawaiians had lived here all their lives. 

92.0 percent of the Filipinos were classed as Filipino Nationals, and 8.0 percent as U.S. citizens. All the Hawaiians were citizens.

Family Life and Background 

In regard to marital status both groups showed great similarity, the Filipinos having the following percentages: Single 11

70.percent; divorced, deserted, separated, widowed or living with common law wives 20.0 percent; married 9.3 percent. The 

Hawaiians had respectively the following percentages 69.8 percent, 20.6 percent, 9.6 percent.

Place of residence at the time the crime was committed shows great differences. The greatest proportion of Filipinos, 45.9 percent, were living with friends or else in labor camps, 24.0 percent were living alone, and only 16.0 percent were living with a wife, parent or near relative. 69.9 percent of the Hawaiians on the other hand were with wife, parent or relative, 15.1 percent were living with friends or in labor camps, and 13.7 percent were living alone. 

76.0 percent of the Filipinos reported they had no children, 16.0 percent claimed one to three children each, and 7.9 percent stated they had more than three children. 83.6 percent of the Hawaiians had no children, 13.7 percent had one to three, and 2.8 percent had over three. 

76.0 percent of the Filipinos had no dependants, 13.3 percent had one to three, and 8.0 percent had four to six. 74.0 percent of the Hawaiians had no dependants, 19.2 percent had one to three, and 5.5 percent had four to six.

The figures on the status of parents show differences which are all the more striking when one considers that the Filipinos are the higher age group. 54.7 percent of this group had lost at least one parent by death, 25.3 percent having lost both and 

29.4 percent having lost one. 34.6 percent had both parents alive and still married. 5.4 percent had a surviving parent who had remarried. Of the Hawaiians 67.1 percent had lost at least one parent, 31.5 percent having lost both, and 35.6 percent having lost one. Only 21.9 percent had both parents living and still married. 15.0 percent had a surviving parent who had remarried. 

6.7 percent of the Filipinos had no living brothers or sisters as against 8.2 percent of the Hawaiians. In both groups three was the number of living brothers and sisters most frequently reported. 12

Education 32.0 percent of the Filipinos were illiterate, 24.0 percent had had less than a 3rd grade education, 18.7 percent stopped their schooling between the third and sixth grades, 14.7 percent stopped between the seventh and ninth grades, and none entered high school. 9.3 percent claimed that they had taught themselves to read and write. 42.4 percent of the Hawaiians stopped school between the third and sixth grades, 31.5 percent between the seventh and ninth, 13.7 percent stopped before reaching the third grade, 11.0 percent entered high school and none was illiterate. 

41.3 percent of the Filipinos had either had less than one year of schooling or none at all. 18.7 percent left school at the age of fifteen, 13.3 at the age of fourteen, 9.3 percent between the ages of eleven and twelve, and 1.3 percent between the ages of seventeen and twenty. Only 1.4 percent of the

Hawaiians had less than one year or no schooling at all. 24.7 percent left school at the age of fourteen, 21.9 percent at fifteen and also between eleven and thirteen, and 13.7 percent left between seventeen and twenty.

Economic Status 

90.6 percent of the Filipinos were unskilled laborers by occupation, including 57.3 percent who had been employed principally in agriculture, mainly on the large plantations. 

6.7 percent had followed semi-skilled occupations but none had even held jobs calling for skilled labor. 80.8 percent of the Hawaiians were unskilled including, however, only 11.0 percent who had been employed in agricultural pursuits. 15.1 percent of this group were semi-skilled and 4.1 percent were skilled laborers. 

77.3 percent of the Filipinos held steady jobs at the time they committed their crime, 9.4 percent held jobs which were not full time or else were of a casual nature. 13.3 percent were unemployed. A considerable contrast to these figures exists in the case of the Hawaiians, only 38.4 percent having been steadily employed, 16.4 percent having been employed 13

at intermittent or casual jobs, and 45.2 percent having been unemployed.

A comparison of the financial status of the two groups shows that of the Filipinos 69.2 percent were earning their living, 13.4 percent had no job but possessed either insurance or a car, and 12.0 percent had neither property nor job; whereas, of the Hawaiians, only 45.3 percent were earning their living, 9.6 percent had insurance or a car, and 27.4 percent had neither property nor job. It must be noted however that in 

17.8 percent of the cases of Hawaiians there was no information on this subject as against no information in the cases of only 5.3 percent of the Filipinos. 

96.0 percent of the Filipinos were not receiving relief of any sort nor were their parents. 1.3 percent were on work relief. 67.2 percent of the Hawaiians were not receiving relief for themselves or parents, but 19.2 percent were on work relief, and in 8.2 percent of the cases parents were on 

Personal Habits 

Information as to physical condition was available on two thirds of the Filipinos and on about half of the Hawaiians. The figures on the Filipinos showed that 94.4 percent of those on whom there was information were in fair or good health as against 100.0 percent in the case of the Hawaiians. 

49.3 percent of the Filipinos neither drank nor gambled, 42.7 percent used liquor, 18.6 percent went to prostitutes, and 

13.3 percent gambled. Of the Hawaiians only 16.4 percent never drank nor gambled, 69.9 percent used liquor, 23.3 percent went to prostitutes and 13.7 percent gambled.

The figures on alcoholic temperance showed that of the 

Filipinos 57.3 percent never drank, 28.0 percent drank moderately, and 14.7 percent intemperately. In regard to the Hawaiians the figures are respectively 28.8 percent, 31.5 percent, and 34.3 percent. 

77.3 percent of the Filipinos claimed they had had no liquor within the three hours preceding the crime; 8.0 percent ad- 14

mitted having had drinks in this period but said they were aware of what they were doing, 6.7 percent said they had had so much to drink that they did not know what they were doing. No information was available in 8.0 percent of the cases. Information in regard to the Hawaiians was unavailable in 32.9 percent of the cases. 43.8 percent said that they had not been drinking in the three hours prior to the crime, 17.8 percent said they had but knew what they were doing, 5.5 percent said they were so drunk they were unaware of their actions. 

42.7 percent of the Filipinos admitted they were promiscuous in their sex relations, while 40.0 percent denied all sex experience. 13.3 percent claimed they had relations only with their wives, 4.0 percent were classed as perverts. 46.6 percent of the Hawaiians were promiscuous, 27.4 percent denied sex experience, 12.3 percent claimed experiences with wives only, 1.4 percent were classed as perverts. On 12.3 percent of the 

Hawaiians, no information was available on this question. 

Group Participation 

Of the Filipinos 88.0 percent did not belong to any organization, 6.7 percent belonged to racial, fraternal or labor groups, mostly racial, and 4.0 percent belonged to the Y.M.C.A. or other character building agency. 76.7 percent of the Hawaiians were without organiation membership, 4.1 percent were enrolled in fraternal or labor groups, and 6.9 percent were affiliated with character building agencies. In 12.3 percent of the cases of Hawaiians there was no information on this subject. 

62.7 percent of the Filipinos had never had any military service, 6.7 percent had had peace time service, and 2.7 percent had been under arms during the World War. However, in 28.0 percent of the cases there was no information on military training. 42.4 percent of the Hawaiians had had no service, 2.7 percent had been in the Army, Navy or National 

Guard in times of peace, and 15.1 percent had been in service during the World War. 39.7 percent of this group gave no report as to their military service. 

93.3 percent of the Filipinos were Roman Catholics and 15

6.percent were Protestants, as against 53.4 percent and 

37.0 percent respectively for the Hawaiians.

Criminal Record 

Of the Filipinos 77.3 percent had never previously been in any penal institution, 21.3 percent had been in a jail or penitentiary, and 2.6 percent had at one time been in the 

Boys Industrial School at Waialee. Only 43.8 percent of the 

Hawaiians had never previously been in a penal institution. 

Thus 56.2 percent had been in such an institution at one time or another, 34.3 percent having been in jail or in a prison, and 35.6 percent having been at the Boys' Industrial School. 

In regard to the type of crime committed, 38.5 percent of the Filipinos committed crimes of a sex nature, 34.7 committed crimes against the person and only 26.6 percent committed crimes against property. Contrasting sharply to this, 72.6 percent of the Hawaiians committed crimes against property, 20.6 percent crimes of sex, and only 6.9 percent crimes against the person.

The four leading crimes for the Filipinos were-sex intercourse with a girl under sixteen years of age, 21.3 percent, assault and battery with a dangerous weapon, 17.3 percent, burglary first degree 10.7 percent, carnal abuse 9.3 percent. 

For the Hawaiians the four leading crimes were burglary 1st degree, 32.9 percent, sex intercourse with a girl under sixteen, 15.1 percent, burglary 2nd degree, 13.7 percent, and malicious conversion, 11.0 percent.

Place of crime shows quite a difference between the two groups. Of the Filipinos 23.9 percent committed their crimes in Honolulu, 29.3 percent in Rural Oahu, 25.3 percent on Hawaii, 17.3 percent on Maui, and 4.0 percent on the other islands. 

72.5 percent of the Hawaiians on the other hand committed their crimes in Honolulu, 13.7 percent in Rural Oahu, 5.5 percent on Kauai, 4.1 percent on Hawaii, and 4.1 percent on the other islands. 

82.7 percent of the Filipinos had no accomplices, whereas this is true of only 69.8 percent of the Hawaiians. 16

Two motives for the crimes identical for both groups, seemed to predominate, a premediated attempt to gain property instigated the crime in 24.0 percent of the Filipinos, and 58.9 percent of the Hawaiians, while a premediated attempt to satisfy sex desires led 29.3 percent of the Filipinos and 17.8 percent of the Hawaiians to commit their crimes. 

In both groups the percentage who pled guilty and so were tried by a judge instead of a jury was very high, 86.7 percent in the case of the Filipinos and 94.5 percent in the case of the Hawaiians. 

66.6 percent of the Filipinos were incarcerated in the calendar year 1935, and 58.9 percent of the Hawaiians. 

57.3 percent of the Filipinos had never previously been investigated by or had contacts with the police. 22.6 percent had had one such investigation, and 15.9 percent had had two to four, and 4.0 percent had had five to nine. In contrast to this 

87.7 percent of the Hawaiians had been investigated previously by the police, 35.7 percent having had two to four investigations, 23.2 percent having had five to nine, 21.9 percent having had one, and 5.6 percent having had ten to fifteen. 

96.0 percent of the Filipinos had never been in juvenile court, but only 57.5 percent of the Hawaiians had never been there. 

97.4 percent of the Filipinos had never been committed to the Boys' Industrial School, nor had 64.4 percent of the Hawaiians. Of the 34.2 percent of the Hawaiians who had been so committed 15.1 percent were committed before the age of 14. 61.6 percent of these commitments in the case of the Hawaiians were for stealing and 23.1 percent for incorrigibility.

None of the Filipinos had ever been recommitted to the Industrial School but 13.7 percent of the Hawaiians had. 

Of the Filipinos 65.3 percent had never been convicted of committing a police court offense, 20.0 percent had been so convicted once, and 10.6 percent two to four times. Only 46.6 percent of the Hawaiians had never been convicted of such an offense, 21.9 percent having been convicted of two to four, 13.7 percent of one, and 12.2 percent of five or more. 17

82.percent of the Filipinos had never been incarcerated in the City County jail, nor had 76.7 percent of the Hawaiians. 

93.3 percent of the Filipinos had never been convicted previously of a felony, 6.7 percent had been so convicted; whereas 

58.8 percent of the Hawaiians had not been so convicted, 32.9 percent having been convicted of one felony, 4.1 percent of three, and 2.7 percent of two.

Of the Filipinos and Hawaiians previously convicted of a felony, 80.0 percent and 90.0 percent respectively had committed a crime against property. 

98.6 percent of the Filipinos had never been in Oahu Prison previously, nor had 76.7 percent of the Hawaiians. 6.8 percent of the latter had been in this prison for from five to ten years, 5.4 percent from three to four years, and 5.5 percent had been there two years. 

98.6 percent of the Filipinos had never been on parole, nor had 78.1 percent of the Hawaiians. 22.0 percent of the latter had been on parole previously and 11.0 percent had violated that parole within one year, 5.5 percent had violated it within two years, 4.1 percent within three to four years, and 1.4 percent within five to eight years. Needless to say these figures deal only with the men studied and do not apply to the many Filipinos and Hawaiians who have never violated parole. 

A glance at this question of parole violation from another angle, that of the number of parole violations, shows that 15.1 percent of the Hawaiians violated parole once, 5.5 percent three to four times, and 1.4 twice. 

96.0 percent of the Filipinos had never violated probation and the 4.0 percent who had had done so only once. 83.6 percent of the Hawaiians had never violated probation and of those who had 15.1 percent had done so once and 1.4 percent twice. 18
