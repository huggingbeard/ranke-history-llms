TO THE STUDENT 

The Workbook for Secretarial Training is organized as cases or duties involving typical office situations. The term "case" is used here to mean a "typical situation." Imagine yourself in an office as you work out these duties and remember at all times that your success as a secretary or stenographer depends upon the kind of preparation which you have for the job. Mere completion of these case studies will not guarantee success in an office. On the other hand, adequate study of the foundational materials in a basic text such as The Training of a Secretary, and then careful completion of the cases in this book should give sufficient background to your training for a beginning position as a secretary or stenographer. It is assumed that you are adequately prepared in shorthand, typewriting, and other basic work. 

The pages of this book can be detached as you work on them. Wherever possible use a typewriter. All letters should be typed, and most of the exercises are so set up that you can type them also. Completed cases should be filed after they have been checked by your instructor, and frequent reviews will help you to fix in your mind the essential lessons in office procedures.

The tests which are provided in this book will serve as a means of checking your progress. The answers to the questions should come naturally as you work out the 

Whenever possible supplement your knowledge by reading authentic books that are devoted entirely to the subjects under consideration. cases. 

Remember that a good worker is well prepared. You would not accept poor work; do not do poor work. iv
