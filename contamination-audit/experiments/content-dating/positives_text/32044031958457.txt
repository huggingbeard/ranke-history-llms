Lawyers and judges receive many inquiries from persons who wish to obtain a child for adoption. This pamphlet has been prepared to help such persons to understand the adop tion laws and procedures and to find a child for adoption in a safe way.

Adoption laws and procedures in Wisconsin reflect the interests of the state and the community in affording protection for the child, the natural parents and the adoptive parents in adoption proceedings.

What is Adoption? 

Adoption is the legal procedure by which a child. becomes through court action, part of a family other than that of his natural parents. 

It is a serious step to all persons concerned. It determines the entire future of the child since it severs his ties with his natural parents and relatives permanently and transplants him into a new family where he will remain until he is grown. There he will receive the care and treatment which will determine the kind of adult which he will become. To natural parents, adoption usually means relinquishing the child forever without the privilege of seeing him or even knowing his whereabouts. To the adoptive parents, it means undertaking the care of a child who will become a permanent member of their family and to whom they will have the same obligations as a child born to them. 

Any minor (a person under 21) present within the state when the petition for adoption is filed may be adopted.

An adult may also be adopted. This leaflet deals. with adoption of minors. The procedure for adults is similar but considerably simpler.

How May a Child be Obtained for Adoption?

The law provides that children may be placed for adoption only by a licensed child welfare agency or a public agency authorized to place children for adoption, or by the parent or guardian after obtaining written approval of the county court. The law also requires consent of the State Department of Public Welfare to the sending or bringing of a child into the state or sending or taking a child out of the state for adoption.

Non-authorized persons, including doctors and lawyers, are not permitted to act as intermediaries in finding children for adoption or making placements. 

But lawyers, in a professional capacity, may represent the parties and handle all legal details required by the adoption laws.

It is recommended that persons seeking a child for adoption make application to a licensed child-placing agency, as such an adoption offers safeguards to the child and the adoptive parents. 

A foster home permit from the Department of Public Welfare or an authorized agency is required for adoptive homes, except those of the guardian or a relative. 

How Does an Agency Help Adoptive Parents? 

An agency does not place a child for adoption until it is legally available for adoption, the rights of the parents have been terminated by a court, and the agency has been appointed guardian. The agency then consents to the adoption. In this way, there is no opportunity for the natural parents to learn where the child has been placed or to attempt to contact him at some future time. If a child is secured for adoption through some other source, it is sometimes possible for the parents to learn the name of the adoptive parents.

The agency's study of the child and his parentage may reduce the risk that the child may develop a serious physical or mental difficulty.

Through study of the child and the adoptive parents, a child placement agency will consider the needs of the child and the wishes of the adoptive applicants and try to place a child where physical characteristics, mental capacities, personality, and religious background will assure the best possible conditions for both child and adoptive family. 

The licensed child-placing agency requires that a child live with the adoptive parents for a period of time, perhaps as long as a year, before the adoption may be completed. During this period, the agency maintains contact with the adoptive parents and the child in order to assure that the placement is a happy one for all concerned. Any difficulties presented by the child in the adoptive home may be discussed with the agency representative who will be able to assist in meeting these problems. If the child should fail to develop normally, if the adoptive family does not desire to continue with the adoption, or if the adjustment is unsatisfactory, the agency will receive the child into their custody again and care for him until another plan is made. 

A child placed for adoption by his parents or guardian without the assistance of an agency is usually adopted as soon as possible without adequate waiting or adjustment period. Because of the everpresent fear that the natural parents may attempt to regain custody of the child before the adoption is final, adoptive parents are usually anxious to complete the adoption as soon as possible. If the child develops a serious mental or physical defect or proves to be temperamentally unsuited to their home after the adoption has been ordered, the adoptive parents must continue their responsibility. 

Will Applicants Receive a Child from the Agency?

There are many more families seeking children for adoption than there are children available for adoption. This is especially true for children under a year old. For this reason the prospective adoptive parents should contact an agency for a child as soon as they know that adoption is the only way for them to have a child in their home. Because of lesser demand, older children are frequently available, and adoptive parents may consider whether an older child is more suitable for their particular situation. 

The child-placing agencies have standards of their own by which they judge applicants. Some applicants may not receive a child because of their being too old, having inadequate income, having inadequate housing, being in poor health, or because a child likely to fit in their home does not become available.

Adoption Procedure in Wisconsin 

Persons who have obtained a child and the necessary written consent to adopt him must petition the court for the adoption. The petition must be filed with the county court in the county where the adoptive parents reside.

In filing an adoption petition it is advisable for the adoptive parents to secure the services of an attorney who will advise them and take care of all the legal aspects of the adoption.

Upon the filing of an adoption petition, the court sets a time and place for hearing the petition, allowing time for the required investigation and recommendation. A copy of the notice must be sent to the agency making the investigation of the suitability of the adoption, and to the State Department of Public Welfare, when its recommendation is required. 

The court also orders an investigation by a competent agency of the environment and antecedents of the child to be adopted to ascertain that he is a proper subject for adoption, and of the home of the petitioner to determine whether it is a suitable home. 

The agency ordered to make the investigation must make a report to the court within ninety days. The report is part of the record of the proceedings. 

When the guardian of the child is the Department of Public Welfare, a licensed child welfare agency or a county welfare department, an additional investigation is not required but such agency must file a report of its investigation.

Recommendation of State Department of Public Welfare 

Unless the consent to the adoption was given by the State Department of Public Welfare, a licensed child welfare agency, or a county child welfare department, the recommendation of the State Department of Public Welfare is required for the adoption of the following minors: (a) An illegitimate minor; (b) A minor who has no living parents or whose parents have had their rights legally terminated; (c) A non-resident minor; (d) A minor whose non-resident parent has executed a written consent to the adoption, valid at the time of its execution.

The Department has ninety days in which to present its recommendation.

Court Hearing 

The hearing is held at the time and place set by the judge. It may be held privately in the judge's offices unless a person with a legal interest objects. 

The petitioner and the child to be adopted, if 14 or older, must attend unless the court orders otherwise. 

No petition for the adoption of a minor shall be granted until he has lived six months in the home of the petitioner. The six months' residence may be waived by order of the court, upon a petition stating the reason therefor, if the court is satisfied that immediate action is desirable and the best interests of the child will be furthered thereby. 

If after the hearing and a study of the required reports and the recommendation of the Department of Public Welfare, the judge is satisfied that the necessary consents have been filed and that the adoption is in the best interests of the child, he shall make an order granting the adoption. The order may change the name of the child to that of the adoptive parent.

All records and papers pertaining to the adoption are closed and kept in separate locked files. No person shall have access to these records except on court order.
