WORK RELEASE-STUDY RELEASE PROGRAM Calendar Years 1971 and 1972 

The Wisconsin Division of Corrections has continued to support and expand its Work Release-Study Release Program for adult offenders. The program offers a two-pronged strategy to meet some of the varied transitional needs of selected clients as they leave the highly structured environment of a correctional institution to live in their home communities. 5419/11 While participating in the program, institutionalized offenders are permitted to attend approved training or educational programs, or to be employed in a local community. The program enables the inmate and the Division of Corrections to utilize many community educational, employment, training, and vocational resources available in Wisconsin. 

Attempts have continuously been made to place clients in the program who have progressed through individualized and group treatment, training, and education programs and have demonstrated positive motivation, maturity, and stability, thus indicating a readiness and potential to profit from a work release and/or study release experience. A work release placement can enable a person to reduce debts and support a family or save money to pay living expenses following release. 

Study release offers to selected clients the opportunity for training or further education in order to expand the individual's vocational or academic career upon release.

New Program Developments The opening of the Community Corrections Center in Milwaukee in April 1972 

permits clients who plan to live in the greater Milwaukee area to become involved in meaningful placements through coordinated efforts of the State Employment Service, parole agents, vocational rehabilitation counselors, and work release coordinators. The Center is a small, pre-release living facility where work release, study release, and parole planning can be coordinated for Milwaukee area clients. 1244415

- 2 - The appointment in May 1972 of six institutional work release coordinators, operating as job finders and counselors, has improved the program's selection and placement processes and afforded more opportunity to match the skills of the individual with available placement resources. Work release coordinators, in cooperation with employers, education and training facilities, other related agencies, and the offenders help plan individual programs of work, study, or training for clients nearing release. 

The introduction of assessment and evaluation centers as a part of the reception process at the Wisconsin State Prison and the Wisconsin State Reformatory has permitted an earlier identification of potential work release and study release clients. After demonstrating a potential for the program, the individual can be referred for a work release or study release placement in which he can take advantage of the wide variety of community employment, training, and academic resources. 

January 1973 marked the beginning of a computerized program information system for the Work Release-Study Release Program. New data collection forms were designed and computer programming initiated, with a primary goal of making available more descriptive and evaluative data to program decision makers and persons interested in the Work Release-Study Release Program. 

Program Future 

Work release and study release have become an important aspect of the total corrections program. Descriptive and evaluative data, however, are still not available which would support or refute the premise, held by many, that participation in a work or study release program greatly increases the probability of a successful transition to parole supervision and reduces the chance of early return to the institutional care. Individual situations can dramatically demonstrate the impact of work and study release participation in the successful parole adjustment of some participants. However, it is possible that those who succeeded might have done so without involvement in the program. It is planned that within two years information will be available which will document the impact of the program in terms of its program objectives.

- 3. 

Passage of an adult furlough law to permit qualified offenders to leave correctional facilities on a temporary basis to seek employment or to enroll in educational facilities would enhance and expand the Work Release-Study Release Program. A furlough program would also add another useful program resource to the Division of Corrections, enabling the Division to satisfy more of the immediate, transitional needs of its clients. 

Discussion of Data 

In calendar year 1971, 413 inmates participated in the program, being placed in 436 work release and study release placements. Information is presented in the appendix tables on the 267 work releasees or the 284 work release placements terminated during the calendar year. Information is also presented for the total 102 study release placements. 

In calendar year 1972, 604 inmates participated in the program, being placed on 658 work release and study release placements. Information is presented in the appendix tables on the 371 work releasees or the 410 work release placements terminated during the calendar year. Information is also presented on the total 178 study release placements.

Both work release and study release have grown steadily since 1967, averaging about a 75 percent increase in program placements per year.

The increase from 1971 to 1972 in work release program placements seems to be a result of an influx of younger offenders on the program because the median age of work releasees dropped from 31 to 29 years of age. 

Minority group work release members nearly doubled from 1971 to 1972 increasing from 74 to 133. This was primarily due to the opening in April 1972 of the Community Corrections Center in Milwaukee, where the largest minority population resides. The proportion of females to males remained constant from 

1971 to 1972, the females representing only about four percent of the work release population.

On the basis of a comparison of the 1972 population with that of 1971, the following statements can be made: A higher proportion of the committing of offenses was those against a person in 1972. A greater percentage of the population had no prior felony convictions in 1972. The population in 1972 had completed more years in school.

The average (mean) earnings dropped from $1,035 per person in 1971 to under $950 per person in calendar year 1972. Again this is primarily due to the opening of the Community Corrections Center, which places work releasees for a shorter duration of time prior to release. 

The in-program success rate* for the Work Release-Study Release Program showed little or any decline from 1971 to 1972. The work release success rate decreased from 92 percent to 87 percent and the study release success rate increased from 72 percent to 76 percent. 

During 1972, every adult state correctional institution placed offenders on the Work Release-Study Release Program. Twelve of the 14 institutions placed offenders on both work release and study release. If placements are located in areas distant from the state institutions, offenders are also housed in halfway houses or county jails. Wisconsin has attempted to make the program a statewide program, striving to meet the transitional needs of its clients.
