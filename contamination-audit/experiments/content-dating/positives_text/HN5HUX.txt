His railroad, the Silver Town Northern, controlled only fifty-five miles of track, including sidings and loading spurs. Three narrow-gauge engines, four combination coaches, and thirty-five assorted freight cars were its entire rolling stock. 

There were times when Mr. Orest was very optimistic over his property-particularly during the summer months when everything was going smoothly; but at other times, he would gladly have given the railroad away. That was the way he was feeling at present. For the past six weeks, Silver Town had been completely isolated from the outside world. The entire length of the Animas Canyon had filled with massive snowslides, and his railroad had

been idle, while he had tried to dig out with man power. It was slow work!

Long since, Silver Town had completely run out of fresh meats, butter, eggs, and other perishable commodities. The Sunnyside 

Smelter had filled their bins with ore concentrates and now was on the verge of closing down until the railroad opened again. On the street-corners of the town they were accusing Mr. Orest of aiding and abetting in the slackening of work. Those miners who had been laid off because of the fullness of the bins used strong language in denouncing him, and yet he was powerless to do a thing! 

No wonder the railroad owner frowned, and in spite of his better judgment found himself interested in what this young Joe Jutton had to say. Joe did not see Mr. Buckel again while in the hospital. At the end of thirty days he was like a caged tiger. It seemed that he must die if he were not allowed to get back to Silver Town. 

Added to the confinement Tubby developed a sense of secrecy which was maddening. The fat youth radiated something he knew and something Joe must not know until the proper time.

To all of Joe's questions regarding the railroad Tubby was noncommittal. "Ith thtill runnin'," was his invariable answer. "Where's Mr. Orest?" "Heth in Thilver Town, buthy."

Joe climbed on the coach and took a seat near the rear end. The train shuffled forward. 

He rode languidly watching the passing scenery. A few miles out of Midvale he straightened up on his seat. Something was happening along here! Beside the right of way was a long windrow of crushed, black smelter slag. Could Mr. Flint be ballasting the line?

Next a double line of new, heavy rails appeared thrown on top of the windrowed slag. Everywhere were new creosoted crossties.

The high bridge was the greatest surprise of all. A small army of men worked on it, replacing the temporary wooden structure hastily built by Joe's volunteers with a steel truss. The train was delayed there almost twenty minutes while one of the steel girders was slipped into place on solid concrete foundations. 

When the conductor came through next

time Joe asked him, "What's taking place on this railroad?" 

The conductor grinned. "It's goin' through a real rehabilitation," he said, and walked on through the coach. 

Above the high bridge, the new, heavy rails had been laid and the slag ballast was under the ties. The track was smooth as a dance floor, and the engine pulling them rattled around the curves as fast as she could turn a wheel. 

Mr. Orest, Tubby, and Mr. Buckel were on the platform when Joe unloaded from the coach. All hurried to greet him. All of them wore grins of joy as though they had not a trouble in the world.
