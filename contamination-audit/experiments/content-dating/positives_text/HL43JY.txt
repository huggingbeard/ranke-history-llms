of Louisiana by adding thereto a new section, to be designated as Section 27 thereof, to authorize a code of ethics for governmental affairs in the State of Louisiana for elected officials, members of the Legislature, employees and other persons, to provide for the establishment of the Louisiana Commission on Governmental Ethics to administer the code of ethics for state employees, members of Boards and Commissions and other persons; to provide for establishment of the Louisiana Board of Ethics for State Elected Officials; to fix the powers and duties of each with respect to the Code of Ethics; to provide for appeals from administrative rulings of the commission and board and agency heads; to authorize the staff of the Department of State Civil Service to render all normal investigative and staff services for the commission and board.

Section 1. Be it resolved by the Legislature of Louisiana, two-thirds of members elected to each House concurring, that there shall be submitted to the electors of the State of Louisiana, for their approval or rejection in the manner provided for by law, a proposition to amend Article XIX of the Constitution of the State of Louisiana by adding thereto a new section to be designated as Section 27 thereof, which shall read as follows: 

§ 27. Governmental Ethics 

1. Preamble; declaration of policy and purpose

A. It is essential to the proper operation of democratic government that public officials be independent and impartial; that governmental decisions and policy be made in the proper channels of the governmental structure; that public office not be used for private gain beyond the remuneration provided by law; and that there be public confidence in the integrity of the government. The attainment of one or more of these ends is impaired whenever there exists a conflict between the private interests of an elected official or government employee and his duties as such. The public interest, therefore, requires that the law protect against such conflicts of interest and establish appropriate ethical standards with respect to the conduct of elected officials and government employees in situations where actual or potential conflicts exist.

B. It is also essential to the proper operation of democratic government that only those best qualified be encouraged to serve the government. Accordingly, legal safeguards against conflicts of interest must be so designed as not unnecessarily or unreasonably to impede the recruitment and retention by the government of those men and women who are best qualified to serve it. An essential principle

Act 528 underlying the staffing of the governmental structure is that its elected officials and employees should not be denied the opportunity, available to all other citizens, to acquire and retain private economic and other interests, except where conflicts with the responsibility of such elected officials and employees to the public cannot be avoided. 

C. It is the policy and purpose of this Section to implement these objectives of protecting the integrity of the government of this state and of facilitating the recruitment and retention of qualified personnel by prescribing essential restrictions against conflicts of interest in state government without creating unnecessary barriers to public service. 

2. Code of Governmental Ethics 

The Legislature is authorized to establish a code of governmental ethics to implement and effectuate the policies and purposes of this Section. 

3. State Employees; Louisiana Commission on Governmental Ethics A. The legislature may establish the Louisiana Commission on Governmental Ethics with its domicile in the city of Baton Rouge. The commission shall consist of five persons to be selected in the manner chosen by the Legislature. 

The Legislature shall provide suitable office space, equipment, and funds for the commission and shall provide for the expenses of the members of the Commission. All investigations and normal staff functions shall be conducted by the Department of State Civil Service. 

B. The Louisiana Commission on Governmental Ethics shall have jurisdiction to investigate all allegations of violations of the code of ethics enacted by the Legislature with respect to all state employees other than state officials covered by part 4 hereof. The powers, duties and responsibilities of the commission shall be prescribed by the Legislature and shall include authority to investigate, hold hearings and make public findings of violations of the code of ethics enacted by the Legislature with respect to all state employees except state officials covered by part 4 hereof. 

C. Appeals. 

1) Whenever any action is taken against a classified employee by an agency head on his own initiative, for alleged violations of the code of ethics, the employee shall have the right to appeal the action to the State Civil Service Commission under the rules and regulations enacted by the Commission pursuant to Article XIV section 15 of this Constitution. 

2) The Commission may be authorized to hear and decide

Act 528 all appeals from action taken by the head of an agency against all persons covered by the code of ethics, other than classified employees and elected officials covered by part 4 hereof. 

3) Whenever action is taken against any employee by the Commission or by an agency head at the order or request of the Commission, the employee shall have the right to appeal therefrom to the Court of Appeals, First Circuit if application to the Commission is made within 30 days after the commission's decision becomes final. The Court of Appeals, First Circuit, shall promulgate rules of procedure to be followed in taking the lodging of such appeals. 

4. Louisiana Board of Ethics for State Elected Officials 

A. The legislature shall establish the Louisiana Board of 

Ethics for Elected Officials with its domicile in the City of Baton Rouge. The Board shall consist of three persons to be selected in the manner chosen by the Legislature. 

No member shall serve while holding public office or while otherwise employed by the state, other than in the capacity of a retired justice of the Louisiana Supreme Court, or retired judge of the Courts of Appeal or District Courts of the State, including the Civil and Criminal district Courts of the Parish of Orleans. 

The Legislature shall provide suitable office space, equipment, and funds for the commission and shall provide for the expenses of the members of the Commission. All investigations and normal staff functions shall be conducted by the Department of the State Civil Service. 

B. The Louisiana Board of Ethics for State Elected Offi cials shall have jurisdiction to investigate all allegations of violations of the code of governmental ethics enacted by the Legislature with respect to all elected state officials, including members of the Legislature. The powers, duties and responsibilities of the Louisiana Board of Ethics for 

State Elected Officials shall be prescribed by the Legisla ture, but shall include the authority to investigate, hold hearings and make public findings of violations of the code of ethics enacted by the Legislature for state elected officials, including members of the Legislature. 

C. The decision of the Board shall be subject to appeal which shall be granted to the Court of Appeals, First Circuit, if application to the Board is made within 30 days after the Board's decision become final. The Court of Appeals, First 

Circuit, shall promulgate rules of procedure to be followed in taking and lodging such appeals.

 Proposing an amendment to Section 1 of Article XXI of the

Section 1. Be it resolved by the Legislature of Louisiana, two-thirds of the members elected to each house concurring, that there shall be submitted to the electors of the State of Louisiana for their approval or rejection, in the manner provided by law, a proposal to amend Section 1 of Article XXI of the Constitution of Louisiana, so that the same may be made to read as follows: § 1. Proposals; procedure; approval; proclamation; multiple amendments; revisions of articles; numbering

Section 1. Propositions for amending the constitution may be made by the legislature at any session, and if two-thirds of the members elected to each house shall concur therein, after such proposed amendment or amendments have been read in both houses on three separate days, such proposed amendment or amendments, together with the yeas and nays thereon, shall be entered on the journal, and the secretary of state shall cause the same to be published in one newspaper in each parish of the state in which a newspaper is published, twice within not less than thirty nor more than sixty days preceding an election for representatives in the legislature or in congress. The legislature shall designate the election at which the said amendment or amendments shall be submitted to the electors for their approval or rejection. The result of said election shall be made known by proclamation of the governor.

If a majority of the electors voting thereon shall vote for 12

Act the proposed amendment or amendments, then such amendment or amendments shall become a part of the constitution, effective twenty days after issuance of the governor's proclamation; provided, however, that any proposed amendment to the constitution which levies a new tax, or which increases an existing tax, or which authorizes the levy or increase of a tax, and which, by reason of enumeration, designation or population classification, directly affects any five or fewer parishes, municipalities or special districts, or a combination of any five or fewer of any of these, shall be adopted and shall become a part of the constitution only if a majority of the electors voting thereon in the state as a whole and also a majority, in the aggregate, of the electors voting thereon in the affected political subdivision or subdivisions shall vote in favor of adoption of the proposed amendment. 

When more than one amendment shall be submitted at the same election, they shall be so submitted as to enable the electors to vote on each amendment separately; provided, however, that the Legislature may propose, as one amendment, a revision of any article of this Constitution. The proposition may set forth either the entire article, as revised, or in their entirety, only those sections, paragraphs, or subparagraphs in which any change is being made, or which are being added; provided that a section or subdivision therein may be repealed by reference without setting forth in its entirety the remainder of the article, section, or paragraph, as the case may be. The proposition for the revision of an article shall have a title containing a brief summary of the changes proposed by the revision.

No proposition for amending the constitution shall be considered unless introduced in the legislature within the first thirty days of its session.

Amendments which have been adopted shall be numbered consecutively, and shall, where possible, refer to the numbers of the articles and sections which have been amended, except as otherwise herein provided.

To supply municipal services upon a district basis to all or a part of the municipality and to charge the cost thereof against the residents and real property owners of such district, the governing authority of the City of Baton Rouge (hereinafter sometimes referred to as "governing authority") is hereby authorized to create one or more municipal service districts within the corporate limits of the City of Baton Rouge as the same now exist, or may be extended or amended from time to time. Any such municipal service district may contain all or any part of the City of Baton Rouge, and two or more municipal service districts may overlap as to all or part of the territory included therein. 

The governing authority of the City of Baton Rouge, before finally creating any such district, shall give notice of its intention to create the district by publishing in the official journal of the City at least once not less than fifteen days prior to the date of hearing, a notice of intention to create such district and fixing a date for the holding of a public hearing thereon, in which all persons interested may be heard on, and make objections to, the creation, boundaries, purposes of the district, and the matter of benefit accruing to their property by reason of the creation thereof. Such notice shall set out the proposed name and boundaries of the district, the purpose or purposes for which it is to be created, and the time, date and place at which a hearing will be held on the creation of the district. After hearing and passing upon the objections, if any, the governing authority may create the district by the adoption of a resolution designating the name of the district, the boundaries thereof, and the purposes for which the district has been created. The governing authority shall hear all objections at the time appointed, or at any time to which the hearing may be adjourned, and pass upon the same, and its decision shall be final and conclusive. At the hearing, the governing authority may eliminate some of the territory or one or more proposed purposes 89

Act of the district, but no new territory or purpose shall be added without a new hearing pursuant to notice as directed above. 

A municipal service district may be abolished, its boundaries may be changed or new purposes may be added or purposes may be eliminated by resolution of the governing authority at any time after the creation of such district pursuant to notice and hearing as hereinabove provided, but no such change or elimination shall relieve any real property or the owners thereof of the liability for taxes to pay indebtedness lawfully incurred while said property was in said district. Where a district is abolished or the boundaries changed in respect to which new properties are eliminated or new properties are added and change in the status of existing ad valorem taxes in these districts are required either as to existing properties or as to properties to be added, any such changes are to be first approved at an election to be held for any such purpose specifying the nature of the proposed change, at which the proposal or proposals submitted are to be approved by a majority in number and amount of the qualified taxpaying electors as is required hereunder in respect to the original imposition of any such taxes. 

A copy of the resolution creating such a district or changing its boundaries or purposes shall be published one time in the official journal of the City. For a period of thirty (30) days from the date of such publication, any person in interest shall have the right to contest the legality of the creation of such district or the change in its boundaries or purposes for any cause; after which time no one shall have any cause or right of action to contest the regularity, formality or legality of such creation or change for any cause whatsoever, and the regularity and validity of all action had in the creation of the district shall be conclusively presumed, and no court may thereafter inquire into such matters. 

Districts created under this Section shall be political subdivisions of the State of Louisiana and a body corporate and politic, and shall have all powers of public corporations including perpetual existence; the power to incur debt and contract obligations; to own property; to sue and to be sued; to do and perform all acts in their corporate capacity and in their corporate names which are necessary and proper for the purposes of their creation; and generally to do and perform any and all acts and deeds necessary to carry out the objects and purposes of their creation. Such districts may incur debt and issue general obligation or revenue bonds, certificates and other evidences of indebtedness secured by unlimited ad valorem taxes levied on all taxable real property in the district, revenues, or special assessments, as the case may be, within the limits, and in the same manner and subject to the same conditions as provided in this Constitution, and under the laws of the State of Louisiana. 90
