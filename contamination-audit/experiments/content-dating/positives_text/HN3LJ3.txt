PRETTY, vivacious Kathleen 

O'Connor was her widowed father's pride and joy. Matt, anxious to show off his daughter and to have the world know her beauty and talent, did not realize he was keeping her from having the fun and good times to which every young girl is entitled. 

To Matt, Kathleen's local success as a drum major was but a stepping-stone to a Hollywood contract and further glory. What he didn't take into account was that 

Kathleen was also her mother's daughter, and that her mother had defied family and friends and had thrown away a promising career which had barely begun in order to follow the dictates of her heart and to sign a contract with Cupid.

HER MOTHER'S DAUGHTER

CHAPTER ONE 

THE BAND blared. A hot sun beat upon the crowd lining the main streets of the small California town. It was a good natured crowd, calling each other by first names, pushing and shoving for a better glimpse of the slender girl in the glittering, short-skirted uniform, whose feet in high white boots pranced in perfect accord with the pounding drums. 

In one hand she carelessly twirled a baton and turned her gay, dazzling young smile with impartial charm from one to the other of her cheering audience. Many of them were her friends. "There's Kathleen," they said. "Some new uniform she's wearing. Her father sent to San 

Francisco for it. Look how it shines!" 7

"It's made of satin," one of the girls explained, while another added, "It must be fun to march at the head of a parade like that. She always looks as if she were having the time of her life." "Well, it's work, too. She had to practice for hours before she could learn to twirl that baton." "She's beautiful, isn't she?" "There's not another drum major in California like her." "Mat O'Connor must be plenty proud of her."

Remarks such as these were incense to her father. A very inconspicuous man, carelessly dressed and easily unnoticed, he made a point of slipping from one group to another, hurrying from block to block in order to catch the first gasp of approval; the hearty round of applause. "She's getting the biggest hand of anyone," he told himself. "But then she always does."

Hat on the back of his head, he wiped the beads of sweat from his brow and tried to catch the eye of the girl in the uniform. "She is letting down a little," he thought, "She smiles as if she were tired."

His gray anxious eyes met the blue ones beneath the shining helmet and instantly the young

head was tossed a bit higher. The smile which had become a trifle automatic after an hour's steady marching was turned on full force. "Atta girl," Matt thought proudly. "All I have to do is look at her and she knows what 

I mean."

He stood with averted head pretending not to hear the enthusiastic comments as the gay little figure proceeded. Wait until they saw her tonight with her accordion! There was to be another parade at eight o'clock and she was to stop in front of the grandstand in the park and play it for the first time.

The lessons had been expensive and the new uniform with the soft white leather boots would keep him broke for a month, but it was worth it to see her like this. Spirited, lovely and gay! 

These parades on Decoration Day and other holidays were the high points of his life. No sacrifice was too great to make for her. And remembering the appointment he had made for seven o'clock at his drug store that evening,

Matt smiled and cut through the crowd to the other side of the square.

CHAPTER Two

CHAPTER THREE

Kathleen bought brightly flowered chintz which she lined and fastened to a metal rod. 

In the mornings with the sun streaming through upon the familiar furniture it was almost homelike, but in the afternoons when the steam heat was off it seemed to reflect her own mood of loneliness and depression.

Aunt Nora suggested had already been interviewed.

A letter from Dean had made her cry in the
