ANNEXATION (C. B, L. 37) Annexation, First Class Township, Act No. 161, May 13, 

1937, 28 Mun. 183; C. B. L. 1936 edition, 37: also Act No 

588, July 2; 28 Mun. 272; C. B. L. 1936 edition 37. Act No. 

161 was hastily passed and approved in order to meet a situation in Allegheny County. It was framed originally to apply only to second class townships, but in the course of passage was amended to include first class townships. It was held up in the Senate and second class townships excluded. The purpose is to prevent a borough or a city from clipping off a certain section of township as soon as that section becomes a "money-maker" by reason of increased assessed valuation through industrial development. The whole or part of the township may be annexed.

Act No. 161 provides as follows: 

Section 1. Be it enacted, &c., That hereafter no township of the first class, nor any part of any such township, shall be annexed to a contiguous city or borough in accordance with any existing law providing for such annexation, unless the voters of the entire township have first consented to such annexation. Whenever any proceeding for such annexation shall be commenced, the same shall not be concluded, and the annexation shall not become effective, until there has first been submitted to the electors of the entire township, in accordance with the election law for the submission of such questions, a proper question to ascertain the will of the electors with respect to such proposed annexation. Such question shall be printed on ballots provided by the county commissioners and shall be submitted at any general or municipal election occurring at least sixty days after a petition shall have been filed with the county commissioners by the corporate

authorities of the borough or city to which such annexation is to be made, asking for the submission of such question. If at any such election a majority of those voting on such question shall consent to such annexation, then such annexation may be made and concluded in the manner provided by law, but, if a majority of those voting on the question shall vote against such annexation, then no further proceedings for such annexation shall be had. Any such question shall not be submitted oftener than once in five years. Nothing contained in this act shall be construed to apply to proceedings under existing law where provision is now, or hereafter, made to secure the consent of the electors of any township to any such annexation.

Section 2. All acts and parts of acts inconsistent herewith are hereby repealed.

Section 3. This act shall become effective immediately upon its final enactment.

Act No. 588 provides for an election when ten per cent of the highest vote cast for any office in any township of the first class contiguous to a city or a borough, or ten per cent of the qualified electors in the portion proposed to be annexed, petitions the council of the city or borough. A referendum is then held on the question submitted. A majority of the persons voting on the question in the entire township and a majority of the persons voting in the city or borough is necessary to effect the annexation. The Act then provides for wards and election districts. The indebtedness of the township is adjusted, if not agreed upon, by commissioners. 

Article IV of the Borough Code of 1927 is repealed, together with other Acts inconsistent.

BUDGETS 

Budgets; Act No. 330, June 4, 1936, 28 Mun. 208, 1936

C. B. L. 73, 369. It will be recalled that at the Session of 1935 budget acts were passed for third class cities, first class townships, counties, school districts and poor districts. Boroughs and second class townships were eliminated. 

The present Act differs considerably from the 1935 Acts and we had much to do with the changes. However, the Bill is not the one we proposed. After we discovered that it was scheduled to go through as an Administration measure, our efforts were devoted to making the Bill as workable as possible. We are now undertaking to make its provisions useful by cooperating in the framing of the budget forms. 

The Act amends the Borough Code, Sections 1006, 1035 

and 1062 as follows:

Section 1. Be it enacted, &c., That section one thousand six of the act approved the fourth day of May, one thousand nine hundred twenty-seven (Pamphlet Laws five hundred nineteen), entitled "An act concerning boroughs; and revising, amending, and consolidating the law relating to boroughs", which was last amended by section four of the act approved the eighteenth day of July, one thousand nine hundred thirtyfive (Pamphlet Laws twelve hundred ninety), is hereby further amended to read as follows:

Section 1006. Duties of Council. It shall be the duty of the council, a majority of whom shall be a quorum: 

Note: Paragraphs 1 to 18 are omitted as they do not change the present law (C. B. L. 1936, pages 71 to 73). The act then continues as follows: 

XVIII. To adopt an annual budget. On or before the fifteenth day of January a proposed budget or annual estimate of revenues and expenditures shall be prepared in a manner designated by the council. The budget shall be prepared on a uniform form prepared and furnished as hereafter provided.

Upon the preparation of the proposed budget, the council shall give public notice of the fact that the proposed budget will be available for public inspection at the office of the borough secretary or such other place as may be designated in a public notice. After the expiration of fifteen (15) days following the giving of such public notice, council shall, after making such revisions therein as appear advisable, adopt the budget, the tax ordinance, and the necessary appropriation measures required to put the budget into effect. The total amount appropriated shall not exceed the receipts of the borough estimated as available for the fiscal year. 

In all boroughs the council shall within fifteen (15) days after the adoption of the budget file a copy of the same with the Department of Internal Affairs.

The Council shall have power to authorize by resolution the transfer of any unencumbered balance of an appropriation or any portion thereof from one purpose to another within the same fund, but transfers to cover lawful deficits and sur-

pluses may be made between the general borough fund and special funds. Such action shall not be taken during the first three months of the fiscal year.

The Council may during any fiscal year adopt by resolution supplementary appropriations, but any resolution authorizing such supplementary appropriations shall provide for unencumbered receipts sufficient to cover such appropriations. 

Payments shall not be authorized or made from the treasury except upon appropriation in the budget or supplementary appropriation resolutions. The council or other officers shall not hire any work to be done, purchase any material, make any contract, or issue any order for the payment of moneys by any agent of the borough which will cause the sums appropriated to specific purposes to be exceeded. 

Section 2. That section one thousand thirty-five of said act which was last amended by section 4 of the Act approved the eighteenth day of July, one thousand nine hundred thirtyfive (Pamphlet Laws one thousand ninety), is hereby further amended to read as follows:

Section 1035. Auditors to Meet Yearly and Prepare a Financial Statement. Financial Report to be Furnished to Department of Internal Affairs. The auditors of the borough shall meet on the third Tuesday of January of each year and shall audit, adjust, and settle the accounts of the tax collectors and all officers of the borough and of any person, committee, and commission receiving or expending municipal funds; and shall prepare a statement thereof which shall contain an audit of the accounts of the last fiscal year and shall also show a complete statement of the financial condition of the borough, giving in detail the actual indebtedness, the amount of the funded debt, the amount of the floating debt thereof, the valuation of taxable property therein, the assets of the borough with the character and value thereof, and the date of maturity of the respective forms of funded debt thereof. 

Such statement shall be prepared within sixty (60) days after the close of the fiscal year. Such statement shall be published by council as ordinances are published or by publication of a summary of such statement one time in one newspaper of 

---

general circulation and shall be in lieu of all other statements relating to indebtedness required to be published by existing law. The amount of any balance or shortage or of any expenditure of a kind, or made in a manner prohibited or not authorized by statute, which causes a financial loss to the borough, shall be a surcharge against any officer against whom such balance or shortage shall appear, or who by vote, act, or neglect has permitted or approved such expenditure.

After such statement has been prepared, it shall be the duty of the secretary of the borough to furnish to the Department of Internal Affairs a report of such statement transcribed on a uniform form prepared and furnished as hereafter provided. 

Such report shall be signed by the auditors and the secretary and shall be filed with the department within ninety (90) days after the close of the fiscal year. Any secretary refusing or wilfully neglecting to file such report shall, upon conviction in a summary proceeding brought at the instance of the Department of Internal Affairs, be sentenced to pay a fine of five dollars for each day's delay beyond April first and costs. If the failure to file such report within the period specified is due to the failure of the auditors to prepare the statement upon which said report is to be based, said fine shall be imposed upon the auditors and not upon the secretary. All fines recovered shall be for the use of the Commonwealth. 

The uniform forms for the annual budget and for the annual report to the Department of Internal Affairs to be used as herein provided shall be prepared by a committee consisting of four representatives from the Pennsylvania State Association of Boroughs and the secretary of the Department of Internal Affairs or his agent.

Such representatives shall be appointed by the president of the organization within sixty (60) days after the effective date of this act. Such representatives shall be chosen from among finance officers or other officers of boroughs who have knowledge of their fiscal procedures. As far as possible they shall be chosen to represent boroughs in the various population groups. The president of the organization shall supply to

the Department of Internal Affairs the names and addresses of such representatives immediately upon their appointment. 

Such representatives shall serve without compensation, but shall be reimbursed by the Commonwealth for all necessary expenses incurred in attending meetings of the committee. 

The committee shall meet at the call of the Secretary of Internal Affairs or his agent who shall serve as chairman of the committee.

In preparing the uniform forms for both budgets and annual reports, the committee shall give careful consideration to the fiscal needs and procedures of boroughs of the various population groups, producing separate forms, if necessary, to the end that such forms shall not be inconsistent with the general administrative practices of boroughs of various types and sizes.

It shall be the duty of the Secretary of Internal Affairs or his agent to see to it that the forms required by this act are prepared in cooperation with such committee. In the event that the committee should for any reason fail to furnish such cooperation, the Secretary of Internal Affairs or his agent shall prepare the forms. After their preparation, he shall issue such forms and distribute them annually as needed to the proper officers of each borough.

COMPENSATION 

Act No. 207, May 21, 1937, 28 Mun. 200, 1936 C.B.L. 109. 118,

Section 1101 is amended by striking out the provisions relating to the annual salary of secretary and police and fixing the amount thereof at the time of the appointment. The 

Section as amended now reads as follows: "Appointed officers of the borough shall receive such compensation for their services as the council shall prescribe."

Section 1128 is amended by striking out the provisions relating to the fixing of a salary by ordinance and extra compen sation. The Section as amended now reads as follows: "The borough police shall receive compensation to be fixed in the same manner as the compensation of other appointed borough officers is fixed."

Section 1101 and 1128 of the Code have long been subjects of controversy and some litigation. The subject is discussed in C. B. L. 1929, pages 149, 150, and C. B. L. 1936, page 106, etc. Originally Section 1101 included the words "solicitor". Prior to this amendment it was impossible to

arrange for flexible compensation to secretaries or police officers; the amount had to be fixed and it became necessary to pass an ordinance. The amendment to 1101 is as follows: "Compensation. Appointed officers of the borough shall receive such compensation for their services as the council shall prescribe." The remainder of the sentence is stricken out.

Section 1128 as now amended provides that the police shall receive compensation to be fixed in the same manner as the compensation of other appointed borough officers is fixed. 

Section 1006 VIII (C. B L. 71) now applies generally and includes officials under 1101 and 1128.

The Act of 1927, P. L. 992, has been a source of annoyance to boroughs. Several attempts were made to repeal it as to boroughs, inasmuch as it has already been repealed as to all other political subdivisions. The attempt was renewed at the 

1937 Session (recommended by the Borough Convention), but failed in the Senate after several Senators changed their vote under the impression that the Act would affect other classes of municipalities, being ignorant of the fact that it had already been repealed as to other political subdivisions, including school districts.

The Legislature imposes additional duties on the secretary (the new Budget Act, for example) and on police officers. Depressions and boom periods may change the proper status of compensation and the local authorities should be in position to make adjustments during the term. A fixed annual compensation is particularly objectionable in the case of policemen, inasmuch as strikes, floods and other catastrophes make it impossible for any council to predict in advance the number, the rate of compensation and the total budget item to properly take care of the police department. In some boroughs there are only one or two policemen, but a fixed hourly fee is provided for extra policemen. This arrangement could hardly be considered as complying with the provision that the police shall receive a stated salary fixed by ordinance. 

It is hoped that the amendment will have beneficial effect; nevertheless, the Act of 1927, P. L. 992, should be repealed.

Act No. 208, May 21, 28 Mun. 189, 1936 C. B. L. 146 to 151.

This Act undertakes to clarify the provisions of Section 1202, LIII of the Code, and the special Act of May 4, 1927, P. L. 

673. The whole subject is discussed at length in C. B. L. 

1929 edition, pages 186 to 189 and 470 to 481, and in C. B.

A councilman may not be foreman on a WPA job and approve bills beyond the limitations of this Act, and will be ousted for doing so. Com. v. Shellhimer, 31 Luz. 135 (1936).

Inasmuch as the Act is important and Clause (d) extends the amounts in which municipal officials may be interested from ten dollars a month to twenty-five dollars a month and from one hundred dollars a year to three hundred dollars a year, we therefore print the Act in its entirety, excluding the introductory paragraph.

LIII. Regulation of Contracts. To make contracts or pur chases in connection with proper legal requirements of the borough: Provided, That all contracts or purchases (other than purchases for water, electric light, and other public works of the borough, or for improvement made by its employees, or where the State or county is a party with the borough to a street improvement) made by any borough involving an expenditure of over five hundred dollars ($500), except those hereinafter particularly enumerated, shall be in writing, and shall not be made except with and from the lowest responsible bidder after due public notice by the secretary published once a week for three weeks at intervals of seven days in one newspaper published in such borough, and if no newspaper is published therein, then by publication in one newspaper printed in the county and circulating generally in the borough, and all bids shall be received, opened, and read in open meeting of council at a time fixed by such notice and not earlier than twenty-one days after the first publication thereof.

LIENS Municipal Liens, abatements, installments, Act No. 

84, April 14, 1937. Mun. 152, 1936, C. B. L. 333. This is an

Act which undertakes to extend to persons who have become delinquent in the payment of municipal claims the same opportunity to clear up the title to their properties as has been extended in the case of tax delinquencies. The Act (No. 84) may be considered a companion Act to Act No. 83. It was found in the discussion at the Borough Convention and in the Committee meetings of the House and Senate that in a number of municipalities liens for street improvements were imposed during the "boom" period, and that these liens, together with penalties and interest, had practically absorbed the entire value of certain lots of ground at their "depression" value. The lots were neglected, unimproved, and unmarketable. In order to at least partially relieve the situation this 

Act was framed to abate the penalties and interest (if the municipal authorities so decide) in order to relieve the financial distress brought about largely through economic conditions and not the neglect of the owners.

The Act provides that all interest charges, expenses and fees, up to and including the year 1935, arising out of service supplied, improvement made and work done or undertaken by any political subdivision, including paving, grading, sidewalks, water pipes, sewers and the opening, widening, vacating of streets, etc., that all such interest charges, expenses and fees be abated, if the delinquent claims are paid as herein provided. The foregoing enumeration is quite as extensive as the lien Act of 1923 itself.

Section one continues by providing that in order to receive the benefits of the Act, 20 per cent of the claims shall be paid on or before December 31, 1937, and 20 per cent each succeeding year until December 31, 1941.

It is to be noted that this Act differs radically from the Act No. 83 in that it is left optional with the political sub-division. 

If the property owner fails to meet any one installment, then he is penalized on his next installment to the extent of one-fifth of the abated interest charges, expenses and fees. If he fails to meet this additional sum, then all interest charges, expenses and fees are added, and the property owner loses all benefits and privileges under the Act. Under Section two the property owner may avoid installment payments by paying the entire amount on or before December 31, 1937.

The Act then contains provisions similar to those in Act No. 83, extending the Act to all cases, whether or not liens have been filed, but shall not apply where the property has been sold to a third party and the period of redemption has not expired. The Act further is extended to sheriff's sales. 

The taxpayer may clear up the title of one parcel of real estate without doing so for all parcels. The Act provides that the political subdivision shall supply in writing to any person desiring to know, the amount of the liens, interest charges, etc. 

Within thirty days after the acceptance of the provisions of this Act, and again during the last week in October, 1937, the corporate authorities are directed to publish a notice in a newspaper to the following effect:

Notice to Property Owners 

Under the provisions of Act No. 84, approved April 14, 1937, all persons owing any county, city, borough, town, township, school district, or poor district delinquent municipal claims for the year one thousand nine hundred and thirty-five and all previous years, may have certain of the interest, charges, expenses, and fees thereon abated by paying the amount or amounts due on such delinquent claims in five equal annual installments if the first twenty per centum installment is paid

on or before the thirty-first day of December, one thousand nine hundred and thirty-seven, or the entire amount or the balance due of such delinquent claims may be paid at face at any time prior to December thirty-first, one thousand nine hundred and thirty-seven. Further information may be obtained from the undersigned."

The Act further follows Act No. 83 by providing that no real property shall be sold for non-payment of municipal claims before December 31, 1937, and that sales may be adjourned and re-adjourned in order that the municipality and the taxpayer may take advantage of the Act.

In order to protect bond holders, Section seven provides as follows: 

"Section 7. The provisions of this act shall not apply to or in any manner affect any assessments which are the sole basis of improvement bonds issued by any political subdivision and which are the security for the payment thereof or to any assessments which have heretofore been assigned by any political subdivision to any contractor in payment of the amount due him under terms of the contract for the improvement for which the assessment was levied." 

This Act, like Act No. 83, became effective immediately upon enactment.

Liens, revival. Act of No. 272, May 28, 1937, 28 Mun. 206, 1936 C. B. L. 352. There is a distinction that is not fully understood by tax collectors and many attorneys between the revival of a tax lien and the revival of a municipal claim. 

The tax claim may be revived by a suggestion of non-payment and an averment of default, but in the case of municipal claims it was necessary that there should be a judgment on the scire facias. In many municipalities, claims were not sued out to judgment on the scire facias and were therefore lost, unless and until a validating act was passed. (Several of these are passed at every Session, including the 1937 session). 

This Act places tax claims and municipal claims on the same basis, so that a lien may be kept alive by a suggestion of nonpayment without the necessity of suing on the scire facias, either in the case of a tax claim or any other municipal claim.

This is accomplished by amending Section 15 of the Act of 

May 16, 1923, P. L. 207, so as to read as follows: "Section 15.

Such tax, municipal or other claim, if filed within the period aforesaid, shall remain a lien upon said properties until fully paid and satisfied: Provided, however, that either a suggestion of nonpayment and an averment of default in the form hereinafter provided be filed either before or after judgment on the scire facias, or else a writ of scire facias in the form herein provided be issued, etc.'

Negligence, Sidewalks, C. B. L. 350. The general principles involved in damage cases against municipalities have been fully established as set forth in the 1929 and 1936 editions. The standard duty of a municipality is the maintenance of sidewalks with reasonable care (Kuntz v. Pittsburgh, 123 Sup. 394). In Hanaman v. Philadelphia, 322 Pa. 635 it was held that a municipality is not generally liable for inadequate police protection, the case in point being a public park. 

To like effect is McGlinn v. Philadelphia 222 Pa. 478, wherein it is stated that the municipality is not the insurer of

absolute safety to pedestrians and not every depression or irregularity will render the municipality liable. 

Negligence, Swimming pool, parks, etc. In McAllister v. Homestead, 322 Pa. 341, it was held that the borough was not liable for the drowning of a boy who fell from a float in a swimming pool maintained by the borough where the evidence did not support the finding of an implied invitation to use the float when the guard was not present.

In Paraska v. Scranton, 262 Pa. 1, it was held that the municipality is liable for injuries sustained by a child falling from a swing in a public park, the negligence being the failure of the city to remove stones under the swing. 

Firemen, liability, 28 Mun. 350, C. B. L. 377. Dirk v.

Zerbe Township, 322 Pa. 350. (affirming case in C. B. L. page 

377). It was held that under the Vehicle Code, in the language of the Code prevailing at the time, a municipality is not liable for damages resulting from negligence of a fire company on parade and not in performance of its proper duties. 

See Act No. 323 June 4, 1937 which adds "or while performing any other duties of such companies."

The city failed to exercise its powers in the collection of its assessment bonds and in keeping them alive. Negligence by the city in enforcing collection resulted in its becoming liable generally under its general taxing power.

PRACTICE AND PROCEDURE 

Appointments. The appointment of borough officers and employees is by no means standard in practice. 

This results from the great difference in the size of boroughs and also to the fact that certain officials are mentioned by statute. There are statutory references to solicitor, supervisor, manager, and others. But there is little reference to engineer and no reference to architect. Practically every large borough has an engineer on part time service. There are thus established certain offices from year to year, although none of these offices has been created by ordinance. 

The question then arises as to whether or not a borough may employ engineers for a particular purpose without creating the office, and without ordaining the particular purpose by an ordinance in which the employment of the engineer is involved. The question was raised in Nuebling v. Topton Borough at 323 Pa. 154. It was held that an express contract for employment beyond special service should be specifically authorized. An express contract being relied upon, no recovery may be had on a "quantum meruit." "The making of such a contract is a legislative act which can be only by appropriate ordinance or resolution with the concurrence of the burgess: Act of May 4, 1927, P. L. 519,

Article X, Section 1007." 

The import of this decision is far reaching. To us it appears to depart from the established principles and practice. 

In general, we consider that Section 1006 contained the duties of council and that Clause VII gave to council the power to appoint "such other officers as it shall deem necessary.' A borough engineer would properly be classified among "other officers." 

We do not think this decision means that council may not appoint an engineer without the approval of the burgess through the medium of an ordinance or resolution. The decision should be read in the light of the facts, and the important distinction between an express contract (relied upon) and the recovery under a "quantum meruit." If the borough had in

practice employed an engineer, appointed by council, we are of the opinion that the engineer could recover for services rendered. However, if the borough embarked on a new project and undertook to employ an engineer under an express contract, that, according to this decision, would be embarking upon a new governmental function, calling for deliberation, and therefore legislative and not ministerial in character. 

Consequently it would require the formalities of an ordinance. 

The distinction is fine and careful analysis should be observed. 

In our publications and forms we have always recommended that there be some formal establishment of offices, or formal recognition of offices already established, so that appointments to these offices need not be regulated by ordinance. This may be done by means of the budget ordinance, or by means of the tax ordinance in which there may be embodied essential fiscal matters, such as the fixing of bonds, the creation of offices, etc. See in this connection 27 Mun. bottom of page 

6a and the expression at the top of page 9a, as follows: “All the aforesaid offices, not heretofore established are hereby established," meaning solicitor, engineer, etc.

The discharge of sewage or industrial waste or any noxious and deleterious substances into the waters of this Commonwealth, which is or may become inimical and injurious to the public health or to the animal or aquatic life or to the uses of such waters for domestic or industrial consumption or for recreation, is hereby declared not

Secretary of Health. 

Section 203 provides for orders to discontinue sewage discharges, and declares the continued discharge to be nuisances. 

Section 204 provides penalties, up to one hundred dollars for each offense and up to ten dollars for each day the offense is maintained, and provides imprisonment, for the officers responsible, of a day in jail for each dollar of fine and costs.

Section 205 provides for reports on existing municipal sewers; and if the report is not filed, the system is declared to be a nuisance.

Section 206 relates to the application for permits for discharge of sewage.

Section 207 provides for the approval of plans, designs, and relevant data by the Sanitary Water Board. 

Section 208 relates to the revocation and modification of permits.

Section 209 provides discharge of sewage after a permit is revoked.

Abatement, installments, Act No. 83, April 14, 1937, 28 Mun. 147, C. B. L. 333, etc. This Act is intended to relieve taxables who failed to take advantage of prior legislation, either through ignorance or disinclination, by permitting them to pay their delinquent taxes on practically the same basis as those who took advantage of prior acts, but without giving them any advantage over those who acted under previous legislation. Whether or not this will work out in practice is difficult to determine, inasmuch as the Act was variously amended in course of passage. The discussions in which we participated were all directed to the end that delinquents who failed to make use of prior legislation be now again encouraged to get rid of all delinquent taxes (with penalties and interest), but that the abatements should be so circumscribed that these delinquents shall not be better off than those who did take advantage of prior legislation; and, further, that the Act be so framed that delinquents may not consider it a legislative policy to abate penalties and interest Session after Session.

The Act is mandatory. For the year 1934 and previous years penalties and interest both are abated; for the years 

1935 and 1936 only penalties are abated. The abatement is mandatory without any action by the taxing authorities, provided delinquent taxes are paid as herein specified. The interest on taxes for 1935 and 1936 continues, but no further interest is imposed after the first installment is paid; provided, of course, the taxpayer further complies with the provisions of the Act.

To receive the benefits of the installment system, 20 per cent of delinquent taxes for 1936 and all previous years (in-

cluding the interest on the 1935 and 1936 taxes), must be paid by November 1, 1937, other 20 per cent installments November 1, 1938, November 1, 1939, November 1, 1940, and the final 20 per cent November 1, 1941. These delinquents are not permitted to let their current taxes remain unpaid during the period from 1937 on, while installments are being paid, and the entire amount of the current taxes must be paid before they become delinquent; that is, before the penalty attaches. If the current taxes become delinquent, one-fifth thereof is added, with penalties and interest, and must be paid at the time the next succeeding installment becomes due. 

If this additional fund is not paid, it is added to the remaining unpaid taxes, with the same force and effect as if such abated penalties and interest had never been abated, and the taxpayer is no longer entitled to the benefits and privileges of the Act.

The second section of the act provides that any taxpayer desiring to clear up all delinquent taxes may do so on or before November 1, 1937 by paying the entire a nount of delinquent taxes, together with interest on the 1935 and 1936 taxes. This may be done without paying the 1937 taxes. 

In Section 3 it is provided that the Act shall apply whether or not liens have been filed, and also to cases where property has been sold for taxes and the period of redemption has not expired. The taxpayer is liable for costs incurred (except solicitor's fees) where liens have been returned for non-payment or filed in the prothonotary's office. 

Section four extends the benefits to successful bidders at sales.

Section five permits a taxpayer to clear up one parcel of land without the necessity of clearing up all parcels assessed against him.

In order that the provisions of this Act may be generally known to delinquent taxpayers, it is provided that the county commissioners cause to be published a synopsis of the Act, and collectors of taxes are directed to enclose with the usual tax notice an additional notice of the date when the next installment is due under this Act.

The Act then provides for adjournment and readjournment of tax sales, in order to permit certain taxpayers to come within the provisions of the Act.

It is further provided that the Act of 1935, P. L. 129 (a nended June 22, 1935, P. L. 444) and the Act of June 4, 1936, P.

L. 10, be continued in force as to any and all taxpayers who have taken advantage of and are in default with the provisions of those acts.

Finally, the Act provides that those taxpayers who undertook to comply with prior legislation, but failed to do so, may nevertheless come within the provisions of this Act. 

This Act is effective immediately upon enactment. 

In this connection, see Act of June 4, 1936, P. L. 10; P. L. 

330, etc., and similar abatement Acts of 1935.

NOTE: The proposed amendment to the Code permitting speed to be tested in boroughs by means of a speedometer was defeated through the influence of the Bureau of Motor Vehicles. 

It was claimed that this form of test might be abused and is difficult in operation. This statement purported to come from the State Highway Patrol. Under rulings of the court, speed in built up districts can be determined only on a measured stretch with two policemen a practical impossibility in most boroughs of the state. No objection to testing by speedometer was raised by any Bureau prior to the Superior Court's decision on the matter. No good reason has yet been shown why speed should not be tested through a speedometer. If the Bureau desires to eliminate speeding and accidents, instead of talking about it, it should permit the use of practical

means. The automobile industry and the World at large depends on speedomenters and similar mechanical devices; why should criminals alone be exempt?

Another amendment was proposed that fines for reckless driving accrue to the municipality. The Bureau also defeated this amendment, as it did not want to lose the revenues for the State. It is inconceivable that the Bureau would prohibit boroughs from arresting for speeding in order that they might arrest for reckless driving and thus have the fines go to the state instead of to the borough. Whatever the motive, the 

Bureau has not satisfactorily explained its position in regard to protecting the public against speedsters. Apparently it is more interested in fines that accrue to the State than it is in practical results.
