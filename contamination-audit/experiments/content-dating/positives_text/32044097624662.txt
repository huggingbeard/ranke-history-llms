PSYCHOLOGICAL SERVICE What a Psychologist Does 

-A psychologist is a person who is interested in the study of behavior. By "behavior" he refers to all the things that people do. Walking, talking, growing, learning, working, playing are examples of forms of behavior. The psychologist is interested in persons, in what they do, why they do, and in what they may be expected to do in the future. The characteristic behavior of a particular person may be called his personality. It is the job of the psychologist to describe personality as accurately as possible, and on the basis of the description, to predict certain aspects of future behavior. Specialized knowledge and particular tools, called psychological tests, have been developed for use in making judgments about personality. Each psychologist has at his disposal certain knowledge and skill in using certain tools or tests. This is the "equipment" he uses in giving descriptions and making predictions. 

It is not the function of the psychologist to decide what is to be done in any particular case. 

That is the social work job. The psychologist can furnish information which may enable worker, teacher, or parent to proceed more wisely. Certain actions aro rather definitely indicated by certain facts as, for example, storilization is indicated in definito fceblemindedness. When this is truc the psychologist may make recommendations.
