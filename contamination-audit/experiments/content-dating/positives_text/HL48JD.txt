Foreword 

We can be proud of our heritage in Kansas and the fact that its founders wrote into the Constitution of the State, the concept of governmental concern for "those individuals who by reason of age, infirmity or other misfortune may have claims upon the sympathy and aid of society.” 

This has been a mandate to the citizens of Kansas through the years resulting eventually in a statute which required the licensing of homes offering care to the aged, ill and infirm. The purpose of a licensing program is to secure for such citizens of Kansas the kind of homes which provide an environment which contributes to their well being and assists them in experiencing more satisfying lives.

The Kansas Legislature in the 1959 session amended the licensing statute making it necessary to effect a revision of the regulations. Recognition is given to the State Fire Marshal Department and the State Board of Health for consultation and help in the revision of these regulations. 

Sincere appreciation is also extended to the members of the Advisory Committee to the Division of Services for the Aging for their help in establishing these revised regulations. 

A license issued by the State Department of Social Welfare is certification that a home meets regulations and the administrator of the home has agreed to provide protection and an environment which contributes to the happiness and well being of the residents. 

MARVIN E. LARSON State Director of Social Welfare.

Introduction LEGAL AUTHORITY 

The 1951 Kansas Legislature passed a licensing law, Chapter 39, Article 9, 1951 Supplement which became effective July 1, 1951. Sections 39-901 to 

39-922 of the General Statutes Supplement of 1957 was amended by Chapter 

206 of the 1959 Session Laws. This amendment became effective July 1, 1959. 

This law placed legal responsibility for the licensing of nursing homes, homes for aged, and boarding homes with the State . 

The law provides for the inspections, investigations and recommendations of homes by the State Board of Health, State Fire Marshal, and the County Welfare Department to determine the conditions existing in each case, and a written report filed with the State Department of Social Welfare which is the licensing agency. (A complete copy of the licensing law may be found in 

Appendix A of this book.) All homes subject to licensing under Chapter 39, Article 9, 1959 Supplement shall comply with all regulations. The word "shall" is used in these regulations which means each regulation is a requirement. 

In accordance with the authority granted by this Law the State Board of Social Welfare herewith adopts the following regulations for the conduct and supervision of boarding homes, nursing homes, and homes for the aged. A copy of said regulations has been filed with the Revisor of Statutes which gives them the effect of Law.

DEFINITIONS Licensing agency shall mean the State Department of Social Welfare. 

Regulations shall mean requirements approved by the State Board of Social Welfare relating to the care, treatment, health, safety, welfare and comfort of the residents living in homes. 

A boarding home shall mean a home licensed to provide care for three or more residents needing personal care.

Nursing care shall mean professional nursing or practical nursing for sick or injured persons who are under the care and supervision of licensed medical practitioners.

Personal care shall mean general oversight of the physical well being of the residents in the home exclusive of nursing care. 

Existing home shall mean a home currently licensed. 

New home shall mean a home never having been duly licensed. 

Reopened home shall mean a licensed home whose license has expired, been revoked or canceled and a new application for a license has been filed. 

Remodeled home shall mean a home duly licensed and in which a structural change has been or is being made. 

Newly constructed home shall mean a home for which plans have been properly developed and a building erected from new or reconditioned material. (7)

Change of ownership shall mean the legal transfer of title to a home from one administrator to another administrator.

Annex shall mean a subsidiary building to the main building located on the same premises having the same address as the main building. 

Administrator shall mean the person who is responsible for the operation of the home. If the administrator is not the licensee it shall mean the person who has been employed by the licensee to have the responsibility of the operation of the home.

Licensee shall mean any individual or other business entity to whom the license is issued.

Resident shall mean all individuals kept, cared for, treated, boarded or otherwise accommodated in any boarding home, nursing home, or home for the aged.

Self-care resident shall mean one who is able to walk about (ambulant) and to care for personal needs and who requires little care and/or supervision. 

Incapacitated resident shall mean: a. Custodial-ambulatory resident is one who is able to walk about and who requires much care and/or supervision. b. Semi-ambulatory resident is one who may be in a wheel chair or spend part of the time in bed and who requires care and/or supervision. c. Bedfast resident is one who requires complete bedside care. 

Approved automatic fire detection system shall mean one tested in accordance with nationally recognized standards and shall carry the label of Underwriter's Laboratory.

Approved sprinkler system shall mean one installed in accordance with nationally recognized standards for installation of sprinkler systems and shall carry the label of Underwriter's Laboratory. 

Application for License LICENSING PROCEDURES 

Application for a license to operate a home shall be made in writing on Form DSW 142, currently revised, supplied by the State Department of Social Welfare before opening or operating a home. 

The application for a license is completed in triplicate, one copy of which remains with the county department of social welfare, one copy may be retained by the administrator and one copy sent to the Director of the Division of Services for the Aging, State Department of Social Welfare, accompanied by the proper licensing fee. A detailed floor plan of the home, prepared according to DSW 149, shall accompany each application. A new floor plan is necessary if there has been change of ownership, remodeling, rearrangement of rooms or in the event a home has been reopened. A copy of menus for one week shall also accompany the application, and Form DSW 142 A, Certificate of Health 

Examination for the administrator.

A new application is required annually at expiration of license, or in the event there is a change in ownership of the home. 

An "amended" application is required in the event of a change in name of the administrator, or change in capacity of the home.

1 

Fee for License

A fee for license is required by Law and shall be paid to the State  before a license is issued. The fee is two dollars ($2.00) for a capacity for not more than five residents; three dollars ($3.00) for a capacity for six to eight residents; and ten dollars ($10.00) for a capacity for nine or more residents.

Issuance of License 

The license to operate a home is issued by the State Department of Social Welfare. The license is issued only for the premises and administrator named in the application and is not transferable. 

The written reports of the State Board of Health, State Fire Marshal and County Departments of Social Welfare shall be on file with the State Department of Social Welfare before a new license or a renewal license can be issued. 

Such reports must establish that the administrator is capable of operating the home and the home meets the regulations. 

A provisional license may be issued for a new home which is temporarily unable to conform to all regulations. A provisional license may be issued for more than one year at the discretion of the licensing agency. 

Revocation of License 

The licensing agency is authorized to deny, suspend or revoke a license in any case in which it finds that there has been a failure to comply with regu lations. 

Fair Hearing 

The administrator or licensee shall be given opportunity for a prompt hearing before the State Appeals Committee following an Order to show cause why a license should not be revoked, be suspended or denied. The hearing shall be held more than fifteen (15.) days past the date of the Order. 

Penalty for Violation

Any person establishing, conducting, managing or operating a home without a license under this Law shall be guilty of a misdemeanor and upon conviction shall be punished by a fine of not more than one hundred dollars ($100) or by imprisonment in the county jail for a period of not more than six (6) months, or by both such fine and imprisonment.

CHAPTER I General Regulations and Social, Economic and Nutritional Aspects Item A. General 

1. Any person contemplating the operation or purchase of a home or seeking a new location shall show on his application that clearance has been made with all city ordinances related to the use of buildings for such purpose. 

2. The licensee or administrator or any staff member of a home shall not serve as guardian of a person or estate for residents in the licensed home operated by the licensee or administrator. . 

3. Homes coming under the provisions of these regulations shall be open at all times for inspection and supervision by authorized representatives of the State , State Fire Marshal, State Board of Health, the county welfare department and co-operating local authorities such as fire marshal and health department. The licensee or administrator has the right to request credentials prior to any inspection. 

4. All required records maintained by the home shall be open to inspection by the authorized representatives of the State Department of Social Welfare, the State Board of Health, the county welfare department and nursing services of the State and County Departments of Health. 

5. Plan for new construction or any additions or alterations in existing homes shall be furnished the licensing agency. A written program which proposes function shall accompany the plan. 

These plans shall be prepared, by a qualified person, in such manner that they are readily understandable and shall include details concerning construction, arrangement and materials. 

New construction, additions or alterations shall not be started until approval has been given in writing by the licensing agency. 

Such plans shall also be approved by the local building inspector. 

6. New homes, including new construction, remodeled buildings and additions to existing homes shall have a minimum floor area per bed as follows, provided, however, the licensing agency in consultation with the co-operating agencies may, in the instance of an existing building of fire resistant material constructed prior to January 1, 1955, for the purpose, approve a lesser floor area. 

Single rooms 

Rooms for 2 persons 

Rooms for 3 or more persons 

90 sq. ft. per bed .80 sq. ft. per bed .70 sq. ft. per bed

Homes licensed prior to January 1, 1955, and continuously since shall have a minimum floor area in rooms for residents of 60 sq. ft. per bed. 

Existing homes shall not house more than six residents in a ward and all others not more than four residents in a ward. 

Bedrooms in newly constructed, remodeled buildings, additions to existing homes, and reopened homes shall open only on a corridor, living (11)

room or dining room. Rooms used as passageways to other rooms or exits shall not be used as bedrooms. 

7. Plans and specifications for a new private water supply system or an addition to an existing private water supply system shall be furnished the licensing agency, State Department of Social Welfare, for the purpose of determining if such system complies with standards. Such plan shall be subject to the approval of the State Board of Health. 

Construction shall not be started until approval has been given in writing by the licensing agency. 

8. Plans and specifications for a new private sewage disposal system or an addition to an existing system shall be furnished the licensing agency, State Department of Social Welfare, for the purpose of determining if such system complies with standards. Such plan shall be subject to the approval of the State Board of Health. 

Construction shall not be started until approval has been given in writing by the licensing agency. 

9. In each home all residents shall be humanely treated and suitably provided with whatever may seem necessary for their safety, comfort, well being and nutritional needs. The licensee or administrator is responsible for planning with the resident, relatives, guardian or the county department of social welfare for medical needs as recommended by the attending physician. 

10. The term "hospital" shall not be used as a part of the name of a home. 

11. A home shall not receive as residents children who are under the age of 

16 years. 

12. No person having an acute communicable disease shall be admitted to a home. 

13. Boarding homes shall not admit, nor keep, any resident requiring nursing care except for a temporary illness. Residents requiring continued nursing care shall be transferred out of the home promptly on order of the attending physician. 

14. Each licensee or administrator shall submit a monthly report to the State 

Department of Social Welfare by the 5th of the following month on Form DSW 145. This report shall include all residents. 

15. Each resident upon admittance shall have the policies of the home carefully and clearly explained to him, to his relatives or to his guardian. 

16. No rules shall be adopted by the licensee or administrator of a home which are in conflict with these regulations. 

17. The current license being in full force and effect shall be posted in a conspicuous location within view of all visitors. 

18. An administrator of a home shall not display a license which has been revoked and such license shall be returned to the State Department of 

Social Welfare. 

19. Each license shall be issued only for the premises and persons named on the application as administrators. This license shall not be transferable. 

20. Residents shall not be housed in unapproved rooms or unapproved detached buildings. 

21. The State Department of Social Welfare and the local county welfare department shall be notified immediately by the administrator or licensee of any emergency, or accident, minor or fatal, occurring in his home.

22. All persons desiring to operate more than one home shall employ an administrator for each home to have full responsibility for the operation of said home.

Item B. Administrator 

1. The administrator shall be a responsible person of good character and reputation. 

2. Before a renewal license is issued, the administrator shall present evidence of attendance at an educational program recognized and approved by the licensing agency. 3. 4.

The administrator shall be a person capable of keeping all required records. 

The administrator and all persons in other responsible positions shall be 

18 years of age or older. 

5. The administrator shall give full time to the management of the home and shall not leave the premises day or night without delegating responsibility to a competent person 18 years of age or older. 

6. The administrator of a currently licensed home shall not engage in any other business or occupation. 

7. The administrator shall have a health examination at the time of application and annually thereafter which shall establish, at the time of examination, physical and mental ability to operate the home and freedom from communicable and mental disease. Form DSW 142 A, Revised, shall be completed and signed by a licensed physician at the time of application and at the time of renewal of license, and shall be attached to the application. 

0 Item C. Personnel 

2 70 1. A staff of persons sufficient in number and of sufficient ability to meet the residents' requirements for care shall be employed. 

In addition to the staff engaged in the direct care and treatment of the residents, there shall be sufficient personnel to provide basic services such as food service, housekeeping, laundry, and plant maintenance. 

In scheduling the hours of work for each female employee, it is important that the administrator be familiar with and comply with 44-639 to 

44-650 of the 1949 General Statutes. 

2. The licensee or administrator shall not employ personnel until careful inquiry into and/or reasonable assurance of good character, training, previous experience and other qualifications. 

3. The persons employed shall be fully informed of the policies of the home in the performance of his duties. 

4. All persons having responsibility for the nursing care of residents shall be 18 years of age or older. No employee shall be less than 16 years of age. 

5. All persons working in the home shall annually submit a report of health examination which shall establish, at the time of examination, physical and mental ability to perform the work assigned and freedom from communicable and mental disease. 

Each new employee shall have a physical examination before employment in the home begins. In cases of emergency, where the needed personal care of a resident would suffer without the immediate employment

of personnel, those persons employed on an emergency basis must have the physical examination completed within thirty days. Employment shall be contingent on compliance with this requirement. 

Report of the health examination shall be made on Form DSW 142 A and shall be on file in the office of the administrator. 

Item D. Physical Facilities 

1. The home shall be so arranged, constructed, and maintained to provide for the health, comfort, and safety for all residents and shall meet the regulations in Chapters III and IV. 

2. The home shall be located on an all-weather road. 

3. There shall be adequate equipment according to the needs of the residents in the home. 

4. There shall be a recreation room or living room and dining room, provided, however, the licensing agency in consultation with the co-operating agencies may give approval for the two to be combined. In a small home, if a kitchen is to be utilized for dining purposes, there shall be sufficient area to accommodate the residents and not interfere with food preparation. 

5. The recreation room, living room, dining room, kitchen, corridors or hallway shall not be used for sleeping purposes at any time. 

6. There shall be a telephone in the home which is available for use by residents.

Item E. Meal Service and Menus 

1. There shall be served to each resident in the home a sufficient supply of good food, properly prepared, to provide an adequate diet based on the nutritional standards as recommended in the National Food Guide (issued by the Bureau of Human Nutrition and Home Economics of the United 

States Department of Agriculture, Leaflet # 288). 

2. The National Food Guide which shows the kinds and quantities of food required for each individual each day provides for the following servings: a. Leafy, green, and yellow vegetables: One or more servings daily. b. Oranges, grapefruit, tomatoes, green peppers, raw cabbage, and other high vitamin C foods: One or more servings daily. 3. c. Potatoes and other vegetables and fruit: Three or more servings daily. d. Milk, cheese, ice cream: Three cups daily. e. Meat, poultry, fish: Two servings daily, one should be meat, fish, or poultry, and the other could be one of the following: 

Eggs: A minimum of four per week, but one a day preferred. 

Dried beans, peas, nuts, peanut butter: Two or more servings a week. f. Bread and cereals (whole-grain or enriched): Four servings daily. g. Butter and fortified margarine: Some daily.

Breakfast Dinner Supper 

7:30 to 8:30 a. m.

5:00 to 6:00 p.m.

There shall be at least three meals served each day with supplementary feedings as necessary. Ample time shall be given for meals as they pro-

1 vide not only nourishment, but social contacts desirable for persons restricted in activity. 

4. The main meal shall be served at noon; the substantial dish for the evening meal shall consist completely or at least partially of protein. 

5. Menus shall be planned at least one week in advance of serving. 

6. Menus showing the foods to be served each day during the current week shall be posted where seen readily at all times as food is prepared and served. Each food plan shall show the date of the week it was actually used, and shall be kept on file for ninety (90) days. Food plans older than ninety (90) days may be destroyed, but it is believed desirable to keep them as a source of reference for a period of a year. 

7. There shall be reasonable adjustments to the personal tastes, desires and condition of residents in the kinds and preparation of the recommended foods. 

8. All special diets prescribed by a physician, licensed to practice in Kansas, shall be kept on file and posted where seen readily at all times as food is prepared and served. 

9. Meals shall be served at tables in the dining room to residents because there is value in meeting as a group. Tray service for residents temporarily ill or bedfast shall be attractive and provision made to serve hot foods hot and cold foods cold. The residents whose food habits are untidy may be served a tray or on an individual table in his room. 

Under no circumstances shall a resident be made to partake of his food in his room should he be capable of getting to the dining room area either by foot or by wheel chair. Again, unless his condition or habits are such that eating in the room is warranted, the resident shall eat all meals in the dining room. 

10. Adequate drinking water shall be offered frequently to bedfast residents and shall be available to all residents at all times. 

11. There shall be evidence of effort to maintain good nutrition, including evidence of adequate understanding of and preparation of special diets, if ordered by a physician. 

12. Residents who require assistance with eating shall be served promptly at times not to conflict with regular dining hours. 

The State , upon request, offers help in meal planning.

Item F. Provisions for Care of Residents 

1. Residents shall be given kind and considerate care and treatment at all times. 

2. Boarding homes licensed to care for six or more residents shall provide adequate personnel dressed, awake, and on duty 24 hours each day. 

3. Facilities shall insure privacy and separation of sexes in sleeping arrangements, except in cases of husband and wife. 

4. Privacy shall be available at all times to residents for interviews with the social worker, visits with family, friends, clergy and professional or for business reasons. 

5. Regular daily visiting hours shall be established for family and friends. 

Members of the clergy shall be admitted at all hours.

6. Relatives or guardians shall be allowed to see critically ill residents at all times. 

7. There shall be a storage space for clothing and personal belongings of each resident. The storage space shall be divided into separate compartments for each resident. 

8. All residents shall be suitably and adequately supplied with clothing. The administrator shall have responsibility for planning with the relatives, guardian, or county department of social welfare for clothing. 

9. Clothing of residents shall be kept clean, ironed, marked and mended and shall be available to them. 

10. Incoming mail addressed to the resident or outgoing mail addressed from the resident shall remain intact and unopened and shall be dispatched with a minimum of delay. 

11. Residents shall be provided with the opportunity for recreational activities suitable to their interests and abilities. 

12. Consideration shall be given to the spiritual needs of the individual resident. The opportunity for participation in religious services shall be provided. 

13. The administrator shall plan with relatives, guardian, or county welfare department for medical care of residents. 

14. Other personal services commensurate with the needs of the residents shall be provided.

CHAPTER II Nursing Care Item A. Provision and Maintenance of Nursing Equipment 

1. Each resident shall have an individual stationary bed (except in the case of husband and wife), preferably metal, not less than 36" wide, which is equipped with good springs that are so constructed that they will not sag; clean, comfortable mattresses, at least 4 inches thick, and pillows. 

The height or any other variation shall be determined according to the requirements of the individual residents. Roll-away type beds, day beds, and cots shall not be used for residents. 

2. Beds shall be supplied with moistureproof mattress covers or moistureproof draw sheets and bed pads, when necessary. 

3. There shall be available at all times a complete change of clean bed linen for each bed. 

4. Each bed shall be supplied with clean sheets large enough to tuck under the mattress, draw sheets as needed, pillow slips, bed spreads, and sufficient light weight blankets to insure warmth. 

5. Sheets and other bed linen shall be changed as often as necessary to provide for personal cleanliness. The minimum shall be at least once weekly. 

Mattresses shall be turned weekly. 

6. Beds, mattresses, pillows and bedding shall be cleaned and aired with each change of residents. Mattresses and pillows, which have been damaged, shall be renovated or destroyed. 

7. Household cleaning equipment shall not be kept in the same area with resident clothing, nursing supplies and equipment, bathroom or employees' restroom. 

8. Convenient facilities for resident handwashing, as well as for employees, shall be available on each floor. Soap and fresh linen or paper towels shall be available at all times. A common towel shall not be used. 

9. Individual towels and washcloths shall be provided for each resident, and shall be changed as necessary. The minimum shall be at least twice weekly. 

10. There shall be, for each resident, enclosed shelves or drawers, which shall be equipped with the following articles:

Towels. 

Washcloth. 

Soap dish. Pitcher.

Item C. Medicines and Drugs 

1. No stock supplies of medicines and drugs (including sedatives, hypnotics and narcotics), other than household remedies, shall be kept. 

2. Each order for drugs or medicines shall be limited to the prescription of a duly licensed practitioner authorized by law to prescribe and dispense medicines in the State of Kansas. 

3. Medicines and drugs shall be given only to the individual resident for whom the prescription was issued, shall be given in accordance with the directions on the prescription, shall be dispensed only by the administrator or by a member of the nursing personnel named by the administrator as responsible and shall be recorded on the nursing record. 

4. Every container of medicine and drugs, dispensed by a physician or on prescription, shall be properly and clearly labelled with the following information: 

Name of the resident. 

Date. 

Name of the physician. 

Directions for use. 

Prescription number. 

5. All medicines and drugs shall be kept in a locked medicine cabinet. The key shall be in possession of the individual in charge. 

6. Individual prescription shall be sent with the resident when he leaves, or destroyed when he dies, unless otherwise ordered by the physician. (Alternate returned to doctor or pharmacist when the resident dies.)

ED 7. Changes of labels, or of medicines and drugs from one container to another, shall be done only by the physician or by a registered pharmacist. 

8. Prescription medicines and drugs not currently in use shall be destroyed. 

9. Medicines with expiration dates shall be destroyed in conformity with such dates. 

10. Insulin, certain vitamin preparations, and other special medicines which are subject to deterioration shall be kept in a refrigerator, at 45 degrees Fahrenheit. 

11. All disinfectants, bleaches, rubbing alcohol, and poisons shall be kept locked, separate and apart from drugs, medicines, and food. 

12. Hypodermic syringes and needles, medicine droppers, and similar items shall be sterilized immediately before each use, except when rendered sterile by individual packaging and autoclaving at proper temperature.

Regular notes, according to the condition of the resident. 

Notations in records shall be signed with the full name of the person writing the note.

Boarding homes shall be considered as meeting this regulation by a monthly summary of observations of each resident.

Item F. Water Supply 

1. Public or municipal water supply shall be used wherever available. 

2. Homes located beyond the limits where water supply from a public distribution system would not be available a reserve supply of water of not less than 3,000 gallons shall be available. A water pump driven by an internal combustion engine capable of providing sufficient pressure shall be available and connected to the water supply. 

3. In areas where a public or municipal water supply is not available, a private water supply shall be provided and it shall meet the following requirements of the Division of Sanitation of the State Board of Health: a. The water supply source shall consist of a properly developed well or spring. Cistern water supplies shall not be connected with the house water supply system. b. The well or spring location shall be such that it will not be subject to drainage or seepage from livestock feed lots, barnyards, privies, septic tanks, sewers, or other similar sources of contamination. 

A well or spring shall be located at least 100 feet horizontally from sources of contamination as indicated above. c. The ground surrounding the top of the well or around the spring house shall be graded so as to divert surface drainage away in all directions. d. A well shall have a water-tight wall to minimum depth of 12 feet to exclude shallow ground water. e. The top of the well shall be surrounded by a reinforced concrete slab at least 4 feet square. Any openings through the well slab shall be sealed liquid tight. f. A spring house shall be so constructed as to exclude the entrance of surface water, rodents, animals, birds, and insects. g. A pit around the top of a well will not be acceptable. h. Any underground reservoir that is a part of a private water system shall be water tight and any openings into the reservoir shall be covered or sealed in a manner similar to that required for a well or spring. i. Any elevated storage tank that is a part of a private water system shall be covered to exclude birds and insects. j. Pumps shall be so constructed and installed to prevent the entrance of contamination into the water supply around packing glands, suction lines, and discharge lines.

Item H. Food Handling Sanitation 

1. The floors in all rooms in which food or drink is stored, prepared, or served, or in which utensils are washed, shall be of such construction as to be easily cleaned, shall be smooth, and shall be kept clean and in good repair. (See Item B, numbers 1 and 2, page 24.) 

2. Walls and ceilings of kitchens and dining rooms shall be kept clean and in good repair. The walls of all rooms in which food or drink is prepared or utensils are washed shall have a smooth, washable surface up to the level reached by splash or spray. 

3. All rooms in which food or drink is stored or prepared or in which utensils are washed shall be well lighted to promote cleanliness and shall be well ventilated. 

4. Hot and cold running water under pressure shall be available in all rooms in which food and drink is prepared or utensils are washed, and the water supply shall be adequate, and shall meet the requirements as hereinbefore set forth. 

5. Soap and single service or disposable towels shall be provided at a lavatory designated for employee handwashing. The use of a common towel shall be prohibited. No employee shall resume work after using the toilet room without first washing his hands. 

6. All counters, shelves, tables, refrigerators, sinks, and other utensils and equipment used in the storage or preparation of food and drink shall be so constructed as to be easily cleaned and shall be kept clean and in good repair.

8. Dishes and utensils shall be washed, rinsed, and effectively disinfected after each usage. 

9. Clean dishes and utensils shall be stored in a clean, dry, and enclosed cabinet which is protected from flies, dust, overhead leakage, and other contamination. Dishes and utensils shall be handled in a manner that will prevent contamination as far as practicable.

Item A. General Requirements CHAPTER IV Fire Safety 

1. A building used as a home shall be structurally sound, in good repair and so arranged and maintained that a reasonable degree of safety will be provided for all residents occupying each floor of the structure. a. All walls, partitions and ceilings shall be constructed of a material that shall provide a fire resistive rating equivalent to lath and plaster, or plasterboard wall. b. FIBERBOARD shall not be used. Fiberboard commonly called wall board is a material used in forming walls, ceilings, and partitions, composed mainly of combustible fiber or material pressed or fabricated into boards or panels. 

2. All homes of two-story frame structure offering care to six or more residents making application for an initial license after July 1, 1959, shall be equipped throughout with an approved automatic fire detection system, or an approved sprinkler system, if residents are to be housed on the second floor.

All homes of two-story frame structure offering care to six or more residents making application for a renewal license after July 1, 1960, shall be equipped throughout with an approved automatic fire detection system, or an approved sprinkler system, if residents are to be housed on the second floor. 

3. Every floor of a home shall have at least two separate and distinct exits and as many more as may be required to provide a safe means of egress. 

These exit facilities shall be as remote from one another as possible and to be acceptable shall be in accordance with the specifications for exit facilities set out under Items F and G, pages 36, 37. 

4. The exit facilities, as required in the foregoing sections, are the minimum number permitted for any home regardless of its size. Large multi-story homes having wings or ells, or homes having a floor area in excess of the ordinary home, shall have a sufficient number of exit facilities in addition to the minimum number required to insure a safe means of egress for all residents housed therein. 

5. Homes shall not have dead end corridors. A dead end corridor in existing homes is defined as one having no way to a window or door. If the corridor is a "trap," it is a dead end corridor and shall not be approved. 

6. The inspector shall require and specify the location of smoke barriers in buildings of large areas having long corridors, ells and wings. 

7. Homes having residents housed above the first floor shall have all interior stairways enclosed in such a manner that will prevent the rapid spread of smoke, gas, or flames from one floor to floors above. At least one stairway enclosure shall have direct outside exit at the first floor level, provided that where the arrangement of the building would not permit the direct outside exit from a stairway enclosure at the first floor level, an outside (31)

stairway may be constructed. Stairway enclosures shall be of a material that will provide a minimum of one hour fire resistive rating. (Lath and plaster, or plasterboard, will comply.) Doors opening into stairway enclosures shall be sufficient in width to allow full use of the stairway exit capacity. Doors shall be located where they will not swing out over the top step of the stair. Doors swinging away from the stairway shall have a minimum 24 inches between door frame and top step. 

8. An acceptable passageway leading from an occupied room to an exit shall be a corridor leading directly to the exit facility, or an unobstructed passageway through a recreation room or a living room. 

9. Homes not having the required number of interior stairways, meeting the specifications for exit facility requirements, as set out under Item F, page 36, shall comply with the requirements for additional exit facilities by constructing exterior stairways. These exterior stairways shall be suitably located and shall be constructed in accordance with the specifications set out under Item G, page 37. 

10. Homes three or more stories in height, shall comply with the Kansas law that requires exterior steel fire escapes extending from the topmost floor to the ground, or shall have, in lieu of the above, interior stairways suitably located and of fire resistive material and enclosed at each floor level, and having a direct outside exit from the stair enclosure at the first floor level. 

A steel fire escape of approved design may be accepted as one of the required exits. 

11. Doors opening onto exterior stair platforms are deemed emergency exits and shall swing out. 

12. If the interior arrangement or structural features of any kind would prevent a safe and speedy means of egress from all occupied rooms of the structure, the inspector shall specify the areas that shall not be used for housing residents. 

13. Elevator shafts, or other vertical openings extending upward through the structure, shall be enclosed with materials having not less than one hour fire resistive rating. Dumb waiter shafts and laundry chutes shall be lined with a tightly sealed fire resistive material or shall be enclosed with a wall having one hour fire resistive rating. All openings into these shafts or spaces shall be protected by substantial doors of a type and material that will retard the spread of smoke, gases, or flames from one floor to floors above. 

14. Each sleeping room shall have at least one outside window. 

15. Floors, roofs, stair treads and handrails shall be kept in good repair at all times. Wall, partition, and ceiling surfaces shall be unbroken. 

16. Flue openings into chimneys whose use has been discontinued, shall be closed up with masonry. Flue openings closed temporarily shall have a tight fitting metal cover. 

17. A basement, used in part for housing residents, shall have two separate and distinct exits, one of which shall be directly to the outside and located in that section of the basement occupied by the residents. The basement rooms housing residents shall be cut off from that part of the basement containing the heating plant, laundry facilities, storerooms, water heater, and similar service rooms by a door, wall, or partition having a fire resistive rating of not less than one hour.

Item B. Special Construction Requirements and Limitations for Frame Build ings Used as Homes 1. A building having wood exterior walls and buildings having brick or stone veneer, asbestos shingles, stucco, or metal exterior wall covering over wood framing shall be rated as a frame structure. 

2. Homes of frame construction shall comply with the general construction and fire safety requirements applicable. 

3. Housing of all residents on the first floor shall be required when the floors above the first floor are not suitably arranged or in a condition to assure safety. 

4. Incapacitated residents shall not be housed above the first floor of a frame. structure, provided, however, that this restriction shall not apply to buildings of frame construction not over two stories in height and having an approved sprinkler system throughout. 

5. At least one outside stairway shall be constructed in a suitable location when self-care residents are housed on the second floor of a frame building.

Item C. Special Construction Requirements and Limitations for Buildings of Ordinary Construction Used as Homes 

1. A building having exterior supporting walls of masonry, brick or concrete and having interior construction of wood shall be rated as a building of ordinary construction.

The upper floor may be designated protected ordinary construction if it meets these requirements: Roof and floor construction and their supports have one hour fire resistance, and stairways and other openings through floors are enclosed with partitions having one hour fire resistance. 

2. Homes of ordinary construction shall comply with the general construction of fire safety requirements applicable. 

3. The housing of all residents on the first floor shall be required when, in the opinion of the inspector, the floors above the first floor are not suitably arranged or in a condition to assure safety.

4. Incapacitated residents and residents requiring restriction shall not be housed above the second floor of a building of ordinary construction, provided, however, that this restriction shall not apply in buildings of ordinary construction having an approved sprinkler system installed throughout the building. 

5. The erection of one or more outside stairways to serve the second floor shall be required, if in the opinion of the inspector, the same would be required to insure a reasonable degree of safety for the occupants of the second floor. 

6. Self-care residents capable of evacuating the building without help may be housed on the third floor of a building of ordinary construction provided one or more approved steel fire escapes extending from the third floor to the ground shall be installed, or in lieu of the outside steel fire escapes there are a sufficient number of enclosed stair wells having stairs and enclosing walls of fire resistive material on the interior of the building, and with fire resistive entrance door to the stair well enclosure at each floor level and an exit from the stair well directly to the outside at the first floor level. (This steel fire escape or fire resistive enclosed stairway is a requirement of the Kansas state law in all buildings three or more stories in height.) 

7. Residents shall not be housed on the fourth floor of a building of ordinary construction. Provided, however, that self-care residents may be housed on fourth floor when building is sprinklered with an approved sprinkler system throughout.

Item D. Special Construction Requirements and Limitations for Buildings of Fire-resistive Materials Throughout Used as Homes 

1. A building constructed with exterior walls, floors, partitions, stairways, and roof of fire resistive material is rated as a fire resistive building. 

2. Homes of fire resistive construction shall comply with the general construction and fire safety requirements applicable. 

3. All floors of a fire resistive building shall be acceptable for occupancy by residents except where parts of the building being used for other purposes would present hazards that would restrict the use of certain portions of the building. 

4. A building of this type, if over two stories in height, shall have outside steel fire escapes or approved enclosed stairways on the interior, as required by state law. Vertical openings through floors shall be enclosed with fire resistive material or otherwise protected to prevent passage of fire or smoke to other floors. 

5. A building of fire resistive material two or more stories in height shall have all interior stairways enclosed with fire resistive material. 

Item E. Fire Safety Requirements Applicable to all Homes Heating and Cooking Apparatus 

1. Furnace and stoves shall be properly maintained and in good repair. 

2. All new, reopened, and newly constructed homes shall have a central heating plant properly installed and vented. 

All automatically controlled heating appliances and furnaces utilizing

gas as a fuel shall have 100% safety cut-off control which will cut off the supply of gas if the pilot light is extinguished. 

All furnaces utilizing oil as a fuel shall have a safety control that will cut off the supply of fuel, in the event the ignition device is inoperative. 

Newly constructed homes having steam or hot water heating plants shall have a cut-off valve between the boiler and all branch lines. 

Heating plants in all homes shall be of an approved type. 

Portable oil heaters shall not be permitted. 

3. Protection of Combustible Material Adjacent to Heating Units a. All types of furnaces, or heaters, excepting pipeless furnaces, shall have top of furnace not less than six inches from ceiling. When the top of furnace is less than 18 inches from combustible material above the heating unit, the ceiling above shall be covered with one inch cement or gypsum plaster or its equivalent, such shield to cover an area above and beyond the furnace of not less than 18 inches to the sides and 48 inches to the front. The distance between the furnace and a combustible wall, partition or material, shall not be less than 18 inches to the side and 48 inches to the front.

Furnaces not having enclosed, insulated tops shall have top of furnace covered with a layer of sand, asbestos or otherwise be effectively insulated.

The above does not apply to gas fixed furnaces constructed and approved for lesser clearance. b. Rooms where furnaces are housed shall be cut off from other portions of the structure housing residents. c. Cook stoves shall have sufficient clearance between the bottom of the stove and the floor to allow free circulation of air within this space. 

A minimum of 4 inches of such air space will be provided. d. The floors under all heating stoves, except approved gas stoves, shall be protected by a metal or asbestos stove floor board. This protection shall extend at least 18 inches in front of the firebox. Heating stoves of the heat radiating type shall have at least 30 inches clearance from adjacent walls. Heating stoves of a circulating type shall have at least 12 inches clearance from adjacent walls. This regulation applies only to existing homes. 

4. Open Fires 

All heating devices shall be totally enclosed. Fireplaces shall be closed. A permanent screen securely fastened is acceptable for closing a fireplace. 

5. Gas Stove Connections and Vents

All gas heating, cooking stoves, hot water heaters, and all gas appliances shall be connected with rigid steel or iron connections. All gas heating stoves or cooking stoves used for heating and hot water heaters shall be properly connected with a chimney or other approved outlet extending to the outside. (This is also a requirement of the Kansas Statute.) 

6. Hot Plates 

Gas hot plates shall be equipped with legs not less than 4 inches in height and shall be at least 8 inches from walls. Hot plates shall be set

on noncombustible material. Hot plates shall not be used except for canning or laundry purposes. 

7. Supplemental Heating for Bathrooms 

When Supplemental heaters are needed in bathrooms properly installed gas or electric wall heaters shall be used. 

8. Flue Pipes and Floor Registers a. Flue pipes for solid or liquid fuel burning furnaces or stoves shall be substantially supported, in good repair, and free of holes or leaks. 

Combustible material within 18 inches from flue pipes shall be protected be fire resistive material. b. Heat ducts or conductors shall be constructed of noncombustible material, in good repair and free of accumulation of lint or other debris. 

Adequate distance to insure reasonable fire safety shall be maintained between the heat duct and combustible material. c. Floor registers shall be kept clean at all times. All filters used in connection with a heating or ventilation system shall be of noncombustible material.

Item F. Entrance and Exit Facilities 

1. Doors used as a means of exit leading outside of buildings shall be kept in good condition and shall open freely. a. Doors opening onto fire escape or outside stairway platforms shall open out except in the rare instances where such a plan is not feasible, when an alternate plan must be approved by the state inspector. 

The inspector may require that certain doors, leading to emergency exits, be hinged to swing in the direction of travel. b. The minimum width of an exit door shall have not less than 32 inches free opening. c. The height of the door shall be not less than 6 feet 8 inches. d. Doors used in openings in partitions or walls forming a stairway enclosure shall be solid wooden doors, laminated wooden doors, wood faced fire door or wood panel door covered with sheet metal and shall contain no glass panels except where small panels of wired glass may be used. e. New homes-exterior doors shall be 36 inches in width. 

2. Exit doors shall be equipped with hardware which will permit opening of the door from the inside of the room or building without use of a key. 

Not more than one locking device shall be permitted on outside door. 

3. Doors to occupied rooms that may be locked to restrict the residents shall be equipped with a locking device or bolt so arranged that it may be opened with ease without the use of a key. A hook and eye locking device shall not be used. 

4. Means for locking the door from the inside of a bedroom or bathroom used by residents shall not be permitted. 

5. Signs bearing the word "EXIT" in plain legible block letters shall be placed at each exit opening designated by the inspector. Directional signs shall be placed in halls and corridors if deemed necessary by the inspector. Lettering of the exit signs shall be red and not less than 5 inches in height.

Item G. Stairways, Halls, and Corridors 

1. Interior stairways and landings shall be of sufficient width to allow quick evacuation of the residents occupying the floor served by the stairway. a. The minimum width shall be 3 feet, without winders. b. Stairways and landings shall have a minimum overhead clearance of 6 feet 8 inches. c. Stair landings shall be kept clear of all obstacles, including pieces of furniture. 

2. All stairways shall have uniform risers and treads in each run. a. The tread shall be not less than 9%½ inches; risers not more than 7% inches. b. No interior stairway shall have a vertical rise of more than 12 feet between floors or landings. All existing homes shall meet this requirement. c. Newly constructed homes shall not have a vertical rise in interior stairways of more than 8 feet between floors or landings. 

3. Substantial handrails shall be provided on both sides of a stairway. 

Guardrails shall be placed around all areaways and shall not be less than 

3 feet in height. 

4. Halls and corridors shall be not less than 3 feet in width in existing buildings and not less than 6 feet in newly constructed homes. They shall be kept free and unobstructed at all times. Stairways, halls, and corridors shall be well lighted at all times. 

5. Exterior stairways serving as emergency exits from the second story, shall be substantially constructed, securely anchored to the building, and shall rest on substantial supports or piers at the ground level. a. The platform at the top of the stairway shall be at approximately floor level and not less than 3 feet square. b. Platforms and stairways shall have substantially constructed handrails not less than 31 inches high, and a second guardrail approximately 15 inches below the handrail. c. Stairways shall be kept in good repair and be painted. The slope of the stair shall not be greater than 45 degrees. If constructed of wood the tread shall be not less than 9½ inches and the riser not more than 7%4 inches. If constructed of wood the width of the stairway shall be not less than 36 inches. d. The supports for outside stairways shall be not less than 4 inches by 

4 inches; shall be substantially cross braced, and shall rest on and be anchored to concrete piers or footings extending into the ground to a depth below the frost line. e. All exterior stairways having a vertical rise measured from the foot of the stairs to the level of the platform in excess of 10 feet shall have an intermediate landing. The stairs may have a change of direction at the intermediate landing. f. The stringers, tread and platform of the outside stairs, if of wood, shall be not less than 2 inches in thickness. g. If steel stairways and platforms are being erected, detailed specifications for their construction should be obtained from the State Fire Marshal Department. Exterior stairways on buildings more than two stories in height shall conform with requirements specified in Sec. 31-101, G. S. 1949, which applies to fire escapes. This section requires that platforms and stairs for fire escapes be constructed of steel.

Item H. Electric Wiring 

1. All wiring shall be in accordance with the National Electrical Code, 1956 

Edition. In cities where electrical wiring installation is regulated by city ordinances, the wiring shall conform to the requirements of the ordinance. 

2. All lighting circuits and appliance circuits shall be protected by fuses or circuit breakers rated at not more than 15 amperes. Overloaded circuits shall be made safe by installing additional circuits to carry part of the load. 

3. The use of extension cords shall be prohibited. 

4. All appliances shall be in good repair. Appliances used occasionally shall be disconnected at the outlet when not in use. 

5. The lead-in wire from radio and TV antennae shall have a lightning arrestor with proper ground, installed where the lead-in wire enters the building.

Metal masts or pipe supporting antenna shall also be grounded by means of a separate grounding wire. 

Supporting masts for antenna shall not be anchored to a chimney.

Item I. Fire Extinguishers 

1. Each building shall be equipped with fire extinguishers of an approved design and type. The extinguishers shall be of a type which is approved for extinguishing fires involving wood, textile, paper, and similar materials, which is known as a Class "A" fire. A 2½ gallon soda-acid, a 5 gallon pump type extinguisher filled with clean water, or a 2½ gallon foam type are approved for Class "A" fires. A CO2 extinguisher of proper size, or a dry chemical equivalent to CO2 shall be provided in the kitchen. 

2. At least one extinguisher shall be provided on each floor of a home. The number, type and location of the extinguishers shall be designated by the inspector. 

3. Fire extinguishers shall be placed in plain view and easy reach. 

4. Soda-acid and foam shall be recharged after each use, or annually in accordance with manufacturer's instructions. Water pump extinguishers shall be kept full of clean water at all times. The date of inspection and recharge shall be recorded on a tag on the extinguisher. 

Item J. Trash and Rubbish Disposal 

All buildings shall be kept free from accumulation of combustible trash or rubbish. Metal containers for receiving same shall be placed in easily accessible locations. Trash shall be removed from the building each evening. 

Ashes shall be kept in metal containers with metal covers and be removed from the building daily.

Item K. Flammable Materials 

The storage of flammable material or liquid in a building used as a home shall be prohibited.

Item L. Plan for Removal of Residents in Emergency 

Every home shall have a carefully worked out plan for removal of residents in case of fire or other emergency requiring evacuation, which shall be planned with, and understood by all the staff so that each will know his part in such emergency. This plan shall be written and posted on each floor for ready reference, and shall be reviewed at intervals in staff meetings. The inspector shall examine the employees as to their knowledge of their duties as provided in the emergency plan. Fire drills for staff shall be conducted at least every three months. This shall include a practice evacuation of all residents who are physically able to be evacuated from the building, and demonstration of the method of evacuation of persons confined to bed. The time and date of the fire drill shall be recorded and available to the inspector.

39-901. Definitions. The following words and phrases when used in this act shall, for the purposes of this act, have the meanings respectively ascribed to them in this section: "Boarding home" shall mean and shall be construed to include any place for the reception, accommodation, board, and personal care and treatment of not less than twenty-four (24) hours in any week of three (3) or more individuals not related to the administrator or owner by blood or marriage, who by reason of aging, illness, disease, or physical or mental infirmity are unable to sufficiently or properly care for themselves, and for which reception, accommodation, board, personal care and treatment a charge is made. "Nursing home" shall mean and shall be construed to include any place for the reception, accommodation, nursing care and treatment of not less than twenty-four (24) hours in any week of three (3) or more individuals not related to the administrator or owner by blood or marriage, who by reason of aging, illness, disease or physical or mental infirmity are unable to sufficiently or properly care for themselves, and for which reception, accommodation, nursing care, and treatment, board, personal care, and treatment a charge is made. "Homes for the aged" shall mean and shall be construed to include any single or multiple living units used for the reception of the aged or other persons where supervised living is provided. The term "boarding home," "nursing home," or "home for the aged" shall not include institutions operated by the federal or state government or any municipal corporation, hospital or institutions for the treatment and care of psychiatric patients, boarding homes for children, under the age of sixteen (16) years, day nurseries, child caring institutions, nor hotels or offices of physicians. "Resident" shall mean and shall be construed to include all individuals kept, cared for, treated, boarded or otherwise accommodated in any boarding home, nursing home, or home for the aged. "Person" shall mean any individual, firm, partnership, corporation, company, association, or joint stock association, and the legal successor thereof. "Licensing agency" shall mean the state department of social welfare. “Nursing care" means professional nursing or practical nursing for sick or injured persons who are under the care and supervision of licensed medical practitioners. "Personal care" means assistance with meals, dressing, transportation, and movement, and such other general oversight of the physical well-being of the residents of the home, exclusive of nursing, as may be required by their condition. 

39-902. Purpose. The purpose of this act is for the development, establishment and enforcement of standards (1) for the care, treatment, health, safety, welfare and comfort of individuals in boarding homes, nursing homes, or homes for the aged and (2) for the construction, general hygiene, maintenance and operation of boarding homes, nursing homes, or homes for the aged which, in the light of advancing knowledge, will promote safe and adequate accommodation, care and treatment of such individuals in boarding homes, nursing homes, or homes for the aged. 39. Administration. The administration of this act shall be under the state department of social welfare as the licensing agency in conjunction with the state board of health, the state fire marshal, and shall have the assistance of local county welfare departments, health, fire and safety authorities and other agencies of government in this state. 

39-904. Powers of state department of social welfare. It shall be unlawful for any person as defined in this act acting jointly or severally with any other person to conduct, maintain or operate, or permit to be maintained or operated, or to participate in the conducting, maintenance or operation of a boarding home, nursing home, or home for the aged within this state except upon license first had and obtained for that purpose from the state department of social welfare as the licensing agency upon application made therefor as provided in this act, and compliance with the requirements, standards, rules and regulations promulgated under its provisions. 

39-905. Application for license. An application for a license for a boarding home, nursing home, or home for the aged shall be made in writing to the licensing agency upon forms provided by it and shall be in such form and shall contain such information as the licensing agency reasonably requires, which may include affirmative evidence of ability to comply with such reasonable standards, rules and regulations as are lawfully prescribed hereunder. 

Such application shall be signed by the owner or person responsible for the operation of such boarding home, nursing home, or home for the aged, or by the duly authorized officer or responsible agent thereof. 

39-306. Issuance of license. Upon receipt of an application for license, the licensing agency shall with the approval of the state board of health and the state fire marshal issue a license if the applicant is fit and qualified and boarding home, nursing home, or home for the aged facilities meet the requirements established under this law. The licensing agency, the state board of health, the state fire marshal and county welfare department or their designated representatives shall make such inspections and investigations as are necessary to determine the conditions existing in each case and a written report of such inspections and investigations and the recommendations of the state board of health, the state fire marshal and county welfare department or their authorized agents shall be filed with the licensing agency. The licensing agency, the state board of health and the state fire marshal may designate and use county departments of social welfare, local health, fire and safety authorities as their agents in making such inspections and investigations and such subsequent inspections and investigations as are deemed necessary or advisable. Such local authorities are hereby authorized, empowered and directed to perform such duties within the areas appropriated to their official duties as prescribed by law. A license unless sooner suspended or revoked, shall be renewable annually upon filing by the licensee, and approval by the licensing agency, and by the state board of health and the state fire marshal or their duly authorized agents, of an annual report and application for renewal upon such uniform dates and containing such information in such form as the licensing agency prescribed by regulation. Each license shall be issued only for the premises and persons named in the application and shall not be transferable or assignable. It shall be posted in a conspicuous place in the boarding home, nursing home, or home for the aged. If application for renewal is not so filed, such

Sta 2 edi license is automatically canceled as of the date of its expiration. Any license granted under the provisions of this act shall state the maximum number of patients for which granted, the person or persons to whom granted, the date, the expiration date and such additional information and special limitations as are deemed advisable. 

39-907. Provisional license. A provisional license may be issued to any boarding home, nursing home, or home for the aged the facilities of which are temporarily unable to conform to all the standards, requirements, rules and regulations established under the provisions of this act: Provided, however, That the issuance of such provisional license shall be approved by the state board of health, and the state fire marshal. A provisional license may be issued for more than one (1) year, at the discretion of the licensing agency. 

39-908. Fee for license. The fee for license to conduct a boarding home, nursing home, or home for the aged shall be: (a) Boarding homes, nursing homes, or homes for the aged for not more than five (5) residents, two dollars ($2); (b) boarding homes, nursing homes, or homes for the aged for more than five (5) and not more than eight (8) residents, three dollars ($3); and (c) boarding homes, nursing homes, or homes for the aged for more than eight SO (8) residents, ten dollars ($10); which shall be paid to the state board of social welfare before the license is issued, and shall be deposited in the social welfare fund. 慢慢 ​DS an ire 

39-909. Suspension, denial, or revocation of license. The licensing agency after notice and opportunity for hearing to the applicant or licensee is authorized to deny, suspend or revoke a license in any case in which it finds that there has been a substantial failure to comply with the requirements, standards, rules and regulations established under this act. Such notice shall be effected by registered mail, or by personal service setting forth the particular reasons for the proposed action and fixing a date not less than fifteen (15) days from the date of such mailing or service, at which time the applicant or licensee shall be given an opportunity for a prompt and fair hearing. On the basis of any such hearing, or upon default of the applicant or licensee the licensing agency shall make a determination specifying its findings of fact and conclusions of law. A copy of such determination shall be sent by registered mail or served personally upon the applicant or licensee. The decision revoking, suspending or denying the license or application shall become final fifteen (15) days after it is so mailed or served. The procedure governing hearings authorized by this section shall be in accordance with section 75-3306 of the General Statutes of 1949 and with the rules promulgated by the licensing agency. A complete record shall be kept of all proceedings, and all testimony shall be reported and copy or copies of the transcript may be obtained by an interested party on request. 

39-910. Power to adopt rules and regulations. The licensing agency shall adopt, amend, promulgate and enforce such rules, regulations and standards as may be deemed practicable, reasonable and necessary with respect to all boarding homes, nursing homes, or homes for the aged to be licensed hereunder as may be designed to further the accomplishment of the purposes of this law in promoting safe, proper and adequate treatment and care of individuals in boarding homes, nursing homes, or homes for the aged in the interest of public health, safety and welfare. Such rules, regulations and standards may be estab-

lished and classified according to the location, building, construction, size and facilities of boarding homes, nursing homes, or homes for the aged and the number and kind of patients and care offered or provided, and shall prescribe records to be kept, kind and frequency of reports and inventories to be made, and requirements for the protection, health, safety, hygiene, welfare and comfort of patients and standards and requirements for care. Any boarding home, nursing home, or home for the aged which is in operation at the time of promulgation of any applicable rules or regulations or minimum standards under this act shall be given a reasonable time, under the particular circumstances not to exceed four (4) months from the date of such promulgation, within which to comply with such rules and regulations and minimum standards. 

39-911. Power to make inspection and investigation. The licensing agency shall make or cause to be made by the county welfare department such inspections and investigations as it deems necessary. The licensing agency may prescribe by regulation that any licensee or applicant desiring to make specified types of alterations or additions to its facilities or to construct new facilities shall before commencing such alteration, addition or new construction, submit plans and specifications therefor to the licensing agency for preliminary inspection and approval or recommendations with respect to compliance with the regulations and standards herein authorized. Necessary conferences and consultations may be provided. 

39-912. Information confidential. Information received by the licensing agency through filed reports, inspections, or as otherwise authorized under this law, shall not be disclosed publicly in such manner as to identify individuals or boarding homes, nursing homes, or homes for the aged, except in a proceeding involving the question of licensure. 

39-913. Inspections. Inspections shall be made and reported in writing by the authorized agents and representatives of the licensing agency and of the state board of health, of the state fire marshal, and of the county department of social welfare, as often and in the manner and form prescribed under the rules and regulations promulgated under the provisions of this act. Free access shall be given to the premises of any boarding home, nursing home, or home for the aged at any reasonable time to carry out the requirements of this section and the provisions and purposes of this act. 

39-914. Care required. A qualified person or persons shall be in attendance at all times upon residents receiving accommodation, board, care or treatment in boarding homes, nursing homes, or homes for the aged. The licensing agency may establish necessary standards, rules and regulations prescribing the number, qualifications, training standard of professional conduct, sobriety and integrity for such qualified person or persons attendant upon the residents. All medical care and treatment shall be given under the direction of a physician authorized to practice under the laws of this state and shall be provided promptly as needed: Provided, however, That no resident who relies in good faith upon spiritual means or prayer for healing shall, if he objects thereto, be required to undergo medical care or treatment. 

39-915. Compliance with other laws. All pertinent laws of this state and lawfully adopted ordinances and rules and regulations shall be strictly complied with in the operation of any boarding home, nursing home, or home for the aged in this state.
