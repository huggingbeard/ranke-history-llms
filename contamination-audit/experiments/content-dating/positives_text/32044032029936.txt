The ABC's of FAIR TRADE 1. Q. What is Fair Trade? 

A. Fair Trade is an established principle of law under which property rights in Trade Brands, and the integrity of the products which bear them, are protected in the public interest against dilution, defamation and degradation by predatory resellers seeking monopoly of distribution through deceptive pricing practices. 

2. Q. Who is benefited by Fair Trade?

A. Everybody. It works for the best interests of consumers, distributors and manufacturers. 

3. Q. How does Fair Trade benefit the ulti mate consumer?

A. It enables him to buy his favorite advertised brands at any store where they are on sale with the knowledge that the price he pays is held by the manufacturer, under the full pressures of competition, at a minimum figure which permits the maintenance of honest quality. It protects him from so-called "bargain" sales which expose him to high, often exorbitant, prices charged for unbranded, unidentified goods. It protects him from the hidden "Quality Squeeze" forced by the "Cut Price Spiral" of un- 4 9 5 8 8 8 [1]

restrained "loss-leader" price baiting-in which concealed quality dilutions at last greatly outrun price reductions. 

4. Q. How does Fair Trade benefit the re seller?

A. It makes consistently available to him merchandise of higher quality per unit of cost-thus stimulating sales through enhanced values. It protects him and his customers from encroachment of resale monopolists. 

5. Q. How does Fair Trade benefit the manu facturer?

A. Fair Trade, by legalizing minimum price maintenance contracts, enables the manufacturer to avoid the quality-reducing pressures of unrestrained cut prices. It thus permits the manufacturer to protect the quality standards on which he built his business and thus maintain the integrity of the trade-marks which identify his products. 

Competition prevents extortionate prices. 

Fair Trade protection prevents pressures to dilute quality to meet uneconomic prices. 

6. Q. How are Fair Trade prices estab lished?

A. The manufacturer determines for his product a price which gives that product, according to its value to the consumer, a persuasive appeal when compared with competitive products offered for the same purpose. The Fair Trade laws permit him to contract with distributors not to sell below the minimum price he announced. [2]

7. Q. Do Fair Trade prices have to be registered or reported to any state or federal agency?

A. No. All that is necessary is that minimum price contracts be made between the manufacturer and one or more resellers in each state having Fair Trade laws-with notice to other resellers of the state. Fair 

Trade operations of the manufacturer may be limited to any number of such states. 

8. Q. Does every reseller have to sign a contract before he comes under the operation of the Fair Trade law? 

A. No. Once the manufacturer has, in good faith, entered into such an agreement with one or more resellers of the product in a particular state, all that is necessary is to put all other resellers of the product in that state on notice of the manufacturer's Fair Trade policy. Those on notice are equally bound. 

9. Q. How are Fair Trade prices enforced?

A. Fair Trade prices are usually enforced through application by the aggrieved party to an equity court for injunctive relief to restrain the price-cutter from continuing to sell below the established price fixed by the 

Fair Trade contract. Damages may be recov ered by any injured party. After an order has been issued by the Court to the pricecutter restraining him from the continuance of the wrongful act, further price-cutting leaves the price-cutter open to punishment for contempt of court. Some courts punish for contempt of court by fine and some by imprisonment, or both. Resort to court action, however, is seldom necessary. [3]

10. Q. Can Fair Trade prices be changed without executing a new contract?

A. Yes. Fair Trade prices can be adjusted by notice by the manufacturer at any time to meet varying economic conditions. 

Notice of these changes should be sent to all with whom the manufacturer deals. 

11. Q. Does Fair Trade legislation stop all price-cutting?

A. No. It only prohibits sub-minimum prices on the particular trade-marked product on which the manufacturer has announced his intention of maintaining a minimum price. 

12. Q. Can a dealer resell at prices higher than those stipulated in the Fair 

Trade contracts?

A. The law in this respect differs in different states. Most states have enacted legislation legalizing contracts, fixing the minimum resale price. Statutes in other states provide that the vendee may not resell except at the vendor's stipulated price. 

The first type of contract fixes the minimum price only, the second type of contract establishes the price as the maximum as well as the minimum. 

13. Q. Do resellers generally favor handling 

Fair Traded merchandise? 

A. Most resellers favor the handling of

Fair Trade products because such products, usually bearing a nationally known and advertised trade-mark, are of high quality and are widely and favorably known. [4]

14. Q. What types of products are best suited to sale under Fair Trade contracts?

A. All types of products sold under a brand name to consumers. All that is required is that the product bear a trade-mark, brand, or name of the producer or owner, and that it be in fair and open competition with commodities of the same general class produced by others. 

15. Q. Who are opposed to Fair Trade?

A. Merchants who claim to sell merchandise at lower prices than their competitors and try to prove this claim by cutting prices on certain popular brands which they use as "loss leaders" or "bargain bait".

Fair Trade is opposed also by manufacturers of unbranded merchandise who prefer to have their resellers left free to cut prices of well advertised, trade-marked goods so as to attract customers who may be "switched" to unbranded or off-branded goods. 

16. Q. Does Fair Trade encourage higher prices? 

A. No. Surveys show that Fair Trade usually effects an actual reduction in prices while enhancing quality and improving values. 

17. Q. What is there to deter the manufacturer of a Fair Traded product from setting his price too high?

A. His own self-interest. No manufacturer dares run the risk of letting his prices get out of line. He knows that if he did his [5]

competitors would soon put him out of business. Competition in trade-marked products is far more severe than in any other class of goods. The manufacturer of branded products knows that, quality-considered, a fair price is the most potent factor in building and holding his market. 

18. Q. What is the Miller-Tydings Act? 

A. Prior to the enactment of this Federal statute, State Fair Trade laws did not protect merchandise used in Interstate Commerce. To meet this situation, Congress passed this Federal Enabling Act which exempts from the Sherman Anti-Trust Act the execution of Fair Trade contracts applying to branded merchandise sold in open competition in Interstate Commerce. 

19. Q. How do Fair Trade statutes differ from Fair Trade Practice Acts? 

A. Fair Trade laws have no other intent or effect than to allow the manufacturer to establish a minimum price below which his product may not be sold. The manufacturer operates under Fair Trade without reference to governmental agencies and need only invoke the law in court when violated. 

So called "Fair Trade Practices” have to do with various phases of distribution and bear no relationship to Fair Trade laws. 

20. Q. Why is the present an especially favorable period for adopting a Fair 

Trade policy? 

A. Because abnormal concentrations of predatory capital seeking profitable employment, and not schooled in the traditions of American business, continues to expose long [6]

established enterprises to intensifying raids, in which little or no respect is shown for that integrity of trade-marks which our Fair 

Trade laws are enacted to preserve. 

21. Q. What are the essential provisions of state Fair Trade laws?

A. All Fair Trade laws provide in substance that there is nothing illegal about a contract specifying the minimum resale price of a trade-marked or similarly identified product, and that wilfully and knowingly advertising or selling such a product at less than the specified price is unfair competition permitting legal redress by any injured party. The Fair Trade laws permit only vertical price treatment; that is, the trade-mark owner may specify the minimum prices at which a product may be sold by others.

There is nothing mandatory about these statutes; they do not dictate that all trademark owners must designate minimum prices. They simply permit a producer to do so if he desires. 

All of these statutes specifically prohibit horizontal price-fixing, which is the fixing of prices by parties normally in competition with each other, such as a group of producers or a group of resellers. Horizontal price-fixing leads to evil results, inasmuch as the normal forces of Fair Trade competition, which would force the reduction of excessive prices, would be eliminated by a horizontal combination.

Resale price minimums, which is all that the Fair Trade laws permit, are fixed by contract between parties who do not com- [7]

J pete with each other; that is the owner of the trade-mark or trade-name on the one hand and the resellers-wholesale or retailof commodities bearing such trade-mark or trade-name on the other hand. The trademark owner is not permitted to utilize Fair Trade unless his product is in open competition with other commodities of the same 

22. Q. What states now have Fair Trade laws?

A. All the states except Missouri, Texas and Vermont, and the District of Columbia. 

23. Q. What is the American Fair Trade 

Council?

A. The American Fair Trade Council is the only national organization of manufacturers having as its sole purpose the protection of Fair Trade and the enhancement of its contributions to the advancement of our national economy. Its membership represents various industries-not simply a single trade group. 

24. Q. Who is eligible to membership in the 

American Fair Trade Council?

A. Any producer of trade-marked goods who practices Fair Trade or has decided to do so.
