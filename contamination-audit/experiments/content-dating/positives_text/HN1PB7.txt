FOREWORD 

THE content of this book has been recorded as a result of several special midweek services held in 

Tremont Temple Baptist Church, Boston, at which time messages were brought on the Second Coming of Christ in its various relationships. 

Because of many requests that these be put in permanent form, I have been led to incorporate them in this book. Well realizing my inability, especially when considering the many splendid works which have been produced by some of our most learned and able teachers of God's Word, I am led by my people, who seem to feel that the Holy Spirit was able to use these messages for their better understanding of the great future events which God's Word very clearly predicts, to lend my effort to this task. I am convinced that God's 

Word is still authoritative and that God has recorded history in advance; also that the preaching of the Gospel demands a presentation of all of its component parts dealing with the three phases of salvation-past, present and future. To neglect that which deals with the future is to deplete the message. 

Besides this, one realizes after contact with many people, inside and outside the Church, that there have arisen many teachers of false doctrines who have been able to draw unto themselves great numbers of people because they have not neglected

FOREWORD 

the great truth of Christ's Second Coming. In the majority of our so-called evangelical pulpits this note is never heard, with the result that the people in the pews who, in the simplicity of their Bible study, recognize the presence of this truth, gladly lend a listening ear to any voice seeming to proclaim that truth.

Many of my brother ministers and closest friends profess to believe in the literal return of Christ, but, on their own admissions, never preach on this subject. Often the excuse is that the Second Coming is something which cannot be fully understood. 

I say with all humility and Christian charity that this excuse is weak; for if these men will give careful study to the subject, seeking rightly to divide the Word of Truth, and attempt to preach from the knowledge already gained, they will soon discover that the great prophetic truths dealing with the future will array themselves in a Spirit-given orderliness and clearness. To my mind, at least, the future is as clearly visualized as that period of time which has already been recorded on the pages of history.

In the following lectures I have employed a style which may seem to some rather technical or legal. 

Perhaps I may be excused for this because of my legal training, and the habit of preparing messages more as briefs to submit to a court. I have tried to make these outlines so clear that they may be easily used by any who may desire to understand and perhaps teach this subject. Scriptural quotations and citations are freely given and oftentimes sentences are put in an abbreviated form. Extra

I have tried to withhold myself from setting forth any pet theories which may be mine alone, and in all places have endeavored to base my message on a "Thus saith the Lord." My prayer in sending this book forth is that all who read may find that joy and confidence in the expectancy of the fulfillment of that Blessed Hope which I, myself, have found therein.

At the beginning of our study it seems needful that we rediscover what we are discussing, or what we mean when we speak of the Second Coming of Christ. Especially is this necessary because Satan has kept many an earnest Christian in a state of confusion as he has tried to unravel the chronology of the future. May I say very definitely that we do not mean the ending of the world. A careful study of Scripture will reveal that God has dealt with man in various ways through the ages or dispensations and that He is dealing in grace with mankind in relationship to their attitude toward His Son during this present age, which age will have a definite ending. Neither do we mean the gift of the Holy Spirit on the day of Pentecost, although some who would seek to spiritualize all of the prophecies dealing with the Second Coming hold this view. However, there is no Scripture which would justify the claim.

A friend told of speaking with one man who professed to believe all of the great doctrines in regard to the Gospel of Christ, but when questioned as to his view of the Second Advent, said, "I believe that

Christ has been coming in all the events of history." May I say that we are quite ready to grant that Christ has been in the movements of history, 13

but that this does not satisfy the Scriptures which teach the truth before us.

Others hold that at the time of physical death 

Christ comes a second time. Again let me say that while we recognize that "To be absent from the body is to be present with the Lord," yet Luke 16 and other Scriptures lead us to believe that the Spirit goes to Him, conducted or conveyed into His presence by the angels. Let us look then at the affirmative side of the question. What do we mean and what do we believe Scripture teaches? 

If you will turn to Acts 1:10, 11 and stand again on the Mount of Olives with the disciples immediately after their Lord had been taken from them, you will hear the testimony of the two men in white apparel who appeared unto them. Surely no other meaning can be given to these words than that "This same Jesus" who has been taken up from them into heaven will so come again in like manner. 

I say that to me this truth means that Jesus Christ, the glorified Man, now at the right hand of God the Father, will one day come again to earth, personally, visibly, and corporeally.

THE VARIOUS ASPECTS THEREOF. 

We speak of the Second Coming as one event, and yet Scripture reveals that this will take place in a twofold manner. Paul, in Titus 2:13, discloses this fact when he speaks of "that blessed hope and the glorious appearing." For the Church it is a blessed hope, after which He appears in glory as He comes to earth with His own.

The Greek words which are used in relation to

this event are meaningful. In I Thessalonians 

3:13 and 4:15, where Paul speaks of Christ coming for the saints, the word Parousia, meaning "presence," is used, revealing the fact that He will manifest His presence first of all to His own. 

In another message we shall deal more fully with this subject. Suffice it to say here that we believe the very next event to take place in the fulfillment of prophecy will be that which is set forth in 

I Thessalonians 4:16, when "The Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout, with the voice of the archangel, and with the trump of God: and the dead in Christ shall rise first: Then we who are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them in the clouds, to meet the Lord in the air." Note that nothing is said of His coming to earth, but rather in the clouds, into which His own, alive on earth, shall be suddenly gathered and transformed into His likeness, and the spirits of just men made perfect, now in His presence clothed upon with a temporary body, will be reunited with the body that now sleeps, in its return to dust or the elements, resurrected and transformed into His likeness and glory. Thus the complete Church shall be taken into His presence, there to abide forevermore.

The other word which describes His coming in its relationship to the world is Epiphaneia, meaning "appearance" (conspicuous, bright, illustrious), and is used in Matthew 24:30, where the Lord speaks of His coming to earth in glory. Also in 

II Thessalonians 2:9. 

It might be wise to note the difference between

the Day of the Lord and the Day of Christ.

In 

I Thessalonians 5:2 we read of the Day of the 

Lord coming upon man as a thief in the night.

The Day of the Lord is always one of judgment, and nowhere does He say that He will come upon 

His own as a thief who comes in the night to break into the home, but rather, in this respect, to an unbelieving world.

In II Thessalonians 2:2 the authorized version speaks of the coming of the Day of Christ and that the man of sin must be revealed first. Westcott and Hort, who give us the best Greek rendition, use the word Kurios, meaning Lord, thus making the passage refer to the Day of the Lord, and not the Day of Christ. The Day of Christ would be that day in which He comes for His own; He is the 

Christ to them alone. The Day of the Lord will be the day of His vengeance and judgment, and, as Paul sets forth here, the man of sin will be revealed before that day. Let us hold then this distinction, remembering that He will come first of all for His own, this being the Day of Christ; and then later with His own when He comes in glory to judge. 

This is the Day of the Lord.

THE COMMON MILLENNIAL VIEWS. 

There are three prevailing views that are held in regard to Christ's coming in its relationship to the Millennial Kingdom: 

1-That which is known as Post-Millennialism. 

The prefix employed reveals the meaning of the view held by those who make this claim-"Post" meaning "after." They believe that His coming

can only be after the Church by its teaching and preaching has established here upon earth a kingdom of perfect peace and sinlessness. Although this may be the most common view, I find no Scripture which, to my mind, justifies the position. 

It is not based on scriptural authority. 

2-The Pre-Millennial View. 

Again the prefix reveals the meaning-that Christ, Himself, must come and set up His Kingdom before any millennial condition, as described in the Bible, can prevail on earth. I believe that all the teaching of Scripture justifies this view and it is the view that I shall maintain throughout this book. Perhaps one passage from Scripture which fell from the lips of our Lord, Himself, will suffice at this time-Matthew 25:31, 32: "When the Son of man shall come in his glory, and all the holy angels with him, then shall he sit upon the throne of his glory: and before him shall be gathered all nations: and he shall separate them one from another." 

3-The A-Millennial View. 

Again the prefix reveals the nature of the interpretation which those who hold this particular view maintain. "A" is the Greek negative, and this simply means that there will be no millennial kingdom, but that when He comes, as they believe He will, it will be to consummate all things and to bring the new heaven and new earth. Perhaps the strongest verse that they might set forth in favor of this would be II Peter 3:10, but I submit that it is dangerous to build any doctrine on a few scat-

tered verses;

Scripture must be compared with 

Scripture. 

Perhaps it might be well to call attention here to the importance which centers in the question of the view one holds of this truth. I, personally, believe that much of our misunderstanding today centers in this question. For instance, one who is a post-millennialist and believes that he and those who follow with him must, through their efforts, establish this Utopian Kingdom, realizes that the element of time does not very greatly enter in. 

Thus he tries practically any measure and often resorts to legislation, reformation, etc., instead of adhering strictly to the great primal and essential task of preaching the Gospel. This is not true of all.

Both those who hold the "Pre" and "A" Millennial View believe in the imminency of His return and must adhere strictly to the program of witnessing, and the proclamation of the message of individual salvation, believing that God is still saving the individual soul and that His program for the world is in the future.

I would further suggest that those who hold the last two views have tied themselves up with a doctrine which in itself demands of them a life that is truly Christian, and of which they would not be ashamed should He come at any moment. I John 

3:3: "Every man that hath this hope in him purifieth himself even as he is pure." Surely this message and attitude of life is needed in the Church today. 

A word of warning! If any of my readers have

taken up this book with the notion that their spiritual fancy will be satisfied and their desire for fads and old-wives' fables be satiated, let me warn them now that they would do well to set the book aside at once. Vagaries, fads, and date-settings have no divine authority and no place in my ministry. 

Satan has caused much harm by such fanaticisms, and we shall hold ourselves strictly to the Word of the Living God. My ministry is based upon that authoritative and divinely inspired Word. He who rejects the inspiration of the Scriptures will of course reject this book. However, in fairness, I do hope such an one will continue further, for I believe he will have his own faith strengthened thereby.

II ITS RELATIONSHIP TO THE MILLENNIUM WILL CHRIST SET UP A LITERAL KINGDOM UPON THE EARTH? 

I-THE EVIDENCE AGAINST THE POST- MILLENNIAL THEORY. 

A EVIDENCE OF JESUS. 

Matthew 13 presents seven parables taught by our Lord, the first of these being the key to the interpretation of the others. Glancing at these briefly, we note that nowhere does He teach that at the time of His coming there will be a condition of universal good or blessedness. In the Tares and 

Wheat He reveals that there will be both good and bad at the time of the end, Satan continuing his work even to the last. 

If we interpret the parable of the Mustard Seed, using the key which He gives in the first, or parable of the Sower, we recognize that the birds which find shelter within the branches of this tremendous outreaching tree, which had its beginning in the small seed, are the agents of the wicked one. This tree represents so-called Christendom. It has grown to almost encompass the world, but its branches shelter many wicked individuals. Have you not heard the expression, "He's a bird"? Who? Some rascal within the Church. Remember, interpret by the key parable, the first. 20

Leaven, in the Scripture and in common usage, has the meaning of corruption. In the Bible the woman (beginning with the first of her race, Mother Eve) is set forth as the introducer of evil.

In the parable it is the woman who injects the leaven into the meal which, as it works, soon corrupts or leavens the whole. Some hold that the "leaven" here means good and not evil. If this were so it would be the one time the word is so used. With this I cannot agree, for I could not place much credence in a teacher who on one day would use a symbol with a meaning of good, and almost the next day use the same symbol with the opposite meaning of evil. In Matthew 16:11, 12 no one can question that our Lord uses leaven as a symbol of corruption when He speaks of the "leaven of the Pharisees and Sadducees." By such interpretation this parable in no way contradicts the others, which it most certainly would if the leaven were given the meaning of good. The final state then would be one of universal goodness and blessedness in contradistinction to that set forth in the Tares and Wheat and again in the Drag-Net. 

We believe that the parable of the Hid Treasure represents a remnant of Israel. Christ having given His all for His own has hid that remnant in a field, which field is the world. We shall speak more of this in a later chapter. Exodus 19:5 reveals God calling Israel His treasure. 

The parable of the Pearl offers a most interesting study, for in every analogy the Church is typified thereby. The pearl is found in the sea; the sea in

Scripture represents nations, and the Church is in

the nations. It has its beginning in a small way and grows through irritation and daily accretion or addition. The Church had its beginning with 120 in an upper room, and the Lord has added daily as many as are being saved, and the true beauty of His Pearl, the Church which is His Body, has been developed through persecution and trouble. 

In the last parable of the Drag-Net, which by the way is not the same as the Tares and Wheat, we find the condition of good and bad; separation and selection at the time of the end. Please note that there must be harmony and not contradiction in the teaching and interpretation of these parables; such is true exegesis.

There are many other places where Jesus teaches this condition of increasing evil upon earth, and the interested reader may study for himself the signs which our Lord said would mark the ending of this age, as recorded in Matthew 24 and Luke 21.

A careful study of II Thessalonians 2:3-12; 

I Timothy 4:1, et seq., and II Timothy 3:1, et seq. will reveal the truth of this statement. James 

5 definitely describes the insatiable desire for riches on the part of men which will mark the ending of this age. Peter, in II Peter 3:3, agrees with his fellow apostles, as does John in his Revelation,

Let me give here a restatement of our case. We believe that Christ will come to take His earthly throne; His feet will stand again on the Mount of

A-THE OLD TESTAMENT EVIDENCE. 

1-It Prophesies a King and Kingdom. 

THE DAVIDIC COVENANT. 

When David would build a house for the Lord, God through the prophet made an inviolate covenant with him. II Samuel 7:16, "Thy throne shall be established forever." This covenant was later renewed with his son, Solomon, at the time when the temple was completed.

Remember that "the gifts and calling of God are without repentance." This was an unconditional covenant given to David, and we find no reason to believe that God will not yet fulfill the same, and set up this Kingdom as promised. 

2-Kingly Prophecies. 

In Isaiah 9:7 we read the matchless prophecy of the coming King, one that we quite readily realize has been partially fulfilled; for indeed His name is to us "Wonderful, Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace." The latter portion of this prophecy, however, must yet be fulfilled. The government has not yet been upon His shoulder. Neither has He ever been privileged to sit upon the throne of David, or to set up this Kingdom, or to establish a universal reign of peace. 

In Zechariah 9:9 we have a prophecy setting forth the fact that the coming Messiah will be the King of Israel. This prophecy was taken up on the day of His so-called triumphant entry-"Behold thy king cometh unto thee . . . lowly and riding upon an ass." Instead of receiving their

King, He was rejected and crucified. However, this despicable act of man does not in any way affect the integrity of God in the carrying out of 

His promises.

In the much maligned book of Daniel the prophet reveals that "In the days of those kings shall the God of heaven set up a kingdom which shall never be destroyed" (Dan. 2:44). He came once to be rejected; He will come again to be accepted and reign.

Therefore, when John heralds the Kingdom he heralds the King. The one whom John proclaims was rejected, for He must first become a spiritual King, and afterward a temporal King. But the fact of this rejection again does not in any way violate God's integrity, and He will still have His King upon the throne of earth.

As I have already shown in the statements which I have set forth regarding the pre-millennial view, very much of Jesus' teaching was given over to that truth which heralded, proclaimed or prophesied a coming Kingdom over which He, Himself, would be

III ITS  I-RE-DEFINING THE CHURCH. 

A-WHAT IS THE CHURCH? 

There is a teaching prevalent today that the Church is found in the Old Testament. We often hear ministers and others referring to the synagogue as the Church. Let us understand immediately that the Church was not known in Old Testament days. Ephesians 3:1-5, "Which in other ages was not made known . . . that the Gentiles should be fellow heirs of the same body," and 9-10 we are distinctly told that "In other ages it was not made known." Colossians 1:26, 27, "Mystery hid from the ages." No one will ever properly evaluate or understand the prophecies in regard to the Church, and especially in its relationship to the second coming of Christ, until they have very definitely discovered this truth-that the Church, the Body of Jesus Christ, is an organism and organization peculiar to this particular age. ...

The first mention of the Church is in the prophecy given by Jesus in Matthew 16:18. You will remember that He has been questioning His disciples as to the opinions which men are setting forth in regard to Himself. Peter has answered and

Jesus, making the question more pointed and per- 33

sonal, now asks: "But whom do you say that I am?" In reply to Peter's confession of his belief in the deity of our Lord, Jesus prophesies: "Upon this rock will I build my church." From this we discover that the Church is something that is to be built in the future from that day in which Jesus speaks.

The Day of Pentecost reveals the beginning of the Church. The interested reader, and the one who would understand, must turn to Acts 2, for this is the record of the birth of the Church. It is the account of the visitation of the Holy Spirit in His fullness upon those who had gathered in the upper room. We are not interested just now with those miraculous signs which accompanied the gift of the Holy Spirit, but more with the accomplishment that the Holy Spirit brought to pass in that day. Two things happened there: (1) They were baptized in the Holy Spirit, this being in fulfillment of the word of Jesus given not very long before, "Ye shall be baptized in the Holy Spirit not many days hence," Acts 1:5. Now, baptism means to immerse in other words to take the object and to plunge that object into the element. This is the recognized meaning of the Greek word baptizo, in : English "baptize." The objects here were the individual believers who had been, up to this time, but disciples, learners or followers of Jesus. I Corinthians 12:13 says: "For by one Spirit are we all baptized into one body." We believe that this refers definitely to the Day of Pentecost wherein the disciples were baptized into the mystical Body of Jesus Christ which we know as the Church. We

also believe that in the moment when a sinner has repented of his sins and turned with appropriating faith to the Lord Jesus that the Holy Spirit baptizes that one into this Body. Some may question this and refer to the fact that on that day the disciples spoke in tongues and seemed to have a display of miraculous power. No one questions this historical fact. (2) Not only do we read that they were baptized in the Holy Spirit, but they were also filled. To be filled is that condition of life which every Christian should continually seek. When we are filled with the Holy 

Spirit, then the power of God must be manifest in our lives. Because they were filled, God could speak in divers languages through them. 

Through the foregoing we quite readily determine that the Church had its beginning on the Day of Pentecost.

Paul, who is the apostle to the Gentiles, and especially to the Church, records many things in regard thereto. His writings are, perhaps, the most instructive in relation to the Church of any which we find in Scripture. This is not because he was any more inspired than any of the other writers, but that God was using Him as an instrument through which He might reveal this message to His own in that day and since. 

In Ephesians 2:15 et seq. he speaks of the "new man." This is not the Christian in his new experience in Christ, but a careful study of the context will show that from both Jew and Gentile the Lord hath made in Himself one new Body, this Body being the Church, the "new man." Ephesians 1:

22, 23 very definitely tells us that "the church is his Body." Other Scriptures that may be referred to are Colossians 1:24; 1 Corinthians 12:20; and

Romans 12:5. 

Another designation for the Church is the Bride of Christ. In the parable of the ten virgins, Jesus teaches that the Bridegroom is to return for the 

Bride. The five wise virgins are those who belong to Christ. Externally they all appear to be in the same condition, but only the wise had oil in their lamps. You will recognize that the oil is always typical of the Holy Spirit. Therefore, only those who belong to the Bride of Christ are in possession of the Holy Spirit. There is a difference which needs to be very carefully noted between profession and possession. Let the one who reads these words be very sure in his own heart that he not only has made a profession of Christ but that he actually possesses Him. Perhaps a word of warning is necessary though, lest someone might become disturbed and begin to seek for some strange, emotional experience in order that he might know that he possesses Christ. You may know beyond any question of a doubt if you will study and believe the promises of God, and in your own heart are certain that you have received Him. Paul in

II Corinthians 11:2 declares to the Church at Cor inth and to the others who are sanctified in Christ that He hath "espoused you to one husband that 

I may present you. . . as a chaste virgin to 

Christ." Surely in the light of these Scriptures no one will question the fact that the Church is the Bride of Christ.

Peter, in Acts 15:14-16, declares that for the first time God has visited the Gentiles "to take out a people for his name." Notice the kindred meaning between the Greek word Lambano, which is used for "take out," and the word Ecclesia, which is the Greek word for "Church" and has the literal meaning "called out." Thus, the people who are being "taken out" in this particular day are being "called out" that they may compose the 

Body and Bride of Jesus Christ. 

But Peter's statement reveals also that the Church is of a transitory nature; that is, it is confined to this present age, particularly between the Day of Pentecost and the time when Jesus Christ shall come again to claim it for Himself. Let every Christian realize that his relationship to Christ is unique and different to a certain degree from that of any other believer in any other age. While all are redeemed, yet the Christian has been redeemed in order that he may become part of this special Body which is to be Christ's own possession, inheritance, and joy throughout eternity.

B-WHAT IS THE RELATIONSHIP OF THE TRUE CHURCH TO CHRISTENDOM? 

Jesus describes so-called Christendom as "the

Kingdom of Heaven," this phrase being peculiar to the gospel of Matthew, which is the gospel of the King. We believe that this represents a period of time during which the King is rejected from earth and must rule His own from heaven. As mentioned in a preceding chapter, the parables of

Matthew 13 are those parables which teach of the Kingdom of Heaven, or the condition of so-called Christendom during this age. While perhaps the terms "Kingdom of Heaven" and "Kingdom of

God" may be used interchangeably in some places, here our Lord is referring very definitely to the Kingdom of Heaven only. It cannot be identical here with the Kingdom of God, as we are told that there will be the presence of evil continually during this period. No one could realize the Kingdom of God with any evil therein. Therefore, we must distinguish and keep these two separate.

That which is called the Church in every-day conversation and usage we might designate as the visible Church. Within this visible Church we are very conscious there are a great many people whose lives are not true and who give no evidence whatever of spiritual life or fellowship with Christ. 

Therefore, the visible Church is not necessarily the true Church. Within the visible Church, however, there is the invisible Body and Bride of Christ, the true Church. It is as though we had a large circle and a smaller circle within that larger one. While we are unable to place any definite alignment or boundaries to this invisible Body, yet we do know that during all the years of this present era there have been thousands and thousands of true believers who have been part of the Body of Jesus Christ, and have been included by the peoples of the world in the organization known as the Church. 

So also we know there has been the inclusion of thousands who have had no spiritual experience. 

It is a church within a church.

II-THE FUTURE OF CHRISTENDOM OR THE KINGDOM OF HEAVEN. 

A-THE DEPARTURE FROM THE FAITH. 

While Christians do not readily agree in the view that spiritually things are growing gradually worse, nevertheless experience and the Word of God prove the truth of this fact. No one would say that we have not advanced in a great many things in our day. Truly "knowledge has increased" even as the prophet Daniel declared, but looking over the situation as we find it in our present day, and recorded in the pages of history, we must admit that beginning very early after the foundation of the Church error and heresy crept in, and these have continued, even unto our day.

In all ages, but particularly in our day, perhaps, there has been a dethronement of Christ as Saviour, Lord, and coming King-this by the very ones oftentimes who profess His name. Sad is the condition that we often find, even in so-called Christian pulpits, wherein men who have been called to the preaching and defense of the gospel of Christ publicly declare their denial of these things. They deem such to be a demonstration of great wisdom and progressiveness, little realizing it has just an opposite result. Several years ago an outstanding liberal preached a sermon on the subject "The 

Peril of Worshiping Jesus." I wonder if he has not hit the nail on the head, for while we would not agree in the interpretation and development of his subject as he preached it that day, yet we do believe that the greatest peril that confronts our age is that peril of worshiping only Jesus-the

humanizing of Jesus Christ, and the separating of 

Jesus and the Christ, robbing Him of His deity, and making Him mere man, and as a natural and logical result of this setting aside all that Scripture declares He is yet to do.

Let me call attention very briefly to some of the evidence that we find to prove the fact that there has been a departure from the faith. Everywhere the designation "Christian Church" is used regardless of what type of organization has been called into existence. It is very interesting to glance at the religious page, perhaps of the New York Times, or some other Saturday morning daily paper, and discover the many organizations that have called themselves a "church." Remembering the primary meaning of the word "Church," we see how wrongly they have taken this designation unto themselves. Most any group that meets on a Sunday is called a church. In II Timothy 3:1-5, Paul very definitely describes this condition: "They have a form of Godliness, but deny the power thereof." There is not a church in the country or in the world wherein some use is not made of the Bible, and yet very little place is given to the authority of this Book, and men are building without the proper foundation. To me it seems neither logical nor honest for any man to take out of the Bible that which he, himself, desires or chooses, and to refuse to take the rest. To such the study of textual criticism gives no justification. Think for a moment-such a one has made himself and his own fallible mind to be the infallible authority. 

This authority belongs to God alone.

The seven letters of the Revelation give to us an unfolding of the spiritual conditions during the Church Age. While we believe that each letter had an immediate message to the particular Church to which it was written, we believe also that it is a prophetic preview of this whole era. A careful reading of the letters with a comparison from history will show how similar conditions have been and how these letters have been fulfilled. Let the reader turn to Revelation 3:14-18, read carefully this description, and ask himself if it does not apply to the spiritual condition of the Church in our day. This is the last letter to the churches. 

Does it thus reveal that we are living in the last days, just prior to the coming? We believe so! 

Immediately following this we find the Lord 

Jesus standing and knocking, not at the door of the individual heart, but at the door of the Church that bears His name, vainly seeking that once again He may obtain entrance. In the very next chapter we read the words: "Come up hither and I will show thee things which must be hereafter." The last reference to the Church has been made until the closing chapters of the Revelation. 

If someone would ask the reason why, may I ask that you wait just a little and in another chapter you will find the answer to your question. Nowhere in the Scripture do we find that God has predicted the Church will become through its own instrumentality and effort a seat of perfection and beauty. Only through its relationship to Jesus Christ and in His coming can this be accomplished.

B-CHURCH FEDERATION. 

The whole trend of present-day Christianity, so-called, or Church development is toward federation or ecclesiastical regimentation. We believe that this is one of the signs that Scripture very definitely teaches and that these things will begin to take place before Jesus Christ comes, but will be completed thereafter. In Revelation 17 we have a picture of the Roman Church. He who is familiar with history will recognize at once that the terrible persecutions and inquisitions have been described herein. Surely God has written history in advance! 

Rome will never come to Protestantism, but perhaps Protestantism may go back to Rome. No, not the Rome of today, for these things will not take place until the true Church, in which will be found both Protestants and Catholics, has been taken out of the world.

Centralization of authority, centralization of denominations and denominational boards and organizations is the cry of our day. The preaching mission had as its primary emphasis the bringing together of all the Protestant denominations. 

Many do not agree with this. I do not, and yet 

I believe that this attempt is very significant in the light of Scripture.

Not only is this condition to be found in the spiritual realm but also in the national, and when we come to another chapter we shall discuss in full the question of the federation of the ten kingdoms set forth in the prophecy of Daniel. Federation of those who will deny Christ, and federation of the Christ-rejecting nations is certain in the future.

C-GOD NEVER LEAVES HIMSELF WITHOUT A WITNESS. 

While the above is true, let us rejoice that always during this age God has His own, and these faithful ones are those who compose His true Church. These are described in Revelation 3:7-13 -the letter to the church at Philadelphia. Those of brotherly love of whom Christ says: "I know thy works. Behold I have set before thee an open door and no man can shut it for thou hast a little strength, and hast kept my word and hast not denied my name." It is for this group that Christ will come again and it is this group that Christ has redeemed through His blood and made His own through His Holy Spirit. Ephesians 5:25, "Christ loved the Church, and gave himself for it; that he might present it to himself a glorious church."

A-THE TWOFOLD Aspect OF CHRIST'S COMING. 

1-He will come first of all for His own in the clouds of heaven.

This is to be a time of gathering out or the time which is referred to in the Book of the Revelation as the first resurrection. In I Corinthians 15, Paul speaks of this, but more particularly in I Thessalonians 4:14-18: "For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so them also which sleep in Jesus will God bring with him. Wherefore comfort one another with these words." Let the reader note that nowhere in this Scripture does it declare that Jesus Christ will come back to earth again, but rather that He will appear in the clouds of heaven. Therefore, we believe that the future of the Church is that "Blessed Hope," the day when suddenly "in the twinkling of an eye" Jesus, Himself, shall come in the clouds of heaven and call those that are upon the earth and alive to be suddenly taken away into 

His presence. 

But we shall not precede those whose bodies have been lying in sleep. Their spirits have gone to be "with him which is far better" and God has given to them a temporary body. Now, in the moment of this coming the first resurrection shall take place; the bodies that sleep shall be raised incorruptible, and their spirits reunited with the resurrected, glorified bodies. Those that are alive shall be suddenly changed into His glory and together with Him His complete Bride shall go in to the presence of the Father. This is the event toward which we continually look. 

2-After this He will come with His saints to earth. 

This is the second aspect of His coming in its relationship to the Church. We find that when He comes to earth the Church will come with Him. 

Jude 14, "The Lord cometh with ten thousands of

his saints." To what else could the words of Jesus in Matthew 24:27-30 allude, wherein He very definitely states that He, the Son of Man, will come again in His glory and power? We must always carefully distinguish between these two aspects of 

His coming.

B-CHRIST COMING FOR THE CHURCH. 

Under the above heading I would like to answer some questions which were set forth in our church calendar and in which people have been greatly interested. 

1-What is God's Purpose for the Church? 

Perhaps a great majority of church members today hold that it is the purpose of God for the 

Church to convert the world. May I say very definitely that nowhere does Scripture declare this to be her function. Jesus said: "Ye shall be witnesses of me," Acts 1:8. And, again, when He gave His great commission it was to "preach the gospel to every creature." Matthew tells us that this commission was threefold: namely, to preach the Gospel, to teach those who were saved, and to baptize them in water, these having already been baptized in the Spirit when they believed, the only means by which they could become part of the 

Body of Christ. This Holy Spirit Baptism is es sential to salvation. Evangelism and world conversion are not necessarily the same. Evangelism or witnessing is the great task of the Church. 

2-Will the Church Convert the World in this Age?

Let us resort to the teachings of Jesus as already mentioned in the parables of Matthew 13. We

find not world conversion at the time of the end, but rather a mixture of good and of evil as represented by the Tares and the Wheat. To this agree all of the epistles, wherein we are never told that the Church will bring all the world in subjection to Jesus Christ. Someone may quote Paul and say, "Does he not declare that 'every tongue shall confess and every knee shall bow'?" Yes, assuredly so, but Paul is looking forward to that day of which we have been speaking, when Christ will come. 

3-Will the Church go Through the Tribulation or 

Any Part Thereof? 

a-FACT OF A TRIBULATION. 

That there will be a period of time on earth in which the peoples of earth shall pass through a condition of tribulation such as has never been known before is the clear teaching of Scripture. In 

Matthew 24:21 Jesus teaches: "Then shall be great tribulation such as was not since the beginning of the world to this time, no, nor ever shall be." There have been those who would tell us that this Scripture was fulfilled in 70 A. D. when Titus, the Roman, took the city of Jerusalem and caused great suffering to the citizens thereof, and destroyed the city. But surely this cannot be the fact, because since that time the world has seen far greater suffering than was evidenced in that day. No, the 

Great Tribulation is still in the future.

A brief reference should be made here to the prophecy of Daniel 9 in which we hear the message of the seventy weeks that have been determined

upon the Jewish people. I shall not now go into the explanation of the meaning of the term "seventy weeks," because we shall take that up under our study of the nations. However, we believe the seventieth week of this prophecy, a period of seven years, has been unfulfilled and will be fulfilled during this period of the Great Tribulation. Other 

Scriptures that refer to this period are Jeremiah 

30:7 and 31:16; also, Matthew 24:15-22. 

b-THE CHURCH WILL NOT GO THROUGH THE TRIBU- LATION. 

I do not believe that the tribulation period can be fully ushered in as long as the Church is here upon earth. II Thessalonians 2:1-8 speaks of the ministry of the Holy Spirit as He restrains evil.

Let us remember that the Holy Spirit, during this age, dwells in believers or in the Church composed of individual believers. Therefore, when the

Church has been taken out of the world, the Holy Spirit will be taken with her and at that time the restraint will be taken away and Satan and evil will have full sway upon the earth.

Again, if the Church is represented (in parable) by the five wise virgins who are the only true believers, we must conclude from the fact that all five were taken in with the Bridegroom to the marriage feast it is revealed to us that everyone truly born from above, possessing that which the oil typifies, namely, the Holy Spirit, will be included when Christ comes for His own. 

I believe that Paul in I Thessalonians 5:9 very definitely teaches that all true believers will be saved out of this condition of suffering and wrath

4. 

upon the earth: "God hath not appointed us to wrath, but to obtain salvation by our Lord, Jesus

Christ." In our previous reference to Revelation 

3:7 and God's statement to the Church at Philadelphia you will remember that He said: "I have set before thee an open door." This is not only an open door of service, but also the door which is shown in Revelation 4:1-an open door in heaven through which the Bride of Christ will be taken into the presence of the Father. And again in 

Revelation 3:10 the promise is "I will keep thee from the hour of trial which shall come upon all the world." From Revelation 3:22 to Revelation 

22:16 there is no mention of the Church whatever. 

Why? The answer is, because all the intervening chapters tell of the condition on earth during the period of the tribulation-the Church is not present, but in heaven with her Lord; therefore no reference could be made to her. 

4-Will the Complete Church have Part in the Rapture?

Some Christians hold the view that only those who are living in actual purity of life and fellowship in Christ will be taken when He comes-those who are looking for Him and expecting Him. The 

Scripture upon which they base this is Hebrews 

9:28, wherein we find the description of Jesus, our

Great High Priest, now present within the Veil and at the right hand of the Father, where He has made an atonement for us His people. Then the declaration is "And unto them that look for him shall he appear a second time without sin unto salvation."

If you will look in the margin of your Bible you will find that the better translation is "apart from sin unto salvation." The Spirit of God is speaking through the writer and drawing the comparison between the Aaronic Priesthood and the Priesthood of Jesus. When the priest would enter in on the 

Day of Atonement to make atonement for the people, he made it for all of the people of Israel; during the period while he was within the second Veil, the people were waiting on the outside for his return. This is a picture of the true Church waiting for her High Priest to return from that place where He now is, within the Veil in the very presence of God. The first time He came to settle the question of sin. This time He comes without any relation to sin at all-"apart from sin unto salvation."

This is the completion of the salvation that has been given to the believer, and consummated in 

His coming for His own.

I believe that all of the Church, then living upon earth and likewise those who have gone to be with our Lord, will have part in the Rapture. Any other view would make salvation a thing of works, and grace eliminates all work. Romans 4:5 and Ephesians 2:8. To say that those who are not actually engaged in looking for or expecting the Lord, although saved, will be left behind is to diminish the work of Christ. To detract from His power to redeem eternally all who believe, seems nothing short of blasphemy. Then again, as there are none living with enough personal righteousness to justify their being taken when He comes, this would exclude all the living; there would be none living to

whom He could come. I John 1:10 "If we say that we have not sin we make him a liar." No, there is none of us who is righteous, none of us worthy to be included in that group, and our only reason for expectancy is simply because of the worthiness of Christ and His promise.

Our Lord is no respecter of persons. He could not be unfair and He could not give Himself unto what we might designate as class legislation. Men of earth do not believe in this and could the God of Heaven? Yet, my dear friends, I Thessalonians 

4:14 tells us that "all who sleep in Jesus will God bring with him." All, without any exceptions, of those who have gone from the scenes of this earth into His presence. Is it logical to believe that all of these died in an attitude of expectancy and looking for Him, or that they died with enough personal righteousness to justify their immediate translation into His presence? Of course everyone who knows and understands will answer "No."

Therefore, would it seem fair of God if all these were included in that day and that He leave behind those whose only misfortune happened to be that they had not died physically before the coming of this blessed event? If He includes one He must include the other, and we believe that all who have believed upon Him in sincerity and truth and have passed from death unto life will be included in that day.

IV-THE NATURE AND TIME OF THE RAPTURE OR COMING FOR THE CHURCH. 

A-ITS NATURE. 

When He comes in the clouds of heaven for

His Church it will be secretly, quietly, and unknown to the unbelieving world until the whole thing has transpired and taken place. I Corinthians 15:51-52-"Behold, I shew you a mystery; we shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed. 

In a moment, in the twinkling of an eye at the last trump: for the trumpet shall sound, and the dead shall be raised incorruptible, and we shall be changed." In the twinkling of an eye most certainly reveals the suddenness with which this blessed event will take place. We shall be changed -yes, physically-the change spiritually having taken place already when we believed, for we then became "new creations in Christ Jesus." "Sleep" in the Scripture refers to the condition of the body during the period after physical death has entered in and the spirit has been released to go into the presence of the Lord. It is in this way that both the bodies that have been sleeping in the earth and the bodies of those "who are alive and remain" and have not yet passed into physical death, that the change shall take place.

B-THE TIME. 

While no man knows the very day nor the hour when this will occur, our Lord has given us signs which are to be observed and we are to govern ourselves accordingly. This is not date setting, and we find no justification for that sort of interpretation. The discourse of Matthew 24 clearly sets forth those things which shall take place upon the earth immediately preceding this day. In I Thessalonians 5:4-5 Paul tells us that we are not chil-

dren of the night that we should be in darkness, but we are the children of light in order that we might understand and know as the Day approaches. 

While we cannot know the day nor the hour, and we do not wish to know, yet we do believe that these things very definitely foretell the near approach of this phase of His coming. 

C-THE REsult. 

It is quite easy to determine the inescapable result of His coming for His own. There will be a separation as with the tares and the wheat, or the good and the bad fish which have been taken in the drag-net. Matthew 24:40-"Then shall two be in the field; the one shall be taken, and the other left. 

Two women shall be grinding at the mill; the one shall be taken and the other left." Luke 17:34— "Two men shall be in one bed; the one shall be taken, and the other shall be left." Notice that in the setting forth of these three illustrations our

Lord has encircled the globe as to time, for while it is morning in one place it is evening in the other. 

Women grind at the mill in the morning hours; men work in the field during the day, and people sleep in a bed at night. Thus, while it may be daytime here it would be night somewhere else. 

Our Lord will include peoples of all nations and all tongues in that day. What a terrible moment of separation it will be when those who have rejected Christ and are left behind suddenly realize this terrible separation, and that the Word so often spoken and so lightly thrust aside has been proven true in actual experience. Let the one who reads

take care that, should our Lord come at the very moment of the reading, he would be taken to be with Him and not only he, himself, but also his loved ones and many others for whom Christ died.

V-THE CONDITION OR PLACE OF THE CHURCH AFTER THE RAPTURE. 

In Chapter 7 I shall deal with the subjects that fall under this particular discussion, but may I here set forth those things that Scripture teaches will take place in relation to the Church immediately after this event? The Church will be taken to be with Him. I Thessalonians 4:14. We shall be taken to His judgment seat, commonly called 

"the Judgment Seat of Christ." I Corinthians 5:10 

"For we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ." I Corinthians 3:13-15-"Every man's work shall be made manifest: for the day shall declare it, because it shall be revealed by fire; and the fire shall try every man's work of what sort it is." Thus, the Christian shall be taken away to the place of his only judgment. This is not a judgment for sin but a judgment for life and work, and results not in eternal banishment from the presence of the Lord but in reward or loss thereof.

From the judgment seat of Christ we shall be taken to the marriage altar of the Lamb. Revelation 19:7-9-"Let us be glad and rejoice, and give honour to him, for the marriage of the Lamb is come, and his wife hath made herself ready." This is the moment when our Lord will present His Bride unto Himself as a creation of beauty and perfection. Paul has described this in Ephesians 5:25-27.

We believe that by this time the seven years of tribulation on earth will have almost expired and now we shall come in glory with Jesus Christ as He returns visibly and actually to the earth a second time. He comes not in humility as He came the first time, but in glory, that He may subdue His enemies, ascend His throne, and reign. Colossians 3:4 "When Christ, who is our life, shall appear, then shall ye also appear with him in glory." We shall be associated with Him in His rule of the age to come. Just what the nature of our work shall be I do not think Scripture clearly unfolds. Let us reserve any further discussion of these things until we enter into the chapter which shall be devoted to this particular aspect of our 

Lord's return. 

I hope and pray each one of you who reads this chapter and who is a child of God and a member of that true Church will recognize this aspect of the coming of Christ in its relationship to you. So many people do not understand, and the Second 

Coming means fear and dread for them. They do not like to hear its message and yet, dear friends, this is the "Blessed Hope" for which you and I have been saved and toward which we should be continually looking. There is no other hope revealed in the word of Scripture for the Church but the hope that is given to us in this glorious truth.

IV ITS  I-THE WORD OF GOD OUR BASIS. 

In studying the question which presents itself in the title which I have chosen for this chapter, it will be our purpose to find our authority in the Word of God alone. A question like this cannot be answered in the writings of men. Therefore, we turn to the Bible as the one and only source. 

The apostle Peter, who had that wonderful experience of being with the Lord on the Mount of Transfiguration, has told us that "We have a more sure word of prophecy" than even the word which was spoken on the Mount. No man or woman who professes to believe in the Bible can afford to neglect this word of prophecy in his or her own study.

The particular text book from which we shall make the greater portion of our study is the much maligned and rejected book of Daniel. The critics have been most unkind to this particular book of Scripture and many have rejected it in their own particular view of Holy Writ. At present I am not interested in the critical aspect and study of the book, and its authorship, but in the message and prophecies which are unfolded in it. The accurate prophecies contained there and fulfilled in the 55

history of the past must forever stop the mouths of the critics as to the question of a Divine imprint thereon. No pious Jew, no compilation of tradition can account for these wonderful and accurate writings of history in advance. 

Perhaps no further word of defense is necessary than to say that our Lord, Himself, recognized its authenticity. We do not believe that Jesus would use fictitious characters in the teaching of the great truths which He taught. We find Him referring to the words spoken by Daniel the Prophet. The most reliable contemporary historian of Jesus' day,

Josephus, speaks of this book and the prophecies that are contained there. The Church fathers rec ognized it and its message. For the one who would know God's message in prophecy, this book of Daniel must be accepted as part of His great infallible revelation. However, if my reader is not quite satisfied to accept that view, may I pray your indulgence and request that you continue in this chapter before making your final decision. Perhaps when you have finished the reading and studying thereof, you may find yourself with a different view, not because of anything that the author shall say, but simply because of your recognition of the inescapable inbreathing and inspiration of the Holy Spirit on the prophecies contained therein.

For many centuries the message of Daniel has been closed, and the majority of teachers of God's Word have entirely ignored it, except for the aforementioned critical study. We believe this is the purpose of God, for in Daniel 12:4 we read these

words: "Shut up the words, and seal the book, even to the time of the end; many shall run to and fro, and knowledge shall be increased." Is not God's purpose revealed in these words to the prophet? 

Do they not indicate that the message of this book is to be revealed only in the last days of the present era in which you and I now live? How accurate is the prophecy of the last phrase! II-A BRIEF BIBLICAL AND HISTORICAL PICTURE OF THE NATIONS WITH WHICH WE ARE CONCERNED. 

A-THE FIRST NATION WITH WHICH GOD DEALS. 

In Genesis 12, God begins to deal with a particular people or nation. Until this time He has been dealing with mankind as a whole. Now he calls

Abram that through him and his seed He may reveal His message to the other nations of the world. 

From Genesis 12 to II Chronicles 36, when the tribe of Judah is finally taken under Nebuchadnezzar, we find God dealing primarily with Israel. 

In a later chapter we shall go more definitely into the whole question as to why God called Abram and why Israel are the chosen people of God. Suffice it to say for our present study that God at this time begins to deal with the world through Israel. 

B-WITH THE FINAL CAPTIVITY GOD BEGINS A NEW CONDITION. 

Often in Scripture we hear the phrase "Times of the Gentiles." The times of the Gentiles began when 

Israel had been finally taken captive and God could no longer deal through His chosen people. This is made quite plain from the statement of Jesus in 

Luke 21:24, "Jerusalem shall be trodden down of the Gentiles until the time of the Gentiles be fulfilled." History proves that Gentile dominion began at the time of the final captivity and has continued until this present day. We believe that it will continue until Christ comes again, at which time the "Times of the Gentiles" will have been fulfilled and come to an end. However, let it be clearly understood that God has not cast aside

Israel by the world government of Gentiles during this period. He still has His hand upon them. As we shall see very soon, God is only interested in prophecy in those nations that come in contact with this people.

III-THE HISTORY OF WORLD DOMINION IN PROPHECY. 

As mentioned above, God in these prophecies only deals with nations that somewhere in their history come in contact with Israel and her land. All the Bible prophecy centers in and around this people and their land-the land that God promised unto Abraham, which extends from the River Euphrates on the north to the River Nile on the south. 

In Daniel 2 we read of Nebuchadnezzar's dream. 

The king dreamed a dream, but when he awoke was unable to remember what it was he had seen in his vision. Finally Daniel, the Jew, whom God had preserved and kept, being filled with the Holy Spirit, tells the king just what he saw in the vision, and also gives the interpretation, at the same time making it very plain that the interpretation is not from himself, but from his God. There can be no questioning as to the meaning of the vision, for 

Daniel very accurately interprets the matter. Daniel 2:31-45-"Thou, oh king, sawest and behold a great image. This great image, whose brightness was excellent, stood before thee; and the form thereof was terrible. This image's head was of fine gold, his breast and his arms of silver, his belly and his thighs of brass, his legs of iron, his feet part of iron and part of clay." Visualize, if you can, this image of a man with the four divi-

sions as Daniel has revealed it. This represents four great world dominions which began with Nebuchadnezzar and are to continue during the times of the Gentiles. The student of history will at once recognize that there have been only four great world dominions and that these are mentioned here in this prophecy. Let us come then to the interpretation that Daniel gives.

A-BABYLON-HEAD OF GOLD. 

Daniel 2:36-37-"Thou, oh king, art a king of kings for the God of heaven hath given thee a kingdom, power, and strength, and glory. And wheresoever the children of men dwell, the beasts of the field and the fowls of the heaven hath he given into thine hand, and hath made thee ruler over them all. Thou art this head of gold."

Referring to the history of Babylon we find this to be the first Gentile world empire, all of the lands of the then known world being embraced within its border and under its government. The approximate dates of this empire are 606 to 538 B. C. The type of government under the kings, beginning with 

Nebuchadnezzar, perhaps reveals the highest type of world government that history knows anything about, this being proved not only by the facts recorded in history, but also by the vision which Nebuchadnezzar receives in that his government was represented by gold, the highest metallic substance. But this dominion was to come to en end, and in Daniel 5 we read the story of the fall thereof. 

Belshazzar has made a great feast for his lords and after they have drunken to the full, we are told that

"in that night was Belshazzar, King of the Chaldeans, slain." It is interesting to note that archæology has verified this record. In 539 B. C. the city was besieged by Cyrus, the Persian king. During the very night of the feasting the besiegers entered in under the gates of the city through the dried up river bed and took the king in the midst of his revelry, slew him, and established their own kingdom.

It may be well for the reader to turn to Daniel 7, wherein we find practically the same revelation given to Daniel himself, this time under another vision. In this vision, Babylon is represented by the lion, the strongest of all animals, the monarch of the animal kingdom, even as Babylon was of the human kingdom.

B-MEDO-PERSIAN. 

Daniel 2:39—"And after thee shall arise another kingdom inferior to thee." In the image the breast and arms were of silver, and silver is inferior to gold. Therefore, Daniel received the message that this particular kingdom would be inferior to the first. 

The second world kingdom was one, but composed of two distinct parts or nationalities—the Medes and the Persians. In this vision we find that there are two parts of the image included, namely, the breast and the arms-thus revealing in accurate prophecy the very nature or twofold division of the subsequent kingdom. This kingdom began in 538 B. C. and ended in 330 B. C. The 

Medo-Persian kingdom fell and was conquered by Alexander the Great, the thirty-year-old son of Philip. The decisive battle was fought at Gauga-

mela in 331 B. C. Death overtook Alexander in his thirty-third year. It has often been said that the young conqueror died of a broken heart because there were no new worlds to conquer. Thus we see in history the fulfillment of the prophecy as given by Daniel in its relationship to this second world empire. 

C-THE GRECIAN EMPIRE. 

Daniel 2:32 "His belly and thighs of brass."

In verse 39 Daniel very definitely interprets this portion of the vision, "And another third kingdom of brass, which shall bear rule over all the earth."

No successor of Alexander was able to hold the vast empire which stretched from Asia Minor to India. After his death it gradually emerged into three divisions-Ptolemies ruling over Egypt, Seleucids ruling in Asia, and Antigonies in the Balkans with the generals of Alexander in power. 

Note how this corresponds to the three parts mentioned in the vision, the belly and the two thighs. 

This Grecian kingdom extended from 330 B. C. to approximately 31 B. C., although the Roman Conquest was felt a great many years before this latter date. The final effort to preserve Greek political integrity was attempted by Cleopatra with the support of Mark Antony, but this effort failed and the decisive battle of Actium in 31 B. c. brought an end to this dominion, it being finally subdued by Rome on this date. In Daniel 7 this empire is revealed by the prophet as a leopard. 

D-ROME. 

It is very difficult to place specific dates as to the beginning of this Roman world dominion, for the

invasions and conquests were gradual. Rome began her conquest of the Mediterranean in 265 B. C. and completed it in 31 B. C. as we have already seen. We read in Daniel 2:33-"His legs of iron, his feet part of iron and part of clay," and in verses 

40 through 43, "And the fourth kingdom shall be strong as iron; forasmuch as iron breaketh in pieces and subdueth all things; and as iron that breaketh all these, shall it break in pieces and bruise. Notice the characteristics of this particular part of the vision. The rule of Rome has always been as of iron. It has broken and bruised all of those who would seek to stand against it. Never has the world witnessed any more tyrannical government than is revealed in the history of this empire. Again, note the two divisions of the empire as represented by the two legs of iron. History fulfilled this in the division of the eastern and western portion of the empire, the eastern having its capital in Constantinople, and the western in Rome. Rome's universal empire ended about 

476 A. D. and from that day to the present date there has not been any further world empire. Do

Genesis 12:2-"In thee shall all families of the earth be blessed." Here is God's promise of a coming man who shall be born of the seed of Abram, through whom all families on earth shall be blessed. This is a very definite prophecy as to 

Jesus Christ. No one who is informed will deny this, and the verification of this fact will be found in Galations 3:16, "He saith not, 'And to seeds,' as of many; but as of one, 'And to thy seed,' which is Christ." Godly Jews. Christians today owe a debt which can never be paid to the Jewish nation for the marvelous skill and integrity which these men of old

Exodus 20:3. "Ye are my witnesses saith the 

Lord, that I am God"-Isaiah 43:12. "They shall declare my glory among the Gentiles"-Isaiah 66:19. Looking back over the history of Israel we discover the sad fact that, as far as this purpose is concerned, they have failed God and humanity.

2-The Testimony of Jesus. 

Matthew 24:32-"Now learn a parable of the fig tree; when his branch is yet tender, and putteth forth leaves, ye know that summer is nigh; so likewise ye, when ye shall see all these things, know that it is near, even at the doors. Verily I say unto you, this generation shall not pass, till all

these things be fulfilled." The things which are to be fulfilled are the signs that He has given of the ending of the age, and of His coming. It has been quite universally agreed by Bible students at all times that the fig tree is used here emblematically of the nation Israel. The choosing of emblems from nature is a very common practice, for the Scotch have their thistle and the Canadians have their maple leaf. The Lord uses the analogy that when the fig tree begins to branch and put forth leaves, we know that summer is nigh; and when we see the nation Israel being revived and restored unto their own land we know the day of His coming is drawing very near. The other prophecy contained in the statement of Jesus is "This generation shall not pass away until all these things be fulfilled." Critics have said this only means the generation of people living in that day, and that this prophecy was fulfilled in 70 A. D. when Jerusalem was destroyed by Titus. There are many reasons why we believe that this is not the case. 

The conditions which the rest of the Scripture says will be apparent in the day of the fulfillment of this prophecy were not present in the destruction by Titus. Immediately following this prophecy He tells us that we shall see "The coming of the Son of Man in power and great glory." This did not take place at that time. Yet it is an integral part of this prophecy and the whole prophecy must stand or fall together.

More than this is the significance of the Greek word Genera which is translated "generation."

According to Thayer in his Greek lexicon, this

word has the meaning of a race of people or a particular stock. Because Schofield in his notes has made this statement some of the critics have rather belittled it and assigned it to the fruit of his mind. 

While I am not offering a defense for Schofield and his notes, although I do believe them to be very helpful and instructive, I say that this sort of criticism is unfair to the entire teaching of God's Word. Much of so-called modern scholarship sets its own standard upon its ability to doubt. There is no greater authority in Greek lexicography than Thayer, and if he gives this interpretation, in all fairness we should at least be willing to recognize the possibility thereof. Taking this meaning of the word we find that our Lord is saying that this particular people will not be destroyed or assimilated by the nations until His purpose in them has been fulfilled and He, Himself, shall come. The wonderful, miraculous preservation of the Jewish people is one of the outstanding demonstrations of God's power.

Let me call your attention to some very interesting facts and events which took place during the days of the war. 

On November 2, 1917, Mr. Arthur J. Balfour, Secretary of Foreign Affairs in Great Britain, wrote to Lord Rothschild stating that Britain was in full sympathy with Zionism, and the government was in favor of establishing Palestine as a homeland for the Jews. Remember that during all of the years preceding this, Palestine had been under the control of people who refused to recognize any such view or take any such attitude. They were the enemies of all things Jewish. History tells us that on December 9, 1917, a little over one month

from the date of the foregoing declaration, General Allenby marched into the city of Jerusalem without a single shot being fired or a single drop of blood being shed.

It is interesting to note that the English in the beginning had sought to force their way in through 

Asia Minor. Their ships lay off the Dardanelles.

Attempt after attempt was made to land the soldiers, but each attempt resulted in a slaughter and failure. Finally, under cover of darkness, the English evacuated the Dardanelles and removed their troops to Egypt. Here they set off on a campaign over the Egyptian Peninsula, following the roads that Moses traveled as he led the children of Israel from Egypt back into their own land. I think one must be convinced that God's purpose was being accomplished here, for immediately we find the victory becoming theirs.

The story of this campaign is fascinating, but for our study it is sufficient to call attention to the fact that the land was taken and the city once again in the hands of those who were friendly to the Jewish people. When the British people, through their duly authorized Secretary of Foreign Affairs, expressed a favorable attitude toward Palestine and the Jew, God immediately responded and gave them the victory and the deliverance of the land.

For the first time since the captivity, the land was back under the control of a people who recognized that it was theirs only for the Jew. When the committee, made up of Jews, was sent to Palestine, their instructions from the British government were that they should do all in their power to carry out

VII ITS  I-A NECESSARY RE-DEFINING OF TERMS. 

Before we can proceed with the study of this chapter it is essential that we re-define the terms that we shall employ. Perhaps there is no word more misused than the word "Christian," especially as it is applied to people in our day. A lawyer friend told of having a client come in for advice. It happened to be a question on marital difficulties. Before he proceeded to give any advice he asked the young lady whether she was a Christian or not. She replied that she was, but he did not seem to be satisfied with the statement. 

Therefore he cross examined her a little as to her Christian experience and soon discovered that her idea of a Christian was a person that was not a Jew. I am afraid that this view is quite prevalent today.

Again there are those who think they are Christians merely because they are nominally affiliated with some so-called Christian organization or church. To clear our own thinking and properly evaluate the important study before us, let me re-define the term as I believe the Scripture states it. Who is a Christian?

A-PRIMARILY A QUESTION OF RELATIONSHIP. 

John 1:11-"He came unto his own, and his 112

own received him not, but as many as received him, to them gave he power to become the sons of God even to them that believe on his name: which were born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God." 

Here is a very definite declaration of the relationship which is absolutely fundamental to the 

Christian life.

While a great many teach today the so-called Brotherhood of man and Fatherhood of God, we do not believe that this is substantiated by the 

Scriptures. Let me say at once that I do believe that all men are brothers in creation and that God is their Father in creation, but we are talking about a spiritual and not a physical relationship. That man through his sin estranged himself from the family of God is the clear teaching of Scripture. 

While many may seek to deny this, yet the fact remains unchanged. It is one thing when we are talking about our fellowmen as our creative brothers and our responsibility towards them; to this all will agree. However, it is an entirely different thing when we say that all men are members of the immediate spiritual family of God regardless of the question of their own personal salvation. 

If this last be true, then the whole teaching of 

Scripture is erroneous. Through sin the estrangement came and in the estrangement the family relationship was broken. Jesus Christ came into the world that He might re-establish this broken link, or as He Himself says, that He might become "the Way" back into the family of God. Salvation comes only by grace, that is, unmerited favor.

God has given humanity every opportunity to prove by their own merit, if any there should be, that this broken relationship may be regained or restored. The whole history of civilization, both profane and religious, is one of complete failure. 

Perhaps this complete failure is demonstrated in its most pronounced outworking in the rejection and crucifixion of the Son of God. 

The simple and understandable declaration of the above Scripture is that in Jesus Christ and one's own personal acceptance of Him the relationship is restored and we become the sons of God in the unique sense in which Jesus speaks. 

In the light of this teaching one can readily see or detect the error which is involved when people claim to be Christians without this personal, individual, and objective relationship in Him. 

B-THE CHRISTIAN AND THE CHURCH. 

The true Church is the family of God on earth and is made up of those individuals who have become, through the experience referred to in the previous paragraph, the sons of God. It is more than a mere ecclesiastical organization. It is more than any denominational affiliation. All of these may be included therein, but the Church of Jesus 

Christ is "His Body"-Ephesians 1:23-composed of all those who have truly believed upon Him and, as seen in another chapter, received from God in response to this act of faith the quickening and regenerating power of the Holy Spirit. With this gift they become new creations in Christ and are baptized by the Holy Spirit into His mystical Body

which is the Church. Thus, we see that the relationship necessary to being a Christian and a member of the Church is not primarily a question of relationship with the physical organization called the Church, but of relationship to Christ. 

Far be it from me to belittle or diminish the need imperative on all those who have accepted Jesus Christ of becoming members of this physical church. The means that He has provided for the carrying on of His work is through the groups or assemblies of believers who are banded together that they may worship Him and spread His Gospel. The Scripture refers to these as the Church, the word being used interchangeably with both of its meanings. However, here we are primarily interested in its use as it applies to the true body of 

Jesus Christ.

With these re-definitions of the meaning of the word Christian and the Church, we may proceed now to the further study of this chapter.

It would be impossible for us to enter into that heavenly kingdom or realm without this essential change. Somewhere in the preceding pages we mentioned the fact that redemption of the body is primarily future. When we believed we were quickened or resurrected spiritually, "We passed from death unto life." But this redemption could not be complete unless it also included the physical. Thank God it does include this, and the resurrection of the body is an integral part of a complete salvation.

I recall an instance in my pastorate in Philadelphia. The aged mother of one of my faithful trustees had passed away. She was born on one of the West Indian islands, and no record of the Idate of her birth was available. As near as she could remember she was well over eighty. I re-

member being summoned one day to the home.

It was a hot Philadelphia summer day and any one who has lived in Philadelphia will know what this description involves. As I went to the room where she was confined, and stood by her bed, I shall never forget my shock. Her frail little body was simply covered with a sheet and, as I looked upon her lying there, all that I could think of was a skeleton. It hardly seemed possible that one could become so emaciated physically and yet exist. A few days afterwards she passed into the presence of her Lord, and I remember the scene at the grave. The devoted son stood there with tear-stained cheeks and sobbing breath as the casket was lowered. I walked over to where he was and, putting my arm around his shoulder, drew him toward me, saying: "Billy, remember that when you see your mother again it will not be in the frailty in which you have laid her away. Lord." How his countenance brightened, as he replied: "Yes, pastor, until He come, and thank 

God, when He does I shall see her in His glory."

Not only will the physical be involved in this sudden change, but also the spiritual. While this refers to the physical, I believe that it refers equally to the spiritual. It is an utter impossibility for any man or woman to enter into the fullness of that

spiritual life, which is the Christ life, while forced to dwell in this frail and weak body. Please understand me, while I believe that none of us have ever entered into the fullness of that experience, nevertheless, I for one am continually seeking to do so, and to lead others into this blessed and complete experience in Christ. The goal is there and we must ever strive toward it, at the same time realizing that the possibility of the full reality of the experience can only be when we have put on our glorified body, and have done away with the present hindrance. What a blessed moment that will be when clothed in an immortal body, resplendent with His glory, we shall enter into the full and blessed experience of eternal life in Jesus Christ. All this will be ours in that day!

A-THE JUDGMENT SEAT OF CHRIST. 

1-The Place of the Christian's Judgment. 

He who really understands salvation knows that he will never be judged for sin. "There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in 

Christ Jesus"-Romans 8:1. The sin question

was finally settled at Calvary. When Jesus Christ gave Himself in death unto sin He bore the sin of humanity, past, present, and future. Sin was nailed in judgment to the Cross-tree. However, the Christian does not escape judgment, for he shall be judged by Jesus Christ on the question of life. Thus, immediately after he has been translated from the earth he will be taken to the judg ment seat of Christ. II Corinthians 5:10-"We must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ." This judgment has to do with Christians only and not with an unbelieving world. In another chapter we will discuss the judgment of the 

Great White Throne. Suffice it to say now that that judgment in no way relates itself to the Christian. I remember a young man giving a testimony in prayer meeting, in which he told of a friend who, bereaved through the loss of a loved one, received a letter from one who would comfort and console. In the letter the friend said: "Remember, you will meet him again at the Great White Throne." The one who had passed away was a dear Christian. It would not be much comfort to know that that one would stand judgment on that day and that they would meet again there. Therefore, let all Christians know that the Great White 

Throne is not for them, but for the unsaved. 

2-The Nature of the Judgment. 

So many times earnest Christian people have approached me and presented the question of the behavior and life of some other Christian. They have sometimes wondered how eternal life could

continue to be the possession of that one who seemed to be crucifying the Lord anew. The error of their view was that they did not fully understand the relationship that had been established when that person accepted Jesus Christ. It is this relationship which brings one into the family of God and makes him a son of God in Jesus Christ. 

I have always tried to explain that those who so separate themselves from fellowship with Christ, while not losing sonship, are indelibly marking themselves with loss at the judgment of that day as well as lost blessing now. I Corinthians 3:1215 tells us: "Now if any man build upon this foundation, gold, silver, precious stone, wood, hay, stubble; every man's work shall be made manifest: for the day shall declare it, because it shall be revealed by fire; and the fire shall try every man's work of what sort it is. If any man's work abide which he hath built thereupon, he shall receive a reward. If any man's work shall be burned, he shall suffer loss: but he himself shall be saved; yet so as by fire." Surely this reveals the nature and severity of the judgment in that day. Christians may feel they are hiding their sinful life from their fellow beings, but let them be assured that they cannot hide it from God. If sin is not uncovered here upon earth, it will be in heaven in that day. 

3-The Result of Such Judgment. 

While we believe the teaching of Scripture to be that eternal life can never be lost or broken, and that the work of Jesus Christ in redemption was and is absolutely sufficient, yet we also believe that

those who have received it may so ignore the privilege and blessing thereof in present experience, that they themselves shall be the losers-not losers of the life, but losers of reward and blessing. I 

Corinthians 3:14-"If any man's work shall abide that he has built thereupon, he shall receive a reward. If any man's work shall be burned he shall suffer loss." That the Lord Jesus has promised reward for those who are faithful is an undeniable fact. I do not believe that any true child of His serves Him with this motive-"We love him because he first loved us." However, reward is part of His promised program. If there are rewards, there must also be losses, and this is the clear teaching in regard to this judgment. Let me say simply that a person who is a Christian can so live and serve Christ that he may be sure of receiving that crown of reward in that day. So may one, having received Jesus Christ in redemption, so neglect the Life that has been given, and the privileges which are his to serve Christ upon earth, that in that day he will receive no reward at all. One of the greatest anticipations of my life is to look forward toward the day when perhaps by His grace and the effort I have been able to put forth in the use of the powers He has given me, I may hear Him say, "Well done, good and faithful servant, thou hast been faithful over a few things, I will make thee ruler over many things, enter thou into the joy of thy Lord." 

Christian friends take heed, be sure you are living in fellowship with Him, and making every moment count in His service, remembering that

service is also purity and loveliness of life as well as the mere oral testimony of lips.

How wonderful that you, dear reader, if you are a child of God, can look forward to that blessed and happy event when together we shall stand at the marriage altar wedded to Him and then sit at His banqueting table and partake of the marriage feast.

V-THE GREATEST RELATIONSHIP OF THIS DOCTRINE TO THE CHRISTIAN. 

Let me speak a word of necessary warning here.

It is one thing to know the theoretical truth of the coming of the Lord and quite another to know it by experience. One may be thoroughly premillennial and yet know nothing of the attitude of waiting for His Son from heaven. 

I Thessalonians 1:9-10-"Ye turn to God from idols to serve the living and true God and wait for his Son from heaven, whom he raised from the dead, even Jesus, who hath delivered us from the wrath to come." It is this condition of expectant waiting that God has linked to this truth so that it may become the greatest incentive to true Christianity of any message contained in the Word. 

There are those who have condemned the teaching of this because they have said that it leads to a condition of contentment and spiritual lethargy. 

How opposite is the case when one really visualizes this event as God has set it forth in Scripture! 

Let me call your attention to the great relationship of this doctrine to the Christian in the varying aspects of the Christian life which it makes real. 

A-INCENTIVE TO PURITY. 

That no person can truly believe in the Lord's return and continue in a life of sin and immorality

is a foregone conclusion. God's Word very definitely declares this. I John 3:2, 3-"Everyone that hath this hope in him purifieth himself even as he, himself, is pure." It is impossible for one to continue in a life of sin and also to be waiting for His Son from heaven. If there is anything that the Church today needs, it is a purging and a practical and real holiness in experience. Could any other truth bring this result more quickly? Yet it is practically precluded from the majority of our so-called Christian pulpits.

We heard the other day of a supposedly conservative evangelical pastor who, when making preparation for an evangelistic meeting in his church, said to the evangelist, "None of that prophetic stuff." Let the preacher and the people in the pews get a fresh vision of this truth, and we shall find that the reality of life will be forthcoming. 

B-INCENTIVE TO TRUE LOVE. 

Perhaps one of the saddest facts in regard to the Christian Church today is that it is often torn asunder by hatred and petty jealousy. Here is 

God's cure for all such. I Thessalonians 3:12-13 "The Lord make you to increase and abound in love one toward another and toward all men, even as we also do toward you, to the end that he may establish your heart unblamable in holiness before our God and Father at the coming of our Lord, Jesus Christ, with all his saints." Dare any Christian continue with a spirit of hatred or jealousy or unkind feeling toward any other when he realizes the truth of this Scripture? Truly, we need to

follow the admonition-"Let not the sun go down on your wrath."

Having come to believe in the imminency of the coming of Christ, it is an impossibility for me to harbor in my heart any such spirit. Oh, that

Christians everywhere might come to know this experience! How wise would be some of our liberal brethren and supposedly evangelicals who like to scoff at and ridicule the truth that we are setting forth, and at those who still believe and live it, if only they too would believe and preach it, realizing that the Spirit of God would bring the result they long for in the life of their people and give to them a more Christlike spirit!

C-INCENTIVE TO PUTTING THE RIGHT EVALU- ATION ON THINGS. 

Matthew 16:26-27-"For what is a man profited if he shall gain the whole world and lose his own soul? or what shall a man give in exchange for his soul? For the Son of man shall come in the glory of his Father with his angels; and then shall he render to every man according to his deeds." How many Christians are investing for eternity? Too often we think of things that are earthly and transient and build only for the present, when in the light of the truth of the Lord's return we are forced to face eternity. Ours is not to seek glory here, but to rejoice that we are privileged to "fill up those things that remain of the sufferings of Christ." His was a lonely pathway here upon earth, and every true follower of His will find the way lonely, too, as His was. Not only

will the way be increasingly difficult, but there will be those within our own group who will ridicule and hurt.

I remember very well an experience that I had when in the pastorate in New Jersey. I had been speaking in a neighboring city and, at the close of the service, was talking with one of the prominent ministers there, one who was supposed to be thoroughly evangelical. During the course of the conversation mention was made of the Second 

Coming of Christ. Because I thoroughly believe it to be the teaching of Scripture and am unashamed to hold this view, I told the good brother how I felt in regard to the question. Everything had been going smoothly until this time. When I mentioned my confidence in this truth and that

I believed the day was fast drawing on for its fulfillment, he bristled up and became quite intolerant. I can recall his very words as he told of his views in regard to these things and how he felt about those of his brethren who, like myself, held them. Hear him for a moment as he speaks: "Why, do you not know that men have been saying this from the very beginning, and yet it has not been fulfilled." I quickly turned to II Peter 

3:3 and said in as kindly a way as I knew how: "My brother, I wish to thank you for one more evidence of the fulfillment of prophecy and the nearness of this blessed and happy day."

Where is the promise of his coming? For since the fathers well asleep, all things continue as they were from the beginning of creation." He was speechless. It was suddenly revealed to him, as it may be to many who will read these words, that their attitude has become one of the signs that tell of the nearness of this event. Though the way seems rugged and hard and the testing almost more than you can bear, keep continually before you the vision of His coming, and you will find it to be the greatest possible help along the way. This precludes a denial of the fulfillment in the future. of that which has to do with the establishment of 

His throne. In Matthew 25:31 we read, "When the Son of man shall come in his glory . . . then shall he sit upon the throne of his glory." This prophecy of Jesus harmonizes in every point with the prophecy of Isaiah and many others contained in the Old Testament. Remember, He offered

6-THE DURATION OF THIS KINGDOM.

5-The Result. 

What the result of this judgment will be is almost too dreadful to mention. However, let us remember that God has been fair and has given mankind every opportunity. The resultant punishment and judgment is self-imposed through man's own voluntary rejection of God and of His

Son. It will be a harvest of deliberate sowing and from the judgment thereof there will be no appeal. 

Surely each of us who has believed can say: "Oh to grace how much a debtor, daily I'm constrained to be."

III-AFTER THE THOUSAND YEARS. 

A-A FINAL KINGLY ACT. 

After the judgment of the Great White Throne; the banishment of Satan and all those who have chosen in life to follow him; speedy preparation will be made for the consummation of all things. 

At this time Jesus, as King of earth, King through redemption and King through conquest, will present the Kingdom of this earth to the Father. "Then cometh the consummation when he shall have delivered up the kingdom to God, even the Father, for he must reign until he hath put all enemies under his feet, and the last vanity that shall be destroyed is death"-I Corinthians 15: 

24-25. Immediately after the Great White Throne this can be accomplished, for we read that "death and hades were cast into the lake of fire"-Revelation 20:14. When Jesus Christ ascended after

His resurrection He presented His potential Bride

B-A RENOVATING FIRE. 

II Peter 3:10-13-"But the day of the Lord will come as a thief in the night in which the heavens shall melt away with a booming noise and the elements will be dissolved by fire, the earth and the works that are therein shall be burned up." 

Scripture reveals that there have been three earths, as follows: First, the past which was covered with water. Genesis 1:2-"And the earth was without form and void and darkness was upon the face of the deep and the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the water." I will not go into any argument as to the particular view which I hold of the first three verses of Genesis. The reader may already have discovered that I believe that there is the setting forth here of the creation of a perfect earth, Isaiah 45:18, followed at some future time by chaos and void and darkness, and the covering of the whole earth with water. This is followed by the reclaiming of the earth which had already been created. Genesis 1:2.

It is to this I believe Peter refers-"The heavens were of old, and the earth standing out of the water," II Peter 3:5. Second, the present earth on which we now dwell. This earth is not submerged in water, but rather divided by water, and the visible portion surrounded thereby. Third, or future that which shall be renovated by fire and

purged thereby. We know that the heart of the earth is a ball of fire. In that day after the unsaved have been cast out of earth and the saved have been taken to be with Christ, then God through His supernatural power will purge and renovate this old earth by fire-II Peter 3:10.

Fire now in the heart of the earth will form the crucible in which this earth will be purged and the new earth, an earth free from corruption and stain of sin, on which the redeemed shall eternally dwell, brought forth.

C-A NEW EARTH AND A NEW HEAVEN. 

The beautiful description of the new heaven and the new earth in Revelation 21 finds its fulfillment here. The earth having been purged and renovated by fire, the Heavenly City, New Jerusalem, now descends and the abode of humanity, that is, redeemed humanity, those who have become the possessors of life eternal, shall be upon this new earth.

Conclusion: 

With the ending of the Revelation and the scenes which are described in the last chapters of this book, God immediately closes the prophetic word. 

He has revealed His entire relationship to earth and those who dwell thereon. Jesus has given the last great promise of His coming-"Behold I come quickly." Beyond this is eternity, which must rest forever in God's hands. He has not revealed what will be the happenings and the delight of that eternal age. We do not need to know, neither do we seek to delve into those things which God has purposely withheld from us. Let us rejoice that we shall be forever with the Lord.

In closing this book may I call attention again to my reason for sending it forth. I have already stated in the introduction that God, through His Holy Spirit, blessed the lectures contained herein to the edification of those who listened. Let each reader give himself earnestly to the study of the 

Scripture itself, realizing that God, the Holy Spirit, will lead him into all truth. If this book proves helpful in its message, making clear the great

chronological prophetic program of God for the future, then I shall feel that my purpose has been accomplished. Above all, my prayer is that this message may lead all who read to a more definite consecration of life and service to Jesus Christ, and that each one may learn the secret of "Waiting for His Son from heaven."
