Preface 

This is written at a time when Protestant Christianity is promoting a new concept of the functions of the Church. The spirit of that concept may be gathered from the new expression now used so generally. That expression is "meeting a challenge". Aside from the fact that the thought conveyed in that expression militates against the traditional concept of the Church's functions, this trite slogan actually threatens to divert the attention of the Church from its true Biblical commission as described by the Lord Jesus, Matt. 28, 19-20. 

The Church is not commissioned to "meet challenges", but to teach the world whatsoever Jesus has commanded it to teach. To “meet challenges" is quite another thing. Jesus will take care of all "challenges". Let the Church take care of its humble and less quixotic work of first fortifying itself against assaults by the enemy. That will best be done by the arduous, faithful, and prosaic care of the souls it now has. 

Let us not go out to fight the devil's windmills only to find that the devil meanwhile has taken our fortress and strength, the Word of God.

This is not idle fancy. While the visible church is preparing to meet future "challenges", it is blissfully ignoring many of the intense assaults made upon it by the forces of darkness. Among these is the renewed vigorous attack by 

Rome. The papacy is subtly undermining the liberty given man anew in the sixteenth century. It is now quite openly seeking to regain its erstwhile power over civil governments, to dictate religious and moral thought for nations, and to that end is appropriating to its use the public press, the radio, and the movie screen. Some of our best young people are lured into this church through mixed marriages, or into

marriages that guarantee for the Catholic church the offspring of these unions.

This is no challenge calling us into remote fields, but a vicious assault on the very heart of Christianity. We can not repel this assault by a mere annual Reformation Day sermon, nor by ridicule, nor by reliance on any inherited bias against the Catholics. It must be repelled by the Word of God, by careful and intelligent instruction backed up by prayer to God for help against this modern Philistine that threatens to ravage our fertile fields. That was Luther's principle; that must be the weapon of the Church of all ages. 

May the Lord awaken us to the actual dangers confronting the Church and to a new dedication on our part to the Church's work. We dare not minimize the danger coming from Rome. We must teach, preach, and exhort tirelessly. 

To do that effectively we must ourselves have studied, in detail, the soul-destroying poison of Roman Catholicism; and we must be able to analyze it in the light of history as well as in the light of the Word of God. It is with the thought of aiding the pastors and teachers in this effort that this manuscript is submitted for publication. 

This is a somewhat extensive elaboration of a brief mimeographed booklet prepared for use in my own congregation. Our Publication Board, members of which had seen a copy of this original essay, has requested that the material be offered to the public in book form. "Unless the Lord build the house, they labor in vain who build it." We have called on the Lord in the building of the house here offered and we call upon Him to further bless it and to use it as a humble contribution to the defense of the walls of Zion. 

E. G. Behm, Lutheran Pastor 

Calvary, Wisconsin.

The Papacy Evaluated CHAPTER I  

The Bible has always been the most cherished book of all true children of God. From it they learned the truths of salvation and in it they found comfort in sorrow and hope in the prospect of death. It was, and still is, to them a safe, dependable guide for faith and life, showing them what to believe and how to live. It was studied in Jewish synagogues and in Christian churches. It was taught to little children like Timothy (2 Tim. 3, 15) and to young men like Paul (Acts 22, 3). Bible teachings were the chief branches of learning in the educational systems of Jewish and early Christian life. Jesus and the apostles recommended it to the 

Christians for searching study, thereby honoring an Old Testament custom with New Testament approval. Never was any other book accorded equal honor. It stood supreme and its authority was challenged neither by the church nor by individuals within the church. 

THE POPE ALONE DISSENTS 

It is then, indeed, surprising that the Church of Rome, which claims to be the only true church and the sole dispenser of God's grace, should so bitterly oppose the authority of the plain teachings of Scripture! It has deposed the Bible from its God-given position of sole authority and has substituted the Catholic Church, saying that the Church of Rome alone is the true interpreter of the will of God, not the Words of Scripture. It thereby forces man under the yoke of obedience to whatever erring men consider good or expedient. More than that, it definitely opposes the reading of Scripture, as

this book will show, even though in late years it has tried to deny this charge.

It speaks vaguely of traditions, as though the Lord had given men revelations in addition to the Bible, which is not true; and it continually emphasizes the claim that the Church alone is competent to interpret the will of God. It says that the Holy Ghost inspires it and that its teachings are an expression of God's will, no matter how much these teachings may be in obvious contradiction to the teachings of Scripture. 

Yes, it claims more. It claims that it has the authority to legislate for all people and that these laws then have all the force of divine authority and power.

THEIR OWN WORDS CONDEMN THEM

J., in "Catholic Truth”, page 465, in an article entitled, "The Church or the Bible", writes: "My dear people, if God has intended that man should learn his religion from a book - the Bible surely God would have given that book to man; Christ would have given that book to man 

Christ did not say (to his apostles) 'sit down and write Bibles and scatter them over the earth and let every man read his Bible and judge for himself'." Catholic has divine faith, and why? Because the Catholic says, 'I believe in such and such a thing'. Why? 'Because the Church teaches me so'. 'And why do you believe the 

Church?' 'Because God has commanded me to believe the

teachings of the Church; and God has threatened me with damnation if I do not believe the Church'". That doesn't leave any authority to the Bible, does it?

In the year 1199 Pope Innocent, in a letter to the bishop of Metz (Lib. II, epist. 141 ad universas Christianas in urbe 

Metensi) said: "Although the desire to study Scriptures is, in itself, not to be censured, however the reading of Scriptures in convents dare not be tolerated. For the divine Scripture has such great depth that not only the uneducated but also the educated cannot obtain full insight into them." He illustrates this with the Lord's command at Mt. Sinai that any animal that touched this mount should be stoned, “in order that no uneducated presume to penetration to the majesty of Scriptures or to preach to others". (Meusel, "Bibellesen Verbote", p. 417.)

In 1229 the Council of Toulouse decreed that it be not permitted the laity to possess either the Old or New Testament books. This decree was repeated at the synod of Beziers in 1233, with the addition that neither priests nor laymen be permitted to possess translations of Latin theological books and only priests be permitted to own such books in the Latin language. Those who owned translations of Scripture were commanded by the Council of Terracona (1234) to deliver them to their bishop within eight days that they might be burned, whether of the clergy or laity. (Meusel, p. 417.)

In 1383 the English divine Wiclif was accused of heresy by the Council of Oxford, because he had translated the Bible and made its truths accessible to the common people. 

In 1486 Archbishop Berchold of Mainz prohibited the printing and sale of all German translations of the Bible, unless sanctioned by certain commissioners, under pain of excommunication.

The council of Trent (1545-63) in its IV session, April 

8, 1545, "Rule III of Ten Rules concerning Prohibited Books", enacted very stringent regulations to control the reading of the Bible. These regulations provided, among other things, under pain of excommunication, that no one be permitted to read an heretical (Protestant) translation of the New Testament and that the Old Testament in any such translation might be read only by those who were "pious and learned" and then only by permission of their priests. Even the approved translations might be read only by those who had permission of their priest and the sale of such approved editions required the permission of the bishop. 

Pope Clement XI in his bull "Unigenitus", issued in 1713 in condemnation of 101 propositions by the Jansenist Quesnel, clearly states where the Catholic Church stands on the matter of Bible reading by the laity. Among the propositions condemned by this pope were such as these: "The seed of the 

Word, which the hand of God watereth, ever bringeth forth its fruits". "No graces are given, save through faith". - "The reading of Sacred Scripture is for all". "To interdict the reading of Scripture, especially of the Gospel, is to interdict the use of light to the sons of light". Romanism as it is, p. 168f. 

In 1800 Pope Pius VII cursed all Bible Societies, including those of the Catholics, as "a scourge of Christianity".

Leo XII did the same. Kurtz, Church History II, p. 107. 

The latter pope said: "It is to be feared that by false interpretation the Gospel of Christ will become the Gospel of men, or still worse, the Gospel of the devil". CHAPTER II  THE LAW DEMANDS PERFECT LOVE 

In the Gospel of John (1, 17) we read: "The law was given by Moses, but grace and truth came by Jesus Christ". 

Here we have the two great doctrines of the Bible. One is the law, given by Moses, telling us what God demands of us; the other is the Gospel, telling us what God has done and still does for our salvation. Both Catholics and Protestants agree that the law condemns everyone, because no one can fulfill its demands. It is in the implications of the second part of the above passage, "grace and truth", that we differ. This "came by Jesus Christ". Grace is the opposite of law. This contrast is clearly emphasized in the passage. While the law demands, grace gives. There is no grace in law and there can be no law, demands, in grace. Grace is free, unearned and undeserved forgiveness. That came by Jesus Christ, not by our works or merits.

Jesus gave us a summary of the law in the words, "Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, with all thy soul and with all thy mind. This is the first and great commandment. The second is like unto it, thou shalt love thy neighbor as thyself. On these two commandments hang all the law and the prophets" - Matt. 22, 37-40.

Therefore the fundamental demand of God is love, love for God and man. If man had this love perfectly and completely he would not sin, neither against God nor against man.

Every sin is a violation of the law of love because it is, every time, the result of our lovelessness. Thus God demands more than just outward obedience. He says, "Give me, my son, thy heart" (Prov. 23, 26). This lack of love taints every deed

of the Christian. None of our deeds satisfy God's demands and the "smallest" sin makes us guilty of the whole law. 

James writes, 2, 10: "Whosoever shall keep the whole law and yet sin in one point, he is guilty of all". Wherewith, then, should man earn God's grace (If indeed, grace could be earned)? Because of this deficiency of love "We are all as an unclean thing and all our righteousnesses are as filthy rags". 

Isaiah 64, 6; and again, Ps. 14, 3, says: "They are all gone aside, they are all together become filthy; there is none that doeth good, no, not one".

SALVATION A WORK OF LOVE 

Since man's real guilt lies in his failure to love with a perfect love, this sin can be expiated and atoned only by love. 

Paul says, Rom. 13, 10: "Love is the fulfilling of the law".

Therefore man can never fulfill the law, nor can he make any satisfaction for his sins, as the Catholics claim in their teachings of the Mass, Penance, Indulgences, and Good Works. 

Only One could make complete satisfaction. That One is God 

Himself, Who alone loves with a perfect love. "God so loved the world that He gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth on Him shall not perish, but have everlasting life". -John 3, 16. This love of God was manifested in the holy 

Son Jesus, Who loved God with a perfect love and willingly became obedient, keeping the whole law; and, as man's substitute, bore man's guilt and suffered sin's punishment, for man, on the cross. "He humbled Himself and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross". Phil. 2, 8. "As by one man's disobedience (Adam's) many were made sinners, so by the obedience of one shall many be made righteous". - 

Rom. 5, 19. This work was a work of love. Jesus says, John 

15, 13: "Greater love hath no man than this that a man lay down his life for his friends". The sacrifice of Himself was

an act of love. That is why He could thereby render satisfaction and atone and expiate the sins of all. 

JESUS THE SACRIFICE OF LOVE 

But let us study this matter of atonement more in detail. 

The Bible teaches that Jesus rendered full satisfaction for our sins. We read, Gal. 3, 13: "Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us" (When God cursed him in our stead on the cross). The Old Testament already pointed to Christ as the sin-bearer, the atoner, the expiator of our guilt. That mission of Christ was clearly stated in Old Testament prophecies, as well as in the many types contained in the forms of Old Testament worship. The victims in the sacrifices, especially the sheep and goats, are described in the Bible as types of the Savior to come. The priests, too, were types of Christ. This is all detailed in the 

Epistle to the Hebrews, chapter 7-10. When sacrifices were made for sin, the priest would first lay his hand on the head of the animal to be sacrificed and confess the sins of the people, thereby indicating that the sins of the guilty (the people) were being laid on the innocent victim. In the same manner God laid the sins of the guilty world on the sinless and innocent Jesus, and Jesus sacrificed Himself to make satisfaction to God for the people's sin. Thus Isaiah says, 53, 6: "The Lord hath laid on him the iniquity of us all". John the 

Baptist said of Jesus: "Behold the lamb of God that taketh away the sin of the world". Jesus says of Himself, "I lay down my life for the sheep," John 10, 15. The same John also says in his first epistle (2, 2) "He (Jesus) is the propitiation for our sins". Again we read, 2 Cor. 5, 19: "God was in

Christ, reconciling the world unto himself, not imputing their trespasses unto them for He hath made him to be sin for

us who knew no sin that we might be made the righteousness of God in him".

Furthermore, they teach that Christ's sacrifice, too, must be constantly repeated in the Mass, in order that these works might be made meritorious. 

MAN IS JUSTIFIED THROUGH THE MERITS OF CHRIST 

No, nothing remains to be done, either by God or by man. "It is finished", is the exultant cry of every believer. When

Where there is full and complete forgiveness, there is also life and salvation. Where does the Bible leave room for the argument that man must bear the punishment for his sins. himself? Why then did Christ die? Would it not be terrible mockery if God should say to us that He had forgiven us but that we would have to bear our punishment anyway? Or is not God able to remit punishment if He is able to remit sin?

BRIEF SUMMARY OF CATHOLIC "GOSPEL" 

The Catholic Church teaches that Baptism removes only the sins committed before Baptism and all original sin, that it thus wipes out the stain of Adam's transgression, charitably

forgives any sins that might have been committed before, and places the person into the Catholic Church. (Rev. Joseph 

Deharbe, S. J., Catechism, "Means of grace, Q. 46". Also

Faerber, Catechism, p. 324.) After that, man's salvation is largely up to him. Grace, in the Catholic church, is not understood as God's favor, earned for man by Christ by reason of which we are pardoned freely, without works or merit; but in the Catholic church grace is understood to be nothing more than an infused agency, or power, which makes man righteous in his own right, not because of any imputed righteousness of Christ. In other words, grace enables man to make his own "nuptial garment, without which no one can be admitted to the heavenly banquet". It is also called, "the supernatural, heavenly life of the soul"- Faerber, Catechism, p. 64. This "grace" is established by Christ in His suffering and has been placed into the hands of the Catholic church to dispense to the believers. It is augmented by the merits of saints who are supposed to have more grace to their credit than they need for their own happiness. This "grace" needs the co-operation of the person to whom it is given. Into this condition of "grace", into this relationship with God that makes man capable of receiving this power, man enters through the door of Baptism. 

He can lose this grace again by committing a mortal sin, which sin is not clearly defined by the Church, but is described in a general way as “a sin in an important matter, done with a clear understanding, and full consent of the will"- Faerber, p. 55. Whether or not one has committed this sin is usually left for the priest to decide in confession. 

If the person dies with only one mortal sin charged against him, he is irrevocably and immediately consigned to hell. It must be his chief aim in life, therefore, to commit no mortal sin because he would thereby lose all his merits and sanctifying grace. He would be excluded from the king-

dom of God. Mortal sin can be forgiven only by the priest in the Sacrament of Penance, Deharbe, p. 63 — Faerber, 137–138 and "Application." In his effort to avoid the commission of a mortal sin, the Church helps him by giving him, through the Sacraments, the necessary portion of grace from the great treasury of grace in heaven piled up by Christ and the saints. 

It works this way: In the Sacraments the sinner has infused into him Sanctifying Grace (Donum Superadditum). This grace restores the fellowship with God and awakens the desire to do good works. Then comes the actual grace which gives the power to perform the good works, always, however, only with the co-operation of the person. These good works are a merit and for their sake God forgives the sinner. Deharbe, pp. 17, 18, 19, 20-Footnote. Thus, grace in the Catholic church means only an aid the Lord gives the sinner in earning his own sanctification. The idea of "free" salvation, unmerited by the sinner but given entirely for the sake of the atonement Christ has made, is foreign to Catholic thought and teaching.

The eternal punishment, hell, is forgiven by the sacraments of Baptism and Penance. The temporal punishment must be expiated by the guilty one himself. Faerber, Q. 407. "How is temporal punishment remitted?" Answer: "Temporal punishment is remitted by performing the penance imposed (in confession), and by fasting, other good works, pious use of sacramentals (holy water, relics, medals), indulgences, and Purgatory".

Here is the explanation of these "expiations" of temporal punishment: Penance, the work imposed by the priest on him who has been absolved from eternal punishment. It is the expiation, in part, of temporal punishment. Remember, the temporal punishment is not remitted. This work may consist of praying the rosary a stated number of times, of fasting, payment of money for some church work, or in the performance of some other work whereby the flesh is mortified. These penances, at the same time, aid the penitent in avoiding the same sins in the future. Fasting means to abstain from food, or certain foods, during the time commanded by the church.

It may be observed as a matter of regular church order; it may be a penance imposed by the priest; or it may be a self-imposed "good work" for the purpose of obtaining additional merits. Voluntary fastings are highly recommended as a sure way of increasing one's merits.

Other Good Works may be any act that is meritorious. 

Not all good works are meritorious; but only those that are done in a state of sanctifying grace and are done with “a good intention”, i.e., with the expressed declaration that they are to be meritorious. — Faerber, Qs. 289–294. 

Pious Use of Sacramentals means the use of holy water, consecrated oil, salt, palms, priestly blessings, etc. In addition to these merits, the person may obtain indulgences, which are gifts taken from the great treasury of the church and applied to the account of the petitioner. These gifts must be earned by the performance of some act of devotion or obedience, such as a pilgrimage, reading a prayer to which an indulgence is attached, taking part in some activity for the good of the church. However, only such works confer indulgences as have been so designated by the pope.

Matt. 22, 11f.

WHAT IS FAITH? 

Faith is the gift of the Holy Ghost to believe implicitly everything God tells us in His Word. It is the gift to trust in and depend on Christ's atoning merits, Christ's redemption, for salvation. Faith is assurance and conviction. The Bible says it is "the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen"- Hebr. 11, 1. Faith is the submission of rebellious man to God and His Word. Faith causes man completely to abandon his own natural ideas concerning the way to salvation, to throw overboard all his own merits as worthless cargo; to surrender all to Jesus, coming to Him "Just as I am without one plea, but that Thy blood was shed for me, and that Thou bidst me come to Thee; O Lamb of God, I come, I come.”

FAITH IS A GIFT OF GOD 

This faith man can not give to himself because he is by nature dead in trespasses and sin (Eph. 2, 1). God gives this

God and the Sacraments. Rom. 10, 17: "Faith cometh by hearing and hearing by the Word of God", also Gal. 3, 27.

WHAT THE FATHERS SAID 

In the early Christian church it had become customary tohave the celebration of the agapai or "love feasts" whenever the Christians met for the celebration of the Lord's Supper. 

This was a feast, eaten by all, to indicate that all Christians were equal before God and that they were one in Christ. When these feasts, however, developed into sinful orgies, they were dropped (1 Cor. 11, 18-22). However, even after this, the members continued to bring bread and wine to be used for the Lord's Supper and for the support of the clergy. These gifts were called oblations and were considered a gift of thanksgiving on the part of the people. Later they were formally dedicated to God as an offering by the pastor, in a prayer that was called "Eucharist". This prayer of thanksgiving was itself a sacrificial act, but the difference between this offering and the Mass is at once apparent. Not the body and blood of Christ, but bread and wine as such, were offered; and the offering was not an atoning sacrifice, but a sacrifice of thanksgiving. It was made not by the clergyman alone but by the congregation. It was called a "bloodless sacrifice" but

that expression did not refer to Christ, in distinction of that sacrifice; but it referred to the difference between it and the bloody sacrifices of the Old Testament. That is what the church fathers say of it (Irenaeus 1 v. 17, 4; 18, 3). It is true, later when the idea of a priesthood developed, the bread and wine were sometimes called the “offering up of the slain Christ" (Cyril of Jerusalem, Cat. Myst., v. 8-10 uses this expression but afterwards adds that the consecrated elements were merely antitypes of the blood and body of Christ, v. 20); but they never understood that expression literally as a true sacrifice that had any virtue or power in itself. Thus Augustine saw in the Lord's Supper a memorial of an accomplished sacrifice (memoria peracti sacrificii).

The idea of a sacrifice of Christ in the Lord's Supper developed gradually with the superstitious ideas of the common people (who were uneducated and lacked Bible knowledge), regarding the power of the priest to change the elements in the Supper into body and blood. CHAPTER IV  THE FIRST CHURCH?

Lutheran should be deceived thereby. 

In the first Christian era there were no organized churches as we have them today; nor was there "one church" in the sense that all the congregations were one body ruled by one person. People in those days were either Christian, Jewish, or heathen. Their only bond was the consciousness that they took the same stand, either for or against Christ. The Christian congregations were very evidently independent of each other in every other respect, except for this invisible bond of faith. As long as the Apostles were alive, important matters were taken to them for advice. We have an example of this in 

Acts 15, where we are told that the congregation in Antioch asked the apostles and congregation for advice in a certain matter. (And, by the way, it was James, not Peter, who gave the advice. James was, undeniably, the leader of the congregation in Jerusalem, not Peter. Peter was there, but James was the leader.) The congregations, on their own authority, also sent out missionaries (Acts 13, 1-3). There is absolutely no

authority for the Catholic claim that Peter was the head of the churches, that he travelled around taking the supervision over the churches, while the churches had bishops under Peter. 

Certainly this was not the case at Antioch, as Acts 15 shows. 

NEVER ONE HEAD 

Even after the congregations multiplied they still maintained their independence of each other, neither recognizing any single apostle as the head of all, nor effecting any fixed. organization. In Acts 1, 15ff, we are told that the congregation in Jerusalem, numbering at this time about one hundred and twenty, elected a successor to Judas. Peter, who was present, made no effort to appoint a successor, which he would have done had he claimed the primacy over the rest of the apostles, or if he had denied the right of congregations to call their own pastors. The affair in which the congregation at Antioch asked the advice of the congregation in Jerusalem (Acts 15) was not decided by the Apostle Peter, nor by the Apostles to the exclusion of the congregation, but by the "multitude", which can only mean congregation. Clearly then, the congregations were at that time not considered as being subject to Peter or to any other authority of the church on earth. Peter himself rejects the idea of any human superior over the churches in his epistle, 1 Pet. 5, 1-5: "The elders. among you I exhort, who am also an elder, and a witness of the sufferings of Christ, and also a partaker of that glory that shall be revealed: feed the flock of God that is among you, taking the oversight thereof, not by constraint, but willingly; not for filthy lucre, but of a ready mind; neither as being lords over God's heritage, but being examples to the flock. THE VERDICT OF HISTORY 

Facts of history are hard to disprove, and history is all against this claim as is also the Word of God. In the first centuries of the Christian era there definitely were no popes. 

Men were not called that nor did anyone exercise that power.

Christian congregations in larger cities and their adjacent territory were under the spiritual supervision of leaders called presbyters, or elders (Acts 20, 17; Rev. 4, 10); or they were called episcopos, meaning overseer and translated bishop in both the Douay and King James versions of the Bible (1 Tim. 

3, 2). These presbyters, or bishops, performed the same functions a pastor performs today. Their jurisdiction did not reach beyond their own parish and they were all equal in authority. Originally they were appointed by the apostles, but only with the approval of the congregations they were to serve (Acts 6, 5; 13, 3; Acts 14, 23; 1 Tim 4, 14).

In the first four or five centuries vacancies were usually filled by the vote, either of the congregation or of the other bishops living in the province in which the vacancy occurred. 

Bishop Cyprian, at about the year 250, wrote an article entitled, "Bishop", in which he stated that "the congregation has the privileged right to elect worthy priests and depose those who are unworthy". If in the year 250, congregations were admitted to have the right to call and depose pastors, how can the claim be sustained that this power has always been vested in the bishop of Rome?

We quote from the article, “Bishop" in the Encyclopedia Britannica: "The early history of the Episcopal order is obscure, but it would appear that the first bishops were established in the chief cities of Christendom, and each bishop had a certain territorial district placed under his superintendence, whence the city was termed the see (sedes) of the bishop, and the district his parish, and subsequently his diocese. In course of time the districts assigned to the bishops became too populous, whereupon the clergy of each diocese, as the case might be, appear to have assembled and to have subdivided the diocese, and to have selected a second bishop, and so bishops

and dioceses were multiplied, according to the wants of the churches until it was thought expedient to reserve the right of erecting new bishoprics to provincial councils and this reservation was made a rule of the church by a decree of the council of Sardica". (Note that never was a bishop appointed by any other bishop, not even by the bishop of Rome). “Meanwhile the bishops of the new sees had grouped themselves round the bishops of more ancient sees, who exercised over them a certain spiritual authority as primates, and presided in their councils".

THE BISHOP OF ROME 

Because of the geographical and political importance of the city of Rome, it being the principal city of the world and the residence of the Roman emperors, the bishop of that city was granted a certain prestige- a prestige he was glad to accept and which he promoted with cunning persistency. His residence in the world's capital also enabled him to represent other bishops before the civil powers and to intercede for individuals in times of persecution. Gradually other bishops looked to him for advice and counsel. When, however, Stephen I, bishop of Rome, (253-257) for the first time in recorded history claimed to be Peter's successor, this claim was most emphatically opposed by the other bishops, such as St. Firmilian of Caesarea, who called him "worse than all heretics" Gore, Roman Catholic claims, page 133.

The Eastern Church never accepted the bishop of Rome as head of the Christian Church. The Western bishops gradually submitted to these claims, especially after the appearance of the Pseudo-Isidorian Decretals, which were letters allegedly written by the popes beginning with Clemens Romanus (about 100 A.D.) and leading up to the time when

the primacy of the pope had been pretty well accepted by the Western church. These false decretals were to be the bridge of evidence over that period of about five hundred years during which no historical grounds for the papacy could be found. 

These decretals are now universally rejected as clumsy forgeries. Even most scholars of the Catholic church now term them as such. However when they appeared they served their purpose of strengthening the claims of the Roman bishop. It took centuries, however, before the bishop of Rome was accepted as the vicar of Christ and leader of His church. It was Leo the Great (about 450 A. D.) who, by his ability to lead and to organize the other bishops into some semblance of unity, really might be called the first pope. By that time the visible church had become corrupt, steeped in political ambitions, and lacking spiritual and moral strength. Even then a virile and healthy church would not have submitted to the Roman claims.

One of the greatest Catholic scholars and authorities for moral theology was Alfonso Maria de'Liguori, born near Naples on September 27, 1696. He was the founder of the

Liguori taught a system of moral theology called Probabilism and Equivocation, which means that the laws of God are not binding, literally; but may be transgressed, if in the opinion of the individual his conscience would so dictate; and that when questioned regarding a sin or crime, the per-

"If the act be not a crime in the witness's opinion (in court) he need not disclose it, and that if the crime be altogether hidden (i. e. where only the criminal himself and the witness know the facts), the witness is not merely permitted, but is actually bound, to say that the accused did not commit it." "A needy man, who has made away, for his maintenance, with property due to his creditors, may affirm to the judge that he has nothing. An adulteress, questioned by her husband as to her guilt, may deny it in any of these four ways: a) that she has not broken the marriage tie, because it is not voided by adultery; b) if she have gone to confession, she may say that she is innocent of the crime, because it has been remitted in confession; c) that she has not committed adultery, using the word in its frequent Biblical sense of 'idolatry'; d) that she has not committed it so as to be bound to tell him of it." Protestant, who contract marriage before a Protestant minister or a civil marriage officer" (L. H. Lehmann in Mixed 

Marriages in the Catholic Church). 

In consequence of this protest, or in spite of it, the Catholic church sustained great losses still, because of the practice of many Catholics marrying Protestants who had the marriage performed by a priest but then permitted the children to be brought up in another faith. In other words, they had made the required ante-nuptial promise to have the children brought up in the Catholic faith, but disregarded that promise after the marriage had received the blessing of the Church. "In order to remedy this, the Holy Office of the Inquisition issued a more drastic decree in 1932, which declared in effect that if the conditions were not faithfully adhered to, then the dispensation must be counted as 'null and void'. Thus, if the parties to a mixed marriage now fail to have their children baptized and educated in the Roman Catholic religion, their marriage is automatically dissolved as far as the Catholic church is concerned" (Lehmann, Mix. Mar.).

This decree has teeth. It declares that it is the Cardinals' strict duty "to notify and make it a matter of conscience binding upon all Vicars and Bishops and all those referred to in Canon Law 1044, who have the faculty of dispensing from the impediment of mixed religion and disparity of worship, that this dispensation must never be granted unless the per-

sons to be married first give assurances whose faithful execution no one may be able to prevent, even by the power of the civil laws to which one or the other of the parties is subject in his present place of residence, or in any future place, if it can be forseen that he is about to change his place of residence. 

Otherwise the said dispensation is null and void".

This "assurance" must be given on a four page document, gotten up in an impressive legal form and couched in forceful language, which must be attested by witnesses and filed with the Bishop of the Diocese before the dispensation will be granted. We are indebted to the Converted Catholic Magazine, 229 West 48th St., New York, for the photostatic copy of this contract in our possession. This is the form used in the Archdiocese of Milwaukee. These documents are carefully guarded to prevent their falling into the hands of Protestants. Even the Protestants signing this document are not given a copy, nor are they made acquainted with all its contents before giving their signature. This is very unfair, since all parties signing any legal document are entitled to know exactly what they sign.

ARE WE MEN OR MICE? 

No good American citizen can conscientiously enter into a marriage under the stipulations of the above ante-nuptial agreement. That would be conceding to the pope civil powers that annul the proper and rightful privileges of an independent government. It would be conceding to the pope the very

right he claims to possess over all civil government, the right to annul and disregard civil laws wherever they do not suit him. Should Protestants make concessions in this, the pope would soon inflict other Catholic church laws on the nation. 

Furthermore, the whole relationship between man and wife would thus be controlled, not by mutual love and consideration, but it would be regulated by a legal form, usually obtained unfairly by misrepresentation and deception. Certain it is that the non-Catholic party could thus never contribute voluntary respect for the faith of his spouse that alone could make a mixed marriage a happy one. He would always have to feel that he is bound by a legal promise, exacted by a foreign power that both despises his religion and denies him any rights. Signing such a document would certainly be signing away his and his spouse's right to live their own married life in their own way. It would be bondage to the pope. The brazen attempt of the pope to take away the rights of a free people to enter upon matrimony according to the ceremony of their own choosing, and to live their married life without submission to any foreign third party, is such flagrant arrogance that any person with a spark of indepen-. dence will turn away from it with disgust. 

THE PROTESTANT SIGNS AWAY ALL HIS RIGHTS 

Protestants do not insist that a mixed marriage must be performed by a Protestant minister. We in no wise infringe on the rights of the Catholic party to the marriage. Though we admit that circumstances in a mixed marriage are in its very essence against a happy and satisfactory married life, we believe that the couple nevertheless has the right to enter that kind of marriage if it so desires.

The Catholic church, however, reaches out into the lives of the couple and determines what its future married life is to be. The Catholic party must bind himself to “do all in his power to bring about the conversion of the non-Catholic". 

There will be a missionary in the home, a missionary under obligations to the pope to make the "conversion" of the non- Catholic his first business. He must strive to do that by "example, prayer, and frequentation of the Sacraments", among the latter being that of Penance in which the Catholic must confess all failures to carry out this promise and must submit to the coaching in his efforts by the Catholic priest. 

The priest has previously had his opportunity to soften up the Protestant for these "missionary" efforts by the course of instruction in Catholic teachings to which the Protestant had to submit as a condition of marriage. 

Nor may the couple determine their own course, even in the most intimate relations. The document has the promise of both that they will not practice birth control. In confession the priest will enquire of the Catholic why this promise is not being carried out, should the children not be born in the numbers he considers normal. All right-thinking people see the danger and, in many instances, the evils of birth control; but to have the pope, through the priest, enforce his ideas. on that subject on a Protestant; to concede to the priest the right of enquiry; and to be bound by solemn promise to him in this matter all that certainly would be an unmanly and humiliating yielding of a sacred right. 

Nor is that all! The Protestant must promise not to interfere with the religion of his Catholic spouse. He may see and deplore the superstition and idolatry of his spouse. 

He dare say nothing. If she wants to decorate the walls and shelves with idols and Catholic pictures, if she wants her rosary and medals exposed to conspicuous view, the Protes-

tant dare not object. He must behold his own children, his own flesh and blood, deliberately brought up in superstition and idolatry, but he dare make no attempt to teach them the truth. He must reconcile himself to the fact that his spouse and children are taught to regard his own religion as having no rights, only the "force of seduction". Christian Marriage, previously quoted, says: "It is customary to regard Protestantism as a religion which has rights. This is an error. Protestantism is not a religion; Protestantism has not a single right. 

It possesses only the power of seduction; it is an error which flatters human nature. Error can have no rights". The

Protestant in a mixed marriage consummated according to Catholic rites is the object of pity, if not scorn, by his spouse and his children. They regard him as a heathen. When he dies he must endure the full extent of purgatorial suffering, not having expiated any of his sins; and if he gets to heaven at all it is only because he didn't know any better than not to join the Catholic church.

THE DENIAL OF CHRIST 

Jesus said: "He that denies me before men him will I deny before my heavenly Father". He says: "Preach the

Gospel to all creatures. By virtue of the solemn promise made before the priest the Protestant is bound to do the first and is hindered from doing the other, even for his own children. If he wants to pray with his family he will be violating the conscience of his Catholic spouse, because the Catholic is strictly forbidden to enter into any religious practice with non-Catholics; and the prayer would hardly be probable in such a family, because the Catholics would be held to pray to Mary and the saints. By paying the church dues and fees demanded by the Catholic church of the Catholic spouse, the Protestant must contribute to the upkeep and expansion of idolatry, superstition, and a soul-destroying religion. Not only

that. The Protestant promises, solemnly and before witnesses, that he binds "his heirs, next of kin, executors, administrators, and/or subsequent guardians and their successors" to carry out the provisions of his promise in the event of his own death. 

He gives to the Archbishop the legal right to "enforce each and every promise" made, even if he should not die. Someday the Catholic Church is going to try to enforce these provisions by recourse to civil law. That time may not be far away.

The Lord enjoins all parents to bring up their children in the "nurture and admonition of the Lord" (Eph. 6, 4). No one dare sign away that obligation. The Lord will hold every parent responsible for the spiritual welfare of the children in his care. We dare not condemn our own flesh and blood to a religion of error and superstition. We must testify to the truth and we must uphold the truth, even, and especially, in the midst of our own families. The responsibility of the Protestants is the greater. We have the truth in the 

Bible. Jesus says, Luke 12, 48: "For unto whomsoever much is given of him shall much be required". 

No one dare submit to the course of instruction required to be taken by the Protestant party to the proposed marriage. 

Not only is that a denial of the truth we once confessed, but the book of instruction, "Instruction for Non-Catholics before Marriage", is glaringly untrue both in its statements regarding Protestants and in its presentation of doctrine. It slanders and ridicules the religion of the Bible and smoothes over and tries to explain away the errors of the Catholic church. "Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God". Even it the Lord should not punish such acts by taking away the faith, loyalty for Christ, the Bible, and the Truth will cause every true Bible Christian and follower of Christ to consider with contempt any effort on the part of a priest of Rome to instruct

him in the errors of Catholicism. No one having a spring of clean water from which to drink, will consider drinking of muddy, poisoned water, not even six times. Anyone that demands that of him is not seeking his welfare. 

Young people, consider well what a Catholic mixed marriage means. Ask those who have entered upon such a relation, observe their family life. Consider whether or not the 

Lord favors and desires a marriage that will entail disloyalty to the ideals of our country, the denial of your faith, and your consent to the distortion and mutilation of the holy estate given for man's happiness and the godly training of children! "The devil as a roaring lion walketh about seeking whom he may devour. Him resist steadfast in the faith." 

If you have been placed before the choice of signing or not signing the Catholic Ante-Nuptual Agreement, then re-. member that your decision will determine your future temporal and eternal happiness. By refusing to sign you will 

1) remain loyal to God and His Truth, 2) preserve your freedom from the sinister tentacles of the pope that would otherwise reach out into your life, 3) serve your Lord by resisting the encroachments by the pope on our civil liberties, 4) prevent the estrangement of your children because of the contempt they will be taught to have for your faith, 5) pre-serve your right to bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord, 6) keep yourself free from compromising your faith and your conscience.

Consider each point well. On the other hand, if you sign, you shall make yourself guilty of a clear denial of the Lord, because when you deny plain truths and evade the obligations of these truths you are in fact denying Christ. 

Christ says, "He that denies me before men him will I deny before my Father Who is in Heaven". You are, by your own

act, binding yourself to certain obligations that will ever be a burden to your conscience. Remember that once you have committed yourself in this matter, nothing but an unusual event of God's grace can later set you free. No one who indifferently signs this document may hope for that to happen. Your faith is put to a severe test. May the Lord preserve you from denial. 

The Word of God is clear. Are you going to obey God or your own carnal inclinations? Marriage that involves a denial of the truth and which demands a continuation of that denial for the duration of married life certainly is not pleasing to God and such a marriage certainly is not made in heaven. 

You must choose between God or Satan; between truth or lies, between duty or indifference, between loyalty to Christ or denial of Him. No true happiness can rest on a marriage that makes mockery of everything the Christian holds dear. 

You must make the choice; you will have to bear the responsibilty of your choice.

God demands of you that you love Him above all things, even much more than you love your Catholic friend. You dare let nothing violate your conscience. If your Catholic friend is put out of his church because your marriage was not performed by a priest and because you were unwilling to dedicate your children to a religion of idolatry, superstition, and lies, that will not be your fault. That will be the fault of the church that makes demands that are unreasonable and unscriptural. If your Catholic friend is not now willing to be guided by the Word of God, then you had better part company now before it is too late.
