REPORT TO THE PRESIDENT United States Tariff Commission 

May 8 1957 

To the President: 

The Tariff Commission herein reports its finding in an investigation made in response to your request dated March 14, 1957, under paragraph 2 of Executive Order 10401, to determine whether and to what extent the tariff quota on imports of alsike clover seed, established by proclamation No. 3100 (20 F.R. 4699) of June 29, 1955, will remain necessary after June 30, 1957.

Introduction 

The above-mentioned tariff quota, which under proclamation No. 3100 will expire at the close of June 30, 1957, was established under the "escape clause" procedure. Proclamation No. 3100 provides that during each of the 12-month periods beginning July 1, 1955, and July 1, 1956, not exceeding 2,500,000 pounds of alsike clover seed entered, or withdrawn from warehouse, for consumption shall be dutiable at 2 cents per pound, and that all such seed entered, or withdrawn from warehouse, for consumption in excess of that quantity during each of these periods, shall be dutiable at the rate of 6 cents per pound.

Alsike clover seed was originally dutiable under paragraph 763 of the Tariff Act of 1930 at the rate of 8 cents per pound (see table 1 in the appendix). Pursuant to a concession granted in the first bilateral trade agreement with Canada, effective January 1, 1936, the rate was reduced to 4 cents per pound. The reduced rate of 4 cents per pound was continued pursuant to a concession granted in the second bilateral trade 886713

agreement with Canada, effective January 1, 1939, until January 1, 1948. On the latter date, pursuant to a concession granted in the 

General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade, the rate on this seed was further reduced to 2 cents per pound.

An escape-clause investigation was instituted by the Commission with respect to alsike clover seed on December 2, 1953, under section 7 of the Trade Agreements Extension Act of 1951, upon application of domestic growers of this seed. On May 21, 1954, the Commis -sion reported to the President the results of its escape-clause investigation and recommended the modification of the concession granted in the General Agreement to permit the assessment of a duty of 4 cents per pound on the first 1,500,000 pounds of alsike clover seed entered, or withdrawn from warehouse, for consumption in any year, and the assessment of a duty of 6 cents per pound on such seed so entered or withdrawn in any year in excess of 1,500,000 pounds. However, by proclamation No. 3059 dated June 30, 1954, the President established, for one year only, a tariff quota of 1,500,000 pounds of alsike clover seed to be dutiable at 2 cents per pound, with imports during such year in excess of that quantity to be subject to a duty of 6 cents per pound (19 F.R. 4103).

By letter dated July 14, 1954, the President directed the Tariff Commission to continue the investigation with respect to alsike clover seed and to submit to him a supplemental report by May 2, 1955, indicating whether it then considered the continuation of the tariff quota beyond June 30, 1955, to be necessary. On April 28, 1955, the Commission reported to the President its finding that it was necessary to continue

beyond June 30, 1955, an amual tariff quota on alsike clover seed, and that the amual quota should be 2,500,000 pounds subject to duty at the rate of 2 cents a pound, with imports in excess of that quantity during any quota year to be subject to a duty of 6 cents a pound. Subsequently, the President issued proclamation No. 3100, referred to above. 

Executive Order 10401 prescribes procedures for the periodic review and report to the President regarding the need for continuing escape-clause actions. 

Paragraph 2 of the executive order provides for a formal investigation by the Tariff Commission to determine whether, and, if so, to what extent, the withdrawal, suspension, or modification of a trade-agreement concession remains necessary in order to prevent or remedy serious injury or the threat thereof to the domestic industry concerned. Upon the President's request of March 14, 1957, referred to above, the Commission instituted on that day an investigation under paragraph 2 of Executive Order 10401 for the review of the escape-clause action taken with respect to alsike clover seed, and public notice of the institution of the investigation and of a hearing to be held April 15, 1957, in connection therewith was given by posting copies of the notice at the office of the Commission at Washington, D. C. and at its New York office, and by publishing the notice in the Federal Register (22 F.R. 1878) and in the 

March 21, 1957, issue of Treasury Decisions. The public hearing was held at the place and time prescribed, and all interested parties were given opportunity to be present, to produce evidence, and to be heard. In addition to the information obtained at the hearing, the Commission in this investigation utilized information obtained from its files, from the United States Department of Agriculture, and from other sources.

Finding of the Commission On the basis of its investigation, including the hearing, the Commission finds that the continuation beyond June 30, 1957, of the modification of the trade-agreement concession on alsike clover seed as set forth in proclamation No. 3100 of June 29, 1955, will remain necessary in order to prevent serious injury to the domestic industry concerned. 

Considerations Bearing on the Foregoing Finding The foregoing finding is based principally upon the following considerations.

Description and uses 

Alsike clover (trifolium hybridum) is an important hay and pasture crop in the nonarid northern States. Alsike clover will endure flooding that would kill most crops. It is a good pasture plant, often remaining in a permanent pasture for many years. Like other legumes, it helps to maintain the productive capacity of the soil. 

Being hardier than red clover, it is particularly suitable in certain soils and climates where red clover does not thrive. It is grown extensively, particularly in low and moist parts of meadows and fields, in most of the area north of the Ohio and Potomac Rivers and as far west as the Dakota-Minnesota boundary. Alsike clover is grown to a limited extent in Virginia, Kentucky, Tennessee, and Missouri, particularly in upland valleys, but in other southern States it is almost unknown. 

The feeding value of alsike clover hay is considered equal to or better than that of red clover hay. Mixtures of alsike with ladino ing.

Future requirements and supplies 

The purpose of the present investigation, as indicated above, is to determine, in effect, whether and to what extent the existing tariff quota on imports of alsike clover seed will remain necessary after June 30, 1957. 

Such a determination necessarily requires some appraisal of what the future requirements and supplies of alsike clover seed are going to be. 

Obviously, the longer the period for which such a forecast is made, the greater the probability of error. For this reason no attempt has been made in this investigation to forecast beyond the 1957/58 marketing year. 

The following table (table A) gives data on the requirements and supplies of alsike clover seed for the 6 marketing years 1952/53 to 1957/58. The data for 1952/53 to 1955/56 are factual; some of the data for 1956/57 (consumption, exports, carry over at the end of the year, and imports) are partly estimated; and, of course, all the data for 1957/58 are estimated. Requirements include the consumption during the year, exports during the year, and the carryover at the end of the year; supplies include the carryover at the beginning of the year and the production during the year (supplies from domestic sources), together with imports during the year (supplies from foreign sources).

The estimated total requirements of alsike clover seed for 1957/58, as shown by the table, amount to 15.2 million pounds and the estimated supplies from domestic sources amount to 13.2 million pounds. Thus, the estimated requirements exceed the estimated supplies from domestic sources by 2.0 million pounds. If the estimates for 1957/58 turn out to be reasonably accurate, therefore, about 2.0 million pounds of the seed could be imported without causing a surplus in the domestic market. Imports significantly larger than 2.0 million pounds, however, could be expected to create a price-depressing surplus in the domestic market and therefore to seriously injure domestic producers in 1957/58. Moreover, such imports would probably result in a carryover into 1958/59 sufficiently large to affect the domestic market adversely in that year as well. 

Explanation of the estimates for 1957/58

The consumption of alsike clover seed has not followed any clearly definable trend. Alsike competes to a considerable extent with red clover, alfalfa, ladino, and certain other legumes.

Thus when the price of alsike clover seed is high relative to the prices of the other seeds, some consumers will substitute the other seeds for alsike. On the other hand, there is a substantial number of consumers who, because of special soil conditions or other factors, will buy alsike clover seed even when the price is high relative to the prices of these other seeds. These consumers create a comparatively large, inflexible demand for alsike clover seed as shown by the fact that in recent years there has been virtually no correlation from year to year between the quantity of alsike clover seed consumed and the prices paid for the seed by consumers. For example, the average price paid by farmers for the seed in 1954/55 was high compared with that paid in the previous year (see table 10) but consumption remained about the same (see table A). In 1955/56 the price went down but consumption also went

down; and in 1956/57 the price has increased substantially but consumption has decreased slightly. 

The estimated consumption of alsike clover seed in 1957/58 of 12.0 million pounds (shown in table A) is the same as the quantity actually consumed in 1955/56 and slightly larger than the probable consumption in 1956/57. 

There appears to be no reason to suppose that changes in the price or in consumer preference for alsike, or any other factors will cause the level of consumption of the seed in 1957/58 to be significantly different from the level of consumption in the past 2 years.

It is estimated that exports of alsike clover seed in 1957/58 will be about 0.2 million pounds (see table A). This figure compares with actual exports of 0.7 million pounds in 1952/53, of 2.7 million pounds in 1953/54, of 0.5 million pounds in 1954/55, and of 0.1 million pounds in 1955/56. The exports estimated for 1957/58, therefore, are not materially different in size from the exports in recent years except for those in 1953/54. The unusually large exports in that year resulted from sales abroad at very low prices of large Commodity Credit Corporation stocks acquired under the former price-support program on alsike clover seed. The CCC has disposed of its stocks and no similar exports are expected in the foreseeable future. 

A carryover of a fairly large amount of alsike clover seed from one crop year into the next is necessary to meet the requirements of the consumers until the new crop of seed is harvested. A carryover that is larger than necessary for this purpose tends to overburden the market. A carryover equal to about 25 percent of

1 consumption is the most desirable size to meet the requirements of consumers and at the same time to avoid interfering with the orderly functioning of the market. 

The carryover at the end of 1957/58 shown in table A is 3.0 million pounds or 

25 percent of the estimated consumption (12.0 million pounds) for that year. The carryover at the beginning of 1957/58 shown in the table is 3.2 million pounds. 

Although this amount is partly estimated, the actual carryover is virtually certain to be very close to it.

The estimated production of alsike clover seed for 1957/58, as shown in table A, is 10.0 million pounds. This estimate, which is based on information furnished by the Department of Agriculture, is about 10 percent larger than the actual output in 1956/57 (9.1 million pounds). The acreage currently planted to alsike, particularly in the high-yield areas in Oregon and California, is substantially larger than the planted acreage of the preceding crop. This increase in acreage is the principal reason for the belief that the production in 1957/58 will be larger than it was in 1956/57. 

Foreign supplies

As previously stated, the Commission estimates that while 2.0 million pounds of alsike clover seed could be imported in 1957/58 without burdening the market, significantly larger imports would result in an oversupply. It is necessary now to consider the quantity of foreign seed that will be available for exportation to the United States.

Canada has for many years been virtually the only source of United States imports of alsike clover seed (see table 13) and will no doubt be the only source of imports in 1957/58. The conditions under which alsike clover seed is produced in Canada are in general similar to those under which the seed is produced in the 

United States.

Production of alsike clover seed in Canada amounted to 9.5 million pounds in 

1953/54, and to 7.6 million pounds both in 1954/55 and in 1955/56 (see table 7). 

The production dropped to 2.9 million pounds in 1956/57 because of severe damage to the crop by freezing in the spring of 1956; the damage to the plants, however, will not materially affect the seed production in 1957/58. On the basis of recent information from Canada respecting the acreage planted to alsike and the condition of the current crop, it is estimated that the Canadian production of alsike clover seed in 1957/58 will be 7-7.5 million pounds. It will be noted from table 7 that in all recent years when the Canadian production of the seed was 7.5 million pounds or more, the exports from Canada exceeded 5 million pounds. appears, therefore, that if the production in Canada in 1957/58 is about 7-7.5 million pounds, as seems likely, that country will have 5-6 million pounds for export, of which a quantity could be exported to the United States which would be considerably in excess of the estimated maximum imports that could be entered without overburdening the domestic market. Prices It

In the first 6 months of 1953 the farm price of alsike clover seed in the United States averaged about 25 cents per pound. In the third quarter of the year it declined to an average of 21.8 cents per pound, and the fourth quarter registered a further decline to only 14.8 cents a pound, the lowest quarter-year average recorded since 1941 (see table 9). It was under these circumstances that application for action under section 7 was made in November 1953. The average price increased to 16.1 cents a pound in the first quarter of 1954 and to 17.5 cents

in the second quarter of the year. The price advanced to 19.5 cents a pound in the third quarter of 1954 and then increased sharply to an average of 29.7 cents a pound in the last quarter. This level was practically maintained in the first quarter of 1955 and then the price declined to 26.7 cents in the second quarter of the year. The opinion is widely held in the seed trade that the unusually small crop in 1954/55 in the United States 1 was the main factor in the price advance in the fall of 1954 and the spring of 1955.

Farm prices declined to the 20or 21-cent level in the last half of 1955 and the first half of 1956. In the second half of 1956, however, they rose sharply, reaching 34.5 cents a pound in September. 

Since then farm prices of alsike clover seed have gradually declined. By January 

1957 they had dropped to 28.2 cents per pound, and by March, to 24.7 cents per pound. The average farm price in mid-April was reported to be 23.1 cents per pound, with a considerable quantity of seed unsold on the farms for which there was little demand. A price of 23.1 cents per pound is 

74 percent of parity.

1956/57. Imports equal to the quota are dutiable at 2 cents per pound and imports in excess of the quota are dutiable at 6 cents per pound. 

In 1955/56, imports amounted to 3.5 million pounds, exceeding the quota by about 1.0 million pounds. It will be seen from table A that in that year requirements for alsike were comparatively high and supplies from domestic sources were comparatively low. It should also be noted that

FURTHER MODIFICATION OF THE TRADE-AGREEMENT CONCESSION ON ALSIKE CLOVER SEED BY THE PRESIDENT OF THE UNITED STATES OF AMERICA A PROCLAMATION 

1. WHEREAS, pursuant to the authority vested in me by section 350 of the Tariff Act of 1930, as amended, and by section 7 (c) of the Trade Agreements Extension Act of 1951, as amended, on June 30, 1954, I issued Proclamation No. 3059 (3 CFR, 1954

Supp. 26-27) modifying item 763 of Part I of Schedule XX (original) 

annexed to the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade, and on June 29, 1955, I further modified the said item 763 by Proclamation No. 3100 (3 CTR, 1955 Supp. 32-33) so as to provide that not more than 2,500,000 pounds of alsike clover seed described in the said item 763 entered, or withdrawn from warehouse, for consumption during each 12-month period beginning July 1 in the years 1955 and 1956 should be dutiable at 2 cents per pound and that any such seed not subject to the rate of 2 cents per pound should be dutiable at ó cents per pound; and 

2:. WHEREAS section 7 of the Trade Agreements Extension 

Act of 1951, as amended, provides that the modification of a tradeagreement concession pursuant to such section 7 shall be for such time as is necessary to prevent or remedy serious injury to the domestic industry concerned; and 3.

WHEREAS the said further modification of the said item 763 for the period of two years ending June 30, 1957, was made with a view to a later determination of the need for continuing the said further modification beyond June 30, 1957; and 4. 

WHEREAS, on March 14, 1957, I requested the United

States Tariff Commission to make an investigation under paragraph 

2 of Executive Order No. 10401 of October 14, 1952 (3 CFR, 1952 

Supp. 105-6), to determine whether and to what extent the tariff quota on inports of alsike clover seed established by the said further modification will remain necessary after June 30, 1957; and 

5. WHEREAS, on May 7, 1957, the United States Tariff

Commission reported to me that as a result of the investigation made pursuant to my request referred to in the second recital of this proclamation the Commission has found that the continuation beyond June 30, 1957, of the modification of the trade-agreement concession on alsike clover seed as set forth in Proclamation No. 

3100, referred to in the first recital of this proclamation, will remain necessary in order to prevent serious injury to the domestic industry concerned; and 

6. WHEREAS section 350 of the Tariff Act of 1930, as amended, authorizes the President to proclaim such modification of existing duties and such additional import restrictions as are required or appropriate to carry out any foreign trade agreement that the President has entered into under the said section 350; and 

7. WHEREAS I find that the further modification of the concession granted in the said General Agreement with respect to alsike clover seed described in the said item 763 to permit the application to such seed of the duty treatment hereinafter proclaimed is necessary to prevent serious injury to the domestic industry producing the like or directly competitive product, and that upon such further modification of the said concession it will be appropriate to carry out the said General Agreement to apply to alsike clover seed the duty treatment hereinafter proclaimed:

NOW, THEREFORE, I, DWIGHT D. EISENHOWER, Presi dent of the United States of Ame rica, acting under the authority vested in me by section 350 of the Tariff Act of 1930, as amended, and by section 7 (c) of the Trade Agreements Extension Act of 

1951, as amended, and in accordance with the provisions of the said Ceneral Agreement, do proclaim-. (a) That the provision in the said item 763 with respect to alsike clover seed shall be further modified during the period July 1, 1957, to June 30, 1959, both dates inclusive, to read as
