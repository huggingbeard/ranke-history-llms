ONWARD CHRISTIAN SOLDIERS 

When Theodore Roosevelt was nominated for the Presidency on the Progressive Bullmoose ticket in August, 1912, his battle cry as he and his devoted followers started for Armageddon was "Onward Christian Soldiers." The Bullmoose candidate was somewhat handicapped in his campaign, he had no issue to make a fight on except his personal popularity, and the prestige and honors conferred on him by the state of New York and the United States, these he capitalized and worked for all they were worth. 

As soon as the Ex-President announced that he was a candidate for the Presidential nomination, a prominent newspaper published the following: 

"The White House. 

Nov. 8th, 1904 "On the 4th of March next I shall have served three and a half years. 

These three and a half years constitute my first term. The wise custom which limits the President to two terms, regards the substance and not the form. Under no circumstances will I be a candidate or accept another nomination. 

"THEODORE ROOSEVELT." 

"There is the statement. It was volunteered. It was peremptory. It was absolute, it has just been renounced and repudiated by the man who made it. He has left the explanation of his moral malfeasance to others. 

The others comprise several types. Personal idolators are among them. 

Personal sychophants are among them. Those who would avenge their retirement from public service by William H. Taft are among them. All of them place themselves and Mr. Roosevelt above the obligations which honor and veracity impose on others."

After the campaign had made some progress and had grown more hectic and interesting and Roosevelt commenced to abuse and vilify Taft; another newspaper published a letter written in 

1908, the year Taft was elected President. "The true friend of reform, the true foe of abuses, is the man who steadily perseveres in righting wrongs, in warring against abuses but whose character and training are such that he never promises what he cannot perform, that he always a little more than makes good what he does promise. and that while steadily advancing, he never permits himself to be led into foolish excesses which would damage the very cause he champions. In Mr. Taft we have a man who combines all of the qualities, to a degree, which no other man in our public life since the Civil war has surpassed; to a flaming hatred of injustice, to a scorn of all that is base and mean, to a hearty sympathy with the oppressed, he unites entire disinterestedness. courage, both moral and physical, of the highest type, and a kindly generosity of nature which makes him feel that all of his fellow countrymen are in very truth his friends and brothers, that their interests are his, and that all of his great qualities are to be spent with lavish freedom in their service. The honest man of means, the honest and law-

The honest wage worker, the honest farmer, the small mechanic or small trader, or man of small means, can feel that in a peculiar sense Mr. Taft will be his representative, because of the very fact that he has the same scorn of the demagogue that he has for the corruptionist, and that he would front threats of personal violence of a mob with the unquailing and lofty indifference with which he would front the bitter anger of the wealthiest and most powerful corporations."

Theodore Roosevelt. 

It is very probably true that Ex-President Roosevelt was influenced by the example of Wm. J. Bryan when he injected religion into the 1912 campaign.

When Mr. Bryan was a candidate for the Presidency against 

Mr. Taft he made the statement that he did not believe the people of the United States would elect a Unitarian president of this Republic. The impression Mr. Bryan wished to convey was that the people of the United States, at least a majority of them, were orthodox christians and would not vote for a Unitarian for President because a Unitarian is not a Christian at all but is really no better than an infidel.

Ex-President Roosevelt showed signs of being a little desperate in the campaign of 1912. He made Bryan's religious attitude his own and adopted the name "Progressive," a name that Mr. Bryan had applied to himself and followers many years previous to 1912. 

The Bullmooser also advocated the Initiative, Referendum, and the Recall of Judges, populist doctrine reminiscent of the days of Simpson and Pfeffer.

When the Ex-President returned from South Africa, it was very evident that a change had come over him. His meeting with 

President Taft was not very cordial or friendly. His friends, those who had believed in him and supported him, did not understand what was the matter. They were loath to believe that he would reverse himself, treat as a "scrap of paper," the pledge he had made to the people, go back on all that he had said and stood for, including the "square deal." 

It was then and it is now very difficult if not impossible for the ordinary normal minded person to understand the reason or the reasons of Theodore Roosevelt's political conduct in 1912.

His countrymen had been generous, had conferred upon him all the honors they could. The majority entertained for him the kindliest feelings and many greatly admired and revered him.. 

His "moral malfeasance," his disloyalty to his party, his friends and himself, and the way he treated William H. Taft, the best friend he ever had, must always remain somewhat of a mystery to most of us.

Theodore Roosevelt believed in War and a number of times. in his writings and speeches had pointed to China and India as horrible examples of pacifism. He probably had a sort of contempt for Mahatma Gandhi, but a wholesome respect and a good deal of admiration for William II, Emperor of Germany. "Looking backward from the pinnacle of my years and experience I am glad to say I have no philosophy but my religion." 

Wilhelm, Ex-Kaiser, Doorn, Holland.

Roosevelt was just like that, he was not a philosopher or statesman but a religionist, a christian soldier; he was also a shrewd, practical politician and in the Presidential campaign of '12 showed that there was in his make-up a large measure of 

Machiavellism. 

Even before he returned from South Africa he commenced to plot against Taft. His faithful lieutenant, Mr. Gifford Pinchot, met him in Europe and no doubt made him thoroughly acquainted with the political situation in the United States and of course told him all about the Ballinger case and the beneficent results he expected from it.

The intrigue against the secretary of the interior was denounced by President Taft as a conspiracy to destroy Ballinger and his usefulness as a cabinet officer. It was all of that and more. The real object as the unfolding of the plot showed was to discredit Taft and his administration, weaken him as a candidate for the Presidency, and to open the way for the nomination of Roosevelt in the Republican National Convention to be held in Chicago in 

June, 1912.

At the conclusion of the Ballinger trial the majority of the investigating committee made the following report: "The charges against Mr. Ballinger appear to have had their origin in a strong feeling of animosity created by supposed differences of policy, respecting the conservation of natural resources. "The accusers evidently had this very much at heart, and evidently were disposed to take a most unfavorable view of the character and motives of anyone whom they supposed to be opposed to their views. They thus came to regard Mr. Ballinger with suspicion and to regard the most natural and innocent acts occurring in the ordinary course of department administration as furnishing of sinister purpose. The evidence has wholly failed to make out a case. "Neither any fact nor all the facts put together exhibit Mr. Ballinger as being anything but a competent and honorable gentleman, honestly and faithfully performing the duties of his high office with an eye single to the public service.

The accusers of Judge Ballinger were perhaps not at all surprised at the verdict that completely exonerated him. They had counted on such a verdict, but the prosecution was not without

In the summer of 1908 Col. Nelson of the Kansas City Star, was a guest at the White House. He and President Roosevelt got into a discussion about the third term. Col. Nelson insisted that he (Roosevelt) should reiterate his position as to a third term, or else he would be accused of bad faith and duplicity. 

The President was greatly exasperated that his former declaration could be doubted, but nevertheless he gave out a statement to the Associated Press renewing the pledge that he made in 1904that he would "under no circumstances accept another nomination for the Presidency." Ex-President Roosevelt returned from South 

Africa in June, 1910. In the following September he made a trip out west filling a number of speaking engagements-one at Ossowatoma, Kansas, the home of John Brown. It was here he denounced the United States Supreme Court and preached a doctrine he called the New Nationalism. 

On his return from the western tour he (Roosevelt) told 

George Von L. Meyer, Secretary of the Navy, that he intended to work for Taft's nomination and election. "Shortly afterwards the New York State Republican Convention assembled, Roosevelt was elected Chairman. The Convention endorsed the 

Taft administration and the Payn-Aldrich Tariff Act. After the New 

York State Convention, as the Insurgents in increasing numbers called on the former President, his determined tone became somewhat modified.

"I am not and shall not be a candidate, I shall never seek the nomination, nor would I accept it if it came to me as the result of an intrigue. * * * * * "Under no circumstances would I accept another nomination.

** * * * "I would regard it as the greatest personal calamity to be forced to accept the nomination for the Presidency. * * * * * "If the utterly unexpected happens and I am nominated I may very probably be defeated, in which case I will not only be assailed but derided. * * "If at this particular crisis, with the particular problems ahead of us at this particular time, the people feel that I am the man in sight to do the job, then I would regard myself as shirking a plain duty if I refused to do it." -From William Howard Taft, By Herbert S Duffey, Published by Minton, Balch & Co., New York.

Ex-President Roosevelt was through refusing now and was about ready to throw his "hat into the ring." "The first indication that Mr. Gifford Pinchot's intrigues were bearing fruit came shortly after the New York State Convention, when Gilson Gardner, a newspaper man, called upon the former President. After the conference Gardner sought out the Senate leader of the Insurgent movement, Robert M. La Follette, of Wisconsin. "Representing himself as the confidant of the Ex-President, Gardner informed La Follette that he (Roosevelt) had instructed him to tell the Senator that he had altered his opinion of Taft, that he now believed the Progressive Republicans should place a candidate in the field to compete with the President, and that Roosevelt wished to urge La Follette to present himself as that candidate for the Republican nomination. 

"With the approach of 1912 the equivocal role played by Roosevelt became more puzzling. Roosevelt's spokesmen were constantly reiterating and reassuring the baffled La Follette leaders that the Ex-President had no intention of becoming a candidate for the nomination. Despite these assurances, the situation became more distracting to the La Follette forces. 

At a meeting of the Progressives held at the Pinchot residence in Washington, Medill McCormick, Gifford Pinchot and Gilson Gardner informed the Wisconsin Senator that there must not be a break with Roosevelt, and that should one come, they would go over to the former President. The political situation in many states was also indicative of the fact that Roosevelt had been duping La Follette and that it was his intention in the near future to supercede him as the Progressive candidate for the Presidency." * * "On the Sunday immediately preceding the Convention, Roosevelt characterized as utter nonsense the rumor that he intended to bolt the Republican Convention, in the event that he was not nominated. Yet that was just what happened. Immediately after the regular Republican 

Convention adjourned, the Roosevelt followers assembled in Orchestra Hall, when they organized a new party which came to be known as the Progressive or Bullmoose party, ex-President Roosevelt was made the party's standard bearer and Hiram Johnson was nominated for Vice- President. Early in the campaign Roosevelt had made overtures for the

support and co-operation of La Follette, but the Wisconsin Senator rejected these advances believing himself to be the victim of Roosevelt's perfidy. 

"La Follette said: 

"In the history of American politics there has never been a primary campaign for the Presidential nomination an approach to the extravagant expenditures made in the Roosevelt campaign. Stimulated by an overmastering desire to win they denounced loyalty to conviction and principle as stubborn and selfish. In the convention they put forth no platform, no issues, and made no fight against the reactionary platform adopted. 

They substituted vulgar personalities and coarse epithets of the prize ring for the serious consideration of great economic problems and for the time being brought ridicule and contempt on a great cause. 

William Howard Taft, By Herbert S. Duffey, Published by Minton, Balch & Co., New York.

The above is quite a strong indictment of Roosevelt and his Christian Soldiers, but perhaps is not any too strong. La Follette was right in saying they put forth no issues nor any platform.

Their one and only issue was Ex-President Roosevelt-he was the "Great Cause."

The party came into existence immediately after the regular Republican Convention had refused to nominate Roosevelt for President. In Grecian mythology legend has it that Athena, goddess of Wisdom, War, etc., sprang from the head of Zeus, or 

Jupiter, a perfect, beautiful woman. Of course, it could not be said that the Bullmoose party resembled, even in the remotest way, a beautiful woman, but the fact that it sprang from the brain of Theodore Roosevelt just reminds us of the legend. 

It was distinctly a one man's party, organized for a specific purpose, that purpose being the election of Theodore Roosevelt 

President. No politician ever did a more audacious thing than did this Ex-President when he bolted the regular Republican Convention. He bolted it because a majority of the delegates voted for Taft instead of for him. He denounced those who controlled the convention as "thieves, robbers and high binders who would steal the peoples' liberties." He denounced Taft as a "hypocrite, a man of flabby intellect, and the beneficiary of fraud." He also severely condemned Elihu Root who was Chairman of the Convention.

Both of these statesmen had been in his Cabinet, they were the ablest members of his official family, and he had said just that, when he was President, and had been lavish, even fulsome, in his praise of them. Theodore Roosevelt more than once spoke of the "lunatic fringe" of that great army of supporters who were always ready to throw up their hats and hurrah for him. That he got tangled in that "fringe" in 1912 he realized and admitted. 

In 1915 he wrote, "There was an element of relief to me in the smash that came to the Progressive party. We did not have many

practical men with us. Under the circumstances the reformers tended to go into "sheer lunacy."

President Taft, who was more intimately associated with Roosevelt than anyone else, and knew him better perhaps than he (Roosevelt) knew himself, said of him, "Theodore Roosevelt is a born fighter; he believes in war, he thinks that war and a warlike spirit keep up the virility of a people. He despises a pacifist." 

It was said of President McKinley that he wept bitterly at the prospect of a war with Spain. It could not be avoided after the battleship Maine had been blown up. John D. Long, Secretary of the Navy under McKinley, was a kindly old gentleman and a good deal of a pacifist. He was president of a Peace Society up in Massachusetts. Theodore Roosevelt was Assistant Secretary of the Navy, and it was very likely the warship Maine was sent to Havana on his order. Cuba at that time belonged to Spain, and the presumption is the Spanish commander in the island resented the sending of the warship and anchoring it in the Havana harbor, probably looked upon it as an act of war, and gave the order to blow it up. Anyway if Theodore Roosevelt wasn't the cause of the war with Spain, he thoroughly approved of it, and as soon as war was declared, he resigned his position as Secretary of the Navy and organized the Rough Riders, who took a part in the battle of San Juan Hill, the major and decisive engagement of the Spanish-American War.

As remarked in another part of this booklet, Theodore Roosevelt was not a statesman or philosopher, and was as little qualified to govern this great country as William II of Germany would be. 

This government was founded by philosophers, able, patriotic men of high moral character, Washington, Tom Paine, Franklin, Hamilton, Jefferson and others. There was not one thing in common between these statesmen of the Revolution and men like Roosevelt and William, the Kaiser of Germany. The first mentioned had no religion, but their philosophy, the two latter had no philosophy but their religion, and their Credo was the same as that of the Medieval Christian Soldier: "Christ came to bring not peace but a sword."

Every age has had its philosophers and humanitarians but when this government was founded, it was the first time that so many able men, men of liberal ideas with minds entirely free from religious superstition, had come together to bring forth a "New Nation conceived in liberty and dedicated to the proposition that all men are created free and equal." 

Our Revolutionary sires believed in liberty and justice; they also believed in peace but they knew when they signed the Declaration of Independence that it meant war with England, because

George III was a Christian Soldier, he was also a German, or near German princeling. "King George the III who began his reign in 1760, resolved to be much more of a King than his two German predecessors. He could speak 

English, he claimed to glory in the name of Briton, and indeed it is not a bad name for a man without perceptible drop of English, Scotch or Welsh blood in his veins."

H. G. Wells, in the Outline of History. 

George Washington was very much of a Tory or Loyalist; he had a strong affection for England and was among the last of that group of eminent Americans to renounce his Allegiance to the Mother country. The pamphlet "Common Sense," written by Tom Paine, an Englishman, perhaps more than anything else influenced him to take the final step. 

Soon after Columbus discovered America, Spain sent her 

Christian Soldiers to Mexico, Central and South America to convert the natives to Christianity and set up a religious monarchy or autocracy such as they had in Spain, Mexico and Peru, were among the first to be invaded and conquered. The inhabitants of Peru had reached a very high state of civilization. The laws of the country were few and severe, and were rigidly enforced. 

Theft, adultery and murder were capital crimes. There were no very rich nor any very poor in Peru. No one in the land was allowed to eat the bread of idleness. Says Prescott, the Historian, except the aged and the sick. Idleness was a crime in the eyes of the law, and industry was publicly commended and stimulated by rewards.

The Peruvians were sun worshipers and built many temples dedicated to the Sun God. The most magnificent was in Cuzco, the capital city. 

Prescott in his history describes it as follows: "The interior of the temple was most worthy of admiration. It was literally a mine of gold. On the western wall was emblazoned a representation of the deity, consisting of a human countenance looking forth from amidst innumerable rays of light, which emanated from it in every direction, in the same manner as the Sun is often personified with us. The figure was engraven on a massive plate of gold of enormous dimensions, thickly powdered with emeralds and precious stones. Gold in the figurative language of the people was the Tears wept by the Sun.' Every part of the temple glowed with burnished plates and studs of the precious metal."

Probably not since the days of Babylon had there been erected. such splendid temples for Sun worship. "What a sight for Christian explorers and exploiters to gaze upon.

What blessings had the gods of Christendom provided for Pizaro and his adventurers. What a Casus belli for a war on Peru." 

H. M. Tichenor in Primitive Beliefs.

When Pizaro and his band of Christian Soldiers invaded Peru they were received as friends and kindly and hospitably treated by the natives. They were allowed quarters in the city of Caxamanka where there was a fortress and accommodations for the soldiers. Pizaro cultivated and encouraged in every way he could friendly relations with the people, and sent a message to the Inca, the Royal Chief, or Emperor of Peru, beseeching him to condescend to visit him and sup with him. He (Pizaro) was anxious to show him his profound appreciation of his kindness and hospitality. The Inca accepted the invitation; he, with his Royal 

Court, would condescend to visit the Spanish commander and sup with him.

When Pizaro received the Inca's reply he immediately began to prepare for his reception. The following account is taken from 

Prescott's History of Peru: "It was Saturday the 16th of November, 1532. The loud cry of the trumpet called the Spaniards to arms with the first streak of dawn, then Pizaro briefly acquainted them with the plan of the assault. "All received orders to wait at their posts till the arrival of the Inca.

After his entrance into the Great Square or Plaza they were still to remain under cover, withdrawn from observation until a signal was given by the discharge of a gun, when they were to shout their war cries, rush out in a body, fiercely attack the Peruvians, put them to the sword and bear off the body of the Inca. "It was a little before sunset when the Royal procession entered the gates of the city. Elevated above his vassals came Atahaullpa the Inca, borne on a sedan, or open litter, on which was a sort of throne made of massive gold of inestimable value. The Monarch's attire was rich and resplendent, and from his lofty station he looked down on the multitude below with an air of composure like one accustomed to command. He was permitted to traverse the Plaza in silence and not a Spaniard was to be seen.

When some five or six thousand of his followers had entered the place, the Inca halted and turning around with inquiring look, demanded, "Where are the strangers?" "At this moment Fray Vincente De Valverde, a Dominican Friar,

Pizarro's chaplain, and afterwards Bishop of Cuzco, came forward with breviary and Bible and a Crucifix. Approaching the Inca he told him that he came by order of his Commander to expound to him the doctrines of the true faith, for which purpose the Spaniards had come from a great distance to his country. The Friar then explained as clearly as he could the mysterious doctrine of the Trinity. Beginning with the creation of man, thence to his fall, his subsequent redemption by Jesus Christ, the crucifixion, and ascension, when the Savior left the Apostle Peter as his Viceregent on earth. This power had been transmitted to the successors of the Apostle, good and wise men, who under the title of Popes held authority over all the powers and potentates of the earth. The Friar concluded by beseeching the Peruvian Monarch to receive him kindly, to abjure the errors of his own faith, and embrace that of the Christians, the only one by which he could hope for Salvation, and furthermore to acknowledge himself a tributary of the Emperor Charles V, who in that event would aid and protect him as his Royal Vassal. When the priest

had finished the eyes of the Indian monarch flashed fire and his dark brow grew darker, as he replied, I will be no man's tributary; I am greater than any prince on earth. As for the Pope you speak of he must be crazy to talk of giving away countries that do not belong to him. I will not change my faith. Your own God you say was put to death by the very men whom he created, but mine (pointing to the Sun sinking behind the mountains) still lives in the heavens and looks down upon his children.' He then demanded of Valverde by what authority he had said these things. The Friar pointed to the book which he held as his authority. 

Taking the book the Inca turned over the pages a moment, then as the insult he had received flashed across his mind he threw it down with vehemence, and exclaimed, 'Tell your comrades that they shall give me an account of their doings in my land. I will not go from here until they have made me full satisfaction for all the wrongs they have committed.' The Friar, feeling greatly scandalized, picked up the sacred volume, and hastening to Pizarro informed him of what had happened and exclaimed, 'Do you not see that while we stand here wasting our breath talking to this dog, full of pride as he is, the field is filling with 

Indians? Set on at once, I absolve you.' "Pizarro saw the hour had come, he waved a white scarf, the fatal gun was fired from the fortress, then springing into, the square the old Spanish Captain and his followers shouted the war cry, St. Jago, up and at them.' Every Spanish soldier on horse and foot rushed into the square and threw themselves upon the Indians, who were unarmed and completely taken by surprise. Stunned by the report of artillery and muskets, and blinded by the smoke which rolled in sulphurous volume along the square, the panic-stricken Indians knew not which way to fly for refuge. Hundreds were trampled down by the fierce charge of the cavalry who dealt their blows right and left without sparing. Every avenue of escape was closed, the entrance to the square was choked up with dead bodies. Finally under the terrible pressure of their assailants they broke through the stone wall surrounding the fortress and a large body of them escaped into the country. "The Indian Monarch, stunned and bewildered, saw his faithful subjects falling around him without hardly comprehending the situation. "Some of the Spanish soldiers, fearing that he might escape, attempted to put an end to his life but Pizarro, who was nearest to the Royal prisoner, defended him, in doing so received a wound on his arm, and his was the only wound inflicted on a Spanish soldier during the fierce and terrible struggle. "The Inca was bound with chains and placed in the fortress. His fate became known and soon spread through the town and country. The charm that might have held the Peruvians together was broken and every man now thought only of his own safety. "The Inca pleaded with Pizarro to spare his life and to regain his freedom offered to fill the room with gold as high as he could reachand standing on tiptoe set a mark on the wall. The chamber was 17 feet by 22 feet and the mark set was 9 feet from the floor. The Inca also agreed to fill an adjoining room twice with silver, and agreed that all should be done in two months. Pizarro accepted the offer, and couriers were sent out of the country, and the devotion of the people was such that the ransom poured in, but a little too slowly for the Spaniards, who, seeing the shining heap of treasure, some of it being plates of gold and silver weighing twenty-five pounds, found their avarice sharpened. Some of the ransom came in the shape of goblets, ewers, vases, as well as fine ornaments, one a fountain of gold with birds and animals of the same

metal about the base, being of such beauty as to awaken the admiration of the soldiery. But beautiful or not, it was all broken up and melted into ingots and the total amount of the gold alone was $15,500,000.00, the most magnificent ransom that history records. But it was all of no avail to save Atahualpa, the Inca. "The day came, Aug. 29th, 1533, when he was led out chained hand and foot and taken to the place where he was to be burned alive. At the last moment the priest Valverde offered him baptism, promising if he would accept the Christian faith, he would die by the garrote instead of by fire. "Imploring Pizarro to protect his young children, he recovered his stoical bearing and died at the hands of those who had ravished his country and destroyed a race of people that had reached a high state of civilization."

Hernando Cortez did the same bloody work in Mexico that Pizarro did in Peru. He destroyed the Aztic government and established in its place a Christian Soldier government, the Catholic Church. 

The year 313 A. D. was an important epoch in the history of the human race. Constantine, so called the Great, became Emperor of Rome that year. He came after Nero, Caligula Commodus, and Diocletian, The Roman Empire was decaying; it had become too corrupt to live. 

The following is from the pen of Henry Thomas, Ph.D. It is an interesting, as well as illuminating, historical account of 

Constantine, Emperor of Rome, (313-337). "Constantine came to the throne by the regular Roman method of killing off all the other candidates. His father was a governor under

Diocletian, his mother, Helena, the daughter of an innkeeper, was a Christian. He inherited his father's ambition and in order to carry out that ambition, he adopted his mother's religion. As he was fighting in one of the Civil wars against Maxentius, one of his competitors for the throne, he was not very successful, he had called upon the Pagan Gods to help him, but these Gods were apparently busy with other matters for he kept on losing ground. Finally in desperation he prayed to Jesus

Christ, the God of his mother, he won the next battle and attributed his success to his prayers, and that is how Christ the Prince of Peace became the War God of the Roman Empire. "In order to convince the superstitious Romans that Christ had really helped him in his battles, he resorted to the old subterfuge, the miracle. 

He said that while he was marching against Maxentius, he saw in the sky a huge fiery cross inscribed with the following words: 'In hoc signo vinces,' by this sign thou shalt conquer. "Constantine was so elated with his new Christian War God, that he made Christianity the State religion of Rome. Up to that time Christianity had been the gentle creed of peace lovers and nonresisters. It now became the arrogant religion of aggressive fighters. It was no longer 'Onward Roman warriors,' the new battle cry was, 'Onward Christian Soldiers,' and at their head marched the tired little Jew of Galilee with a heavy wooden cross upon his back. "Having adopted Christianity, or what he mistook for Christianity as the State religion of Rome, the Emperor murdered his wife, his oldest son and a nephew, and then settled down to rule Rome with a new kind of despotism. He helped to organize the Church upon the model of the

For several hundred years the Christian world was engaged in warfare with Mohammedanism. There were nine expeditions to the Holy Land to rescue the tomb of the Savior from the Moslems or Turks. These religious wars were known as the Crusades. "The leader of the first Crusade was Peter, the Hermit, a half witted monk who went all over France and Germany, preaching hate against the Moslems and raising an army to make war upon and destroy them. He was encouraged and aided by Pope Urban the II, who called a council of the clergy as well as the laity at the city of Clermont (1095). He told his audience that if they enlisted in this holy Crusade they would not only find favor in the sight of Christ, but there was a wonderful opportunity for looting; Palestine was a rich and prosperous country, inhabited by Jews and Turks, all infidels and everyone deserving death at the hands of the Christian Soldiers, all of their possessions were legitimate booty and there would be an equitable distribution of it among the faithful followers of the Army of the Lord. "After the Pope's address, the religious fervor and enthusiasm of the people was very great; they clamored for a leader, and demanded that they be allowed to go forth at once to make war upon the enemies of Christianity. The effect of the above list on one who has never been blessed with any great riches is very impressive indeed. The feeling that grips the average individual as he scans the nst is, what a wonderful thing it must be to be on the United States pension roll and draw down from one hundred six and a quarter to two hundred and twenty-five bucks-not per annum, but per month-and for life, that is the best part of it. No more financial worries. Depressions may come and depressions may go, but it cannot touch the above fortunate ones. On the contrary, the present depression has been quite a boon to these favorites of fate. The cost of living has descended to about one-half of what it was when these pensions were granted. So the purchasing power of the one hundred six and a quarter bucks has grown to two hundred twelve and a half, and the two hundred twenty-five berries has leaped or mounted to four hundred and fifty berries per month.

Now there is the case of young lawyer X. Before he was drafted he was practising law. He was not exactly a briefless young attorney, but approximately so. He had a great deal of trouble balancing his budget. He was sent to France and on the way over contracted a bad cold. He went into a hospital as soon as he reached the other side. The cold seemed to go to his head, throat and lungs, so he had a complication of diseases-asthma, pneumonia, influenza and a young physician pronounced it a case of cocci-streptositic myocarditis. He partially recovered, was sent home and later on applied for a pension under the Disability Act. It was granted, so he is now receiving regularly two hundred and twenty-five bucks per month. He has built himself a nice home, furnished it, has a beautiful car, a fine growing family and he has made some profitable investments. He bought some vacant lots, put up some cheap homes on them and rented them to some young men with families. He collected the rents regularly as long as the boys had jobs. When the depression came, all of them lost their jobs but one, who is working for the government. 

So only one of his homes is rented now.

The writer has heard hints of fraud in this pension roll. It does seem that there is room for the practice of fraud under such a statute as the Disability Act. 

What is total disability? Is it really a disease, or just an appetite? Is it physical, mental, moral or a combination of the three? Is it a contagious disorder like appendicitis and "tonsil removal"? What if there is a case of fraud in the above roll?

What if a pensioner is getting a pension that he is not entitled to, i. e., getting money under false pretenses from the U. S. treasury?

It does not necessarily mean that the ex-soldier is a bad man. 

He is a better man than his medical advisor who testified to his disability, and both of them are better men than the U. S. senator and U. S. representate who voted for such a law as the Disability 

Act. If a crime has been committed, the two last are the principals, and the two first mentioned are accessories to the crimefor in the law itself is the initiative and motive for the crime. 

Even if there is no fraud in this pension roll, the very size of the monthly payments to the veterans constitute a crime against the taxpayers of this country. What reason, what wisdom, what common sense, sanity or justice is there in paying out such huge sums to men who never did anything to earn the money? 

The amount our government will pay to the veterans by 1945 will be $21,500,000,000.00. The ordinary mind can hardly conceive of the immensity of such a sum. It is about four times as much money as there is in circulation in the United States. It is perhaps twice as much as all the gold and silver money in existence. 

Where is such a tremendous sum of money to come from? Is there any other source from which it can come except from those who work? Isn't it just a tax on labor and nothing else? Isn't it from those who toil and sweat in the factories, on the farms and in the mines and forests that these billions must be collected by tax gatherers, put into the U. S. treasury and from there syphoned out into the pockets of the ex-soldiers? What did the U. S. Congress have in mind when they voted to donate to the veterans more than twenty thousand million dollars. Did they think they had earned it, or was just a bribe to the soldiers for the votes and influences?

If the U. S. Congress had started out after the war to corrupt the ex-soldiers and bankrupt the taxpayers, could they have done any better than they have done? Do they think there is no limit to the tax burden they can impose on the people of this country? 

Some observers have come to the conclusion that the average congressman never thinks about anything except his reelection, which he seeks by every possible means, fair or foul, to bring about.

A few years ago there was a primary election in the state of Pennsylvania. Its results were something of a revelation to the ordinary citizen. It showed what a high regard candidates for the U. S. Congress have for senatorial honors, and what an enormous money value they place upon the same. 

An investigation revealed the following:

Leaving ethics out of it, what are we to think of the business judgment of men willing to pay such prices for a $10,000 job? 

The first named had been a member of the U. S. Senate. Mr. 

Pinchot was one of ex-President Roosevelt's chief lieutenants, his most trusted Christian Soldier. Mr. Vare was a political statesman of type well known in this country, indeed, that is very numerous both inside and outside the U. S. Congress. But Mr. Vare evidently was slandered too much. The difference between him and his slanderers was not enough to justify any abuse or criticism.

Participation in the World War cost our country between twenty and thirty billions of dollars. Since the end of the war, the United States congress has voted more billions than that to the ex-soldiers. As stated above, the amount, according to Gen. 

Hines, that the government will pay by 1945 to the veterans in pensions, bonuses, etc., will be twenty-one thousand five hundred million dollars. What will be paid after that is problematical. 

It cannot be accurately estimated. The amount will depend on the lives of that great army on the pension roll. 

An examination will no doubt show that the experience of the government with pensioners is that the relation between disability-total or otherwise-and longevity is a very intimate one. 

It is quite safe to predict that for forty years after 1945 the business of paying pensions will be a very large and important one, and it looks now as if up to 1945 and even much longer one of the chief functions of our government is going to be to collect taxes and turn the same over to the Pension Bureau for distribution to the ex-soldiers.

Our government is now paying to ex-soldiers for pensions, hospital service, etc., around one billion dollars a year. This amount, under pension laws in force, will probably increase for several years to come, and by 1975 or 1985 there will be another thirty or forty billions added to the $21,500,000,000. These vast, incredible, almost inconceivable sums have been deliberately im-

posed on the people by the United States Congress. Two or three generations will feel this heavy burden. The present one and two yet unborn. Two or three generations must toil and sweat, live in poverty, skimp, deny themselves the comforts and many of the necessities in order that the great army on the pension roll, with their families, may be supported in ease and idleness. 

The people should not stand for this incredible, infinite outrage, but should rise up in wrath and their might and demand. that every dollar of war indebtedness, including pensions, be wiped out by 1945.

The ex-soldiers are not to blame for the situation. The senators and representatives are the guilty ones. They are sent to 

Washington to represent all the people, not any particular group. 

We would like to address a few questions to Senator McSwillington and Representative A. Von Billike. How did you arrive at the large amount that you have donated to the veterans? Did you make a careful and conscientious study of the problem of pensions, the bonuses, etc.? Did you make any effort to find out what would be right, just and fair to both the taxpayer and the ex-soldiers? Of course you did not. You have no inclination, no ability or genius to do anything of that kind. You evidently had but one idea, and that was to make yourself solid with the veterans, to be so liberal and generous to them that they would not fail to remember you on election day. You gave no thought to the taxpayers nor to where these billions were to come from. 

It is very likely both of you are machine-made statesmen, and take orders from the political organization in the city from which you come, an organization that has its counterpart in nearly every municipality of any size in our country, all modeled after Tammany Hall.

Now there is the pension roll. No one knows better than you that there is fraud in it. Will you take the initiative and move for an investigation of it? Of course you will not. You haven't near that much courage. You both would become lion-tamers before you would do such a thing. You have a wonderful amount of nerve when it comes to criticising and abusing the opposite party, and misrepresenting and vilifying the president of the United States. But when it comes to standing up for what is just and right, regardless of your own interest and that of the party and the organization that nominated and elected you, you have just as much backbone as an empty gunny sack-no more. 

You are not representing the people at Washington. You are representing yourself, and the veterans, and the organization that nominated and elected you.

The majority of the people of the United States approved of the above, and there wasn't a soldier boy that went to France to fight Germany that did not thoroughly understand what he was fighting for.
