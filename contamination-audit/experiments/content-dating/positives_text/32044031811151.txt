CHAPTER I. 

JURISDICTION. 

The exclusive jurisdiction in matters of divorce in Maryland is vested by statute in the Equity 

Courts. 

Code, Art. 16, secs. 37-42. 

Amended Act 1939, Ch. 558. 

The concurrent jurisdiction formerly exercised by the Legislature was withdrawn by the Constitution of 1851.

Md. Const., Art. III, sec. 21. 

The statute conferring jurisdiction on the courts of equity limits and prescribes such jurisdictions. 

Murray vs. Murray, 134 Md. 653.

The Equity Courts in such cases sit as divorce courts and not by virtue of their general equitable jurisdiction. Insofar as is consistent with the statutes relating to divorce, the Equity Courts are governed by the principles established by the ecclesiastic courts in England prior to the American Revolution. 

Fisher vs. Fisher, 93 Md. 298.

For causes arising within this State, the Court has jurisdiction without regard to the duration of the domicile herein. 

Adams vs. Adams, 101 Md. 507.

DIVORCE AND ANNULMENT 

In the above case adultery was committed in this State, both husband and wife being non-residents, but the wife established a domicile here a few months prior to the filing of her bill of complaint. 

For causes arising outside the State, either the Plaintiff or Defendant must have resided within this State for more than two years next preceding the filing of the action.

Code, Art. 16, sec. 40. 

In such cases the burden rests on the complainant to satisfactorily prove such two years' residence herein.

Willingham vs. Willingham, 162 Md. 539. 

VENUE. 

The proceeding must be filed in the County or City of Baltimore wherein either the complainant or the respondent resides. If the defendant be a non-resident, then it must be filed where the complainant resides.

Code, Art. 16, sec. 37. 

FOREIGN DIVORCES. 

"A state cannot exercise through its courts jurisdiction to dissolve a marriage when neither spouse is domiciled within the state". 

American Law Institute, "Conflict of Laws", sec. 111.

Thus notwithstanding the full faith and credit clause of the Federal Constitution, and the rules of

comity among the states, a decree of divorce rendered in one state is frequently impeached and denied recognition in another state on the ground of lack of domicile of the parties therein. 

Walker vs. Walker, 125 Md. 649.

In the above case the husband's claim to domicile in Nevada was fictitious. Held: Irrespective of whether or not the summons was served on the wife in this state, the Nevada Court had no jurisdiction. 

The Court further held that in order to constitute a change of domicile, it must be with the intention not so much of their remaining but of the abandonment of the former domicile as a place of residence, and excludes any definite intention to return to the place of previous domicile.

CHAPTER II. 

DIVORCE A VINCULO MATRIMONII. 

The grounds for divorce a vinculo matrimonii are provided by statute and are five in number, as follows:

First: Impotence of either party at the time of the marriage.

Second: Any cause which by the laws of this 

State render a marriage null and void ab initio. 

Third: Adultery.

Fourth: When the Court shall be satisfied by competent testimony that the party complained against has abandoned the party complaining, and that such abandonment has continued uninterruptedly for at least three years, and is deliberate and final, and the separation of the parties beyond any reasonable expectation of reconciliation. 

Fifth: When the husband and wife shall have voluntarily lived separate and apart, without any cohabitation, for five consecutive years prior to the filing of the bill of complaint, and such separation is beyond any reasonable expectation of reconciliation.

Code, Art. 16, sec. 38 (as amended by Act 

1937, Chapter 390; also, 1939, Chapter 538).

FIRST. 

IMPOTENCE. 

Where by organic defect of the male or female, existing at the time of marriage, there cannot be a natural and perfect coition-vera copula—between the parties, and the defect is permanent and incurable, the case comes within the legal definition of impotence, and will authorize a divorce a vinculo. 

J. G. vs. H. G., 33 Md. 401-405.

SECOND. 

MARRIAGES WHICH BY THE LAWS OF MARY- LAND ARE VOID AB INITIO. 

The exact meaning and limits of this ground have never been defined or ruled on. No statute declares any class of marriage void ab initio.

By the common law, a marriage of a person under the age of 7 years was void, and therefore is in Maryland. This is also true of a marriage by one already married.

The statute prohibiting miscegenation impliedly renders such a marriage void.

Code, Art. 27, sec. 365, provides: 

"All marriages between a white person and a negro, or between a white person and a person of negro descent to the third generation, inclusive, are forever prohibited, and shall be void, etc." 

In Jackson vs. Jackson, 82 Md. at page 30, the 

Court said: "The statutes of Maryland preemptorily forbid the marriage of a white person and a negro and declare all such marriages forever void. It is, therefore, the declared policy of this State to prohibit such marriages.” 

Under Code, Art. 62, secs. 1 and 2, giving a table of prohibited degrees embracing both lineal and collateral relations, those by consanguinity as well as by affinity, and pronouncing them alike void, with-

out regard to the degrees of consanguinity or affinity, same must be understood as void upon judgment or decree, for that purpose found or passed, and not prima facie void. It does not necessarily mean void ab initio. 

Harrison vs. State, 22 Md. 468.

Marriages which are voidable, and not void, are not included in this ground for divorce a vinculo. 

Thus, duress, coercion, drunkenness, insanity (not then adjudicated) at the time of the marriage ceremony render the marriage voidable only. Same may be ratified. 

Brown vs. Scott, 140 Md. 258 (gross fraud as to character and social position of husband). 

Montgomery vs. U'Nertle, 143 Md. 200 (drunkenness). 

By the statute of 15 George 2, Ch. 30, it is declared that the marriage of a person under commission of lunacy shall be "null and void to all intents and purposes whatsoever".

Bishop in his work on "Marriage, Divorce and Separation", Vol. I, sec. 602, states this statute is of too recent date to be common law with us. In 

Ewing vs. Moore (Md.) unreported, but referred to in 2 Alexander's British Statutes (2nd Ed.), 1014, it was held that the marriage of a lunatic not included in the class set out in 15 George 2, C. 30, are not void, but are voidable only.

By analogy, the marriage of a person, previously adjudicated lunatic under the laws of Maryland, is ground for divorce a vinculo. Where, however, one of the parties is insane, but not so adjudicated at the time of the marriage, same may be ratified in a lucid interval. 

The fact that a religious ceremony is essential to a marriage in this State, does not require that the ceremony and consent be simultaneous. Therefore, if a person insane at time of marriage ceremony should thereafter, in a lucid interval, ratify same, the marriage is no longer void nor voidable. See:

Bishop, Marriage, Divorce and Separation 

(1891 Ed.), Vol. 1, sec. 614.

ADULTERY. 

Adultery "is the voluntary sexual intercourse of a married person with a person other than the offender's husband or wife”. 

Bouvier's Law Dict. (1926 Ed.), page 51.

Gieselman vs. Gieselman, 134 Md. 453. 

Miller vs. Miller, 140 Md. 60.

Where the parties are both guilty of adultery¹, or where the wronged party has connived at the offense² or condoned it,² relief will be denied.

FOURTH. 

ABANDONMENT. 

The essentials of abandonment within the purview of the Maryland statute are as follows: (1) Ceasing to live together as husband and wife; (2) It must be with the intent on the part of the party complained of that the marriage relation should no longer exist; (3) It must be without justification on consent; (4) It must endure for three years continuously prior to the filing of the suit for divorce; (5) Without reasonable expectation of reconciliation.

The fact of desertion may be proven by a variety of circumstances, as for instance, leaving with a declared intention never to return to his wife; marrying another woman; living in adultery abroad; absence for a long time; not being necessarily detained by occupation or business, or otherwise; husband making no provision for wife, being of ability to do so; providing no home for her or prohibiting her from following him. 

Gill vs. Gill, 93 Md. 652-655.

Husband's refusal to resume relations constitutes abandonment. 

Barnett vs. Barnett, 144 Md. 189.

Sheets vs. Sheets, 173 Md. 692.

FIFTH. 

VOLUNTARY SEPARATION OF THE PARTIES FOR FIVE YEARS CONTINUOUSLY PRIOR TO THE SUIT. 

Whenever a husband and wife voluntarily shall have lived separate and apart for five consecutive years prior to the filing of the bill of complaint, and such separation is beyond any reasonable expectation of reconciliation, the Court may decree a divorce a vinculo matrimonii to either party. 

Chapter 396, Laws of Md., 1937, (Code, Art. 16, sec. 38). 

"The important question of fact in such a case is whether the separation of the parties has been voluntary." 

Campbell vs. Campbell, 174 Md. 229.

There must be a physical separation of the parties by common consent, with a common intent not to resume marital relations, physical separation without such intent is insufficient. 

France vs. Safe Dep. & Tr. Co., 4 Atl. (2nd) 717.

If the separation is caused by the husband's misconduct in making a violent attack on the wife, that would in itself, and apart from other considerations, prevent the separation from being mutually voluntary within the purview of the Act. 

Campbell vs. Campbell, 174 Md. 229-236.

The statute is retroactive. 

Campbell vs. Campbell, 174 Md. 229.

A preponderance of evidence is sufficient on question of separation being voluntary and without reasonable hope of reconciliation. 

France vs. Safe Dep. & Trust Co., 4 Atl. (2d) 717-719.

CHAPTER III. 

DIVORCE A MENSA ET THORO. 

This is a partial divorce; one not from the bonds of matrimony but merely from bed and board. It is "practically nothing more than a request for judicial permission to live separate and apart, and which must result in the condition described by an eminent judge, of throwing the parties back upon society in the indefinite and dangerous character of ‘a wife without a husband and a husband without a wife' " 

Gellar vs. Gellar, 159 Md. 236, 241.

Such a divorce does not destroy the spouse's rights in each other's property, such as dower and rights as heir. 

Hokamp vs. Hagaman, 36 Md. 517.

Property held by the entireties is by such a divorce converted into a tenancy in common. 

Reed vs. Reed, 109 Md. 690.

But the statute gives to the Court full authority to award to the wife, as part of the decree, such

properties as she had when married, or otherwise the value thereof. The decree may be forever or for a limited time; it is revocable by the Court upon joint application of the parties. Such a divorce may be decreed although a divorce a vinculo is prayed, provided the causes be sufficient to entitle the party thereto.

Code, Art. 16, sec. 39. 

There are three causes for a divorce a mensa et thoro: (1) cruelty of treatment, (2) excessively vicious conduct, and (3) abandonment and desertion.

Code, Art. 16, sec. 39. 

These three causes are exclusive.

Etheridge vs. Etheridge, 120 Md. 12. 

The law of this State looks with disfavor on such divorces.¹ To obtain such a divorce upon any of the grounds contained in the statute, the causes of separation should be grave and weighty, and such as to show that the duties of married life cannot be properly sustained.² 

¹ Bonwit vs. Bonwit, 169 Md. 189-194; Porter vs. Porter, 168

CHAPTER IV. 

CORROBORATION. 

The testimony of a plaintiff in a divorce case must be corroborated.

Code, Art. 35, sec. 4. 

Admission of ground for divorce by the respondent shall not be taken as conclusive.

Code, Art. 16, sec. 42. 

This statutory requirement being principally intended to prevent collusion, little corroborative evidence is required where there is no appearance of collusion. 

Roeder vs. Roeder, 170 Md. 579.

The corroboration must extend to every element in the plaintiff's testimony necessary to justify the relief sought, but need not in itself be sufficient to warrant that relief, nor go to every particular statement in the plaintiff's testimony, it being sufficient if it lends substantial support to that testimony as regards material and controlling facts.

Jacobs vs. Jacobs, 170 Md. 405-409. 

Corroboration is to be distinguished from burden of proof, and may be satisfied although the weight of the entire testimony is in favor of the defendant. 

Porter vs. Porter, 168 Md. 296.

CHAPTER V. 

SEPARATE MAINTENANCE. 

A wife may sue for alimony, although she does not ask for divorce. 

McCaddin vs. McCaddin, 116 Md. 567. 

Hood vs. Hood, 138 Md. 360.

Such action must be brought in the jurisdiction wherein the defendant resides. It cannot be amended to a divorce proceeding in which alimony is made an incident thereto. 

Woodcock vs. Woodcock, 169 Md. 40-46-48.

The requirements of a bill for permanent alimony without a divorce, are the same as those for a divorce a vinculo or a mensa et thoro. 

Outlaw vs. Outlaw, 118 Md. 498. 

Hood vs. Hood, 138 Md. 355, 361. 

Wilson vs. Wilson, 152 Md. 632. 

Miller vs. Miller, 159 Md. 204-206.

The causes must be sufficient to support a decree for divorce either a vinculo or a mensa et thoro. 

Wendel vs. Wendel, 154 Md. 11-20. 

Ayares vs. Ayares, 163 Md. 388, 396. 

Kirkwood vs. Kirkwood, 165 Md. 547, 553. 

Simmont vs. Simmont, 160 Md. 422, 425.

The testimony in support of the bill must be sufficient to prove such facts as would have entitled the complainant to a divorce a vinculo or a mensa. 

Silverberg vs. Silverberg, 148 Md. 682, 691. 

Melson vs. Melson, 151 Md. 196, 205.

The burden of establishing by a preponderance of evidence the facts relied upon rests on the complainant as in an action for divorce. 

Silverberg vs. Silverberg, 148 Md. 682.

If the obligation for corroboration of the plaintiff's testimony, imposed by Code, Art. 25, sec. 4, applies to a proceeding for permanent alimony without a divorce, the corroboration need be but slight, the nature of the proceeding being such as to exclude the idea of collusion. Cf. 

Engelberth vs. Engelberth, 159 Md. 700. 

Silverberg vs. Silverberg, 148 Md. 682, and 

Weigand vs. Weigand, 155 Md. 643, 648.

CHAPTER VI. 

ALIMONY. 

Equity has inherent jurisdiction to award alimony as fully as were the ecclesiastical courts of England. 

Outlaw vs. Outlaw, 118 Md. 498, 502.

This power has been recognized and continuously exercised by equity since 1689. By the Act of 1771, Ch. 12 (Code, Art. 16, sec. 14), courts of equity are empowered to hear and determine all causes for alimony as fully as same could be heard and determined by the laws of England in the ecclesiastical courts there. That is insofar as said principles are consistent with our Code. 

Emerson vs. Emerson, 120 Md. 589 (Cf. dissenting opinion).

"Alimony is the money allowance payable under a judicial decree by a husband to his wife, or former wife, during their joint lives or until the remarriage of the wife, so long as they live separately, for her support and maintenance."

Knabe vs. Knabe, (Md.) 6 Atl. (2nd) 366. 

"In the case of death of one of the parties, the remarriage of the wife or their mutual consent to live together, alimony ceases.' 

Hood vs. Hood, 138 Md. 355. 

Knabe vs. Knabe, (Md.) 6 Atl. (2nd) 366, 369.

A provision in a decree for divorce based upon an agreement between the parties for the payment "as alimony" of a gross sum in installments in discharge of all claims by the wife against the husband by way of alimony or otherwise, not being limited to the joint lives, was not a provision for alimony. 

Bushman vs. Bushman, 157 Md. 166, 171, 173.

A decree requiring the husband to pay charges for taxes, public dues and assessments, ground rent and mortgage interest against the home owned by him and his wife as tenants by the entireties, cannot be supported as an award of alimony. 

Roberts vs. Roberts, 160 Md. 513, 522, 523.

The remarriage of a divorced wife extinguishes the husband's obligation to pay under an award of alimony, but does not have such effect as regards

an allowance to the wife during her life, which although termed alimony, is not such in fact but is the result of the incorporation in the decree of an agreement between the parties. 

Spear vs. Spear, 158 Md. 672.

The remarriage of the husband does not relieve him from alimony payments to his former wife. 

Marshall vs. Marshall, 164 Md. 107.

On award to the wife and children in a decree a mensa of the exclusive possession of the home owned by the parties as tenants by the entireties was improper in absence of evidence that same was necessary for the effective operation of the decree of divorce or for the wife's protection. 

Roberts vs. Roberts, 160 Md. 513.

AMOUNT OF ALIMONY. 

In general, the allowance and amount of alimony are matters resting in the sound discretion of the chancellor, to be exercised with reference to established principles and upon a view of all the circumstances, including the husband's estate, his capacity to work, and the condition and means of his wife. 

Fairbank vs. Fairbank, 169 Md. 212, 217.

In allowing alimony, expenses chargeable against the wife or against the property which she and her husband own as tenants by the entireties, are to be considered, together with the financial circum-

stances of the parties, their station in life, expenses incurred by the wife as a result of the husband's failure adequately to provide for his family, the age and physical condition of the parties and the children, the expense of educating the latter, and other facts and circumstances. 

Roberts vs. Roberts, 160 Md. 513-523.

In fixing the amount of alimony, the court may in its discretion in lieu of testimony, accept the agreement of the parties in regard thereto, and if fairly made and not in aid of a divorce, may incorporate same in the decree.

Emerson vs. Emerson, 120 Md. 584. 

Dickey vs. Dickey, 154 Md. 675. 

Bushman vs. Bushman, 157 Md. 166.

MODIFICATION OF AWARD OF ALIMONY. 

Alimony allowed in either a decree awarding a divorce a mensa et thoro or a vinculo, or in a decree for alimony alone, is subject to modification by the Court from time to time as to conform to changed conditions.

Emerson vs. Emerson, 120 Md. 584. 

Marshall vs. Marshall, 162 Md. 116 (disapproving dicta to the contrary in Mc- 

Caddin vs. McCaddin, 116 Md. 562). 

Knabe vs. Knabe, 6 Atl. (2nd) 366, 369.

A decree of divorce, which in accordance with an agreement of the parties, makes provision for the

wife, beyond the period and scope of alimony allowances, is not subject to modification. 

Spear vs. Spear, 158 Md. 672. 

Marshall vs. Marshall, 162 Md. 116, 110.

The obligation of the father to support the infant offspring may be a factor in determining the amount of alimony which should be awarded the wife, to whom their custody has been awarded. 

Bushman vs. Bushman, 151 Md. 166. 

Cohen vs. Cohen, 174 Md. 61.

Alimony pendente lite will not be allowed to a wife who has ample means of her own. 

Hood vs. Hood, 138 Md. 360.

ENFORCEMENT OF ALIMONY AWARD. 

If the allowance falls within the definition of alimony, the Court has power to enforce same, whether said allowance is founded on the agreement of the parties or otherwise. 

Emerson vs. Emerson, 120 Md. 584, 597. 

Dickey vs. Dickey, 154 Md. 675, 681.

However, if the allowance, though termed alimony in the decree, does not fall within said definition, it is merely a debt and not enforceable by imprisonment. 

Bushman vs. Bushman, 157 Md. 166, 174.

Though provisions for support of the children and maintenance of the wife are commonly embraced in the same decree, those for the support of the

children are not alimony, but merely debt, and consequently cannot be enforced by imprisonment as for contempt of Court. 

Bushman vs. Bushman, 157 Md. 166, 170171.

This is done not only for convenience and clarity, but in order that the enforcement of the decree may remain in the more flexible and helpful jurisdiction of a court of equity and subject to the punitive processes which it may use to enforce its decrees rather than be limited to the inflexible and unproductive remedies provided by the criminal law. 

Knabe vs. Knabe, (Md.) 6 Atl. (2nd) 366, 371.

CHAPTER VII. 

CUSTODY OF OFFSPRING. 

The custody and support of a child may be secured under the divorce statute, irrespective of whether the divorce is granted or denied. 

Code, Art. 16, sec. 39. 

Simmont vs. Simmont, 160 Md. 422.

Another method is provided by the general custody statute under which original jurisdiction is conferred on Courts of Equity as to the custody and support of children, upon petition filed by the father, mother, or the child itself, by a relative or next friend.

Code, Art. 16, sec. 80.

The Court has power to award permanent custody or guardianship of an infant. 

Alston vs. Thomas, 161 Md. 617.

In such cases the interests of the child are paramount. 

Kartman vs. Kartman, 163 Md. 21. 

In Barnard vs. Godfrey, 157 Md. 264, after the parties had been divorced in Pennsylvania, by a decree which made no provision for custody or support of the children, the father filed a petition for custody under the above statute. The Maryland 

Court awarded the children to the wife and decreed the father should pay $200 per month for their support.

Upon grant of a divorce for the wife's adultery, the custody of a child of the marriages was properly awarded to the husband, since this would involve the care of the child by the husband's mother. 

Durr vs. Durr, 161 Md. 66-70-71. 

But where the father has not repudiated his moral obligation, the child cannot sue the father in equity to secure an increase in the amount of the father's contribution. 

Yost vs. Yost, 172 Md. 128 (divorce in 

Pennsylvania, father was granting the mother adequate support for child regularly).

AMOUNT OF SUPPORT MONEY. 

In allowing alimony, it is better practice to separate the allowance for the support of the wife from that of minor children, since the latter is not alimony and is based upon different principles. 

Roberts vs. Roberts, 160 Md. 513, 524.

In a case wherein the Court awarded one of the minor children to the wife, it was error for the chancellor to fail to require the husband to provide for its maintenance. 

Simmont vs. Simmont, 160 Md. 422, 433.

Parties to divorce proceedings cannot by agreement between themselves fix the amount necessary for the support and maintenance of their minor children, so as to bind the courts, but a court may adopt the amount agreed upon and incorporate it in the decree, in which case it may modify the decree in this respect, when it shall be made satisfactorily apparent that new or changed conditions or circumstances make a modification necessary. 

Kriedo vs. Kriedo, 159 Md. 229, 232.

Slacum vs. Slacum, 158 Md. 107. 

This is now expressly so provided by statute 

(Code, Art. 16, sec. 39-A). 

Provisions in a divorce decree as to custody and maintenance of a child in accordance with a previous agreement of the parties, are not contractual in their nature, but were an adjudication by the

chancellor in the exercise of his chancery jurisdiction. 

Slacum vs. Slacum, 158 Md. 107, 110-111.

Provision for support will be subject to change from time to time as to material alteration in the substantial circumstances. 

Slacum vs. Slacum, 158 Md. 107, 110-111. 

A daughter 18 years old is a minor child for the purpose of a divorce decree allowing support for minor children. 

Roberts vs. Roberts, 160 Md. 513, 524.

CHAPTER VIII. 

AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSES TO DIVORCE ACTIONS. 

In addition to the negative defense, i. e., refutation of the truth of the grounds of divorce, either a mensa or a vinculo, the respondent may defeat the action by an affirmative defense, to wit: by (a) res adjudicata, (b) condonation, (c) recrimination, and (d) estoppel. 

(a) Res Adjudicata. 

The doctrine of res adjudicata is applicable to divorce proceedings. 

Wagner vs. Wagner, 76 Md. 311. 

Yost vs. Yost, 172 Md. 128 (foreign decree).

As in other instances where this doctrine applies, the general rule is that the parties are bound by the

prior adjudication not only on all matters in controversy, but also as to all facts and points, which with reasonable diligence might have been then presented. 

Bowers vs. Bowers, 135 Md. 453.

In a separate maintenance action, the court in denying permanent alimony, necessarily decided that no grounds for divorce were shown, therefore the same facts cannot be the basis for an action of divorce. 

Wilcox vs. Wilcox, 150 Md. 119.

Where a divorce on the ground of abandonment is denied, the same period of time involved therein cannot be made the basis of a subsequent suit. 

Dicus vs. Dicus, 131 Md. 87. 

Boggs vs. Boggs, 138 Md. 422. (b) Condonation. "Condonation", says Mr. Bishop, "is the remission, by one of the named parties, of an offense which he knows the other has committed against the marriage, on the condition of being continuously afterward treated by the other with conjugal kindness."

Bishop, M., D. & S. (1891 Ed.), Vol. 2, sec. 269. 

Full knowledge is requisite. 

Duckett vs. Duckett, 143 Md 551, 559. 

Daiger vs. Daiger, 154 Md. 501. 

Kremis vs. Kremis, 163 Md. 223.

The condonation need not be express; it will be presumed from conduct such as the voluntary cohabitation with knowledge.

(c) Recrimination. 

Recrimination in divorce law applies where each party has a ground for divorce, a mensa or a vinculo,

against the other, whether the same¹ or a different ground from that relied on by the other party;2 but ground for a vinculo divorce is not barred by recrimination which constitutes ground merely for a decree a mensa.3 

In Green vs. Green, 125 Md. 141, 145, the Court stated that it was an application of the clean hands doctrine.

CHAPTER IX. 

OTHER DEFENSES. 

A divorce may be denied for certain grounds, though not raised by the respondent, to wit: connivance, collusion, laches and estoppel.

Ground justifying an absolute divorce is not barred by recrimination consisting of conduct which constitutes a ground merely for a decree a mensa et thoro. Pryor vs. Pryor, 146 Md. 683;

If recrimination appears though not set up, and accidently disclosed by examiner's question, it is a bar to divorce. Green vs.

CONNIVANCE. 

Connivance in divorce law is the married party's corrupt consenting to evil conduct in the other whereof afterwards he complains.

This defense arises most frequently in actions based upon adultery.

Appletoft vs. Appletoft, 147 Md. 603. 

Tabeling vs. Tabeling, 157 Md. 429. 

Fonger vs. Fonger, 160 Md. 610, wherein the husband's connivance in his wife's adultery was held to bar him from relief.

COLLUSION. 

"Collusion in divorce law is a corrupt combining of married parties to procure a sentence or judicial order by some false practice.”

Bishop, M., D. & S. (1891 Ed.), Vol. 2, sec. 240.

It may consist in the suppression of facts by joint arrangement. 

Harne vs. Harne, 141 Md. 123.

Yet an agreement between the parties, not involving an imposition upon the Court, to expedite the litigation as by amending a bill for divorce to one asking for a decree a vinculo, by agreement as to alimony entered into as a basis therefor, is not collusion. 

Bushman vs. Bushman, 157 Md. 166.

LACHES. 

Where a party has slumbered on his rights following the occurrence of a matrimonial offense, a court will be indisposed to grant him relief, and "will be inclined to infer either an insincerity in the the complainant, or an acquiescence in the injury whether real or a condonation of it". 

Kremelberg vs. Kremelberg, 52 Md. 553, 557, wherein however, the Court held that in the absence of statutory provision to that effect, mere lapse of time will not operate as a bar to a divorce on the ground of adultery.

ESTOPPEL. 

The doctrine of estoppel applies in actions for divorce. Thus where the parties are living separate and apart either under a separation agreement or without it by oral agreement or acquiescence, neither is entitled to a divorce on the ground of abandonment. 

Kremelberg vs. Kremelberg, 52 Md. 553, 557. 

Antrim vs. Antrim, 169 Md. 418. 

France vs. Safe Dep. & Tr. Co., 4 Atl. (2nd) 717.

But a living apart under a separation agreement is not a bar to a divorce for a subsequent cause, such as adultery. 

Melson vs. Melson, 151 Md. 196, 201. 1

One who obtains a divorce a mensa on the ground of abandonment which was of character and duration entitling him to a divorce a vinculo, cannot thereafter secure the latter class of divorce on the ground of abandonment. 

Miller vs. Miller, 153 Md. 219.

But if at the time of the a mensa decree the abandonment was sufficient only for that class of divorce, then the party after the lapse of the necessary time is not estopped from obtaining a divorce a vinculo. 

Code, Art. 16, sec. 41. 

Where a decree a vinculo makes no provision for the support of the children of the marriage, the wife is not estopped from suing her former husband for money expended by her in supporting the children committed to her. 

Boggs vs. Boggs, 138 Md. 422.

CHAPTER X. 

APPEAL. 

There is a presumption of fact in favor of the chancellor's findings of fact for the purpose of review on appeal, it having the force of the evidence on which they rest, no more and no less, though in doubtful cases, turning on the credibility of witnesses whose testimony has been taken in open court, weight is given to the fact that the chancellor having seen and heard the witnesses, was better

able than is the appellate court to decide on their credibility.

Jacobs vs. Jacobs, 170 Md. 413, 414. 

The statute provides for reviving the suit in the lower court on death of party before the appeal has been perfected. 

Thomas vs. Thomas, 57 Md. 504.

Revivor unnecessary where party dies after the appeal has been perfected. 

France vs. Safe Dep. & Tr. Co., 4 Atl. (2nd) 717.

A husband who has obtained an absolute divorce for desertion cannot appeal because the chancellor failed to find the wife guilty of adultery. 

Fries vs. Fries, 166 Md. 604. 

The fact that the chancellor admitted and considered testimony which should have been excluded, detracts from the weight to be given to his decision on appeal. 

Kremis vs. Kremis, 163 Md. 223. 

Failure by the husband to comply with an order passed after entry of his appeal and transmission of the record to the appellate court, requiring him to pay the wife's costs and counsel fee on appeal, was not ground for dismissing the appeal, the order not forming part of the case before the court. 

Sollenberger vs. Sollenberger, 164 Md. 697.

ANNULMENT. 

Jurisdiction of Courts. 

By statute in Maryland, the circuit courts for the several counties and the Superior Court for Baltimore City, upon petition of either party, and the circuit court for the several counties and the Criminal Court of Baltimore City, on indictment, are empowered to inquire into and determine the validity of any marriage, and declare same contrary to the table in said statute, or any second marriage, the first subsisting, null and void. 

Code, Art. 62, sec. 14. 

Notwithstanding said statute, "Courts of Equity in this State have authority to determine the validity of a marriage charged to have been produced by abduction, terror, fraud or duress, and that authority rests upon their general jurisdiction to set aside contracts affected by fraud, etc.". 

Wimbrough vs. Wimbrough, 121 Md. 621. 

Ridgely vs. Ridgely, 79 Md. 298.

LeBrun vs. LeBrun, 55 Md. 496. 

Fornshill vs. Murray, 1 Bland, Ch. 479.

The authority, however, to annul a marriage within the prohibited degrees in Baltimore City is by said statute confined to the Superior and Criminal courts. 

Ridgely vs. Ridgely, 79 Md. 305.

TERRITORIAL JURISDICTION. 

"A State can exercise through its courts, jurisdiction to annul a marriage under the same circumstances which would enable it to dissolve the marriage by divorce."

Restatement of Conflict of Laws, sec. 115. 

Such circumstances would be either (a) the marriage ceremony performed in this State irrespective of domicile of the parties; or one or both of the parties being domiciled in this State, though the marriage ceremony had been performed elsewhere. 

Of course in annulment proceedings, the validity of the marriage (except where local public policy, as in case of miscegenation involved) is governed by the place where performed and not that of the forum. Cf. 

Fornshill vs. Murray, 1 Bl. 479. 

Jackson vs. Jackson, 82 Md. 17 (dicta).

The question of territorial jurisdiction has never been directly passed upon by the Maryland Court of Appeals, but its decisions and dicta therein are consistent with the above statement of the rule. 

Thus in several cases wherein the jurisdictional question was not raised, annulment suits were considered on the merits, where the ceremony was performed here, although both parties were domiciled elsewhere;¹ and in several other cases annulment 

1 Brown vs. Scott, 140 Md. 259; Corder vs. Corder, 141 Md. 114; 

Montgomery vs. McNertle, 146 Md. 200.

suits were considered on their merits, where both parties are domiciled in this State, but the ceremony was performed elsewhere.¹

QUERY: Have the Maryland Courts jurisdiction of a case wherein one spouse is domiciled here and the marriage ceremony was performed elsewhere? 

In Elfont vs. Elfont above, 161 Md. 458, the Court held it unnecessary to pass on the question, because same was raised for the first time on appeal.

WHO MAY SUE. 

Applications for a decree of nullity under the Statute must be made by a party to the marriage. 

Thus a divorced husband cannot sue for the annulment of his divorced wife's subsequent marriage to another on the ground of the invalidity of such divorce. 

Ridgely vs. Ridgely, 79 Md. 298.

After the death of one of the parties no proceedings can be had to have the marriage annuled, yet, where the title to property depends upon the question of legitimacy, the question of the validity of the marriage may be inquired of incidentally, either at law or in equity. 

Fornshill vs. Murray, 1 Bland, 479.

GROUNDS FOR ANNULMENT. 

A marriage may be declared null for any cause which rendered it at its inception wholly or partially invalid; where either party was incapable of understanding the nature of the marriage ceremony, because of insanity, or while under the influence of intoxicating liquors or drugs; where the consent of either party was induced by fraud or duress, or by mistake as to the nature of the ceremony or the identity of the person; or where there was a legal subsisting marriage; or where there is an absence of a formality required by statute. 

38 Corpus Juris "Marriage", sec. 124, p. 1350.

The grounds for annulment may be classified as follows: 

(1) Legal impediments such as an existing prior marriage. Miscegenation; want of age. (2) Impotency, or lack of physical capacity. (3) Lack of mental capacity to understand the nature of the marriage contract, due to insanity, drunkenness, drugs, etc. (4) Lack of consent, due to fraud, duress or coercion, or due to mistake as to the nature of the ceremony or the identity of the person. 

Want of Age: The age of consent to a valid marriage at common law, which still prevails in this

State, corresponds with the age of puberty, to wit, 14 years for males and 12 years for females. 

1 Blackstone's Commentaries, 436. 

Marriage of person above that age is valid though statute law forbids same.

Miscegenation: "Maryland statutes peremptorily forbid marriage of white person and negro, declaring all such marriages forever void. Therefore, it is a declared policy of this State to prohibit such marriages. Though these marriages may be valid elsewhere, they will be absolutely void here so long as statutory inhibition remains unchanged." 

Dicta from opinion in Jackson vs. Jackson, 82 Md. 17.

QUERY: Whether parties to a marriage contract lawfully and completely solemnized, can limit or modify its effect, postpone its consummation or lessen its obligations by private agreements between themselves? Not necessary to be answered. 

Samuelson vs. Samuelson, 155 Md. 639. 

(2) Impotency. 

If impotency is ground for annulment in jurisdiction where the marriage was performed, it is ground for annulment in a proceeding filed in this State. 

There is no Maryland statute prohibiting the marriage of the impotent or those otherwise physically unfit. Impotency, however, is by statute (Code,

Art. 16, sec. 38) a ground for a divorce a vinculo matrimonii.

J. G. vs. H. G., 33 Md. 401. 

(3) Lack of Mental Capacity. 

To render a marriage invalid on account of the insanity of one of the parties, it must be shown clearly and convincingly that such party was unable to understand the nature of the contract of marriage and the legal consequences naturally deducible therefrom, mere weakness or imbecility of mind or eccentricity or partial dementia being insufficient. 

Elfont vs. Elfont, 161 Md. 458, 471.

In the Elfont case above, the testimony of experts that the husband was suffering from dementia praecox both before and after the marriage, was held insufficient to overcome other evidence which showed that he had been engaged since his marriage in various classes of work which he could not have performed had he been mentally enfeebled, and that he had written intelligent letters to his wife. 

If at the time of the marriage ceremony one or both of the parties are in such a state of intoxication that they were unable to realize what was transpiring, and the meaning of the ceremony, the marriage will be annuled. 

Montgomery vs. U'Nertle, 143 Md. 200.

(4) Lack of Consent. 

What constitutes fraud as a ground of annulment defies exact definition. 

Where the misrepresentation of fraud relates to accidental matters, such as rank, fame, fortune, habits, temperament or the like, they do not go to the essence of the contract and are not sufficient to justify the annulment of the marriage, but where the fraud relates to essential matters affecting the health or well being of the parties themselves or their offspring of the marriage, it is sufficient for this purpose. 

Brown vs. Scott, 140 Md. 258, 268. 

Oswald vs. Oswald, 146 Md. 313, 316. Cf. 

Corder vs. Corder, 141 Md. 114.

Thus where a professional swindler, whose life had been largely spent in prisons and reformatories, and who after the marriage was arrested and sentenced for crimes previously committed, induced an eighteen-year-old girl to marry him by false representations that he had left college to enter the World War in which he had served four years; that he was an honorable member of society, occupying a responsible position, justified an annulment. 

Brown vs. Scott, 140 Md. 258, 271.

Lesser misrepresentations were held sufficient to annul the marriage of a sixteen-year-old girl and

733 her father's chauffeur, where the marriage had never been consummated. 

Corder vs. Corder, 141 Md. 114.

That plaintiff was induced to marry the defendant by the latter's representation that her former husband was dead while he was in fact living though divorced from her, is not cause for annulment, though the plaintiff belongs to a church which regards a marriage to a divorced person as invalid. 

Oswald vs. Oswald, 146 Md. 313, 317.

By the weight of authority a marriage will not be annuled becaused of concealment by the woman of previous unchastity¹ but concealment of her pregnancy from her husband who had not himself sustained improper relations with her is considered fraud such as is ground for annulment.²

Allegations that a woman had induced an 18-yearold boy, with whom she had sustained sexual relations, to marry her under threats to send him to jail because of her alleged pregnancy, was held to constitute fraudulent concealment and there being no consummation would, if proven, constitute grounds for annulment. 

Lurz vs. Lurz, 170 Md. 428.
