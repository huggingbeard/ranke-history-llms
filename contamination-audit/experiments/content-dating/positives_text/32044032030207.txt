Summary of the Economic Opportunity Amendments of 1967 

​+ The 1967 amendments to the Economic Opportunity Act comprise a complex piece of legislation. To aid in the eradication of poverty existing programs have been altered and new programs devised. Greater legislative direction, both programmatically and structurally, is given to all programs. 

There is an increased thrust toward coordinated comprehensive local planning in all phases of the war against poverty, including a particular emphasis on comprehensive employment programs. The more important amendments to the Act are summarized below. Authorizations for Economic Opportunity Act programs are explained at the end of this summary. 

The reader should note that although the summary treats amendments to each of the programs under separate headings, several amendments discussed under the heading of "General Provisions" in this summary will also affect such programs.

Job Corps (Title I-A) 

This title was rewritten. The amended Job Corps provisions describe the program structure and criteria in greater detail, giving the program increased legislative direction. The minimum age for enrollment is lowered to 14 years. Specific screening and selection procedures are statutorially outlined, including provision for non-resident centers. 

To encourage completion of training, the amended Act provides for variable personal allowances (from $35 to $50 a month) and requires generally, that enrollees remain in the program at least 90 days before being eligible for readjustment allowances.

To insure that the purposes of the Job Corps are being effectuated, a provision is added directing that the United States Employment Service be used to the fullest extent for placing enrollees. Further, the Secretary of Labor is directed to determine the status and progress of terminees and assure that their needs for further training are met. 

New cost and enrollment limitations require that annual direct per enrollee operation cost not exceed $6,900. The amended Act further 2

directs that a 25 percent female enrollment in the Job Corps be achieved during the fiscal year 1968 and that a 50 percent female enrollment be attained as soon as practicable.

The amendments expand the Director's authority to carry on demonstration projects. Specifically included are projects involving model community vocational education schools to be operated through State educational agencies. 

Work and Training for Youth and Adults (Title I-B) 

The amendments combine the Neighborhood Youth Corps and various adult work-training programs into an overall comprehensive program for the purpose of providing participants an unbroken sequence of services to enable them to obtain and hold employment. 

A comprehensive work and training program is to be locally administered by a prime sponsor which would be a public or private nonprofit agency. Such a program may include types of activities previously authorized (e.g., NYC activities and projects under the "Nelson" and "Scheuer" amendments), a new activity designed to encourage private businesses to hire the disadvantaged, and concentrated work and training programs. 

The latter programs are to serve urban and rural areas with high concentrations of low-income persons and rural areas experiencing serious problems of outmigration. Not more than 20 percent of title I funds may be reserved for programs in this category. 

The description of out-of-school NYC programs is changed by removing the upper age limit, thereby providing a more generalized direction for an adult manpower activity. Special provisions are made to fund school districts directly for in-school NYC programs. 

All title I-B programs in a community (except for in-school NYC) are required to be consolidated into a comprehensive program and funded through a prime sponsor.

The amendments also provide for technical assistance and training to be provided for these programs. 

Special Impact Programs (Title I-D) 

The amendments modify the Act's special impact program, making it more explicitly an economic development authority. This includes provisions for financial and other incentives to businesses to locate in urban ghetto areas and for general community development activities.

Community Action Program (Title II) 

The Act's title II provisions authorizing urban and rural community action programs were rewritten. Under these provisions, as rewritten, a community action agency is defined as a State or a political subdivision of a State, or combination of such subdivisions, or a public or private non-profit agency designed by such a governmental entity or entities. capable of conducting a comprehensive community action program. The 

Director may, however, designate some other public or private non-profit agency as a community action agency if (1) the community action agency serving the community fails, after having a reasonable opportunity to do so, to submit a satisfactory plan for a community action program or fails to carry out such a plan, or (2) neither the State nor a qualified political subdivision (or combination thereof) is willing to designate either itself or a separate public or private non-profit agency to be a community action. agency.

Upon the decision of their governing officials, political subdivisions may elect not to be included in the program of a community action agency designated by a State or another subdivision or group of subdivisions. If the local officials decide to exercise this option, then the political subdivision or a public or private non-profit agency designated by it is eligible to be designated the area's community action agency on the same basis as other subdivisions or their designees. 

Community action programs which are State or local governments or combinations of local governments must be administered through “community action boards" (of not more than 51 members) being so constituted that (1) one-third of the members are public officials (including the chief elected officials) or their representatives, (2) at least one-third are representatives of the poor, and (3) the remainder are representatives of major groups or interests in the community. Any other community action agency must be governed by a board with this same basic structure.

The powers of governing boards must include the determination and enforcement of major personnel, fiscal and program policies, the appointment of senior staff members, determination of program plans and priorities, and approval of applications for title II assistance. Where the principal representative board is a "community action board", it must have full opportunity to participate in development and implementation of programs and projects for the poor with maximum feasible participation of residents of the area and groups served. Such boards must be so established that the poor and residents of the area concerned will be 4

able to influence the character of programs affecting their interest and regularly participate in the planning and implementation of such programs. When subsidiary boards are to have major policy-making responsibilities within particular areas of the community, they must be broadly representative of these areas and provide adequate opportunity for the membership of elected public officials.

The foregoing changes in title II become effective on July 1, 1968, but a further period to February 1, 1969 is allowed to complete the transition in communities where newly-designated community action agencies will be replacing existing ones.

The funding of local title II programs, except "special-emphasis" programs, is to be through community action agencies. However, limited purpose projects may be independently funded when they are needed. to serve the needs of low-income families in communities in which there is no community action agency or in other cases where the local community action agency gives its approval.

Upward Bound, Head Start, Follow Through, and Family Planning programs which have been operating under general community action agency authority are added to the list of statutory "special-emphasis" programs. The amendments also add to these programs a new Health 

Career Program, an Emergency Food and Medical Services Program (to be conducted in conjunction with the Department of Agriculture and the U.S. Public Health Service), and a Senior Opportunities and Services 

Program for the elderly. The Legal Services Program is forbidden to defend persons who have been indicted or otherwise formally charged with a crime except in extraordinary cases. 

In his discretion, the Director may allot funds for these special-emphasis programs by a different formula from the one used for local initiative funds, so long as no more than 12½ percent of the funds for a program is spent in one State. 

The Act continues the requirement that the level of Federal assistance for 1968 be 80 percent rather than the 90 percent permitted in 1967. 

Local share contributions under titles I-B and II may be pooled within the same community for purposes of meeting the statutory requirements. The cost of developing and administering community action programs generally is not to exceed 15 percent of the program's total cost. As a condition of title II assistance after July 1, 1968 community action agencies must adopt a "systematic approach" to program planning, operation, and evaluation.

Community action agencies are encouraged to develop neighborhood centers through which a number of different services and activities can 5

be conducted with full neighborhood resident participation. They are also encouraged to establish housing development and service organizations to help meet the housing needs of low-income families. 

The role of State agencies under title II is broadened considerably. In addition to their technical assistance functions, they are to take a more active role in operating title II programs and in coordinating State activities with assistance under title II. 

The provisions governing research and demonstration (now called "pilot") programs have been amended to emphasize rural development programs to prevent migration to cities and to provide for local review of new pilot programs before they are commenced. The provision for local review, subject to reconsideration by the Director of OEO, parallels the Governor's review of title II programs generally, except that the review is by the local community action agency or where there is none, by the governing officials of the city, county, or other major political subdivision. 

The review occurs only at the start of a new pilot program; no exception is made for programs run by colleges or universities. 

The $15,000 salary limitation has been retained, and made applicable to all local initiative and special emphasis programs, but the Director is now authorized to issue rules and regulations permitting exceptions in appropriate cases involving specialists or higher prevailing local salary rates.

Henceforth, a community action agency's cost of developing and administering programs under title II may not exceed 15% of the total cost of its title II programs unless the limitation is waived for one or more periods not to exceed six months.

A new provision calls for evaluation of all title II programs under standards developed and published by the Director. Reports of evaluation studies are to be made public and the results are to be considered in deciding whether to renew assistance.

Migrant and Seasonal Workers (Title III-B) 

The program for migrant and seasonal workers is rewritten expanding the types of aid that may be extended. Such aid includes day care for children, education, health, improved housing and sanitation, legal services, consumer training and counseling, the promotion of increased community acceptance of migrants and their families, and equipping migrants and their families through education and training to meet the changing demands in agricultural employment brought about by technological advancement.

Small Business Assistance (Title IV) 

The amendments revise the eligibility standards for the small business assistance program. Special attention is to be given to small business concerns in urban and rural areas of high concentrations or proportions of unemployed or low-income individuals. Not less than 50 percent of the amounts loaned by the program is to be allotted to small business concerns in urban areas having high concentrations of unemployed or low-income individuals or to business concerns owned by low-income persons. The 

Administrator of the Small Business Administration, in consultation with the Secretary of Commerce, is given the authority to provide technical assistance and management training. The amended Act further provides that the President, if he deems it necessary, may transfer the technical assistance and management training functions to the Secretary of Commerce.

Day Care (Title V-B) 

The amendments establish a separate, new program of financial assistance to public and private agencies and organizations for day care projects. This program is to be focused on children of low-income families or families living in areas with high concentrations or proportions of lowincome families whose parents desire to accept employment or undertake vocational training.

Domestic Volunteer Service Programs—VISTA (Title VIII) 

Title VIII was substantially amended to authorize the Director to carry out and coordinate a full range of volunteer activities of assistance to the poor. Its designation was changed from "Volunteers in Service to 

America" to "Domestic Volunteer Service Programs" to reflect its broader scope. Title VIII, as amended, now provides authority for the conduct of part-time, full-time, and special volunteer programs. The amendments specificaly authorize local community service volunteer programs, programs specially designed for the elderly (both as volunteers and as beneficiaries), demonstration projects involving VISTA and the Teacher Corps to assist in the rehabilitation of youthful criminal offenders, and special programs designed to stimulate and to initiate improved methods of providing volunteer services and to encourage wider volunteer participation.

The revised title VIII requires the Director to take steps to coordinate the programs authorized under this title with one another and with other

Federal, State, local, and national programs of volunteer service. The 

Director is specifically authorized to consult with the heads of Federal, State, local, and national agencies responsible for any program related to the purpose of the Economic Opportunity Act in order to encourage greater use of volunteer services in such programs and to establish systematic procedures for recruitment, referral, orientation and taining of volunteers serving in such programs.

Coordination 

The amendments generally revise the Act's coordination provisions in title VI expanding the role of the Economic Opportunity Council. The 

President is empowered to designate the chairman and members of the Council.

General Provisions 

The amendments require that agencies and organizations desiring to serve as delegate agencies in comprehensive work-training and community action programs have the right of expeditious appeal to the Director if their applications are rejected by a prime sponsor or community action agency. The Act, as amended, also requires that a recipient agency in these programs and in migrant programs be afforded an opportunity to show cause why such action should not be taken in the case of a suspension of assistance or a refusal to refund programs. The Act further requires that an opportunity for a hearing must be afforded a recipient agency in such programs before assistance is terminated. 

Public announcement of the making and results of all research and demonstration contracts is required.

Joint Federal agency funding of programs is facilitated by permitting one Federal agency to be designated for all to serve as administrator of the funds, and permitting the establishment of a single local share requirement according to the proportion of the funds advanced by each agency. 

The Director is required to prepare and submit to Congress a five-year antipoverty action plan. Such plan is to be updated annually. 

The use of EOA funds for any partisan or nonpartisan political activity or voter registration in connection with elections for public or party office is prohibited.

The amendments also contain provisions authorizing a full scale investigation of Economic Opportunity Act programs and activities by the General Accounting Office, with a final report due to Congress by December 1, 1968.

Authorization 

The amendments provide for the first time a two-year authorization for Economic Opportunity programs. The measure authorizes an appropriation of $1.98 billion for the fiscal year 1968, and $2.18 billion for the fiscal year 1969. The authorizations are allocated by titles (and in some cases parts of titles) for fiscal year 1968, but not for the fiscal year 1969. 

This allocation is a substantial change from the fiscal year 1967 authorization in which there was extensive earmarking of funds for specific programs within titles. The specific authorizations for fiscal 1968 are as follows: $295 million for carrying out programs under title I-A, Job 

Corps; $476 million for carrying out programs under title I-B, Work 

Training for Youth and Adults; $60 million for carrying out programs under title I-B, Work Training for Youth and Adults; $60 million for carrying out programs under title I-D, Special Impact Program (a substantially new economic development program); $950 million for carrying out programs under title II, Community Action Programs of which $25 million of this authorization must be reserved for a new emergency food program;* $20 million for carrying out programs under title III-A, Rural 

Loan Program; $27 million for carrying out progams unde titre III-B, Assistance fo Migrant and Seasonal Workers; $10 million for carrying out programs under title IV, Employment and Investment Incentives (activities under section 406); $70 million for carrying out programs under title V-A, Work Experience and Training; $25 million for carrying out programs under title V-B, Day Care projects; $16 million under title 

VI for administration and coordination; and $31 million for carrying out programs under title VIII, Volunteer Programs.
