In planning your training-depending upon your desires, time at your disposal and your ambitions-you can train every other day, or four or five times a week. A four-day training schedule is an ideal method. The secret of gaining success with weight training is to make demands upon the muscles, then have days of rest in which nature builds strength and endurance to not only meet these demands but to provide more strength for future demands. Gains in strength are made rapidly; after the short initial training period you will find the starting poundages to be easy for you and you will require additional weight. It has been the accepted practice to train rather hard, certainly harder than usual, at least one training day per week. On the other training days go through a nice comfortable workout.

An exercise should be practiced for a long enough time to bring the blood racing to the working muscles, with the materials which are required for continuation of the movement and to build and replace the broken-down tissue.

There has been an endeavor to arrange the exercises in groups so that one poundage will serve for a number of movements. You may prefer, after using the course for a time, to shift the exercises a bit, the object being to classify groups of exercises which you can best perform with identical weights.

Exercise No. 1-Lateral Raise, Breathing. After loading the dumbells to desired amount, stand in the position of attention, heels together, toes out, forming an angle of forty-five degrees, the dumbells resting at the side of the thighs, knuckles out, arms hanging loosely at sides. From this position, keeping the knuckles up and the arms straight, raise them to well above shoulder height. Pause for two seconds, then lower. Inhale as the weights are raised slowly; exhale as they are slowly lowered. 

Exercise No. 2-Two Dumbell Curl. This develops primarily the muscle of the front of the arm-the biceps, the muscle nearly everyone displays when you want to see his "muscle." Start by permitting the dumbells to hang against the front of thighs, palms front, arms quite straight to begin; raise the dumbells slowly and steadily, keeping the bells parallel to the front, up until they touch the body. Exercise No. 3-Punching Movements. Using the same weight as in Exercise No. 1, practice the various punching movements such as straight left and straight right, upper cut, swing, and hook, alternately with each hand. 

As the weight is light, ten movements in each style of punching-forty in all-are not too many.

Exercise No. 4-Two Arm Press. Principally develops the back of the arm and the shoulders. Take a dumbell in each hand; pull them to the shoulders so that the palms are front. Keeping the bells parallel with your front, press them slowly and steadily to arm's length overhead. Lower in the same manner and repeat. Inhale while pressing; exhale while lowering the bells. While pressing with palms front is the usual way, you can vary your position and develop additional muscles by pressing with the palms toward each other and even, at times, with the knuckles front. 

Exercise No. 5-Rowing Movement. A very good exercise to develop the upper back and shoulders as well as the arms and the latissimus dorsi muscle, the muscle that runs from the side of your waist to the armpit. 

Take a bell in each hand, holding them with the knuckles front, keeping the legs stiff; lean over until the upper body is at right angles to your legs. With no body movement, keeping knuckles front, pull the bells up as high as you can, keeping them away from the body as you complete the movement. Do this slowly and steadily with arm movement only. For variation you can practice this movement with the arms moving alternately.

Exercise No. 6-Side Exercise. Take a bell in one hand. (Performing this exercise with one dumbell is better than with two. With two, one has a balancing effect on the other and there is less muscular benefit.) If you are starting with the bell in your right hand, bend as far to the left as possible. Come back to the center, and then bend to the left as far again as you can. Bend as far as you can, even a bit toward the front. Continue for at least twenty movements. 

Exercise No. 7-Raise on Toes. Hold a dumbell in one hand, using the free hand for support by grasping the side of a doorway, a mantel, a chair. Raise on one toe at a time. Thirty times with each toe are not too many. Shift the position of the foot somewhat so that at times the toe is turned out, at others turned in, and sometimes straight to the front. Raise slowly and steadily. When one leg is well exercised, perform the same movement with the other foot and the dumbell in the other hand. 

Exercise No. 8-One Dumbell Swing. This is a good back exercise but also brings many other muscles into play. Stand with the legs a comfortable [12]

distance apart; swing the bell well back between the legs, then up until it is higher than your head. Change while at the highest possible position, lowering until it is well back between the legs; come up again and change when the bell reaches the highest point. The free hand can rest upon the knee as the weight goes back between the legs. When the bell reaches its highest point your back should be quite straight.

Exercise No. 9-Deep Knee Bend on Toes. One of the best leg-developing exercises. Take a dumbell in each hand, standing with the feet in the position of attention. Pull the dumbells to shoulder height and hold them in the most comfortable position. Lower the body, balancing on toes, holding the knees well out, the body comparatively upright. Raise to the upright position. Continue until tired, at least fifteen movements. Inhale while rising; exhale while lowering the body. 

Exercise No. 10-Shoulder Shrug. Develops the trapezius muscles which give the shoulders their slope, as they extend from the neck to the edge of the shoulders. Take a bell in each hand, holding the knuckles front; keeping the shoulders slightly forward, raise them as high toward the ears as you can. Then lower them to the starting position. Continue the movement until tired, which should be more than fifteen counts. Several variations are possible with this exercise. When the shoulders are raised, roll them back as far as you can, lower them, raise in the same position, then roll them as far forward as possible when they are at their highest point. Thus the shoulders are performing a sort of semicircular movement. 

Exercise No. 11-Stiff-Legged Dead Weight Lift. Take a bell in each hand; standing with the heels together, in the position of attention, hold knuckles front, keeping the knees stiff. Bend over until the bells almost touch the floor; straighten up, leaning well back at the completion of the movement. Continue until tired, not less than fifteen movements, inhaling as the body straightens, exhaling as it bends forward. This is one of the best movements for developing the muscles of the back, as well as all the organs and glands of the mid-section. 

Exercise No. 12-Compound Exercise. Compound exercises are especially beneficial. They keep the muscles involved in continuous action, thus bringing a larger share of rich blood to the working muscles with resulting beneficial effect. While it is optional which four exercises you choose to employ in this compound series, you will find that a good movement is one which consists of the two arm press, stiff-legged dead weight lift, rowing motion and the deep knee bend. THREE ADDITIONAL EXERCISES TO DEVELOP THE NECK, CHEST AND ABDOMEN 

These have a very fine effect as they stimulate the vital sections of the body. While it is not essential that you practice these three additional movements, you will find them highly beneficial. 

Exercise No. 13-Neck Exercise. Over a million York head straps have been sent out with various outfits, notably the Complete "Home Gym," the "5 in 1," dumbell and Iron Boot combination, and the "Big Ten Special" with its many appliances. Perhaps you have a head strap. If so, practice the variety of good neck exercises the head strap permits. If you don't have a head strap, use a strip of cloth or rope; tie it to the dumbell; extend it around the head; leaning forward, hands on knees, raise and lower the head and the weight, which of course develops the neck. Variations of this movement are possible by lying on a bed or high table, to raise and lower the head, thus exercising the front of the neck; lying on the side to exercise the side of neck.

Exercise No. 14-Two Arm Pull-Over. This exercise is perhaps the best chest developer. I say perhaps, because the deep knee bend with very heavy weights, creating as it does great demands upon the body, is a great chest builder, but it will not build the deep chest and the fine pectorals which result from the regular practice of this two arm pull-over. 

Lie upon your back. Use a weight that is not too heavy as this is a breathing exercise. Start with the arms extended, weights above the face. Lower them slowly behind head, breathing deeply as the weights are lowered. 

Hold them at the low position for two seconds, filling the lungs as completely as you can with air, then raise the dumbells up to and past the starting position, exhaling fully. Continue this movement, inhaling and exhaling. It not only has a healthful effect, a muscle-building effect, but improves the posture, flattens the waist and strengthens it as well. 

Exercise No. 15—Abdominal Exercise. There are many good abdominal exercises. Two of the best are the leg raise and raising the upper body, while the feet are held under some heavy object. But the exercise we will suggest here is also one of the best, perhaps better than either of those just mentioned. Lie flat upon your back, arms extended behind head. 

Simultaneously raise the hands, the upper body and the feet. They should meet at the highest possible point above the body. Lower both, straightening body, extending chest, pulling in waist, and repeat. You will quickly find that this is a splendid result-producing exercise.

Continue this movement with a regular cadence until tired. Twenty movements should be possible after a bit of practice.

This movement is called the "good morning exercise," instead of merely the bend over, because it is similar to the movement of bowing to another and saying good morning in an old-fashioned way.
