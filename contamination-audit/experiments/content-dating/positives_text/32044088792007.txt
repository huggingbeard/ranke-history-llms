GIFT OF GAETANO SALVENINI ITALY AND THE UNREDEEMED ISLES OF GREECE THE DODECANESE THROUGH THE AGES 

By the shores of Asia Minor, begirt with the wine-dark sea, that kisses eagerly the coast of the Hellenic mainland, and scattered upon the direct route that leads from the renown city of Constantine to the one built by Alexander the Great, lie the twelve fair islands of Dodecenese, enchained on the furious chariot of maddened Fascismo. 

Dodecanese derives her name from two Greek words, "dodeca" twelve, and 'nesos' island. The Dodecanesian Group includes the following islands: Rhodes, Leros, Calymnos, Cassos, Carpathos, Cos,

Nissyros, Telos, Symi, Patmos, Chalki, Astypalea. Also picturesque 

Castellorizo, small Lipsos and some uninhabited islets. The very name of each and every one of these islands is of Hellenic origin. 

Through tre "long train of ages", from the dark Minoan period, to the enlightened modern days, the Twelve Islands have always been populated by industrious Greeks. Greeks who have sponsored education, cultivated the fine arts, systematized the sciences, and spread the benefits of civilization in all directions. 

The science of medicine recognizes Dedocanese as her motherland. At romantic Cos, Hippocrates, the father of the healing art, was born. Rock-ribbed Telos gave humanity the first woman poetess. Volcanic Nissyros is credited with the most beautiful maiden Damatos, on whom the first historic phlebotomy was made by the Dodecanesian physician Melampous. Picturesque 

Cassos produced Dion, the historian. Famous Rhodes adorned the Pantheon of the "glory that was Greece" with Andronicos the philosopher, Peisandros the poet, Timachides the linguist. Impressive Leros is the birthplace of Pherekides the historian. Peaceful Patmos shelterd St. John the Divine, and sent Xanthos to sow the seeds that stirred the Greeks to revolt against Turkey in 1821. 

Arid Symi's Nireus led three well trimmed ships to Troy. Carpathos and Calymnos, Astypalea and Chalki are known for their gallantry and patriotic actions. Their valiant children often sacrificed their lives and fortunes so that Freedom may live. 

The Dodecanesians were Greeks during the pre-historic epoch as proven by the researches of the archeologists. The numerous coins, the many vases, the magnificent ornaments, the beautiful statuettes, the marble inscriptions, all these silent testimonies that were brought to light either by the local farmer's unsuspecting plow or with the aid of scientific excavations, confirm beyond any reasonable doubt their Hellenic ancestry. The archeological museums of Europe and America number among their precious possessions many such findings. 

During the fifteenth century before Christ, Danaos and Cadmos have found in Rhodes civilized and advanced communities, and -2-

they built on the acropolis of the island the famous temple of 

Lindian Athena.

In 1194-1184 B. C., the Hellenic people of the Twelve Isles participated in the Trojan war. About thirty war vessels were contributed to the Trojan expedition from the islands Nissyros, Carpathos, Cassos, Cos, Calymnos and Leros, according to the "catalogue" of the Acheans, the allies of Agamemnon. Rhodes assisted with nine ships, and Symi gave three. Homer, the blind poet of Ionia, praises separately each and every one of these Grecian nymphs of the Archipelago, and sings heroic chants for their gallant services to their Hellenic motherland. 

The athletic sons of Grecian Dodecanese were victorious at the Olympic games of 464 B. C. Cleomedes the boxer, Leonidas the wrestler, and the three sons of Diagoras, were from these Grecian isles. Pindar's lyre praises Diagoras, whose sons at the same hour have won in three different games. 

During the golden age of Pericles, Dodecanese was inhabited with patriotic Greeks. Greeks who offered themselves to the 

Atheneans, who were fighting so bravely against the Persian hordes, in order to save Europe from the Asiatic conquerors.

Athens triumphed, and European civilization was rescued. 

Dodecanese remained Greek during the third, second, and first century before Christ, in spite of the prosecutions and persecutions inaugurated against the heroic islanders by their despotic rulers. One of such despots was also the dominant Cassius (42 B.C.). This assassin of Julius Ceasar, had terrorized the inhabitants. He had put to death their political leaders and he had sent to exile their most prominent patriots, all those who abhored the Roman yoke. To such oppressive measures the Dodecanesians responded with renewed vigor and unfailing enthusiasm for their freedom. Their efforts gained for them, under the Roman Emperor Claudius (51 A.D.), the "ancient state and laws", as described on two stones found in the island of Rhodes. 

These liberties however that were won with long struggles and bloody sacrifices, were abolished by Vespasius at 70 A. D., and the brave sons of Dodecanesos resumed the just but unequal fight against their Roman masters.

In the year 730 A. D., Dodecanese was constituted as part of the Byzantine Empire. It is at this period that the name "Dodecanesos" is used for the first time in history, given to the twelve islands by Theophanes. It was then applied to these Aegean islands, with the exception of Rhodes, where mighty Colossus proudly stood, and Cos, the fatherland of Philitas, the poet, and

Xenocrates the linguist. The group however included daring 

Icaria, and the small Castellorizo. 

From 1309 to 1522, the Greek islands of the Mediterranean sea were under the Latin supremacy. They were divided into three groups: Rhodes, Cos, Calymnos, Leros, Nissyros, Telos, Symi,

Chalki and Castellorizo, or "Castel Rosso" as now called by the Italians, belonged to the Knights of St. John, except in 1450, when 3

the Pope gave the "Red Castle" to the King of Naples. Astypalea, Carpathos and Cassos were property of the Venetian families of Quirini and Cornaro, with the exception of a short occupation of Cassos and Carpathos by the adventurous Knights. They remained in the possession of these families down to the Turkish conquest of 1537. Patmos and Palmosa, and the small island of Leipso that lies near this island where the Apocalypse was written, was regarded as a holy land. 

After the Latin conquest of Constantinople, the island of roses, Rhodes, bold Carpathos, and liberty loving Cassos, were occupied by the Greek family of Cavalas, and latter by the Greek Empire of Nicaea.

The Turkish conqueror of the Grecian Islands, had granted special privileges to the islanders, by virtue of which only a small amount of taxes were paid to the Porte per annum, known as "Maktou". Although the inhabitants had their own courts and their own local laws, under their Mohamedan rulers, their yet burning desire was their liberation. So when the Greek revolution commenced in 1821, and spread like a wild fire throughout the Hellenic provinces, Grecian Dodecanese took up arms for freedom. 

The Dodecanesians from the islands, the Dodecanesians that were scattered all over the civilized world, volunteered their lives and fortunes for the liberty of their mother Greece. To their enthusiasm Turkey responded with reprisals and onslaughts. The island of Cassos was set in fire. Cos suffered severely. Rhodes was terrorized by a Turkish force. On all the Greek islands the bloodthirsty Moslems were let loose. Girls were taken prisoners, and shut up in he reins. Men were captured and sold as slaves in the markets of Stamboul. Capodistrias, that eminent statesman of modern Hellas, mentions with praises, their many struggles, and the Greek historians credit righteously their supreme sacrifices. 

Their blind devotion to the cause of Greek Independence, won for them their union with Hellas. Dodecanese, became part of the newly established Greek nation, and remained as such until 1830, when the twelve islands were returned to the Ottoman Government in exchange of Euvoia, for this island is nearer to Greece. 

Given back to Turkey, by the Protective Powers, they remained under the Mohamedan rule until 1912. In the course of this time, the Dodecanesians enjoyed special privileges, which were respected by the different succeeding Sultans, because to abolish them would have been similar to subjecting the poor peasants either to starvation or complete expatriation.

During the Cretan Insurrection, however, the Turkish Government had sent to the island Symi a Turkish governor with troops. 

The intention of the Porte was to assimilate the administration of the Islands with that of its other provinces. The indomitable 

Symiotes protested to the British Foreign Office, basing their complaints upon the rights guaranteed to them by the firman of Mohamed II. The English government intervened, and the Turkish governor was recalled. But again in 1869, the Turks blockaded the unenslaved Symi. Deputy-Governors and garrisons were 4-

sent there and in other islands. The liberty-loving Greeks of Dodecanese, determined to safeguard their rights and their privileges, sent a deputation to London, which presented their claims to Lord Clarendon. Their remonstrances were communicated to the Grand Vezir Ali Pasha in Constantinople, who denied "any intention of modifying the fiscal system hitherto prevailing in the islands; the sole subject of the Porte was to introduce a better system of administration". But this declaration was not corroborated by the subsequent events. Symi was blockaded again in 1893, and its notables were imprisoned. Five islands were annexed to the Sanjac of Chios and seven to that of Rhodes.

But in spite of all these, the islands retained their special privileges, and the rights of their local self-government, until the Turkish revolution of 1908, when the Young Turks wished to establish uniformity in the governmental system of the islands, as in other parts of the nation. In 1909 the Porte suppressed their privileges, and made the islanders subject to conscription. The 

Greek natives again protested, and the Turkish government agreed to content itself with the old system "until a definite decision on the subject was reached". In 1912, the decision was reached. It consisted in the complete annulment of the insular privileges. But before this was executed, the islands were confiscated by Italy. 

THE ITALIAN OCCUPATION 

On September 25th, 1911, the Italian Charge d'Affaires at

Constantinople presented an emphatic note to Turkey, in which it was stated that the Italian Government decided to proceed to to the military occupation of Tripoli and Cyrenaica, in order to safeguard its dignity and its interests. On September 29th, a state of war was declared, and on November 5th Tripolitania and Cyrinaica were, by a royal decree, annexed to Italy, under the generic name of Libya. The fighting continued in a desultory fashion, and the war against Turkey was carried on in other spheres. On April 18th, 1912, an imposing Italian squadron appeared off the entrance of the Dardanelles. The Italian fleet cruised unmolested about the Aegean, cutting cables and shelling various points, both in the mainland, and on the Turkish islands. 

On April 22nd, Astypalea was occupied. On the following day

Italy took possession of the other Dodecanese isles, except the Island of Rhodes, where the Italian army, numbered 12,000, and aided by thirty two vessels of war, landed on May 4th. The Turkkish forces on the island were 1,200. With the assistance of the 

Greek inhabitants, who looked upon the Christian Italians as their liberators from the Mohamedans, the Turks were taken prisoners at the battle of Psinthos. Five dead and wounded were the casualties. 

-5-

General Ameglio, on his return from the battle of Psinthos, issued the following Proclamation to the "inhabitants of the island of Rhodes": "Italy, connected with you by glorious recollections, and kindred civilization, feels bound by the evolution of the war to proceed to the occupation of your island. By order of His Majesty the King of Italy, Victor Emmanuel III, my August Sovereign, I enter into my civil and military functions amongst you, and I declare that, though Italy is in a full state of war with the Government and army of Turkey, she considers the peaceful population of Rhodes as friendly and at the same time to affirm henceforth the highest respect for your customs and traditions." 

To the Rhodian Demogerontia (Assembly of the Elders), the General declared that: "The Turkish supremacy has ended on 

Rhodes and on the other islands, by the temporary occupation of them by Italy, and the future of the islands cannot be other than their autonomy."

On his first visit to the Metropolitan of the island, and in the presence of the Elders, General Ameglio said: "I assure you most emphatically that after the termination of the Italo-Turkish war, these islands temporarily occupied by Italy, will receive an autonomous system of government similar to that of Samos, and the Turk will return no more. (Avrebbero uno stato d' autonomia comme per esempio Samos, et il Turko non ritornera mai). These I say as a general and as a Christian, and you must consider my words as words of the gospel." 

On May 12th, the Italian Rear-Admiral Ernesto Presbytero issued and caused to be posted throughout the island of Calymnos a declaration in the name of His Majesty, the King of Italy, announcing that: "from to-day all authority of the Ottoman Government ceases on the island, which under the supervision of the Italian Government will be self-governe d".

Sig. Giolitti also declared that the occupation was purely military, and that "Italy could not pretend to annex territories of 

Greek Nationality."

To the above official assurances for autonomy, the Greek people of the Twelve Islands, responded with marked enthusiasm. The Italian army of occupation was hailed as their emancipator. Bygone memories of Italo-Hellenic fraternalism were brought to their rejoicing minds. The heroic sacrifice of Santa Rosa, who died for the freedom of Hellas in 1821, the enlistment of numerous Greeks in the Italian Army, to offer their lives and fortunes for the emancipation of Italy in 1859, 6-

the displayed philhellenism of Riccioti Garibaldi, who led a company of Italian patriots to Thessaly, and championed the cause of Greece in 1897, the unselfish and wholehearted help rendered to the Italian refugees by the islanders of Eptanesos during tre Revolutionary War of Italy, all these were sincere expressions of brotherly love between the peoples of the two neighboring nations.

Upon the strength of the friendly ties that were uniting the Greeks with the Italians, and resting their hopes on the official promises of the representatives of Italy, the words of whom were told to accept as those of the gospel, the Dodecanesians called a congress of delegates from every island, who met at Patmos, to thank their liberators, and to draft treir laws for self-goverment. On June 17th 1912, they passed unanimously a resolution, of which the following is a translation: "The federation of the islanders of the Aegean, consisting of representatives of the islands occupied by the Italians, gathered in the church of the Monastery of the Apocalypse of Saint John the Evengelist in accordance with the mandate which they have received and the wish of the mandatories, hereby: "(I) Express to the brotherly Italian nation, her King and her government, the infinite gratitude of the aforenamed islands for having delivered them from the intolerable yoke of the Turks. "(II) Proclaim the firm determination of the people of these Christian islands to undergo any sacrifice rather than return under the frightful tyranny of the Turks. "(III) Proclaim the permanent national wish of the Aegeans to be united with their mother country, Greece. "(IV) Proclaim the complete autonomy of the liberated islands, based upon the official documents and the verbal statements of General Ameglio, on the proclamation of the Admirals commanding the powerful Italian fleet, as well as on their acquired privileges of self government, and on the doctrine that a country once delivered from the Turkish domination shall never return to it. "(V) Name the whole of the liberated islands "Aegean State." "(VI) Give as symbol of this State a blue flag intersected in the middle by a white cross, and as emblem, the portrait of 

Apollon Helios. 

"(VII) Consecrate as laws of the State the laws and the particular customs of each community, besides the laws of our free 

Greek brethren.

"(VIII) Solicit support of the present decree from the benefactress Italy and from the other civilized powers, and also the goodwill for the recognition of the Aegean State. "(IX) Postpone for the time being their resolution for the election of the supreme chief of the State, as also of that of the 

Legislative Council. "(X) Provisionally suspend their labours and charge MM Th. 

Olympitis, merchant, G. Drakidis, lawyer, and N. Petrides, merchant, to transmit a copy of the present decree to General Ameglio, commander of the Italian Army at Rhodes. They will ask him to make their resolution known to H. M. the King of Italy, to the Chambers of the liberal and glorious Italian nation, and to the Royal Government of Italy, and at the same time to hand over copies of this decree to the Governments of the other Great Powers. "Signed on the 4th of June (O. S.) 1912 

The Representatives of the Aegeans" (Here follow the signatures of the representatives and the names of the islands to which they belong.)

The Patmos gathering and these resolutions proved that the Greek islanders took for granted the official declarations of the Italians, and they believed that the government at Rome was imbued with humanitarian feelings. They were bitterly disappointed. The Italian authorities of Dodecanese began at once to curtail their liberties, to watch their movements, to banish from the islands their leaders. The Italian Press endeavored to justify the permanent occupation of the Christian isles, claiming that the inhabitants were satisfied with the status quo of their fatherland.

On October 15th 1912, Italy signed at Lausanne a treaty with Turkey, in which it was stipulated that: "The two Governments undertake the responsibility to give, immediately after the signing of this treaty, the order of recalling their officers, their army, and their political employees, that is, the Ottoman Government from Tripoli and Cyrinaica, and the Italian Government from the islands that it occupies in the Aegean Sea". 

This agreement, also, betrayed to the Dodecanesians the insincere promises of the 'general and christian' Ameglio and those of the Rear-Admiral Presbytero, that the islands were not going to be returned to the Turks but they were to be granted selfgovernment.

The people met in insular gatherings, and held plebiscites in every island to express their unwillingness to be handed back to -8-

Turkey, asking for their union with their mother Greece. At these meetings, the Italian army of occupation, displayed its true label, with arrests, imprisonments, brutal treatments. 

And while the natives of the Twelve Islands were subjected to all sorts of humiliations by the Italian intruders, their kinsmen at Mytelene, Chios, Samos, and the rest of the nearby islands were liberated from the Turkish yoke by the victorious fleet of Greece, during the Balkan War, that commenced in October 1912. The Italian occupation thus turned out to be the sole obstacle that prevented the Hellenic navy to free the Dodecanese with the other islands. It has embittered considerably the Dodecanesians, to see the adjoining islands under the Greek flag, while they still waited for the fulfillment of the promises for autonomy, or for joining their destiny with that of Hellas.

When on May 23rd, 1915, Italy entered the European holocaust, in betrayal of her Allies of the Triple Alliance, she did so after she was assured by the Secret Treaty of London, signed on April 26, 1915, "that Italy shall obtain full possession of all the islands of Dodecanese, at present occupied by her". 

To the Italian financial interests those strategic islands, that command the route that leads to the rich fields of Asia Minor, to Constantinople, and to Egypt, were the thirty shekels Italy demanded, in order to desert her former Allies, and offer the innocent blood of the Italian workers and peasants a sacrifice to the Moloch of economic benefits that were hidden behind the world conflict.

But the rapid development of the war events, the entrance of the United States upon the battle fields of Europe, the acceptance of the Fourteen Points, the participation of Greece on the side of the Allied Powers, the foundation of the League of Nations, and the declarations of the belligerent Allies that they were fighting for the self-determination of the peoples, placed the London Treaty in the diplomatic waste basket. 

The Twentieth Article of the League covenant, accepted by Italy, abolished all secret treaties. 

President Wilson announced in the United States Congress, on 

January 9th, 1918 that, "a readjustment of the frontiers of Italy should be effected along clearly recognized lines of nationality. 

The nationalities which are now under Turkish rule should be assured an absolutely unmolested opportunity of autonomous development. What we demand is that the world be made safe for every nation that wishes to live its own life, determine its own in- 9-

stitutions, to be assured of justice and fair dealing by the other people of the world as agaisnt force and selfish aggression. The day of conquest and aggrandizement is gone by". And the same great American, declared in Paris, on the 23rd day of April, 1919, that "when Italy entered the war she entered upon the basis of a definite private understanding, with Great Britain and France, now known as the Pact of London. Since that time the whole face of circumstances has been altered. Many Powers, great and small, have entered the struggle with no knowledge of the private understanding".

On the same day, Colonel House had made a reservation in the Peace Conference, stating that the boundary lines laid down, should not be construed as an acceptance of the Treaty of London. 

The world conflict terminated with the victory of the Allied Powers. Greece's services and sacrifices in the united front were great and of the utmost importance. The Greek casualties, compiled by the U. S. War Department, were 5,000 dead and 21,000 wounded. These figures do not include the number of Greeks who were killed or wounded while fighting under the American flag. or under the banners of the other allied nations. Nor do they represent the gory tributes of the Dodecanesians, upon the altar of Mars, and their contribution of forty-five commercial steamships to the Allies, for the transportation of troops, munitions and supplies.

The Dodecanesians fought bravely, and died contented, for they believed that the day of their liberation was not afar. The leading statesmen of Europe and America guaranteed their freedom. 

Lloyd George stated on January 5, 1918, that "equality of rights among nations, small as well as great, is one of the fundamental issues this country and her Allies are fighting to establish in this war. Government by consent of the governed, must be the basis of any territorial settlement. Territorial settlement must be secured based on the right of self determination or the consent of the governed".

For the recognition of this right of self determination, the Dodecanesians, appealed to humankind. Their beseeching voices were heard across the Adriatic, over the Mediterranean, and beyond the Pillars of Gibraltar. Attentive ears were found, and faithful breasts of representative men became the repositories of their righteous cause. In Italy as well as in England. In France and in the United States. In every civilized nation of the world where a scintilla of love for justice and freedom remains, men of 10-

prominence became the champions of the emancipation of the Aegean Islands from Italy, and their restoration to Greece. 

Sir Edward Grey in the early part of 1914 endeavored to induce Italy to evacuate the islands. 

Signor Bissolati, the Italian Minister, resigned because as he declared "he had failed to move Baron Sonnino from the doctrine of the permanent retention of the Dodecanese", stating in his speech at the Scalla Theatre of Milan, on January 11th, 1919, that "the annexation of this undisputed Greek land could only serve the negative interest of keeping Italy and Greece apart. 

Should Italy offer at the conference to restore the Dodecanese to Greece, she should invert the present situation and win a sure friend in the Southern Adriatic, and South Albania”.

Ten days after the Italian Minister made this statement, the American experts on Italian boundary Settlements, recommended on article four of document 31, "that Rhodes and Dodecanese be assigned to Greece. Over 80 per cent of the population of Rhodes and the Dodecanese are Greek Orthodox. They are bitterly opposed to the present Italian occupation, and should be assigned to the mother country".

This clear-cut statement of the American Commission truly expresses the prevailing sentiment in every one of the twelve Greek Islands. The inhabitants are bitterly opposed to the Italian regime. Their bitterness increases, due to the tyrannical measures that the Fascist authorities are ruthlessly imposing on the Hellenic islanders.

The United States Senate, mindful of these conditions, and of the unalienable rights of Greece over the islands,passed unanimously the resolution of senator Lodge No. 324, which quoted from the Congressional Record of May 17th 1920, page 7160, reads: "Resolved: It is the sense of tre Senate that the Northern

Epirus (including Coritza), the twelve Islands of the Aegean, and the Western coast of Asia Minor, where the Greek element predominates, be awarded to Greece and should become part of the Greek Kingdom."

Italy consented to award the Greek islands to their mother country. On August 10th 1920, the Treaty of Sevres was signed by the representatives of Italy, Bonin and Carlo Galli, and those of Greece, Venizelos and Romanos. The First Article of the Treaty gives to Greece complete sovereignty over the Hellenic isles of Dodecanese. The second stipulates that Rhodes is to remain under 

Italy, for fifteen years, and governed by an autonomous system -11-

of local administration. After this period, the Rhodians were to decide in a plebiscite, which was to be held under the auspices of the League of Nations, in the same manner as that of the Saar, for the purpose of choosing their final destiny. Greece, agreed, in the Third Article to recompense Italy for all her expenses on the islands, excepting those of military nature. 

The same day that the Treaty of Sevres was signed, Turkey had affixed her signature to the peace treaty with the Allies, renouncing in article 122, her titles, rights and interests in favor of Italy. This was done, so that the Italian Government obtained possession titles on the islands that she granted to Greece. 

The Italian army of occupation prepared to evacuate the islands. Temporary passports were issued to the travelers, good only up to the time of the cession of Dodecanese to Greece. Throughout the Archipelago, on every Grecian isle the natives were rejoicing for their thraldom's end, and for their long dreamed reunion with Hellas, from whom they were separated in 1830. Hellenic songs of patriotism and freedom were sung freely in the streets of the islands. The azure standards waved felicitations to the contentedly furling tricolored banners. The Greeks were embracing fraternally and kissing the Italian carabinieri. Pandemonium of bliss reigned in all the Sporade Islands. But this happiness was short-lived. 

Italy was slow in transfering the islands to Greece. She was eagerly watching for a pretext to abrogate the Sevres Treaty, as she annulled the agreement that was reached between Venizelos and Titoni, on January 29, 1919, which was also for the surrender of Dodecanese to Greece. The excuse then was, because the Allies had made certain decisions regarding Asia Minor, and due to some declarations of the Albanian Nationalists. The ostensible reason was now found, in the ruins of the unfortunate Greek expedition into Asia Minor. Such was the opportunity sought by the Government of Rome. On August 9, 1922, Mr. Metaxas was orally notified that Italy renounced the treaty of Sevres.

But when on July 24, 1923, the permanent treaty of peace was signed at Lauzane, between the Allied Powers and the Ottoman Government, Greece presented again her claims on Dodecanese, based on the doctrine of self-determination, and on the various agreements that she concluded with Italy, including those of the unratified Treaty of Sevres. Article 16 was inserted in the Lauzane Treaty, which stipulates that "the fate of the islands is settled or to be settled by the parties interested" (le sort de ces. territoires et iles etant reglé ou a regler par les interesses.) - 12-

On February 25, 1924, the Prime Minister of England, spoke in the House of Commons as follows: "I find that in concluding. with the Italian Government the agreement of April 1920, regarding Jubaland, Lord Milner made a written reservation in the sense that this agreement could only become effective as part of the general settlement of all the issues raised at the Peace Conference. 

These issues,as the Italian Government have often been reminded, embrace the question of Dodecanese,and the Italian Government, have for their part recognized that the Treaty which they con- Icluded with Greece on August 10 1920, was the result of agreement between the Allies, and that the settlement of the Dodecanese Question is not only the concern of Italy and Greece". 

By J. N. CASAVIS THE BRUTALITIES OF ITALIAN FASCISM 

So far Italy did not settle the Dodecanesian Question with the "parties interested". On the contrary, the Government of Mussolini continues to inflict upon the unfortunate Dodecanesians all the brutalities of Fascismo.

The Christian Italians have endeavored to achieve, within a score and three years, whatever the infidei Turks, during four centuries of domination over the Dodecanese, could not accomplish. That is, to undermine the imperishable institutions, and the tenacity of the character of the Greek people. 

The Italian Goverment has forced the Greek islanders to introduce the Italian language in their schools. A catholic priest was appointed to supervise the education, to remove teachers who are not persona grata to the Government, and to close the schools not conforming with the Royal Decree of 1926. It has compelled the 

Greek teachers to go to Rhodes, and learn the Italian tongue, and it has established its own schools, for the sole purpose of antagonizing those of the indigenous population, and obliterate thus the Greek learning, which was left unmolested by the Mohamedan conquerors. (To be continued)

The Dodecanesian schools, while under the Ottoman rule, were religiously respected by the Turks. They were effectively controlled by the Orthodox Metropolitan and his assistants, the "Ephoria". They acquired a reputation for the excellence of their instruction, and according to statistics at the date of enforcement of the Decree, illiteracy in the islands was practically non existent. 

Their maintenance was not born by the Government, but they were supported by the inhabitants, and by the Dodecanesians abroad. Since the enactment of the Education Decree, the Fascist

Government refused to accept the financial assistance offered to the schools by the islanders from America and elsewhere. The result was, the closing of all the High Schools, except that of Rhodes, which is also threatened to be discontinued. 'The systematic policy of the Italian Government to either Ital- 

- 13-

ianize or expatriate the Greek people of the Twelve Isles, met with vigorous resistance in Calymnos and at the other Greek islands. The fascist authorities, to quell the protesting Calymnians, have sent to Calymnos, a destroyer's crew, which with the assistance of the troops, surrounded the principal town, and dosed the offending natives with liberal quantities of castor oil. In Rhodes, all the members of the "Ephoria" were banished from the island, for, like the Calymnians, they objected against the Education Decree. The local Greek lawyer who was employed to draft their resolution for presentation to the proper Italian authorities, was also sent from his home to exile, without legal process and without any other explanation than the notification of the Governor that he was an obstacle to the Italian progress. 

Six school children of the village Psinthos of Rhodes, who were overheard by the carabinieri singing the Greek national hymn, during their recreation hour, were arrested, beaten and imprisoned for the night. The oldest of these infant offenders was ten. Women have been also maltreated for similar offences. At Maritza, a coffee house has been closed for three years, because a school boy without the knowledge of the propietor painted upon the outer wall of the building in chalk a rude representation of the Greek flag..

The spiritual subjects of the Pope, have forbidden the religious thodox community, enjoyed complete freedom in matters of muniprocessions of the islanders, permitted for centuries by the Mohamedans. While under the Turks, the members of the Greek Orcipal administration, religion, marriage and inheritance. Now they are subjected to constant irritations and injustices. The divine services of the churches of Holy Trinity and Evangelistria at

In Alaerma the Holy Gospel was trodden under foot, by intruding soldiers. The icon of the resurrection of Christ, and many others were broken to pieces. And in order to satyrize the 

Orthodox clergy, the Christian followers of the Vatican, painted on the walls of the church of Panagia-Pegani, a priest in natural size, having the legs of a stork. 

These premeditated acts of debasement, directed against the mother church of Christendom, were succeeded by the eager efforts of the Italian Embassy at Constantinople to persuade the Oecumenical Patriarch, to break off with the Dodecanese, and allow the establishment in the Twelve Islands of the Autocephalous Church, independent of the Patriarchate. Neither the conferences held between the 

Ambassador and the Metropolitans of Sardis, Chalkidon and Elioupoleos, were satisfactory to the Italian purposes, nor the dispatching to Constantinople, under military escort, of the bishop of Rhodes, and Leros, to ask for the ecclesiastical autonomy, resulted in favor of the Italian ambitions. The Dodeca-

The Italian Governor General of the islands, unable to attain by persuasion or bribery the separation of the Church, issued in 1934, a Protocol decreeing the independence of the Dodecanese from Constantinople. This malignant interference of the hateful despots of the Greek islands, was met, like the Education Decree, with uprisings, bitter and bloody. In Leros, that is a veritable fascist encampment, the unarmed but brave natives, rose in revolt against their masters. Calymnos did likewise. The Rhodians of Salaho, had paid their tribute to Liberty with blood, inprisonment and exiles. The churches of the islands were shut down as a protest and draped in black. The Government of Italy forces upon the Greek islanders, the lowest of servility. The pictures of Mussolini, in his sternest mood are sold to the inhabitants so that they should decorate their walls with them. At sun set, and at the sign of the gun, the

The fascist Government has taxed heavily the islanders, and it has confiscated the property of many Greek natives which was given to Italian settlers, such as are often shipped from the Italian peninsula to replace the native population. The authorities rest upon the assumption that all land belonged to the Sultan. and since that Italy succeeded Turkey, the land therefore belongs to the Italians. "One case in particular" states Mr. Booth, in his book 'Italy's Aegean Possessions', "just outside the urban district of Rhodes, reveals a state of affairs that in any other civilized community would be considered fantastic and impossible. The proprietor in question, rented a portion of his land to the Italian forces for exercise of the troops during 1917. showed that the Government had the intention of granting this property to the new Italian Hotel de Luxe for use as a golf course."

The Italian militarists make no secret of their intentions. 

Men of prominence, frankly stated to a special correspondent of the "Westminster Gazette", that "Rhodes for us is a base to be used in the next war. For that purpose, specially since the war will be with a country which has a negligible fleet, it will serve admirably."

The Italian Government has carried out a systematic moral and material persecution of the islanders. It has practically abolished the right of the people to elect their local Government. The candidates are often chosen by the authorities, and the elections of mayors have been cancelled three and four times, if the elected were not subservient tools to the Government. It has prohibited the circulation in the islands, of the Greek newspapers known for their sympathetic attitude for the Dodecanesians, and students from the University of Athens, were beaten for having such papers in their possession. It has even refused the donations that the 

Dodecanesians abroad have sent for the earthquake stricken natives of Cos, on April 23, 1934, when thirty were killed, one hundred wounded and the houses of many ruined.

The Government of Italy, has exiled from the Aegean Islands the most prominent citizens. Schoolmasters, doctors, lawyers, clergymen, merchants, and all those who are proud of their Hellenic ancestry, about which Thomas Jefferson, wrote to that famous 

Greek scholar Adamantios Korais as "the splendid constellation of sages and heroes, whose blood is still flowing in your veins, and whose merits are still resting as a heavy debt, on the shoulders of the living, and the future races of men." These persecutions and prosecutions, reduced the population of the islands, from 148,832 of 1912, to less than 60,000 to-day, and the exodus continues.

These grievous conditions that blot modern civilization, tend also to keep Italy and Greece apart, promote animosity among the nations of the Mediterranean, and endanger the peace of the world. Their abolition lies in the restoration of the unredeemed 

Aegean isles to their mother Greece, for the prompt realization of which all liberty loving people should assist.

The Leageer ITALY AND THE UNREDEEMED ISLES OF GREECE THE DODECANESE THROUGH THE AGES 

By the shores of Asia Minor, begirt with the wine-dark sea, that kisses eagerly the coast of the Hellenic mainland, and scattered upon the direct route that leads from the renown city of Constantine to the one built by Alexander the Great, lie the twelve fair islands of Dodecenese, enchained on the furious chariot of maddened Fascismo. 

Dodecanese derives her name from two Greek words, "dodeca" twelve, and 'nesos' island. The Dodecanesian Group includes the following islands: Rhodes, Leros, Calymnos, Cassos, Carpathos, Cos,

Nissyros, Telos, Symi, Patmos, Chalki, Astypalea. Also picturesque 

Castellorizo, small Lipsos and some uninhabited islets. The very name of each and every one of these islands is of Hellenic origin. 

Through tre "long train of ages", from the dark Minoan period, to the enlightened modern days, the Twelve Islands have always been populated by industrious Greeks. Greeks who have sponsored education, cultivated the fine arts, systematized the sciences, and spread the benefits of civilization in all directions. 

The science of medicine recognizes Dedocanese as her motherland. At romantic Cos, Hippocrates, the father of the healing art, was born. Rock-ribbed Telos gave humanity the first woman poetess. Volcanic Nissyros is credited with the most beautiful maiden Damatos, on whom the first historic phlebotomy was made by the Dodecanesian physician Melampous. Picturesque 

Cassos produced Dion, the historian. Famous Rhodes adorned the Pantheon of the "glory that was Greece" with Andronicos the philosopher, Peisandros the poet, Timachides the linguist. Impressive Leros is the birthplace of Pherekides the historian. Peaceful Patmos shelterd St. John the Divine, and sent Xanthos to sow the seeds that stirred the Greeks to revolt against Turkey in 1821. 

Arid Symi's Nireus led three well trimmed ships to Troy. Carpathos and Calymnos, Astypalea and Chalki are known for their gallantry and patriotic actions. Their valiant children often sacrificed their lives and fortunes so that Freedom may live. 

The Dodecanesians were Greeks during the pre-historic epoch as proven by the researches of the archeologists. The numerous coins, the many vases, the magnificent ornaments, the beautiful statuettes, the marble inscriptions, all these silent testimonies that were brought to light either by the local farmer's unsuspecting plow or with the aid of scientific excavations, confirm beyond any reasonable doubt their Hellenic ancestry. The archeological museums of Europe and America number among their precious possessions many such findings. 

During the fifteenth century before Christ, Danaos and Cadmos have found in Rhodes civilized and advanced communities, and 2-

they built on the acropolis of the island the famous temple of 

Lindian Athena.

In 1194-1184 B. C., the Hellenic people of the Twelve Isles participated in the Trojan war. About thirty war vessels were contributed to the Trojan expedition from the islands Nissyros, Carpathos, Cassos, Cos, Calymnos and Leros, according to the "catalogue" of the Acheans, the allies of Agamemnon. Rhodes assisted with nine ships, and Symi gave three. Homer, the blind poet of Ionia, praises separately each and every one of these 

Grecian nymphs of the Archipelago, and sings heroic chants for their gallant services to their Hellenic motherland. 

The athletic sons of Grecian Dodecanese were victorious at the Olympic games of 464 B. C. Cleomedes the boxer, Leonidas the wrestler, and the three sons of Diagoras, were from these Grecian isles. Pindar's lyre praises Diagoras, whose sons at the same hour have won in three different games. 

During the golden age of Pericles, Dodecanese was inhabited with patriotic Greeks. Greeks who offered themselves to the 

Atheneans, who were fighting so bravely against the Persian hordes, in order to save Europe from the Asiatic conquerors.

Athens triumphed, and European civilization was rescued. 

Dodecanese remained Greek during the third, second, and first century before Christ, in spite of the prosecutions and persecutions inaugurated against the heroic islanders by their despotic rulers. One of such despots was also the dominant Cassius (42 B.C.). This assassin of Julius Ceasar, had terrorized the inhabitants. He had put to death their political leaders and he had sent to exile their most prominent patriots, all those who abhored the Roman yoke. To such oppressive measures the Dodecanesians responded with renewed vigor and unfailing enthusiasm for their freedom. Their efforts gained for them, under the Roman Emperor Claudius (51 A.D.), the "ancient state and laws", as described on two stones found in the island of Rhodes. 

These liberties however that were won with long struggles and bloody sacrifices, were abolished by Vespasius at 70 A. D., and the brave sons of Dodecanesos resumed the just but unequal fight against their Roman masters.

In the year 730 A. D., Dodecanese was constituted as part of the Byzantine Empire. It is at this period that the name "Dodecanesos" is used for the first time in history, given to the twelve islands by Theophanes. It was then applied to these Aegean islands, with the exception of Rhodes, where mighty Colossus proudly stood, and Cos, the fatherland of Philitas, the poet, and

Xenocrates the linguist. The group however included daring 

Icaria, and the small Castellorizo. 

From 1309 to 1522, the Greek islands of the Mediterranean sea were under the Latin supremacy. They were divided into three groups: Rhodes, Cos, Calymnos, Leros, Nissyros, Telos, Symi, Chalki and Castellorizo, or "Castel Rosso" as now called by the

Italians, belonged to the Knights of St. John, except in 1450, when 

3-

the Pope gave the "Red Castle" to the King of Naples. Astypalea, Carpathos and Cassos were property of the Venetian families of Quirini and Cornaro, with the exception of a short occupation of Cassos and Carpathos by the adventurous Knights. They remained in the possession of these families down to the Turkish conquest of 1537. Patmos and Palmosa, and the small island of Leipso that lies near this island where the Apocalypse was written, was regarded as a holy land.

After the Latin conquest of Constantinople, the island of roses, Rhodes, bold Carpathos, and liberty loving Cassos, were occupied by the Greek family of Cavalas, and latter by the Greek Empire of Nicaea.

The Turkish conqueror of the Grecian Islands, had granted special privileges to the islanders, by virtue of which only a small amount of taxes were paid to the Porte per annum, known as "Maktou". Although the inhabitants had their own courts and their own local laws, under their Mohamedan rulers, their yet burning desire was their liberation. So when the Greek revolution commenced in 1821, and spread like a wild fire throughout the Hellenic provinces, Grecian Dodecanese took up arms for freedom. 

The Dodecanesians from the islands, the Dodecanesians that were scattered all over the civilized world, volunteered their lives and fortunes for the liberty of their mother Greece. To their enthusiasm Turkey responded with reprisals and onslaughts. The island of Cassos was set in fire. Cos suffered severely. Rhodes was terrorized by a Turkish force. On all the Greek islands the bloodthirsty Moslems were let loose. Girls were taken prisoners, and shut up in harems. Men were captured and sold as slaves in the markets of Stamboul. Capodistrias, that eminent statesman of modern Hellas, mentions with praises, their many struggles, and the Greek historians credit righteously their supreme sacrifices. 

Their blind devotion to the cause of Greek Independence, won for them their union with Hellas. Dodecanese, became part of the newly established Greek nation, and remained as such until 1830, when the twelve islands were returned to the Ottoman Government in exchange of Euvoia, for this island is nearer to Greece. 

Given back to Turkey, by the Protective Powers, they remained under the Mohamedan rule until 1912. In the course of this time, the Dodecanesians enjoyed special privileges, which were respected by the different succeeding Sultans, because to abolish them would have been similar to subjecting the poor peasants either to starvation or complete expatriation.

During the Cretan Insurrection, however, the Turkish Government had sent to the island Symi a Turkish governor with troops. 

The intention of the Porte was to assimilate the administration of the Islands with that of its other provinces. The indomitable 

Symiotes protested to the British Foreign Office, basing their complaints upon the rights guaranteed to them by the firman of Mohamed II. The English government intervened, and the Turkish governor was recalled. But again in 1869, the Turks blockaded the unenslaved Symi. Deputy-Governors and garrisons were — 4 —

sent there and in other islands. The liberty-loving Greeks of Dodecanese, determined to safeguard their rights and their privileges, sent a deputation to London, which presented their claims to Lord Clarendon. Their remonstrances were communicated to the Grand Vezir Ali Pasha in Constantinople, who denied "any intention of modifying the fiscal system hitherto prevailing in the islands; the sole subject of the Porte was to introduce a better system of administration". But this declaration was not corroborated by the subsequent events. Symi was blockaded again in 1893, and its notables were imprisoned. Five islands were annexed to the Sanjac of Chios and seven to that of Rhodes.

But in spite of all these, the islands retained their special privileges, and the rights of their local self-government, until the Turkish revolution of 1908, when the Young Turks wished to establish uniformity in the governmental system of the islands, as in other parts of the nation. In 1909 the Porte suppressed their privileges, and made the islanders subject to conscription. The 

Greek natives again protested, and the Turkish government agreed to content itself with the old system "until a definite decision on the subject was reached". In 1912, the decision was reached. It consisted in the complete annulment of the insular privileges. But before this was executed, the islands were confiscated by Italy. 

THE ITALIAN OCCUPATION 

On September 25th, 1911, the Italian Charge d'Affaires at

Constantinople presented an emphatic note to Turkey, in which it was stated that the Italian Government decided to proceed to to the military occupation of Tripoli and Cyrenaica, in order to safeguard its dignity and its interests. On September 29th, a state of war was declared, and on November 5th Tripolitania and Cyrinaica were, by a royal decree, annexed to Italy, under the generic name of Libya. The fighting continued in a desultory fashion, and the war against Turkey was carried on in other spheres. On April 18th, 1912, an imposing Italian squadron appeared off the entrance of the Dardanelles. The Italian fleet cruised unmolested about the Aegean, cutting cables and shelling various points, both in the mainland, and on the Turkish islands. 

On April 22nd, Astypalea was occupied. On the following day

Italy took possession of the other Dodecanese isles, except the Island of Rhodes, where the Italian army, numbered 12,000, and aided by thirty two vessels of war, landed on May 4th. The Turkkish forces on the island were 1,200. With the assistance of the 

Greek inhabitants, who looked upon the Christian Italians as their liberators from the Mohamedans, the Turks were take n prisoners at the battle of Psinthos. Five dead and wounded were the casualties. 

5-

General Ameglio, on his return from the battle of Psinthos, issued the following Proclamation to the "inhabitants of the island of Rhodes": "Italy, connected with you by glorious recollections, and kindred civilization, feels bound by the evolution of the war to proceed to the occupation of your island. By order of His Majesty the King of Italy, Victor Emmanuel III, my August Sovereign, I enter into my civil and military functions amongst you, and I declare that, though Italy is in a full state of war with the Government and army of Turkey, she considers the peaceful population of Rhodes as friendly and at the same time to affirm henceforth the highest respect for your customs and traditions." 

To the Rhodian Demogerontia (Assembly of the Elders), the

General declared that: "The Turkish supremacy has ended on 

Rhodes and on the other islands, by the temporary occupation of them by Italy, and the future of the islands cannot be other than their autonomy."

On his first visit to the Metropolitan of the island, and in the presence of the Elders, General Ameglio said: "I assure you most emphatically that after the termination of the Italo-Turkish war, these islands temporarily occupied by Italy, will receive an autonomous system of government similar to that of Samos, and the Turk will return no more. (Avrebbero uno stato d' autonomia comme per esempio Samos, et il Turko non ritornera mai). These I say as a general and as a Christian, and you must consider my words as words of the gospel." 

On May 12th, the Italian Rear-Admiral Ernesto Presbytero issued and caused to be posted throughout the island of Calymnos a declaration in the name of His Majesty, the King of Italy, announcing that: "from to-day all authority of the Ottoman Government ceases on the island, which under the supervision of the Italian Government will be self-governe d".

Sig. Giolitti also declared that the occupation was purely military, and that "Italy could not pretend to annex territories of 

Greek Nationality."

To the above official official assurances for autonomy, the Greek people of the Twelve Islands, responded with marked enthusiasm. The Italian army of occupation was hailed as their emancipator. Bygone memories of Italo-Hellenic fraternalism were brought to their rejoicing minds. The heroic sacrifice of Santa Rosa, who died for the freedom of Hellas in 1821, the enlistment of numerous Greeks in the Italian Army, to offer their lives and fortunes for the emancipation of Italy in 1859, - 6-

the displayed philhellenism of Riccioti Garibaldi, who led a company of Italian patriots to Thessaly, and championed the cause of Greece in 1897, the unselfish and wholehearted help rendered to the Italian refugees by the islanders of Eptanesos during tre Revolutionary War of Italy, all these were sincere expressions of brotherly love between the peoples of the two neighboring nations.

Upon the strength of the friendly ties that were uniting the Greeks with the Italians, and resting their hopes on the official promises of the representatives of Italy, the words of whom were told to accept as those of the gospel, the Dodecanesians called a congress of delegates from every island, who met at Patmos, to thank their liberators, and to draft treir laws for self-goverment. On June 17th 1912, they passed unanimously a resolution, of which the following is a translation: "The federation of the islanders of the Aegean, consisting of representatives of the islands occupied by the Italians, gathered in the church of the Monastery of the Apocalypse of Saint John the Evengelist in accordance with the mandate which they have received and the wish of the mandatories, hereby: "(I) Express to the brotherly Italian nation, her King and her government, the infinite gratitude of the aforenamed islands for having delivered them from the intolerable yoke of the Turks. "(II) Proclaim the firm determination of the people of these Christian islands to undergo any sacrifice rather than return under the frightful tyranny of the Turks. "(III) Proclaim the permanent national wish of the Aegeans to be united with their mother country, Greece. “(IV) Proclaim the complete autonomy of the liberated islands, based upon the official documents and the verbal statements of General Ameglio, on the proclamation of the Admirals commanding the powerful Italian fleet, as well as on their acquired privileges of self government, and on the doctrine that a country once delivered from the Turkish domination shall never return to it. "(V) Name the whole of the liberated islands "Aegean State." "(VI) Give as symbol of this State a blue flag intersected in the middle by a white cross, and as emblem, the portrait of 

Apollon Helios. 

"(VII) Consecrate as laws of the State the laws and the particular customs of each community, besides the laws of our free 

Greek brethren. 7-

"(VIII) Solicit support of the present decree from the benefactress Italy and from the other civilized powers, and also the goodwill for the recognition of the Aegean State. "(IX) Postpone for the time being their resolution for the election of the supreme chief of the State, as also of that of the 

Legislative Council. "(X) Provisionally suspend their labours and charge MM Th. 

Olympitis, merchant, G. Drakidis, lawyer, and N. Petrides, merchant, to transmit a copy of the present decree to General Ameglio, commander of the Italian Army at Rhodes. They will ask him to make their resolution known to H. M. the King of Italy, to the Chambers of the liberal and glorious Italian nation, and to the Royal Government of Italy, and at the same time to hand over copies of this decree to the Governments of the other Great Powers. "Signed on the 4th of June (O. S.) 1912 

The Representatives of the Aegeans" (Here follow the signatures of the representatives and the names of the islands to which they belong.)

The Patmos gathering and these resolutions proved that the Greek islanders took for granted the official declarations of the Italians, and they believed that the government at Rome was imbued with humanitarian feelings. They were bitterly disappointed. The Italian authorities of Dodecanese began at once to curtail their liberties, to watch their movements, to banish from the islands their leaders. The Italian Press endeavored to justify the permanent occupation of the Christian isles, claiming that the inhabitants were satisfied with the status quo of their fatherland.

On October 15th 1912, Italy signed at Lausanne a treaty with Turkey, in which it was stipulated that: "The two Governments undertake the responsibility to give, immediately after the signing of this treaty, the order of recalling their officers, their army, and their political employees, that is, the Ottoman Government from Tripoli and Cyrinaica, and the Italian Government from the islands that it occupies in the Aegean Sea". 

This agreement, also, betrayed to the Dodecanesians the insincere promises of the 'general and christian' Ameglio and those of the Rear-Admiral Presbytero, that the islands were not going to be returned to the Turks but they were to be granted selfgovernment.

The people met in insular gatherings, and held plebiscites in every island to express their unwillingness to be handed back to 8-

Turkey, asking for their union with their mother Greece. At these meetings, the Italian army of occupation, displayed its true label, with arrests, imprisonments, brutal treatments. 

And while the natives of the Twelve Islands were subjected to all sorts of humiliations by the Italian intruders, their kinsmen at Mytelene, Chios, Samos, and the rest of the nearby islands were liberated from the Turkish yoke by the victorious fleet of Greece, during the Balkan War, that commenced in October 1912. The Italian occupation thus turned out to be the sole obstacle that prevented the Hellenic navy to free the Dodecanese with the other islands. It has embittered considerably the Dodecanesians, to see the adjoining islands under the Greek flag, while they still waited for the fulfillment of the promises for autonomy, or for joining their destiny with that of Hellas.

When on May 23rd, 1915, Italy entered the European holocaust, in betrayal of her Allies of the Triple Alliance, she did so after she was assured by the Secret Treaty of London, signed on April 26, 1915, "that Italy shall obtain full possession of all the islands of Dodecanese, at present occupied by her". 

To the Italian financial interests those strategic islands, that command the route that leads to the rich fields of Asia Minor, to Constantinople, and to Egypt, were the thirty shekels Italy demanded, in order to desert her former Allies, and offer the innocent blood of the Italian workers and peasants a sacrifice to the Moloch of economic benefits that were hidden behind the world conflict.

But the rapid development of the war events, the entrance of the United States upon the battle fields of Europe, the acceptance of the Fourteen Points, the participation of Greece on the side of the Allied Powers, the foundation of the League of Nations, and the declarations of the belligerent Allies that they were fighting for the self-determination of the peoples, placed the London Treaty in the diplomatic waste basket. 

The Twentieth Article of the League covenant, accepted by Italy, abolished all secret treaties. 

President Wilson announced in the United States Congress, on 

January 9th, 1918 that, "a readjustment of the frontiers of Italy should be effected along clearly recognized lines of nationality. 

The nationalities which are now under Turkish rule should be assured an absolutely unmolested opportunity of autonomous development. What we demand is that the world be made safe for every nation that wishes to live its own life, determine its own in-

stitutions, to be assured of justice and fair dealing by the other people of the world as agaisnt force and selfish aggression. The day of conquest and aggrandizement is gone by". And the same great American, declared in Paris, on the 23rd day of April, 1919, that "when Italy entered the war she entered upon the basis of a definite private understanding, with Great Britain and France, now known as the Pact of London. Since that time the whole face of circumstances has been altered. Many Powers, great and small, have entered the struggle with no knowledge of the private understanding".

On the same day, Colonel House had made a reservation in the Peace Conference, stating that the boundary lines laid down, should not be construed as an acceptance of the Treaty of London. 

The world conflict terminated with the victory of the Allied Powers. Greece's services and sacrifices in the united front were great and of the utmost importance. The Greek casualties, compiled by the U. S. War Department, were 5,000 dead and 21,000 wounded. These figures do not include the number of Greeks who were killed or wounded while fighting under the American flag, or under the banners of the other allied nations. Nor do they represent the gory tributes of the Dodecanesians, upon the altar of Mars, and their contribution of forty-five commercial steamships to the Allies, for the transportation of troops, munitions and supplies.

The Dodecanesians fought bravely, and died contented, for they believed that the day of their liberation was not afar. The leading statesmen of Europe and America guaranteed their freedom. 

Lloyd George stated on January 5, 1918, that "equality of rights among nations, small as well as great, is one of the fundamental issues this country and her Allies are fighting to establish in this war. Government by consent of the governed, must be the basis of any territorial settlement. Territorial settlement must be secured based on the right of self determination or the consent of the governed".

For the recognition of this right of self determination, the Dodecanesians, appealed to humankind. Their beseeching voices were heard across the Adriatic, over the Mediterranean, and beyond the Pillars of Gibraltar. Attentive ears were found, and faithful breasts of representative men became the repositories of their righteous cause. In Italy as well as in England. In France and in the United States. In every civilized nation of the world where a scintilla of love for justice and freedom remains, men of -10-

prominence became the champions of the emancipation of the Aegean Islands from Italy, and their restoration to Greece. 

Sir Edward Grey in the early part of 1914 endeavored to induce Italy to evacuate the islands. 

Signor Bissolati, the Italian Minister, resigned because as he declared "he had failed to move Baron Sonnino from the doctrine of the permanent retention of the Dodecanese", stating in his speech at the Scalla Theatre of Milan, on January 11th, 1919, that "the annexation of this undisputed Greek land could only serve the negative interest of keeping Italy and Greece apart. 

Should Italy offer at the conference to restore the Dodecanese to Greece, she should invert the present situation and win a sure friend in the Southern Adriatic, and South Albania".

Ten days after the Italian Minister made this statement, the American experts on Italian boundary Settlements, recommended on article four of document 31, "that Rhodes and Dodecanese be assigned to Greece. Over 80 per cent of the population of Rhodes and the Dodecanese are Greek Orthodox. They are bitterly opposed to the present Italian occupation, and should be assigned to the mother country".

This clear-cut statement of the American Commission truly expresses the prevailing sentiment in every one of the twelve Greek Islands. The inhabitants are bitterly opposed to the Italian regime. Their bitterness increases, due to the tyrannical measures that the Fascist authorities are ruthlessly imposing on the Hellenic islanders.

The United States Senate, mindful of these conditions, and of the unalienable rights of Greece over the islands,passed unanimously the resolution of senator Lodge No. 324, which quoted from the Congressional Record of May 17th 1920, page 7160, reads: "Resolved: It is the sense of tre Senate that the Northern

Epirus (including Coritza), the twelve Islands of the Aegean, and the Western coast of Asia Minor, where the Greek element predominates, be awarded to Greece and should become part of the Greek Kingdom."

Italy consented to award the Greek islands to their mother country. On August 10th 1920, the Treaty of Sevres was signed by the representatives of Italy, Bonin and Carlo Galli, and those of Greece, Venizelos and Romanos. The First Article of the Treaty gives to Greece complete sovereignty over the Hellenic isles of Dodecanese. The second stipulates that Rhodes is to remain under 

Italy, for fifteen years, and governed by an autonomous system 

11.

of local administration. After this period, the Rhodians were to decide in a plebiscite, which was to be held under the auspices of the League of Nations, in the same manner as that of the Saar, for the purpose of choosing their final destiny. Greece, agreed, in the Third Article to recompense Italy for all her expenses on the islands, excepting those of military nature. 

The same day that the Treaty of Sevres was signed, Turkey had affixed her signature to the peace treaty with the Allies, renouncing in article 122, her titles, rights and interests in favor of Italy. This was done, so that the Italian Government obtained possession titles on the islands that she granted to Greece. 

The Italian army of occupation prepared to evacuate the islands. Temporary passports were issued to the travelers, good only up to the time of the cession of Dodecanese to Greece. Throughout the Archipelago, on every Grecian isle the natives were rejoicing for their thraldom's end, and for their long dreamed reunion with Hellas, from whom they were separated in 1830. Hellenic songs of patriotism and freedom were sung freely in the streets of the islands. The azure standards waved felicitations to the contentedly furling tricolored banners. The Greeks were embracing fraternally and kissing the Italian carabinieri. Pandemonium of bliss reigned in all the Sporade Islands. But this happiness was short-lived. 

Italy was slow in transfering the islands to Greece. She was eagerly watching for a pretext to abrogate the Sevres Treaty, as she annulled the agreement that was reached between Venizelos and Titoni, on January 29, 1919, which was also for the surrender of Dodecanese to Greece. The excuse then was, because the Allies had made certain decisions regarding Asia Minor, and due to some declarations of the Albanian Nationalists. The ostensible reason was now found, in the ruins of the unfortunate Greek expedition into Asia Minor. Such was the opportunity sought by the Government of Rome. On August 9, 1922, Mr. Metaxas was orally notified that Italy renounced the treaty of Sevres.

But when on July 24, 1923, the permanent treaty of peace was signed at Lauzane, between the Allied Powers and the Ottoman Government, Greece presented again her claims on Dodecanese, based on the doctrine of self-determination, and on the various agreements that she concluded with Italy, including those of the unratified Treaty of Sevres. Article 16 was inserted in the Lauzane Treaty, which stipulates that "the fate of the islands is settled or to be settled by the parties interested" (le sort de ces territoires et iles etant reglé ou a regler par les interesses.)

On February 25, 1924, the Prime Minister of England, spoke in the House of Commons as follows: "I find that in concluding with the Italian Government the agreement of April 1920, regarding Jubaland, Lord Milner made a written reservation in the sense that this agreement could only become effective as part of the general settlement of all the issues raised at the Peace Conference. 

These issues, as the Italian Government have often been reminded, embrace the question of Dodecanese, and the Italian Government, have for their part recognized that the Treaty which they con- Icluded with Greece on August 10 1920, was the result of agreement between the Allies, and that the settlement of the Dodecanese Question is not only the concern of Italy and Greece". 

THE BRUTALITIES OF ITALIAN FASCISM 

So far Italy did not settle the Dodecanesian Question with the "parties interested". On the contrary, the Government of Mussolini continues to inflict upon the unfortunate Dodecanesians all the brutalities of Fascismo.

The Christian Italians have endeavored to achieve, within a score and three years, whatever the infidei Turks, during four centuries of domination over the Dodecanese, could not accomplish. That is, to undermine the imperishable institutions, and the tenacity of the character of the Greek people. 

The Italian Goverment has forced the Greek islanders to introduce the Italian language in their schools. A catholic priest was appointed to supervise the education, to remove teachers who are not persona grata to the Government, and to close the schools not conforming with the Royal Decree of 1926. It has compelled the 

Greek teachers to go to Rhodes, and learn the Italian tongue, and it has established its own schools, for the sole purpose of antagonizing those of the indigenous population, and obliterate thus the Greek learning, which was left unmolested by the Mohamedan conquerors.

The Dodecanesian schools, while under the Ottoman rule, were religiously respected by the Turks. They were effectively controlled by the Orthodox Metropolitan and his assistants, the "Ephoria". They acquired a reputation for the excellence of their instruction, and according to statistics at the date of enforcement of the Decree, illiteracy in the islands was practically non existent. 

Their maintenance was not born by the Government, but they were supported by the inhabitants, and by the Dodecanesians abroad. Since the enactment of the Education Decree, the Fascist

Government refused to accept the financial assistance offered to the schools by the islanders from America and elsewhere. The result was, the closing of all the High Schools, except that of Rhodes, which is also threatened to be discontinued. 

The systematic policy of the Italian Government to either Ital- - 13 —

ianize or expatriate the Greek people of the Twelve Isles, met with vigorous resistance in Calymnos and at the other Greek islands. The fascist authorities, to quell the protesting Calymnians, have sent to Calymnos, a destroyer's crew, which with the assistance of the troops, surrounded the principal town, and dosed the offending natives with liberal quantities of castor oil. In Rhodes, all the members of the "Ephoria" were banished from the island, for, like the Calymnians, they objected against the Education Decree. The local Greek lawyer who was employed to draft their resolution for presentation to the proper Italian authorities, was also sent from his home to exile, without legal process and without any other explanation than the notification of the Governor that he was an obstacle to the Italian progress. 

Six school children of the village Psinthos of Rhodes, who were overheard by the carabinieri singing the Greek national hymn, during their recreation hour, were arrested, beaten and imprisoned for the night. The oldest of these infant offenders was ten. Women have been also maltreated for similar offences. At Maritza, a coffee house has been closed for three years, because a school boy without the knowledge of the propietor painted upon the outer wall of the building in chalk a rude representation of the Greek flag.

The spiritual subjects of the Pope, have forbidden the religious processions of the islanders, permitted for centuries by the Mohamedans. While under the Turks, the members of the Greek Orthodox community, enjoyed complete freedom in matters of municipal administration, religion, marriage and inheritance. Now they are subjected to constant irritations and injustices. The divine services of the churches of Holy Trinity and Evangelistria at 

Archangelos, in Rhodes, were interrupted by carabinieri, who thrust into the churches and proceeded into the Altar, in order to arrest the priests, who were still dressed with their sacerdotal vestments. In Alaerma the Holy Gospel was trodden under foot, by intruding soldiers. The icon of the resurrection of Christ, and many others were broken to pieces. And in order to satyrize the 

Orthodox clergy, the Christian followers of the Vatican, painted on the walls of the church of Panagia-Pegani, a priest in natural size, having the legs of a stork.

These premeditated acts of debasement, directed against the mother church of Christendom, were succeeded by the eager efforts of the Italian Embassy at Constantinople to persuade the Oecumenical Patriarch, to break off with the Dodecanese, and allow the establishment in the Twelve Islands of the Autocephalous Church, independent of the Patriarchate. Neither the conferences held between the 

Ambassador and the Metropolitans of Sardis, Chalkidon and Elioupoleos, were satisfactory to the Italian purposes, nor the dispatching to Constantinople, under military escort, of the bishop of Rhodes, and Leros, to ask for the ecclesiastical autonomy, resulted in favor of the Italian ambitions. nesians the world over, who saw behind the anxiety of the insular authorities to separate the churches of the islands from their Mother Church, the glittering Papal throne, did whatever was humanly possible for the protection of their faith. 

The Italian Governor General of the islands, unable to attain by persuasion or bribery the separation of the Church, issued in 1934, a Protocol decreeing the independence of the Dodecanese from Constantinople. This malignant interference of the hateful despots of the Greek islands, was met, like the Education Decree, with uprisings, bitter and bloody. In Leros, that is a veritable fascist encampment, the unarmed but brave natives, rose in revolt against their masters. Calymnos did likewise. The Rhodians of Salaho, had paid their tribute to Liberty with blood, inprisonment and exiles. The churches of the islands were shut down as a protest and draped in black. A sail boat, full of escaping Calymnians, was sunk by an Italian destroyer, at the begining of February, 1935. 

The official representatives of the Italian fascists, Lieutenant 

Matsei and two carabinieri have beaten to death the eleven year old Greek lad Kostakis Kavassilis, because he had entered the private gardens of the Governor of Dodecanese, in Rhodes, and begged for alms. The child was rushed to the hospital, but died there within 24 hours. The unfortunate mother Olga, was forced to keep silence.

The Government of Italy forces upon the Greek islanders, the lowest of servility. The pictures of Mussolini, in his sternest mood are sold to the inhabitants so that they should decorate their walls with them. At sun set, and at the sign of the gun, the 

Greeks, must uncover themselves and stand at fascist salute, no matter where they happen to be. The severest of punishment has been inflicted upon peasants for inadvertent disregard for this regulation. The unfortunate islanders to avoid infractions are saluting every well dressed stranger who visits the islands, for fear that he may be Italian.

The fascist Government has taxed heavily the islanders, and it has confiscated the property of many Greek natives which was given to Italian settlers, such as are often shipped from the Italian peninsula to replace the native population. The authorities rest upon the assumption that all land belonged to the Sultan, and since that Italy succeeded Turkey, the land therefore belongs to the Italians. "One case in particular" states Mr. Booth, in his book 'Italy's Aegean Possessions', "just outside the urban district of Rhodes, reveals a state of affairs that in any other civilized community would be considered fantastic and impossible. The proprietor in question, rented a portion of his land to the Italian forces for exercise of the troops during 1917. At the close of the war the troops continued to use the land without paying rent, despite several demands from the proprietor that he be permitted to cultivate it. The owner on noticing some laborers engaged in enclosing the land with a fence, complained to the authorities, only to be told that the land was no longer his. Subsequent inquiry 

15-

showed that the Government had the intention of granting this property to the new Italian Hotel de Luxe for use as a golf course." 

Italian Imperialism has fortified the peaceful islands, preparing for war against Turkey. It has built on the shores of the bay of 

Leros, which is situated on the direct route between two of the greatest channels of international traffic, the Suez Canal and the Bosporos, barracks, aeroplane hangars, and an arsenal on a large scale, and it has installed big modern guns, facing Asia Minor, where the mineral wealth of Lycia, and the coal fields of Heraklea lie". The Italian militarists make no secret of their intentions. 

Men of prominence, frankly stated to a special correspondent of the "Westminster Gazette", that "Rhodes for us is a base to be used in the next war. For that purpose, specially since the war will be with a country which has a negligible fleet, it will serve admirably."

The Italian Government has carried out a systematic moral and material persecution of the islanders. It has practically abolished the right of the people to elect their local Government. The candidates are often chosen by the authorities, and the elections of mayors have been cancelled three and four times, if the elected were not subservient tools to the Government. It has prohibited the circulation in the islands, of the Greek newspapers known for their sympathetic attitude for the Dodecanesians, and students from the University of Athens, were beaten for having such papers in their possession. It has even refused the donations that the 

Dodecanesians abroad have sent for the earthquake stricken natives of Cos, on April 23, 1934, when thirty were killed, one hundred wounded and the houses of many ruined.

The Government of Italy, has exiled from the Aegean Islands the most prominent citizens. Schoolmasters, doctors, lawyers, clergymen, merchants, and all those who are proud of their Hellenic ancestry, about which Thomas Jefferson, wrote to that famous 

Greek scholar Adamantios Korais as "the splendid constellation of sages and heroes, whose blood is still flowing in your veins, and whose merits are still resting as a heavy debt, on the shoulders of the living, and the future races of men." These persecutions and prosecutions, reduced the population of the islands, from 148,832 of 1912, to less than 60,000 to-day, and the exodus continues.

These grievous conditions that blot modern civilization, tend also to keep Italy and Greece apart, promote animosity among the nations of the Mediterranean, and endanger the peace of the world. Their abolition lies in the restoration of the unredeemed 

Aegean isles to their mother Greece, for the prompt realization of which all liberty loving people should assist.
