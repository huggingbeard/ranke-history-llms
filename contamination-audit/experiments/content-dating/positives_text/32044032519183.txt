FOREWORD 

This publication is a companion to the 1980 Edition of Characteristics of State Plans for Aid to 

Families with Dependent Children under Title IV-A of the Social Security Act (SSA-80-21235) which reports selected administrative, eligibility, and need determination data by State as of September, 30, 1979. These Research Tables present the same information for the nation as a whole in a series of tables which represent the State's implementation of the Social Security Act into State plan for AFDC. 

Each table carries an index key in the upper right corner, coded to the pertinent section of the State pages in the Characteristics book. For example, Section III of the report of each of the States covers "Need Determination," and subsection A, paragraph 1, reports the State's declaration about "Persons Included in the Grant." Accordingly, the "Research Tables" coded IIIA-1 shows all 54 jurisdictions classified by the kinds of persons each State includes as part of the assistance unit: the needy child, a parent or caretaker relative, the second adult in the home as permissible under Federal regulations, the unemployed parent, and "essential persons" as defined by the State. 

A user of the tables can identify from them the States that would be of interest to him and then refer to Section IIIA-1 of the pages of those particular States in the Characterisics publication for the details upon which the classification is based. Thus the two publications complement each other.

A new table, "Basic and Special Circumstance Items Included in State AFDC Standards" has been added which should give needed administrative direction in developing more adequate provisions for needy children, by administrators and legislators charged with that responsibility. 

The accumulative impact of past administrative direction on eligibility and payment is shown in Table C "Amount for All Basic Needs, as Defined by the State in Its Need Standard and Amount of 

Payment after application of Any Method Used to Limit Payment."

Information relative to total expenditures, recipient count and average payments are not reflected in these research tables. When reproducing these research tables identify source of data. 1 3 6 9 7 82

AGE AS AN ELIGIBILITY FACTOR IN AID TO FAMILIES WITH DEPENDENT CHILDREN Federal requirement: Title IV of the Social Security Act defines Aid to Families with Dependent 

Children as assistance to needy children who are under the age of 18 years*, or age 18 but under the age of 21 years if they are regularly attending a school, college, or university or taking courses of vocational or technical training designed to fit them for gainful employment. A child age 16 or older who is not attending school full time must register with the Work Incentive Program (WIN) and cooperate in referral for work or training.

Item: IIA-3 Residence RESIDENCE AS AN ELIGIBILITY FACTOR IN AID TO FAMILIES WITH DEPENDENT CHILDREN 

Federal requirement: The Federal act still contains the requirement that a State plan may not include any residence requirement more restrictive than the maximum in the act for Aid to Families with Dependent Children, which is: 1 year immediately preceding the application or born within 1 year immediately preceding the application if the parent or other relative with whom the child is living has resided in the State for 1 year immediately preceding the birth of the child. 

However, the April 21, 1969, decision of the United States Supreme Court (Shapiro v. Thompson) forbids any residence requirement which excludes any individual who resides in the State. A State may still require that an individual be a resident, defined as a person living in the State voluntarily with the intention of making his home there and not for a temporary purpose. Residence is retained until abandoned. Child is a resident of the State in which he is living on other than a temporary basis.

Federal definition: The term "dependent child" means a . . . child . . . who has been deprived of parental support or care by reason of death, continued absence from the home, physical or mental incapacity of a parent which must be expected to last at least 30 days, or unemployment of a parent' and who is living with (stated relative) in a place of residence maintained by one or more of such relatives as his or their own home, or in a foster family home or private child care institution as permitted under the Federal act. 

The plans of all States follow the Federal definition of dependent child, with variations in the specifications of what constitutes "continued absence from the home," the definitions of incapacity, and the modifications in the lists of relatives. STANDARDS OF ASSISTANCE Federal requirement: Section 233.20 of the Code of Federal Regulations, Need and Amount of 

Assistance, requires (1) that "the determination of need and amount of assistance ... will be made on an objective and equitable basis and all types of income will be taken into consideration in the same way except where otherwise specifically authorized by Federal statute," (2) that “a statewide standard, expressed in money amounts to be used in determining (a) the need of applicants and recipients and (b) the amount of the assistance payment," and (3) that "if the State includes special need items in its standard (in addition to the items considered basic for all applicants and recipients) the items and circumstances under which they will be included must be described and the State must consider them in determining the need of all applicants and recipients requiring them."

Item: IIIC-1a Method of Limiting Payment METHODS USED BY STATES TO DETERMINE THE AMOUNT OF THE ASSISTANCE PAYMENT TO AN ELIGIBLE FAMILY 

Federal requirement: The amount of assistance that an eligible family receives, usually as a monthly payment, is determined by subtracting the income available (and countable as income under Federal regulations) from the total amount recognized by the State in its standard of assistance (Table IIIB) for the appropriate number of persons in the AFDC assistance unit. When the income, if any, plus the amount of the assistance payment equals the standard, the State is said to "meet full need." However, there is no Federal requirement that a State pay the full amount of need so determined. There is a requirement that if payments of full need, as determined, "are precluded by maximums or insufficient funds," adjustments will be made by methods applied uniformly statewide (45 CFR 23.20(a)(3)(viii). Such methods are usually some kind of percentage reduction (or "ratable reduction") applied to the standard of need or to the budgetary deficit between the family's available countable income and the amount of the State's standard of assistance. The payment which has been determined in this manner is further limited by an arbitrary maximum on the assistance payment for a family with a given size in some States. 

As a consequence of this broadly permissive Federal policy, State plan provisions reflect social attitudes toward assistance to people in need as well as the fiscal ability of the State and fiscal effort it is willing to exert. As a result fewer than half the States provide assistance payments that meet full need as defined by the State. A detailed table of the different methods of limiting payment (Table B) follows, but, in summary. a. 24 jurisdictions (23 States plus Guam) pay the amount set by the need standard; b. 3 States pay full need for smaller size families but limit the amount for a larger family by allowing smaller increments per person for additional family members beyond the sixth person (Washington) or by allowing no additional per person increments (i.e., setting a "family maximum"); (Kentucky and Oklahoma) and c. 27 jurisdictions (24 States plus the District of Columbia and Puerto Rico and Virgin Islands) 

make payments which do not meet the amount of their need standard.

Item: IVA Extension of Program- 

Unemployed Parent PAGE 1 

UNEMPLOYED PARENT 

Federal requirement: The Federal definition of "unemployed" includes "any parent who is employed less than 100 hours a month or exceeds that standard for a particular month if his work is intermittent and the excess is of a temporary nature." In order to comply with Federal requirements, the unemployed parent (1) has been unemployed for at least 30 days prior to the receipt of aid; (2) has not without good cause, within such 30 day period, refused a bona fide offer of employment or training; (3) has six or more quarters of work with a 13-calendar quarter period ending within one year prior to application for aid or within such period received or was qualified to receive unemployment compensation.

The Federal law (effective in January 1968) prohibited the receipt of assistance from AFDC for any week in which the father received unemployment compensation; and Federal regulations implementing the law required an unemployed person to avail himself of this potential resource by applying for it. A decision by the Supreme Court in June 1975 (Philbrook v. Glodgett) held that the unemployed parent could choose whether to apply for unemployment compensation or for AFDC. This ruling governed State plan provisions until the Congress included in the Unemploy ment Compensation Amendments of 1976 (PL 94-566) a section which amends Section 407 of the 

Social Security Act (AFDC-UF). The new provision, effective October 20, 1976, requires an unemployed parent to apply for unemployment compensation for which eligible and requires the AFDC agency, in determining the amount of assistance to be paid to the family, to subtract from the amount of the possible AFDC payment the amount of the unemployment compensation that the parent receives.

The family must also meet all of the other eligibility requirements of the AFDC program.

Item: IVB Emergency Assistance Eligibility Conditions PAGE 1 EMERGENCY ASSISTANCE TO NEEDY FAMILIES WITH DEPENDENT CHILDREN 

Federal requirement: This amendment to Title IV-A makes Federal financial participation available in aid to families where both parents are present and able-bodied or employed but a child is threatened by destitution or homelessness because of an emergency situation. It may also be used to meet emergency needs of families that are eligible for or receiving AFDC. The program is optional, and its broad legislative base makes possible many variations as each State defines the scope of its program. 

The State plan must specify the eligibility conditions in addition to those in the Federal act; the emergency needs that will be met; what services will be provided; provision that emergency assistance will be given forthwith; if migrant workers with families will be included, whether emergency assistance will be available to them Statewide or for what part(s) of the State. (FFP is available only for assistance authorized during one period of 30 consecutive days in any 92 consecutive months.)
