DRILL SIX I. HOW TO SOLVE PROBLEMS 

A problem is made up of two parts: 

1. A group of facts 2. 

A question to be answered about the facts Finding the answer to the question about the facts is called solving the problem.

Solving problems is one of the chief purposes of arithmetic. You must learn how to solve problems if you want to be good in arithmetic. 

To solve a problem, answer these questions and do these things: 

1. Read the problem carefully three times. 

2. What are you asked to find in the problem?

The question tells you what you want to find. 

The question is set off by a comma or begins with a capital letter. 

The question usually begins with How many, or How much. 

3. What are you told which will help you find the answer?

This is told you in the facts. Hunt for them. 

4. Should you add or subtract to find the answer?

Every problem has words which tell you what you must do. 

Watch for these telling words. Learn them. 

5. Do the work; that is, add or subtract the numbers. 

6. Have you done the work correctly?

Read the question to see if you have named the answer correctly. 

Go over your work to see if you have made any mistakes in it. 18
