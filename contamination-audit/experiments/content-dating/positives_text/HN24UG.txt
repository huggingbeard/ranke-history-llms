CRACK UP! 

By Dana Bartlett 

Captain Jerry Aurelius has a tough assignment when he is ordered to the civilian airport to command the Army flyers. The situation becomes more difficult when, on top of horrible weather conditions, Jerry is faced with the open opposition of the airport's manager, Duke Hamilton. Kay 

Lundy, lovely, young and a thoroughbred, runs the restaurant at the field. She adds at first to Jerry's troubles, for all her sympathies are with Clate Duffy, and the other civilian flyers. Jerry comes to want

Kay's friendship for more reasons than one. 

With the climax of a series of exciting events, Jerry finds his vindication and it is evident that against terrible odds he has achieved much for the Army air service.

JERRY AURELIUS' wind-burned face was hard as he approached the Grand Central Airport, goosing his throttle to clear the cylinders of his motor. The twoplace plane he flew had become as much a part of him as his arms and legs. He knew her little tricks, every intonation of her voice, and there were times when he thought the Curtiss Falcon knew him almost as well. 

He was certain she had emotions almost human, for if he tossed her around the sky, just trying to relieve his pent-up feelings, he could feel her displeasure; but if it was necessary to skirt a storm or move directly into one, so that the two-place was thrown about even more madly, she seemed to understand that it was not his fault, and showed no resentment at all. Then her motor droned high and shrill and confident, defiant as though she were challenging all the elements to force her down to the far ground below.

Aurelius had had two thousand hours on this ship. 

He had learned a good deal in that flying time, and in other countless hours in other planes. But much that he had learned would be of no help now, in the task that lay before him. The assignment had come as a shock, one which shook the whole air service, the edge not

He had chosen to regard this one as of a piece with most; rumors which were none the less fabulous for originating at Washington, and being blazoned to the world by the daily papers. "You will take command," his orders had read, "of a squadron of planes and pilots who are proceeding to the Grand Central Airport, Atlantic Division, for the purpose of flying the United States mails, as soon as cancellation of current commercial contracts becomes effective."
