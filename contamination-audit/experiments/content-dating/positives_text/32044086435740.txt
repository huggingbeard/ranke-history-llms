A Latter-day Prophet 

Why does humanity build monuments to men that are dead? Certainly not to please those who have passed on. Rather to perpetuate among the living in more lasting form than frail flesh the ideas of virtue, courage, patriotism, art, religious principles, or other noble qualities that the deceased exemplified and the builders of the monument love. It is a cultural act. Other motives than the pervading desire of gain inspire it. In thoughtful people it arouses a wholesome curiosity; for those who live in the realm of ideas and not wholly in the realm of the senses, life is always interesting and the more unusual its manifestation the more it grips the mind. 

In the monument that marks the birthplace of Joseph Smith the unusual is prominent. Commonly a monument is a local institution. For example, two monuments carry on to succeeding generations the name of the patriot, Nathan Hale -one in Connecticut where he was born, the other in New York City where he died for his loved country. Each monument was raised by funds largely of the city where it stands. 

But the idea and the funds that set up this polished shaft to the memory of Joseph Smith crossed almost the continent to perform their

The search for the boulder from which it was hewn, the transportation of it and its base over soft roads up the hill to this site, were difficult and interesting labors. 

But the unusual extends in a far greater degree to the life that had its beginning here in 1805 and its end in Western Illinois less than forty years later. That short span covers a period of this country's history, when it grew from a fringe along the Atlantic seaboard west to the Missouri river and was preparing for the rush that carried it to the Pacific. The spirit of adventure was never so high as in those days, for fertile land, new possessions, were the reward of all who would move westward. Joseph Smith took a vital part in this movement, establishing communities in three states and projecting that venturesome exodus westward to the Rocky Mountains. 

Fresh in the minds of men of that time were the ideas of political liberty for which the Revolution was carried on. The fetters of superstition were being broken by the awakening spirit of science and invention. It was a fitting time for a bold religious message to the world. Joseph 

Smith was the author of the boldest that had come forth in eighteen centuries.

At the age of twenty-four Joseph Smith published a volume of more than five hundred pages called the Book of Mormon. This was the first printed word for which he was responsible. It may seem curious to you who may doubt his mission, that this book was not about himself. 

It did not mention the great vision he had had. It did not touch in a direct way on the work he had announced he had been called to do. It did not even treat of any people in whom the world had particular interest. To transcribe and publish this volume required much time and all the money he could get together. He declared it to be the translation he had made under the direction and inspiration of the Lord of ancient writings on golden plates given him by an angel. These plates, while seen and handled by eleven witnesses who left their testimony of the fact, are no longer in the possession of man.

The book, however, is its own proof. If you will read it with a prayerful heart you will receive testimony from the Lord of its truth. It has been the greatest single influence in converting people to the divinity of Mormonism. It is a precious record. Reading it will increase righteousness, deepen one's knowledge of the doctrines of the Gospel, give one a clearer understanding regarding the events of the future.

Jesus Christ. Members were inducted into it by baptism and the laying on of hands for the gift of the Holy Ghost. The immersion in water was done in the name of the Trinity and for the purpose of the remission of sins. The forgiveness was founded on the atonement of Jesus Christ. 

An essential possession of the Church and one that has given it strength and coherence is priesthood, which Joseph Smith declared was conferred on him and his companion, Oliver Cowdery, by heavenly messengers-the Apostles Peter, James and John. With its authority he was able to organize the Church and administer its ordinances. 

A religious system should be judged by the depth of its influence on the lives of its adherents. 

We moderns do not care so much about its effect on the few zealots who more or less separate themselves from the practical affairs of the world. We will know how it affects the thought and action of those who plow and reap, tunnel for coal and ore, hammer and saw, weld and rivet, buy and sell, study, teach, practice the professions, sing, carve, paint. So judged, Mormonism, founded by the man whose monument you see, more profoundly actuates its followers than any system on earth. 

Brigham Young and other wise leaders have done their part, but the fundamental doctrines and organization were taught by Joseph Smith.

Moreover, he would have been the last to claim them as the product of his genius. They were revealed by the Author of the Church which bears

His name Jesus Christ. When the common practice of the members of an organization is to pay one tenth of their income for the Lord's work, such as supporting the poor and building churches, when almost all mature members and many of the youth carry ecclesiastical responsibility as teachers or presiding officers, when they aim to have one per cent of the population carrying their precious truths abroad as missionaries, incidentally paying their own expenses, it must be acknowledged that this religion is no side issue of life but its main issue.

The man who began his mortal career on this spot proclaimed, as was said, the boldest doctrine since the provincial carpenter of Nazareth_announced Himself the very begotten Son of God.

Joseph Smith taught that life is eternal, that the spirit of man is immortal, that the intelligence and individuality we possess today we will never lose worlds without end. Also, he taught that the eternity of the spirit extends backward into the past as well as forward into the future. 

Mortality is but a drop in the midst of the ocean of time. Mortals dwelt together as spirits and learned and loved and strove before the glorious occasion when they sang together at the laying of the foundation of this earth, where they were to enjoy the great adventure of mortality. 

This doctrine of the pre-existence of our spirits, familiar to the writers of the Bible, but which Christianity at the time of the apostasy forgot, gives back half the meaning of life.

Joseph Smith taught the literal resurrection of the body, which Jesus taught and which He demonstrated even to the doubting Thomas. To attain permanent possession of our bodies is one of the principal objects of being born into this world. The curse of the rebellious spirits who followed Lucifer and were cast down to hell was that they could never have bodies. In the time of 

Jesus they preferred to clothe themselves with the flesh of maniacs and swine rather than suffer their curse. God, the Father, possesses a material body; Jesus, the Son, has a body of flesh and bones, and when the objects of mortal existence have been attained, we who are of their family and kind will also possess an eternal clothing of our spirits.

As procreation is biologically the greatest purpose of life, so the union of the sexes normally interests every human being. The teachings of 

Joseph Smith regarding marriage have advertised his name and the Church he founded far more extensively than any other act or doctrine. His teaching that within the Church and under severe restrictions a man might marry more than one wife was, however, a minor part of that doctrine. 

In the Church of God formerly it had been allowed at times, and it had been forbidden at others. It was discontinued in this Church in 1890. The essential part of the tremendous revelation was the eternity of the marriage covenant when sealed on earth by that apostolic authority that has power to bind on earth and have it bound in heaven.

To believers in such devilish doctrines as that marriage is in itself an evil, or that sex unions may be entered into for temporary and experimental purposes with the prevention of the birth of children, such exalted doctrines as the eternity of marriage will not appeal. But to the man who cleaves to his wife as to the other half of his soul, who regards the fatherhood of his children as a partnership with God in the most beautiful of His divine purposes, this doctrine will be comprehensible and sweet. 

Talk of playing on a harp for a thousand years at a time, or sitting for an eternity gazing into the eyes of Jesus, sounds like strange jargon to our ears, but in Joseph Smith's day these were the supposed rewards of the faithful. In opposition to this he declared that all the intelligence we acquire in this life we will carry into the life to come. Is not that belief incidentally a great impulse to education? He taught that the reward of the faithful would be the opportunity of making eternal progress in learning and power. 

Being of God's family and kind, if we have accepted and proved faithful to His Gospel, we may take part with Him in creative work until as the millenniums roll by we may learn from Him to be 

Gods and creators, organizers of infinite matter in infinite space over infinite time. 

The message of Joseph Smith, the Prophet of

God, whose life began on this spot, was that the last dispensation of the Gospel has begun, that it warns mankind to repent of sin, selfishness and

Joseph Smith's warning voice declared that the testimony of the Elders' of Israel will be followed by the testimony of wars and rumors of wars, earthquakes, the sea heaving itself beyond its bounds, tempest and pestilence and fear and consternation causing men's hearts to fail them. 

The prophetic voice of this modern witness for God foretells the near advent of Jesus, whom the Jews by wicked hands had crucified on Calvary, to begin His millennial reign, and that the wicked when that terrible day comes will cry out in agony for the rocks to fall on them to hide them from His face, but that the Saints shall welcome Him with joy.

ARTICLES OF FAITH Of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints 

1. We believe in God, the Eternal Father, and in His son, Jesus Christ, and in the Holy Ghost. 

2. We believe that men will be punished for their own sins, and not for 

Adam's transgression. 

3. We believe that through the atonement of Christ, all mankind may be saved, by obedience to the laws and ordinances of the Gospel. 

4. We believe that the first principles and ordinances of the Gospel are:- −(1) Faith in the Lord Jesus Christ; (2) Repentance; (3) Baptism by immersion for the remission of sins; (4) Laying on of Hands for the 

Gift of the Holy Ghost. 

5. We believe that a man must be called of God, by prophecy, and by the laying on of hands, by those who are in authority, to preach the Gospel and administer in the ordinances thereof. 

6. We believe in the same organization that existed in the Primitive 

Church, viz: Apostles, prophets, pastors, teachers, evangelists, etc. 

7. We believe in the gift of tongues, prophecy, revelation, visions, healing, interpretation of tongues, etc. 

8. We believe the Bible to be the word of God, as far as it is translated correctly; we also believe the Book of Mormon to be the word of God. 

9. We believe all that God has revealed, all that He does now reveal, and we believe that He will yet reveal many great and important things pertaining to the Kingdom of God. 

10. We believe in the literal gathering of Israel and in the restoration of the Ten Tribes; that Zion will be built upon this [the American] continent; that Christ will reign personally upon the earth; and, that the earth will be renewed and receive its paradisiacal glory. 

11. We claim the privilege of worshiping Almighty God according to the dictates of our own conscience, and allow all men the same privilege, let them worship how, where, or what they may. 

12. We believe in being subject to kings, presidents, rulers, and magistrates, in obeying, honoring, and sustaining the law. 

13. We believe in being honest, true, chaste, benevolent, virtuous, and in doing good to all men; indeed, we may say that we follow the admonition of Paul, We believe all things, we hope all things, we have endured many things, and hope to be able to endure all things. It there is anything virtuous, lovely, or of good report or praiseworthy, we seek after these things.
