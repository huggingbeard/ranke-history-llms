FOREWORD 

The law of Summary Process is one of the most intricate branches of practical jurisprudence. Many attorneys recognize that its study must be undertaken after their law school days are over. In most cases it is only through bitter experience that the technical requirements thereof make themselves apparent.

To meet this situation the author has developed this volume as an expositive study dealing with this legal structure built by the legislatures and the courts for over a century. The author feels that the fundamentals and basic requirements of this specialized phase of intricate jurisprudence are herein described in detail. Moreover, he hopes, that as a reference book and as an aid in developing this knowledge, this volume will prove invaluable. 

An exhaustive analysis of the General Laws and of the Massachusetts Reports is the foundation upon which this work has been laid. Various sources have been consulted as text.

The author is extremely thankful to his many friends for their kind interest and encouragement.

Isaac Horenstein

GENERAL CONSIDERATIONS Section 1 Definition 

Summary process is a common method of obtaining the possession of real property without much fuss or ceremony.

It is an action moulded by the statutes and the courts to subserve the purposes of justice in a manner peculiar to itself.

It is a means of restoring to one the possession of lands from which he has been ejected or detained by force, or from lands unlawfully withheld from him by one who is in such position that he cannot dispute the claimant's right. 

Warren vs James 130 Mass 540

Section 2 Nature of the Remedy 

The entire fruit of a judgment is a mere writ of possession by which the officer is required to eject the tenant. 

King vs Dickerman 77 Mass 480 (11 Gray)

RIGHT OF ACTION Section 11 

Generally

The jurisdiction of the action, the form of its process and the method of procedure are fixed by the statute called "SUMMARY PROCESS". 

Gen. Laws (Ter. Ed.) Chapter 239 

Section one provides that—"If a forcible entry into land or tenements has been made, if a peaceable entry has been made and the possession is unlawfully held by force, if the lessee of land or tenements or a person holding under him holds possession without right after the determination of a lease by its own limitation or by notice to quit or otherwise, or if a mortgage of land has been foreclosed by sale under a power therein contained or otherwise, the person entitled to the land or tenements may recover possession thereof under this chapter. 

A person in whose favor the land court has entered a decree for confirmation and registration of his title to land may in like manner recover possession thereof, except where the person in possession or any person under whom he claims has erected buildings or improvements on the land, and the land has been actually held and possessed by him or those under whom he claims for six years next before the date of said decree or was held at the date of said decree under a title which he had reason to believe good."

Section 12 Forcible Entry 

Where the relation of landlord and tenant does not exist, a person entitled to the possession of the land or tenements cannot maintain the summary process in case of forcible entry and detainer, unless there has been an actual forcible entry or forcible detainer, by violence or threats of violence, in taking or keeping possession, or some act or threat of force adapted to alarm the party, or deter him from an apprehension of forcible resistance. 

Saunders vs Robinson 46 Mass 343 (5 Met.)

An entry to be forcible must be made by the use of actual force and violence, or by the menace of force, accompanied by arms and a manifest intent to carry such threat into effect, or by a show of force calculated to create terror and alarm, by an exhibition of arms, a display of numbers, or other means manifesting an open visible determination forcibly to make the entry or forcibly to resist the entry of another.

Commonwealth vs Dudley 10 Mass 403

9, Sec. 12] 

Mere unlawful entry after being forbidden without the use of any violence or threats, or any show of determination to use force is not sufficient. 

Saunders vs Robinson 46 Mass 343 (5 Met.)

Defendants with a workman went to a tenement in the occupation of the plaintiff but in which there was no one at the time, and the door of which was fastened with a padlock; demanded the key from the plaintiff's servant; and on his refusal ordered their workman to enter the premises through a hole in the floor. The workman did so; and by his assistance and with the aid of an axe which they brought with them, they removed the padlock, and entered and kept possession of the premises. 

They used no violence in word or act to the plaintiff's servant. It was held there was no forcible entry. Carrying an axe to a saw mill could not be supposed to excite terror. The removal of the padlock after a peaceable entry was a mere mechanical force not directed against the plaintiff's servant.

Striking the tenant with an iron bar by the landlord's agent is unjustifiable.

A temporary possession was obtained by barricading the doors of a building early one morning; and, upon the arrival of the tenants they took possession and ejected the intruders. Commonwealth vs Shattuck 58 Mass 141 (4 Cush.)

In general, the same circumstances of violence or terror, which will make an entry forcible, will also make a detainer forcible. Commonwealth vs Dudley 10 Mass 403 

The holding by force is a forcible detainer. 

Mitchell vs Shanley 81 Mass 319 (15 Gray)

A mere refusal to leave upon demand without force is not a forcible detainer.

Commonwealth vs Dudley 10 Mass 403 

Kiernan vs Linnehan 151 Mass 543

The possession must be attended with such circumstances as might excite terror, and prevent the claiming of his rights, such apparent violence offered in deed, or word to the person having unusual offensive weapons, or being attended by a multitude of people. 

Commonwealth vs Dudley 10 Mass 403 

Saunders vs Robinson 46 Mass 343 (5 Met.) 

Refusal to open door is not a forcible detainer.

Commonwealth vs Dudley 10 Mass 403 

Where a party was in the unlawful possession of a farm lying partly in Massachusetts and partly in Connecticut, and the owner entered on the Massachusetts side and demanded possession of the whole, and the other party, being then a short distance on the Connecticut side, and having an ox goad in his hand, thereupon threatened violence to the owner with that weapon if he did not leave the farm, the owner abandoned his demand for possession by the threat. It was held that this was a forcible detainer of that part of the farm lying in Massachusetts.

Benedict vs Hart 55 Mass 487 (1 Cush.) 

It has been held that the owner of house might resist those who came to remove personal property from the room of a tenant, the owner having no means of knowing whether their claim was rightful; and, that it was necessary and reasonable to ask them to wait until the tenant returned.

Possession in Plaintiff

A person who has never been in possession, though he has the legal right thereto, cannot maintain the action for forcible entry and detainer, or unlawful detainer.
