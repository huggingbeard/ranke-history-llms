The Birth of a Nation 

The Birth of American Independence Was the American Revolution Great Britain had won America from France, and North America was now England. She found that, although she had encircled her name with glory, she had increased her burdens. To find the means of defraying that debt was the first task of her legislators. In order, therefore, to lighten the burden upon themselves, their thoughts turned to the Colonies in America. On this account, the Revolutionary War had been waged. 

Three weeks after the shot first heard round the world in the battle of Lexington, the Second Continental Congress assembled in Independence Hall at Philadelphia on May 10, 1775. This Congress legislated for the Thirteen Colonies during the war, and here began a union movement which made a new nation in America. 

Who Was the First Commander-in-Chief? 

Artemus Ward was commissioned the first Commander-in-Chief of the Colonist forces, the post held later by George Washington. On 

June 15, 1775, John Adams suggested the nomination of Washington, then present as delegate from Virginia, for the high office of the Commander-in-Chief. The members unanimously approved the choice. Artemus Ward, Israel Putnam, Phillip Schuyler and Charles 

Lee were appointed Major-Generals. 

On What Day Did Washington Take Command of the Continental 

Army?

On Monday, July 3, 1775, Washington formally took command of the American forces at Cambridge, Massachusetts. 

[Page Two]

Who Wrote the Declaration of Independence? 

A committee consisting of Thomas Jefferson, John Adams, Benjamin 

Franklin, Roger Sherman and Robert R. Livingston was instructed to prepare a Declaration of Independence, which on July 4, 1776 was almost unanimously adopted. Thomas Jefferson wrote the famous document, John Adams secured its adoption in a three-day debate, and Timothy Matlack, a penman, wrote the final draft. 

Who Were the Only Two Presidents to Sign the Declaration of In dependence?

The only two Presidents to sign the Declaration of Independence were John Adams and Thomas Jefferson on August 2, 1776.

Where Was the Declaration of Independence Introduced to the 

World?

The Declaration of Independence was read by Washington's orders at the head of the Army then in New York. This memorial document completed the breach between England and America. 

What Was Washington's Password? 

The password was "Saint Patrick." Eleven of his generals and his naval commander, John Barry, were Irishmen. 

Where Was the First American Flag Raised?

At the opening of the Revolution the Colonies used a great variety of flags. The first flag of the United Colonies called the "Great 

Union," which bore thirteen alternate red and white stripes with the Crosses of St. George and St. Andrew at the corner, was raised by 

Washington on January 1, 1776, at Cambridge, Massachusetts. On

June 14, 1777, Congress adopted a resolution introduced by John Adams that "the flag of the United States be thirteen stripes, alternate red and white, and the Union be thirteen white stars in the blue field." The latter were arranged in a circle. 

On What Day Was This Country Called the United States of 

America?

The Declaration of Independence changed the name of "The United 

Colonies" to the title of "The United States," and on September 9, [Page Three]

1776, this country was first officially called the "United States of America."

What Soldier, Later to Become President of the United States, Rowed 

Across the Delaware With George Washington?

James Monroe, a soldier among those who rowed across the Delaware with Washington, on Christmas night, 1776.

When Was the Great Seal of the United States Adopted? 

Congress adopted the Great Seal of the United States on June 20, 1782. 

Where Did Washington Resign His Commission? 

Washington bade his officers farewell on December 4, 1783, at headquarters in Fraunce's Tavern, New York; resigned his commission as Commander-in-Chief at the State House at Annapolis, Maryland, on December 23, 1783; and retired to his home at Mount 

Vernon, Virginia. 

Was Washington Ever Wounded in Battle?

Washington never received a wound in battle, though he was fired at several times during the Monongahela fight in 1755. 

What Was Washington's Answer to Peace? 

"If we desire to secure peace it must be known that we are at all times ready for war."

CONSTITUTION ADOPTED 

Independence and peace did not immediately produce all the advantages which had been anticipated by the people. 

During the war, the States had agreed upon Articles of Confederation, but they were now found insufficient to accomplish the purpose of a National Government. The next step was to unite these States as a single nation. A National Constitutional Convention met in Independence Hall, Philadelphia, on May 14, 1787, for the purpose of revising the Articles of Confederation and electing a President of the United States. All the States except Rhode Island were represented. 

[Page Four]

After much deliberation, an entirely new constitution was adopted on September 17, 1787, and established on July 2, 1788, having been ratified by nine States. It was to go into effect March 4, 1789. 

How Many Delegates Signed the Constitution of the United States? 

Thirty-nine of the fifty-five delegates signed. The framers of the 

Constitution tried to avoid having political parties control a Presidential election.

Where Will One Find the Originals of the Declaration of Inde pendence and the Constitution of the United States?

The originals of the Declaration of Independence and the Constitu tion are now in the Library of Congress at Washington, D. C., having been placed there in 1921. During the last war, they were removed to the bullion depository at Fort Knox, Kentucky, for protection from air destruction. The Declaration of Independence escaped being stolen by the British during the War of 1812. 

FIRST ELECTION 

The first Presidential election was held on Wednesday, January 7, 1789, but because of the delay in the formation of Congress, the ballots were not counted until the 6th day of April.

In all elections prior to 1804, the candidate with the greatest number of electoral votes became President, while the one with the second number became Vice President. 

George Washington, receiving 69 votes, was elected President; John

Adams, having 34, was elected Vice President. 

Washington received news of his election at his home at Mount Vernon, Virginia, on April 14, 1789, and left immediately for New York, the seat of the government for its first two years. He left his home on April 16th, and it took him seven days to make the 235-mile journey to the Capitol. 

Thirteen years elapsed between the Declaration of Independence and the inauguration of the first President of the United States. 

DUTIES AND POWERS OF THE PRESIDENT 

The President of the United States must be a native born citizen, have been fourteen years a resident within the United States, and must have attained the age of thirty-five years. An American Indian born after 

1924 and a natural born citizen is eligible to be elected President. [Page Five]

The Constitution fixes the terms of the President and Vice President at four years. The President's term of office commences at noon on January 20, the date having been changed from March 4th beginning with

Franklin D. Roosevelt's second term. 

The duties of the President are to see that the laws of our Constitution are justly executed. This he can do by choosing wise men for counselors, upright men to rule the people, and by appointing honest men to protect our treasurers. If these men do not faithfully execute their duties, the President should immediately remove them.

The President of the United States may be removed from office at any time he is guilty of malfeasance, which is an illegal act of office. 

The success of the administration of any President must depend on the ability of his cabinet officers.

PRESIDENTIAL OATH OF OFFICE 

"I do solemnly swear that I will faithfully execute the office of President of the United States, and will to the best of my ability, preserve, protect, and defend the Constitution of the United States." 

The purpose of the American government is expressed in the preamble to the Constitution. [Page Six]

First Administration: Federalist 

George Washington took the oath of President of the United States on the balcony of Federal Hall, now the present Treasury Building, Nassau Street, corner of Wall Street, New York City, April 30, 1789. 

Chancellor Robert R. Livingston administered the oath. The members [Page Nine]

of Congress then attended divine services in St. Paul's Chapel, conducted by Bishop Provost, Chaplain of the Senate. John Adams was seated as Vice President on April 21. 

Which Was the Taller-Washington or Jefferson?

Washington and Jefferson were the same height-6 feet, 2 inches. 

Abraham Lincoln was the tallest-6 feet, 4 inches.

Describe Washington's Physique. 

Washington's hands were so large that his gloves were made to order. He wore a number thirteen boot and his walking shoes were number eleven. He had chestnut brown hair and several different pairs of false teeth. That's why the expression around his mouth varies in his different portraits. An enormous eater, he enjoyed two bottles of wine at dinner and a drink of rum or whiskey in the morning. He was one of the greatest pie eaters in our history, having pie at every meal. 

Did George Have a Fondness for the Ladies?

Throughout his entire life, he had a soft heart for the fair sex, especially the good-looking ones. 

How Many Slaves Had Washington on His Estate?

George was a successful business man. When he died in 1799 his property, exclusive of his wife's and the Mount Vernon estate, with 150 servants and 300 slaves, was valued at $530,000. 

Where Was the First Session of Congress Held? 

The first Congress was held at New York City; the second to sixteenth inclusive, at Philadelphia; all since at Washington, D. C. 

Now, all regular sessions begin on the first Monday in December; all others are special.

What Was the Most Important Business Demanding Attention by the First Congress?

Getting the machinery of the government into working order. The subjects of commerce and finance and the organization of subordinate departments and the judiciary. [Page Ten]

In What Year Was the First Census Taken? 

The first census of the United States was compiled in 1790. The population was 3,921,326 of which 695,655 were slaves. 

The largest cities were Philadelphia-43,000; New York-33,000; 

Boston-18,000; Baltimore-13,000; Pittsburgh was the largest settlement of the West. Nassau Hall at Princeton, New Jersey, was the largest building in the nation. 

Postoffices numbered 75. Between New York and Philadelphia there were only five mail deliveries per week. In 1884 there were over 50,000 postoffices.

Where Was the First Meeting of the Supreme Court Held? 

The Supreme Court held its first meeting in the Royal Exchange 

Building, Broad Street, New York, on February 1, 1790. John Jay was the first Chief Justice. 

When Were Negroes First Permitted to Vote? In the year 1619, slavery was introduced into Virginia. In this year, a Dutch vessel brought twenty African slaves to Jamestown. 

The Negroes were permitted to vote from the beginning of the government. However, in 1790, their vote was prohibited. This was the seed that later caused a Civil War. The Fifteenth Amendment, adopted on March 30, 1870, provided all citizens the right to vote.

When and Where Was the First Bank of the United States Chartered?

Alexander Hamilton, Secretary of the Treasury, suggested that Congress agree to assume the debt by leveling taxes on imported goods, the distillation of spirits and by the establishment of the First Bank of the United States, incorporated at Philadelphia on February 25, 1791, with $10,000,000 capital stock-one-fifth subscribed by the government and remainder by the public. The bank was in existence until 1811.

What Amendments to the Constitution Are Called the Bill of Rights? 

The Bill of Rights commonly referred to is the first ten amendments to the Constitution. There is no evidence that Massachusetts, Georgia and Connecticut ever ratified them. [Page Eleven]

When Was the Cornerstone of the White House Laid?

The cornerstone of the White House was laid on October 13, 1792. 

James Hoban, a native of Ireland, designed the estate. The mansion is about 200 feet long. On the grounds is the Executive Office 

Building, built in 1902, where the President has his office. The 

White House and grounds are valued at over $20,000,000. 

Did Washingon Write His Own Correspondence? 

Washington's correspondence was written by his aides-Hamilton, Jefferson and Randolph acted as his draughtsmen.

Why Was Jefferson's Inauguration Such An Important Event in Amer ican History?

The inauguration on Wednesday was an important event in American history for it was felt that by his triumph, the control of the Government was taken from the hands of the so-called "upper classes" and put into the hands of the "plain people." 

President Jefferson, instead of delivering a speech at the opening of Congress on December 8, 1801, established a precedent by sending a written message.

What President Compiled His Own Edition of the Bible? 

Jefferson wrote three books and compiled his own edition of the Bible. He also wrote one of the most famous documents ever published in history, the Declaration of Independence. On account of his talents, he was styled "The Sage of Monticello." 

What Was the Greatest Real Estate Deal in History? 

The most important event of Jefferson's Administration was the purchase of Louisiana Territory from Napoleon on April 30, 1803, for $15,000,000. This territory, more than one million square miles, extended from Canada to the Gulf of Mexico, and from the Mississippi River to the Rocky Mountains. This purchase doubled the size of the United States. The United States took possession of Louisiana on December 20, 1803. 

What Celebrated Statesman Was Killed in a Duel? 

In a challenged duel, on July 11, 1804, Vice President Aaron Burr shot and mortally wounded Alexander Hamilton at Weehawken, New Jersey. Only one shot was exchanged. Hamilton had fired in the air. Burr and Hamilton had been bitter rivals both in law and politics. Hamilton left seven children and a debt of $55,000. 

He died in his prime, at the age of 47. Burr, who once lacked but a single vote to make him President, was arrested and tried on a charge of treason. He was acquitted for want of proof. Burr, the son of a President of Princeton College, New Jersey, lived to be eighty.

Why Was the Twelfth Amendment Submitted to Congress? 

In 1804, the Twelfth Amendment to the Constitution was submitted to the people, and ratified by thirteen of the States. It ordained [Page Eighteen]

that the electors indicate their choice for President and Vice President. The idea originated with the Republicans, in order to provide against the chance of another disappointment such as had threatened them in 1801.

In What Year Did the Electors Vote for a President and a Vice Pres ident Instead of for Two Candidates for President?

The Constitution of the United States having been amended, the electors voted for a President and a Vice President in the Presidential Election, November 13, 1804.

James Madison, Alexander Hamilton and John Jay wrote a series of articles called "The Federalist" explaining and supporting the Constitution.

What Are Some Curious Circumstances Connected With the First 

Five Presidents?

In the ages of John Adams, Jefferson, Madison and Monroe there was a regular sequence, each being eight years older than his successor. Like Washington and John Quincy Adams, they were all inaugurated in their fifty-eighth year, and with the exception of the latter named, closed their terms of office in their sixty-sixth year. 

At the age of 66, James Monroe retired from office and returned to Virginia. His financial affairs being greatly involved, Congress voted to give him $30,000 in settlement of certain claims aganst the Government. Like Thomas Jefferson, he died poor in money, but rich in honor; and like John Adams and Thomas Jefferson passed away on the anniversary of the Independence of the country he served, July 4, 1831, in New York City. He is buried in Richmond, Virginia. 

Thomas Jefferson said of Monroe: "If his soul were turned inside out, not a spot would be found on it".

Monroe was elected to the Hall of Fame in 1905.
