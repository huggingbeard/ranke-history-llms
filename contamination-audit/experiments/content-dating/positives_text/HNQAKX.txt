WHILE THERE IS TIME...

1. 

E have built grandly, we have built augustly. 

Walls upon walls we have built, towers upon towers, Piled granite on steel and steel on granite; high in air, Lifted competing pinnacles; deep in earth,

Sunk vaults for our treasure, built shelters against the impermissible. 

Lavish of marble, prodigal of glass,

We have dotted the wide land with our functional. 

Packing-case monuments to the functional, packing-case mind. 

We inherited glory and we have made of it splendor. 

We inherited a vision, and whittled it down to Things. 

The intangible, we said, is illusion; the imponderable is the unreal. 

Therefore, get Things, fill hollow lives with Things. 

We have swept from our tables our fathers' bread and wine, Shrugged off with pitying smiles ten thousand years 

Of humble, searching hands, hungrily groping

Toward something, discerned, divined, that might be Reality. 

We ourselves do not have to grope. We have attained. We know. 

Reality, we point out, is the thing seen, The three-dimensional that can be grasped and weighed And speaks to man through silver discs, exchangeable For houses, women, reputation, power. 

We have sown dollars and harvested starvation. 

Listen, the mounting anger of the famished!

Not in remote deserts only, in soil-stripped valleys. 

Here! Even here. Around us, within us even there! 

The famished who cry for bread, and are given concrete and steel! 

Why do we hunger, we, whose tables are laden?

"It is nothing," we say. "What we need is another drink,"

As we watch, in awe, the cellular growth, the cancerous Multiplication of dollars, seeing therein Reality 

Made manifest to man, the golden token

Of the New Dispensation, of Revelation re-viewed. 2. "God is dated," we say. "We will create

Our own divine order, shaped to the new time. 

Let there be gods," we say, and there are gods, Ingeniously, from the shadow of our lusts,

The substance of our pride, fashioned and set on high 

Where all mankind may marvel and bow down.

Look where they loom and from their altars smile 

Sardonically upon the generation

That fashioned them and has by them, in turn, Been fashioned. See them, mountain-high, Shutting the sun off, blotting out the stars 

That speak of light and law and love, beyond 

All endings or beginnings. What do these 

Blank idols know of light or love or law? 

Look, they have eyes but cannot see; and ears, But cannot hear; tongues, but they cannot speak.

They have no life save as we give them life. 

Our incense is their breath. 

Listen. In harsh cacophonies,

From kneeling devotees, the litany 

Rises and falls. "There is no good or evil,

There is no right or wrong. 

Deliver us from absolute standards, Holy Relativity! 

Holy Expediency, repeal

The law of Cause and Effect!" 

Listen, how urgent, how devout, the supplications: "Give us, this day, whatever our lusts demand! 

Money, give us a sense of superiority!

Success, hide from us our inner bankruptcy! 

Sex, give us ecstacy, without responsibility! 

Ease, perpetuate our softness! 

Give us beauty, Cosmetics, at bargain-basement rates!

Appearance, let us get by, whatever may be inside! 

Entertainment, fill our emptiness, Though you deaden our minds and poison our children! 

Holy, holy, holy Irresponsibility! 

Great Trinity, sweet Three-in-one- 

Greed, Sensation, Sex, these three! 

But the greatest, in cash returns, is Sex."

Through the shrill litany, impassioned, urgent, Come other voices. They are the nations speaking: "This is not you, America! 

You of the Great Dream, the compassionate heart, Alien to hate, eager to love 

Whom yesterday you hated! 

Abject, at the feet of your idols, America, this is not you, Straightforward, clean, courageous, true!

In word and spirit and action, be as you once were, America!

Inspiration, hope and fulfilment to men in chains, We need you in your glory, we need you in your fire!

Ancients or babes in freedom, though we be, Ourselves by false gods lured to the chasm's brink, Divided and besieged, we need your light. 

Let it shine clear again! Let it shine clear! 

Without you, as you have been, we others have no hope." 

Thus the appealing nations. The idolators, Absorbed in worship, give no answer; only 

Two crows, perpetually circling the altars, Reply, cawing, the one, "Oh, yeah?" - the other, "So what?"

Let no one say, we have no god. We have found 

New gods for a new day, and they have richly endowed us. 

Why are we hungry? Is there then no food 

Served in the Pantheon? Is no restaurant there? 

Our gods give us so much. Why stint on food?

We drip know-how, we sparkle sophistication, we exude magnificence. 

Human desire and human will, human brain and human ingenuity, United at last in excelsis in us, elect of the elect! 

Is there no bread in Gilead? 3.

We look in awe at the splendor that we have made, The unprecedented, super-colossal success that we are! 

But somewhere a man like ourselves, ourselves, who has fed On the stone and steel we have fed on, drunk the oil we have drunk, And is famished, too, and knows not for what he is famished, 10

Speaks and a bomb drops, and our towered splendor 

Is poisonous rubble, and our silver highways, rivers 

Of frantic humanity fleeing, humanity starving, Humanity killing to snatch a mouthful; humanity

Trampling, and dying in ditches while the river flows on; 

You and I, trampling and trampled, and dying in ditches, While people, people, our wives and children and neighbors, Frantic in fear and hunger, trampling and trampled, flow on. 

What will they say in that hour, the gods we have fashioned, The idols we drilled from mountains, the djinns we distilled From the earth's golden blood, what will they have to say To people, trampling and trampled, to people groping Through pestilential darkness for loved ones lost? 

What will they have to say to a dead age, To a dead ten thousand years of maturing life? 

What will they have to say to a stricken remnant, Seeking to muster courage to begin anew? 4. 

Who am I to ask phantoms what they shall speak? 

Who am I to question our idols, to play the prophet, To judge a generation, a nation, a people?

It is not I who speak, it is not I who judge. 

I am not I in this judgment.

I am the pine the gale might use to chant the unity of all being. 

I am the oak through whose dark leaves the wind Might mourn the separation of man from man. 

In a fearsome hour I am a reed wherethrough 11

Eternity speaks to Time. I am Man,

In whom Eternity speaks, deep in the frightened heart. 

Frail instruments though they be, through man and man 

Eternity trumpets judgment. From the infinitudes 

Of space and time, I hear the Voice. 

From depths beyond depths in my own being, I hear it: "Because you have built without Me, your buildings shall be deserted; 

Your marble, your glowing glass, melted; your mahogany conferenc rooms,

Invaded by dust that is death. Your streets shall be streets of death, Your stately canyons echo the drums of death. 

Your meat and your drink shall be death, and your breathing, death, Unless... while there is time. . . while yet there is time... 

You remember, America, what you have been, You thrill, America, to what you can be, You weep, America, for what you are!" 

I heard Eternity speaking out of the heights of light: "Doom has shaken the sea-depths and summoned the tidal wave. 

Run, run to the high places! Run to the Mountain! 

God waits on the high places. God waits on the Mountain.

There is no life, except on the high places. 

Except on the Mountain, there is no Tomorrow."

Eternity spoke through silence more shattering than thunder. "You built a nation upon the challenge of all men equal and free. 12

You shook the world with your vision. Men bowed before it in awe. 

They bow no more. Their salutations no longer are paeans. 

They see the splendor, but seek in vain for the glory." "Voice in the heart," I cried, "is this my burden?" "You are your nation," said the Voice. "You, you, are America.” "I? Voice in the heart, not I!" "You! Who but you? "In the service of gods that starved your soul and fed your pride and your lust, You have failed mankind, betrayed your light and the lights your light once lighted. 

Men hunger for hope and you sing them hymns to your idols. 

Men ask for guides, and you send them the blind and the dumb. 'Show us the image of freedom!' they cry, and you send them the youthcorrupting Image of naked women, seduction and violence and crime!" "We are not all slayers of the Dream," I cried. "We are not all corrupters and poisoners of youth!" "You suffered the poisoners and failed to strike down the corrupters. 

Under the spell of your idols, your anger found no sword 

To smite the evil, and faded in pious lamentation. 

On the lips and not in the heart were the hymns you sang. 

On your timid tongues no flame burned, in your tepid souls no fire!

Now the millions your torpor betrayed rise up against you! 

On all the continents they rise, in waves they rise, 13

Waves that shall overwhelm you, waves that shall crush your bastions, Quench on your headlands the beacons that lighted of old

The perilous seas for men famished and men enslaved, Unless, while there is time, You run, run, to the high places, run to the Mountain, And bring to the starved and yearning, from the eternal Radiance,

A vision of life, so bright with hope, so charged with power, fulfilled in love, That hearts that are ashes shall burst into flame, and souls, dead and buried, shall rise and shout,

Seeing America-its idols forsaken—a prophet again, Leading the world to the freedom that rests in the will of God." 6. 

Up from the Vale of the Idols, the litany rose and fell: "There is no Mountain or Valley, there is no height or depth, There is no freedom save only as there is freedom from God!" "God," I cried, "forgive our folly!”

The Voice of the Valley: 

"Relieve us, idols, once and for all, Of God, and His Voice in the heart." "Awakening and creative love, forgive our madness!" 

The Voice in the heart: "Between the Valley and the Mountain, Is set the ultimate grapple: 

The will of man or the will of God." 14

"Oh, yeah?" cawed the crow, circling the heads of the idols. "So what?" his mate, from a dead tree, mocking, cawed.

Through fumes of incense, drunken voices: "Preserve us, preserve us, Liquor, from seeing what fools we are!" 7. 

I cried: "Forgive, great God, the One God, the God who is Love and Law, Forgive the treason against Thee, the treason to this our land, America!-built on faith in Thee, by men guided by Thee, And on words on the Mountain spoken to people who hungered for Thee And learned, there, how to live with each other and, there, to trust one another

To follow the ways of peace with each other and to live at peace with mankind.

Forgive the pride of the mind, forgive the sloth of the soul That sought in the brightness of things tangible and temporal, A substitute for the harder-won, eternal substance." 

The stillness of green pastures after rain 

Dropped on the noisy earth, the eternal stillness

In which the planets make their rounds, the stillness 'Through which God speaks to men; and when the Voice 

Spoke, it was as though silence itself were speaking: "Hope of mankind, long since, and still its hope,

America, of the great heart, forgiver and giver among nations, While there is time, bring men, through lives made new, The vision of mankind made new. Then men 15

Shall fly to you from earth's remotest valleys 

To drink the springs you drink of, light their torches 

From yours, and with you build a world

That men can live in together, in peace, and grow in, together, To new dimensions of love and freedom and vision. 

Voice of the nations: "Divided and besieged, we need your light, America! 

Let it shine clear again! Let it shine clear! 

Without you, as you have been, we others have no hope." 

The Voice in the heart: "America, the choice is set: 

The Valley or the Mountain? 

Choose, America, Americans! 

Between man's chaos and God's order, choose!" 

Like a bugle, up from the Valley, a voice, and another: "I am sick of the stench of the idols!" "I am sick of indulgence, Sick of indiscipline!" "I am bored, bored, bored with negation. 

Are there no affirmations?" 

Other voices: "There is no goal or purpose, There is no order or meaning.

Zero, by zero multiplied, is the Moral Law. 

Zero, by zero divided, is all men know of God!" 16

Yet other voices: "I am hungry for purpose!" "I am hungry for challenge!" "Give me work that shall have meaning!" "Give me something to live for, Bigger than myself!" "Give me the answer to division!" "Give me the answer 

To fear and hate and greed and lust!" 

I cried: "Help me to achieve the greatness, beyond plaudits, That waits beyond ambition's fevered reach!" 

Voices, voices, and the Valley with voices seething: "The Mountain, the Mountain! Show us the way to the Mountain!" 8. "There is a bridge to cross," said the Voice. "A bridge?" "It is narrow. You have to go, one by one, And there is a toll to pay.' "A toll? In what coin paid?” "The will to see, the heart to choose." "To see?" "Yourself, in the Eye of God." "To choose?" "God's way against man's way." "The toll," I said, shrinking, "the toll is high." 17

"Only for cowards," said the Voice. "The brave pay freely. 

Life in a new dimension waits on the Mountain: 

Peace in the heart, and with your brother, peace; 

Yet ever trumpets sounding, ever the sword 

Ready for battle against the undying dragon. 

Laughter waits on the Mountain; ecstasy, Such as the Valley never dreamed of; adventure,

Such as the Valley cannot know. Miracles 

Wait on the Mountain, miracles of weakness, made strength,

Of birth and growing, and dying and resurrection. 

The miracle of America, clear-eyed, and boldly choosing 

God's road to a world remade, waits on the Mountain. "Words, become life, and life, become revolution, Wait on the Mountain; the fall-out of God's compassion 

Waits, irradiating flesh, transfiguring 

Instinct, motive, desire; exploding

In men and nations successive, unending reactions 

Of life-restoring, inexhaustible radiance— 

This, this, all this, waits, waits on the Mountain!" 

I gasped, "I will pay the toll." And as I spoke

It seemed as though all earth and heaven 

Were one vast Eye, as deep as love, as dark as judgment, And, as compassion, bright. 

And people, in an endless file

From worship at black shrines in stifling valleys, Approached, and looked, and saw themselves as they were. 

And I, who had mocked the idol-worshippers,

Saw that I too had bowed to idols, offering up To that gaunt god, Ambition, those whom I most loved. 18

And suddenly, it seemed, I heard America speaking: "While there is time, I will arise and go, I will go to the Mountain.

I have spent my substance upon the altars of idols; 

In the Valley of Folly and Madness I have wasted my heritage. 

Now on husks, on husks, I feed, and am famished for bread. 

My Father has meat for His servants, and will not let me hunger. 

Out of the depths of what I have seen that I am, I will arise, and go, I will go to my Father,

While there is time, while yet, while yet there is time." 19
