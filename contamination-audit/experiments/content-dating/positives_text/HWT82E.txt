Introduction 

E believe that in the Church lies the hope of the WE believe that in the world. But we must understand what the Church is. 

Many think of the Church as a denomination or even as buildings. The Church is a living organism. It is made up of men, women, and children who fight for God's new world. This new world is to be a world where God's will is done. Jesus gave us the pattern for it, and then He began remaking the people who in turn would remake the world. "These that have turned the world upside down are come hither also," is one way their enemies described the Church in the New Testament.

Christianity is a revolution. Christians are revolutionaries. 

Their business is to turn things upside down until they are right side up as God wants them. This is the Church's job. 

When God is in control of this world, then the Church's job is done. People with this conception smell the smoke of moral battle. They know there is a war going on that is not confined to foreign battlefields. The line runs through every one of us, through homes, industry, society, and government.

Because evil is organized, it is powerful. Good people are often too individualistic. The time has come when the very life of America depends upon the unity of the Christian

forces. We must battle the subversive forces of greed, lust, and hate with a united mind. We may yet usher in the greatest revolution of all time, whereby the Cross of Christ will transform the world.

CHAPTER I Building Sound Homes

Hear, O Israel: The Lord our God is one 

Lord: and thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might. And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be in thine heart: and thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up. And thou shalt bind them for a sign upon thine hand, and they shall be as frontlets between thine eyes. And thou shalt write them upon the posts of thy house, and on thy gates. 

-DEUTERONOMY 6:4-9

I 

"What have they seen in thine house?"—II Kings 20:15. HIS morning at three o'clock I was called out of my 

THIS 

bed to the telephone by a mother who was trying to locate her daughter. She asked me if I knew any of the young people who went out with her on the party. Unfortunately I did not. So I tried to go back to sleep. But I lay awake for hours. I spent the early part of this morning thinking about that family.

Home life once made America great. The Puritans, the 

Cavaliers, and the pioneers built stout homes of logs they hewed out of the forest and bricks sent over from England. 

But there was another stoutness in their homes. They were strong in loyalty and discipline and character. Once homes like those were the glory of America. Is it true of us today?

We might inspect our homes. You know the kind of inspection our soldiers go through every day, or once a week. 

But I am thinking of another kind of inspection—the kind that a true friend would make if he were to visit in our home.

We love to have guests. We give them the run of the house. They have a passkey. They come and go at will. They

have free access to the icebox and the kitchen, and if they stay long enough they see us pretty well as we are. Now usually when guests like that have left, they sit down and write a courteous "thank you" note. It is often a very conventional message. I have been wondering what such friends would say if, after having the freedom of our home, they wrote us exactly what they thought. They might surprise us. They might even disappoint us, or anger us, if they were honest enough to tell us exactly what they had seen in our house. I wonder if they would see in our home the qualities the nation most needs today.

We have a situation something like that in a ruler's home in the Old Testament. Hezekiah was one of the last of the fairly decent kings that Israel had. He had enough religion for himself in times of emergency. Once when the Assyrian army thundered at the gates of Jerusalem, he humbled himself enough to go to the temple and pray. God heard his prayer and delivered the city. Then he was critically ill-was going to die. Again Hezekiah turned humbly and prayed.

God saved his life. When he got well from that illness an embassy from Babylon came to congratulate him on his recovery. He feasted them sumptuously, entertained them royally.

He showed them his treasures, his wealth, and his military power. When they left, the keen-eyed prophet Isaiah came to his home and said, "What have they seen in thine house?”

Hezekiah told him-wealth, power, entertainment, pleasure.

Suddenly the prophet, fastening his eyes upon the king, pronounced upon him and his nation the most tragic doom -"All that you have, that your fathers have stored up, shall be taken to Babylon, and your sons mutilated and made domestic servants in the palace of the king of Babylon." 

Any of you who read this story carefully will feel that doom was pronounced upon Hezekiah, not so much because of what those guests had seen, but because of what they had not seen. What they should have seen was the traditional 

Hebrew home - a godly family disciplined children genuine love—and unity. The unity of the king's family might have inspired unity in the nation. Here is a lesson that writes itself in red letters across the sky, showing us the tragic influence of one leading family upon the life of a whole nation. If these guests had seen children living as 

God wanted them to live, if they had seen His laws written on their doorposts or as frontlets between their eyes and written in their hearts as well, if they had heard the glorious story of the God who had delivered His people out of Egypt and brought them out of the land of bondage, if they had seen a God-inspired home that revealed an invincible quality that ran through the nation, how different might have been the story!

Hezekiah was too busy being king to be a real father, too busy governing to be a friend to his boy. For it was his son, Manasseh, neglected, immature, and undisciplined, who led Israel into orgies of idolatry and immorality that

had never before been known in the history of the Jewish people!

Hezekiah was like the banker who is more concerned with the deposits of his customers than with the spiritual deposits in the lives of his sons and daughters. He was like the doctor who seems to be able to cure the ills of all his patients, but never gets to the disease in the characters of his own children. He was like the lawyer who can plead the cause of any stranger, but who never pleads the cause of God before the bar of his own family. He was like the statesman, in the prohibition era, who was working for social reform in Congress while the daily newspapers of the country were carrying on the front page the story of his own son's drunkenness. Conventionally religious parents are not enough. Fathers and mothers can be "fairly decent" 

Christians and yet let their homes go to pieces and their nation collapse.

Conventional religion is never enough for anybody. During the past year I was called to officiate at a funeral. In that home, in the hour of crisis, they all believed in immortality. The bereaved mother, the children, and the grandchildren had some kind of faith in life after death. But they lacked a fighting faith for their own lives and for their nation now. If all homes in America were like that home there would be little or no hope for the future. That kind of religion is better than none. But it is amazing and distressing to see how little positive effect it has on the com-

munity. Think of the fathers and mothers you know who have enough religion for themselves in an energency-or think they have—and yet who never make their home a force in the life of the nation. 

Some great impending tragedy usually wakes the family up. Maybe the trouble is the son. Maybe it's the daughter. 

Maybe the home is not 

Maybe it's the father and mother. altogether broken, but seriously bent. Some years ago, an elder talked to me about the need for a new relationship with his son. I faced him with the fact that his son was merely walking in his footsteps. He resolved to see him and be honest with him. They met in a hotel room not far from his son's college. Returning from the trip, he told me how they had talked matters over quietly and frankly. The father confessed his own failure and knelt and prayed for forgiveness. When he finished, his son said: "Wait, Dad,

I want to pray about myself." They had never felt so close together. That is the way to start a new home. It is a glorious thing, but even that isn't glorious enough. The new home must become a force. A reconstructed family-a few people lifted out of the mess they are in-is not enough. 

America has got to have fighting families-homes that pull the nation together. That kind of home was once the moral grandeur of America.

It is time to break the conspiracy of silence. Things are not well with America. Divorce is rapidly becoming a part of the American way. One marriage in every 5.6 ends in

court. Experts have reported that there are 500,000 abortions annually in our country. Syphilis and gonorrhea have increased in some sections as much as 34 per cent. Juvenile delinquency has risen to alarming proportions. The age of disillusionment has dropped in multitudes of cases to thirteen or fourteen. Walk along our streets and see for yourselves.

The war abroad is serious, but the war at home is more serious. Victories on the battlefront, bought at the cost of good American blood, cannot offset the moral loss in America over one week end.

It is time for everybody to change. It is time for every family to change-not merely to get out of some domestic mess, but to begin to fight for the life of our nation. Here is the place for pioneering in America. We can make our homes like outposts, like forts. We can recapture those simple homespun virtues that will make us invincible within and unconquerable without. America has a destiny. She must give a lead in remaking the world under God's direction. That's what our homes can fight for.

Due to the exciting news of battles, very often the stories of true heroism at home are neglected. Up in Washington today is an industrial statesman. Five years ago as a technical expert he visited a small South Carolina town. He had been there often. But on this trip he saw something different. He felt a new spirit. In the old days the policy had been one of "crack down." Now it was co-operation. It all began with the manager's wife. She used to leave her home deserted,

and had insisted on living in a fashionable city thirty miles away. There she and her children would have better advantages and more cultured associates, more pleasure and excitement. It was her husband's business to run the factory. 

Then a new patriotism was born in her. She started to listen to God. Back she went to the mill town. The first thing she and her husband did was to take down the picket fence that kept her home aloof. She began to meet the wives of the workers and have them in her home. She saw an answer to the chief problems of the town-division, drunkenness, absenteeism, and bad temper. Other families joined in the fight for a new spirit. 

Into this situation came the technical expert. He had always believed that religious faith combined with technical knowledge would bring a new day in industry. Now he saw it in action.

When he went back to his home in New England he started to put to work what he had seen. The teamwork he has built between labor and management has affected the lives of more than 80,000 workers, and he now occupies one of the most strategic positions in his industry in the country. 

Behind his new philosophy and his new spirit lies a sound home in a quiet Southern town. Is your home one of those production centers? That wife is now far happier than when she spent her time at cocktail parties, smoking endless cigarettes and playing poker. From remade homes such as

hers will come the sons and daughters who will make the new world God wants for the next generation. 

Out in California is another pioneer home. When I knew this family the father had already been in the fighting area of China without seeing his wife and children for two years. 

The family was finding it hard making ends meet. So the nineteen-year-old son left college and went to work in a war plant, one of the largest airplane factories in the world. 

You would say that for that lonely mother to manage her home with three teen-age children and limited resources, would be enough. But one day her nineteen-year-old son came home and said he believed that if a new spirit could be born in the factory they could turn out twice as many planes!

The family decided to act. First they all agreed to get up at six so they could help with breakfast and have time to meet unhurriedly together to listen to God, and make all their plans with the wisdom He would give. The son went to his job with that kind of unity and teamwork behind him. 

Then another worker at the factory caught the idea. Soon two homes began to plan together. 

This new spirit of Moral Re-Armament became active in the plant and in one way or another found its way to thousands of workers. Two shop stewards describe the results: "Tested over the last two years," they said, “it (1) increases war production, (2) inspires teamwork, (3) creates enthusiasm on the job, (4) cuts absenteeism, (5) saves war mate-

rials, (6) develops unselfish leadership, (7) insures right approach to wage adjustment, (8) answers labor turnover problems."* 

The president of the union has stated: "There is no question whatever in my mind that there are planes on the fighting fronts today that would not be there but for the enthusiasm and unselfish leadership these workers have brought into the ranks of labor."

When we see homes like these taking such responsibility and achieving such results, we can take hope. “What have they seen in thine house?" Like spring sending through the earth the throb of quickening life to be felt by every plant and shrub, so must we feel the quickening power of 

Jesus Christ. Has your home felt it and responded? Do your friends see there the things the nation must have or perish? Tanks and infantry may form the front line in the battle abroad. The family is the front line in the battle at home. 

I close with a poem from "Pull Together Canada," the

Victory Revue which is doing so much to bring home to our neighbors to the north what they are fighting for. In this 

Revue a young mother from London recites in the inimitable cockney dialect some great home truths which she had learned during the blitz.

CHAPTER II The New Industrial Revolution

Beware that thou forget not the Lord thy

God, in not keeping his commandments, and his judgments, and his statutes, which I command thee this day: lest when thou hast eaten and art full, and hast built goodly houses, and dwelt therein; and when thy herds and thy flocks multiply, and thy silver and thy gold is multiplied, and all that thou hast is multiplied; then thine heart be lifted up, and thou forget the Lord thy 

God... and thou say in thine heart, My power and the might of mine hand hath gotten me this wealth. 

But thou shalt remember the Lord thy God: for it is he that giveth thee power to get wealth, that he may establish his covenant which he sware unto thy fathers, as it is this day. And it shall be, if thou do at all forget the Lord thy 

God, and walk after other gods, and serve them, and worship them, I testify against you this day that ye shall surely perish. As the nations which the Lord destroyeth before your face, so shall ye perish; because ye would not be obedient unto the voice of the Lord your God. 

-DEUTERONOMY 8:11-20

II 

"Beware that thou forget not the Lord thy God." 

-Deuteronomy 8:11. 

N the last chapter we told two stories of homes that were IN fighting for a new spirit in industry. They were humble, simple homes, like ours. There is a song, grown popular in 

England, entitled "The Ordinary Man." The chorus goes like this: "For the new world we're wanting we'll certainly find In the heart of the ordinary man, When we leave all our greed and our grousing behind 

And listen to God for His plan."

The future depends upon ordinary people like us, and ordinary homes like ours. So let us take up where we left offwith the thought that homes that pull together can pull the nation together.

The major problem in many homes is division. Factions pull the home apart and weaken it. Conflict robs the family of efficiency. Lift this to the level of the nation, and we see the conflict complex that pulls management and labor apart. 

There is a deep-seated idea abroad that management and labor have got to fight out their differences to the finish.

Many people doubt that we can ever have unity. Some believe that management will have to crush labor. Others believe labor will have to destroy management and that they can never live together in peace. We have seen the answer to broken homes-families united, with a new purpose and with a new spirit. We must also find the answer to a broken nation. For America is like a broken home so long as management and labor live in industrial conflict.

The romance in two stories of the last chapter lay in the thrilling manner in which two ordinary homes affected a textile mill down in the deep South and one of the great airplane factories in the West. There isn't a home represented here this morning that cannot play a part in uniting industry. Wherever you work, there is your opportunity. Every family has some connection with management or labor, or both. There is the chance for you to do something. Every secretary, every labor leader, every executive, is a key person. The humblest or the highest, the richest or the poorest, can see two things, and act on them. One of them is the fact that human nature can be changed. The second is that God has a plan for industry.

We have got to face the facts. The threatened strikes in industry illustrate how superficial is the unity forced upon labor and management by the war. The government may hold them together while the fighting is going on abroad. 

But when that is over, unless a new spirit of friendship and personal caring is born here at home, we are in for more

serious times. I grew indignant when I heard the statement of the head of an industrial plant. He ground his teeth in rage and said, "When this war is over we'll put labor back in its place with a vengeance." But that fiery remark was somewhat neutralized by another, and this time it was a leader of labor. "We have got to work with the bosses till we have won the war. But then-there's only one way to deal with them—use lead on them like they did in some other countries."

Of course these are extreme statements. The managers of industry whom I know personally wouldn't say a thing like that; nor would the labor leaders I know. But the feeling of conflict and struggle, in varying degrees, is certainly present in America. The war news must not occupy all our thoughts. This problem on the home front needs intelligent and immediate solution.

Last summer in a great industrial city I was invited to a luncheon for business men. I sat across the table from a former mayor. His face was lined with the cares of many a political battle. During the meal I asked him what he thought of the war. I can never forget his sober reply. He expected internal trouble in Germany within two years. 

Hitler would be ousted. Within another year, he thought, the Axis might surrender and our boys start coming home. 

Then, he said, here in America we will have the greatest revolution of all. It would last ten years. He did not think it would be violent and bloody in every section, but he did

believe it would be decisive. His city was a hot spot of seething unrest. Then he added, "I am sixty-two years old, and

I never expect to see any more days of tranquility and peace.' دو I came back to Richmond deeply stirred. We imagine we live so far from that kind of thing here in some of our quiet communities. But the last nine months have led me to believe that what that mayor said may actually happen. 

One characteristic of these last eventful years is that the unexpected thing did happen. Again and again we were taken unaware. Things we never dreamed could come to pass took place and we accepted them as though ours were simply an age of unpredictable happenings. Such blindness is a sign of moral decay. Someone has reminded us that when reason abdicates, events take charge. And events have gotten out of hand. The moral order of our age is collapsing, and anything can happen. Pearl Harbor was a symptom. 

Russia's treaty with Germany; the fall of France; Germany's attack on Russia; Bataan; Singapore-all of these with their unsuspected suddenness are symptomatic of our times. We live on the very brink of the abyss. And nothing could bring this series of surprises to a climax more logically than for us to win the war abroad and then be astonished to have a revolution at home.

Senator Byrd predicts a war debt of $300,000,000,- 

000.00! Not one of us can be blind to what that means.

And to face that debt after the war with divided, warring

factions in industry is plain national suicide. Families in debt need teamwork-every person pulling together, working, saving, economizing, loving each other and fighting for common victory! You know what happens to a family in debt, with no co-operation and teamwork! Well, it's like that with a nation, only worse!

Here in Virginia we have a chance to lead the nation. 

Feeling has not yet run so high here as elsewhere. Many leaders of industry in Virginia are sound, Christian menand so are many leaders of labor. This week, two great labor conventions will be held in our State. Have they an answer to this major problem? They could find the simple philosophy that will give the answer. Are our business men ready with a solution?

My conviction is that Christ has an answer. It is a simple answer. Of course there are many complex details that require experts. But the simple principles that Christ offers will make the details easier. In fact, there is no other solution.

When Jesus first gave the answer it was a matter of life or death. The destiny of the nation depended on its response. 

He put it up to them. He did not shout his demands. He crashed no gates. But he made the issues plain. It meant dealing with greed. It meant getting together with your adversary. It meant acknowledging your own wrongs when you saw them. It meant that men could become brothers, and God could be their Father. Then, there would be plenty

for everybody's need-though not enough for anybody's greed. God would be in control of everything. It would mean a new world. They refused to change and within forty years the fatal hour arrived. Jerusalem fell.

Today it is the same. We are not left quietly to choose, and make up our minds, as though it merely involved us individually. We are like people drifting toward the fatal falls on the Niagara River. We are like people standing on the railroad track while a Silver Meteor is speeding down upon us. We have got to act quickly. The choice is between 

God-control and materialism, between Christ and chaos. 

We have got to change now or perish!

Out in California the other day an address was delivered in the Commonwealth Club of San Francisco. This sentence was widely published: "Now is the time for America to produce within her borders the pattern of the new world order which every nation wants."

Last month I was in Logan, West Virginia, the very heart of the coal fields. Opposite my hotel window across the river lies one of the largest power plants in the world. It consumes a solid car of coal every hour in the day and night. 

Its electric current goes out all over the Southern States, as far south as Florida. All the week I was there, I kept wondering if some community could unite its factions, create a new spirit, resolve its differences, and thus become a spiritual power plant to light the nation.

It's so easy to hope some other community will do it. Why

should this kind of teamwork and unity not be achieved in 

Virginia? America got its start in Virginia. We can lead again if here at home labor and management get together on a new level of co-operation and friendship. 

Materialism divides; God's plan for industry unites. 

Christianity declares that industry was made for man and not man for industry. In the days of the old family business at its best, there was a warm personal relationship between employer and employee. There was a family feeling and real intimacy. Industrial developments of recent decades have changed this into a largely impersonal relationship. The two groups, management and labor, often negotiate at a distance. The machine age tends more and more to crowd out the human values. These must be restored. The machine age will destroy us unless we have friendship, understanding, caring, and a united purpose. We must maintain cooperation, of course, but it must be much more than that. 

Why not real friendship between leaders of labor and management? That's where the wives have a part to play. Their families too. Their homes can become the planning centers for a new spirit in industry. 

The old evangelism tended often to produce good men, Christian men, in both groups. It made them fair in their business dealings. It made managers decent to their employees. It made workers honest and efficient. But it did not carry the guidance of God into every detail of their business or their job. We need much more than that today.

We need a Christian revolution that will entirely transform industry; that will create lasting teamwork between labor and management; that will make the will of God the deciding factor in every issue.

The theory of class war is the next "ism" that threatens to engulf the world after the present war is over. The only answer is a demonstration of God's government in industry-industrialists and union leaders who seek to carry out God's will in their work. Men who take the philosophy of 

God-directed teamwork throughout industry have the answer to the philosophy of class war. You cannot win a victory over a bad idea with bullets and bombs. An idea can be defeated only by a better idea. When Christianity is really practiced the world will see the best idea. Unless enough leaders both in management and labor are willing to practice it as Jesus Christ intended us to practice it, the idea of Karl Marx will rise above the fire and smoke of a warruined world and claim the future.

We should see how fertile war makes the human soil for subversive ideas. Subversive ideas ride in on waves of secular thinking and immoral living. They are secretly at work undermining confidence and faith. They attack wherever there is a soft spot or an unguarded life. They are insidious and persistent. In a very real sense the war has given such forces the best opportunity they have had in twenty-five years. The seeds of class hatred and revolution take root most quickly in decaying moral character. Bitterness, resent-

ment, misunderstanding, are like broken walls in a fort. 

They make holes through which the enemy comes pouring in. Snobbishness and pride engender jealousy and hate, and all the devils laugh with glee. We are fools. We are like blind guides leading the blind. Once Jesus said that some men were intelligent enough to discern the signs of the weather, but they would misinterpret the signs of the times. 

We are doing that in broad areas of American life today. 

We may still have time to carry out God's will, and press the philosophy of God-controlled industry throughout the nation. But the time is short. Some powerful changes will have to take place quickly. We must see a few of the simple fundamentals at once.

First, we must see that industry exists in order to supply the material needs of the nation. It was never designed to let men "feather their nests." It is a divine plan for using

God-given resources and dedicated brains to see that all the children of God have what they need for full, free, happy life. This plan of God is the forgotten factor in our modern industrial world. This is the great idea that can get us all together. One for all and all for one. There is enough for everybody's need. But we have made it a grab bag, on the principle of every man for himself.

Then, we must see that management and labor are not opponents in a struggle for power, but, under God, are partners in the battle for production. God never intended that they should struggle against each other for power and

prestige. United they can pull the nation together and show the world how democracy can work. Personal relationships, woven with trust and friendship, can give an answer to every "ism." 

And here, too, is an answer to our other ills. Union leaders and employers who are guided by God will have the secret weapon that can conquer absenteeism, slowdowns, and wage disputes. For the true battle line is not between management and labor, but between the selfish materialism in management and labor on the one hand and Christian patriotism in management and labor on the other hand. The fight today is between selfishness and patriotism, between greed and God. The battle line runs through the whole industrial world.

One of the vicious weapons of the enemy is confusion. He distorts the issue. He tries to make people class conscious. 

He wants to separate men of good will by drawing a line between the boss and the worker. He has had some success, too. He keeps men apart by saying, "He is a manager, you are a worker." He divides men who otherwise would have everything in common and who would really love each other.

This is one place where our families have a part to play. 

The wives of business men, industrialists, and labor leaders can inspire their husbands. They can understand the job these men have to do. They can send them into the busy, noisy world on top spiritually, mentally, and physically. And

husbands can give the same careful thought to their homes that they give to their business. Homes can become the production centers for teamwork in industry. 

The secret is very simple. Human nature can be changed and God will give us a plan if we are humble enough to listen.

One person changed can bring a new spirit to a home. 

Families changed can affect industry. We can begin today. 

Cyrus McCormick got an idea and worked it out, and revolutionized agriculture. Patrick Henry got an idea and worked it out, and started the American Revolution. 

Thomas Jefferson got an idea and worked it out, and revolutionized government. God has the greatest idea-for industry, for government, for society, for a new world. Make this our idea and we shall usher in the greatest revolution of all time, whereby the Cross of Christ will transform the world.

CHAPTER III The Art of Listening

Now it came to pass, as they went, that he entered into a certain village: and a certain woman named Martha received him into her house. And she had a sister called Mary, which also sat at Jesus' feet, and heard his word. But 

Martha was cumbered about much serving, and came to him, and said, Lord, dost thou not care that my sister hath left me to serve alone? bid her therefore that she help me. And Jesus answered and said unto her, Martha, Martha, thou art careful and troubled about many things: but one thing is needful: and Mary hath chosen that good part, which shall not be taken away from her. 

-LUKE 10:38-42

III 

"One thing is needful."-Luke 10:42. ECAUSE this story of Mary and Martha is about two women, it would be easy for many of us men to miss the point. It deals with a major problem in all our lives. Let no man sit back and feel that it is for women only. Then, somebody will be tempted to raise the age-old question as to whether Martha was better than Mary, or Mary better than Martha. And we would miss the point altogether. For Jesus cared for both these women. He loved to visit in their home. It took them both, with their brother 

Lazarus, to make up this family. And as Edgar Guest suggests, "It takes a heap o' livin' in a house t' make it home.” 

There must have been a good deal of the right kind of living there to make it the kind of place to which Jesus would love to go. He came one day when Lazarus was absent, and 

Mary and Martha received Him.

The story of this visit, and what happened in this home, will remind us that every time Jesus comes near anybody something happens. For one thing He brings out the good and the bad in sharp focus. Sometimes an emergency does that. Sudden success or sudden tragedy may strip away

the pretense that so often camouflages our real selves. 

But nothing throws such light upon us as nearness to Jesus. 

No one can come close to Him without revealing himself, his characteristics, his flaws, his virtues. It is in His presence that we most clearly see ourselves as we are. 

There are times when we don't like for Him to come too close to us. There may be other people who are much more keenly aware of what needs to happen to them than some of you who need to change much more. You know how to keep Jesus conveniently at a distance; how to keep Him just far enough away so as not to be seriously disturbed; how to hold Him off at the point of indulgence and compromise. Many people vaguely, if not precisely, know where 

Jesus would disturb them most, if He came close enough and they sat quietly and listened. Let us be honest about that.

This visit to the home in Bethany sets Martha and Mary in bold relief. They represent two distinct types. Martha is the "practical" type. She is capable, efficient, energetic.

She loves to work. She would head the Red Cross today, or the Woman's Club. She could not only manage a home, but she could manage any community enterprise. She is the modern secretary, schoolteacher, housewife, club president, business woman. Our world is coming more and more to depend upon such people. We are training them to be welders, chauffeurs, Waves, Wacs, and executives. Most labor leaders, most business executives, most professional men,

are of this type. That's why this study of Martha is so absolutely pertinent. Most people need this lesson more than any other. We must discover what it was in Martha that Jesus rebuked so severely.

Mary is the "thoughtful" type. She loves quiet. She thinks deeply. Nothing pleases her more than a good stimulating book. She loves conversation that has point and pith. Yet solitude is never irksome. She knows the value of being still, and alone. In the church Martha expresses her loyalty mostly in work, while Mary expresses hers mostly in worship. It is natural for Mary to listen. The fact that when one listens God speaks is to her the most important of all facts. Mary put listening to Jesus first. Martha put cooking first.

That day when Jesus came, Mary went to the parlor and Martha to the kitchen. The contrast is vivid. In the quiet room Mary sat listening to every word Jesus said. In the hot kitchen Martha busied herself with the dinner. One can well imagine the enthusiasm with which she began her work. 

Like many a housewife who loves her home she started working with a song in her heart and perhaps on her lips. 

Business women start out in the morning with eagerness.

In this modern madhouse of our business world dispositions change between nine in the morning and noon. The exacting duties and irritating interruptions tax the strongest personalities. And "explosions" occur even when people love the thing they're doing. Certainly Martha loved doing things

for Jesus. Fixing a meal with Him in the home was a pleasure. She began joyfully.

It is not easy for us moderns to discover what it was that Jesus so sternly corrected in Martha. It is not easy to see it in ourselves. What sort of wrong thing did Jesus find in a woman who was working hard, trying her best to please 

Him, and doing it because she loved Him?

Something went wrong. We are not told exactly what it was, but many of us do know how easy it is for things to go wrong in the kitchen. Perhaps she burned the soup! We all know how easy it is for things to go wrong in the store, in the factory, in the office, as well as in the home. There may be several reasons. But in this case Jesus Himself throws light on the main reason. When busy men and women lose their temper, their self-control, their poise and dignity, and often their friends or their jobs in the bargain, the main reason is the one Jesus points out in this story. 

You can see Martha standing in the parlor door, exasperated. The perspiration is running down her face. She is wiping her hands with her apron. Seeing her younger sister sitting quietly, calm and collected, was more than she could absorb. She burst out: "Lord, dost thou not care that my sister hath left me to serve alone? Bid her therefore that she help me!" Think of the last time you lost your temper.

Remember the last time you spoke hot words to your family when there were guests present. Recall the last wave of resentment that engulfed you. See yourself as you look at Mar-

tha. You are often like that. Other members of the family or other workers in the office know the usual symptoms when you are about to "explode." Perhaps you just sulk when you are exasperated, or feel self-pity and suffer from hurt pride. Think of the terrible burden of this sort of thing that rests upon the life of our nation, our churches, and our homes. Ordinarily it is good people, hard-working people, who at one time or another get into this fit of temper. 

Jesus clarified the situation: "Martha, Martha, thou art anxious and troubled about many things; but one thing is needful: for Mary hath chosen that good part, which shall not be taken away from her." The simple truth is that Martha had failed to listen. At times Mary might have failed to work. People usually think that of her. Whether that be true or not, what Mary was doing then was the most important thing anybody can ever do. No amount of busy activity can supply it.

Bad temper, resentment, and criticism almost always come from a bad balance in people's lives. Too much activity on one side with too little clear thinking on the other. 

Too much work with too little worship. Granted that the opposite might also be true, still it would not be the problem of many Americans! The constant emphasis of this age is upon action. The times drive us. We speak of "rush hours." 

But all the tendency of our time is "rush." Work is rushed. 

Pleasure is rushed. Eating is rushed. Even going to church is rushed. No wonder the world is full of nervous wrecks.

It would have been a different story if Martha had spent half an hour in the parlor before she went to the kitchen. 

She might have gotten a whole new plan for dinner. She and Mary might have found a basis for family teamwork. 

Many things could have happened differently if Martha had listened before she began to work. Most of Jesus' meals as they are recorded in the New Testament were simple meals. Heady Martha, self-willed, proud, ambitious to please, thought she knew best.

There are times when we should sit relaxed and at ease, with heart and mind attentive, with ears open and our wills responsive. God can speak to us at any time. But we need the discipline of regular and unhurried times of quiet. The most needed thing in millions of anxious, over-burdened, and frustrated lives is this Voice. It is usually a “still small voice," and the noise and rush of our modern world can drown it out. This failure in Martha led her to three of the greatest of all sins. 

First of all, she doubted Jesus' care. Aside from the pain this must have caused Him, think of the unhappiness that must have surged through Martha. Was not the divine caring the very gist of His teaching? Not even sparrows fall without God's concern. The very hairs of Martha's head were numbered. Do you ever go to your office with a deserted feeling? Do you lack that sense of security that comes from knowing that God cares for you? 

Then, secondly, Martha criticized her sister. 66 my

sister hath left me to serve alone!" Criticism is a symptom. It reveals the state of your own inner life. When you criticize, your discerning friends all see through you.

That is why they are often silent and embarrassed when you say things about other people. This can happen between sisters. It often happens in the church or in the office just as it does in the home. It is so easy to think that our unhappiness or anxiety is caused by someone else. It is so easy to settle the problem superficially by wishing "Mary" might be different. We want a new spirit in the home. We say: "If only so-and-so were changed." But in every unhappy situation, the person to begin with is yourself. 

The third and perhaps the worst symptom of all was that Martha grew dictatorial. She even told Jesus what she thought He ought to do. "Bid her therefore that she help me!" It is hard to believe that Martha so far forgot herself. 

But when one doubts God's care and condemns other people then anything can happen. Such people would like to tell God how to run the world. All the dictators are not in 

Europe or in responsible positions at home. Think of the henpecked husbands and abused wives. Think of the children frightened at their bossy parents. Think of servants whose initiative has been destroyed. Think of bosses who stifle all creative spirit in their shop. Think of labor leaders who try to tell management how to run the plant-and managers who try to tell the labor leaders how to run their unions. Instead of dictating to each other, management and

labor could listen to God together. The dictatorship of the 

Holy Spirit is the answer to all other forms of dictatorship. 

Martha's trouble is the trouble with the world. Why will people not listen to God? Is there anything more logical, once you accept the fact of God, than that He can speak?

Think of the changes that will go on in your life, your home, your business, and your nation if you listen to His Voice.

The fact is, people don't want to change. It is so comfortable for people who are physically comfortable to just stay as they are. It is the uncomfortable who revolt. A revolt is brewing now. The choice may be between listening to the 

Voice of Jesus or the sound of machine guns in our streets. 

However, it is certainly to Martha's credit that she came to Jesus with her troubles. What He said surprised her greatly and we can certainly see what might have happened. She might have retired with wounded pride, hurt feelings. She could have gone away irritable and resentful. 

I am sure she did not do that.

Perhaps her sense of humor saved her. She saw herself objectively-hot and bothered, all nervous and excited, wringing her hands in her apron. Why can't we always see ourselves? Executives seeing themselves in some irritable mood would split their sides laughing at themselves. They ought to see what some quiet clerk sees. Thank God for eyes that, trying to look sober, reveal a merry twinkle when we run amuck. It doesn't seem funny until we get the objective view. I think Martha did. "Mary was right,”

she would exclaim. "One simple dish would have been enough. Then we could have listened together." She burst out laughing at herself with maybe a few tears, and exclaimed, "How stupid of me. What a failure I have made of it! I am so sorry." And running to Mary she would kiss her and say: "I was dead wrong, sister. You were right.

Forgive me." Then the wonderful transformation took place. And perhaps, after all, it might have done as much for Mary as for herself. 

The next day perhaps Lazarus came home, and Martha would see the point more clearly. She would meet him with, "Guess who was here yesterday!" And he guessed by the light in her eyes. "What did He say, Martha? How is the work progressing? What are the next steps for us? What is in His mind?" And Martha would have to say: "I'm sorry, brother, I was too busy. You will have to ask Mary." 

Do you think this is overdrawn? Does Jesus not come to your home? Tell us then what He has been saying to you lately. What is His plan for you and your home, for your school, your office, your union? Surely you get the mind of

Jesus on these points. Then can you tell us what He said to you yesterday?

Martha learned her lesson well. In the next two scenes we can see how great was the change in her. First, when 

Lazarus her brother died. They are in the midst of a crisis. 

This time Martha is taking charge of the funeral arrangements. She is placing the guests and managing everything.

And Mary is alone in her own room. But Martha is no longer “anxious and troubled." She is the picture of patience, calmness, grace, and tenderness. Slipping quietly to 

Mary's room, we hear her knock gently and whisper: "The

Master is come, and calleth for thee." This is a busier and much more exacting occasion than when a Friend dropped in for dinner unexpectedly. But now there was a new spirit in her heart.

The second scene is at the banquet which Simon, a citizen of Bethany, is giving Jesus. This time the meal is not for three people, but for thirty at least. All the disciples are there, and the leading men of the community. And Martha is serving the dinner. She is hostess for Simon, her neighbor. 

But she is not now anxious or troubled. She is not now critical and irritated. She is not now dictatorial. She moves about the room with quiet dignity, unruffled, undisturbed, and at peace. The change is almost unbelievable. She shows what Jesus can do for anyone who will listen and obey!

This same change can come to any family today. The idea is very simple. The ten-year-old son of a labor leader put it in a nutshell when someone asked him, "Who's boss in your home, your dad or your mother?" He replied, "God's Boss in our home!" I know that family and I know their simple program works. In fact, we adopted it in our own home a few years ago.

Imagine the picture. A father, headstrong, willful, and impulsive, who was unwilling to take the family wholly

into his confidence; a mother, cautious, often fearful, and thinking primarily about her daughters' prospects. An eldest daughter, "just like her dad," which led to many a tussle between them. Two younger sisters, as different as can be, who found it hard to pull together, and did not often try. 

A son who liked to go off camping with his dad just to “get away from all these women!" An aunt who had been in the home since the first child was a baby, and had to be handled with kid gloves. She often felt “left out" of things to which only father and mother had been invited. It wasn't altogether an ideal home! "Just like my family!" I hear some of you whisper. Well, maybe we are a typical American family. Sometimes dad's outspoken will prevailed. Sometimes mother got her own quiet way. Often the children did what they pleased. What we lacked was a co-ordinating authority that all would gladly accept together.

Then father got the idea of making God the Boss in his own heart and in his own home as well. He started to listen to 

God the first thing in the morning. It was very different from merely reading the Bible and saying a prayer. Waiting quietly for thoughts from God was a revolutionary experience. Soon other members of the family began to do the same. It meant many changes all around. In that early morning hour thoughts would come to different ones as to where they themselves needed to change. The shopworn idea of always expecting the other fellow to change first

was debunked. We all decided to start with ourselves, and then to welcome suggestions from other members of the family instead of resenting them. We found the healing power of honest apology. Obvious and hidden divisions in the family began to disappear one by one. We found what an exhilarating experience it is to care for others and to live to make them great. A new feeling of personal responsibility came to each one, and whether it was dad's work, mother's housekeeping, or the children's homework, it was one for all and all for one. We found that God can gloriously give a common mind alike on major issues and minor points, and we had found a common objective. It was to remake the world, starting with ourselves.

Our home became a weapon in the battle for a new America. Families were invited in. Serving meals to twice the ordinary number became real fun and helped to develop the family teamwork. Once we thought we needed a larger house. We discovered we needed larger hearts. We are fighting for constant change in each other and we are trying to enlist every other family in the battle. We see what it would mean to the nation if a million homes gave their first and best hour each day to listening to God for 

His plan. If ten million homes-twenty million!

It is a sufficiently venturesome program to catch the children's imagination. We all get up much earlier than we used to. Incidentally we find that the secret of getting up on time in the morning is to get to bed on time the night

before! We battle to have this time of listening at all costs every day. For as one member of the family remarked, “If you're too busy to listen to God, you are far more busy than 

God ever meant you to be." We write down the thoughts that come as we listen so that we can remember them and be sure to carry them out. You know the old Chinese proverb, "The strongest memory is weaker than the palest ink." 

Sometimes a thought comes and we wonder, "Is it guidance, or just a whim of my own?" There are two useful tests. One is the moral test. Does it square with the standards of Jesus —absolute honesty, purity, unselfishness, and love? The other is to talk it over with those whose one concern is to find and to do God's will, and listen together. 

It is intriguing to imagine what that new spirit could mean to industry. For millions there would come strength to do more than they ever did before, and to do it with greater ease. Strain and weariness would vanish overnight. 

First Martha does not listen. And as a result she loses her temper preparing a meal for three. Or Martha does listen and prepares a banquet for thirty and does it with ease and grace. We talk of increased production. Here is the answer. 

God knows more about running an industry or a labor union than the best-trained executive or labor leader on earth. And the guidance of God is available to anyone who is humble enough to listen.

Think what schools would be like with such a spirit. 

Teachers would look upon their work as a part of God's

plan-training a generation with which He could build a new and better world. Would truancy stop? Would juvenile delinquency decline? Would pupils learn more in class and do their homework in shorter time? Think of a class in high school listening to God, with every boy and girl changing for the better daily and fighting for the best in each other. Then education would achieve its great aim. "And all thy children shall be taught of the Lord; and great shall be the peace of thy children." Just think what making

God real to a whole school would mean. 

Few people are more harassed today than government officials. In spite of all their efforts they are assailed by criticism and opposition. One of the symptoms of the times is animosity. People are generally unhappy and their normal life is frustrated. Government leaders have to bear the brunt of this. Duties are multiplied. Demands grow more insistent. Many say: "What's the use?" An hour of quiet listening every morning, with mind and heart receptive to the Holy Spirit, would make such a difference. No one can doubt the thread of divine guidance that runs through the history of this nation. Washington listened at 

Valley Forge and gave our nation freedom. Lincoln listened in a great crisis and preserved our Union. Benjamin 

Franklin said: "I have lived, Sir, a long time, and the longer I live the more convincing proof I see of this truth:

God governs in the affairs of men." Why don't we all listen to God?

The future may well depend on what happens in America. Will management and labor meet on the basis of listening to God? Will education seek a higher Wisdom? Does what we have on our pennies mean anything—“In God 

We Trust"? Can bent and broken homes be made sound?

It may seem to some a far cry from that family scene in Bethany to this industrial age. But the principles of world change are there. Will we listen to God and let Him make us different? That is the question. The answer will determine the future of our world.

EROD'S father, old Herod the Great, was probably HEROD'S old Herod the was probably the bloodiest monarch that ever lived. He murdered his own mother. He had his wife executed. He murdered so many of his children that Caesar Augustus in far-off Rome made the quip that he'd rather be Herod's sow than his son. 

Young Herod was a chip off the old block. In his early youth he was educated in Rome, and in all the vices of such a capital. We have not learned any new ways of sinning since the days of Rome. Fresh from his schooling in the capital of the Empire, he set the moral tone for the youth of Palestine. 

He was attractive, fascinating, debonair, and courteous. He dashed up and down the provinces fanning the wild flame of youth. He went over into Arabia and fell in love with 

King Aretas' daughter, and after a swift romance, married her. On their honeymoon they visited Herod's brother Philip in Idumea. During their prolonged visit, Herod fell in love with Philip's wife, Herodias. Herodias, taking her little daughter Salome with her, fled with Herod to his palace. 

The bride of only a few months was sent back to her father's

home and Herod and his mistress shut themselves up within the palace and defied both Philip and Aretas. Old King 

Aretas, to avenge his daughter's dishonor, led an army against Herod, but met a crushing military defeat. Herod, meanwhile, with Herodias and young Salome, ruled the country in which Jesus grew to manhood. 

About that time John the Baptist began to preach by the River Jordan. Great crowds came to hear this prophet, attracted by his fiery eloquence. He lived on simple fare and was clothed with the majesty of long silent months of prayer and meditation. One day Herod and his courtiers must have come down to hear John preach. Perhaps someone warned him of Herod's presence. Timid friends might well have said what people say today: "Be careful what you publish, the advertisers might not like it." "Be careful how you talk, your biggest stockholder is listening." "Be careful how you preach, your largest contributor is in the front pew.” “Be careful what you say, you'll lose a lot of votes."

But John rose to speak in bold and fearless grandeur. He turned to Herod and said, "It is not lawful for you to have your brother Philip's wife; put away thy brother's wife. 

Bring forth fruits meet for repentance." And to the astonishment of everybody, Herod heard him gladly. They thought he would instantly put John to death. He was arrested, but he was put in Herod's private prison in the palace. During the days that followed, Herod often visited John, and listened to him with deep interest. He began to tidy up his

life. He was moved by the influence of such a man. John was the only man who had ever told him the truth. 

Then Herodias began to see the danger of this new menace. Such a woman never feels secure with only her bodily form and her painted features and her clever subtlety to depend on. She felt, what many a woman feels today, the peril of her false position. Often a man slips down into hell clutching at the skirts of a pure, true woman; but many a woman, slipping down into hell herself, has sought desperately to drag her man along with her. Herodias must protect herself! Herod must not be permitted to change! 

She planned his downfall well. She waited for his birthday. The wine sparkled and every effort of persuasion led the king to drink and drink. Watching for those familiar signs in his face, Herodias gave the signal. Salome, her own daughter, would be her bait. Gracefully she danced before his inflamed eyes. His nostrils dilated, his neck grew thick, his sensual mouth opened. How Salome had grown! How young, how desirable she seemed! The inner restraint snapped. He burst out, "How much do you charge? Name your price, even to half my kingdom," he shouted with an oath. The trap was sprung. Salome closed the bargain and announced the price: "The head of John the Baptist."

We see Herod trying to collect his wits. He couldn't do that not to the best man he ever knew! There must be some way out-but, his oath! He had sworn. The eyes of all the crowd were fixed upon him. He couldn't think

straight. Why had he drunk that last glass? No one was speaking. Everyone was looking. He had sworn. It was too late. "Go ahead!" he cried hoarsely.

And John, the only man who had ever had the courage to tell Herod the truth, was beheaded. Salome and Herodias got his head in a dish. John's followers tenderly took up his body and carried it away for burial, and then went hurriedly and told Jesus. 

In the palace the lights shine all night now. Everything seems natural again. Herod is his old self and everyone seems happy. There is eating and drinking and merrymaking. Herodias feels relieved. She has had a narrow escape. Herod might have become a different man if she had not got rid of John. It was a close call-she had saved herself. And so the months pass.

Then, rumors began to filter into the palace of Herod. 

Another prophet is on the road! Crowds throng the streets of Capernaum, and gather on the hillsides, and now and then Herod's soldiers had reported masses of interested listeners. And the words were just the same: "Repent—for the kingdom of heaven is at hand!”

Finally the story got to Herod, and he turned pale. "Repent! Repent!" It sounded like John. "Now!" exclaimed 

Herod, "it is John, whom I slew-come back to life again!" 

How like the tolling of a bell come those challenging words, "Put away thy brother Philip's wife." Repentance could mean nothing else to Herod. Whatever the sin, whether it

be adultery or dishonesty, or whatever it be, repentance means one thing, always one thing. It means being sorry enough to quit. No amount of tidying up the superficial wrongs and habits of life will greatly matter unless we go deep to the heart of the sin we love and cling to, and by the help of the divine Spirit, tear it from our life. 

A year passes-two years—and Herod makes his annual trip to Jerusalem. So many Jews of Galilee go up to feast days in that old capital that Herod's presence is needed to keep the peace. And so it happens that one morning, on the eve of the Passover feast, a message comes to Herod from Pilate, that a Galilean is being sent to him, because all Galileans came under Herod's jurisdiction. The name of the prisoner is Jesus and He comes from Nazareth.

The New Testament gives this scene with some details. 

Herod is glad to have a chance to see the prophet he once thought might be John come back to life. But the deep concern for his own soul has gone. What prompts him now is curiosity. Once he could have responded to Jesus as he had to John, but sin's deadening, blinding power has done its work. In Herod's judgment hall Jesus is absolutely silent. 

Not one word escapes His lips. Herod talks and babbles and demands a miracle. Finally, he puts on Jesus a robe of mockery and sends Him back to Pilate to be condemned. 

Herod lost his chance. He knew in person the two greatest characters of his age. He had held John as a guest

in his personal prison and had stood face to face with Jesus the Son of God.

Soon after the death of Jesus, Herod lost his throne. In bad favor at Rome, he was banished into Gaul, where tradition has it that he soon died a horrible death-decayed with one of those dreaded social diseases, choking to death at last on his own blood.

With this story as a background, let us think of the need for moral character and the tragedy of not having it. Herod could represent an individual or he could represent a nation. 

In fact, politically, Herod was the nation. His word was law. He had the power to do as he pleased. In a democracy 

Herod could represent all of us. We are the rulers of America. We do as we please. We have the power to run things the way we want them. The only laws we cannot tamper with are the laws of God.

Consider Herod as an individual. Desire controlled him. 

What he wanted he took. When he tired of the princess of 

Arabia, and wanted to flee with Herodias, he argued that a marriage vow shouldn't make three people unhappy. Neighbors might have opinions about whether it was right or wrong. But he could get by with it, and so he did. Until he met John. 

John represents God. That is the function of a prophet.

Man to man John had the courage to tell Herod that he was breaking God's law. The words were backed up with

the kind of character and divine authority that got under 

Herod's skin.

Nansen had an instrument in the Arctic seas so delicate that it registered far underneath the frozen waters the presence of the Gulf Stream. Deep within the human life of the most depraved, there is a heart that now and then responds to genuine character. Herod was not all bad. There was still some righteous instinct that God could touch. Honesty, purity, unselfishness, still struck chords of response.

There is that much good in everybody. One can imagine 

Herod's sudden moderation. He drank less; he thought more deeply. He was more approachable. Visions of a whole new pattern of life began to lure him, and surely now and then he would cast wistful glances after that new ideal.

John tried to show him the way out. Adulterers need forgiveness, cleansing, and the power to rise into a new life of purity. That is what John called Herod to accept. But the one condition on which forgiveness, cleansing, and a new life of power could be his was repentance. This is the very heart of the New Testament message. But repentance is not always as easy as one might think. John did not minimize the difficulty. He insisted that some very definite acts must result from the right kind of repentance.

For one thing he must see the wrong in such a light as to make him hate it. Sometimes men see the consequences of their sin, and they are so undesirable that they do hate it. 

But this attitude may not last long. The old appetite grows

and the sin becomes gradually as attractive as ever. Real hatred of sin comes when they know how God feels about it. 

They might learn that from the Voice that thunders the Ten Commandments from Mt. Sinai. Or they might learn it from the Voice that appeals from Mt. Calvary. But until a man knows how God feels, repentance is superficial. 

The next step is to forsake it. A youngster was once asked the difference between remorse and repentance. He said, "Remorse is when you say you are sorry and do it again.

Repentance is when you are sorry enough to quit." It is a decision of the will; and as soon as we mean business, God provides the power. The will to be different is God's great opportunity. Herod toyed with the idea. He saw the point. 

Maybe he felt the time would come when he would take 

John seriously enough to make the break. His change would absolutely have rocked the nation. And that's what John was fighting for.

Then Herod would have to confess the wrong. The simple principle of confession is that it should be as public as the wrong. If the nation knew about the sin then the nation should know about his repentance. Sins done in the open cannot be wholly repented of in a corner. Somebody has got to know about the repentance if it's real. 

The next step is to put things right to restore. “Put away thy brother Philip's wife," was John's fearless demand.

Of course full restitution is not always possible. Herodias might refuse to return to Philip. He on the other hand

might not receive her back. And then, too, the daughter of 

Aretas might have married again. But as far as Herod could go in that direction he must go if the new life, with forgiveness and healing, was to be his. 

While John was fighting for this new life in Herod, Herodias was desperately trying to avert the change. She represents the interests in our own world that fight to keep people like they are, or to make them worse. On a national scale she stands for the subversive forces. Try to imagine the industries and rackets in America that would go out of business if everyone turned to God in true repentance. Our very economic order would be affected. Some of it stands in the same position as Herodias. If Herod changes, she is finished.

Herodias was smart. She hated John the Baptist with bitter hatred. She schemed and plotted for his destruction. 

She had weapons available for the battle. 

For one thing she had Herod's past life to start with. 

She knew his weaknesses. In the intimacy of their association she could get in a thousand subtle words. She could discredit John. She could sow seeds of mistrust. Her lies would bear upon his mind. Crafty, sly, with all the deadly charm of a woman's prostituted power, she fought. Herod must not change.

She had the alliance of the glass. It takes a tremendous amount of resolution to break a habit. One needs every possible encouragement. I know some addicted husbands

who could win this battle against alcoholism if their wives would join them in the struggle. Some wives play the part of Herodias more often than they think. I have never known a man wholly to throw off the well-established habit of drunkenness who had a wife who even moderately drank herself. What chance did Herod have when the person closest to him was seeking in every possible way to undermine every good impulse? As a weapon to battle the appeal of John the Baptist, this had tremendous power. He would certainly take a drink at his own birthday party!

Again, she brought into play the powerful force of sex. 

In every possible way she would strike at his greatest weakness. She did not have the alliance of the abundance of modern art in magazines and advertisements, which is especially designed to play upon the sex instinct. But she used her young and graceful daughter, Salome. Herodias and 

Salome could easily be given a hundred names today. Perhaps a thousand things that are sold today are directly or indirectly exploiting the moral weakness of men and women. 

Hostility to any vital Christian force in our world will come from many sources. It flares whenever religion cuts across human selfishness and vested interests. The religious trust in Jerusalem treated Jesus as Herodias treated John. 

And the money-making clique with which these religious leaders worked saw in the spiritual awakening Jesus was bringing the end of their regime. The only reason that so much organized religion does not suffer violent persecution

today is because it is not morally incisive. Prophets demand change. Most of them are crucified or lose their heads. 

Still further, Herodias had the crowd on her side. Maybe they did not understand the terrific issue. Maybe Herodias just banked on their moral apathy. Maybe they were with her hand in glove to save Herod from John. Maybe they were all afraid of Herodias, and dared not hesitate to be her tool. Maybe they preferred the glamor of Salome to the moral challenge of the prophet. According to the record it was finally the crowd that forced Herod to kill John. Once trapped into giving his oath he could not break his word with all that crowd looking at him. In that dramatic moment he had not one single courageous friend. 

It may be possible to draw a striking analogy between America and Herod. I do not mean of course that the average citizen is as bad as Herod. But in whatever stage of moral need the average citizen may be, he is at least like Herod in this-he needs to be changed. And that is true of the nation as a whole. Impurity is the besetting sin of millions. We are in danger of becoming an immoral nation. We have forgotten that morals are the secret of morale. As the British labor statesman, Lord Elton, has so well put it in his book, St. George or the Dragon: “Morale, which is to say morals, is the foundation of successful war as of successful civilization." American soldiers in the last war saw the collapse of France twenty-five years ago on the streets of Paris and Marseille. They may not have recognized

it then. But French patriots have recognized it now. Those 

American soldiers of 1918 did not dream that they would ever see the same symptoms on the streets of American cities. 

They see them now. What then will America be like in 

1968? It took twenty-five years in France. It may not take so long here if we go on the way we are going. 

Think under what conditions this generation has grown up. There was the wild orgy of gambling and speculation in the 1920's. The postwar world was struggling to get to its feet. Bootlegging was at its height. Flapperism was rampant. Individualism determined our personal and international attitudes. We simply believed in doing as we pleased. We defied all moral and economic laws. Then came 

1929. That sobered us. During the early 1930's we were almost like the prodigal son with his wasted patrimony, facing starvation. 

Where was John the Baptist? America was ready to listen. Like Herod, millions went down to the River Jordan. 

The religious leadership of this country had its greatest opportunity. But instead of preaching repentance we joined in every kind of scheme for social reform. Plans were formulated. Agencies were set up. Programs were launched. 

Many of these had real merit. And as a result of true national repentance, they might have had permanent value. 

But the essential challenge to the heart of this country was not in social reform.

The one clear voice through those years was indeed like the voice of John the Baptist crying in the wilderness. "National and world problems remain the same because the root problem-human nature-remains unsolved. Until we deal with human nature thoroughly and drastically on a national scale, nations must still follow their historic road to violence and destruction."

Many of us who love the Church are deeply humbled to have to make the admission of our failure. Herod often sat in the pew but few prophets got arrested or even interviewed. He often went home from church comforted and soothed. Sometimes he even brought about social changes and got on committees for civic betterment. But religion did not dig into the corrupt heart with a sharp "Repent!" No man or woman living in adultery should ever be at ease sitting in a church. No dishonest person, with the memory of lies or stolen goods upon his conscience, should ever find comfort from a sermon until he has confessed his sin and made full restitution.

Today another crisis is upon us. Perhaps never in human history has a generation been so morally unprepared for a great war. Maybe the one thing that will give us our second chance is the breakdown of our materialistic philosophy of life. Herod was a Sadducee. That was first-century materialism. He did not believe in immortality. But when the message of Jesus reached his ears Herod exclaimed, "It is John, whom I slew, risen from the dead." It is the perfect

illustration of this fact, that when conscience stirs, our finespun theories will vanish and our stupid philosophies will be brushed aside. Mounting casualty lists may make us think. "It is appointed unto men once to die, and after that the judgment."

We have imagined that morale is something else than righteousness. What help are "wine, women, and song" when Death blows on our cheeks? What good are amusements, and even social or religious plans that deal with life's externals, when moral calamity threatens to engulf us? Repent. Turn completely about. Forsake sin in every form. 

God, and God alone, can save us. Does this call seem now to come with the added emphasis of a world beyond the grave? 

We must change or decay. "John" always has a difficult task to fulfill. He is the untarnished Truth. His words are like fire. They are an axe laid at the root of the tree. He is a warning of the impending wrath of God. He is righteousness aflame with a passion. He is the call to personal, social, and national change. 

In one generation he comes in the garb of St. Francis of 

Assisi. In another in the fierce and fiery eloquence of John 

Knox. In still another in the persuasive power of the Wesleys. In our generation "John" is sure to be overlooked by millions and hated by millions more. But you can tell when he comes if you listen: "Let him who stole, steal no more." "Bring forth fruits meet for repentance." "Every tree which bringeth not forth good fruit is hewn down, and cast into

the fire." He will come preaching "the baptism of repentance for the remission of sins." You can be sure of that. 

And the result of personal change can be seen in social change. "He answereth and saith unto them, He that hath two coats, let him impart to him that hath none; and he that hath meat, let him do likewise." If everybody cares enough and everybody shares enough, everybody will have enough. 

The vital element in the Church is calling insistently for personal purity, absolute honesty, and a genuine caring for the other person. The most powerful spiritual force in our world today is pointing to the only path to national security. "Three thousand miles of ocean do not change this fundamental problem-and will not save us if we fail to solve it. 

The symptoms may differ in Europe and America. The disease is the same. What is the disease? Isn't it fear, dishonesty, resentment, selfishness? We talk about freedom and liberty, but we are slaves to ourselves. "The only possible alternatives today are collapse or Godcontrol. And collapse is simply the selfishness of all of us together. You and I, if we are selfish, are part of the disease; just as you and I, if we are God-controlled, can be part of the cure."

I once heard a missionary to India describe a leper: "He was slowly rotting to death. His fingers and feet were gone. 

There was a hole in his face where his nose and lips had once been. I could see his teeth like a death's head looking out of that decaying body. He was once a strong man,

strong and clean, but the hidden germ of leprosy spread the poison, finally showing a spot here, another there, until that man was decaying.” Only a miracle could restore that leper to health. Only a supernatural miracle of the Spirit can bring to this country a new life of moral soundness. 

The disease is sin. The treatment is repentance and the cure is the Cross of Christ.

CHAPTER V The Government of God

And it came to pass, when Ahab saw Elijah, that Ahab said unto him, Art thou he that troubleth Israel? And he answered, I have not troubled Israel; but thou, and thy father's house, in that ye have forsaken the commandments of the 

Lord, and thou hast followed Baalim. Now therefore send, and gather to me all Israel unto mount Carmel, and the prophets of Baal four hundred and fifty, and the prophets of the groves four hundred, which eat at Jezebel's table. So

Ahab sent unto all the children of Israel, and gathered the prophets together unto mount Carmel. And Elijah came unto all the people, and said, How long halt ye between two opinions? if the Lord be God, follow him: but if Baal, then follow him.

"If the Lord be God, follow him: but if Baal, then follow him.” 

-1 Kings 18:21. 

HOUGHTFUL Americans want an answer. The 

TH problems before us are too colossal for any individual or political party. Men are groping. The present is becoming more complicated every day. The future is dark. Last week in a barbershop where one picks up a lot of modern thought, a man said soberly, "If things go on two more years as they are going, our country will be in one hopeless mess." The national debt at such a sum as leaves the mind almost a blank; the moral deterioration of youth and the powerlessness of mature people to stem the tide; the impotence of new laws and stricter justice; race riots and industrial disputes-all these fairly stun us. And events are moving so swiftly. Could we get hold of one basic truth, one common solvent for our problems?

Today we are engaged in total war. We are fighting for total victory. But I believe we shall never achieve total victory unless we fight for total Christianity. 

Our side winning the war will not be in itself a total victory, for there are evil forces plotting within the nation as well as without. William Penn saw the issues clearly in his

day. “Men must be governed by God or they will be ruled by tyrants." The full and complete answer to tyranny outside a nation and within is the government of God. The answer to the totalitarian state is total Christianity. In a word, making God the Boss.

Long centuries ago a Hebrew prophet saw what William Penn saw, and called upon his nation to face it. "If the 

Lord be God," he cried, "follow him: but if Baal, then follow him!" It was a time when men had to take sides. There was no middle ground. They had to decide whether they and their nation would be governed by God or ruled by tyrants. 

Today, as in Elijah's time, two mighty forces are bidding for the heart of the world. We also are in the midst of one of those critical and creative periods of history when the future of our nation can be decided for a thousand years to come. Individuals cannot make the choice merely as individuals. The old-time evangelist has stressed the tragedy of men and women individually going to hell. That happens in every generation. But today we live in a time when enough individuals choosing to go to hell will pull the nation down to hell with them.

The choice we each make determines the way America will go. And we need an Elijah to say to us as a nation: "If the Lord be God, follow him: but if Baal, then follow him." We have got to decide who will be the boss. 

Most of you are familiar with that dramatic meeting on

Mount Carmel. The leaders of the nation were present. The king was involved in the issue. The nation was teetering in the balance. Like America today, Israel could go either way. A hard push would send her in either direction. It was a day of destiny, a day of choice, a day of decision. It was to be a turning point in the nation's history. 

The children of Israel had come out of the wilderness into a new land as pioneers. Behind them were leaders who had been led by God. The true God and His holy laws had been written into the nation's constitution. The Ten 

Commandments had been like the Mayflower compact. The founders of America like the pioneers of Israel took God as their King. They laid broad and strong foundations for liberty and security. They saw that no nation could rise to greatness and remain strong that did not obey God. "The 

Lord shall be king over all the earth," wrote Zechariah.

Deep at the heart of national philosophy, both in Israel and in America, was the simple idea that God must be the Boss. 

Then the thing happened in Israel which is happening in America today. True religion began to get confused with materialism. In Israel it was more dramatic and more observable. Materialism had a god. Baal was his name. He had his priests and his temples. He was the bane of Israel's existence, because he offered to them the things the human instincts crave. Baal was the god of the flesh. At first he was an agricultural deity and the people associated productiveness with him. He sent the rain, he warmed the soil and

brought forth harvests. But soon he became the god of license and immorality. His priests encouraged the people to follow their natural inclinations. His worship cut at the very heart of Israel's religion. It was idolatry in its most insidious form. Baalism was materialism deified. It was complete selfishness. Its chief expression was lust. So Baal began competing with Jehovah.

On one side self-indulgence and materialism pressed upon the people, on the other the faith and moral discipline of their fathers. They began to be confused. They took a little of Baal and a little of Jehovah. They put morality on the basis of relative values. They watered down the stern laws demanding uncompromising obedience laws of purity and honesty and tempered them to permit a low moral standard. The worship of Baal and Jehovah got mixed. It was hard for the average, ordinary person to know where the line of separation ran. And one of the most subtle dangers in America now is that same kind of confusion. 

Confusion is always the devil's strategy. He blurs our moral judgment. He creates fogs and deals in shades and mixed colors. He hates the light; he despises blacks and whites, strong contrasts, and clear issues. Millions of people in America live in moral fogs. They see only greys and browns. They move in spiritual twilight, confused and agitated. The Christian philosophy of life has gotten mixed with selfishness. Compromise has permeated our entire social life. The phrase, "The American Way," has come to mean

something vastly different from what our fathers intended. 

With some it is a kind of Baal worship that tolerates Christian ethics only to the extent that they do not seriously control people. Some people say they worship God but actually obey Baal. Others worship Baal but cannot wholly get away from Christian ideas. Modified immorality on the basis of cleverness guides millions of people. Modified dishonesty, within the letter of American law, guides millions more. 

Unselfishness limited strictly to one's own family is a common rule. A kind attitude toward people in general but a bitter attitude toward some people in particular is another. 

Baal and Jehovah are mixed in people's minds. 

In such a confused and critical age we need prophets. 

Not prophets in the sense of men who can predict the future. A real prophet is not a soothsayer or a mere predictor. 

A real prophet is a man who sees where the issues of life and death lie, who can set before the nation the essential choices. 

Elijah was a real prophet. He saw that Israel as a nation could not go on with God and Baal. The time had come when it must be God or Baal. Elijah was fearless and uncompromising. He faced the four hundred and fifty prophets of Baal, the king, and the people. It was to be an "eitheror." They had to get on one side or the other. And God vindicated His prophet with an answer that sent the multitude away saying, "The Lord, he is the God; the Lord, he is the God."

The choice before us today is just as plain. We must

choose between total chaos and total Christianity. Will it be materialism or Jesus Christ? Baal or God? Selfishness or unselfishness? Immorality or purity? Will we live for ourselves or for the other fellow? Every day brings us nearer the fatal hour when we must decide nationally for God or against Him.

We need leaders like George Washington who know that the basic issue today is not political or economic but moral. "Let us raise a standard to which the wise and honest can repair. The event is in the hand of God." It is 

God or Baal; discipline or indulgence; right or wrong. 

This message must be got to the millions: "If the Lord be 

God, follow him: but if Baal, then follow him."

Man's confusion results from his unwillingness to let God wholly rule his life. We have been governed by our instincts, our desires, our hates; we have been governed by people's opinions and by human wisdom; we have been governed by false philosophies and false ideals. For all our pious protestations we have consistently refused to be governed by God. And the result is myriad forms of tyranny masquerading under the name of freedom. The answer to all this confusion lies in the government of God. 

God's government in you and me, in home, factory, politics, agriculture, in the prosecution of the war, in the plans for world peace, at the peace table, in the swing from war production to peace-time industry. Everywhere, the government of God!

Behind this thought lies solid logic. We believe in God. 

He made us. We did not just happen. We are strange creatures and this is a strange universe. But God made it and He put us here. We did not come from any cosmic accident. We are part of a plan, a divine plan. The surest fact on which to build our thesis is this: "In the beginning 

God." We have come from a supernatural force. Behind all the bewildering scene is God.

God knows what He is about. He knows what is best for every single one of us, for our homes, our business, and our nation. The very assumption of God leads straight to

His sovereignty. If there is a God at all, He has the right to direct our liyes and our destiny. In one of the darkest periods of history St. Augustine rediscovered this great fact and dreamed of the City of God, a perfect society on this earth. This truth must blaze forth again to all the world. 

A nation led by God would lead the world. "And nations . . . shall run unto thee because of the Lord thy God," cried

Isaiah. A nation's glory is to demonstrate God's government. Here is the high aim of any country. Here is the clue to a nation's destiny. America's future depends upon her accepting and demonstrating God's government. If she listens to God and obeys Him she can be great among all nations. Here in America we have the people, the means of communication, the political forms, the wealth, the

prestige, and the ability. We must establish God as our 

Ruler and give this ideal to the nations. 

Slowly there rises before the student of Scripture the unfolding plan. God lays down simple, fundamental laws which set forth the picture of life as He wants it. These 

God-given laws are the foundations of morality. Men began to live by them. As they lived by them they became increasingly aware of a Presence, and they learned to converse with that Presence. Men walked with God. They talked with Him "as a man talks with a friend." Men began to tell what God said to them. Out of one man's experience of God came a specially chosen family. Out of this specially chosen family came a specially chosen nation. As long as they carried out God's plan the nation prospered. But as life became more complex and complicated, many people began to do as they pleased. God's plan was ignored and His laws were flouted. Troubles multiplied. Greed and hate and arrogance became nation-wide. God's plan appeared to fail. Back and forth the nation swayed between peace and war, between prosperity and calamity, between temporary efforts at obedience and prolonged periods of rebellion. Elijah was one of the many who tried to stem the tide. At last the patience of God seemed to be exhausted. 

The nation tottered and fell.

But the experience of that nation would be a lesson for all the world. Israel showed what it will be like for any nation that refuses to make God the Boss. Jesus came to set forth

in even clearer terms the choice we have to make. By parable and miracle He made His truth plain. On the cross He made it inescapable and offered men a full experience of God. After His resurrection He offered His followers the gift which only the prophets had known before. His Spirit would be available to all, men and women, old and young, parents and children alike. He would guide them to teach the nations and bring the whole world under 

God's control.

His followers would meet the complicated issues of life with clear direction and with a super-Power. They would aim to make their nations Christian. They would fight the powers of Baal which through the centuries have always destroyed men's countries. The Church would be an army under orders. The objectives would be clear, the strategy inspired, and the victory certain. At the very center of their philosophy would be the fact that God would speak directly to their hearts and minds. He would guide them in every detail of their life. He would show them how to fight for the New Order wherever they were. Men would listen and He would speak. Obedient men would unite and work together until God became the Boss in home, industry, and government. For nineteen hundred years this challenge has been ringing through our world. 

For a generation now our national thinking has been nearly void of this simple but revolutionary fact that men can be in touch with God, that in every situation men can

know the will of God, that they can know exactly what God wants them to be and do, that there is always adequate and accurate information available. When men listen God speaks.

Think what it will mean in such tragic days if men turn to God and listen to His voice. Think what it will mean to you, personally, to your home, to your business. Think what it will mean to a city's government, to a state legislature, to our national Congress. Surely many of us love 

America enough to change and make God the Boss. But how?

Let me tell you of two men and how they made God the Boss. One is a mechanic. Before the war he was working in a shipyard on the day shift. Times were slack. It looked as if he or the man on the night shift might be laid off at any moment. He left his machine in such shape that it took the night-shift man valuable time to get it in order. 

If the machine was running "off," it was the other guy's business to find it out. He was looking out for number one. 

If anyone was to be laid off he wanted it to be the other fellow. Still, he went to church. His business and religion were in different departments of his life. He didn't even see the issue. He felt pretty satisfied with himself. Then a prophet's voice got his ear. He began to see things straight. 

Either he was a Christian or he wasn't. It shocked him. 

For days he fought the idea back and forth. He had to be really Christian or stop pretending.

Now, you may think that nobody but God has a right to talk to you like that. Your actions and your motives are your own. But beware how you duck and dodge when 

God talks to you through another person. If anybody can help you hear what God says, then he is a prophet. He makes the issue clear. He draws the line. He faces you with the choice.

The mechanic decided for total Christianity. He decided that he would trust God. If being a one-hundred-percent

Christian meant that he would be laid off, then he felt he could trust God to see that he had something to eat and a place to sleep and enough to wear. So he began. 

He took time at the close of the shift to clean and arrange the tools. He left a note explaining any deviation or irregularity in the machine. He started to live for the other fellow. And then a miracle happened. The man of the night shift wanted to know about the change. He saw the point and agreed to work on the same principle. They united their forces. They began to plan for a new spirit in the whole shop. Those two mechanics have showed thousands what total Christianity could mean to a nation's industry. 

They listened to God, ready to obey Him no matter what it cost. And a plan emerged. First it was the simple things. 

They learned to do the simple Christian things where they worked. They took the spirit to their homes and then started planning to make it national. 

Only this past week I read of the other—a printer in a

western city. One day a political candidate came to him and asked him to set up some campaign material. The political aspirant was running for a local office. The election itself was a symbol of what American democracy stands for, a free election in a free country. That is one of the things we're grateful for. Well, the printer refused to print the campaign literature. The candidate indignantly demanded a reason. The printer had read the copy and he said, "That sort of stuff is not the basis on which a man should run for office! All you're doing is running the other fellow down, instead of building up a positive platform." "That shows you don't know anything about politics," retorted the candidate. "You never elect yourself in this game; you simply uproot the other fellow from office." The would-be office holder clamped his hat on his head and stamped out. But the printer held his ground. 

Two hours later the candidate returned. "I've thought it over," he said; "I'm converted. Help me write a positive platform, and let's get the campaign going on that basis." 

Then the printer began to set forth his views: "There are thousands of men on the battlefront," he said, “willing to die for this country. What we need at home are more men who will live for it—who will sacrifice and fight that God's will can operate-men who are willing to lose some of their trade and some of their friends if need be." 

Thus one man, a very ordinary citizen, began to influence his city's life. Later in the campaign one of the city coun-

cilmen declared that the very union label of that printing shop began to stand for Christian ethics and truthfulness. 

The news analyst who wrote the story added this sentence: "This sort of action is vital national service. That printer is fighting this war as much as if he were in uniform." 

That's what total Christianity means. It is the application of God's will to every part of our nation's life—to labor and management, to every political party, to every business, to every home, and finally to the Peace Conference that will some day come if God gives us a military victory.

It means the will of God totally applied. 

In the words of a modern prophet, Frank Buchman, who for more than twenty years has pressed this message of 

God's control: "People believe that their leaders should be guided by God. But the rank and file must be guided too. A Godguided public opinion is the strength of the leaders. This is the dictatorship of the living Spirit of God. Only Godcontrolled men will make God-controlled nations to make a new world." 

Is not this the philosophy we need? Is it not the Church's job to shout it from the housetops? Must we not get it into our nation's fibre before the diplomats and generals get around the peace table? We are engaged in total war. We are fighting for total victory. But we shall never achieve total victory unless we fight for total Christianity.
