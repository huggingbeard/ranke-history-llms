RESEARCH ON THE MIDDLE EAST External Research List No. 4.23 

This publication records social science research on the Middle East completed between October 1963 and September 1964. It is one of a series of lists based on the catalogue of social science research on foreign areas and international affairs compiled by the External Research Staff from information submitted by scholars and research centers throughout the United States. Lists of completed research are published annually in the fall; those of research in progress, annually in the spring. 

Data for each citation are as complete as the Staff's files permit. In listing studies the following new style has been adopted: book, monograph, paper, and Ph.D. dissertation titles are underlined; articles are enclosed within quotation marks; research is noted as such. Items preceded by an asterisk are available on loan from the External Research Staff (Room 8642; Code 

182, ext. 2948) to GOVERNMENT OFFICIALS. Requesters are reminded that authors' rights to their texts should be strictly honored. Non-governmental requesters, and persons desiring items not preceded by the asterisk, should address their inquiries directly to the author whose affiliation (department if applicable) or home address is given in parentheses following his name. Where available, annotations, supplied by the author or prepared by the External Research Staff, follow the bibliographical information. The Department of State assumes no responsibility for the accuracy of the statements of fact, nor does it necessarily endorse the conclusions expressed. All abbreviations are taken from 

Webster's New Collegiate Dictionary. 

The Staff welcomes letters from scholars and research organizations giving the current status of unfinished projects already in the External Research Catalogue and reporting new projects on which they are starting. Scholars are invited to send annotations with their research topics for inclusion in next spring's lists.

Copies of this list, as well as the others in the series, are sent to the libraries of universities that offer graduate degrees in the social sciences, to directors of area study programs and to faculty members who are engaged in relevant teaching or research. The catalogue itself is available at the address below for use by students and private scholars as well as by government officials. 

Since the Spring 1964 edition, there are seven instead of ten lists, the three separate Asian registers having been consolidated, and the Great Britain and Canada list having been incorporated into that of Western Europe.
