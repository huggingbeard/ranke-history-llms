PERSONS UNDER THE SUPERVISION OF THE FEDERAL PROBATION SYSTEM, FISCAL YEAR 1962 Introduction 

In any correctional system which is reponsible for the care and treatment or supervision of persons convicted of criminal offenses by the courts, it is essential to have a sound statistical procedure which furnishes a strict accounting of the persons committed to its oversight and provides a means of measuring and evaulating the operation of the system. The Federal probation system has been the responsibility of the Administrative Office of the United States Courts since the establishment of this Office in 1940, and within the Office, the Probation Division is charged with the overall administration of the probation offices and officers. 

The statistical accounting for persons under the supervision of Federal probation officers has been quite limited through the past years and has not been able to supply the factual answers to many questions that arise concerning the operation of the system. In 1959 under the guidance of the experts from the National Archives a new method of reporting was introduced which required an individual report form on each person received for active supervision to be prepared and a copy furnished to the Probation Division. Carbon copies of this form were retained in each office and upon the removal of the case from supervision the copy with merely the addition of the date and the reason for removal completed the report to the Probation Division.

Standard Reporting Forms 

The establishment of this standard form was an essential first step in the direction of a better statistical system. However, the statistics obtained were based entirely on the reports furnished from some 100 different probation offices and there was no check available on the completeness of the reporting nor was there a systematic accounting of all the persons who became the responsibility of the different probation offices. A control form was introduced as a monthly report which indicated the number under supervision at the beginning, those received for supervision, those removed from supervision and the number pending at the end of the month. Presumably the number of individual reports

of those received for supervision and those removed tallied with the monthly control totals. There was no check between the statistical reports received from the clerks of court accounting for all defendants sentenced and the number of those placed on probation each month as reported by the probation offices. Consequently, there were discrepancies to be noted between the court statistics stating the number granted probation and the reports of the probation officers accounting for those received for probation supervision.

Expansion in 1963 

Starting with the fiscal year 1963 another step was taken in the direction of more complete information on criminal defendants and probationers. The probation offices were requested to furnish information on each person investigated as to sex, age, race, prior record, whether or not a presentence investigation was made and the recommendation made as to probation, if any. Information on sex, age, and race had been reported on the forms accounting for persons received for probation prior to 1963 but not information on the last three items above mentioned and none of the information had been reported on persons investigated who were not received for probation supervision. These reports from the probation officers were checked against the report of defendants convicted received from the clerks of court. This not only made possible the addition of the personal characteristics information above enumerated to the court statistical card but also made it possible to check out and resolve the differences between the two sets of reports from the same district. 

Another important change in 1963 was to request the probation officers in submitting the reports on persons removed from supervision for violation or by warrant to give detailed data concerning the nature of violation or offense or the reason for which the warrant was issued. 

Prior to 1963 as will be seen in the succeeding pages of this report, there was no way to subclassify persons removed for violation to ascertain who were involved in technical violations as compared with those involved in new minor or major criminal offenses. Starting with the year 1963, it will be possible to analyze the violation removals in terms of the nature of the violation and seriousness of the offense. involved. 

In studying the probation data reported it soon became clear that there was no common practice among the 

---

district offices as to how and when a person was removed from supervision when he became involved in a new offense. 

The most usual pattern of removal was through the use of a violation warrant. In some instances where a person was involved in a new offense no warrant would be issued but the case would be merely reported as closed. In other instances no warrant would be issued and although the defendant was incarcerated in a state or local institution, he would be continued on the active supervision roll. Such variations in practice prevent the making of any satisfactory comparisons of the criminal behavior of persons under supervision of the Federal probation officers.

One further project has been undertaken during fiscal year 1963 to provide a better check on the criminal behavior of persons under supervision. The Federal Bureau of Investigation creates a criminal record file on persons for whom fingerprints are received whenever a person has accumulated two or more sets of fingerprints. Through the efforts of the United States Parole Board and probation officers flash notices are sent to the FBI on all persons released for supervision so that whenever after this notice is received a new fingerprint of the individual is filed with the FBI, a copy of his record is returned to both the Parole Board for cases under their control and the probation officers for persons under their supervision. Arrangements were made to have these criminal record sheets returned to the probation officers through the Probation Division and its statistical unit. 

Two copies are received one copy is immediately forwarded to the probation office and the other copy is kept in the central statistical unit as a record of criminal arrests. This procedure was initiated January 1, 1963. 

It has clearly demonstrated that there are many arrests, convictions and sentences that are imposed on persons under supervision, particularly probationers, and in some instances, parolees, who are not reported as removed from supervision. It is clear therefore that the statistical data now available even as it has been expanded in 1963 still cannot accurately and consistently account for the known criminal behavior of persons under the supervision of probation officers with any degree of completeness. 

Further Plans for 1964 cate A further step is being contemplated for fiscal year year 1964 that should accomplish a complete accounting of probationers and parolees who are the responsibility of United States probation officers. Two basic sets of files - 3. 

---

would be kept by the probation offices with equivalent information in the central office of the statistical unit in Washington. These files would be: a) A file on all persons under active supervision of the probation officers. This would correspond fairly closely to the information that is now available. (b) A file of persons who are the responsibility of the probation officers but who are not under active supervision. These would include all persons removed from supervision whose cases were not closed; and all persons released to the responsibility of the probation officer from institutions under parole or mandatory release, who are in a detainer or custody status at the time of release. This latter group is not accounted for by any statistical control at the present time either in the Bureau of Prisons or Parole Board or the Probation Division. 

In addition persons who are in military service, those who have been deported, and who are incarcerated in mental institutions during their period of probation would be classified as inactive.

Standard instructions would be issued with respect to those who would be taken off active supervision and placed in the inactive file. Persons who were arrested and convicted and who were sentenced to incarceration for a definite period of time, possibly 60 days or more, would be removed from active supervision irrespective of the official action of the court and would remain in an inactive status until they returned to active supervision or the case was formally closed and the defendant discharged. 

One of the grave weaknesses of the present probation operation as far as the systematic accounting is concerned lies in the fact that many probationers who are removed from supervision by the issuance of a warrant are never again accounted for by the probation officer. 

Presumably a person who has a term of probation is to be accounted for until either the term of probation expires or action by the court revoking probation or discharging the probationer is taken. It is manifestly not proper to remove a person from probation who is imprisoned on a ninety-day sentence locally but who nevertheless still has two years of probation left to do and never again account for him during the subsequent two-year period. The suggestions that are being made 

---

for additional accounting control in 1964 would for the first time make it possible to strictly account for all persons placed in probation status by the court and all persons released from institutions who are under control of the Parole Board with a parole or mandatory release status.

It will be observed that the greatest use of probation is found in the districts of the First Circuit, 56 percent, and the least use of probation in the District of Columbia, 25 percent. It should be pointed out that the criminal jurisdiction of the District Court of the District of Columbia differs considerably from the rest of the United States. Only a small portion of the criminal business in the District of Columbia arises from Federal crime. This court serves as a court of general jurisdiction for violation of the District of Columbia Code and thus has before it large numbers of offenders who are charged with the ordinary felonies, i.e., robbery, burglary, larceny, etc., which in other parts of the country are prosecuted in the state court systems. 

The districts in the Third and Fourth Circuits used probation in more than one-half of the defendants convicted while those in the Fifth and Sixth Circuits used probation in slightly less than 40 percent of convictions.

Movement and Caseload of Probation Officers 

*- As has already been indicated, the United States probation officers are responsible for the supervision of persons placed on probation by the district courts and those persons released by the Parole Board from institutions under parole and mandatory release. In addition, the probation officers have three other rather small groups for whose supervision they are responsible, i.e., parolees released from military institutions, persons placed on probation by United States commissioners and a group of persons who have been classified as having been given deferred prosecutions. This latter group is not - 9 -

defined by any law. It is made up of persons whom the 

United States attorneys, under a policy set by the Department of Justice, do not formally prosecute for Federal offenses but defer prosecution and informally have the person report to the probation officer for a period of time. This plan was originally inaugurated in the Eastern District of New York to be used primarily for juveniles or youths who might be successfully handled informally rather than to initiate a formal prosecution.

Closing Note 

It must be again pointed out that the information received during fiscal year 1962 concerning persons removed for violation was not as complete or as uniform as is desired. In the first place, the nature of violation was not reported so that in the figures that have been cited, both technical, and minor and serious offenses, are lumped together. Furthermore, from information now at hand, it is realized a substantial number of cases which should have been identified and counted as violators for the reason that the probationers had been convicted and committeed for criminal offenses, were never reported. Thus, too much reliance cannot be placed on these data as representing carefully established violation rates for probationers. Controls that are being exercised during fiscal year 1963 will permit a much better and more accurate analysis of violators next year. First, the reports being received in 1963 will permit the identification and separation of minor and technical violators from those that indicate commission of new criminal offenses of a major or serious nature. 

Secondly, the 1963 data will be much more complete as to the reporting of new violations and offenses as a result of special efforts being made during the present fiscal year through checking FBI criminal record sheets and checking with the field to ascertain specifically the nature of the violation.

Further, the data in this report are limited to the type of information that was available in fiscal year 1962. These data present some new facts that have not heretofore been available for summary analysis. However, it is only a beginning in the direction of more complete and accurate information concerning both persons who are placed under supervision of the Federal probation system and the administrative practices and procedures which are followed in carrying out the responsibility of 23.

supervision. Considerably more detail will be available for the fiscal year 1963. It is planned to have more complete information as to those who are sentenced in the district courts as well as better detail concerning those persons under supervision who are again engaged in criminal conduct. If the present program can be carried out during the fiscal year 1964, there should be starting with that year a strict accountability for all persons who in any way become the official responsibility of the United States Probation System. · 24 -
