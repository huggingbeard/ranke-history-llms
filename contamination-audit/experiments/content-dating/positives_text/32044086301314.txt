"CHECK-UP" 

Introduction HIS BOOKLET is not a philos- THIS 

ophy but, the writer believes, will make an appeal to lovers of truth. The writer is not a youthful novice bent on some visionary ideas, but has had more than a halfcentury of close association with circles embracing culture, social activities, politics, journalism, corporation life and the great proletariat. 

No apology is made for employing unequivocal and unvarnished language. There is no need for whitewash, but we must speak without "fear or favor" whether the truth hurts or helps-the latter is to be preferred. Merchants from time to time take inventories. Finnancial institutions undergo periodic examinations. Wise individuals are glad to submit to regular physical tests. Then why should we not as a people welcome any form of true inspection, constructive criticism or effective purging, which may tend to aid in the important business of wise government honestly managed? 

Note the uproar in many high circles when "investigation" is suggested. suggested. The title, "Check-Up", outlines the thought in mind. Let us individually and collectively get down to real introspection and help each other and the world at large in taking a real account of stock by starting on a genuine "checkup".

What follows mainly deals with the U. S. A., the land of our birth and the soil we love. On the other hand, many things are said which relate to other countries, particularly the great democracies such as 

Britain, her dependencies, France and others. For convenience and simplification I have separated this treatise into a series of episodes in the order which I think is most important.

It may be added that this small book was finished in September, 1938, hence before our general election, and the several crises which rapidly developed in Europe. This is not said by way of apology, but to make clear there has been no need of subsequent revision.

Episode Two 

NE of our standard lexicons defines Patriotism as "love of one's country: the passion which moves a person to serve his country, either in defending it from invasion or in protecting its rights and maintaining its laws and institutions". 

In the face of country-wide lawlessness, political debauchery, an increase of depraved and degrading social life, business chicanery and corruption have we any justification for smugly calling ourselves "patriotic"? Years ago Samuel Johnson wrote: "Patriotism is the last refuge of a scoundrel". Have we changed the force of these caustic words more than a century later? 

Coming up the harbor some years ago, a foreign visitor, pointing to the Statue of Liberty, asked a returning native what it was. After explaining, our citizen glibly said "It really represents the land of the spree and the home of the knave".

When our election season draws on we are reminded of its advent by shoutings within and without, waving of the flag, tub-thumping, breast-beating and strident speeches without a real pretense to genuine oratory and little semblance of logic. Yet all these vociferating individuals and mobs claim, to be "patriots" the only saviors of their ward, city, state or federal government. Their platforms alone have the final panacea for every existing evil. To fail to elect them would mean the "demnition

PATRIOTISM 

bowwows" for the downtrodden masses, the middle classes and, to their way of talking, stark ruin for all. 

And the "professional" patriot's promises! Quick abolition of poverty, work for all, recreation to balance, lower taxes, fine opportunities for the college youth, better housing, finer school facilities, continually diminishing hours of labor, "ceiling for wages ever rising" and thus on they rant. Their palaver would almost cause the simple folk to shed tears. These cogs in the political wheel, some very small some very large are merely the "records" manipulated by the "big boss", euphemistically called "leader". And do these wheels spin for love of country? Only a nitwit would so believe. Observe the recent charges and counter charges as to the unquestioned and flagrant use of money and position to win. elections. Pothouse politician is a far better term than patriot. An almost superficial observation of the places where the majority of this ilk "gang up" would dispel any idea of patriotism. Was ever lawlessness more rampant than right now?

We have been witnessing the greatest number of criminal racketeering trials in all of our history. 

The day of the petty crook has been displaced by the huge machines dealing in dollars up to six figures. Murder, kidnaping, torture and blackmail have been neces-

sarily allied to the chief crimes. 

The poor have been deluded into thinking of "easy money" by the pittances of nickels and dimes. In short, Utopia and the Elysian Fields have been pictured to these unfortunates and Ultima Thule in the form of some day striking the "big stake"! "Big business" and "little business", too, have in plenty of instances evaded the tenets of forthright dealing and some have even brazenly broken the rules of commercial honesty. When, as happens occasionally, some are brought to the bar, they affect indignation and amazement at any questioning or arraignment. High-priced counlutely ignorant of what genuine

Patriotism means. They have grown up among sinister influences and dwarfing ideals. Saluting the flag is viewed as a routine drill. 

We are thinking of the quiet and inconspicuous multitude who have a genuine right to be called "patriots" but have become dazed and hopeless in the face of these gigantic evils and wonder what can be done, if anything, to check or correct them. In this country it is always difficult to arouse the serious and well-meaning when a crisis arises: nonetheless it must be done! 

Good citizenship meetings, arousing such powerful sentiment as to resel immediately begin every legal pudiate any group or party which device to thwart any inquiry or trial (see Episode on The Courts). 

It is related of the president of a great corporation that he sent for one of the most noted counsel practicing in this country and discussed his business. At length the learned lawyer said, "I take it you wish my advice as to what you cannot do". 

The retort was immediate: "No, sir, I want to know what we can how far we dare go do with- out getting caught". It may be thought I have strayed from the topic of this Episode, but not at all these groups claim to be "patriots".

Indeed, many of them would feel insulted to be questioned. They are quite ready to shout, "The Stars and Stripes Forever". Occasionally one gets the "stripes". In some measure, many of these are absois allied to baneful forces. The taunt of being a renegade or traitor to any political group should intensify the real patriot's purpose to see it through. After a victorious election, then the patriot's job is not to fold hands and quit thinking all has been won. We have lived long enough to see great triumphs followed by defeat, even years later, because of the placid contentment of the patriots. The "old gangs" lie awake nights to see how to offset defeat. They make that their because practically all of daily thinking and living. 

Patriotism needs the words of 

Jesus: "No man having put his hand to the plough, and looking back, is fit for the kingdom of

God". The kingdom of Patriotism requires only those who hold fast and always look forward.

Episode Three EMOCRACY is "government by the people-exercised directly by them as a whole or their elected agents". Do we qualify? 

The United States holds its professed democracy by a republic but the system is represented by the Kingdom of Great Britain and its dependencies, the Republics of 

France and Switzerland and the

Sovereignties of Holland, Belgium and the Scandinavian countries.

The Versailles Treaty has placed 

Czecho-Slovakia, Poland and other smaller former monarchies in a most equivocal situation as very recent crises have proven. Not by any elasticity of imagination can the North and South American pseudo-republics be identified as democracies.

Taking present world history, it would appear as though the British Empire and its dependencies were the leaders in genuine democracy. 

Does not the British parliament set a real example of stabilized and orderly government without the slightest trammels on debate and discussion? No longer is Britain slowed down or hampered by the "gentlemen's club" the Peerage.

These fine and well pedigreed lords have been allowed to slumber on in practically restful oblivion. The removal of their ancient privileges was but a sign of progressive and successful democracy. 

History shows many republics limited or otherwise but the average duration has been short. 

DEMOCRACY 

The United States has lasted little more than a century and a half as such on professed democracy, but are the signs those of progression or collapse? At the outset of this Episode the question is "Do we qualify?" A "check-up" is timely. 

Is this a government of the people in reality? Does democracy imply a government of all of the people, or of some? Is it not a fact that from President down to

Alderman our system has become so involved that we have a vast "Circumlocution Office"? We are not clear but what the old-fashioned convention plan was more representative than the primary election process. The former, at least, developed live leaders and unafraid to speak their convictions before all. 

What chance has the humble and unknown citizen to shape the customary election? The ward-heelers early begin to select their candidates for the common offices and easily secure nominating petitions for filing and placing on the ballot. The occasional attempt to file independent petitions is viewed as brazen interference. Thus the lowly citizen simply steps up to the ballotbooth to vote for the group candidate: or, give up in disgust.

We have taken more time to mention the bottom-round because all the way up this system prevails with greater skill in proportion to the value of the office. As one goes higher we are confronted with the

ugly word "patronage". If the machine-made candidate wins, how many offices are at his disposal; what are they worth; what are the prospects of additional emoluments? Never in the history of this republic have there been such wholesale "inducements" to regiment thousands of voters and for what purpose?

The insatiable greed and lust of office: to remain in power at any price and continue as petty tyrants. The Episode on 

Patriotism necessarily weaves into this subject as well.

If our republic continues to move in the present direction, then the hour is at hand when disaster will come. If leaders are to be chosen by cliques, cabals, secret groups, underhand conspiracies and class organization, then we shall cease being a democracy and become fettered and throttled by some form of dictatorship to which reference is made in a later Episode.

What means the enormous enlargement of state and federal employees except the ultimate use of their ballots to retain in power these petty bosses, party chieftains and higher legislators? Balance any budget in the face of these undeniable facts? A combination of the present backbreaking program must end in state and national bankruptcy and that will spell what? Revolution! 

Scores of our good people are merry and joke about some of the "workers" on civic and other "projects" because they see little evidence of labor. Are these "workers" at fault? By no means. In dire need and distress they are readily recruited for some of the most fantastic and grotesque services ever dreamed of. During the past few years "projects" have been developed that never have or can be of lasting value to the land as a whole. The writer thinks it an unspeakable affront to pauperize whole masses of people in order to use them for extending the curse of unbridled ambition and power.

Relief our people in millions have needed, but the straightforward and wholesome method should have been through state and local boards wholly apart from political pressure. What we say has no bearing on one party more than another. The present plan is degrading, humiliating and enervating. Honest direct relief, handled by competent and sympathetic agents, not interested in an applicant's creed or party, would more quickly cut down the load than in any other way and help the needy maintain self-respect. Those thus aided would the more quickly seek their former or other employment.

The answer, however, is that the democracy-destroying demagogues want the long rolls continued and padded. 

It was recently published that federal employees had reached peak proportion. Why? Not merely added bureaus, groups and committees because of more work but the payroll would appear maintained because of involved and time-consuming questionnaires. For example: any accounting firm of repute in New York City or else-

where could simplify income tax blanks corporate or individual 

so that a layman could quickly grasp the situation and easily complete; the existing forms make necessary armies of clerks in Washington and elsewhere. But democracy must yield to party expediency.

If business, large or small, had to manage its affairs by the methods usually employed in city, state and federal offices, it would finally collapse. Some may say many of these chiefs and leaders are not always cognizant of the management details. We prefer to believe they have the seared conscience which does not care to be troubled by awkward questions or fussy meddlers. As we have pointed out, elsewhere, there are noble and notable exceptions as relating to each Episode, but we must judge by the mass and our daily experiences and revelations, hence the question of our democracy is a grave issue and worthy of the study of the best citizenry.

Episode Four 

CAPITAL AND AND LABOR 

THIS caption to me has always seemed offensive. It arouses the class-spirit and fans the smoldering sparks into flaming antagonism. 

We use the phrase the better to attack its inconsistency. After all, is not Capital "labor" and Labor "capital"? Whoever drew the line, first established class-war and its sequelae bitterness, misunderstanding, prejudice and even hatred To always identify wealth as "capital" is untrue and dangerous. 

The Scriptures have much to say against the rich man who abuses his wealth and misuses his position, but Christ plainly says: "The poor ye always have with you" and, doubtless we always will, notwithstanding the blatant outgivings of the professional poverty-abolitionists. Wealth itself may be innocuous, for it is the principle of its acquisition or disposition which determines the result. The myriads of poor are in as great peril of condemnation as the few rich.

One of the most misquoted passages of Scripture is that of St. Paul to Timothy, "For the love of money is the root of all evil". Usually the sentence begins with "money".

There, again, wealth of itself is proven harmless, therefore, we emphasize the "love" of it in the heart is the peril. Some at the bottom stratum of society without money spend years scheming how to get wealth with utter disregard as to the means employed. 

To return to our argument as to the evil of drawing a line between Capital and Labor may we enquire by what logic the masses constantly proclaim Labor to mean brawn, muscle and sinew? Why are business leaders, scientific workers, research experts and commercial engineers excluded, so commonly, from Labor? Is the latter an aristocracy of mere manpower and the former considered as dilletante hoarders? All too frequently the college youth has a battle to prove he is, or may be, as great a producer as the man using two fists. 

There are those who almost lay claim to belong to Labor because of overalls and grime. With such, clean clothing and a white shirt spell an adherent of Capital. The 

United States has just legislated a "Wages and Hours Bill". To use the language of its proponents "putting a floor beneath one and a ceiling over the other". All this for Labor! To these enthusiasts not a thought is ever given to the "capitalist" who may spend ten or fifteen hours in a day to help the very class which denies him the right to be included in the ranks of Labor.

Engaged in a momentous business discussion, a visitor was seated in the office of a friend, the head of a large manufacturing plant. 

They were interrupted by the shrieking of the boiler-room siren.

The visitor asked what it was. The head of the concern said: "I need 10

no time-piece to tell me it is five o'clock if you glance from the window you will see the throngs leaving and if I were to show you through the factory in five minutes it is unlikely you would meet an employee". He closed by remarking: "Would God I could leave as they are doing, but I will be fortunate if I get away by nine." Labor, as a whole, seems to think of Capital in terms of long vacations in 

Europe, winter in Florida or the 

Riviera, handsome power yachts, luxurious ocean-front hotels, etc.

Labor now tries to pillory such men as Henry Ford and long ago swept him into the ranks of Capital, but is much thought given to his years of toil and care to create a vast new empire for the profit of the millions now employed the world over? When did he cease being a part of Labor? What made him a scape-goat and an offender? All over the industrial world are men similar to Henry 

Ford once in favor in days of struggle and poverty, but now capitalistic pariahs. 

As for the sins of Capital there is none who would apologize for them but they represent a very small percentage of the true-hearted, sincere and sympathetic corpor ate leaders. The New York Stock 

Exchange was the victim of a yellow-journal attack for the half-dozen who shamelessly betrayed their privileges and the fusillade ignored the hundreds of members who have passed through the ordeals of depression and denunciation scatheless.

Naturally, I cannot discuss Labor unless it includes what is called "Organized". So far as present statistics go, it would appear there are now about 7,000,000 in the leading unions. This, therefore, leaves nearly 30,000,000 unorganized. 

Union leadership has had little of sweet savor. Hark back to the days of Terence V. Powderly and his "Knights of Labor". Think of big Bill Haywood and his "I. W. 

W.". When initiated, the aim may have been sincere but bad management reaped their destruction in the very nature of the case. A wiser and more successful administrator was the late Samuel Gompers of the A. F. L. and the existing president William Green appears to be tending in the Gompers direction. 

The hastily begotten C. I. O. gives every evidence of bad management. Beating "organized labor" tomtoms, threatening spineless legislators and cajoling weak political leaders have placed on our federal and state law-books statutes and regulations which could not be more unfair and unjust than they are. Interpreted by boards in many cases without judicial or even practical experience, the nation's business has been prevented from the real recovery which would have been ours long ago.

It has at last reached the consciousness of even a few labor leaders that much of this legislation must be amended and even repealed if the U. S. is to have a chance. 

In August there was published a 11

list of the leading nations showing the ranking percentages of recent recovery and the United States was the laggard! What comment is required to show the lack of confidence on the part of business, large or small, in the political leaders of our land? 

Another thought: since unorganized labor appears to be nearly four times as large as organized why the attempt on the part of the latter to force unionization on the other and larger body? Glib and amusing are among the many replies. We need to enroll our "brothers" to prove the solidarity of labor. The unorganized have no chance for betterment in dealing with the "bosses". Organization brings improvements in the crafts and better separates the high-grade workmen from the unskilled. These groups can force higher wages, etc. Their platitudes are endless. 

It is past being amusing to have the organized forces attempt to prove the other group is so unintelligent as not to know what is best for them. 

Has the "collective bargaining" body the only wisdom of the workers? Presumptuous, to say the least. As a matter of fact the lust for power and pelf (union dues galore) seems to be the activating principle.

Recent attempts to create political adjuncts to the unions have not resulted in material gains. The unafraid state or city executives have been far more successful than the others. The amazing thing is to find how a comparatively small body of voters can intimidate a candidate. Beyond all else, a genuine American candidate is not to be the tool of any class, clique or group but must represent all the people of his district or commonwealth. 

The Wagner Act very "kindly" allows a worker to remain outside a union, if he chooses, but what a life it makes for him with any number of union men about! "Scab" is a filthy word, but readily applied to an honest man who abides by his convictions.

In the thirteenth chapter of the 

Apocalypse we read: "And he causeth all, both small and great, rich and poor, free and bond, to receive a mark in their right hand; or, in their foreheads: and that no man might buy or sell, save he that had the mark, or the name of the beast, or the number of his name”, from which follows Satan's number, the mystic 666. While it is true those who teach the imminent 

Second Coming of Jesus make spe cific use of this passage, nonetheless, it is quite striking when it coincides with the "closed shop". 

Unionism relies on three factors to coerce and frighten the unorganized (1) the strike (2) the picket (3) ostracism. One would think if the union had such paramount advantages it should not be difficult to persuade others to join, but it never seems to occur to the organized to attempt that friendly process. Is it not rather a sign of inherent weakness? We go farther and say that if two groups are employed in the same plant then the 12

union could readily demonstrate its superiority if there were any.

Failure to secure what it claims as its rights is followed by a strike and everything thrown into confusion by such action. Present day strike technique assumes that these men "own" their jobs and always have. The plea of "creative wealth" and thus ownership is specious, but it overlooks "creative investment" which first gave opportunity to the workers. From this assumption they proceed to the now ugly "sit-down" and "stay-in" program. "Human rights" are loudly exploited over "property rights", but who is to determine where one begins and the other ends? At any rate, consistent law must hold such demonstrators as trespassers.

In most jurisdictions, that principle has been recognized and a few determined officials have ejected these demonstrators, but, invariably, accompanied by violence and disorder. Again, if union blessings were so potent why this resistance and warfare? Many officials, while recognizing the legal aspects have weakly surrendered to supposed expediency. 

If correctly reported, no less a personage than Madame Perkins not only condoned the sit-down strike but intimated it might yet become legalized! But the strike assumes added significance when picketing begins. (Picket, by the way, is a war-word). First, the union says striking is legal, but its idea is somewhat different from many others even the legally minded. Episode Sixteen BUSINESS 

IT may be asked, is there such a being? Our study is based on a composite of at least a score of outstanding leaders in finance, industry and education, covering an extended period. If there were ever a time when real acumen and well-balanced judgment were needed in executive posts it is now. 

Success or failure largely depends on wise and skilful leadership.

Men with splendid foresight are in demand. Beset by political, social and industrial upheaval the need of masterful executives cannot be denied.

Perhaps the best method of portraying the ideal head is to show the opposite and then draw our conclusions. Some executives live in advance of their enterprises and blaze the way for the organizations. 

Others merely keep step with the business and many, alas, are falling behind and seem to wonder what is going on. It is fairly easy to predict the outcome in each case.

There is a class of executives who fondly believe they can keep every detail of management under their own hats. This group will spend long hours at their desk and trifle with details that properly belong away down the line. Long hours do not always indicate good administration. Some are able in a day to set in successful motion more than others could accomplish in a week. The revolutions of the 

THE IDEAL EXECUTIVE 

clock-hands are not as important as the well-ordered adjustments of the brain. 

Some executive heads explain with gusto that they welcome suggestions from their junior officers and supervisory staff, even if the opinions advanced are directly contrary to their own views. Many are sincere in inviting these views, but some resent suggestions. Many an executive head has by his genuine lack of frankness caused a real loss to his organization. "A burnt child dreads the fire," and even a polite rebuff to a young and ambitious official will cause him to hesitate on future suggestions. There are executives who pride themselves on being good "mixers" and yet never learn the art of unbending and fraternizing. There are others who easily cultivate real comradeship and sympathetic understanding: these are they who know the value of a kindly slap on the shoulder and the virtue of calling a chap by his Christian name.

In many instances it is quite unlikely that much of the issue of "collective bargaining" and lately affirmed labor legislation would have come into existence had the principle of real brotherhood and equality been genuinely understood.

By the growth of enormous corporations it has become impossible for the executive heads to fellowship with the "rank and file" but, next 42

to that, the maintenance of genuine cordiality must be cultivated with the junior officers, supervising officials and departmental chiefs. 

They are, after all, the contact men who deal with the thinking, feeling, operating army.

Among others of the unwise executives are those who fail to recognize that age and length of service are invaluable assets. All things being equal, the judgment of such employees is worth heeding. In last year's debates relative to the Supreme Court it was discovered that the "Nine Old Men" had an immense following. The talk of "young blood" needs to be tempered by the fact that only age gives experience. Professor Pitkin says "Life Begins At Forty" and supplements by telling us of possible "careers" after that age. Why not?

Another unwise executive is the man who properly delegates the largest responsibility to his immediate subordinates and yet tries to keep a "string" on their simplest acts and frequently surprises them by annoying inquiries involving the most trivial transactions. The skilful and acute executive is the one who gladly hands over a task with the desire to hear no more about it. But one may interject that junior executives sometimes make mistakes. Quite true, and so do the executives! Errors in any business are but the natural process of growth and must be charged to "gain and loss", even of experience if not in actual dollars. There are some executives who think occasional employment of "efficiency experts" or "personnel directors" will remedy any organizational defects, real or imagined. 66 

Many an "efficiency expert" has retired by demonstrating his own inefficiency and there have been personnel directors " whose achievements might have improved farm labor. It was never any evidence of genius to have a peripatetic expert go into an organization and "fire" helpless clerks or other employees, and thus cut the payroll. 

These cuts have often brought costly reactions. 

There is a class of supervisory official which frequently run about asking irresponsible juniors the same question which has already been answered by the supposed "right-hand" man. This attitude is harmful and betrays lack of confidence in the one most to be trusted. 

Another administrative error has been the belief that men are merely cogs in the wheel of industry and business need only "standardize".

This word has been overworked. 

There is still plenty of room for individualism and personal initiative. Many administrative heads have been beguiled by charts and graphs into the idea that their operations, costs, etc., ought invariably to coincide with the maps. It is an error to judge individual units, even carrying on the same business, by the sole rule of the graph. As a matter of fact, each corporation is in a large sense "a law unto itself". Charts are valuable as a means of study but the individual 43

unit must consider the territory of operation, means of transportation, housing, proximity of raw material, racial trends and the general possibilities.

Perhaps imperfectly the writer has sketched the negative side of the "ideal business executive".

These factual considerations may not have outlined what the model administrator should be. At any rate, business requires men of large vision, socially-minded, absolutely fair and square men who are called by the poet "sun-crowned". 

Men who know that their staffs, "white-collar" and all, are to be viewed as having a fair balance with stockholders who realize dividends in contented workers are as important as dividends in cash. 44
