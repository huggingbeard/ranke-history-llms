LAOS ANTI-NARCOTICS DRIVE OPENS WITH SEMINAR [Article: "The Anti-Narcotics Drive Is Getting Organized"; 

Vientiane, Agence Lao Presse, French, 30 October 1971, pp

A3-A4] 

Vientiane The first seminar on narcotics control, organized by the national police, opened this morning under the chairmanship of Lith Lunammachack, director general, in the presence of Nith Singharath, chairman of the interministerial commission for the application of the anti-narcotics law, controller second class Chanthaboun Luangraj, the deputy director general, top-level personnel of the Ministry of Interior, and several members of the interministerial commission. 

The purpose of this seminar, which is to last for 3 weeks, is to familiarize the responsible authorities with the techniques tested abroad in the area of an anti-narcotics drive.

The second goal to be reached will consist in promoting close cooperation among the services involved for the complete and systematic application of Law 71/5 which is in effect and which bears on "the prohibition to grow poppies throughout the Kingdom of Laos or to process, consume, or trade opium and its derivatives."

The participants in the seminar total 45 and are members of city police and criminal police forces as well as of all the various provincial commissariats of the kingdom. 

For 3 weeks the seminar's participants will attend courses and will become initiated in the field in methods of investigation and interrogation under the direction of instructors specialized in the drive against narcotics.

---

In the course of the opening session this morning, controller third class Khamseng Vorasarn outlined the seminar's program in a few words and introduced the 45 participants. 

Lith Lunammachack in turn, in the course of a lengthy address, underscored the dangers of drugs and stressed the imperious need for close cooperation among all departments of the police and Interpol [International Police] to fight this "20th century plague." 2662

ARGENTINA MARIHUANA SEIZED BY POLICE IN ROSARIO [Article; Buenos Aires, La Nacion, Spanish, 12 November 1971, p 5] 

Rosario. Two important operations were carried out by the police and in one of them they managed to seize a large quantity of drugs. Shortly afterward they arrested a youth who was the owner of a plot of marihuana. 

The official operations were performed by brigades of the public morality section. In the La Florida district Leopoldo Tomio, a 23-year-old Italian, single, and Norberto Guaglia, 29, Argentine, were arrested and a plastic bag with cigarettes made of Indian hemp, also known as marihuana, found in the possession of the latter.

Guaglia told police that he had bought the seeds for the weed in Buenos Aires and that he planted them in Funes on the farm of a relative. 

When the police arrived at the farm the marihuana crop had been destroyed. 

These arrests are related with an incident which took place in the Alberdi district some days ago. A police brigade approached a certain spot in the neighborhood where they were keeping a house under close surveillance. 

When the police arrived they were met with gunfire. There were no casualties and the occupants fled through the rear of the house. When the police entered the house they found drugs and other items. The police, after evaluating the materials found there, concluded that the persons who had escaped were criminals linked to the band which killed Subofficer Acosta and Agent Estrada in the jurisdiction of the 7th precinct about a month ago. NEWS BRIEFS BOLIVIA DRUG CONTROL 

La Paz, Presencia, Spanish, 31 Oct 71, p 6

According to Bolivia's Interpol chief Maj Ormachea, his organization is conducting investigations to determine where drug consumption centers are located in Oruro and other cities of the nation's interior. Maj 

Ormachea is traveling to Cochabamba to investigate the reported existence of marihuana farms. He also stated that narcotics control centers would be established in department capitals so that the illegal manufacture of cocaine would be eliminated. CHILE CITIZENS, POLICE LAUNCH CAMPAIGN AGAINST MARIHUANA-USING YOUTH 

Article; Santiago, Chile, Tercera de la Hora, Spanish, 14 October 1971,

P 217 More than 100 minors and adolescents of both sexes, possessing 50 kilos of marijuana, stimulants, and other drugs, were recently captured by a combined force of Carabineros and investigation officers in spectacular roundups. In the capital, the cleanup campaign against the Silo elements, hippies, and bushy-haired vagabonds was begun by the residents of the sector north of the San Miguel commune themselves. Their attack on the marijuana users was quite violent, causing incidents of such proportions that the police were obliged to intervene with tear gas. 

In Valparaiso the police offensive was without precedent, and in the Vina del Mar sector alone, hippies and siloists fell into the hands of the Investigators. In Rancagua the cleanup campaign was handled by the Carabineros, who found 50 youths smoking marijuana in the "Campo

Lindo" sector. The group of young people included girls from the Girls' 

School, the Business Institute, and Sacred Heart High School. 

In a nutshell, this organized attempt at eliminating the deterioration of our youth and compelling parents to assume responsibility for their children has resulted in over 100 arrests and the seizure of as much as 50 kilos of marajuana, stimulants, and other drugs. 

The most serious incidents occurred around the Carlos Valdovinos tenancy in the sector north of the San Miguel commune when some 40 enraged inhabitants decided to go out and rid their area of vagabonds, hippies, and potheads. The confrontation began shortly after 2100 hours and grew to such proportions that Carabinero intervention was required, necessitating complete intervention in order to separate the fighters. 

In the midst of the battle where all kinds of striking weapons were used the most afflicted were the hippies, who were finally forced to flee. Nevertheless, several were captured, and in one hippie's possession no less than 15 kilos of marijuana was found. Among the seized -8 

- 15.

were Rene Bobadilla, 19 years old, who said that they were planning a soul music festival in Isabel Riquelme Plaza when the residents appeared and attacked them. Others arrested were Hugo Enrique San Martin 

Espinoza and Octavio Segundo Carpa Olguin. 

On the police scene in several communities of San Miguel and others in Santiago, the idea of inhabitants organizing into groups to pursue hippies and marijuana users as the only defense of the integrity of their homes has spread.

Valparaiso Valparaiso (Luis Azocar) 

In an unprecedented offensive, begun early Saturday and carried on through Wednesday morning yesterday along the Vina del Mar coast, 50 marijuana users fell into the hands of the police. All captives, under 18 years old and from well-to-do districts of Santiago, were placed under the jurisdiction of the Fourth Criminal Court, along with an undetermined quantity of cannabis seed, stimulant pills, soporifics, and pamphlets from the so-called Youth Power. 

The wave of hippies spread over Ciudad Jardin last Wednesday afternoon, where a sizeable number of youths were noted seated in public plazas and walkways or lying on the sand all along the bathing beaches north toward Quintero; they were also at Las Torpederas of Valparaiso. 

Although there were no incidents and the announced march in support of Silo did not take place, the hippies caused the police worry, and beginning early Friday a special Carabinero and investigation duty was on hand to combat the sale of stupefacients. The hunting party was also closely concerned with one of the strangest crimes this area has seen in recent years -- the assassination of a small boy in El Recreo, and enigma which grows more complicated as time goes on. 

Fifty-nine arrested youths were placed under the jurisdiction of the Fourth Criminal Court, and according to the magistrate's orders, they will be personally handed over to their parents. The police also disclosed that the "floating population of drug addicts" obliged traffickers to go to greater lengths in peddling their goods, and so in addition to the varied gamut of stimulants, a specific one was added which the police did not wish to comment on; according to the hippies it has been selling for 100 to 200 escudos for a bottle of 40 capsules.

Commercially, this medicine which is used as an hallucinogen for addicts is worth only 8 escudos. Rancagua

Rancagua (Gabriel Canton, correspondent) day 52 students and youths accompanied by their respective parents appeared before the Second Local Court. They had been arrested by police from the Machali substation at 1730 hours last Tuesday at the 

- 16 -

place called "Campo Lindo" where they were found in lush green surroundings devoting themselves to smoking marijuana. The arrested youths, who had been placed under the court's jurisdiction by their own parents, were taken to the police unit some 3 kilometers from the site, where their place of residence had to be verified, and then they were handed over to their parents. Among the marijuana users were 47 boys, students from the Men's Night School. There were only five girls, students from the Business Institute, the Girls' School, and Sacred Heart High School. 

The arrest of the 52 marijuana users was the culmination of a subtle campaign by the Carabineros. They put out a dragnet to detect these groups, finding that for a month a number of teenagers had been meeting here at "Campo Lindo, a beautiful place situated at the bottom of the foothills, south of the so-called Copper Highway, to smoke and do other things. The affair took on an aspect of major seriousness when young girls associated with this drug-addict movement centered around Machali were announced as having run away from home in two reports to the local police inspector headquarters.

The chief suspect is 32-year old Dr Rigobert M. from Uganda, who was arrested in a boarding house. All the people arrested are said to be artists and students, most of whom have no fixed abode in West Berlin. The narcotics seized -- a total of 16 kilograms of marijuana were brought into Berlin from Nairobi, and are said to have a value of roughly 25,000 marks. 

The customs authorities had suspected for some time that the narcotics smugglers had found a new route, because traffic via the GDR airport at Schoenefeld had become too risky. At Tempelhof airport on Sunday, the attention of a customs officer was drawn to a 29-year old Congolese who said that he had nothing to declare.

When the official indicated that he wished to examine his suitcase, the Congolese pretended to have difficulties in understanding the language and disappeared with suspicious haste. Officials thereupon examined the suitcase with great care and noticed the "smell of mothballs" typical of narcotics. In fact, they found 15 kilograms of marijuana in the suitcase. 

As the owner could not possibly know of their discovery, the customs officials decided on a ruse: they closed the suitcase and put it with other checked baggage. 

Their calculation proved correct: 6 hours later, the African returned, picked up his suitcase and was just walking off with it when he was arrested.

WEST GERMANY FRG LEGAL AND POLICE ACTIONS AGAINST NARCOTICS POSSESSION [Article; Hanburg, Die Welt, German, 4 November 1971, p 24] 

One Ton Hashish Confiscated; Three Turks Arrested 

Tuesday night two policemen confiscated one ton of hashish with a market value of five million marks after a shoot-out in an abandoned gravel pit near Mannheim. The narcotic, in 20 jute sacks, was loaded on a Turkish semi-trailer which two automobiles from Garmish-Partenkirchen and Heidelberg had led to the lonely spot on the Rhine between Altlussheim and Neulussheim. 

According to the report of the North Baden Land police director's office, this was the largest amount of hashish which one has so far been able to confiscate at one time in the Federal Republic. Three of the smugglers were arrested, two are still at large. 

The police owe their booty to the attentiveness of a man who lives near the gravel pit. Around 2100 o'clock he had seen the semi-trailer and the two cars drive by. He noted that suddenly all the lights went out on the vehicles and that they turned into a side road which leads to the gravel pit. The resident became suspicious. He telephoned the police station in 

Hockenheim and reported his observations. A patrol car was immediately sent out, The two policemen hid their vehicle near the gravel pit and crept to within approximately 10 meters of the semi-trailer and the two cars. The narcotics smugglers, who were in the process of unloading several sacks, discovered the policemen at the same moment and began to shoot at them. Both policemen returned fire. After they each gave a warning shot they aimed at the vehicles. As was subsequently determined, however, none of the smugglers was wounded during the nocturnal gun battle. The policemen were also not hit. 

When one of the smugglers Pled, a policeman, covered by his colleague, followed him. The other four smugglers also tried to escape in the darkness after they had apparently shot all the bullets in their pistols. During the course of the night it was possible to capture three. They were Turkish citizens between 30 and 40 years of age. Interpol, the Federal Criminal Office, and the customs authorities have been called into the investigation, which is being carried out by the Mannheim public prosecutor's office. 7909

WEST GERMANY TEACHERS TO BE FAMILIARIZED WITH DRUG PROBLEMS [Article by Hans-Werner Loose; Hamburg, Die Welt, German, 6 November 1971,p 3] 

The state of Northern Rhineland-Westphalia is searching for ways to effectively stem the tide of drug addiction in its schools. As a first measure, the Minister for Public Worship and Education has issued a directive, according to which 2,000 teachers are to be familiarized with this problem by attending special courses. The information gap between teachers and students is to be closed as rapidly as possible. 

No clouds of cannabis smoke roll through the assembly halls of the schools in six Rhine and Ruhr cities and the riflemen's hall in Siegburg, while teachers take the courses programmed by Ministerial Councillor Walter. "White Afghan," "red Lebanese," "light Turk," or "dark blue Pakistani," all being various grades of hashish may be viewed under glass in the anteroom. Getting a fix is confined to the screen while a 28-minute movie entitled "Druge" is running.

Dr Wolfgang Chneck, a teacher until a few weeks ago, now scientificeducational collaborator of the Duesseldorf state institute for teacher training, describes the course of studies in drug addiction as a pilot program for the Federal Republic Chneck gives one of the reasons for the lack of information: "Unfortunately is problem has caught us at the time when school reform is at the center of attention and preoccupies all educators." 

Few qualified lecturers are available on the subject of "joints" and "trip."; research has not yet arrived at a unanimous judgment; the drug trade flourishes as never before. recently as 2 years ago, the narcotics division of the Northern Rhineland-Westphalia state police consisted of a part-time employee; now there are three officials and two filing clerks. In 1970, 3,000 offenses against the narcotics law were the subject of criminal prosectuions in this, the most populous federal state; in the first 9 months of this year alone, the numbers had risen to 5,000. The share of 14-18 year olds jumped from 733 to 1,695. 

The figure of hashish smoking college students rose very slightly, but that of - 67 -

high school students caught with "dream drugs" rose from 435 in 1 year to 688 in 9 months. By comparison with 113 kilograms of cannabis seized last year, 478 kilograms were impounded by September 1971.

The comparable figures for hard drugs leading to physical addiction are a particular worry to criminal investigation personnel and physicians: the quantity of raw opium seized rose from 33 grams to 6.2 kilograms; that of morphine from 13 grams to 640 grams; and for the first time seven heroin capsules found their way to the police store. 

The problem is no longer confined to the cities only. Werner Figgen, Minister for Labor, Health, and Social Affairs, says that the share of rural juveniles is rising.

High schools, once denounced as the only strongholds of hashish, were merely the forerunners. Wolfgang Ohneck from the state institute for teacher training feels that the danger is much greater in the vocational and middle schools. He knows that even 12-year olds are to be found among the customers of the pushers who recently have been exploiting group psychology meetings and tempt the class spokesmen with joints and LSD.

Students at vocational high schools earn money but, for the most part, are not as judicious as high school students when it comes to "the stuff." 

Ohneck's apprehensions are founded in fact: "Many vocational students do not know the differences in quality of drugs and thereby multiply the danger to themselves." This same problem now encountered with wage earning students will, in Ohneck's opinion, soon have to be faced with regard to the middle schools.

The teachers to be trained in the problems of drug abuse will attend short courses; after that they are not to bore their pupils in special periods devoted to the minutiae of Indian hemp. Instead they are to touch on, and discuss, the dangers of narcotics in the course of teaching various subjects. During a 1-day meeting of educators, it was suggested that a "confidential teacher" should be available at each school a kind of father confessor at the desk who would not feel obliged to call in the police, who would counsel, not punish. 

For December two seminars are planned, in which 30 educators and school supervisory personnel will work together with physicians, psychologists, and social workers on a graduated catalogue of procedures. The product of their deliberations on narcotics which are to last 2 1/2 days each -will be published and distributed to all teachers in the state as an aid to orientation.
