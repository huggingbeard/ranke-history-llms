It must be admitted at the outset that no conclusive proof of the date has yet been discovered. The weight of the evidence, however, seems to favor the belief that the original section constructed in 1695 still stands, and that this, and the addition built about 1712, constitute the present building. There have been some subsequent changes, notably the lowered ministers' and elders' gallery, the low ceiling over the North section and the plaster coating on the outside walls. 

But the essential structure has not been altered since the second decade of the eighteenth century. 1

Against Dr. Smith's arguments, we submit the following:

The Meeting House does appear to have been built in two sections with one section both smaller and more primitive than the other. Some of the reasons for this belief are (1) the ground plan, (2) The position of the shutters, (3) the wainscoting and (4) the blocked up window or door.

The present ground plan of the Meeting House is cruciform. 

That such would have been planned deliberately by the Friends of that time is almost unthinkable. As far as we know, it is the only case of an old Meeting House in this form in the world. 

The design could easily have originated from the addition of a new part to the original, which probably is what occurred.

The Friends would not have put that date stone there if there had been in their minds the slightest doubt of the accuracy of the dates given! This, of course, raises the question of what evidence they may have had at that time that we do not have today. The Welsh are noted for the accuracy of their traditions. The time lapse from 1695 to 1829 is not too great for accurate group memory. We naturally wonder also, what documentary evidence may have disappeared since 1829. 

There are known to us today eight or nine pictures of Merion Meeting House made prior to about 1865. Of these, seven show the south gable with what would appear to be either a date stone or a recess where a date stone may formerly have been. Most of these pictures indicate a recess rather than a stone. That which is probably the oldest of them all is in the English edition of Robert 

Sutcliff's "Travels in America" (York — 1811). The author says that he sent home to England a drawing of Merion Meeting House, so that this illustration is presumably based on Sutcliff's own sketch. 

It definitely shows the stone or recess. However Sutcliff in his printed work does not refer to it as he probably would have done had the stone been in position to be read when he was here. Several such stones have been stolen in this vicinity. Perhaps this had already been taken. But still the recollection of its inscription might well have been the basis for the date given on the stone of 1829.

CONCLUSION 

The study of the Preparative Meeting records for this period, somewhat conflicting though they may seem upon superficial reading, lead to the net conclusion that the Friends here are dealing not with a mere log cabin but with a building of real value. They want "hookes" for the windows. (To keep the shutters open and to prevent them from banging as now?) They are building and locking a closet for Friends' books. They are anxious that the building be "secured." They are fitting it up with benches. It is probable that it is the same size as the present back end because what appear to be the same benches are still in use though altered by the addition of backs and ends. There is a loft. The old subscriptions are not all paid. They are talking in February about an addition to the building-not a replacement. In April they are to "see for stones to build a meeting house." It this merely an inept expression referring to the addition? Is the contemplated addition of 1704 the one that appears finally to have been completed about 1713. It is very noticeable that in the expenditure of the £267 of 1712-14 there is mention of carpenters, shingles, nails, etc., but no mention of masons or of stones. 

Who knows whether the bulk of this money is for the meeting house or whether it may be in part at least for horse sheds? 

It begins to look as if along about 1705 or 1706 or perhaps even later the Friends had started to build their addition. Perhaps they built the stone walls and then left it unfinished. In 1711 

Cadwalader Morgan signed his will leaving twenty pounds for the work. However, he arranged things so that the money would not come into the possession of the meeting until they had made the new part of the meeting house actually "a building to the said use." Perhaps this will is rather a gladfly to sting them into activity to finish the work than an indication that a meeting house is in mere contemplation, as Dr. Smith would have us believe. 

All during this period the women-and for all we know the men too-have been meeting regularly in this building. Evidence regarding the men's meeting during certain years has perished or been lost. Marriages have been held regularly in this house in almost every year during the period under discussion. The structure whose year is given on the date stone; whose date is established

by the sudden use of it for all marriages; the house whose existence is definitely named in the humble cleaning item in the women's minutes has been used constantly since that time. It has been added to and altered, but it still stands to welcome to its worship any who wish sincerely to seek for truth in the love of God and man.

In this spirit the Friends of Merion Meeting are striving to interpret the will of our Heavenly Father in terms of our lives today.

We carry on the following activities to participate in which an invitation is cordially extended: 

Meeting for Worship is held every First-day (Sunday). It is based on silent prayer and meditation. It is an effort to approach our Father in the spirit of reverent search for truth, and to feel 

His Presence in the Soul.

In conjunction with our Meeting for Worship, we occasionally hold a series of conferences on important topics for the purposes of information and of inspiration.

Our First Day School endeavors to give a sound and liberal interpretation of Christianity and to inculcate love toward God and peace and good will among men. 

To complete our program for the boys of our community, we sponsor Cub Pack Merion 113 which is still in the experimental stage and our Boy Scout Troop, Merion 2 which has already rendered some service to boyhood.
