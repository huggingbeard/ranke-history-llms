"There's no sense in going further-it's the edge of cultivation.'

So they said, and I believed it-broke my land and sowed my crop, Built my barns and strung my fences in the little border station

Tucked away below the foothills where the trails run out and stop: "Till a voice, as bad as Conscience, rang interminable changes 

On an everlasting Whisper day and night repeated-so: 'Something lost behind the Ranges. Lost and waiting for you. Go!'"

The fact that there seems so little left on this physical planet for the adventurous spirit to conquer, constitutes a challenge to those who have the courage to "Go and find it. Go and look behind the Ranges."

Among the masses of humanity, who are in this world for so short a time, the balance swings heavily to those countless men, women and chilxi

dren who, because the stress of existence surges over them, cannot seem to gain control of circumstances, and fail to find or follow the trail.

This book is written with the hope that its readers may reach a better understanding of their personal relation to Life by evaluating their individual potentialities for Happiness, which is herein termed the system of "Egonalysis." 99 

Ex-President Eliot of Harvard has illustrated how much might be accomplished by fifteen minutes' study a day, which represents approximately a hundredth part of all the time there is. If an individual wishes to be happy, would it not be worth while for him to spend at least this small fraction of his allotted time studying and systematically checking his life? The psychologist tells us that "Life is largely a matter of habit." Are any of us too old to gamble no more than one penny of our daily "time dollar" in forming (or re-forming) habits which should help us to attain every man's rightful goal-Happiness?

The author explains herein the mechanics of "Egonalysis" in simple every-day terms and in story form. The reader who will undertake to apportion time and effort to study his own case will be surprised at the new and happier outlook on life he will be able to gain. xii

There is nothing supernatural or mystic about this system. It may be applied to men, women, or children of any age or creed. It is merely a systematic summing up of the various forces which influence daily life, a digest of our findings in visual form. Gerald Sanford settled back in his comfortable office chair, the weighty matters of the present seeming far removed from the thoughts that were running paramount in his mind. The evening before he had spent in the company of a neurologist, and Sanford had always been prone to think of neurologists as brothers-under-the-skin to medlers. Since he had been forced into neurological company without any choice in the matter, he could not seem to escape the impression that this dynamic little man with the twinkling eyes and radiant smile "had something." "Oh well," he thought, as he signed the letter he had just written to his new friend, Dr. Starbuck, "I have an important conference at 2:40; just enough time for forty winks if my secretary calls me at 2:20.”

So Sanford dozed off and the letter he had just signed fell to the floor.

Leaning lazily against the tree, feeling that it was too much effort to get up, Sanford turned the page. Here in diagram form appeared various ego-charts, and even his own with the percentages marked opposite each item. "So this is what Dr. Starbuck meant when he talked of using graphs and percentages to keep a personal record of Happiness. For some time he studied the map and charts, especially those elements that related to his own position. "So this is what Dr. Starbuck meant by 'Egonalysis'-'to take stock.' Why that has been an every-day term with me for years. My business requires the keeping of accurate stock reports, balance sheets, profit and loss statements; and I use the best system of charts and

graphs obtainable, covering each of my various business interests. However, is it possible that 

I have neglected 'to take stock' of my personal happiness? But, who ever heard of a standard of Happiness? Happiness to me would probably mean boredom to the next fellow. Yet, Dr. Starbuck has told me of some interesting Egonalysis cases. Perhaps it would be worth while to try this system seriously.' 

Puzzled and intrigued, Sanford decided "to take stock" of himself, but first he would analyze and compare some of the charts before him and get to the basis of the system. 

He started making mental notes of points which interested him most, feeling assured that he could soon uncover weaknesses in the Egonalysis system which he would show to Dr. Starbuck with an inward feeling of "I told you so," though he was far too conservative to let the Doctor know how impossible this program appeared to him. 

His first note was: "What is Happiness? Is there any set standard by which to gauge one's self?"

Surely everyone should know elementary bookkeeping and how to keep personal accounts well balanced, and anyone should know how much of one's income should be apportioned to each column." 

He found no complications in this item. "But,' he reflected, "this deals with figures and they're cut and dried. It will be different, and not so simple a matter, with some of the other factors." 99 

Work: The second item piqued him. "Why should he 'take stock' of his work? He had been a hard worker ever since his father's death had forced him to leave college. He had always enjoyed his work, too, and had contributed his fair portion to the world's goods, but now he could afford to 'lay off' somewhat, and he rather liked it." 

He could understand the first two ratings each being 90 per cent. 

Recreation: Strange as it seemed to him now, he had never given much thought to play. He was inclined to wonder why he had been rated only 60 per cent. He closed his eyes and began to evaluate his recreations. It was the first time he had thought of this side of his life as a sepa-

rate unit, and it was surprising to recall the tremendous amount of energy he had expended on recreation and what a slight amount of intelligence had been used in planning it. Life had become so complex that his recreation required much planning and expensive equipment and he had lost sight of the meaning of the word in unconsciously striving for amusement. Recreate to rebuild; yes-that was his troublehe worked so hard at recreation that often he actually tore down more than he built up. In the future he would give more thought to this item and plan his play-time to get the fullest value out of it. The old saying came to his mind: "Work hard while you work, play hard while you play, but do neither to excess."

Diet: It was easy to check this fourth item as 

80 per cent. He ate substantial food, not often too much; and drank intoxicants very sparingly. 

Exercise: This item, of course, had quite a deal to do with number four, and 70 per cent was a just percentage on this score, for recently it had become too easy to excuse himself from his exercises if the slightest opportunity arose; he would "jack himself up" on this too. 

Rest: Sanford had never had trouble sleeping, but he was surprised to see that his rating on this score was only 70 per cent. At first, he

would have thought it would add up to 100 per cent. Recreation, Exercise and Rest he knew were interrelated, but why should he fall so short of a general high average? Of course, he had recently acquired the habit of playing bridge at the Club in the late afternoons, missing his customary rides in the park; and the combination of less exercise and fresh air, not to mention the many evening social engagements, were cutting into his usual hours of sleep and dissipating his energies; but it had been a gradual, almost unconscious change of habit. Perhaps this "Egonalysis System of taking stock" would be worth giving serious thought to. Certainly he never would have allowed the standards of his business interests to deteriorate from lax habits, nor could he imagine himself accepting a business statement of such importance which painted so disturbing a picture without trying to do something about bettering it.

His rating on the first section of the "Egonalysis Chart"-his personal average-was 58 per cent. That would stand improvement. Now he was anxious to get to the percentage of his Conscience. It salved his feeling of resentment at so poor a showing to think that in this next section he would be able to retort vigorously and easily prove that his rating under this item had been miscalculated.

Looking over the Egonalysis Chart of his Conscience rating, Sanford felt that it had been grossly under-rated. He considered himself a very public-spirited man, a generous giver, and -here he had to stop, for when he analyzed his contributions to society, he admitted to himself that they could all be summed up in monetary gifts, made without sacrifice on his part. 

He knew very little of the church or of the various activities to which he contributed; he had been too busy to interest himself personally in the conduct of their affairs. He had felt that his contributions to the budget were sufficient cooperation and when they had been made he had dismissed them from his mind.

Sanford was being aroused to discover whether there were other ways in which he could do something constructive. As he thought this matter over, there were so many avenues open to him

that he realized it would require serious consideration to decide which one would be most beneficial to humanity as a whole. At this point he began materializing his desire for happiness. 

He had used little actual planning in connection with his personal life. Strange too, for every opportunity of making his happiness score 100 per cent had been his, but here he was with half of his life gone, and he had to admit that his chart average of 53 per cent was very nearly correct. A chance saying came to him: "The man who lives to himself lives a small life." 

Perhaps that had been the basis of his trouble. 

It was not intentional, but nevertheless was evident, that most of his energies had been expended on Self, and his life, although full of possibilities, was literally a "small life." He recalled that Dr. Starbuck had said that the majority of maladjusted people who came to him for help could trace the origin of their trouble to self-adulation or self-pity in some form. Sanford saw where he had failed to coordinate, cooperate and compensate. 

Even success, when attained, is not always satisfying, for one can only use a certain amount of anything for one's self and then the Law of Balance demands that you share it with others or you become satiated. As he realized how poor his gifts to Society had been, he reached a bet-

ter understanding of his personal relationship to life, its potentialities, and the truly happier life attainable by unselfish living.

Looking out, his eyes followed a mountain stream, sometimes disappearing amongst the trees and then emerging once more, always growing in size until it watered the valley below. His Conscience began to awaken to the great responsibility that was his, due to his advantages and position in life. 

For the first time in his life, Gerald Sanford, born into money, surrounded by all the luxuries and comforts of life, began to analyze the value of wealth in its relationship to Happiness.

The real power of wealth to make one happy is a conscientious investment, not for further material gain, but toward constructive assistance to mankind. Clearly this would not mean charity in the sense of organized giving, as he had understood it, but rather the intelligent application of brains and money to projects which would provide opportunity for the less fortunate to think for themselves and build toward a keener appreciation of life.

In wondering how he could best turn the resources at his command to such a type of constructive work, he visualized life as a great, vast library, with thousands of books on many and varied subjects, all carefully catalogued and indexed to assist those desirous of gaining specific information. There were so many interesting books in the library that Sanford realized a lifetime would not be time enough to read them all. He also knew there were thousands of people who might enjoy some of the books, but who could not use the indexes; and still others who might enjoy the contents of the books, but who could not read.

Still there were a host of people, some able to read and anxious, who had to pass by because the struggle for existence did not leave them time enough to read; and there was yet another group, who, although they had the time, had never realized the value of knowledge, and thus passed by without a thought.

Finally, there was a group who were so engrossed in reading that they lived and died, continually gaining knowledge, which they had neither time nor inclination to pass on to others, thereby becoming mere vast reservoirs of thoughts which became stagnant from want of use.

So Sanford conceived his simile of Life and knew that it would be his duty to arouse the interest of people to the possibilities of the library, to teach them to read, how to use indexes; to afford time for many, perhaps to guide them; and finally and most important of all, to keep the library and its resources before the people so that they might visualize the opportunities within their reach and continually draw from this 

Fountain of Knowledge.

Sanford believed there would not be so much unhappiness if more people learned to use the library correctly. He believed that too many used the reference books and based firm policies on them without making a sufficient study, while if they had been guided by the Egonalysis Charts they would have become less confused, for they would have drawn correct conclusions by thinking through the problems of balanced development. Fifteen Minutes a Day with Yourself

Fifteen Minutes a Day with Yourself

Happiness is defined in the dictionary as "the state of pleasurable content with one's condition in life" which at first glance might be thought to imply the gratification of every wish, but one immediately realizes that that is an impossibility. However, only a fine margin of distinction exists between the definition of Happiness and Contentment. The dictionary definition of 

Contentment being: "a state where one is not disturbed or disquieted by desire, although not every wish is gratified."

It has been said: "Only the present is ours, the past is no more and the future may not be ours. "Therefore, it is the present that must be lived to the fullest in the pursuit of Happiness. 

To make the most of the present, however, definite plans for the security of the future must be made.

Let us check up on ourselves by asking the following questions: Do I know myself? Do I know what I want? Am I willing to make the necessary sacrifices to attain the station in life 63

that I should be filling? Do I think constructive or destructive thoughts? Am I prepared to change my ideas if I find that my concept is in error? Does Happiness to me mean a life of lagging energy and limited interests or a life of vitalized energy and effort directed to a definite goal? Am I improving the state of my existence or am I drifting from habit? If I set a goal, even though I may never reach it, will it not act as a stabilizer to direct my life? These are important questions to anyone since the first definite move towards a more abundant life must be consciously one's own.

A realization of the world's constant changes and interplay of facts and forces is essential to a clear understanding of life's progress. The 

Great War brought about a recognition of this to many people, but already a new generation has sprung up who are anxious for change and who are unaware of how consequences of world conflict influence their personal lives. This same fomentation of human effort is found in government, in economics, in religion and in education,in fact, there seems to be an increased velocity of our individual and social energies, but with no set standards by which to direct them. 

How are we as individuals to find Happiness if we sail blindly, influenced by uncontrolled forces,

with our old standards changing and new ideals still in the making? Why, after all, should we expect Happiness? The answer is that we must, as individuals, learn to move forward in rhythm with the forces which govern life. If we are too fearful to move, we shall evolve eventually into human flotsam cast upon a sea of inadequacy. We shall suffer and have ourselves to blame. We are a part of this shifting life force and it is up to us to discover how we may find Happiness in its midst.

Let us consider some of the fundamentals in the progress to a more abundant life and then approach the problems of achieving it with an open mind, sincerity of purpose, and a determination to balance the factors constituting our personalities. Having recognized life to be an everchanging force, we must keep our minds receptive to choose and adjust its influences. Our efforts must converge to a balanced whole. Like the 

Captain of a modern ship or the pilot of a modern plane, we may sail or fly blindly only if our gyro-stabilizer and gyro-compass keep us in balance and on our course.

FINANCES-Factor 1 

Definite figures, properly apportioned and carefully budgeted, are the best method of keeping tab on one's financial position. So, let us examine these and thereby learn the power of financial stabilization which is so vital to enduring Happiness.

Physical and mental suffering can often be traced to unbalanced Finances. Faced with a definite need of readjustment, caused by careless disregard of financial matters, we will see how important it is to study the evaluation of expenditures in relation to income. How best to utilize the income will be shown in a study of the 

Finance Chart shown herewith. The freedom of choice given us as human beings, if wisely used in relation to our finances, can bring us great Happiness. 

Money cannot buy Happiness, but it can buy one-tenth of our total Happiness quotient. Beyond that it is acting as a compensator for one or more factors that we have intentionally or unin-

tentionally disregarded. This is a fact that should be clearly understood and until we accept it as true we cannot hope to attain a high Happiness percentage. It is important to remember that money was invented by man as a convenience to simplify the cumbersome method of barter, and that back of money or barter is physical and/or mental effort.

As Egonalysis is intended to help all classes of human society-those who may be suffering from extreme poverty, or those who may be supplied with money and the luxuries it can buy, but suffer because of the lack of self-discipline, and in between these extremes the gradations of poverty and wealth, of dependency and self-adequacy-we cannot attempt to lay down set rules or even fixed proportions to be followed, and if we could, it would only tend to deprive the individual of his initiative in choosing for himself. We can, however, put down general rules and approximate proportions applicable to all.

No matter whether the income is small or large, its utility in meeting our daily needs and securing the future is the most important factor. 

Let us then consider investing in insurance; it covers a vast field of security value and builds up a reserve against loss by fire, storm, sickness,

old age and death, and should be carefully studied and considered by everyone. There are many reliable sources of information on insurance to assist in securing the future by apportioning a small amount of the income to security value. 

Usually, the "forgotten man" is one who failed to prepare for unforeseen emergencies during the days of plenty, and therefore, a proper interpretation of this term would be "self-forgotten man. 99 We must concentrate on Finances to balance our budget for present needs, and in so doing retain a margin of safety for the future which we will call a "safety-valve fund." In this safetyvalve fund we will make as adequate preparation for the future as possible; now let us allow for errors of judgment or failure to live up to the standard set. Overestimation or underestimation of personal ability is a common mistake, but figures leave no margin for human error, so let us put a small sum aside as a "safety-valve fund" to serve as a continual reminder that habit is an important means of building up balance and though misjudgment is not always fatal it should be allowed for.

The following story will show how this system should work in an average case:

John and Mary were a couple who lived from

day to day with no thought for the future. They had enough income to live on comfortably, but did not plan for unforeseen emergencies. They had never been jarred to the realization that emergencies would arise, and at the end of the month they never had a cent left over. 

It was so comfortable just to drift along from habit and not bother to think of the future. Then John's brother died, leaving a ten-year-old son, named Tom, with no resources to provide for his care. 

John and Mary took the boy to live with them, but they were not prepared for this added expense, and now it became necessary for them to become money-minded.

First, they decided upon a budget to take care of household and living expenses. This worked successfully and at the end of the month there was enough money with which to start a savings account. Then they brought Tom in on their cooperative plan. They started a "safety-valve fund," which was made up of extra monies earned or saved, which served to meet emergencies or expenditures made thoughtlessly which were not included in the budget. 

Of course, this fund made them more alert to their reckless spending, especially since they made a game of seeing who could add the most to the account, and the one who came out behind

at the end of the month had to pay an extra penalty amount, but as they progressed the demands on the fund grew less. This fund gave them a feeling of security and self-assurance. John subscribed to the fund by giving up a Club and the smoking of cigars; Tom, by earning extra money selling papers after school; Mary, by saving on various household commodities. The fund grew so rapidly that soon there was enough to take out education insurance for Tom, which made him very happy, as he felt that he would not be such a responsibility upon his foster parents when he became of college age, and he was especially anxious to receive an education. The fund continued to grow and finally there was enough money for John and Mary to take out a twenty-year endowment policy. 

The "safety-valve fund" might properly be called a "psychological fund," as it functions to secure the mind against possible failure to live up to a set goal. It is wise to forecast our factors and leave an amount for a "safety-valve fund," with which to adjust shortcomings or meet emergencies. 

Let us skip over a period of years and note the benefits John and Mary derived from a keener concentration on finances.

The company with which John was connected went into bankruptcy. For months and months 

John looked for another job, but to no avail,he was too old and no one wanted to bother with an old man when there were younger ones available who could do the work quicker. John was thankful that he had a savings account to draw on, but it, too, began to dwindle away. However, he was sufficiently fortified to keep up until the planned for brighter day dawned-the twentyyear endowment policy became due. 

John drew $2000.00 and with the savings that were left he moved to a small town where he opened a store. He bought a modest little home with a garden plot and chicken yard, on the instalment plan. Tom had finished his education and was one of the town's leading officials,—he took care of the instalment payments on the house. John, Mary and Tom are all living very happily and are leading influences in their town, teaching others the value of a budget and "safety-valve fund."

The problem of Finances resolves itself into apportioning the income to meet daily needs, balancing the budget, reserving for security and "safety-valve" purposes. By keeping a daily budget, you will be surprised to find out how much money you spend on unnecessary items

which you could otherwise use in securing the future. You will find interest in readjusting your expenditures to conform more to your needs.

Through the medium of charting your finances you will see how interdependent are all the factors of life and how important it is for you to chart them to have an intelligent and visual record of accomplishment by which to regulate your life. By practice you will learn how properly to apportion your income. However, there are other factors which cannot be divided into figures, so let us first be sure to understand how properly to balance our accounts and then apply the rules learned in balancing our personalities.

After deciding on how much should be allotted out of the income for each Chart item, we are faced with the element of probability. If we dwell morbidly, we may foresee so many barriers to be surmounted that the task may seem not worth the effort. If this is your reaction, one or more of your component parts are not functioning in rhythm. You must learn to face the unknown with a spirit of intelligence and planning, and you will derive happiness in seeing your plans materialize, bringing you ever closer to your goal. 

You may think at first that existence would be dull if every item of life were cut and measured

according to a set pattern, but each one must find an individual balance which is satisfying to himself. Anyone who has watched the strained faces at a gambling table in Monte Carlo can only pity those who stake their all on a chance. Some may keep in reserve a larger "safety-valve fund" than others to enable them to take chances, but only an abnormal mind can crave the excitement of risking one's all.

Let us start a financial budget as the first step in learning to use the Egonalysis system on the other nine factors. Soon we will find it easy to apply the same evolution to each of the factors that go to make up our lives.

OCCUPATION-Factor 2 

The foregoing chapter deals with the material phase of life, i.e., economic welfare. We will now discuss occupation and observe how it acts as a connecting link between our physical and mental health and general well being. The following lines contain a vivid truth: "Absence of occupation is not rest; 

A mind quite vacant is a mind in distress." 

Perhaps you, oppressed by occupation, have longingly mused of the pleasure of sailing in cool breezes on a luxurious yacht, or of following the

gay seasons, free from the worries of the necessities of life, but let us study the lines: "Happiness depends, as nature shows, Less on exterior things than most suppose." 

Work is a stabilizing factor, and therefore, it is important to find occupation that is a satisfying outlet for the expression of Self. 

You may ask yourself, "How may I choose and apportion my work to derive the most from it?”

The depression brought about the difficulty of finding suitable and satisfying employment, and perhaps more than ever before brought the nation to the realization that work in itself is not always satisfying; that it must offer a fundamental creative element to express one's Self. "But," you may wonder, "how can work, in this machine age of mass production, be made creative?" Any work well done can be made creative if it affords the necessities of life and an opportunity to better one's condition. But it is at this point that many fail to comprehend or give thought to the significance of "condition of life." "Station in life" is a phrase that has certain value in our social system, but this phrase must not be confused with "condition in life."

The latter is dependent on the co-ordination of our factors and work binds together the physical and mental factors.

America has numerous examples of great men and women who have made their way against almost unbelievable odds. We have created individual and national wealth, but we are faced with the problem of mass poverty. The youth just out of college is faced with the difficulty of finding work and is inclined to believe that the opportunities which former generations enjoyed are not available to him. The individual who suffered loss because of the depression wonders if the effort is worth while. Yet there has never been a time when individual effort backed by the spirit of the pioneer would be more fully repaid than during this period of transition.

It is true enough that many of the old standards have changed and a reappraisement of the values of present standards is necessary. The call is to the pioneer spirit, not to develop new lands nor to acquire vast personal wealth, but to utilize for the benefit of society as a whole the lands and wealth already possessed. That is, whether a C.C.C. worker, a factory worker, or a bank president, explore your potentialities, exercise your earning capacity to its limit and view your work as it relates to world distribution, if you would appreciate the significance of your job.

Do not lose your opportunities for the present in fantasies of the future by living in the hope that something better will turn up, when the turning

up should be done through the use of your own spade. There are sufficient assets in this world to supply every individual with a living. Each person is a part of the force of production and consumption and each has a part in its scheme. 

A person suffering under physical hardship may oftentimes rank higher in the scale of personal happiness than someone more prosperous who repudiates his convictions and endures a sense of maladjustment. Since work is a welding force in life it must be carefully planned. After deciding the form of work that best suits your qualifications, you must then aim to accomplish success in that field and do the job a little better than it could be done by anyone else. It may be necessary to do types of work other than that for which you are best fitted, but so long as you keep the progressive spirit you are bound to succeed. Long hours and diligent study may be required, sacrifice of companionship and even the derision of friends for a while, but all these will come into adjustment when you have built your foundation. Such is the history of men who have accomplished success, and it is only possible to surmount hardships and maintain happiness when a relative value of the goal and the sacrifices necessary to attain it are understood.

Eventually we rejoice in or suffer from the results of our efforts, therefore, we should weigh and measure them with care. Work is essential to security and Happiness, but we must not assume that financial gain is the ultimate goal of all work. Of necessity we must provide a living for ourselves and dependents, though beyond a living the results of our efforts may not bring returns of money, but may compensate in physical and mental well-being beyond any realization which we expect. How important then is the study of life's occupation with a care to all interrelating factors. As we grow in our work we should keep in mind the opportunity of bettering our "condition in life."

NOURISHMENT-Factor 3 

As we apply our budget and become more familiar with the expenditure of money, a point that is brought strongly to our attention is the percentage spent for food and its preparation. 

No matter how small the budget or how we attempt to economize, nourishment is an essential item and it is right that an adequate amount should be apportioned for keeping the physical fires stoked. We should not, however, lose our sense of values and become slaves to food.

As there are people who need a Chart as a reminder of how extravagant they are, and others of how miserly they are, so we find people who over-indulge in eating and drinking, and others who undermine their health by stinting on the item of food. Therefore, everyone should have a fair knowledge of vitamins and how properly to balance a diet to their needs. It would not be common sense for an office worker of sixty to attempt the same diet necessary to keep a day laborer of twenty well nourished. 

Another important part of nourishment is beverage. As the human body is 90 per cent water, we can readily appreciate the necessity of replenishing this supply. But we have an added choice in fermented or distilled drinks, and here we are faced with an abuse more acute than overeating, for although the effects of either to the individual are serious, excessive use of alcohol inevitably carries with it social evils and demonstrates the close relation of the individual to the whole of society.

EXERCISE-Factor 4 Let us next consider Exercise. The football star and the invalid spinster naturally require different amounts of exercise according to their

strength and needs. However, exercise and nourishment are closely allied and can be followed with a minimum of effort when your individual Ego Chart has been worked out to your needs. 

These two factors are necessary for physical and mental health and each plays its part in the elimination of poisons from the system, as well as furnishing bodily sustenance. Science has accomplished a lot in the control of disease, but it can only offer guidance to the individual in his choice of how to preserve health. More thought is constantly being given to preventive measures. 

It would be interesting to see statistics on how much illness could be spared if every individual had a balance sheet of his nourishment and exercise and conscientiously tried to control health by means of a record. 

It may surprise you, when your Chart is worked out, apportioning a reasonable balance to each factor, to see how little thought you have been giving to the important item of health. 

Everyone should have a physical examination once a year, and a physician or Life Extension Institute can guide you as to how to adjust these factors, but you are the one to carry out the rules and check on the accuracy of the advice to your needs.

REST-Factor 5 

Let us consider Rest in its correlation to wellbeing. Rest, like work, can be made constructive or destructive to the physical and mental faculties. Drowsiness, as well as insomnia, may be a warning of some nervous disorder. Fatigue from mental work generally produces insomnia, but this can also be true of physical work. Therefore, each individual must determine the proper amount of rest for his own needs. 

You may wonder why Rest, or Sleep, is considered as one of the ten factors of individual Happiness. In our discussion, however, we must remember a property of human life represented in our diagram as line AB, the nerves. A prominent neurologist has termed the nervous system the telephone system of the human body and as our bodies and minds are its operators they must have rest to carry on their duties of transmitting and relaying messages.

The problem of how to control forces, both conscious and subconscious, that do not seem related, but have such a distinct bearing on each other is what we must learn. The reader understanding this question will quickly get a grasp on his own position, for it is just this necessity of compensative correlation of the elements comprising our lives that is conducive to Happiness.

No one should become morbidly introspectivejust the reverse. 

Here is a simple test to the question: "Where shall I begin in this study of Happiness?" The preceding sections have dealt with factors which for the most have been settled by circumstances of long standing. Here, however, is our starting point for the factors to come, and we should ask ourselves these simple questions: "How soundly am I sleeping? Do I have excessive and disturbing dreams? Do I have nightmares? Do I feel the need of more sleep than I am getting? As I drop off to sleep am I conscious of muscular contractions and cramps? Do I feel rested when I awaken?" 

If you find that your rest is not satisfactory, it may be traced to the factors of Nourishment and Exercise, for nervous disorders come from these as well as from mental troubles. Those who suffer from insomnia are not always aware of how sleepless nights affect their entire being, for the subconscious can hide many facts. 

The old saying that "one rotten apple in a barrel will make a barrel of rotten apples" does not imply that all the apples will rot in a day, but anyone who has found such a barrel in his winter supply will know "disappointment" and the truth of the remark. Isn't this also true of our factors

of life? We think we are storing up reserves, but if forced suddenly to draw on them we find that one by one they are not as well fortified as we thought them to be.

Offhand, it sounds impractical to keep a record of Rest, but many a Doctor would be helped to a correct diagnosis by just such a self-kept record. 

Let us remember then that Work and Rest are the two factors going hand-in-hand to weld and keep oiled the active functioning of our lives, but do not let us forget the importance of all ten factors. They are vital to the smooth running of the system as a whole and should interlock one with the other just like teeth in a gear. 

EDUCATION-Factor 6 

Education is another factor which vitally affects every phase of life, and is a continuous process throughout life, adding appreciation to our existence and power to our search for Happiness. Both terms Recreation and Education cover vast potentialities for the person who will avail himself of the opportunities offered. It is necessary for each individual to plan his recreational and educational programs just as carefully as he does any of the other items. 

How many times have you heard people wishing they could spend a year or more in some

large metropolis to take advantage of its opportunities and yet if you asked the average resident of that metropolis about those same opportunities he would tell you that he was too busy to avail himself of this or that advantage. 

No matter where we live we have opportunity for recreation and education, but the choice of intelligently planning it is ours. There is so much going on that if we merely drift, in the end we will probably find that we have been diverted but not truly recreated or educated. We may allow these factors which are so important to our personal Happiness to become dissipated and thereby create a restless desire for amusement, which would finally prove meaningless and boring. 

Life may be wrecked or personal Happiness may be lost if there is no balancing of the recreational and educational factors. We can all of us look into our past and for one reason or another find fault with our education, but that does not alter the fact that we still have within our power the opportunity of educating ourselves. 

A man who had lived many and varied experiences in his life, who had risen from the position of an apprenticed boy to a governing officer, who had known poverty and wealth and who had book learning as well as a wide training in the school

of experience, finally concluded: "Of all the thrills and pleasures of life, there is only one which remains constant throughout: the pleasure of using one's mind." The privilege to use one's mind is open to all who are normal. 

No matter whether we have had adequate advantages, or have failed to have them, there is much of interest in the world and within easy reach if we keep alert to our opportunities. There is only one's self to blame if the Education Column does not show a high percentage.

EMOTIONAL CONTROL-Factor 7 

In Egonalysis, we find the emotions are integral parts of our human system. Emotional control is a combined and compensated functioning of the mind, body and nervous system. At a meeting of nerve doctors it was unanimously voted to term sanity as "the power of an individual to adapt himself to any environment in which he might be placed."

Here is a very simple way to test your emotional reactions: Perhaps you have been placed under severe physical or mental strain, how did you react? Were you able to control your feelings of fear or anger, love or hate, joy or grief, —or did you lose control of yourself to the extent

of making a scene? Review your behavior under such circumstances. Were your actions such that afterwards you felt ashamed of them or were you able to practice control?

Some people who feel emotions very deeply do not always show them, but adjust themselves to circumstances, through Self-Control, and maintain balance and sanity under stress. Do not, however, confuse this type with the emotionally subnormal, who do not react to any stimuli, which indicates abnormality. We must strive to reach a balanced personality by exerting a conscious control over our emotions and by not allowing them to govern us.

We hear much controversy about heredity and environment. We know that heredity exists in the human animal, and should be reckoned with, but we also know that environment has an important and undeniable influence on development of the human being. It then becomes our duty, as an individual, to study heredity and environment so that we may bend every effort toward compensating and adjusting these two important factors which influence personality. 

A man inherits only three fingers on one hand, -unconsciously from infancy his entire personality adjusts itself to this handicap and he does not feel any hardships, unless it be an occa-

sional embarrassment. The same man lost two fingers on his other hand in an accident after he had reached a mature age. Here is a case of environment, in which he must prepare for a long struggle to reach the same stage of ease that seemed natural to him with his hereditary abnormality.

In the first instance he did not have perfect heredity, but he compensated from infancy so that it became a most complete adjustment. In the second instance, after heredity and environment had worked to mature his personality, it was not so easy to compensate and readjust himself to the unnatural situation; he was forced to use conscious effort.

We must see what part heredity and environment have played in forming our personality, and by conscious effort try to compensate for the elements that are not satisfactory to ourselves. 

There is no justification in saying: "Oh I can't help that-I inherited that." There are shining examples in history of men who have had outstanding careers notwithstanding severe handicaps both hereditary and environmental. 

There is no more important factor by which to apply the rules of compensation and aim at balance than in Emotional Control. The gradual, almost unnoticed slipping into moods of undue

hilarity or unwonted depression, the letting go of one's temper, and a thousand and one minor and almost unconscious feelings which we may allow to become our master must be controlled. 

At first such inadequacies may seem so slight that we overlook them and others excuse them, but often such instabilities grow until more serious idiosyncrasies develop. Often they are not even noticeable except to the trained eye until the individual becomes to a degree emotionally unbalanced.

RECREATION-Factor 8 

"It is surely obvious that no one can be argued into optimism since no one can be argued into happiness." Hundreds of thousands of our

American people are failing to take advantage of the tremendous opportunities offered for the eighth factor-Recreation, because they have lost sight of the fact that re-creation means a renewed creation which in turn implies action. 

Does the individual who passively watches an entertainment or who listens to a concert have a sense of re-creation? Only as far as he is emotionally stirred or mentally aroused,-but he has lost the great opportunity for finding Happiness in recreation unless he is able to participate in

the action-to express his own desires and aspirations.

We can combine recreation with exercise and education, but we must not confuse it with rest or relaxation, for the latter factor is a subconscious passivity while the former is a conscious activity. In our final chapter we shall see the importance of conscious relaxation as it applies to the subconscious, but for the present let us concentrate on recreation.

Once again we are faced with the need of making an intelligent choice as to our recreational activities, but herein lies our freedom. No matter what line we take up, be it athletics, art, science, agriculture or a game of cards, we have in it our greatest opportunity for self-expression and self-perfection.

Why should we become a nation of people who depend on amusement (derived from the verb meaning to stand idle) when we should be finding recreation?

The Machine Age has offered man much leisure time and man has turned to the machine for amusement, too indifferent to his own well-being to realize the need for self-expression found in recreation. If you question this statement, take a trip to any of the state or private mental insti-

tutions and visit the occupational therapy department. Here you will find human beings who are trying to regain balance in re-creating muscular and mental control by means of looms, forges, knitting, weaving and countless devices. 

Has our education failed its purpose of teaching people how to live? Has it crowded out man's need for free self-expression? Has the human animal become the slave instead of the master of the machines he has created? We have seen the need for action as a basic law of life,—are we then going to allow our minds to snap because we have tried to mechanize them, only to find that the law of life will not be denied?

Egonalysis does not permit one to do so, for no man will sacrifice his happiness when re-creation plays such an important part and can be so easily attained. Argument is no longer necessary, for only the moron will consciously fail to make the effort of self-expression, and once the beginning has been made, the danger of too much rather than too little recreation will have to be faced. 

The average person loves a garden and surely there is no finer illustration we can use. No matter whether the garden is only a window-box in the city or part of a vast estate in the country the same law of life governs them all. From

the time we dream of how we want our garden, continuing on through the preparation of the soil, the purchasing of the seeds, the planting of them, watering, weeding, and spraying against parasites, until we see our flowers in full bloom there has been action and self-expression unhampered by any but our own desires. 

Certainly there will be improvement to work out for the coming year. We can ourselves clearly see wherein we have failed, if we are honest, and by winter we will have found so much to study that even if we are conscientious in this recreational factor we will find ourselves wishing for more time than we have allotted ourselves for recreation. From the physical labor to the cultured satisfaction of tending and seeing our success we have affected all the other factors on our chart, each sharing in the benefits derived from self-expression.

Why then do we Americans have so few home lots laid out in gardens? Is it a commentary on how little we understand the need for recreation? 

We have failed to distinguish between amusement, idle selfless entertainment, and recreation, the free and unmolested self-expression through which the individual readjusts and lessens the strains of daily life, and rebuilds the power of meeting problems with a renewed energy and balance.

SEX-Factor 9 

"We are again brought to that rhythmic balance of expression and repression which is so dominant a feature in our civilized life, but is also to be seen in all animal life."'* Sex-write down the word and continue with your first reaction to it. Do you unconsciously recoil from a discussion of the subject,-do you morbidly crave to satisfy a secret curiosity, or do you frankly want to know how best to compensate sex with the nine other factors of your life, so that it too will become part of your balanced personality? 

Perhaps the simplest test you can use is to ask yourself whether Sex, to you, means possession or unity. Perhaps nowhere is the paradox of life brought more clearly to our attention than in this question of sex, for we find it to be the only means of continuing the race, and yet we have so long considered it a subject to be discussed behind closed doors and to be relegated to the hidden depths of obscenity. 

If you wish to find Happiness your conception of sex must imply unity - never possession. 

Unity in sex is an art which means a careful consideration of the partner and a realization that the ego must be lost in its desire to give satisfac- 

*Havelock Ellis.

tion to the mate, and never should be practiced as a means of personal release. Mating is a study in cooperation and should become an expression of cultural happiness free from any feeling of guilt or selfish gratification.

The paradox phrased by a Chinese philosopher, Laotzu-The greatest happiness is no happiness-expresses the underlying principle of cooperation, that we will find unity in direct ratio to what we give, and satisfaction will be found in the new creation resulting from the union. We have already seen the close affiliation between the mental and the physical, so it should be easy to see that when we come to that factor which unifies two individuals we must give care that the factors of one are in compensation with those of the other.

Often we hear it said of young people who are about to marry,-"Oh that match will never last -their interests are so totally different." Our divorce courts prove that our present method of mating is far from perfect. How then can we better this situation?

First, begin with a careful physical knowledge of what marriage means. There is much literature written to help the young people of today learn in a scientific way the facts about nature and man's reaction to them.

In our study of the various factors we have found compensation necessary to achieve balance and now we find cooperation essential, for we are no longer dealing with ourselves alone but have a partner to consider. Looking over our factor of Recreation we found the need of unhampered expression,-now we go further and find the need of self-expression seeking union and the results: re-creation. 

This sounds easy and literature is filled with the glamorous stories of Romance, but how many unsuspecting brides have been shocked or disgusted into a shattered romance because they knew nothing of human sex relations? Unknowingly husband and wife have stumbled through ignorance into a life of dissension instead of cooperation, and the art of love-making has turned to a legalized concubinage offering little or no satisfaction to the helpless pair. Yes, the physical relations of marriage should first be clearly understood.

Having a physical knowledge, we should then assure ourselves of personality-adaptiveness. In trying to reach our conclusions what greater help can we have than a permanent record of our lives in the form of Egonalysis Charts? If a person is not willing to share in the study of the past with its relation to a united future with the loved one, certainly that person is looking at love from the

standpoint of possession and not unity. This is why you have kept your Egonalysis Chart as permanent records so that you could offer a scientific and accurate record to assist in planning for life's greatest adventure. 

The heedless cry of the crowd is heard: "But what of Romance?" and "Le Bon" in his book "The Crowd" gives the answer when he says: "Crowds being only capable of thinking in images are only to be impressed by images," and Romance without a scientific and factual basis is a graven image of the worst type. The dictionary gives its definition as "any wild and extravagant story of the imagination" and so it is for the countless homes that might better be termed "Hells on earth."

Compare the recreation found in having a garden to married life, and see if there is not great similarity. First, there is the choice of location and the mental picture of what we wish to see develop. This might be compared to searching for a mate and looking into the future of a life together. Preparing the soil and courtship are analogous and planting the seeds and marriage are both basic laws of re-creation. 

Finally, spraying the flowers and keeping them weeded and watered are comparable to the adjustments necessary for a harmonious married

life and the care necessary to rearing children. 

We have now reached romance or the cultural enjoyment of the garden and the home, but they have been built on careful planning and scientific knowledge and not merely on a romantic hope.

Our choice of seeds must be made with thought as to the type of soil in which they will prosper and just so we must come to realize that certain matings of human beings are better adapted than others and will have a far better chance of flowering into a happy home. Anyone who has seen the countryside in springtime should have felt the rhythm of life with its eternal cycles of "Expression and Repression." 

Is a garden less beautiful because we use a studied care in the number of seeds we plant? 

Are we to be deprived of the joy and the sense of well-being derived from working in the soil or do we revel in the feeling of being closer to the universal heart-throb? Have we spent our energy and love to have it cut down in ruthless slaughter? If we answer no to these questions in the case of our garden how much stronger we should feel about our human garden-the home. Why not face the facts of a planned family, of the joy of sex relations and the need of universal peace without which no home can feel secure?

Replace the graven image, Romance, with the living god, Life. Consult the psychologist and admit that the ego is of uppermost importance to each individual and then have faith that from a scientific union of two egos will be born a new generation who will give thanks to their parents for a healthy body, an active mind and rejoice in the opportunity to carry on and perfect the living immortality of the human race. Re-creation is the ultimate goal of marriage, co-operation should be the foundation upon which it is built. 

If you have carefully studied Egonalysis up to this point you will see why the greatest happiness can be obtained in a monogamous marriage between men and women who come together in sex relations for the first time. This is the ideal toward which humanity is striving, for no dual standard is based on cooperation, and the jealousy of true love is not possessive but springs from the knowledge that perfect oneness can only be reached by two individuals whose union was based on the desire for each other; that they through the law of life might continue mankind, conceived in happiness and born into the world with every chance of obtaining happiness and a further realization that from peace within the individual, the home, the community, and the state will finally evolve an universal peace among nations.

This is no romantic dream of an Utopia-it is a scientific approach to the evolution of the human animal toward a social order of happiness based on the law of life. 

One final element necessary to every garden is sunlight and in our human lives it might be likened to the need for truth. Just as we plant our gardens with faith that the sun will rise, we must govern our lives in the light of absolute sincerity and have faith that the result will mean 

Happiness of Achievement. From Akenahten to 

Christ the prophets tell us that the law of truth is interpreted through brotherly love. Today in the light of modern science we are beginning to learn how to apply truth with intelligence. 

Just as the earth is comparable to our bodies, the air to our minds and water to our nervous systems, each being scientifically cared for and laid open to the sun, so too we must have faith that in the knowledge of truth rather than in the fear of ignorance our personalities will strive toward the perfect sphere of a balanced personality and we will find with the poet that there is no compromise with Truth: "To thine own self be true, And it must follow, as the night the day, Thou canst not then be false to any man."

CONSCIENCE-Factor 10 

The final factor, Conscience, is defined by the dictionary as: Consciousness; Knowledge; the faculty of judging one's conduct with reference to some standard of right and wrong. It might seem that this factor, more than any other, would affect our state of happiness, but it is hard to distinguish between the relative importance of the various factors, and our job is to coordinate all ten so that they will produce a balanced and rhythmic personality.

Liken Conscience to the flux used to produce the balanced union of metallic ores, or again, to the conductor of a symphony orchestra who is responsible for the proper tempo and blending of the various instruments which produce rhythmic performance. Quickly, you inquire, "But how can I do more than I am doing to find Happiness? 

I have not found it as yet,-show me how."

The derivation of the word Conscience is "with" and "science" and if you will follow three simple yet scientific laws you will find more happiness than you knew existed. Just before you retire at night, divide the "Fifteen Minutes 

A Day With Yourself" into three periods of five minutes and during each period study one of these laws.

First, see if the ten factors of your life are well compensated, for correct compensation is the first law governing happiness. Are we getting a balanced coordination between the various elements so that one or more factors do not suffer because we abuse some other factor? Consider for example the alcoholic and see how over-indulgence of the nourishment factors affects one after another of the remaining factors until, in extreme cases, it kills all happiness long before it causes physical death. 

Try to see wherein your life is lacking and where it is strong and build in a balance by means of rating your chart on the basis of 100, denoting perfection. Having done this, spend five minutes in self-education by finding out the second law underlying Happiness-namely, cooperation.

In every thought there will be an example of how the law of cooperation is the basic law of a full, happy life. And we must understand the term cooperation, for it means to labor with, and does not imply ease and waste. Are we working with ourselves and others for a happier life for all? Are we informed on what other people throughout the world are doing to find happiness and how they are succeeding? By looking forward in our own lives and keeping an open mind

we will see that every day offers us an opportunity to find personal happiness by means of intelligent cooperation with others. 

The final law which governs happiness is Concentration. We cannot hope to accomplish anything unless we have the ability to apply ourselves and yet we find it difficult to visualize our own personalities. The charts have given you a clear picture of your factors with a low average; your reading should have given you ideas on what you want to accomplish in the future, so now concentrate for five minutes on your desired end. Modern psychology has taught that we have a conscious and also a subconscious mind. This latter may have a strong influence on your behavior and can best be influenced by concentration just before going off to sleep. But to be able to influence the subconscious you must be in a relaxed state, so free from tension that your conscious mind may even find difficulty in retaining the desired end.

To quote from the book "The Common Sense of Drinking" by Richard R. Peabody, who had great success in curing alcoholism, "The relaxation procedure is as follows: the patient is instructed to recline in a chair and think of himself as being numb, heavy, limp and relaxed. If Mr. Peabody and others have found this simple means of autosuggestion worked in the cure of heavy drinking, certainly it is worthwhile for you to practice for five minutes a day in an effort to find happiness.

No matter what your problem is, concentrate on the fact that you are building a stronger and more balanced personality to meet it and that you no longer fear its power over you for you have and are continually gaining strength. Do not become introspective and approach your weakness from the negative side, but tell yourself of the benefits and the pleasure you will derive
