Preface S THE title "MIND YOUR OWN BUSINESS" indicates, this manual is intended to caution the reader to attend to his every-day affairs. It is a compilation of daily do's and dont's. No attempt is made to cover each and every circumstance. That would be an impossibility. But some of the most common mistakes, errors, and pitfalls in every-day affairs and transactions are shown. 

No attempt is made to instruct the reader in the law of the particular locality of his residence. No legal opinions are intended. It is merely a concise handbook of business suggestions similar to first aid hints to a person injured. 

There are instances when an attorney, insurance agent or tax expert should be consulted.

The Special Section devoted to Members of the Armed 

Forces should prove very helpful, as it is very appropriate. 

It was conceived with the idea in mind to assist those about to be inducted or those already in service. The Soldiers' and 

Sailors' Civil Relief Act of 1940 with its latest amendments appears exactly as passed for the reader to study. 

The forms shown in this manual are not intended as standard forms that can be used in every State. You are cautioned to consult the laws of the locality in which you reside and also see an attorney.

Keep this book available at all times. Refer to it again and again. As a practicing attorney and former member of the judiciary, the writer is certain that you will find many occasions to use, and that you will become a better business man or woman by resorting to it constantly. 

If assistance is rendered in guiding you against any of the usual pitfalls and errors, the author will feel that his aim has been accomplished, and that you will be able to "MIND YOUR OWN BUSINESS," and that justice will prevail.

For MEMBERS OF ARMED FORCES Protect Your Rights! 

If you are going to volunteer into the service or have already been inducted, protect your property rights and arrange your business affairs before you leave and you will eliminate much inconvenience, unnecessary delay and extra expense. 

List Assets: 

The first thing to do is to make a list of whatever property or assets you may possess, such as real estate, personal property, automobile, insurance, money in the bank, notes, stocks and bonds, etc. 

Power of Attorney: 

If you possess real estate which may be rented, leased, mortgaged, or sold during your absence, it is wise to have it either conveyed to a person or execute a power of attorney authorizing someone to act in your place to take care of this so that it will not be necessary for you to be personally present. 

A Power of Attorney may be given to your wife, father, nother, brother or sister (as the case may be), giving them authorty to act in your place, to perform certain specified duties, such as igning your name, selling your real estate, borrowing money, or ny acts necessary or incidental thereto. 

This Power of Attorney may also include the right to sell our car, or borrow money on it, endorse and cash your checks, withdraw money from the bank, endorse notes or other documents, r for the adjustment of fire, liability and life insurance policies. 

Make several copies of the Power of Attorney so that, if one lost or you have more than one transaction, you will have availble copies. 

A power of Attorney can be revoked at any time prior to being ted upon, so that if you change your mind or make different 578249 3.

Other Matters: 

Whether you are in the service or about to go into a service, your attention is directed to obtaining as quickly as possible certain certified copies of documents that may be necessary for receiving some of the benefits while in the service. In most instances the following certified copies should be obtained: 

1. Your own birth certificate. 

2. Your marriage certificate. 

3. Your divorce decree. 

4. Death certificate of former wife and dependents. 

5. Birth certificate of each and every one of your dependents.

Guardianship: 

Be sure you obtain advice concerning the law of guardianship in your own particular case of any and all your children so as to determine who would be the guardian of the person and property of said child or children and also what authority said guardian would have.

Social Security: 

Members of soldiers' families may be entitled to certain benefits under social security. To determine whether they are eligible, or not, they should write a letter to the Public Assistance Agency or Department of Welfare at the Capitol of the state nvolved.

Death in Service: 

If a person dies while serving in the armed forces of the United States, his property will still descend according to law but here are many instances in cases of wills to determine the legal omicile as to where the will should be probated because of the ecessary changing residences of the person in the armed forces. is wise for the person making the will, if he is serving in the 5.

armed forces to recite in the will that he is in the armed forces and to declare where his domicile of choice is.

It is also advisable to consult an attorney concerning the form and jurisdiction requirements which usually depend on each indi vidual case.

Army Discharge: 

When you receive your Army discharge, record it immediately in the County of your residence, so that you may preserve that record, which may prove very valuable to you, and so that when ever desirable you may obtain a certified copy of it. 

Soldiers' and Sailors' Relief Act: 

While you are in the service of the United States, all of your property rights will be protected by virtue of the Soldiers' and Sailors' Relief Act of 1940, with its amendments. The purpose of the act is to "strengthen and expedite national defense by making provisions for the suspension of certain proceedings and trans actions which may protect the civil rights of a person in the military service." The act does not provide or authorize an automatic moratorium as to civil obligations during military service. It was not the purpose of the act to give a person a chance to act in bad faith toward any just debt. However, it does give the Court dis cretion to stay or suspend a legal proceeding and thus protect you while you are in the service.

Life Insurance: 

Many life insurance companies have now made provision fo desirable methods for handling premiums during the time the insured is in the service. Contact a reliable insurance compan 

The insured soldier or sailor is advised to give special though for instructions on his policy, concerning the designation of th beneficiary, right to surrender the policy for cash value, surrende ing dividends, making loans, and changing beneficiary.

Birth CERTIFICATES 

There are many instances now that require the production of a birth certificate either for yourself or for members of your family. 

It is wise to take steps immediately to obtain birth certificates for each and every member of your family. In most states, if the birth occurred prior to about 1910 or 1915, the birth was probably not recorded or registered; whereas, if it occurred since that date, the birth may be on file and properly registered. 

The first step is to make application to the Registrar of Vital Statistics or Board of Health at the Capitol of the state where the birth occurred, requesting that a search be made to determine whether the birth was ever registered.

If you are certain that the birth was never registered, then obtain the proper applications and affidavits for getting a "delayed certificate of birth."

Deeds 

There are various forms of deeds conveying real estate. Perhaps the two most common are Warranty Deed and Quit-Claim Deed.

Warranty Deed: 

A Warranty Deed warrants title and peaceable and quiet possession usually and, even though the person warranting the title does not possess good title, he guarantees and can be sued on that title in many instances.

Quit-Claim Deed: 

A quit-claim deed merely releases or relinquishes whatever title he has; that is to say, if the property is encumbered, he does not warrant or agree to clear the title but merely gives whatever title he possesses.

What Kind of Deed to Give and Receive: 

In purchasing property, it is wise to procure, if possible, a 

Warranty Deed. On the other hand, never sign a Warranty Deed unless you are sure of or agree to warrant the title. 

Joint Tenancy Deed: 

A modern, stream-lined form of deed is a Joint Tenancy Deed, which means that "A" and "B" and others receive title and the urvivor gets it without the necessity or expense of an estate. This s sometimes referred to as a deed with Rights of Survivorship. t has been adopted in most states. 

More than two persons can hold title in that manner. Caution, owever, is urged in attempting to make a "home-made” Joint Tenancy deed. Because it is a special form of deed, the exact nguage of the statute is usually required, i.e. Colorado: "Not in nancy in common, but in Joint Tenancy, the survivor of them." 

The statute of the particular state involved should be resorted to. 9.

Contents of Deeds: 

Be sure the deed contains the proper names of grantor and grantee, accurate description, acknowledgement properly executed before a Notary Public, whose seal and notarial commission expiration date are affixed.

Revenue Stamps: 

It is customary for the vendor, or seller, to place the proper amount of revenue stamps on the deed. Under our present revenue act, it is necessary to place 55c revenue stamps for every $500 of actual consideration, or fraction thereof. If the consideration is less than $100 or the deed is made for clearing the title purposes, and the deed so recites, no revenue stamps are necessary. These revenue stamps can be purchased usually at the local post office or the office of the County Clerk and Recorder. 

Delivery and Recording: 

It is a good practise to record the deed in the proper county immediately after its delivery so that, in the event it is lost, the title will not be defective, as it then appears of record. Recording an instrument gives notice to the world of the rights of the purchaser. 

To be valid, a deed must be delivered during lifetime, unless there is an escrow arrangement. An attorney should be consulted, however.

Water Rights: 

If water rights are involved, be sure that the water right is transferred by the deed and also the water certificates are properly endorsed.

Treasurer's or Tax Deeds: 

Treasurer's deeds or Tax deeds usually do not convey good and merchantable title. Many times they are defectively issued. 

They merely convey rights based on a tax sale or certificate. Quiet title suits or suits to remove clouds are sometimes necessary. This necessitates extra expense and the inconvenience of litigation in order to clear the title. • 10

Wills 

What Is a Will?

A will is a legal document made by a person disposing of his property in a certain manner at his death. 

Necessity of Will: 

A will is not always necessary. Sometimes, however, it is very advisable. Under no circumstances should a person make his own will without checking the law of the particular state involved. The form, wording, and number of witnesses and formalities are very important. If not done correctly, it may prove to result in expensive litigation.

If No Will: 

If no will is made, the property descends to the heirs according to law. The laws of the various states differ as to who are heirs and what share they receive. Check the law to determine whether a will is practical in your case or not. If you make no will and there are no heirs, the property escheats or goes to the state after a certain number of years. 

"Home Made" Wills: 

Do not attempt to write your own will to save a few dollars attorney's fees. It may cost your loved ones many times that fee years after you are gone. A "home-made" will may be declared invalid by a Court or jury, thus making the property you leave go to some one other than to whom you intended. It is wise to consult an attorney who will assist you and eliminate great expense, administration costs, and unnecessary taxes. If you have made a home-made will, take it to your lawyer and let him read it. 

Witnesses: 

In getting witnesses for a will, it is wise to get young people, persons over 21, who are not beneficiaries under the will. 11

Revoking or Change of Will: 

A will does not take effect until death, and can be revoked or changed at any time before death, by following the laws concerning revocation of the state where it is made or executed. It is best to consult an attorney concerning revoking a will. 

Usual Defects in Wills: 

There are numerous instances where "home-made" wills have been contested in the Courts. Most states have statutes governing the legal technical formalities concerning the execution of wills. 

Examples: 

Many wills have been attacked because the testator or one making the will did not declare it to be his last Will and Testament before the attesting witnesses.

Some wills were declared invalid by the Courts because the witnesses did not see the maker sign it or the witnesses did not sign in each others' presence.

Some have been contested because there appeared alterations or apparent changes made on the document.

BUYING, SELLING Real Estate Buying Real Estate: 

If you are buying real estate, it is of the utmost importance that you take certain steps to protect yourself. A title examination of the Abstract is suggested in most cases so that you may know just how the title stands. Omitting this phase of the transaction has proved very costly to many persons. The mere fact that you are buying a piece of property from a person you know or trust is no guarantee that the title is good, merchantable, and marketable. 

Years from now, when you attempt to sell or encumber the property and a title examination is made, you may discover that a defect or a number of defects exist which could have been corrected at the time you purchased the property. Then it may be too late. The seller may be dead, or his whereabouts unknown, or, if located, might not be financially responsible. Title examination is a wise investment. Usually a nominal fee is charged for this work. 

Defects in Title: 

There are many items that your attorney might find wrong with the title; for example: 

1. Outstanding unpaid mortgages, 2. Outstanding deeds of trust, 3. Liens, 

4. Attachments, 5. Judgments, 6. Estate, inheritance, or income taxes, 7. General property tax, 8. Assessments for oil, curb, gutter, water, sidewalk, etc. 

9. Leases, 10. Land contracts, 11. Oil and mineral reservations, 12. Bankruptcy proceedings, 13. Suits pending or Lis Pendens, 14. Easements and encroachments, 13.

15. Party wall agreements, 16. Restrictive covenants, 17. Tax deeds or treasurer's deeds, 18. Estates Items to Think About: 

In purchasing property you must be sure of the type of deed you are receiving and obtain an accurate description of the property. 

Sometimes a survey is suggested. 

Be sure to discuss the matters of encumbrances, pro-rating of taxes and special assessments, date of possession, collection of rents, and who accepts the tenant at the time of purchase. Also be sure you have proper insurance.

Be sure and obtain an Abstract of Title, brought to date, as you may be subject to extra expenses. 

See that the deed has proper revenue stamps, proper acknowledgement, proper names and proper description. 

Selling Real Estate: 

If you are selling real estate, the first step is to have a written agreement with the purchaser, concerning the terms, conditions, etc. In most instances it is advisable to consult an attorney and explain in detail those matters to him. He will see that the proper adjustments are made for you and will advise you what you are legally responsible for such as revenue stamps, recording fees, abstract expense, pro-rating taxes, assessments, water, rents, and insurance. 

Your attorney will also advise you as to what type of deed to deliver, proper description, and exceptions.

For types of deeds, see Section on Deeds. 

Avoid Lawsuits: 

If you will look over the various list of cases pending in our Courts, you will find that the most numerous type of dispute grows out of the real estate transaction. In most instances, it could have been avoided by using caution! • 14

Why Get a Receipt? 

Receipts 

Time and time again a person pays a debt. Whether it be for fixing the car, settling the grocery bill, or the rent-be sure to get a receipt!

Your request for such a receipt should not embarrass the one who received the money. 

You are not distrusting him.

You are merely getting a signed statement from him that he is paid.

The insurance agent thinks nothing of giving you a receipt that you paid your premium.

Your post office voluntarily hands you a receipt for the money order you send.

Your light and telephone companies give you a receipt without your request when you pay the bill.

So it should be with all transactions involving money. 

It is good, sound business to do this. 

The mind is flexible and very forgetful. 

A witness may be present when you pay the cash but, when you need that witness, he may not be available. The witness may have died, or moved away, or may have forgotten the incident. 

A receipt saves money, worry, embarrassment, and, maybe, a lawsuit.

Form of Receipt: 

The receipt you receive does not have to be a printed one. 

If possible, however, have it contain in ink, the DATE, AMOUNT, MEMORANDUM of what it is for, and SIGNED by person receiving the money. the A simple suggested form or receipt is as follows: 15.

Denver, Colorado

June 1, 1943 

Received of Tom Jones Fifty Dollars (50.00) in full settlement of labor (painting and fixing roof) on house on 1943 

Victory Avenue. 

Do not lose or misplace your receipts. 

John Smith

File your receipts away systematically, either alphabetically or chronologically.

Do not destroy your receipts for at least ten or twelve years. 

Nobody knows when the matter may be presented again. 

Some states have statutes of limitations (outlawing of claims) for six years or more.

Most states have laws which do not permit a bill to outlaw during the period of time that a person conceals himself or goes out of the state.

Value of Receipts: 

Receipts also can be used to show the items of expenses which perhaps are deductable for income tax purposes. 

You can show your receipt to the Collector of Internal Revenue if ever called to do so.

You can use it to show how much you paid for a certain article, or for certain labor, perhaps in litigation, or perhaps for appraisal purposes.

Agreements 

There comes a time when a business transaction involves a contract, agreement, or lease.

Sometimes a person agrees to buy or sell real estate or personal property or agrees to rent a farm or hire a laborer. This suggestion is made: Make a written memorandum of the agreement or transaction. Put in the dates, terms, and conditions. Put the exact amounts involved in it, the date of payment, the amount of ments, the rate of interest, the accurate description, and all other matters appearing to be material. pay- • 16

Labor Agreement: 

If it is a labor agreement, do not leave anything unwritten or to the imagination. It is better to have too much in a contract than too little. Overlooked items cause disputes, which result oftentimes in litigation.

Leases: 

If it is a lease, whether business or farm, do not trust to the memory and have an oral agreement. The party may die or some argument may arise between the parties to the contract and a lawsuit may result to have the Court or jury construe what you orally agreed upon. Have it down in black and white and less trouble will arise.

Of course, if the transaction involves any great amount or any serious conditions, an attorney should be consulted. 

Farm Leases: 

Farm leases arise out of the contract created by the landlord and tenant; usually they are written. In most states if the lease is to exceed one year it should be reduced to writing to conform with what is commonly known as the "Statute of Frauds." There are many kinds of leases and tenancies, such as by will, by lease, or by sufferance. These different tenancies require different kinds of notices so caution should be taken concerning the type of notice to be given to preclude a holding over.

House Agreements: 

The same rules discussed above as to farm leases are applicable to house leases or business leases. It is wise to have a written lease in all instances. It is also practical to have a recital concerning an option to renew the lease so that you will not be ousted from the premises prior to the expiration of the original lease. The following clause is recommended as an option to renew: "It is also mutually agreed by and between the parties and made a part of the consideration of this lease that the lessee shall have the option to renew at the afore-described premises for a term of one year from the date of the expiration of the original lease above, under the same terms and conditions as above recited." 17.

Insurance Insurance Is Wise: 

Insurance is a wise investment. Real estate should always be insured; so also with some forms of personal property. If a person holds a mortgage or deed of trust on a house or a car securing a note for the unpaid balance due, he should see to it that he has the proper insurance clause on the policy to cover any loss he may incur. See that the insurance agent has made out the policy for the correct description of the property, length of time, sufficient amount, and correct names. 

Don't Over-Insure! 

Do not over-insure; yet do not under-insure. Get a reliable insurance agent or real estate agent to write your policy in a reliable company. Have the property appraised for insurance purposes. 

Watch the expiration date so that it will not lapse.

When the property is sold or the encumbrances satisfied, either transfer or cancel the policy so that you will not lose any of its value. If the property becomes vacant, notify your insurance agent to put a vacancy clause on the policy. 

Read the policy over and discuss it with your agent to determine what coverage you have. It may be that you want additional coverage, such as extended coverage, hail, acts of God, etc. 

Don't Listen to Gossip!

Don't listen to side-walk gossip or advice on special subjects of insurance, law, etc. Curbstone opinions may prove costly. 

Consult your attorney, real estate agent, or insurance broker.

Value of Insurance: 

Insurance is a Poor Man's friend and a Rich Man's Asset. 

Insurance is like a life boat-you have to carry it when you don't need it in order to have it when you do. 

Honestly, Insurance is a good policy. • 18

Take Inventory: 

Take inventory of what you have. Take inventory of what insurance protection you possess. You probably possess a home, household goods, car, or business, or, perhaps, all of them. 

Do you have insurance to cover fire, windstorm, hail, smoke damage, explosion, and extended coverage? A fire insurance policy alone does not give you protection against most of the forms of Acts of God. 

Do you have just fire and theft on your car? What about collision, liability, property damage, or comprehensive coverage? 

If you have a business, do you have public liability, burglary, plate glass, and extended coverage? Talk this over with your real estate or insurance agent. He'll be glad to explain it in detail. 

Witnesses 

If you are ever called upon as a witness in a case, the following hints and suggestions are made: 

Be honest, sincere, and frank in your testimony. Remember a Court or jury has to be the judge of what you say. 

Do not get excited. Take it easy—you are not on trial. You are merely giving information of what you heard or observed. 

If you are called as an expert witness, you are merely giving your opinion.

If you do not understand the question, ask the Court or the attorney to have it repeated. Many times an answer is given to a question by a witness without knowing the true meaning of the question and you may give an incorrect or confusing answer. 

Think before you answer. Do not be too hasty. Don't be too "smart" or try to act funny. Remember that a life or the liberty of an individual or his property rights may depend on what you say. 

Don't get angry at the lawyer or the judge. They are only trying to abide by the Court rules. 19

Letters Letters You Write: 

Time and time again, an individual writes an innocent letter to a friend, acquaintance, business firm, or others. Years later, that same letter turns up in Court with your signature on it. Will you remember what you wrote, why, the circumstances, etc.? 

Be Careful WHAT YOU WRITE. Keep in mind that you don't know who will lay their hand on that letter, or when, or for what purpose.

KEEP A COPY Of All Letters YOU WRITE, if possible, and file them away. 

By retaining copies of letters you send, you may be able to prove such important details as knowledge, notice, etc., which may be vital in winning a lawsuit.

Letters You Receive: 

Oftentimes a letter you receive may prove invaluable. If you are corresponding on a matter of business for something that is important, do not destroy the letters you receive. Who knows but that someday your authority to do something or not to do something may be questioned? If you could just lay your hand on that letter you received showing you were given authority to do itsuch as repairing the house or ordering something to be furnished by your landlord! Then what if he denies it later? SAVE THOSE

LETTERS for several years. You can always destroy them later. • 20

Income Tax, KEEPING RECORDS 

Prevailing war conditions have created a situation making the filing of income tax returns very important to virtually every man, woman, and child. It will prove very valuable to keep clear records of your income and outgo. Purchase a small book and diligently keep records with this. Ten minutes a day every day may save hours and hours of time later. Place "Items of Expenditures" on one page and "Items of Receipt" on the other and be sure to keep the dates, amounts, and items with some degree of accuracy. Many times a checking account is advisable to assist in this record-keeping which, incidentally, also serves as a receipt. 

Keep copies of all income tax returns made. If necessary, consult an attorney or tax expert to guide you in this matter. 

Don't wait until the last day to file your return. Prepare it early. If you have kept proper books, you should be able to start preparing them shortly after the first of the year. If you keep your records properly, it will make the income tax return very easy to fill out.

Don't get caught with mixed up records. Be sure to have proof for proving your deductions, records, receipts, etc. It is a good policy to know how much you earn.

Lawsuits 

What to Do If You Are Sued:

If you are being sued, you will probably be served with legal papers—often called a summons. Follow these directions: 

What to Do When Served: 

Take all the papers served upon you to an attorney. Discuss the matter fully with him. Remember the date you were served, where, the manner, and by whom. Don't wait and put the matter off. Many summons contain either a 3-day notice to appear on a motion, or 10 or 20 days from date of service to reply, answer, or plead to the complaint or petition. If you let that time expire without legal advice, you may lose your rights and it may prove costly or embarrassing to you. 

Don't Sign Anything!

Don't sign any papers accepting service or acknowledging anything until advised by your attorney. 

Tell Your Attorney the Truth! 

Tell your attorney all the facts frankly and in detail. Bring him all your correspondence concerning it. Do not lie to your legal adviser. Remember the truth always comes out. You may fool your attorney but you won't fool the judge or jury. Don't overlook telling your attorney everything you know. What might appear to be an insignificant fact may change the whole complexion of a case. 

Don't Talk About the Case! 

Don't talk about your suit or make any statements to anyone without consulting your attorney. An admission made to what appears to be an innocent third party may prove to be embarrassing to you because he might be a witness on the other side the day of the trial. 23.

Accidents.

If you are involved in an automobile accident or any other kind of an accident, these simple rules should be followed: 

DO NOT LOSE YOUR HEAD. Excitement proves very costly. 

DO NOT TALK TOO MUCH. Make no statements to anyone who is not in authority. Answer the officer's questions. 

BE COURTEOUS, kind and helpful to the injured.

MAKE A FULL REPORT of the accident, keeping in mind your version of how it occurred, various distances, etc. 

DO NOT DISTURB ANYTHING until the proper officer arrives.

TAKE NAMES AND ADDRESSES of any and all witnesses at the scene of the accident. If possible, get a written statement from them. 

If you have insurance (and it is wise to carry it), NOTIFY

YOUR AGENT, or the Company. 

IF YOU HAVE INCURRED ANY SERIOUS INJURIES OR DAMAGES, consult an attorney at once and he will advise you how to proceed.

Releases Many persons involved in a lawsuit, accident, or transaction are called upon to sign what is commonly known as a Release. 

A release is a statement that you no longer have a claim. It is not only a receipt, but also a complete satisfaction of your rights. 

Do not sign any papers with the word Release or Satisfaction upon it, unless you have had independent legal advice concerning what you are doing. Many times shortly after an accident, a paper is thrust in front of the person injured to sign for a nominal sum without realizing the amount of damages or injuries incurred or the seriousness of the accident.

READ OVER CAREFULLY WHAT YOU SIGN. If in doubt as to what it means, never sign it! Consult your attorney and be sure it is proper. • 24

Checks 

Most persons who are engaged in a business, and many who are not, have a checking account. A checking account is a very simple, systematic, beneficial way to keep accurate records of expenditures and income.

Advantages: 

The check not only serves as a receipt but is also evidence or proof of payment for deductions for income tax purposes. Put on the check a memorandum of what is being paid-it will prove very helpful.

Number and File: 

It is wise to number all checks and file them away. Make out your check stub accurately so that you will have correct balances and will never be guilty of overdrawals. 

Other Hints: 

Never make out a check in blank or leave an endorsed check. lying around. It is not wise to post-date checks.

It is suggested, when possible, to have a checking account in joint tenancy so that, if illness or death intervenes, the survivor can withdraw without the necessity and expense of an estate. 

If you lose a check, immediately stop payment. 

Whenever possible, in endorsing checks, make a restrictive endorsement, which means only the person to whom the check is endorsed can cash it; for example "Pay to the Order of John Jones.'

Homestead: 

Many states provide for the protection of the home or resi dence of a person by permitting him to place what is known as a "homestead entry" on his property. This is usually done by making a marginal entry at the County Clerk and Recorder's office or an affidavit or statement filed with the County Clerk and Recorder in the County of the location of the property. 

This exemption is for the protection of the home against certain types of creditors. This entry can be made usually by either spouse provided that they are a resident of the State and residing on the property as their home. This usually protects them from levy or attachment in various amounts, usually Two Thousand Dollars.

Care and caution are urged to the one claiming a homestead. 

They should be sure to consult the law of the state or consult an attorney concerning the proper way to take advantage of this exemption.

Other Exemptions: 

The statutes of the various states usually provide for certain exemptions to a judgment debtor or a person who is sued. These range from a certain per cent of wages to certain specified articles and the laws of the various states should be consulted before making such a claim.

It is wise to consult an attorney because certain statutes require a written claim of exemption to be filed on a certain specified date.

The 

Soldiers' and Sailors'

Civil Relief Act The original Soldiers' and Sailors' Civil Relief Act was adopted in 1918 because of the emergency of the First World War. On October 17, 1940, the present act was approved by the President and certain amendments have been enacted since that time.

This legislation affects and applies to every man or woman in military service of the United States or its Allies, no matter how or when inducted. 

Millions in the service have heard about the Act and have even talked about its provisions. 

In the following pages the exact wording of the Act and latest amendments appear. If you are about to be inducted, some of its provisions may apply now. If you are already in the service, read the Act, and you will find there are many situations affecting your everyday transactions of life.

Of course, in the limited pages of this manual, it is impossible to comment on the Act or take its provisions piecemeal and give advice thereon. 

When a concrete problem arises, knowledge of the Act will assist you in presenting it to the proper person for expert advice. • 36

The Soldiers' and Sailors' Civil Relief Act of 1940 and Amendments SHORT TITLE 

This Act may be cited as the Soldiers' 

and Sailors' Civil Relief Act of 1940. 

Purpose of Act 

In order to provide for, strengthen, and expedite the national defense under the condi- emergency tions which are threatening the peace and security of the United States and to enable the United States the more successfully to fulfill the requirements of the national defense, provision is hereby made to suspend enforcement of civil liabilities, in certain cases, of in persons the military service of the United

States in order to enable such persons to devote their entire energy the defense needs of the Nation, and to this end the following provisions are made for the temporary suspension of legal proceedings and transactions which may prejudice the civil rights of persons in such service during the period herein specified over which this Act remains in force.

DEFINITIONS 

to 

The term "persons in military service" and the term "persons in the military service of the United

States," as used in this Act shall include the following persons and no others. All members of the Army of the United States, the United

States Navy, the Marine Corps, the 

Coast Guard, the Women's Army

Auxiliary Corps, and all officers of the Public Health Service detailed by proper authority for duty either with the Army or the Navy. The term "military service," as used in this Act, shall signify Federal service on active duty with any branch of service heretofore referred to or mentioned as well as training or education under the supervision of the United States preliminary to induction into the military service. The terms "active service" or "active duty" shall include the period during which a person in military service is absent from duty on account of sickness, wounds, leave, or other lawful cause.

The term "period of military service," as used in this Act, shall include the time between the following dates: For persons in active service 37

at the date of the approval of this Act it shall begin with the date of approval of this Act; for persons entering active service after the date of this Act, with the date of entering active service. It shall terminate with the date of discharge from active service or death while in active service, but in no case later than the date when this Act ceases to be in force. 

The term "person," when used in this Act with reference to the holder of any right alleged to exist against a person in military service or against a person secondarily liable under such right, shall include individuals, partnerships, corporations, and any other forms of business association.

The term "court," as used in this Act, shall include any court of competent jurisdiction of the United

States or of any state, whether or not a court of record.

TERRITORIAL APPLICATION OF ACT FORM OF PROCEDURE The provisions of this Act shall apply to the United States, the several States and Territories, the District of Columbia, and all territory subject to the jurisdiction of the United States, including the 

Philippine Islands while under the sovereignty of the United States, and to proceedings commenced in any court therein, and shall be enforced through the usual forms of procedure obtaining in such courts or under regulations as may be by them prescribed.

When, under this Act any application is required to be made to a court in which no proceeding has already been commenced with respect to the matter, such application may be made to any court.

PROTECTION OF PERSONS SECONDARILY Liable 

Whenever pursuant to any of the provisions of this Act the enforcement of any obligation or liability, the prosecution of any suit or proceeding, the entry or enforcement of any order, writ, judgment, or decree, or the performance of any other act, may be stayed, postponed, or suspended, such stay, postponement, or suspension may, in the discretion of the court, likewise be granted to sureties, guarantors, endorsers, accommodation makers, and others, whether primarily or secondarily subject to the obligation or liability, the performance or enforcement of which is stayed, postponed, or suspended.

When a judgment or decree is vacated or set aside in whole or in part, as provided in this Act, the same in the discretion of the court, may, likewise be set aside and vacated as to any surety, guarantor, endorser, accommodation maker, or other person whether primarily or secondarily liable upon the contract or liability for the enforcement of which the judgment or decree was entered.

EFFECT OF ACT 

Nothing contained in this Act shall prevent- (A) the modification, termination, or cancelation of any contract, lease, or bailment or any obligation secured by mortgage, trust deed, lien or other security in the nature of a mortgage, or (B) the repossession, retention, foreclosure, sale, forfeiture or taking possession of property which is security for any obligation or which has been purchased or received under a contract, lease, or bailment, pursuant to a written agreement of the parties thereto (including the person in military service concerned, or the person to whom this Act is applicable, whether or not such person is a party to the obligation), or their assignees, executed during or after the period of military service of the person concerned or during the period specified.

Default JUDGMENTS; 

AFFIDAVITS; BONDS 

In any action or proceeding commenced in any court, if there shall be a default of any appearance by the defendant, the plaintiff, before entering judgment shall file in the court an affidavit setting forth facts showing that the defendant is not in military service. If unable to file such affidavit plaintiff shall in lieu thereof file an affidavit setting forth either that the defendant is in the military service or that plaintiff is not able to determine whether or not defendant is in such service. If an affidavit is not filed showing that the defendant is not in the military service, no judgment shall be entered without first securing an order of court directing such entry, and no such order shall be made if the defendant is in such service until after the court shall have appointed an attorney to represent defendant and protect his interest, and the court shall on application make such appointment. Unless it appears that the defendant is not in such service the court may require, as a condition before judgment is entered, that the plaintiff file a bond approved by the court conditioned to indemnify the defendant, if in military service, against any loss or damage that he may suffer by reason of any judg ment should the judgment be thereafter set aside in whole or in part. 

And the court may make such other and further order or enter such judg ment as in its opinion may be necessary to protect the rights of the defendant under this Act. 

Any persons who shall make or use an affidavit required under this section, knowing it to be false, shall be guilty of a misdemeanor and shall be punishable by imprisonment not to exceed one year or by fine not to exceed $1,000, or both.

In any action or proceeding in which a person in military service is a party if such party does not personally appear therein or is not • 40

represented by an authorized attorney, the court may appoint an attorney to represent him; and in such case a like bond may be required and an order made to protect the rights of such person. But no attorney appointed under this Act to protect a person in military service shall have power to waive any right of the person for whom he is appointed or bind him by his acts. 

If any judgment shall be rendered in any action or proceeding governed by this section against any person in military service during the period of such service or within. thirty days thereafter, and it appears that such person was prejudiced by reason of his military service in making his defense thereto, such judgment may, upon application, made by such person or his legal representative, not later than ninety days after the termination of such service, be opened by the court rendering the same and such defendant or his legal representative let in to defend; provided it is made to appear that the defendant has a meritorious or legal defense to the action or some part thereof. Vacating, setting aside, or reversing any judgment because of any of the provisions of this Act shall not impair any right or title acquired by any bona fide purchaser for value under such judgment.

STAY OF PROCEEDINGS 

At any stage thereof any action or proceeding in any court in which a person in military service is involved, either as plaintiff or defendant, during the period of such service or within sixty days thereafter may, in the discretion of the court in which it is pending, on its own motion, and shall, on application to it by such person or some person on his behalf, be stayed as provided in this Act, unless, in the opinion of the court, the ability of plaintiff to prosecute the action or the defendant to conduct his defense is not materially affected by reason of his military service.

FINES AND PENALTIES ON CONTRACTS, ETC. 

When an action for compliance with the terms of any contract is stayed pursuant to this Act no fine or penalty shall accrue by reason of failure to comply with the terms of such contract during the period of such stay, and in any case where a person fails to perform any obligation and a fine or penalty for such non-performance is incurred a court may, on such terms as may be just, relieve against the enforcement of such fine or penalty if it shall appear that the person who would suffer by such fine or penalty was in the military service when the penalty was incurred and that by reason of such service the ability of such person to pay or perform was thereby materially impaired.

STAY ON VACATION OF EXECUTION OF JUDGMENTS, ETC. 

In any action or proceeding commenced in any court against a person in military service, before or during the period of such service, or 41.

within sixty days thereafter, the court may, in its discretion, on its own motion, or on application to it by such person or some person on his behalf shall, unless in the opinion of the court the ability of the defendant to comply with the judgment or order entered or sought is not materially affected by reason of his military service- (A) Stay the execution of any judgment or order entered against such person, as provided in this Act; and (B) Vacate or stay any attachment or garnishment of property, money, or debts in the hands of another, whether before or after judgment as provided in this Act. 

DURATION And Term of STAYS 

Any stay of any action, proceeding, attachment, or execution, ordered by any court under the provisions of this Act may, except as otherwise provided, be ordered for the period of military service and three months thereafter or any part of such period, and subject to such terms as may be just, whether as to payment in installments of such amounts and at such times as the court may fix or otherwise. Where the person in military service is a codefendant with others the plaintiff may nevertheless by leave of court proceed against the others.

HOMESTEAD ENTRYMEN PER- MITTED TO LEAVE ENTRIES TO PERFORM Farm Labor 

During the pendency of any war in which the United States may be engaged while this Act remains in force any homestead entryman shall be entitled to a leave of absence from his entry for the purpose of performing farm labor. Nothing in this section shall excuse any homestead entryman from making improvements or performing the cultivation upon his entry required by law. The provisions of this section shall apply only to persons whose applications have been allowed or filed prior to the effective date of this Act.

INCOME TAXES; COLLECTION DEFERRED; INTEREST 

The collection from any person in the military service of any tax on the income of such person, whether falling due prior to or during his period of military service, shall be deferred for a period extending not more than six months after the termination of his period of military service in such person's ability to pay such tax is materially impaired by reason of such service. 

No interest on any amount of tax, collection of which is deferred for any period under this section, and no penalty for nonpayment of such amount during such period, shall accrue for such period of deferment by reason of such nonpayment. The running of any statute of limitations against the collection of such tax by distraint or otherwise shall be suspended for the period of military service of any individual the collection of whose tax is deferred under this section, and for an additional period of nine months beginning with the day following the period of military service. The provisions of this section shall not apply to the income tax on employees imposed by section 1400 of the Federal In surance Contributions Act.

TRANSFERS TO TAKE ADVANTAGE OF ACT 

Where in any proceeding to enforce a civil right to any court it is made to appear to the satisfaction of the court that any interest, property, or contract has since the date of the approval of this Act been transferred or acquired with intent to delay the just enforcement of such right by taking advantage of this Act, the court shall enter such judgment or make such order as might lawfully be entered or made, the provisions of this Act to the contrary notwithstanding.

REVOCATION, ETC., OF INTERLOCUTORY ORDERS

SEPARABILITY OF PROVISIONS 

If any provision of this Act, or the application thereof to any person or circumstances, is held invalid, the remainder of the Act, and the application of such provision to other persons or circumstances, shall not be affected thereby.

TERMINATION OF ACT 

This Act shall remain in force until May 15, 1945: Provided, That should the United States be then engaged in a war, this Act shall remain in force until such war i terminated by a treaty of peace proclaimed by the President and for six months thereafter; Provided further, That whenever under any section or provision of this act a proceeding, remedy, privilege, stay, limitation, accounting, or other transaction has been authorized or provided with respect to military service performed prior to the date herein fixed for the termination of this Act, such section or provision shall be deemed to continue in full force and effect so long as may be deemed to be necessary to the exercise or enjoyment of such proceeding, remedy, privilege, stay, limitation, accounting, or other transaction.

STAY OF ENFORCEMENT 

A person may, at any time during his period of military service or within six months thereafter, apply 58

to a court for relief in respect of any obligation or liability incurred by such person prior to his period of military service or in respect of any tax or assessment whether falling due prior to or during his perod of military service. The court, after appropriate notice and hearing, unless in its opinion the ability of the applicant to comply with the terms of such obligation or liability or to pay such tax or assessment has not been materially affected by reason of his military service, may grant the following relief:

In the case of an obligation payable under its terms in installments under a contract for the purchase of real estate, or secured by a mortgage or other instrument in the nature of a mortgage upon real estate, a stay of the enforcement of such obligation during the applicant's period of military service and, from the date of termination of such period of military service or from the date of application if made after such service, for a period equal to the period of the remaining life of the installment contract or other instrument plus a period of time equal to the period of military service of the applicant, or any part of such combined period, subject to payment of the balance of principal and accumulated interest due and unpaid at the date of termination of the period of military service or from the date of application, as the case may be, in equal installments during such combined period at such rate of interest on the unpaid balance as is prescribed in such contract, or other instrument evidencing the obligation, for installments paid when due, and subject to such other terms as may be just.

In the case of any other obligation, liability, tax or assessment a stay of the enforcement thereof during the applicant's period of military service and, from the date of termination of such period of military service or from the date of application if made after such service, for a period of time equal to the period of military service of the applicant or any part of such period, subject to payment of the balance of principal and accumulated interest due and unpaid at the date of terminaice or the date of application, as the tion of such period of military servcase may be, in equal periodic installments during such extended period at such rate of interest as may be prescribed for such obligation, when due, and subject to such other liability, tax, or assessment, if paid terms as may be just.

When any court has granted a stay as provided in this section no fine or penalty shall accrue during the period the terms and conditions of such stay are complied with by reason of failure to comply with the terms or conditions of the obligation, liability, tax, or assessment in respect of which such stay was granted. 59.
