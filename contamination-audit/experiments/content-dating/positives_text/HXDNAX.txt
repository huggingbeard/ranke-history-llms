Preface 

THE glory of Spanish culture and chivalry which was carried to the far ends of the earth during the "Golden days of Spain" reached its most romantic charm along the drowsing shores of Florida, where, in 1565, the intrepid Spanish explorers founded the city of St. Augustine, considered by many as the oldest city in North America.

The fort which the Spaniards erected on the site now occupied by "Old Fort Marion," was, for more than two hundred years of its turbulent history, the frequent scene of romance, intrigue and sore tragedy. Nearly a hundred years ago 1833-there was discovered, under the ramparts of the old fort, amongst other gloomy cells, a "secret dungeon," tightly sealed which, on opening, disclosed human skeletons on the verge of turning to dust, the hapless creatures doubtless having been sealed in there alive to die!

The author chanced to visit the old fort in 1912 and it is around the romantic events of the ancient fortification that is fashioned the "Legend of Old St. Augustine, the tragic story of a Cavalier and his beauteous Castilian sweetheart. The poem was ten years in writing. The story begins in "Sunny Spain," moves across the sea to the New World and to the frowning fortress where the lovers were immured and where Florida, with her climate of sentient charm and mystery adds deepening interest to the romance of the gentle Lolita and her ardent and deathless Cavalier.

A Florida Idyll

Soft sandaled night crept in across the land

Dropping its sable mantle about the quaint old town.

Hushed was the restless murmuring of the strand; 

Stilled, the sylvan lanes where starlight drifted down.

Oppressed by some dull weight of sensate care, Weary of mirth and drone of moving feet,

Enticed by dewy moonlight-the sensuous air. 

And the strange sad mystery of each fading street.

I wandered on and musing crossed an ancient moat, Half filled with earth, its sides with grass o'er grown. 

A time scarred road shaped up in days remote, Upswept toward a portaled wall of stone. 

Behold a stolid square of rugged crouching walls, With ramparts grinning toward the open sea. 

Strange weight of masonry: weird darksome halls,

Grey with ageing time and mute with mystery. 

Perched above its dim escarpment, Soothed by balsamed midnight air, Long I rested; sweet contentment, Lulled and eased and held me there;

Far away seemed life's commotion,
