RUDYARD KIPLING, in his St. Andrews address on Independence, once broke through that reserve by which we all pretend that we are not alone in the world. This novel does the same thing, gently, poignantly, in beautiful and deeply moving pages. Here, etched unforgettably, is the picture of one woman who found that neither love nor affection nor friendship nor children nor anything else can dull the realization that much of life is a lonely story. 

Sara, whose life it is, will awaken a sharp chord of recognition in nearly every reader. Through her are revealed what so many people feel, what so seldom has been put in words.

She was married to Phillip Vandelt in an empty London church. The music of the organ was like a loftily built house that they would live in; the voices of the choir were the garden of their house.
