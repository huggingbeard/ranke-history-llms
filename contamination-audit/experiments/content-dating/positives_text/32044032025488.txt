In order to engender confidence and trust in yourself it is advisable to select the easiest questions first. Concentrate on the question you are answering and don't worry about the previous or subsequent questions. Never criticise a question, instead try to answer it. If the question may be answered in several ways, take one stand and discuss it the way you know best, unless the question states that you are to explain or discuss the several methods.

When answering a question in auditing, it is well to remember that it is being asked of the candidate in his capacity as an auditor, investigator or critic of accounts and not as a constructive accountant.

When answering a question in law, there s no need for writing a page or more of rambling opinion, when all that is required is a knowledge of the legal principle. Moreover, don't waste time arguing whether or not the facts as stated in the question could be proved, or that the situation could not arise in practice. Instead, assume that the facts are pracicable and that they are provable.

If the problem requires that you furnish the cost per unit, or when solving an annuity or amortization problem, it is advisable to submit a work sheet showing how the amount was determined.

The answers should be supported with short, apt and appropriate reasons. The can didate should cultivate a clear lucid style and state his answers in short sentences clearly, concisely and precisely to the point. 

If you are furnished with ruled paper, write on the lines and not between them. Avoid irrelevancy, sweeping assertions, vague gen eralities or straying from the question, and important also is to write legibly, for no matter how thoroughly you are able to answer the questions with ability, intelligence and accuracy, it will be of no value unless the answers can be read by the Examiner.
