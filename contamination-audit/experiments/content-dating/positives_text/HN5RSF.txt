The Guthre 

This book is not offered as the whole answer to the call for skill in physical combat, but solely as an addition to established methods. 

Some of the matter may not seem to be directed to you. Please therefore try to fit your individual circumstances into those instructions which are most adaptable.

The illustrations in this book are intended to help you develop a routine of attack and defense for yourself. Practice them all, but only one at a time.

The material contained herein has been written and illustrated from lectures and demonstrations given to combat and defense units all over 

America. 

The purpose of this book is to prime the imagination of those who are likely to be called upon to defend our America. Its purpose is to inform them that there is no "superior race." Superiority comes only to those who earn it through mental and physical alertness and training. 

Many letters from leading citizens complimented the author on the first edition of this book. Letters from men in the service particularly were greatly appreciated. These letters have all been helpful, for which the author is grateful.

Arrangements have been made with the publishers so that this book can be purchased by groups in civilian or national defense in quantities at reduced prices.

FOREWORD 

Jungle fighting in the South Sea Islands and the sneaking methods of the Jap patrols, early in the war, revived the myth of their unmatched abilities in man to man encounters; a myth built up over the years through Japanese estimates of Ju Jutsu, or Judo, as mystery fighting which the "whites" would be unable to solve. 

But what are the facts?

A story has been told of a vicious fight at the Tokyo Military Academy; some time before the present war started. An 

American amateur boxer, Captain Warren Clear of the United

States Military Service, was matched with the Ju Jutsu champion of the Imperial Japanese Army;-Captain Kitamura. 

With bland and smiling hypocrisy, the Japanese invited the match to provide a comparison of the relative merits of the American art of boxing as against Japanese Ju Jutsu. In truth, being egotistically confident of victory, their purpose was to prove the superiority of the yellow Japanese over the white races.

No quarter was to be shown. The fight was to continue until one or the other contestant was unable to continue. 

After several grueling, savagely-contested, five-minute rounds, the 185 lb. American conquered the 200 lb.-6 foot Japanese champion, who was carried unconscious from the ring. 

In this fight, the brutal, vicious character of Ju Jutsu as a fighting technique was savagely established, proving that for both attack and defense, it was something all American soldiers and marines should know. Ju Jutsu, or Judo, as it is sometimes called, is now a feature of the intensive training of our fighting men.

Jules Martell, (formerly champion Ju Jutsu wrestler of the United States Army) is the author of this book. He knows all the deceits, turns, tricks and subterfuges of Japanese Ju

Jutsu. While champion he met and defeated the Japanese champion Yamahiati, breaking his arm after but 28 seconds of fighting. This match was staged in Seattle, Washington, July 4th, 1917. [3]

These two victories over Jap champions, one by an American Ju Jutsu wrestler and one by an American boxer, demonstrate the ability of Americans to outfight the enemy even at his own extolled style of merciless hand-to-hand combat. 

Mr. Martell was a deadly bayonet fighter in night-patrol work with the Wild West Division (91st) in World War No. 1. 

He was wounded shortly before the armistice. His gruesome experiences in applying Ju Jutsu during those terrific days made him resolve to forget it and to teach, instead, the rules of fair play.

But the present hate-ridden war times have rekindled the former flame and he feels it to be a patriotic duty to instill Americans with a strong fighting spirit. 

Martell spent his youth on a large cattle and horse ranch in the American West. Thrilling stories are told of his calfwrestling, bull-dogging and horse-breaking days. Yet he is a business man, too; having been an executive in a number of corporations and is now in business on Long Island. He attended the University of Montana, also the University of Minnesota and has travelled extensively in Europe, Asia and Africa. He speaks several languages, among them being the strange and guttural tongue of the Sioux Indians.

Dan Edwards (most decorated "Soldier of Fortune" and the hero of Lowell Thomas' book "This Side of Hell" reveals his judgment of Martell in an autographed book in which he wrote: "To Martell, the most versatile man I have ever met-Boy, how I would like to fight with you some time but not against you."

In this book, we are proud to have the privilege of bringing to you Mr. Martell's illustrated instructions which reveal the manner in which, as the champion, he unleashed his dynamic, physical energies in the vicious art of Ju Jutsu which today our fighters cannot learn too well; tricks he so thoroughly understands and which will make any Jap or Nazi cry “Uncle”.

UNCOVERING THE "MYSTIC" JAP 

Ju Jutsu has long been cleverly clothed by the Japanese in a robe of mystery but is as cockeyed as it is fraudulent. The "art" of Ju Jutsu is so primitive that enlightened peoples should not give it a scientific place in their fighting program. It is, in this book, given consideration just as we analyze the instincts of the gorilla or other wild beast. And only as an aid in fighting a race of people who parallel the wild beast in their wartime tactics. It is a true reflection of the mental operation of the Japs as developed throughout the years, toward other peoples. From it we can judge the mental and physical reactions of this predatory people.

Not so long ago the Japs were a primitive people living under conditions little above the animal. Having remained so low as to be inferior to other nations, they naturally became a closely knit people with a marked national individual similarity. Hatred of other races was taught the Japanese by the small clique of selfappointed intellectuals. Ju Jutsu and other physical trickery was taught as a means of elevating the low morale of a decadent race. 

It was planned as a tonic for a national inferiority complex. 

The objective thinking of the Japanese was, and still is, to become superior to other peoples by subterfuge and systematic trickery. The practice is general. It radiates from the single Jap in his relation with other people; from Japan in its relation to other countries. The Japanese welcomed the idea, that by taking advantage of the rules of fair play as universally practiced by civilized peoples-they could become factors of recognized importance among the nations of the world. The rules of civilization by which and for which man progresses is ignored systematically or violated by the Japanese. The Japanese cancel honor with hatred; fair play with foul play; truth with trickery.

By their own acts they have ostracized themselves from any rights to relationship with civilized nations. The Japanese can now best be understood when compared to the animal kingdom. 

They especially ape the most ferocious members of the cat family. And thus we find the Jap imitator cleverly mimicking the slinking habits of the wild and ferocious feline. 

Let Us Analyze the Man of Nippon 

He will suffer unending hardship and patience to ambush his adversary. [5]

He will learn the rules of American fair play, to become more efficient at Japanese foul play.

He endures hunger and cold or other self-inflicted suffering, even death, to prove to himself his physical superiority. 

His every move is instinctively covered by subterfuge. 

His every thought is camouflaged and deeply hidden by a facial expression entirely devoid of human personality. 

With the entire human race he uses his blank countenance to deceive.

Like the beast of prey, he lives in constant fear of being discovered in his foul tactics.

He is constantly planning to destroy that which, by his own reasoning, gives him an inferior complex. 

He therefore revels in the minor things of life in which he excels, and recoils from demonstrations by which his inferiority may be exposed.

His mind concentrates on small details, in the search for human weaknesses in others as vulnerable points for Japanese advancement.

He imitates, copies or mimics with such perfection that he appears to have talent. When analyzing the Japanese industrial and military history, it becomes most evident that it has been developed entirely on mimicry, subterfuge and treachery. The 

Japanese people, in such an environment, have developed their feline nature and like a beautiful housecat, invited or not, have presumed to join the domestic family of the world. 

There Is No Mystery 

To create confidence in their people the Japanese developed the system of Ju Jutsu, or bodily mayhem. This course was developed with the hope that the inferior Jap might win from the rest of the world the respect that the fighting tomcat has in Hogan's alley. Like the alley cat, the Japanese plan to create fear and will do everything in their power to develop a respect for a prowess claimed as theirs alone. They want the world to fear Ju Jutsu. 

The foregoing picture of the Japanese is offered to counteract the foolish statements widely circulated; "that we do not understand Ju Jutsu, or Judo, or whatever name one may call [6]

this Japanese 'mumbo-jumbo.'" The Japs would like nothing better than to have us believe that they have invented or created something new which can belong only to them. 

The Jap's "art of self-defense” or personal combat, lies no deeper than the cat's adeptness to climb, scratch, spit, lie in ambush, etc. There is nothing secret: nothing unusual about Ju 

Jutsu. Its only value in combat is its unfairness. And, in the civilized world, people for hundreds of years have been scorned for taking unfair advantage.

If anything, man for man, the Japanese are inferior physically, scientifically and industrially to most every other people on earth and especially to Americans. This statement is grounded in the fact that they have inferior eyesight, inferior physique and are entirely devoid of imagination and initiative. They depend entirely on attacking enemies where they are temporarily vulnerable. And today our only apparent vulnerability is due to the rules and standards of civilization which for centuries we have been taught to respect. 

Should Americans decide to disregard all rules of fair play, and to meet foul play with foul play in any human struggle, be it war, or whatnot, they would be the most destructive force on earth. Up to now, we have developed only the higher ideals of our people. Should we develop the baser instincts taking the worst characteristics of each, in a country of all nationalities, the human mind cannot conceive the savage and destructive war machine that could be created. 

Americans, for the Present, Must Forget Humaneness 

Americans do not "hate" and "fight" because they want something others have. But they will always fight to the end that they shall not be robbed of their country nor of their liberty. 

It is but a short step from civilization to barbarism-from fairness to foulness, but Americans are so firmly grounded in the golden rule, that it becomes necessary to re-create our nearly. lost fighting instincts. In many instances it is only a matter of removing the restraints of civilization to uncover a fighter. In all cases it means that we must turn the pages of civilization backwards. Those whose duty requires them to become more proficient at man-to-man battling must study this retreat to instincts, piece by piece. Any American who will devote a brief period each day to analyzing and practicing hand to hand combat will [7]

very soon be far superior to any Japanese no matter how steeped that enemy may be in the so-called Japanese Ju Jutsu. 

To acquire speed and skill in hand to hand combat one must consider every part of the human body from head to toes. Each part of the body must be evaluated and its importance in crippling the whole body must be understood and remembered. Yet, no two people will get the same benefits from a study of hand to hand combat technique. Due to man's difference in size and native skill, each person must develop those phases of such a course best adapted to his own personal capacities. 

In this book various factors of the human machine are pointed out. Each of these is important. Don't depend entirely on some single trick. Remember-the real winner has more than one string to his bow, for even a great champion can miss his first try.

The eyes of the fighter are by far the most important factor in deciding the outcome in a man-to-man struggle. 

Your opponent's eyes can fool you, or trick you. Also by using your own eyes skilfully you can mislead your opponent and make him set himself in a position you desire for your next move. The eyes can feign helplessness; or in other words "play possum." The eyes can frighten; show hatred; create fear;-or even freeze your opponent.

Without eyes man is useless as a fighting machine. So protect your eyes well at all times. This does not mean that you should close or cover them. Learn to protect yourself by taking the offensive in keeping your opponent from injuring your eyes. But always keep your hands free for attack or defense.

Getting at your opponent's eyes is the best guard for your own eyes. 

If your opponent throws things to blind you such as dirt, sand, etc., turn your head sideways so that the nose will protect the one eye, turn and return head into position. Never shield your eyes with your hands. Hands are fighting tools. The reason your opponent throws dirt is to take your hands out of the fight and thus give him a free try at your vulnerable spots. 

Your Hands-Use Them But Protect Them, Too

The hands are more than fighting tools. They can be used to express feelings or to mislead opponents. Strike with them, [8]

hammer with them, gouge with them, throw, lift, slap, scratch, push, pull, guard. But besides those things the delicate play of action, gesture or motion which can deceive or trick an opponent has immense value.

Strange as it may seem, the hand is a one way mechanism. 

It is strong in one direction and weak in reverse. This is so of the whole hand from the finger-tips to the wrist; as a matter of fact, it is true of all the jointed parts of the human body. 

The hand is vulnerable to injury by many simple twists and turns. Some hands are more developed than others but they are all weak if attacked at the right time in the right way. 

The thumb is always easy to bend back, or any finger can be bent back into breaking or dangerous position. 

Your hands should be used with speed. Out fast and back fast until you get what you want. Never permit your hands to linger within clutching reach of your opponent. 

Arms are strong when folded near the chest. Power lessens as the arms are extended. The proper use of this knowledge is a great advantage in man-to-man combat. 

Legs are much like arms. They can hit a terrific blow, but lose power in extended position. 

Study and memorize the weak areas of the human body. (See Illustration No. 1.) Attack there with intensity when pitted against an enemy. But, be very careful when practicing with friends.

When striking with hands or feet, strike fast and deep and snap back with all the speed you possess. Fashion your hands so as to hit the small nerve centers. Strike as if you expected to penetrate the target.

Never look your opponent in the eye except to deceive him. 

Watch his hands; his feet. His attack comes from them; not from his eyes.

Some of the savage acts of mutilation practiced by Japanese officers upon American prisoners are evidence to prove how low they will go to prove to themselves and to their ignorant soldiers their superiority over the white race. From such an enemy we can expect only uncivilized treatment. Beware! At no time give them an opening by exercising the usual sympathies and squaredealing which are second nature to Americans. [9]

JU JUTSU 

Ju Jutsu combat is a combination of all known types of physical combat developed by man. It is a flesh and bone, man to man, personal struggle, compiled from the methods of all lands and from the instincts of all predatory animals. 

Ju Jutsu is the only contest among humans where no rules are observed, no quarter asked and none given.

This instinctive combat is not difficult to master but it is difficult to get real Americans to violate all the rules of civilization and, without restriction, to revert to killer instincts. 

Dogs, cats, and other animals can conduct exciting contests in playful moods-entirely opposite to the struggle when in deadly earnest. But, Ju Jutsu is never playful. It has no rule except to physically disable opposition.

Every weakness in anatomy is a vulnerable spot and accepted point of attack.

Every trick in subterfuge becomes common procedure in decoying or misleading an adversary. Deception is the theme of operation; speed the technique of execution.

Deceit :-The Main Factor 

All life is sustained through the survival of the fittest. This does not always mean the strongest, but those that fit best into local and immediate conditions. In Ju Jutsu, deception, with intent to mislead and to throw off balance, plays an important part. It is of equal importance for those on defense as for those taking the offensive. [ 11 ]

Stealth, ambush, patience, anticipation and imagination are all factors in deception. Determination, concentration and coordination are factors in building individual confidence and explosive action.

Knowledge is a factor in learning Ju Jutsu combat but not a prerequisite. The wolf has it, the weasel has it and the Japs have it. So do most other predatory animals. 

Knowledge can be utilized in many ways far beyond the realm of instinct.

Let us first briefly analyze the attributes or senses of the killer animal. 

SIGHT: good because of patience, concentration and alertness and a true synchronization of and with the other senses. 

SMELL: keen due to a constant search and alertness for the unseen things.

HEARING: always tuned in to the general surroundings and responded to immediately. 

FEELING: always a messenger of strange contacts.

These, plus taste and lessons from experience, are the fighting equipment of animals.

These senses in animal are usually aroused beyond normal by sex or hunger; seldom for the love of killing. Ferocious animals play rough games with each other without injury. Man with knowledge and intelligence has an advantage over the ignorant animal if he will throw himself into the fray with full determination and confidence. The objective is to subdue, to maim, to kill.

Win Confidence Through Understanding 

Man can easily learn the most vital and vulnerable parts of the human body and the utilization of things within reach at the time of combat. He has more imagination. He can develop teamwork into large coordinating units. He can develop diversity by exchange of knowledge. He can pre-analyze his opponents and thereby avoid fear and he can create fear in his antagonist [12]

Ju Jutsu Is Always Serious Business 

There are many ways of killing or maiming in Ju Jutsu combat. They should be developed slowly through study and practice. 

Nearly every phase or hold is dangerous if carried out. Use utmost caution if practicing with a friend. Do not play rough in practice. Bones and joints snap much more easily than expected.

Action-And More Action 

Plan your work and work your plan. Your fight is man to man. Size up the situation, determine your objective and then explode, and keep exploding until you've won.

When the time comes for action, don't hesitate but demonstrate fast and furious with but one object in mind and that is to subdue your antagonist.

Take Every Available Advantage 

Some doubt may arise in your mind about your size. That does not make as much difference as it would seem. Small men are faster. Large men are stronger and slower, but both will cave in when you hit their vital spots. Don't worry about their sizejust aim at a vulnerable spot and explode. The weasel gets the rabbit and the wolf gets the sheep or the lion the deer by speed and explosive action. 

Noiseless night capture-carry a small piece of rope about four feet long-one end in each hand-approach your opponent from the rear. Throw the rope over and around his head-turn around fast and you will have a half hitch around his neck. Pull him up on your shoulder and strangle him into noiseless subjection. 

Ju Jutsu is not a pastime: not a game: never a friendly contest: nothing but the art of sudden death.
