He was right, so right! I melted my cap in the small kitchen spoon. I placed a small wad of white cotton in it and drew the precious dreams up into the syringe. 

I thought of the hot dog counter and remembered the smell of grilling franks. I had a vision of myself trodding up and down behind that lousy counter-once I walked it, twice, three times. Then I jolted the H.

It must have been Chi-chi's first time to cut, and she goofed. It was so pure that it hit me with the impact of a road grader going downhill. I hadn't jolted for nearly seven months and I was very susceptible. 

A funny thought hit me as I sank onto a floor pillow in the living room filled with the sound of recorded jazz. 

Ninety per cent of all addicts kicked back to H-for once in my life I belonged to a majority! That was very funny, and I was laughing softly when I heard Chi-chi scream. 156

"He's dead! Goddamn it, Tony, I tell you he's dead."

I tried to open my eyes, but it was just an outside annoyance. Who was dead? Nobody I knew! "The bastard!" I heard Tony growl. "Why did he have to die here? The cheap s.o.b. could've died in an alley. He'd have been just as happy. Who did he come with? That good-looking redhead?" "She's just a tramp!" Chi-chi shrilled. "She's stoned. 

Look at her." "Well, we've got to get them out of here." 

I felt someone shaking me and I opened my eyes. Tony was leaning close. "The heat!" 

I shot up out of my seat. The police. Where? "Look, get your friend out of here!" "Who?" "Him!" I followed the point of his finger to Don's body slumped in the corner. "He's dead from an overdose.” "Don!" I screamed. "He can't be dead!" "Look at him, sister!" Chi-chi screamed, shaking Don, his head slumped to one side. "If that ain't dead, I'm a three-headed mongoose from South India." 

Well, she wasn't that, but neither was Don my responsibility. There was nothing I could do for him now. It was too late for him. And suddenly I had the feeling it might be too late for me!

In a moment of weakness I had destroyed everything I'd worked months for! I fled from the apartment and bolted downstairs, staggering as I bounced against the walls, and finally stumbled into the street. 

Oh, God! What had I done?

I tore at my hair as I ran down the street, dodging through the traffic at intersections, past The Monkey Bar and onto the main plaza. 

Don was dead . . . dead . . . Dead! He was just another dirty hype in a gutter alley, dead from too much heroin. 

That's what the cops would say, and they would be right this time. That's how hypes die, and suddenly I was afraid. 

That wasn't how I wanted to die. 

I didn't want to curl up with an overdose of H and die with no one to care except the pusher who'd miss my gold. 

I forced myself to walk faster and faster up Mesa Avenue.

I was practically running when I stumbled against an isolated phone booth.

Groping into my bag, I discovered that I had only a quarter in change, but I used it to call Amon. The hospital 157

operator asked who it was and I told her that it was an emergency. I could hear her begin Amon's page call. Bong.

Bong. Bong-bong. Bong. 

It repeated several times and I felt despair creeping up my legs and freezing my whole body. Amon knew and he'd never answer. "Hello. Dr. Morris." "Amon!" "Valerie. What's wrong?" "Amon. I need you!" I cried. "Are you at home?” "Almost." "I'll be right there. You'll be there? That's a promise?" "Yes, yes. I'll be there Amon. Please hurry." 

I clutched the telephone to my breast as tears flooded from my eyes. Amon understood! There was no question, no prying, no doubts or fears. He simply understood that I needed him and he was coming. 

At that moment, I knew I loved him. 

I didn't know then that love can come in a moment of genuine understanding between two people, and that it is moral strength which would help me to fight a problem that would plague me the rest of my life.

There was so much that I had to learn from Amon. His love was like a gentle cloak that surrounded me as I told him what had happened when he burst into the apartment ten minutes later. "Amon, are you still willing to help me, even after this?”

I knelt at his feet and buried my face in his hands. "I need you, but I can't hold you." His thin surgeon's fingers felt packed with power as he raised my chin and forced me to look into his eyes. "I told you once, Valerie. Love is a two-way street. It has to be mutual, and forgiveness has to be the same thing. If you really want me, I'll help you." 

I laid my cheek into his palm. "Amon-Amon, I do." 

Then he lifted me and touched his lips to mine and I yielded to him as I had never done before. It was like the beginning of an avalanche and I ran gleefully to meet it. "Amon, I love you!" 

He kissed my eyes and throat. His hand swept down the front of my body. "Valerie, my darling. I love you." 

Then he kissed me again and again until my lips were bruised. Now I had a real reason to fight my habit, and now I was armed with Amon's love. 158

Of course, I knew that the miracle I found with Amon was not a cure. Love is never a cure for anything and dope addiction is always a sickness. It is like having a horrible disease in your blood that will never go away.

Now, many months after our marriage, with my first baby on the way, I still have the urge to take heroin. It comes at unexpected times, when the day has been especially trying or when I'm sick with the repeated nausea of pregnancy, or when Amon and I have a disagreement. That's when it comes back to haunt me like a ghost that will never, never die.

The hunger for heroin will live forever in my body, and at times of crisis it curls up inside of me like a screaming, hungry tiger. I don't think it will ever die. I don't believe 

I will ever be completely free.

I know this because I am a dope addict. I am one now, and I always will be. 

God help me, and those like me.
