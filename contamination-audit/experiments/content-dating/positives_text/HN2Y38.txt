INTRODUCTION 

This GUIDE is the outcome of a request from a number of merber agencies of the Children's Welfare Federation for aid in answering the questions about children from birth to six years propounded to workers in CHILD HEALTH 

CENTERS by parents, grandparents, or guardians.

Apparently it has been too little recognized by doctors, nurses, educators and social service workers that the problers confronting each new generation of parents are perennial and that these questions, no Katter how trivial, irrelevant or whimsical they may appear to professionally trained persons, are of vital importance to the parents and should not be brushed off lightly. 

Either these questions should be answered on the basis of the best available medical-socio-psychologic knowledge or the answer should be "we don't know exactly", and the reasons given for the lack of satisfactory information. 

In an attempt to meet this request, spokesmen of agencies interested in and affiliated with the Children's Welfare Federation were requested to submit sample questions which in turn were referred to experts in the various fields of child care. The end result is the presented material which admittedly is fer from perfect and is frankly experimental in its present form. It is illuminating that sore of the "simpler" questions have been found most difficult to cope with authoritatively. However, the phrasing of the answers to controversial questions represents the majority opinion of a representative committee.

It is hoped that this initial effort will be productive of rany criticisrs and a host of new questions submitted so that subsequent revisions of this Guide ray be of greater service to the community. 

This Guide, therefore, is intended for use in Child Care Centers by nurses, teachers, social workers, volunteers or anyone else who has to interpret to parents in simple language the advice of experts in various fields related to child health services. It is an attempt to correlate and integrate the present knowledge concerning the infant and pre-school child in such a way that it may be presented to the parents or guardians most convincingly, wost specifically and rost sirply. Mis information may lead to great confusion and result in nore harm than good. What to leave unsaid is frequently just as important as what to say. Above all, it is important to obviate the criticism so aptly expressed by the Chinese philosopher "He who knows, does not tell; he who tells, does not know".

To The Worker Suggestions for Using the Manual 

Be friendly towards the parent. Do not scold her or "ball her out," or "lay down the law." Avoid harshness or severity even if you think she is handling her child badly or is irresponsible or at fault in some other way. Remember that her mistakes may be due to ignorance, or to her own personality problems. You will get much farther in gaining her cooperation if you are warm and sympathetic. Parents need this. 

Use simple language. This is not always easy. It means that you will have to pay attention to your words and choose better ways of saying things. You have more education and more technical knowledge than the parent, and are probably more accustomed to big words. Therefore, you must stop to think which words and ideas may not be clear to her. This does not mean that you must never use technical words. But when you do, make sure the parent understands. It takes skill and experience to use simple language and yet not "talk down" but it can be done and it is important. 

A good saying to remember is "Never underestimate the intelligence of people nor overestimate their education." 

Avoid making the parents worry. Sometimes for emphasis, or in order to make a point clear, the doctor or nurse will say things in such a way as to make the parents worry. This is unfortunate. Of course, some parents are much more prone to worry than others. When you sense that the parent is over-anxious, be especially careful how you phrase your recommendations. This type of parent is likely to be literal and conscientious and go to extremes. 

Do all you can to reassure anxious parents. A worried, anxious mother cannot take the best care of her child. With more casual, easygoing parents you can use a different approach. Be careful not to fall into any one stereotyped method of handling all parents. Use different techniques with different people.

If the question is not clear, or if it seems unusual or out of place, try to find out what prompts it. Many, many times the question which the parent puts in words is not the one she is really trying to ask or has in mind. In fact, she may scarcely know herself what she is trying to ask. Try to draw her out. Say "What did you have in mind?" "Why did you ask that?". "What do you mean by so-and-so?"

GROWTH STANDARDS 

It is important to remember that the physical and mental growth of the child develop together. Every advance in physical development is dependent upon the growth of the brain and nerves. Because of this it is unwise to expect either mental or physical habits to develop until the right time has arrived. There are wide variations in individuals due to heredity as well as to environmental, physiological and endocrinological differences in the individual. For example, two entirely healthy children one year of age may vary as much as 10 to 12 lbs. in weight. In general each child should be permitted to develop along its own individual pattern and should not always be expected to "keep up with the Joneses. There has never yet been a completely satisfactory and fool proof table of norms for development. 11 The workers will do well to keep these broad principles in mind when answering the following questions.

PHYSICAL AND MENTAL 

1. At what age should the soft spot on my baby's head close?

The soft spot (anterior fontenelle) closes at some time between 12 and 18 months. Medical opinion should be secured to explain early or delayed closing. 

2. What is normal height, weight for my child?

Height and weight are variables, depending upon build. This depends on physical structure and heredity. Growth patterns are not absolutely uniform from child to child. The weight of children under 

1 year quickly reflects their general health. Height is not so quickly influenced by the general health of the child. 

3. Why is my child so puny?

If he is frequently or chronically ill he should be under constant medical supervision. Many normal, healthy children vary in their size in the same way as adults. It depends in a large part on who their forbears were. 

4. My child does not gain as much as another child. What should I do?

Weight is not the only indication of health but rather the total progress of the childs' growth and development. Other important signs of good health are color of skin, activity, rest, happiness, appetite, and muscle tone. If these are satisfactory, the rate of gain is not too important. 

5. How often should a baby be weighed?

Healthy young babies need not be weighed oftener than once a week. 

When older, once a month is sufficient, if the child is sleeping and eating well and otherwise appears to be gaining.

Physical and Mental 6. 

How much should my child gain in the first year of life?

The average birth weight is 7 to 7 lbs. It is normal to double the birth weight by 5 months of age, triple the birth weight by one year. At five years, the infant usually weighs twice as much by the end of the first year. 

7. How often should nermal children from 2 to 6 years be weighed and measured? 

About twice a year. 

8. When do babies sit up? 

Babies learn to sit up between 6 and 9 months. At first they sit leaning forward in a slumped position when they are placed sitting. 

Gradually they sit more erect until they even fall backward if not supported. Finally, they learn to keep their balance and get up to a sitting position without help. 

9. Why does my baby fall over when he sits. up?

He falls over because his sense of balance has not yet developed and his back muscles are not yet sufficiently strong. He may be developing more slowly than some children do; or you may be expecting him to develop more rapidly than he should. He may have a physical disorder such as rickets. If you are worried about it, ask the doctor, 10. When can I prop my baby up with pillows?

When an infant begins to enjoy being held sitting and lifting his head forward when placed on his back, that is a sign that he may be propped up with pillows for short periods each day. 

11. How may I know when to start using a high chair for my baby?

A high chair may be used when a baby can sit in one comfortably without slumping. Usually this is sometime between 6 and 9 months.

Some mothers use a soft pillow in the high chair at the baby's back when they first put him in it. He should be taken out of the high chair as soon as he shows signs of being tired. 

12. When do normal babies stand?

Some babies will support their weight if held standing as early as 

5 months. Between and 11 months most babies will stand, if both hands are firmly held. By 11 or 12 months they can pull themselves up in their pen or crib. Soon after they stand alone.

13. 

When do babies walk?

Between a year and 18 months of age, healthy children begin to take a few steps alone. Some children are very cautious about walking alone, others are more adventurous. Do not try to hurry a child's development - you may do more harm than good because he will walk when he is ready. "Baby walkers" are not recommended. 

14. When do babies see?

Babies see at birth, but useful vision begins later. They begin to look at a person's face between one and two months of age. They notice large objects in the distance first. They also notice moving objects first. Between 3 and 4 months they like to look at their hands.

15. What should I de about my young child who stutters?

Many children stutter occasionally when they are learning to talk and during periods of rapid growth or pressure for social adjustment. The mother should relax and wait patiently until the child says what he is trying to say. Do not make him say it over or correct him except perhaps by saying it slowly and correctly after him in an understanding way. If the speech difficulty continues, she should seek special help.

18. If my child is ambidexterous, what should I do?

Teach him to use the right hand because it is a right-handed world. 

19. My child is slow in developing. 

What can I do about it?

There are many causes for slow development. Consult a doctor since an expert opinion should be obtained. The exact level of development can be estimated to a fair degree at 4 to 5 months.

Probaby not.

With congenital defect it all depends on what the cause of the trouble is. Each case requires very careful study by a doctor.

ROUTINE CARE PERSONAL CARE BATHING 

21. How often should a baby be bathed after its first few weeks of life? 

A daily bath is recommended for young babies. However, individual needs should be considered, such as the baby's health, the temperature of the home, the mother's general condition and other home responsibilities. Great care should be given to holding the baby securely. Care should be taken not to get soap and water in the eyes and to make the bath a pleasant experience. 

Is it better to bathe an infant in the morning or evening?

This depends on the family routine, the temperature of the home and the baby's needs. It is better to bathe before a meal than immediately following. 

23. Which is better - a lap bath or a table bath? 24.

The baby can be bathed either on the lap or on a properly covered and equipped table top. 

What is the best soap to use? 

Any mild unscented soap. 

25. Do I bathe him in the same water when he urinates in his bath?

Yes. Face and head have already been washed. The objections are purely aesthetic and have nothing to do with health. 

26. Can I wash my baby's face with soap and water? 27.

Yes, but it is not necessary. Be sure to keep the soap out of his eyes. 

Should he be bathed when he has a cold?

Ordinarily it is not wise to give tub baths during a real cold. A sponge bath may be given. 

28. How often should the eyes be bathed?

Eyelids should be cleansed only at time of the daily bath. 

29. How should I clean my baby's nose?

Clean only when necessary. Make a special swab with cotton, moisten lightly with water, pinch dry and remove from tooth pick. Hold the head securely while cleaning the nose. 

30. How should the ears be cleaned? 

Only the outside of the ears should be cleaned.

Cotton shirts are just as good. They are more easily laundered and most doctors prefer them. Wool and silk or wool and cotton in winter may be desirable

Birds-eye is the old standard. The gauze variety is highly satisfactory.

Renove the stool if any. Soak diapers in borax solution until ready for washing. Wash in mild soap, rinse 2 or 3 times, and hang in the sun to dry. Boil one to two times a week if unable to dry out of doors.

Change diapers after nursing as your baby is apt to have a movement while feeding. Wash the baby's diapers carefully as directed above. 56. How long should a sun bath be given a new baby?

This will of course depend on the weather. In general, it is best to start with one or two minutes exposure, increasing a minute a day front and back up to 15 minutes. This should be given during the early spring and fall months, between the hours 11 A.M. and 2 P.M. In the summer it should be before 11 A. M. and after 3 P.M. Children of fair complexion should be watched carefully. Your baby's skin is sensitive and sunburn should be prevented, There is no advantage in extreme browning of the skin. Be careful when sunning baby that he does not get hot, perspiry and uncomfortable. If the weather is too cool to undress the child entirely, the face, hands and feet can be exposed for short periods of time. 

57. Should the baby's eyes be covered during the sun bath?

Usually not. In direct sunshine the crib or carriage should be turned so that the sunlight is utilized without direct glare in the baby's eyes. 

SLEEP 

58. Should I strap my baby down in bed and keep him covered all night?

He needs some freedom particularly if he is awake in the morning sometime before you go to him. This is baby's best play time and he needs freedom then. He should not be restrained by a strap. The bed clothes should not be allowed to press the feet down or to prevent free movement of the feet and toes. A rolled blanket placed crosswise inside the lower end of the bed will take the weight of the bed clothes off the child's feet. 

59. Is my child's bed rocking serious?

As a rule, no. Many children rock themselves to sleep. However, if he does it a great deal it may mean that the time of putting him to bed should be changed. Ho may be either overtired or not tired and bored with bed. Most often it clears up spontaneously by the 4th year. 

60. May I rock my child to sleep? 

Yes, but don't jounce him. 

61. What can I do to prevent my young baby from sleeping on one side of his. head all the time?

Try turning his crib in the opposite direction during the day time so that he will have to turn to the other side to watch things. Put him to sleep in different positions. Put a pillow under the mattress if he rolls. There is no harm even if his head flattens a little; frequently by the end of his first year or usually by the third year this has cleared up. Peoples' heads are not always symmetrical. 

62. When is my baby old enough to sleep on his stomach?

As soon as he is born if he is a healthy active baby. Use a firm mattress but do not use a pillow.

Whatever position the baby chooses. It may be advisable to turn him from one side to the other from time to time. See questions on Posture. Page 34.

It depends on the individual baby. When a baby sleeps through his feeding time this means that he is changing his routine normally as he is growing older. If a well infant is hungry he will awaken by himself.

65. What sleeping garments are advisable?

Dress the baby according to season. Garments should be light weight and absorbent, not too confining. In winter light warm clothing is advisable such as cotton shirt, diaper and nightgown with drawstrings at bottom. 

For older children two-pants sleeping suits are suggested.

If it is not so tight about the neck to create danger of strangling and if it does not confine the child too much. Some other sleeping garment is better when a child reaches the age when he wants to stand up in the crib 

64. What should the temperature of the baby's sleeping room be?

Cool, but not too cold. For a very young baby the temperature of the room should not go much below 70 degrees fahrenheit; for the older baby it should not go below 55 degrees fahrenheit. There should be fresh air in circulation. 

68. How shall I get my child to bed without crying if he objects. too strenuously?

This depends upon the establishment of good routines. These are easily upset. Fre-school children should be told before bedtime by such statements as: "Just a few more minutes. "When the clock strikes." "When the hand of the clock is here." "Daddy will come in to say goodnight." "We'll sing a lullaby." "Take Teddy to bed. "Take Teddy to bed. Teddy's tired." Make going to bed as pleasant as possible. Some bedtime rituals are advisable such as story telling, reading, singing and prayers.

Although it is true that it is not advisable to pick the child up everytime he cries, nevertheless, excessive crying is a sign that something is wrong. 

Do not be afraid of picking him up and comforting him. "Let him cry it out" is an idea which is not encouraged these days. 

70. What shall I do about his calling after he gets in bed?

What you do depends upon his reasons for calling. All children do this. 

Expect it. Go to him two or three times when he calls, then calmly and firmly say he must go to sleep and do not answer him. He may get out of his crib and come to you. Say very little but just take him back repeatedly if necessary, Be reasonable but not over-indulgent. Pretending you are asleep may help. If it becomes a real problem, ask the doctor for advice. 

His day routines may need to be change d. It may be that he is getting too little attention in the daytime.

Go to him, take him up and reassure hir. Night fears may be a result of anxiety due to many different factors. They may be caused by digestive disconforts or by vivid imagination which reaches a peak at 

5 years of age. Then too, children have difficulty in distinguishing between what is real and what is not. They think a shadow is really a wolf or a bear. 

If fearfulness is unduly upsetting, ask a doctor about it. 

Do not make fun of the child.

73. Is it alright for him to sleep in my bed at night?

As a rule this is not a good practice and never advisable for an infant. 

74. Shall I take him into my bed in the morning?

This depends upon the household routine and circumstances. This practice as a daily hebit should not be condoned, but it may merit as any occasional treat for the child. 

75. Is it alright for him to sleep in the same room with his parents? 

No, not if it can possibly be avoided. It puts a strain on marital relations which are likely to disturb the child more than parents realize. Children are often awake when parents think they are asleep.

76. Should children sleep in the same bed?

It is better for them to sleep alone. Their movements during sleep disturb each other more than is realized and one child or the other is harmed by it. Boys and girls should be separated by the time they reach six years of age. 

77. My child does not sleep at nap time. Shall I give it up?

A rest period or a quiet period even if he does not sleep is healthful. 

78. How old should my child be before I give up his. afternoon nap?

It depends on the child. Many children indicate when this should be. 

If he does not sleep when he is put down for a nap in a quiet, cool, shaded room and if he appears wide awake, it may help to let him look quietly at books for the nap period. A quiet rest period or quiet play period alone is desirable up to 4 or 5 years of age. However, sleep is an individual matter and night sleep may be better if the day nap is given up. 

79, Should I wake my child from his afternoon nap? 

1 to 2 hours of sleep in an afternoon is sufficient if the child has had an adequate night's rest.

Good sitting posture depends to a large extent upon the furniture used by the child. No child can be expected to sit up straight if his legs are hanging with feet unsupported. Dental Decay or caries is a localized disease attacking first the enamel and then the dentine of the teeth. The onset is insiduous but it progresses rapidly once the dentine has been reached. It always starts on an external surface and works inward. The surface showing the greatest predeliction to decay are those least accessible to the buffering action of the saliva such as contact points between teeth and developmental fissures of the molars. Under no circumstances can minerals be withdrawn from the teeth as they can from bone. As far as is known, quality or hardness of teeth is in no way related to their vulnerability to decay. 

100% caries prevention is not yet possible. The number of cavities can be reduced by adequate diet with a minimum of sweets. This is borne out in institutions where a standardized dietary can be rigidly enforced. 

It is essential that children's teeth be examined at regular intervals so that cavities can be detected and filled in their incipiency. Where decay has become extensive enough to cause pain, extraction is in most instances the only resource. 

Does the mother's. prenatal diet have any appreciable effect upon the structure of her child's teeth? 313 Any diet that is nutritionally adequate for its mother's well-being is ample for the dental requirements of her unoorn child. Microscopic exarination of ground sections of deciduous teeth demonstrates that the portion formed before birth is uniformly of better quality than that formed after birth. Such mineralization as occurs prenatally in the deciduous dentition requires such a small amount it is inconceivable it could cause any drain upon the mother's store of minerals. 

The permanent teeth do not celcify until after birth. 

314. Can dental caries be reduced by dietaty means?

There is evidence to indicate that a high protein-high fat diet with a minimum of carbohydrate, reinforced into Fall, Winter and Spring with adequate Vitamin D, will reduce the number of teeth attacked by caries. While the cause of dental decay is still somewhat controversial, the initial lesion is always external and seems initiated by localized carbohydrate breakdown.

EARS 

Hearing is the most important sense in the development of a baby's speech and language. It is imperative, therefore, that parents be aware of the need for good aural health care.

Any abnormal symptom of the ear such as pain, discharge, should have immediate medical care. If such treatment is delayed hearing loss may result.

Parents should be counseled to watch for signs of impaired hearing. If they have any doubt about the child's hearing, they should consult a doctor. 

357. What is the first sign of faulty hearing in young children?

If a child doesn't learn to speak and does not understand ordinary speech like other children of his age; if he doesn't hear the doorbell or turn when you come into the room, he may have faulty hearing. 

In children who have learned to talk, marked changes in the child's voice (e.g. a toneless voice, very loud or very soft voice) may be a sign of beginning deafness. Also a tendency to leave off the "s" sounds in words if previously developed may indicate it. 

358. What is a very simple hearing test that can be used with infants.?

A normal infant above the age of a few weeks will generally respond to moderately loud sounds such as: (1) the sound of a voice, (2) the snapping of your fingers, (3) the shaking of rattles, (4) the ringing of door bells, (5) clinking of his feeding bottles, etc. 

Where there is no response to such sounds, see your doctor. 

359. How can a mother test her child's hearing? 

When the baby isn't looking at you, whisper the baby's name at a distance of twenty feet or ring a small bell, shake a rattle or squeak a favorite toy. If the baby turns toward you.almost every time, he hears you. If he doesn't respond, repeat the test at ten feet or five feet until the baby knows what to do. Then attempt to get response at greater distances. 

360. If a child has. not learned to talk by the time he is. three years old is. he necessarily deaf?

No, there are many reasons why children do not learn to talk; defective hearing is one very probable cause. Certainly the child should be examined by an ear doctor who will test his hearing and advise you.
