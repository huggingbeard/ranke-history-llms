Bob Hoffman's SYSTEM OF SWING BAR TRAINING 

The York swing bar is completely new to the physical training, strength, health and development seeking public. Yet it has been used for many years by a very few of the world's most outstanding physical specimens. (It could be called their secret training method!) It has played an important part in developing their magnificent bodies, their great strength, particularly their classic torsos. 

The York swing bar has many advantages; with it all the exercises of a good barbell course may be performed. It makes possible the practice of the heavier, more result producing dumbell exercises and the completely new swing bar exercises. It has a place in physical training for it produces most excellent results. "Handsome is as handsome does," the old adage informs us, and this York swing bar will bring you handsome results.

With the complete York swing bar set you receive the very popular course known as Bob Hoffman's Simplified System of Barbell Training. 

This course, made at the beginning of the war so that it could be used by large groups of men, and used at home, too, by those who have little time, has brought wonderful results. The simplified system of barbell training used in conjunction with the dumbell course and the swing bar course will bring you a fine degree of strength, health and development. Starting Exercise 

You should spend considerable time in reading this course. Don't read it once and then put all away except the list of exercises. Study the advice given, study the description of the various exercises carefully, and make an effort to follow the advice as offered, the exercises as described. 

Do not follow other courses made by a friend or others who consider themselves experts. The York courses are tried and proven; there is a good reason for all they contain. Practice the movements exactly as they are offered in the course unless practice proves to you that one of the earlier exercises would suit you better if it were in the latter part of the course or vice versa. Be sure to practice the full course each training day. By reading the course many times and learning the correct method to perform every exercise you will speed your progress. However, make haste slowly, be satisfied with the progress you make; don't try to rush things by increasing your poundages too rapidly. Give nature an opportunity to build an increased store of strength and energy for you. 

But remember, too, that only those who constantly strive to progress, to handle more and more weight as time passes, make the best gains. To be one of these you must work hard and intelligently. To merely keep fit with better than able to be around health, with twice the strength of the average man is very simple and can be done with moderate training. 

In planning your training, depending upon your physical desires, your [10]

energy, time at your disposal and your ambitions, you can arrange to train every other day, or four or five times a week. A four day training schedule is an ideal method. The secret of gaining success with weight training is to make demands upon the muscles, then have a day or days of rest in which nature builds strength and endurance to not only meet these demands but to provide more strength for future needs. Gains in strength are made rapidly; after the short initial training period you will find the starting poundages to be easy for you and you will require additional weight. As your strength increases, you gradually add to the weight of the set, until, through your progressive gains, before long there is a noticeable improvement in your strength and development. While it is not wise to work too often, as we phrase it, "on your nerve," handling limit poundages in each movement, gritting your teeth and perhaps straining a bit to perform the last one or two movements, you must at times make demands upon your muscles so that they will gain as desired. It has been the accepted practice to train rather hard, certainly more strenuously than usual, one training day a week. When you become an advanced weight man, on this hard day you will try at times to exceed your best records of the past. On the remaining training days of the week go through a nice comfortable workout. 

If you are completely out of condition at the start, or not at all strong, have not exercised for a long period, it will be wise to start the movements with as little as five repetitions. Unused muscles will stiffen up slightly after the first session of exercise. You will feel them a bit for a day or two after this initial training day, but usually the stiffness will leave after that never to return again, as long as you practice your weight exercises at least occasionally. If your starting condition persuades you to start with five repetitions, you will practice this number of movements the first day, rest the second, practice five again the third day, rest the next day, then increase the movements to 6, rest a day, 6 again, rest a day and practice 7 movements. This system of increases is called the double progressive method. Gains are made so gradually that the muscles hardly feel it, and you are scarcely conscious of it, until one day you discover that you are much stronger, much better built, with broader shoulders, fuller, deeper chest, bigger arms and legs. If you start with five repetitions work up to ten in this manner. Very likely you will be able to increase the weight at this point and then continue with the double progressive system as described until you reach 15 movements. 

Reduce the repetitions to 10, add to the weight, work up to 15 again, then add to the weight, perform 10 repetitions, work up to 15 again, etc. 

Extend Yourself at Times 

But on your heavy day, the day you extend yourself considerably harder, you use a somewhat different system. Seldom will the swing bar [||]

be loaded to the point where it compares to a heavy barbell movement, but the advice I am offering here applies to training with your barbell, your single dumbell, too, as well as the swing bar. On this day on which you make demands upon your muscles, you seek to jar them out of their usual routine. So select a heavier weight, one which permits only 7 or 8 movements even when you exert yourself vigorously. After a short rest, perform 7 or 8 more movements with the same weight, and the same exercise. Many body builders use the 3 times 5 system, practicing each exercise with a heavy weight, in 3 series of 5 each. You need at least 15 movements with this form of training, so that a larger than usual supply of blood will come rushing to the aid of the working muscles to supply them with the materials they need to continue the exercise and to replace the broken down tissue. This is the way that gains are made. 

But on the other days of the week you should continue your progression from 10 to 15 repetitions. After you have reached what is termed the "Sticking point," when it is difficult to continue with the increases in repetitions and poundages, perform at least 12 repetitions. 

As in the simplified barbell and simplified dumbell systems of training, there has been an endeavor to arrange the exercises in groups so that the same poundages will serve for a number of exercises. The exercises which can be performed with less weight are in the first group, and the more vigorous exercises, which bring into action the larger, stronger muscles of the back and legs, are in the latter group. Men vary in strength and in leverage in the various muscle groups. One exercise may be easier for you than for others so you may prefer, after using the course for a time, to shift the exercises around a bit, the object being to classify the exercises in groups which permit you to handle the same poundage. Use a light weight in the first series of movements and a heavier weight in the second series. You can best determine just how you should make changes, if it is necessary to make any changes in the sequence of the exercises, by studying the repetitions and poundages you have employed in the past as registered in your record book. 

In all the exercises the weight you select should enable you to practice the desired number of repetitions without jerking or wrenching. You should perform the movements with moderate slowness so that you can feel the weight every inch of the way. In fact, as you become strong enough to handle all the weight of your set you will be wise to perform the exercises even more slowly in the future. Those who follow the swing bar courses seldom have a set of weights which weigh over a hundred pounds. Although this weight will bring good results, it is only a moderate poundage as compared to the 300 pounds or more which advanced barbell men use in some of the movements, particularly the [12]

exercises which involve the action of the legs and back. While feeling the weight every inch of the way, up and down, you should also inhale and exhale forcefully. Inhale as the weights go up or as you exert force, exhale as the weights are lowered, breathing as deeply as possible throughout.

After you have trained for a time you will find yourself capable of handling much more weight in some movements than in others. For instance, the world's record in the back lift is 4300 pounds, in the harness lift 3600, yet the world's strongest men frequently use not more than a pair of 20-pound dumbells to practice some movements, particu larly breathing exercises while lying on the back on a bench. These courses have been so arranged that a wide variety of training procedure can be practiced. When time is ample you can follow each individual exercise the desired number of times, resting between each exercise. But if time is limited you can practice the movements as compound exercises. 

Compound exercises should consist of three or more movements without setting the weight down. In the single dumbell movements this is not possible as first one arm is exercised and then the other, but with the barbell and the swing bell, a number of movements can be practiced in succession. In the simplified system of barbell training and the simplified system of dumbell training, the exercises have been arranged in groups designed to be practiced together. By using compound exercises with the simplified barbell system it is possible to complete one set of ten exercises in five minutes. The usual advanced barbell man may only use 50 pounds in the first three exercises, 75 in the next four, and 100 in the last three. Although these are not great weights, the continuous action will make the best strength athlete feel the exercise severely and benefit from its practice. But seldom is your time so short that you must hurry through your exercises in five minutes. There are 1440 minutes in each day, but it does prove just how little time can be beneficially utilized to practice barbell movements and it does prove that no persons can honestly say that they don't have time to exercise. Surely you can arrange your schedule so that you can allot from twenty minutes to an hour of training time, three or four times a week, for these beneficial, health increasing, body bettering exercises. It is more fun and more comfortable to take your time, rest between movements and walk around the room breathing deeply, maintaining good posture between exercises. 

As you carefully read the description of the exercises, as you prac tice them for a time, you will notice that most of the muscles of the body have had their turn at action, certainly all the principal groups; but one of the rules of physical training is that best results are had when the muscles are developed from every possible angle. So you need variety in your training and must practice numerous exercises. On the days [ 13 ]

when you are more ambitious, you may desire to complete two or even three of the courses, using the barbell, the single dumbell and the swing bar. Your endurance and recuperative powers will increase rapidly if you follow the rules of health, avoid entirely or cut to a bare minimum habits which are certainly not beneficial. Although the extent of the harm they produce is debatable, such as the drinking of alcoholic bev erages, or smoking, be moderate in all things, obtain sufficient sleep, rest and relaxation, maintain a tranquil mind, consume good food at meal times only (if you eat plenty of good fresh meat, milk, eggs, fruit and vegetables, you will not have need for food concentrates or vitamins) and, of course, there is one other, the most important of the essentials of health, the right kind of exercise. By following these simple rules your progression is assured.

It has been found that the best time to exercise is at least an hour or two before meals, or an hour or two after meals. This could mean right after coming home from work, if the usual habit of eating will not take place for over an hour, or it could mean training any time during the evening, well after the evening meal, when digestion is far along and the stomach is well settled. Some men like to get their exercises over so that they can devote the evening to other activities, while others prefer to practice the movements an hour or two before retiring. Completing the exercises too soon before bedtime is not too good, for the exercises are stimulating, they build energy and create wakefulness to a degree. But if you train an hour or two before your bedtime it will permit a vigorous workout with your weights (if you don't pant a bit and perspire profusely, you are not working hard enough and cannot expect to obtain the full measure of benefit from your exercises), then take a lukewarm bath, hardly more than body temperature, then rest, read or study for an hour or so and you'll enjoy a perfect night's sleep.

You will obtain from exercise what you put into it. We wish you the fullest measure of success. May you always be strong, healthy and happy.

If the movement is entirely new to you, or if you have not exercised for a time, the weight of the swing bar alone will be sufficient. Extend the swing bar overhead, keeping the legs which have been placed in the usual position with the feet a comfortable distance apart, straight, swing or rotate the body round in a circle. 2. Forward raise without body movement. This movement is started with the swing bar against the thighs, and with arms straight, with shoulder action alone, no body movement. The weight is lifted or swung to a position overhead and well back. Lower the bell to the starting position and repeat. The exercise should be done with reasonable slowness so that the weight can be felt every inch of the way. 

3. Gun type of swinging exercise. This movement is a good one, similar to one of the chief exercises in gun swinging practiced by the men of our armed forces in this war and the one which preceded it. A gun weighs a little over nine pounds, and since it cannot be increased in weight progressively, it becomes too light after a time. But you can increase the weight of the swing bar until it offers very substantial resistance. When fully loaded, a very good man is required to swing it. 

Stand with the feet in the usual position for these exercises, feet a comfortable distance apart, usually 18 to 24 inches, depending upon the height of the man, with the swing bar hanging in front of the body, arms straight, back bent forward slightly. Swing from this position to the side and overhead, turning the body, rotating on the balls of the feet as the weight extends overhead and to the side, then back to the starting position, and in a similar twisting manner swing the bar overhead to the left. When this movement is performed with a regular cadence, a big half circle will be followed from a position overhead on the right, to overhead on the left. With practice, you will be able to put your entire body into action with some movement of the legs and considerable raising and lowering of the body as the swing bar is swung through a big half circle. 

4. Triceps exercise. This exercise enlarges and strengthens the muscles of the back of the arm in a rapid and inspiring manner. Hold the bell in front of the body, palms front in the position of the regular two arm curl, curl the weight up and over, in back of the head, where the swing bar rests upon the shoulders. From this position, lift or pull and push the swing bar to arm's length overhead. Lower to the position back of head and repeat for ten or fifteen movements.

7. Swing from side in an arc overhead. Hold the swing bell with the knuckles front, turn to the side and bend so that the bar touches the floor or ground to the right of the body with the bell perpendicular to the front. Hold arms as straight as possible. Lift and swing the bell up and over in a huge circle until it touches the floor in a position similar to the starting position, but to the left of the body, back to the starting position and continue this movement for 10 to 15 repetitions. 

8. Body twisting exercise. This is an easy exercise, provides a partial rest between the more vigorous swinging and twisting movements. Raise the swing bar until it rests on the shoulders back of the head. Stand with the feet a comfortable distance apart, twist the body as far as you can to the right, round, round, farther around, then come back quickly with a snap toward the center. Then twist far to the left, and snap back to center. Continue the movement for double the usual number of counts, that is, 20 to 30. 

9. Continuous pull up and press. One of the very best weight exercises, many consider it the very best. Feet in usual position, start with the swing bar at floor level, keeping back flat, pull up to a position at chest, and then press to arm's length overhead. Lower to chest, back to position near the floor and repeat the movement, 10 to 15 repetitions. 

Variation can be provided by pulling the weight up and over the head, resting it momentarily back of head on shoulders, pressing to arm's length, lowering to chest, then to a position near the floor and continuing the movement. 

10. Rise on toes. This movement can be practiced with the weight resting upon the shoulders or hanging in front of the body, resting against the thighs. There are three variations of raising on toes: one is [18]

practiced with the feet in the military position, heels together, toes out equally forming an angle of 45 degrees; another with the toes extended straight to the front, and still another in an exaggerated pigeon-toed manner with the toes together and the heels well apart. You should find ten movements in each style, thirty in all, about right. As an advanced movement you can raise on one toe at a time. Do not rock forward, raise the body with the strength of the feet and toes alone. Good results are had by standing on a board one or two inches in thickness, starting with heels on the floor, providing a much greater range of movement. 

11. Regular curl. Grasp swing bar with both hands, palms front, arms straight but against the thighs. Slowly, while holding elbows at sides, without body movement, lift or curl the weight until it touches the chin. Lower slowly and repeat 10 to 15 movements. This exercise develops the biceps, the large muscle of the front of the upper arm. 

12. Bend over or "Good Morning" exercise. This exercise is similar to the bowing of men to each other in the old days when they were going to church. Stand with the feet in the usual position, raise the weight up and over back of the head until it rests upon the shoulders, keeping the knees straight, bend forward as far as you can, back to the upright position, leaning far back, then forward and continue the movement 10 to 15 repetitions. This movement is somewhat similar to the stifflegged dead weight lift, but it differs in the fact that the neck and shoulders receive some good exercise as they support the swing bar. 

13. Deep knee bend on toes, bar held overhead. Stand with the heels together, weight held at arm's length overhead, toes out at an angle of 45 degrees, keeping the back erect, spreading the knees far apart lower the body until the buttocks touch the heels. Then return to the starting position. As an advanced exercise you can come up with a jump, leaping well into the air as the upright position is reached. This exercise builds balance, control of the body, and particularly develops the muscles on the outside of the thighs. 

14. Shoulder shrug. The trapezius muscles, extending from the neck to the shoulders, the muscles which impart the pleasing, curving slope to the strong man's shoulders, are developed by this exercise. 

Stand with the feet in a comfortable position, heels together or apart as preferred, knuckles front, keeping the body erect throughout, the arms straight. Bending the arms robs the trapezius of some of the benefits it should receive. From the position with the swing bell resting against the thighs, lift or shrug the shoulders as high as you can, endeavoring to touch the ears with them. This is an easy movement as men become strong enough to shrug one shoulder with 400 pounds supported from [19]

it, so you can continue the movement for twice the usual number of repetitions. 

15. Compound exercise. To practice a compound exercise, you select three good movements from the exercises in this course and without stopping at the end of the first or second exercise continue until three are practiced. This method of training has many advantages: it keeps the muscles in action for a longer period without too great fatigue, keeps the blood coursing rapidly through the body, speeds up the respiration and the perspiration and builds a degree of all around physical excellence. You can vary the three exercises you practice with this movement as you like. 

16. The straddle hop, the popular army and navy exercise, fits in here. While it is easier to practice with the barbell, the swing bar can be used, but should be held at the chest. In both courses, the main object of Exercise No. 16 is to endeavor to make yourself a bit breathless so that you will benefit by the breathing exercise which follows. Chests expand more rapidly when there is a real demand for more air rather than through just forcing air into the lungs. Hold the bar at chest, stand with the heels together, jump so that the feet are placed a fair distance apart, from two to three feet, depending upon the height of the individual, jump back to center, continuing the movement with a regular cadence until tired and breathing hard. You probably will practice at least 50 hops, continue for a higher count if you like. Charlie Debus, bent press champion, has performed 60 hops with 400 pounds upon the bar. This movement develops the feet and calves, puts spring and endurance in your walk. 

17. Two hands pull over. The best exercise to develop the pectorals, the muscles of the chest, the latissimus dorsi muscles which extend from the side up to a position back of shoulder, the deltoids or shoulder muscles, and deepen and enlarge the rib box. Lie on your back on the floor or, better still, on a bench if you have one available. 

Start with the arms extended overhead. Keeping them straight, lower them in a quarter circle to a position below the back of your head. 

Breathe as deeply and forcefully as you can, as the weight lowers force more and more air into the chest, hold the breath for the count of two in this lowest position, then raise the bar to the starting position and continue on down to the thighs, exhaling the air from the lungs as you go. If you are too breathless when you start this exercise, you will have to be satisfied with the quarter circle movement from over head to back of head, but when your "wind" improves, describe the full half circle from back of head to thighs and return. Only a moderate weight should be used in this exercise as it is a breathing, chest expanding exercise rather than a muscle developer. [ 20 ]

18. 

Abdominal raise. All body builders seek a well muscled abdomen which is strong, yet slender, and possesses a washboard like appearance. Specializing in the abdominal raise is the way to obtain such a development. If you have an inclined abdominal board such as we have in the York Barbell Gym, most fellows have them in their home training quarters, you will practice this sit up or abdominal raise in the inclined position. The feet are thrust under the strap at the head of the board and the sit up is practiced in a variety of ways. The hardest is with the bar back of head. It's easier on the chest, and still easier the farther down the bar is lowered as this lessens the resistance. When you become an advanced weight man the bar, of course, should be in the position where it provides the greatest resistance. Practice this movement until the abdominal muscles are thoroughly tired. Better results are obtained if the knees are kept straight. Bend forward trying to touch the knees with your nose. When you bend back and the body is flat on the floor or board, expand your chest and pull in your waist. If you do not have an abdominal board you can thrust your feet under any weighty object or have a training mate hold them down for you. 

19. Leg biceps curl. Although the legs have had a pretty fair workout through the practice of this course, this one additional exercise will round them out, build your strength, and create a powerful appearance. 

This exercise is best practiced by fastening a pair of York iron boots to the bar of your swing bar. You lie on your face, with the shoes strapped to your feet either upon the floor or better still upon the abdominal board or a bench, and curl the legs until the weight touches the body. 

If you don't have iron boots, you could tie the weights to the feet although this is a bit more difficult. 

20. Neck exercise. The neck is the most conspicuous part of the body when clothed. A round, well muscled, column-like neck is the mark of a strong, virile man. Fat, thin, scrawny necks show that the individual is lacking in energy, strength and vitality. By all means you should practice special movements for the neck, although your neck development will be greatly improved by practicing the general course. 

If you have a head strap, and most every body builder has, just thrust the rope through one or more of the plates you have with your set. The first exercise is taken with the hands resting upon the knees, raising and lowering the weight with the neck muscles. Another good movement is practiced lying on your back upon the bench with the weight raised and lowered to develop the front of the neck. Many good movements are possible with the head strap. If you don't have a head strap, and until you procure one, you can practice the wrestler's bridge movement which is a part of the second swing bar course. [21]

1. Twisting while holding front. Extend the swing bar to the front, at shoulder height, knuckles up. Rotate it as far one way as you can, return to center with a snap, then turn slowly, far to the opposite side, back to center and continue the movement until tired. 

2. Push out in front. Start with the swing bar at the chest, knuckles up. Thrust the bell well forward until arms are straight, weight at shoulder height. Then back to center and continue for 10 to 15 movements. [22]

3. Stiff arm two hand swing. If there were only one single exercise which could be practiced with the swing bar, and this were it, the swing bar would play an important part in physical training with weights. 

But there are scores of good movements, nevertheless this one exercise will provide great benefit in building strength, internal vitality, endurance, and muscular development. It splendidly develops all the muscles of the back, from the heels, the thighs, the small of the back, the vital region of your body, up through the muscles which flank the spine, to the base of the head. Arms and shoulders receive their full measure of development too. Keep the feet in the usual position, a comfortable distance apart, the knees kept straight throughout the movement, lean forward, keeping arms straight throughout the movement, until the bar approximately touches the floor or ground. Swing the weight to arm's length overhead and in back of head. Perform the exercise slowly enough that the weight can be felt every inch of the way up and down, continue for 15 repetitions. At times, when you want to try yourself out, this movement can be performed more rapidly and with a high number of repetitions. Many fellows when training together try to excel each other with this movement. A few years ago when 265-pound Big Dave

Mayor was heavyweight lifting champion, he and I had a contest swinging two fifty-pound dumbells; we did not have swing bars in those days. 

I won the contest with 34 swings, and what a workout it provided. 

Stiff armed swinging has always been a favorite with John Davis, the young man who won the world's weight lifting title at Vienna when he was just 17 years of age. He became the world's best weight lifter and holder of records in the heavyweight class. This exercise has accounted for much of his magnificent development and great strength. He became strong enough to practice ten repetitions with 150 pounds. But don't be discouraged if 50 pounds seems an effort for you. John Davis, John

Grimek, Steve Stanko and others of the York champs had to make their start too, years ago. 

4. Rolling, held in front. This is an easy movement, designed to develop the fingers, wrists and forearms. With the York Big 12 Special there is a specially designed roller to practice this movement, but the swing bell will serve. Hold the bell at arm's length, and shoulder height in front of the body, knuckles up, with an action of the fingers alternately grasping and releasing the bar, turn it until your fingers and arms are tired. Turn it one way, then the other. Persistence in the practice of this movement will reward you with fine finger, hand and forearm strength, which is an asset in any walk of life. 

5. Two hands snatch swing. This identical movement was particu larly popular with the Egyptian weight lifters who, at one time, held more than half of all the world's records. They excelled in two hands [ 23 ]

snatching and cleaning, and this is the exercise they used to teach their body the proper positions. Even if you do not have the slightest desire to be a star weight lifter, it is still a good body developing movement which teaches balance and coordination to a great degree. Start the movement with the bar resting on the floor or near the floor in front of body, feet a comfortable distance apart. The bar is raised as in the stiff armed two hand complete swing, except as the weight reaches head height, you step well forward with one foot, lower or rock yourself into very low position with the front leg bent as it would be in a single legged squat, the rear leg stretched well back and held as straight as possible. 

Come back to the erect position by pulling up the back leg, lower the bar to a position near the floor and continue. With the practice of this movement you will be walking around your training quarters, but even a small room will not prevent the practice of this excellent movement. 

6. Back hand curl. This is the opposite of the curl which is No. 

11 in the first course. Then the palms were held front; in this movement the knuckles are front. This manner of curling is a bit harder and of course brings the reward of well developed forearms. The bell is curled from a position at thighs to chin height, keeping the elbows against the body. At one time I held the official world's record in this style of curling with 145 pounds. Since then some of our great weight lifting champions, Dave Mayor, John Davis, Steve Stanko, Al Berger, put it up to its present 165. As with the usual exercise of this course, continue from 10 to 15 repetitions. 

7. Swing from center to side overhead. This movement starts from the same position as No. 3, the stiff arm, full body swing of this course. 

It differs in the fact that instead of merely extending the swing bar overhead, it is lifted or swung up to a position at the right, body twisted so that the bar is perpendicular to the front, then back to the low position in center, and swung with stiff arms and turning body far up to the left. This is an excellent movement and develops many muscles, particularly those of the midsection and the vital regions of the back. 

8. Side to side bend. As the name implies, this exercise develops the muscles of the sides. Stand with heels together, place weight on back of shoulders, then bend as far as possible to the right, back to center, then far to the left. Continue this movement for double the usual number of counts, 20 to 30. 

9. Teetotum or twisting dead weight lift. This is an original exercise which appeared first in the Simplified System of Barbell Training. 

It's a good one too, which will put a lot of strength and endurance in your back. After practicing this exercise for a while, one holiday, I dug a big fish pool in our rock garden all by myself. 135 wheelbarrows full [24]

of dirt. I wheeled out, dug it loose with a pick, shoveled it into the wheelbarrow, climbed out of the hole and wheeled the barrow up an incline and dumped it. Did this 135 times during the day. That day's work would have been worth at least two dollars anywhere. But a movement such as this will give you the power and endurance to do the things you must do at your work, or the things you like to do during your periods of recreation. Stand with the feet a comfortable distance apart, hold the swing bar with knuckles front, resting in front of the thighs. 

With the legs as straight as possible, bend and twist to the side so that the bar touches the floor or ground at the side of the right foot, in a position perpendicular to the front, back to center and then down in a similar manner to the left, back to center and repeat 10 to 20 move. ments. 

10. Hopping on toes or stationary running. This exercise is designed to strengthen the feet, develop the calves and build general all around strength and agility in the body. It can be performed in a variety of ways, stationary running, or better still, running around the gym, the yard or up and down stairs if you have the facilities for practicing in this manner. Hopping from foot to foot, as in rope skipping, or jumping up and down with both feet will bring the desired results. 

Employ the style that you prefer, continuing the movement until your muscles feel it. The Goosestep can also be practiced in this exercise 

No. 10 to provide variation. Walk around the gym yard or your training room, raising the legs high as you walk, toes pointed forward, knees straight. This movement brings best results when practiced progressively with the iron shoe, but it will have an excellent effect practiced in this manner too. 

11. The regular rowing motion. Stand with the feet a comfortable distance apart, legs straight, lean forward so that the upper body is at right angles to the legs, the bar hanging down to a position near the floor. Without moving the back, entirely with arm and shoulder action, pull the swing bar up to chest, lower and continue for 10 to 15 movements. This exercise is one of the best for developing the upper back, it's fair for developing the latissimus, and when practiced with a heavy weight is one of the best for developing the arms. It builds the Brachialis 

Anticus, the deep lying muscle of the upper arm, which adds greatly to its bulk, strength and appearance. This is one of the good old standard exercises and should be included in most every training course in some form. 

12. High pull up. This movement when practiced with a heavy weight is a good one. You will notice that it is a part of the Simplified 

System of Barbell Training, that it is placed in the third series where [25]

heavier weights are used. The Royal Canadian Air Force men have limited time so they practice just three exercises, the deep knee bend, the high pull up and the continuous pull up and press. In this high pull up you stand with the feet a comfortable distance apart, the bar resting between the feet and parallel with them. Lifting with moderate slowness, pull the swing bar up until it reaches a point at head height. With a heavy weight, it is customary to touch the bar to chin, but with a light or moderate weight you can go high. This differs from the stiff arm swing in the fact that the arms are bent throughout and the bar is lifted much closer to the body, providing a different muscular action with additional developmental effects. 

13. Deep knee bend, feet wide, pressing while squatting. Hold the swing bar on chest, keeping feet flat to floor, lower as far as possible, pressing the weight overhead while squatting. Continue this movement for the usual number of counts. This exercise has been included in this swing bar course in an endeavor to make every course complete in itself, so that all of the muscles will receive benefit, and the body a well rounded workout even if only the swing bar exercises are used on a particular day. The thigh muscles are very strong; a hundred pounds is light enough in this movement. The leading strength athletes of the country use 300 or more. Henry Steinborn, the strong man of wrestling, a score of years ago made a world's record with 552 pounds in this style. 

So to obtain the full measure of benefit it will be necessary that you advance progressively, continually endeavoring to handle more and more weight, until you are using real poundages in this movement. The swing bar will be used in a variety of ways and by all sorts of people who have a variety of physical desires. Some men will use it on one of their two easy or tinkering days' training; to others it will be the chief training medium. Regardless of your method of training on other days, your desires and physical ambitions, you will make no mistake in practicing this exercise in its usual place in this course, even if you are accustomed to handling more weight. A very wide position of the feet will make the exercise sufficiently different that benefits will accrue. 

14. Rotating shrug. This is a form of trapezius developing exercise which differs considerably from the shoulder shrug of the first course. 

This exercise is practiced with a circular, rolling motion. To get the idea of it, start in a position similar to the shoulder shrug of course No. 1. 

Lean the shoulders forward, raise them, extend them well back, lower and turn them well front, raise and continue this rolling, circular motion. For variation you can move them forward, raise them, roll back, lower, raise and roll forward. Continue this movement until tired, as these muscles when developed are strong enough that they can handle a considerable number of repetitions. [ 26 ]

15. Compound exercise. As instructed in exercise 15 of the first course, the object of this exercise is to select three good exercises from this course and perform them in succession without pause or setting the weight down. 

16. In this exercise it is your object to exercise so that you get the body in a breathless state or at least a heavily breathing state, so that you will receive the maximum of benefit from the exercise which follows this one. The straddle hop, stationary running, running up steps which requires the greatest exertion, will produce the heavy breathing condition as well as building muscle and endurance too. Select the exercise you prefer in this No. 16. 

17. Press up and lower behind neck, breathing. Lie on floor, or better still on a bench if you have one. Your back is down, the swing bar rests on your chest to begin. Press the weight to arm's length overhead, then keeping arms straight and breathing deeply and forcefully lower to below the head. Hold the breath for two seconds, raise exhaling, lower to chest and continue for double the usual number of counts; 20 to 30 is not too many. With a heavy weight as can be used with a barbell, this supine pressing is one of the best exercises. It is one of the few exercises in which heavy weights can be used while breathing deeply. Usually the employment of too much weight, as in the two hands pull over, spoils the movement as a breathing exercise, making of it a muscle building movement, but in this style of supine pressing the best strength athletes use 200 pounds and often considerably more. It develops the arms, the shoulders, particularly the muscles of the chest, and expands the rɩb box. 

This has always been a favorite of mine and 1 have thought that it deserves considerable of the credit of building my chest from the 36 inches it measured, when I first started barbell training a score of years ago, to the 52 inches it measured at the time the photo was taken which appears on the cover of this course. I was 41 when that picture was taken, three years ago, as this is written. 

18. Leg raise to develop the abdomen. The leg raise in conjunction with the sit up or abdominal raise will build an outstanding development of the midsection. The leg raise brings the lowest section of the abdomen into play and serves as an admirable reducer or normalizer of the lower abdomen where fat forms so easily when maturity is reached. You can practice this movement with the legs alone to begin, then with a pair of iron boots with the swing bar fastened to them. Continue this movement until tired, certainly not less than 10 to 15 repetitions.

20. The wrestler's bridge. In the preceding 19 exercises of this course all of the muscles have had plenty of work to do. While nearly all dumbell exercises can be practiced with a single dumbell instead of two, twice as much time will be required to complete the exercises, so if there is a desire to perform more dumbell exercises (and they provide an ideal form of training), a York adjustable 80 pound dumbell set or the York 7 in 1 set should be procured. But in this course you will find a number of the superior body building movements which are best performed, or can be performed only with a single dumbell. By practicing with one hand and then with the other, there are sufficient exercises for a good workout if this course alone were used some days. And the exercises are so excellent in character that good results could be obtained if they were repeated more than once. 

1. Toe touching while holding the weight overhead. Extend your arm to full length overhead while holding a single dumbell. Stand with the feet two feet apart. Holding the bell overhead, reach down and touch the toe on the side of the foot opposite to the side holding the bell. This can be made a little more difficult when desired by touching the toe on the same side as the arm supporting the weight. 

This exercise strengthens particularly all of the muscles of the midsection. It is a rapid builder of balance, teaches control of the muscles, improves the action of internal organs and glands. Repeat the desired number of repetitions coming back to the erect position each time after touching the toe. Repeat with the other hand. Keep eye on bar throughout. If this exercise is entirely new to you, practice without the weight for a time until you become accustomed to it. 

2. Side press. This is one of the very best for developing the entire shoulders and upper back. Stand with the feet about 18 inches apart, the toe on the side holding the bar extended straight to the front, the [29]

toe on the other foot advanced somewhat, toe pointing away from the body at a slight angle. Press the dumbell to arm's length overhead. Keeping the palm front, lower the bell until the hand is about shoulder height, well out from the body and slightly behind. As the bell comes down, harden the back muscles with a conscious effort. As you press the bell overhead lean slightly away from the bell and it will almost go up by itself. In no time at all you can use 35 pounds or more in this exercise. 

3. Bend over rowing and curling. Obtain a low box or stool. Take the dumbell in your hand, lean forward so that the weight is resting with the palm of the non-lifting hand upon box or stool, stand with the legs straight; the back should be at right angles to the legs. In this position you can practice the regular curl with the palm up, the back hand curl with the knuckles up, and two distinct forms of rowing movements, one pulling the weight up with the elbow close to the body and the weight pulled until it touches the shoulder, the other with the elbow well extended from the body pulling the weight up until it touches the chest. These movements are even more beneficial than similar movements practiced with the body in the erect position as there is no support for the arm and real muscle action is involved rather than leverage or fulcrum-like movements when the elbow can be rested on the hip while standing erect. 

4. Side bend to develop the sides. Hold the dumbell in your hand so that it rests against the side of the leg. Extend the non-lifting arm slightly away from the body, now lean far to the side away from the weight, come back to center, then bend far to the side again. Continue until you can feel fatigue in the side muscles, which might be as many as twenty movements. Change the weight to the other hand and practice a similar movement bending away from the weight. There is another good result producing way to practice this movement. Hold the bell in the position used in the side press when the weight has reached its lowest point, now lean far to the opposite side. After continuing this movement until you feel it, no less than twelve repetitions however, change weight to the other hand and exercise that side too. 

5. One arm swing. As an exercise this movement is usually practiced changing from hand to hand, although some strength athletes prefer to continue the movements with first one hand and then the other. This is the method that Alda Ketterman employs. She has swung the 55 pounds of the wartime dumbell thirty repetitions with her left hand. If you intend to use this method swing the bell from a position an inch or so from the floor and between your legs, which are placed a comfortable distance apart, to full arm's length overhead. The non-lifting [ 30 ]

hand should be placed upon the knee, the back kept rather flat, the arm straight throughout the lifting. If you are changing from hand to hand, the change is made as the bell reaches its highest point before starting to come down. This movement can be practiced for many repetitions, 10 or 12 with each arm not too many, 20 or 24 in all. As the weight is changed from hand to hand while overhead, the non-lifting hand is placed on the opposite knee. 

6. Deep knee bend with dumbell overhead. This is an excellent movement, brings all the muscles of the body into play, teaches balance, control of the body, and leads to proficiency in one hand lifting when you have the desire to demonstrate your strength in that manner. Stand with the feet a comfortable distance apart, keep the feet flat, heels to floor, bend or collapse down into the full squat position with the side of the body resting on the thigh. This is the low position of the one hand snatch, the one hand jerk and the bent press. Practice of this movement will build skill and control of the body which will make it possible for you to create a creditable performance in these popular lifting styles. 

Detailed description of how these and all other recognized lifts are performed can be obtained from the popular book, "Weight Lifting." When you have performed the desired number of repetitions with this movement, 10 to 15, use the other arm for the same number of movements. 

7. The one hand clean and press or jerk. In this movement the single dumbell is drawn from a position between the feet, which are a comfortable distance apart, to the shoulder, and then pressed or jerked overhead. Lower to the shoulder, then to the floor and continue with the movement the desired number of times. The bell extends perpendicular to the front throughout. When you reach the point of handling a heavy weight, you can jump at the start of the jerk and lower your body by squatting into the low position as in exercise 6 or lowering the body by a wide fore and aft split. 

8. The one hand swing and split. Most body builders, in practicing exercise No. 5, change from one hand to the other as the exercise progresses. The feet remain in one position. This type of one hand swinging, No. 8 in your course, is performed similar to the standard lift of the same name. Some lifters have attained sufficient skill in this style of lifting to swing more than their own bodyweight in passable style. Start the movement standing with the feet a comfortable distance apart, lay the dumbell on the floor between the legs and well back. Reach down and grasp the bell, placing the non-lifting hand upon the knee, keep the back as flat as possible. With a fast, powerful motion pull the weight to the front and up. I believe by this time that you will agree that this is a good course and from the feel of your muscles that you can expect good results. 

Practically any and every barbell exercise can be practiced with the swing bar. However, if you have a barbell, you may as well use it in the practice of barbell movements and lifting motion exercises such as are contained in the famous Four York Courses, and the almost equally renowned Simplified System of Barbell Training. In some of the movements, particularly with a heavy weight, the longer bar adds to the convenience of performing the movements. But if you should only have the swing bar, or only have a dumbell set, and have changed one of your dumbell bars into a swing bar, you can see how easily you can follow all barbell movements with the swing bar. The exercises of a good barbell course contain the following standard movements, all of which can be performed with the swing bar, and most of which are contained in the swing bar course. 

1. HIGH PULL UP OR WARM UP MOVEMENT. 

2. TWO HANDS CURL. 

3. STRADDLE LIFT. 

4. CONTINUOUS PULL UP AND PRESS. 

5. DEAD WEIGHT LIFT. 

6. ROWING MOTION. 

7. SHOULDER SHRUG. 

8. RISE ON TOES. 

9. DEEP KNEE BEND. 

10. HIGH PULL UP. 

The swing bar is a very versatile piece of training equipment enabling you to practice the best dumbell exercises, practically any barbell exercise and a great many swing bar exercises which cannot be done with any other type of equipment or are not contained in any other course. While tremendously heavy weights, as are used by the lifting champions, cannot be employed with the swing bar, the movements are so different, and so excellent in their developmental effect, that the strongest man will obtain a good workout through the practice of the exercises contained in this Swing Bar course.
