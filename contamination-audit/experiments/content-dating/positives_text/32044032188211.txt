Chapter INTRODUCTORY; MISCELLANEOUS 

The manner of prosecuting criminals in the state of New 

York is regulated by the "CODE OF CRIMINAL PROCEDURE OF THE STATE OF NEW YORK." The Code was enacted in 

1881 (Chapter 442) and became effective September 1, 1881. 

Sec 41 P.L.; Secs. 1, 963 Code.

Stated generally, all steps relating to practice and procedure must be traceable to some specific provision of the Code. There is no power to indulge in procedure not sanctioned in the Code except where there is a conflict with higher authority. For example, a statutory limitation may not deprive a person of a constitutional right. Peo. v. Glen, 173 NY 395. Further exceptions have been justified on the theories that (1) courts have inherent power to conform their records to conceded or apparent facts where mistakes have been made (Peo. ex rel. 

Hirschberg v. Orange Co. Ct., 271 NY 151), and (2) courts have the right to alter judgments obtained by fraud. Matter of 

Lyons v. Goldstein, 290 NY 19.

Criminal Action Defined. A criminal action, or a special proceeding of a criminal nature, is a proceeding by which a party charged with crime is accused and tried. Sec. 5 Code; 

Peo. ex rel. Commrs. v. Cullen, 151 NY 54.

Crime Defined Kinds. There are no common-law crimes in the state of New York. Peo. v. Ingber, 248 NY 302.

A crime is an act or omission forbidden by statute and punishable. A crime is either a felony or a misdemeanor. A felony is a crime which is or may be punishable by death or imprisonment in a state prison. Any other crime is a misdemeanor. An act done with intent to commit a crime, and tending but failing to effect its commission, is an attempt to commit a

crime. Sec. 2 P.L. An indictment is necessary in a felony case.

Art. 1, Sec. 6 NY Const. 

Petty offenses, involving no moral guilt, are not crimes. Peo. ex rel. Burke v. Fox, 205 NY 490. Unless a minor offense is specifically designated as a misdemeanor, it is not classified as a crime.

Unsubstantial Errors. Mistakes. Errors relating to form rather than substance do not render proceedings invalid unless it be established that a party was unduly prejudiced thereby. 

Sec. 684 Code. 

Defectively entitled depositions and affidavits are not deemed invalid if they refer intelligibly to the pending proceeding. Sec. 

683 Code.

Parties. In a criminal proceeding, the people of the state of New York are the plaintiffs. In a special proceeding of a criminal nature, the prosecutor is the complainant. The party prosecuted is the defendant. Secs. 6, 7, 950 Code; Sec. 18a Gen eral Construction Law. 

A party to a felony is either a principal or an accessory. Sec. 

26 P.L. Accessories are deemed principals in misdemeanor cases. Sec. 27 P.L.

A principal is one who either commits a crime or aids and abets in its commission, whether present or absent. Peo. v. Cohen, 223 NY 406. One who induces another to commit a crime is also a principal. Sec. 2 P.L.

An accessory is one who, after the commission of a felony, conceals or aids the offender to escape arrest or prosecution. 

Sec. 2 P.L. 

Corpus Delicti. 

Corpus delicti, the body or substance of the offense, means the existence of a criminal fact. In a narrow sense, the term is sometimes used to designate the body of the deceased victim. Peo. v. Palmer, 109 NY 110.

Corporations. Corporations are brought into court through the issuance and service of a summons. A corporation is re-

3 quired, after indictment, to file a verified plea or demurrer thereto. Voluntary appearances, without the issuance and service of summonses, are permissible. Fines are collected in the same manner as upon judgments in civil actions. Secs. 675682 Code.

Counsel may interpose a plea of guilty on behalf of a corpor ation. Sec. 335 Code. 

Children. A child under seven is legally incapable of committing a crime. Sec. 816 P.L. A child between seven and eleven inclusive is presumed to be incapable of committing a crime, but the presumption may be rebutted. Sec. 817 P.L.

A child of more than seven and less than sixteen years of age may not be deemed guilty of any crime, but of juvenile delinquency only, except where the offense is punishable by death or life imprisonment. Sec. 2186 P.L.; Peo. v. Roper, 259 NY 

170; Peo. v. Murch, 263 NY 285. However, any other person concerned therein, whether as principal or accessory, may not urge this disability for his own protection. Sec. 2186 P.L. 

Compromising Crimes. A misdemeanor may be compromised with leave of court except when the crime was committed by or against an officer of justice during the performance of his duty, or riotously, or with intent to commit a felony. Sec. 

663 Code.

The compromise is effected upon the acknowledgment in writing of the complainant that he has received satisfaction for the injury, in which event the court may order all criminal proceedings stayed and discharge the defendant. The reason for the order must be recited therein and entered upon the minutes. 

Sec. 664 Code. Such an order is a bar to another prosecution for the same offense. Sec. 665 Code. ·Stolen Property. Unless temporary detention of stolen property is necessary for prosecution, the court, upon satisfactory proof of title, may direct its delivery to the owner. The reasonable and necessary expenses incurred in the preservation of the stolen property must be paid before it may be retrieved. Secs. 

686-688 Code.

Stolen or embezzled property must be claimed within six months of the conviction; otherwise, it is applied for the benefit of the poor of the city or county. Sec. 689 Code.

Extraordinary Investigations. 

Extraordinary investigations are conducted by the attorney general under the direction of the governor by executive order. Sec. 62 Executive Law.

The authority of the attorney general therein is circumscribed by the scope of the executive order and the statutory authority upon which it is based. Matter of Turecamo Contracting Co., 260 AD 253; Matter of Cranford Material Corp., 174 

M 154.

A justice of the Supreme Court is designated by the governor to preside over litigation arising in the extraordinary investiga tion. Sec. 153 Judiciary Law. 

Civil Rights, Etc. A person sentenced to life imprisonment is deemed civilly dead. Sec. 511 P.L. A sentence in a state prison for any term less than life, forfeits all public office held by the prisoner and suspends, during the imprisonment, all his civil rights. Sec. 510 P.L.

No person convicted of a felony has the right to register or vote at any election. Where, however, the imprisonment is in a reformatory or house of refuge, such right is not lost. A felon who loses his right to vote may have such right reinstated by an unconditional pardon, or by obtaining a certificate of good conduct by the board of parole in which said disability is spe cifically removed. Sec. 510a P.L.; Sec. 152 Election Law; Sec. 

16 Executive Law. 

Causes of action against a prisoner held by the person injured are preserved. Sec. 512b P.L.

A conviction never operates as a forfeiture of a prisoner's property. Sec. 512 P.L. However, a committee may be appointed of the property of a person sentenced to life imprisonment, and a trustee may be appointed of the property of a person sentenced for more than one year and less than life. Secs. 320, 350

Correction Law. 

No legal stigma or legal disability attaches to a "youthful offender" despite any imprisonment. Secs. 913e et seq. Code.

Counsel Visiting Prisoner. Each place of confinement has visiting days and hours for ordinary visits. Counsel, however, require greater latitude and privacy in conferring with prisoners. Usually, the filing of a notice of appearance entitles counsel to a notice by the clerk addressed to the warden noting such appearance, thus providing the necessary facilities. Where counsel is unable to or has not decided to file such appearance, yet finds it necessary to confer privately with a prisoner, upon oral application to the court having jurisdiction, a permit will usually be issued allowing the required visit. The rules and customs will vary in different localities. 

The mere fact that a notice of appearance has already been filed by one attorney does not bar a visit by any other attorney; the court is empowered where necessary to permit the same for cause shown.

Foreign Representatives. Functionaries of foreign nations are not liable to punishment in this state but are returned to their own countries for disposition. Sec. 25 P.L.

Bill Of Rights. The first ten amendments to the Constitution of the United States are not operative upon the states. Twining v. New Jersey, 211 U. S. 78. However, each state has practically identical constitutional safeguards. 

Freedom From Compulsory Self Incrimination. This topic is discussed in the introductory chapter for the reason that its application may present itself at practically any and every stage of a criminal proceeding.

As noted above, the Fifth Amendment to the federal Constitution is not considered because it is binding only upon the Government and its agencies and does not constitute a limitation upon the states. Peo. v. Adams, 176 NY 351. Our state laws, however, provide the necessary protection.

It is elementary that no person may be compelled in a criminal action to be a witness against himself, nor may a person

charged with crime be subjected to any more restraint than is necessary for his detention. Sec. 10 Code; Art. 1, Sec. 6 NY 

Const.

The privilege need not be claimed to be effective unless the investigation is directed against someone other than the witness. Peo. v. Truesdell, 259 AD 282; Peo. v. Seaman, 174 M 792.

The protection and privilege is waived when the defendant voluntarily takes the stand. Connors v. Peo.. 50 NY 240; Peo. v. Guidici, 100 NY 503; Peo. v. Slover, 232 NY 264.

Under our state Constitution and Code as aforesaid, a person may not be compelled to disclose facts or circumstances tending to prove his connection with any criminal offense, whether a charge is pending or not. Nor may a person be compelled to disclose the sources from which information can be obtained that might connect him with a crime. The assurance of absolute immunity from prosecution is necessary to create an exception to this rule. Peo. ex rel. Taylor v. Forbes, 143 NY 219. 

In the last named citation, the facts plainly indicate the extent of the rule. There, a poisonous gas was injected into a dining room, resulting in death and injury. The gas emanated from jugs containing chemicals and substances for the generation of poisonous gas. The witness before the grand jury boarded in the house whence came the jugs. His roommate was one of the persons suspected. Held, that it was a proper case for the assertion of the constitutional protection. Whether innocent or guilty, a combination of facts and circumstances that might form a link in a chain of evidence sufficient to subject a person to the hazard of a trial upon a criminal charge, warrants the exercise of the privilege.

The right extends to every element of judicial compulsion tending to bind the witness to a crime. Where it is plain, however, that the answer cannot possibly subject the witness to a criminal prosecution, the protection is not available. Peo. v. 

Lewis, 14 M 264. Unless it is perfectly clear, however, that there is no danger of injury or peril of prosecution, the witness is the sole judge as to the effect of the evidence sought to be elicited from him.

The right prevails irrespective of whether the witness is

INTRODUCTORY-MISCELLANEOUS 

7 called before a grand jury, coroner, committing magistrate or other body. Peo. v. Ferola, 215 NY 285.

The answer to the question need not connect the witness directly to a crime. The privilege is available if the answer would tend to disclose merely a link in a chain of evidence which might effect a conviction. Peo. v. Priori, 164 NY 459. 

A lawyer may avail himself of this shield if the answer might lead to a prosecution for the forfeiture of his license to practice. Matier of Kaffenburgh, 188 NY 49.

The protection extends to disclosures sought by legal process only. For example, while a witness may not be compelled to produce records by any process which treats him as a witness in a situation where production would result in the possibility of a prosecution against him, records obtained from a person's control without the use of process against him as a witness, may be used evidentially against him. Also, records turned over to a receiver, trustee or other similar representative may in turn be transferred to and used by prosecutors. Peo. v. Mandel, 90 M 170.

A penal provision to the effect that the failure of a person to produce his books upon demand in certain instances shall presumptively establish culpability, is not a violation of the privilege. Peo. ex rel. Woronoff v. Mallon, 166 AD 840.

Further Specific Cases. The rule is not violated by compelling a defendant to rise for the purpose of identification. Peo. v. Gardner, 144 NY 119. Taking a defendant's shoes for comparison with footprints is likewise no violation. Peo. v. Van 

Wormer, 175 NY 188. Compulsion to fingerprinting is also proper. Peo. v. Sallow, 100 Misc. 447.

A physician despatched to jail to examine a prisoner's mental condition, may testify concerning his opinion thereon, provided no testimony is presented of statements made by the prisoner thereon. Peo. v. Kemmler, 119 NY 580.

Where the defense of insanity is interposed, the examination of the defendant in an anteroom by medical experts for the people, is not violative of the safeguard. Peo. v. Truck, 170 NY 203.

In other words, while the law will jealously guard a person's rights, it will not permit the rule to be carried to an absurd result.

JURISDICTION 

Any question of jurisdiction presents issues relating either to (a) jurisdiction of the person, (b) jurisdiction of the subject matter, or (c) territorial jurisdiction.

JURISDICTION OF THE PERSON 

In a criminal action, the court is not concerned with the background of the defendant's physical presence in the jurisdiction. 

How he came or was brought is irrelevant. An illegal arrest, for example, does not affect the court's jurisdiction over the person of the defendant. Peo. v. Iverson, 46 AD 301. Nor does it matter that the defendant was illegally and forcibly brought into the jurisdiction. Peo. v. Dileo, 194 AD 793.

JURISDICTION OF THE SUBJECT MATTER 

The jurisdiction of a court over the subject matter, however, namely, the offense charged, cannot be acquired even by consent if it does not otherwise exist, and the objection thereto cannot be waived. Peo. v. Jones, 142 AD 180. That the court is not invested by statute with jurisdiction over a certain offense, is therefore a fatal bar to jurisdiction.

TERRITORIAL JURISDICTION 

Generally, a crime must be prosecuted in the county where the same was committed. In respect of minor offenses, the statutes relating thereto may restrict such prosecution to more limited areas. Occasionally, it is difficult to confine the crime charged to a given county, in which event provision is made for specific or alternative territorial jurisdiction, as hereinafter discussed.

Boundary of Different Counties. Jurisdiction vests in either county where a crime is committed on the boundary of two or more counties or within five hundred yards thereof. Sec. 135

Code. 

Several Counties. Where parts of a crime or acts which are necessary elements thereof transpire in different counties, jurisdiction vests in any county wherein the acts were committed. 

Sec. 134 Code. 

A promise of marriage in one county followed by seduction in another county, confers jurisdiction in either county. Peo. v. Crotty, 9 NYS 937.

Jurisdiction in Kings County has been sustained on a charge of manslaughter where a defendant purchased wood alcohol in Kings County, had the same adulterated in New York County, made partial delivery to customer in Connecticut, and the deceased ultimately purchased same in Massachusetts. Peo. v. Licenziata, 199 AD 106.

A defendant who, while out of the state, induces the perpetration of a crime in New York County, through an innocent individual, is properly indicted and tried in said county. Peo. v. 

Dryden, 250 NY 154.

Receiving Stolen Property. An indictment for criminally receiving stolen property must be found in the county where the property was received. The scene of the original larceny is immaterial. Peo. v. Zimmer, 174 AD 470.

Grand Larceny-Automobile. 

An indictment for grand larceny based upon the stealing of an automobile may be found either in the county where the property was stolen or in any county into which it was taken or found. Peo. v. Spivak, 237 NY 460. Conspiracy.

Conspirators may be indicted in any county where an overt act is committed by them, or by any one of them, in furtherance of their common design, though the crime which is the object of the conspiracy is to be committed out of the

JURISDICTION 

11 state. It matters not that at the time of the overt act some of the conspirators were elsewhere. Peo. v. Summerfield, 48 M 242. 

Criminal Libel. Libel against a resident confers jurisdiction either in the county where the paper is published or where the party libeled resides. The defendant may change the venue to the county where the libel was printed by posting a bond securing the complainant's expenses in case of a conviction. Sec. 138 

Code. 

Where a non-resident is libeled, jurisdiction vests in the county where the libel is printed and published. If the paper does not, upon its face, purport to be published in a particular county, jurisdiction vests in any county where the paper is circulated. Sec. 138 Code.

Vessel. A crime committed on board of a vessel navigating a river, lake or canal is triable in any county through which said waterway passes or on which it borders. Sec. 136 Code. 

Carrier. Any county through which a railway train, omnibus, truck, aeroplane or other carrier passes or ends its run has jurisdiction of a crime committed therein. Sec. 137 Code. 

Atlantic Ocean.

Jurisdiction extends into the Atlantic Ocean two nautical miles distant from the shore at the high water mark. Sec. 11a Code.

Chapter COURTS 

The court of original summary jurisdiction depends usually upon the nature and gravity of the crime. The basis for said jurisdiction must be found either in the Constitution or statutes. 

Supreme Court. The Supreme Court, which is our highest state court of original jurisdiction (Art. 5 Judiciary Law), may assume jurisdiction of all crimes committed in the county triable by indictment. The Supreme Court has the power to assume jurisdiction of an indictment originating in the County Court or Court of General Sessions and to refer cases to such courts for disposition. Art. 6, Sec. 1 NY Const.; Sec. 22 Code. 

In respect of minor crimes usually tried in Special Sessions and similar tribunals, jurisdiction attaches only after the presentment of an indictment or the issuance of a certificate authorizing a prosecution by indictment. Secs. 22, 57 Code.

Whether or not it has assumed jurisdiction, the Supreme Court has the right to admit an accused to bail. Sec. 22 Code. 

County Court-Court of General Sessions. County courts have jurisdiction of all indictable crimes committed or triable in the county, subject to the power of removal by the Supreme 

Court. Secs. 22, 39 Code; Art. 6 Judiciary Law. Formal re moval is essential. Peo. ex. rel. Albanese v. Hunt, 177 M 151. 

Jurisdiction attaches to minor crimes upon the issuance of a certificate directing the prosecution thereof by indictment. 

Secs. 39, 57 Code; Sec. 31 NYCCCA.

New York County is the only county without a county court. 

In said county the Court of General Sessions exercises practically identical jurisdiction in criminal matters. Secs. 50, 51, 961

Code.

Court of Special Sessions. Courts of Special Sessions have existed since 1744. Peo. ex rel. Murray v. Justices, 74 NY 406.

They have jurisdiction generally of misdemeanor cases and paternity proceedings. Secs. 56-64a Code; Sec. 60 NYCCCA;

In New Yok City this court is usually conducted by three judges (34 NYCCCA); elsewhere, by one justice of the peace.

These courts may be divested of jurisdiction by obtaining a certificate that the charge be prosecuted by indictment (59

Magistrate's Court-Police Court. The prosecution of minor misdemeanors and violations of ordinances is conducted in various police courts and before justices of the peace throughout the state (74 Code), before district courts in Nassau County, and before city magistrates in New York City. Sec. 102

NYCCCA. As committing magistrates, these judges are authorized to hold defendants to answer before higher courts in those instances where they lack summary jurisdiction. These courts are not courts of record. Sec. 2 Judiciary Law; Sec. 10 Code.

Jurisdiction may be divested where a defendant obtains a certificate that the charge be prosecuted by indictment (39, 57, 59 Code); also, where an indictment is found before commencement of trial. Sec. 59a Code. 

Domestic Relations: Children. As to children under sixteen years of age and support and other family proceedings, the Children's Court Act of the State of New York is applicable through the state, except in New York City and Chautauqua and Ontario counties. In New York City, such matters are gov erned by the Domestic Relations Court Act of the City of New

York; in the counties of Chautauqua and Ontario, by the childrens' courts established therein.

APPELLATE COURTS 

Court of Appeals. The Court of Appeals, our highest state court (Art. 3 Judiciary Law), entertains appeals from (a) Ap pellate Divisions of the Supreme Court, (b) lower courts when constitutional questions are properly raised, (c) lower courts when leave to appeal has been granted either by the Appellate Division or Court of Appeals, and (d) trial courts directly in death cases. Art. 6, Sec. 7 NY Const. 

This court consists of seven judges and may be increased to eleven. Five members constitute a quorum and the concurrence of four is necessary to decision. No more than seven judges may sit in any case. Art. 6, Sec. 5 NY Const.

Appellate Division. The Appellate Division is a branch of the Supreme Court. There are four departments of this court throughout the state, designated as the First Department, Second Department, Third Department and Fourth Depart ment. This court entertains appeals from the Supreme Court, County Court and Court of General Sessions, except in death cases. In New York City, appeals from the Court of Special

Sessions are also heard by this court (520 Code), as well as appeals from convictions by city magistrates of criminal contempt.

Sec. 40 NYCCCA. 

The court consists of seven justices in the First Department, seven justices in the Second Department, five justices in the 

Third Department and five justices in the Fourt Department. 

No more than five justices may sit in any case. Four justices constitute a quorum and the concurrence of three is necessary to a decision. Art. 6, Sec. 2 NY Const.; Art. 4 Judiciary Law. 

County Court. This court has appellate jurisdiction outside of New York City, and entertains appeals from courts of special sessions, police courts, police magistrates and justices of the peace, provided the same are allowed by it or the Supreme 

Court. Secs. 39, 749-751 Code; Art. 6 Judiciary Law.

IMPEACHMENTS 

Impeachments against officers of the state are presented by the Assembly after a majority vote therefor (12 Code) and tried by the Senate, together with the judges of the Court of 

Appeals. Sec. 13 Code; Art. 6, Sec. 10 NY Const. Articles of im peachment need not be in any particular form; a resolution by the Assembly reported to the Senate is sufficient. 

Articles of impeachment are delivered by the Assembly to the president of the Senate. Sec. 118 Code. Upon delivery of an impeachment, the president of the Senate is required to convene the court of impeachment not less than thirty nor more than sixty days from the date thereof. Sec. 17 Code.

The president of the Senate is required to cause a copy of the articles of impeachment to be served on the defendant at least twenty days before the date fixed. Sec. 119 Code. If unable to effect personal service upon the defendant, the court may order service by publication. Sec. 120 Code.

The president of the Senate acts as presiding judge. In his absence, such duties devolve upon the chief judge of the Court of Appeals. In the absence of both, the court may elect the presiding judge. Sec. 14 Code. The officers and clerks of the

Senate act in a corresponding capacity upon the trial of an im peachment. Sec. 15 Code. 

Prior to entering upon their duties, the court must take an oath or affirmation truly and impartially to try and determine the impeachment. Sec. 18 Code. 

Objection to the sufficiency of the impeachment must be in writing but need not be in any specific form. The denial of the charges may be oral. The majority of the court determines the sufficiency of the impeachment. Secs. 123, 124 Code.

In the event that the accused pleads guilty, or refuses to plead, he must be convicted. If the charges are disputed, the is-

sues are tried. Sec. 124 Code. The concurrence of two-thirds of the members present is necessary for a conviction; otherwise, the accused must be declared acquitted. Sec. 125 Code. The judgment of the court of impeachment is in the form of a resolution. Secs. 126, 127 Code.

Punishment by the court of impeachment is limited to removal from office and disqualification from holding further office. Sec. 128 Code. However, the accused is subject to indict ment and punishment according to law. Art. 6, Sec. 10 NY Const.; Sec. 131 Code. 

An impeachment suspends the exercise of his office until an officer is acquitted. Sec. 129 Code; Art. 6, Sec. 10 NY Const.

 PROCEEDINGS IN COURT OF SPECIAL SESSIONS IN NEW YORK CITY In General. The Court of Special Sessions of the City of 

New York, designated as an inferior court of criminal jurisdiction (2 NYCCCA), has jurisdiction of misdemeanor cases committed in the city, except libel cases. In certain instances, less important misdemeanors may be summarily disposed of by city magistrates. Sec. 31 NYCCCA.

Notwithstanding that jurisdiction has been acquired by Special Sessions, it may nevertheless be divested in case an indictment has been lodged against the defendant for the particular crime at any time before the actual trial, or a certificate has been issued that the charge be prosecuted by indictment. Sec. 

31 (1-c) NYCCCA. 

Cases reach Special Sessions (except paternity proceedings, which originate there) where a defendant has previously been held to answer to said court by a city magistrate. When a defendant has been bound over accordingly, the district attorney is required to file an "information" in Special Sessions accusing the defendant of the crime involved. The "information” serves the general purpose of an indictment and is to be distinguished from the information used elsewhere to perform the office of a complaint.

The district attorney is not authorized to file an information in Special Sessions unless and until there has been a previous holding by a city magistrate (Peo. v. Dillon, 197 NY 254) or has been directed by the grand jury to prosecute by information. Peo. v. Hughes, 161 M 405. The district attorney is required to be represented at all sessions of the court. Sec. 37 

NYCCCA.

All charges are triable in the county where the crimes were allegedly committed. Sec. 32 NYCCA. Jury trials are spe cifically barred (31 (4) NYCCCA), whereas such trials are permissible in Special Sessions outside of the city. 

Youths from sixteen to eighteen years of age inclusive may receive the benefits that flow from being adjudged "youthful offender" and thus save themselves from the legal stigma and disqualification resulting from from a conviction. Secs. 31a-31h

NYCCCA. 

Separate parts handling cases against adults are provided in the various counties of the city. Sec. 33 NYCCCA. 

Each part of Special Sessions is presided over by one or three justices; if three justices sit, the judgment of two of them represents the judgment of the court. Sec. 34 NYCCCA.

A convicted woman may be committed to a reformatory or institution instead of a penitentiary or workhouse, provided she has not previously been an inmate of a state prison or penitentiary; such commitment to be for an indefinite term but she may not be detained for more than three years. Sec. 35

NYCCCA. 

In the absence of any statute to the contrary, the practice and procedure in the Court of General Sessions shall apply to Special Sessions insofar as its jurisdiction and organization will permit. Sec. 31 NYCCCA; Peo. v. Hammerstein, 150 AD 212; 

Peo. v. Rosenkrantz, 123 M 335.

Except for the fact that the trial is without a jury and some insignificant variations, the procedure is substantially the same as that prevailing where an indictment has been filed, treated elsewhere in this work. The rules of the court should, of course, be consulted as a precautionary measure. 

Though it is not a court of record and deals with criminal causes in a narrow range, the court is competent to exercise generally the powers of higher courts of general jurisdiction, including the power to vacate judgments obtained by fraud. 

Hogan v. Supreme Court, 295 NY 92. 

Paternity Proceedings. Special Sessions also has exclusive jurisdiction to try contested paternity proceedings, which are

civil proceedings. Sec. 60 NYCCCA. Such a proceeding is also quasi-criminal in nature and expands into criminal orbits after judgment against the defendant if he fail to comply with orders of the court. The purpose of the proceeding is to assure support for the child by the putative father. 

A proceeding to establish the paternity of a child and to compel support must be brought within two years after the birth of the child, unless (a) paternity has been acknowledged by the father in writing or by furnishing support, or (b) the child, if under sixteen years of age, is or may become a public charge. 

Any county in the city has jurisdiction to try the proceeding. 

Sec. 64 NYCCCA. The Corporation Counsel prosecutes the mat ter if the Department of Public Welfare is the complainant.

Sec. 77 NYCCCA. 

The proceeding is initiated by a complaint, in which the defendant is charged with being the father of the child. Sec. 64 (2) NYCCCA. The court may issue a summons, to be served upon the defendant personally, or issue a warrant for his ar rest. Sec. 64 (3) NYCCCA. 

The proceeding may be brought by the mother, guardian of the child, any person standing in parental relation to or being the next of kin of the child, or any charitable or philanthropic corporation. In case the mother or child is or may become a public charge, the Department of Public Welfare may institute the proceeding. Sec. 64 (1) NYCCCA. 

With the approval of the court, upon notice to the Commissioner of Public Welfare, the dispute may be compromised. Sec. 

63 NYCCCA. Proceedings do not abate upon the death of the mother, or if she become insane or disappear. Sec. 66 NYCCCA.

Any adjournment of the trial may be conditioned upon an undertaking to be furnished by the defendant for his appearance, if the adjournment be asked either by him or the complainant. Sec. 68 NYCCCA. 

Should the defendant fail to appear for trial, not only may any undertaking be forfeited and the amount thereof applied on account of payments directed by the court, but the trial may proceed without him. Sec. 67 (2) NYCCCA. The trial is held without a jury. The public may be excluded.

The mother and defendant are competent to testify. If the mother be married, she and her husband may testify to nonaccess. If the defendant shall offer testimony of access by others at or about the time charged in the complaint, the same need not be corroborated. Sec. 67 (1) NYCCCA; Comm'r v. Koehler, 284 NY 260.

Upon the defendant's motion, the court must order the parties to submit to one or more blood-grouping tests, but the results thereof are admissible only where definite exclusion is established. Sec. 67 (1-a) NYCCCA. 

Should the court find against the defendant upon the trial, an order of filiation is entered declaring paternity and ordering support of the child, specifying the amount and manner of payment. Support is directed until the child becomes sixteen, but for cause shown later, may be continued during the child's minority. Sec. 69 NYCCCA. 

The court may require payments to be made to the mother as trustee, or some other person or duly authorized corporation, but if the child is or may become a public charge, the Commissioner of Public Welfare is made the trustee. The trustee must account to the court periodically. Arrears may be cancelled, in the discretion of the court, and the trustee substituted. 

Sec. 70 NYCCCA. 

In the event that paternity be established, the court may place the defendant on probation, upon such terms as the court may deem proper, or require him to furnish an undertaking to secure the payments ordered, failing in which or in default thereof the defendant may be committed to the workhouse for a period of one year. Liability continues in such event and Commitment proceedings may be renewed if the defendant persist in his defaults. The violation of probation also subjects the defendant to imprisonment. Sec. 71 NYCCCA. The court exercises continuing jurisdiction. Sec. 72 NYCCCA. 

Burden of Proof. A novel standard of proof applies upon the trial of a paternity proceeding. The testimony must be "entirely satisfactory" in order to warrant an order of filiation.

This test rests somewhere between the rule of "fair preponder-

ance" in civil cases and the rule of "reasonable doubt" in criminal cases; stronger than the former and below the standard of the latter. Comm'r v. Unger, 264 AD 894; Comm'r v. Ryan, 238 

AD 607. 

Outside New York City. Affiliation proceedings outside of

New York City are governed by the Domestic Relations Law (Art. 8). 

REMOVAL OF CAUSE FROM SPECIAL SESSIONS 

The Court of Special Sessions in New York City may be divested of jurisdiction when it is determined that the charge should be prosecuted by indictment, in which event the matter is presented by the district attorney to the grand jury. Sec. 31

NYCCCA. 

An application for a transfer may be made to the Supreme Court, County Court or Court of General Sessions, upon at least two days' notice to the district attorney. Sec. 31 NYCCCA.

A transfer is effected when the court certifies that it is "reasonable" that the charge should be prosecuted by indictment. 

Sec. 31 NYCCCA. 

Such an application is addressed to the sound discretion of the court. Peo. v. Rosenberg, 59 M 342; Peo. v. Hughes, 161 M 

405; Peo. v. Juskowitz, 173 M 685.

Generally, to warrant the granting of such relief, one of the following circumstances should appear: (a) That a case presents intricate and complicated questions of fact rendering a jury trial proper. (b) That difficult questions of law are presented. (c) That a property right is involved. (d) That a decision may become a precedent which will regulate a matter of general interest. (e) That the defendant cannot have a fair trial in Special Sessions.

(f) That the case is of exceptional character and may be far reaching in its effect. (g) That the punishment is severe and includes a forfeiture of the right to continue business (Peo. v. Gantz, 41 M 542), or the forfeiture of a position and pension. 

Peo. v. Willis, 59 M 371. (h) That there is a probability of a protracted trial which would interfere with the dispatch of judicial business in Special Sessions. Peo. v. Schumann, 146 M 395.

Pending the determinaton of an application for a certificate, proceedings may be stayed for a period not exceeding ten days. 

Sec. 31 NYCCCA; Sec. 57 Code. 

The granting of a certificate that it is reasonable that the charge be prosecuted by indictment, stays further proceedings. in Special Sessions except admission to bail, and requires a transmission of all records to the district attorney. Sec. 31

NYCCCA. 

The approval of a direction by a grand jury that an information be filed in Special Sessions does not preclude the Supreme Court from granting a certificate that the charge be prosecuted by indictment. Matter of Dodge, 276 NY 444.

Substantially the same procedure is applicable to courts of 

Special Sessions outside of New York City and to cases pending in magistrates' courts. Sec. 57 Code.

PROCEEDINGS IN MAGISTRATES' COURT IN NEW YORK CITY 

The Magistrates' Court of the City of New York, an inferior court of criminal jurisdiction (2 NYCCCA), is set up into various districts throughout the city, the boundaries of which correspond as far as practicable with police precincts (100 

NYCCCA), subject to other distribution as required with respect to special courts within the system, such as the Felony Court, Night Court, Adolescent Court, Wayward Minor Court. and Municipal Term.

The various special branches above noted have been established to accomplish a more efficient administration of the criminal

laws in the congested metropolitan area, and also to conform to modern scientific views in furtherance of justice and the póssible rehabilitation of certain classes of persons charged with crime. 

The distribution of said special courts throughout the city conform to requirements best suited to meet the needs of the population. Each special court is usually subject to statutes peculiarly applicable thereto, found in the New York City Criminal Courts Act and also rules promulgated by the court itself. 

In a broad sense, the court is treated on substantially the same basis as the police courts throughout the state within the purview of the Code and Penal Law. 

A charge must be tried within the district unless it is one entertained by one of the special courts, and in case it appears that the same is brought in the wrong district, the magistrate is required to transfer the same to the proper one. Sec. 106

NYCCCA. 

A magistrate has power to punish as for a contempt any disorderly or contumacious conduct which tends to interrupt the proceedings or impair the respect due to the court. Sec. 102-b

NYCCCA. 

Nature of Jurisdiction. Stated generally, a city magistrate has summary jurisdiction of persons charged with violations of laws or ordinances known as "offenses" as distinguished from aver violations designated as "misdemeanors" or "felonies." Some minor misdemeanors are also within his summary jurisdiction. Secs. 31, 102, etc. NYCCCA.

Unless a violation of a statute or ordinance is specifically classified otherwise, it is safe to assume that it is merely an "offense.”

As to matters concerning which the magistrate does not possess summary jurisdiction, a hearing before him is not a prosecution but an inquiry. Peo. ex rel. Pringle v. Conway, 121 M 

620. The principal function of such a hearing is to determine whether a crime has been committed and also if there is reasonable ground to believe that the defendant is guilty thereof. If both are found, the defendant is held or let to bail pending ac-

tion by the grand jury in a felony case and Special Sessions in a misdemeanor case.

It is also the proper function of such a preliminary examination to perpetuate the testimony of a witness who may not be available later, either because of the fear that the defendant will influence his testimony or interfere with such appearance. 

Peo. v. Albero, 119 M 339.

Proceedings are predicated on a formal instrument designated as an "information," sometimes called a "complaint."

The Information-Nature. 

An information is a written allegation made to a magistrate that a person is guilty of a designated crime. Sec. 145 Code. It must be verified before the magistrate. Sec. 148 Code; Peo. ex rel. Livingston v. Wyatt, 186 

NY 383. The terms "information," "deposition" and "complaint" are interchangeable in everyday practice. 

The information herein discussed must be distinguished from the document bearing the same designation which is filed by the district attorney in Special Sessions to correspond with the functions of an indictment.

Speaking broadly, the information in any case performs the same office as an indictment. Its object is to bring the defendant to the bar of justice. Peo. ex rel. Travis v. Knott, 204 AD 379.

Contents of Information. The information must recite the essential elements of the crime charged. The material facts constituting the offense must be stated. It is fatal to omit a principal element of the crime charged. Sec. 149 Code; Peo. v. 

Wacke, 77 M 196.

Where the crime is statutory, the precise facts necessary to constitute the offense, as contained in the statute, must be alleged. Peo. v. Wacke, 77 Misc. 169. So long as it contains the essential allegations, an information is valid though it be inartificially drawn or crudely composed. Peo. v. Pillion, 78 Hun 

74. Relaxed rules governing form must not be confused, however, with the strict requirements as to the necessary substance of an information.

An information stating that the defendant struck the com-

plainant, without alleging that said act was done wrongfully and unlawfully, has been held to be insufficient for a conviction, since it did not show that the defendant was guilty of a designated crime. Peo. v. Hiley, 33 M 168.

An information which does not charge a designated person with a specified crime is insufficient to confer jurisdiction. Matter of Both, 200 AD 423.

If an information is based upon information and belief, it is defective unless accompanied by a statement setting forth the source of the information and the grounds for the belief thereof. Peo. v. Cramer, 22 AD 189.

It matters not that the informant is actuated by malice, hatred or revenge. Peo. ex rel. Willett v. Quinn, 150 AD 813.

It has been held, however, that an information sufficient to authorize a magistrate to issue a subpoena for the purpose of investigating whether a crime has been committed, must be verified, must recite facts sufficient to show that the complainant is acting in good faith, and that the complainant has reasonable grounds to believe that a crime has been committed by some person named or described. Peo. ex rel. Livingston v. 

Wyatt, 186 NY 383.

Defective Information. The fact that a defendant pleads guilty does not constitute a waiver of his right to attack an information which legally charges no crime. Peo. v. Williams, 135 

M 564.

A defective information is cured if essential elements omitted are supplied upon a hearing. Peo. v. Shenk, 142 NYS 1081. 

Summons. 

The summons issued in this court bears the name of the complainant, the name of the person summoned, and also contains a brief description of the offense charged, and may be served by the complainant, a peace officer, or any person designated by the magistrate. Sec. 116 NYCCCA.

Disobedience of a summons subjects the offender to arrest to answer the crime charged.

Summonses in blank are issued to police officers, attested in the name of the chief city magistrate. When filled in and coun-

tersigned by them, they have the same force and effect as though issued by a magistrate. Sec. 116 (h) NYCCCA.

Subpoena. Subpoenae may be served in any county of the city without any endorsements being required. Disobedience of a subpoena is now punishable as for a contempt. Sec. 102-b (4)

NYCCCA. 

Bail. A city magistrate has power, within prescribed limits, to admit a defendant to bail. Sec. 103 NYCCCA.

Short Affidavit. A "short affidavit” is an affidavit submitted in a felony case (also assault in third degree) by a peace officer stating that the complaining witness is unavailable or that the evidence is incomplete. The magistrate may thereupon commit the defendant for a period not to exceed forty-eight hours, unless the defendant consents to a longer adjournment. 

In case of a personal injury, the hearing may be adjourned from time to time in a proper case. Sec. 107 NYCCCA.

Rights of Defendant. Upon apprehension and appearance before a magistrate, a defendant must be informed of the charge against him and of his right to the aid of counsel. Sec. 188 

Code; Sec. 81 NYCCCA; Peo. v. Griesman, 149 NYS 63. If requested by the defendant, the court must also require an officer to communicate a message to the defendant's counsel, without charge. Sec. 189 Code. The magistrate is required to wait a reasonable time for the appearance of counsel. Sec. 190 Code.

Provision has been made for free communication with relatives or friends. Sec. 115 NYCCCA. 

The hearing may be adjourned for good cause. Sec. 191 Code.

The refusal to allow an adjournment in order to obtain counsel is equivalent to a deprivation of the right to cross-examine, and a conviction under those circumstances will not be sustained. 

Peo. v. Sperduto, 221 AD 577.

A defendant's application for an adjournment in order to enable him to obtain counsel is properly denied, however, where it appears that he has been warned sufficiently in advance by the district attorney to obtain counsel and it also appears that

the defendant is not inexperienced in criminal matters. Peo. v. 

Urban, 179 AD 446.

The defendant has the right to cross-examine witnesses for the people and to obtain subpoenae required by him for the attendance of witnesses in his behalf. Secs. 194, 195 Code.

At the close of the people's case, the magistrate must inform the defendant of his right to make a statement or waive the same and that his waiver cannot be used against him. Sec. 196

Code. If the defendant chooses to make a statement, the procedure to be followed is described in sections 198 to 200 of the Code.

Where it is not shown that a defendant was informed of the charge against him, of his right to counsel, and of his right to waive making a statement, a statement made by him before a magistrate is not properly received. Peo. v. Stein, 221 AD 500.

A waiver is an intentional relinquishment of a known right. 

Otherwise, it cannot be claimed that a defendant waived his rights. There must be full knowledge of their existence and an intention of relinquish them. Peo. v. Abetti, 152 NYS 890. 

Waiving Examination. The accused may waive his right to a hearing and consent to be held for the action of the grand jury or Special Sessions, depending on the nature of the crime charged. Secs. 190, 194 Code. The accused or his counsel thereby merely states in substance "the defendant waives examination." All that the defendant does by waiving an examination is to say that so far as he is concerned the people need offer no evidence in said court.

The magistrate may, however, proceed with the hearing despite the waiver. Sec. 190 Code. A district attorney has the right, also, to insist on a hearing despite the waiver. Peo. v. Albero, 119 M 339. Such a preliminary examination would be useful to a prosecutor when he fears that witnesses will not be available in later proceedings, in which event, in a proper case, the testimony adduced before the magistrate could be introduced upon the trial.

Removal. The Supreme Court or County Court (including

General Sessions) has authority to remove a proceeding to the proper court for prosecution by indictment, by a finding that it is reasonable that the charge be prosecuted by indictment, thus affording the defendant the opportunity of a jury trial. However, such power extends only in case the charge be a misdemeanor as distinguished from an "infraction." In the latter case, removal for such purpose is not proper. Sec. 57 Code; Peo. v. Schacher, 181 M 769. rial. A trial may be had on Sunday or a holiday. Sec. 101

NYCCCA. 

A defendant must be tried on the information and not on the warrant. Peo. v. Olmstead, 74 Hun 323.

A variance between the testimony and the information is not reviewable on appeal unless objection is properly made upon the trial. Peo. v. McCarthy, 250 NY 358.

If it appears that a crime was not committed or that there is no sufficient cause to believe the defendant guilty thereof, he is discharged (Sec. 207 Code); otherwise, he is held to answer the charge (Sec. 208 Code) and the papers are transmitted to the court next acquiring jurisdiction, or to the district attorney of the county in case of a misdemeanor. Sec. 221 Code.

In case of a defendant previously arrested or convicted of a felony, the magistrate, if he discharge the defendant, must reord his reasons therefor, together with the complete criminal record of the defendant. Sec. 207a Code.

A discharge by a magistrate is not a bar to a renewal of charges or a subsequent indictment. Peo. v. Dillon, 197 NY 254. 

In those instances where a magistrate does possess summary jurisdiction, as specifically provided, his powers are enlarged accordingly and he is thereby empowered to make a final determination, subject only to review by appeal in a proper case. 

Sitting as a Court of Special Sessions. Section 130 of the 

New York Criminal Courts Act describes those cases where a city magistrate may hold a court of Special Sessions. This enlarged power is useful in disposing of many minor crimes aț first hand. The enlarged power aforesaid does not, however,

carry with it all the powers of a city magistrate as such; e. g., a magistrate sitting as a Court of Special Sessions is thereby deprived of the right to try a case on Sunday. Peo. v. Citarelli, 247 AD 53.

The magistrate is required to advise the defendant that he has the right to be tried by Special Sessions, if it be one of the misdemeanors in question. However, at any time before the magistrate hears any testimony upon the trial, demand to be tried in Special Sessions may be made by the defendant, or the district attorney, or the legal representative of the city or state department in charge of the prosecution. Similarly, the magistrate is empowered to direct a trial in Special Sessions. Sec. 

131 NYCCCA. 

In the event that the parties do not consent that the trial be held by the magistrate sitting as a court of Special Sessions, the magistrate presides as a committing magistrate and either discharges the defendant or holds him for Special Sessions, as the evidence warrants. Sec. 131 NYCCCA. 

In the event that the magistrate sits as a court of Special Sessions, he is endowed with all the powers and jurisdiction vested in Special Sessions. Sec. 131 NYCCCA. 

PROCEEDINGS IN DOMESTIC RELATIONS COURT IN NEW YORK CITY Under the provisions of the "Domestic Relations Court Act of the City of New York" the legislature established the "Domestic Relations Court of the City of New York" to exercise jurisdiction over children and in family support proceedings. 

Said court succeeded the Children's Court of the City of New York and the Family Court Division of the Magistrate's Court. 

The legislature is authorized to create such a court by the ex press terms of the Constitution. Art. 6, Sec. 18. 

The Domestic Relations Court is a court of limited jurisdiction (not a court of record) and possesses no powers not expressly conferred in the Act. Andrews v. Andrews, 185 M 970; 

Tell v. Tell, 53 NYS2d 94, 98.

Parents are chargeable for expenses incurred by the city in

maintaining children committed by the court, in proper cases, recoverable in the Domestic Relations Court in a separate proceeding instituted by the appropriate city department. Sec. 56-a 

DRCA. 

In addition to its administrative departments, the Domestic 

Relations Court includes two branches known as "Children's

Court" and "Family Court." As the names indicate, the former division deals with children generally and the latter with support proceedings.

The titles used in proceedings in said court usually include the name of the county, the division in which the proceeding is brought, and the nature of the proceeding. 

Children's Court Division. This division exercises jurisdiction in proceedings involving children generally, except support, and includes their custody, commitment, parole, probation or other disposition, as the circumstances require. Jurisdiction also extends to any adult charged with an act or omission in respect to a child in violation of law, provided the crime be less than a felony. Sec. 61 DRCA; In re Rosen, 54 NYS2d 632; Peo. v.

Denny, 50 NYS2d 435. In enforcing a penal statute, however, the court is limited to those violations which are collateral to its primary jurisdiction. Gardner v. Domestic Relations Court, 184 

M 44.

A "child" is a person actually or apparently under sixteen years of age. A “delinquent child" is one who commits a crime or is otherwise obnoxious as described in the Act. A child who is "delinquent" is adjudged guilty of "juvenile delinquency."

Such an adjudication, however, carries no stigma legally and effects no disqualification of civil rights. Children in other categories are also subject to the jurisdiction of the court, such as "neglected" and "destitute" children, as defined in the Act, or physically handicapped minors.

Proceedings are initiated by the filing of a petition by any person or agency interested, setting forth the facts which allegedly confer jurisdiction. A summons is thereupon issued for the purpose of effecting the necessary appearances before the court. Wide latitude is granted in the use of summonses by

authorized agencies and officers, subject, however, to accounting therefor to the presiding justice. A copy of the summons must be served upon the person to whom it is directed, at least one day before the return date. In a proper case, substituted service may be ordered, and if necessary, a warrant of arrest or other process issued. Disobedience of process is punishable as for a contempt. The public may be excluded upon any hearing. Secs. 

71, 72-a, 73, 77 DRCA.

As the circumstances require, a child may be committed, discharged, released on bail, placed on probation, or paroled. The 

Act goes into detail concerning the places where a child may or may not be detained or imprisoned and the various dispositions which the court is empowered to make after determining the issues. Secs. 81, 82, 83 DRCA; Matter of Kingsley, 183 M 

727. The court is granted wide powers assisted by experts and institutions, to determine also the mental fitness of a child and in providing assistance to the physically handicapped. Sec. 85

DRCA. 

The court is empowered to modify determinations made by it, as changed or other circumstances require, or upon the usual legal grounds that warrant a new hearing. In cases where there is a conflict between an institution and an interested person as to the continued detention of a child, the remedy is for the person aggrieved to file a petition, to which the custodian must file an answer, and the issues determined by the court. Sec. 86

DRCA. 

At least once each year, each "delinquent" child committed to an institution must be visited by a justice of the court. When practicable, a child is remanded to an agency or institution of the same religious faith. The same policy is followed in respect of guardians. Secs. 87, 88 DRCA. 

Not only does an adjudication of delinquency create no disqualification of any kind, but the fact that a child appeared before such a court or made any incriminating statements therein, may not be shown. Sec. 84 DRCA.

Family Court Division. This division has jurisdiction in proceedings to support minor children or other dependents.

A

husband is chargeable with the support of his wife and children, and if able, may be required to pay a fair and reasonable sum therefor as directed by the court. The amount of support may be ordered on the basis of means and earning capacity. Lorenzo v. Lorenzo, 183 M 46; Tell v. Tell, 53 NYS2d 94, 100. If the father be dead or incapable of furnishing support, the mother is chargeable for the support of a child. If the parents are unavailable for said purpose, the grandparents are so chargeable. 

In case of a dependent over seventeen years of age, the court may apportion the support among the members of the family. 

The failure to obey a mandate for support warrants a finding of "non-support" which is a crime punishable by imprisonment. 

Secs. 92, 101, 102 DRCA.

Proceedings are initiated by the filing of a petition by any interested person, called the "petitioner" and directed against the "respondent." The designations "complainant" and "defendant" may not be used. The petition is amplified by a "petitioner's statement" which forms part of the case history. The petitioner is interviewed by a probation officer. Sec. 112 DRCA.

The person sought to be charged must either be a resident of the city at the time the petition is filed, or found in the city provided the petitioner be a resident. If a parent, and neither a resident nor found in the city, it must be shown that he or she was previously a resident and either failed to provide support or abandoned the dependent. Sec. 103 DRCA; Almandares v. 

Almandares, 60 NYS2d 164.

A probation officer visits the home, interviews relatives and others, investigates the material allegations, and renders a report. Records are checked for other relevant proceedings and social agencies contacted for any available information regarding the parties. Banks and employers are required to furnish pertinent information. Secs. 114, 115, 116, 117 DRCA.

The respondent is notified of the application and invited to tell his side of the case on a form known as "respondent's statement" which also becomes part of the case history. The court investigators check into his statements but they are expressly forbidden to disturb the respondent's relations with his employer. Efforts are made at conciliation, which, if successful,

culminate in an agreement which is submitted to the court for approval. Sec. 118 DRCA. 

The investigating section of the court is required to present the petition to the court, with the case history and a summary of the essential facts. In its discretion, the court may act before the preliminaries are concluded. Where no agreement has been reached, a summons is issued, or, in a proper case, a warrant of arrest. Secs. 122, 123 DRCA.

For the protection of the petitioner, provision has been made for "certificates" to show either that a warrant has been issued or that "an order of protection" has been made, which may be exhibited to officers when their assistance is required or desirable. Secs. 125, 126 DRCA.

The summons may be served personally or by mail, except that no default order may be entered in the latter case without proof of the receipt of the summons. Upon the return date, a hearing is held and an appropriate order entered. Secs. 127, 128

DRCA. The respondent must be timely informed of his right to counsel and of the charge against him in case of a trial. 

Spouses are competent witnesses against each other. A respondent is presumed to be able to furnish support, which, like any other presumption, may be rebutted. Sec. 131 DRCA.

Failure to comply with the order of the court warrants the arrest of the respondent. If the respondent is employed by or draws a pension from the city, deductions may be made therefor. An additional remedy against available property is a writ of seizure, utilized in case of disobedience and in case the respondent has either fled or threatens to flee. Such a writ also lies before the entry of an order for support if the respondent has fled and his whereabouts are unknown. Secs. 132, 133

DRCA. 

The court is empowered to suspend sentence or suspend the enforcement of any order and place the respondent on probation, upon such terms as the court may deem proper. Any disposition may be revoked in the court's discretion. The respondent may also be required to furnish an undertaking to secure compliance with the order of support. Secs. 134, 135, 136, 139, 151, DRCA.

The pendency or termination of proceedings in the Supreme Court or elsewhere do not preclude the granting of relief in the 

Domestic Relations Court in a proper case. Sec. 137 DRCA; 

Bergen v. Bergen, 186 M 34; Schacht v. Schacht, 58 NYS2d 54.

CORONER'S INQUEST 

A coroner's inquest is strictly an ex parte inquiry to determine the cause of death and if possible the responsibility therefor. Matter of Senior v. Boyle, 221 NY 414; Peo. v. McGonegal, 136 NY 62;; Gould v. State, 181 M 884

The coroner, a county officer, is under a duty to inquire into the cause of death or dangerous wounding when (a) the killing or wounding is done by another person; (b) sudden death occurs under such circumstances as to indicate criminality; and (c) in suicide cases. Sec. 773 Code; Ehlers v. Blood, 175 M 72.

In cities having a population of more than five hundred thousand, a jury of not less than nine nor more than fifteen is chosen for that purpose. Sec. 773 Code. Witnesses may be subpoenaed by the coroner (Sec. 775 Code) and warrants issued for the arrest of persons chargeable with the crime. Sec. 773 

Code. Disobedience of a subpocna is punishable as for a criminal contempt. Sec. 776 Code. 

A surgeon or physician must be summoned by the coroner to inspect the body and render an opinion as to the cause of the death or wounding. Sec. 775 Code. Testimony of witnesses must be reduced to writing and filed with the county clerk or clerk of the city court having jurisdiction. Sec. 778 Code. After inspecting the body and hearing the testimony, the coroner must render his decision. If a jury has been selected as above noted, they must render their verdict. Sec. 777 Code. In a proper case, an autopsy may be held. Sec. 2213 P.L.: Gould v. State, 181 M 884.

The finding of the coroner or jury upon the inquest must be certified by a paper called the "inquisition" signed by the coroner or the jury, as the case may be, and setting forth (a) the name of the victim; (b) when, where and by what means he was killed or wounded; and (c) so far as ascertainable, the guilty person. Sec. 777 Code,

A warrant may be issued by the coroner for the arrest of the person named in the inquisition. Sec. 780 Code. After an arrest, a certified copy of the inquisition and testimony must be furnished by the clerk with whom the same has been filed to the magistrate before whom the defendant is brought for exam ination. Sec. 784 Code. 

When a defendant is brought before the magistrate, proceedings upon the charge contained in the inquisition are the same as if a warrant of arrest had been issued by a magistrate on an information. Sec. 783 Code.

In the absence of the coroner, a justice of the peace is empowered to discharge the duties of the coroner. Sec. 789a Code. 

Abolished in New York City. The office of coroner has been abolished in New York City. Violent and suspicious deaths are investigated by a medical examiner, who is empowered to perform autopsies. A written report of the investigation is prepared by the medical examiner in lieu of an inquisition, and copies of records are transmitted to the district attorney of the county. Sec. 1571 Greater New York Charter. The powers and duties of a medical examiner, however, are non-judicial. Matter of Senior v. Boyle, 221 NY 414.

In New York City, a city magistrate may perform the necessary functions if the medical examiner is absent or unable to discharge his duties. Sec. 789 Code. 

Records. The Commissioner of Health is required to pre scribe and prepare methods and forms for obtaining and preserving records and statistics of autopsies, and shall require those performing autopsies to record essential data. Sec. 778a Code.

Attendance of Interested Parties. It is the duty of the surgeon performing an autopsy to permit the attendance of a person, or his medical representative, likely to be the defendant in a civil or criminal action in which the autopsy may become pertinent, or the representative of the deceased person. Sec. 778a

SEARCH WARRANT 

In General. A search warrant is an order signed by a magistrate, commanding a peace officer to search for and produce personal property. Sec. 791 Code. Its purpose is to aid in the detection and punishment of crime.

It may be issued only upon probable cause, supported by affidavit describing the person, property and place to be searched. 

Sec. 793 Code; Sec. 8 Civil Rights Law; Art. 1, Sec. 12 NY 

Const.

The execution and return of the warrant to the issuing magistrate must be made within ten days after its date; within five days if issued in the city and county of New York. If not executed within the time mentioned, the warrant is void. Sec. 

802 Code.

Where the supporting affidavit states positively that the property is on the person or in the place to be searched, the magistrate may insert a direction that it be executed at any time of the day or night; otherwise, the magistrate must direct that the warrant be executed in the daytime. Sec. 801 Code. 

If the property mentioned be stolen or used as the means of committing a felony, it may be taken, on the warrant, from any place or person. If it be intended to use property as a means of committing a crime, it may be taken, on the warrant, from

the prospective criminal or anyone to whom the same was entrusted for purposes of concealment. Sec. 792 Code.

If the warrant or the grounds upon which the same was issued be controverted or attacked, the magistrate must hold a hearing thereon. Sec. 807 Code. Restoration must be directed if the probable cause noted in the supporting affidavit be not substantiated. Sec. 809 Code. If the warrant and the execution thereof be sustained, the magistrate must cause the same, together with the papers and property, to be transmitted to the court having general jurisdiction, for prosecution. Sec. 810 Code.

A search warrant may not be used to determine title to property, although stolen. When a controversy arises, the parties are relegated to their civil remedies. Peo. ex rel. Simpson v. 

Kempner, 208 NY 16.

If wrong property be taken, it must be restored to the person from whom it was taken. Sec. 809 Code. 

Search warrants provided in other branches of the law are not here considered. 

Unlawful Searches. Unreasonable searches and seizures are prohibited. Sec. 793 Code; Sec. 8 Civil Rights Law; Art. 1, Sec. 

12 NY Const. An unlawful search may be resisted. Secs. 1846, 1847 P.L. A person who procures a warrant maliciously and without probable cause, or a peace officer who exceeds his authority in executing the same, is guilty of a misdemeanor. 

Secs. 811, 812 Code; Sec. 1847 P.L.

Use-Disposition of Illegally Seized Property. The author ities are not in accord upon the question whether a court has inherent power, before trial, to order the return of property illegally seized. Some cases hold that the power exists and should be exercised. In re Both, 200 AD 423; Peo. v. Jakira, 118 M 303;

Peo. v. Manko, 189 NYS 357. Others are committed to the doctrine that papers or articles, fruits of a crime, are legitimate evidence against a defendant, without regard to the manner of their taking. Peo. v. Adams, 176 NY 351; aff'd 192 US 585; Peo. v. Strollo, 191 NY 42; Peo. v. Belsky, 177 M 125; Peo. v. Richter, 127 M 410.

39 It would appear that the power to restore, if it does exist, must be exercised before trial.

Where it is claimed that seized papers are unrelated to any crime, a motion to restore will be denied if the prosecutor shows that their retention until trial is necessary for the prosecution. 

Peo. v. Chiagles, 237 NY 193.

As an incident to a lawful arrest, a person or his property may be searched, without a warrant, for contraband or other evidence of crime. Though a search be unlawful, the evidence disclosed thereby is admissible against the accused. He is confined to a civil action for damages against the offender, or a prosecution for oppression against the offender, or the filing of a complaint for the purpose of disciplining the offending officer. Peo. v. Defore, 242 NY 13.

The federal rule forbids the use of evidence unlawfully seized. 

Byars v. US, 273 US 28. 

SUMMONS 

Nature-Issuance. A summons is an instrument signed by a magistrate commanding a person to appear at a designated time and place to answer a complaint. It must be based upon a deposition subscribed by the informant or prosecutor. The summons is used generally for minor offenses in lieu of a warrant of arrest. Secs. 148, 150 Code.

Service. It may be served by the complainant, a peace officer, or by any other person designated by the magistrate. Sec. 

150 Code.

Disobedience. Failure to appear in response thereto constitutes a contempt, which the magistrate is empowered to punish by a fine not exceeding twenty-five dollars. A warrant may also be issued for the arrest of the defaulting party. Sec. 

150 Code.

Corporations. A summons directed to a corporation must be returnable not less than ten days after issuance (675 Code) and

must be served at least five days before the return date, by delivering a copy to an officer and exhibiting the original. Sec. 

677 Code.

Where a corporation has been indicted, jurisdiction is acquired by the service of a summons upon it in the same manner provided in a civil action. The summons must be served at least four days before the return date. It is issued and signed by the clerk of the court or the district attorney. Sec. 681 Code.

WARRANT OF ARREST 

There are many types of warrants of arrest, depending on the issuing court and the status of a particular proceeding. 

Nature-Contents. A warrant of arrest is an order addressed to any peace officer commanding the arrest of a person. 

It is issued in the name of the people and is signed by a magistrate. Sec. 151 Code. It must be directed to and executed by a peace officer. Sec. 153 Code.

Generally, a peace officer is a policeman, constable or sheriff; also, many other persons. Sec. 154 Code.

A warrant of arrest will not be issued except upon a deposition setting forth facts tending to establish the commission of a crime. Sec. 149 Code. The warrant must specify the name of the defendant, or if it be unknown, the defendant may be designated by description or a fictitious name. The offense complained of must also be stated. Sec. 152 Code. 

Execution. A warrant issued by a judge of a court higher than an inferior court of jurisdiction may be directed generally to and executed by any peace officer in the state. Sec. 155 Code. 

A warrant issued by a magistrate may be directed generally to any peace officer of the county. If the defendant be located in another county, the warrant may be executed therein upon the indorsement of a magistrate of said county allowing the execution therein. If directed to a sheriff, it may be served in any county upon the written direction of the sheriff of the county of service indorsed upon the warrant. Sec. 156 Code. In New

When a defendant appears and pleads to a charge, he waives the objection that the warrant, which was served in another county, was not indorsed by a magistrate of said county before service was effected. Peo. v. Cuatt, 70 M 453.

Challenging by Habeas Corpus. A person arrested under a warrant issued by a magistrate charging him with a crime is not obliged to await an examination before the magistrate, but may at once resort to the writ of habeas corpus. The Supreme 

Court will examine the deposition upon which the warrant was based and unless there is evidence therein to sustain it, the warrant will be declared a nullity, thus entitling the defendant to his discharge. Peo. ex rel. Perkins v. Moss, 187 NY 410.

BENCH WARRANT 

The bench warrant is similar to the warrant of arrest. It is usually issued where a defendant defaults in appearance or has been indicted.

When a bench warrant has been directed by the court, the clerk, upon application of the district attorney, issues the same, directed to one or more counties. After an indictment has been found, the district attorney is empowered to issue a bench war. rant. Sec. 300 Code. A bench warrant may be served in any county, in the same manner as a warrant of arrest, except that when it is served in another county it need not be indorsed by a magistrate of said county. Sec. 304 Code.

Nature. The process by which the attendance of a witness is effected is called a subpoena. Sec. 607 Code.

A subpoena duces tecum is issued when the production of records or chattels is desired. Sec. 613 Code.

Reasonable expenses of witnesses on behalf of the defendant may be charged to the county, in the discretion of the court.

MATERIAL WITNESS

ARREST Definition-Manner. 

Arrest is the taking of a person into custody that he may be held to answer for a crime. An arrest is made by an actual restraint or by submission to the custody of the arrestor. Secs. 167, 171 Code; Peo. v. Marendi, 213 NY 600. 

Time. If a felony be charged, an arrest may be made at any time. If it be a misdemeanor, an arrest may not be made on 

Sunday, or at night, unless by direction of the magistrate en dorsed upon the warrant. Sec. 170 Code. 

Aid-Resistance. An arresting officer may call on a bystander for aid, the refusal of which constitutes a misdemeanor. 

If a citizen be killed or injured while aiding an arresting officer, or his property be damaged thereby, a cause of action is created against the municipality. Sec. 1848 P.L.

When an arrest is made for a misdemeanor not committed in the presence of the arresting officer, an officer attempting to search the person arrested may be resisted, and a defendant, under those circumstances, is not guilty of unlawfully resisting an officer. Peo. v. O'Connor, 257 NY 473.

Refusal to admit an arresting officer to premises after notice of authority and purpose, justifies breaking doors, windows, etc. 

Sec. 175 Code. 

By A Peace Officer. A peace officer may, without a warrant, arrest a person (1) for a crime committed or attempted in his presence, or (2) when the person arrested has in fact committed a felony, or (3) when a felony has in fact been committed and he has reasonable cause to believe that the person arrested has committed it. Sec. 177 Code.

An enactment making it the duty of the police to arrest all persons guilty of violating a law or ordinance "with or without a warrant" means with a warrant where a warrant is required by law and without a warrant where it is not required by law. 

Such an enactment does not authorize a policeman to arrest a person for a misdemeanor without a warrant unless committed in his presence. Greater N. Y. Athletic Club v. Wurster, 19 M 443.

By A Private Person. A private person may arrest another for a crime committed or attempted in his presence, or when the person arrested has actually committed a felony, although not in his presence. Sec. 18 Code; Peo. v. Defore, 242 NY 13.

Distinction Between Officer And Individual. 

There is, therefore, no distinction between a peace officer and a citizen in respect to the right to arrest for a misdemeanor. If committed in their presence, either has the right to arrest. Gold v. Armer, 140 AD. 73.

In a felony case, however, if a peace officer has reasonable cause to believe that the person arrested is the felon, he is shielded, whereas a citizen makes an arrest for a felony at his own peril if in fact he arrests the wrong person. Westbrook v. 

N. Y. Sun Assn., 32 M 37.

Illegal Arrest. Generally, it is no defense to a criminal prosecution that the defendant was illegally or forcibly brought within the jurisdiction of the court. Peo. v. Eberspacher, 79 Hun 410. 

An illegal arrest does not nullify a conviction, provided the court has jurisdiction of the offense. Civil remedies, however, are available against the arrestor. Peo. v. Iverson, 46 AD 301; 

Peo. v. Jeratino, 62 M 587. A public officer who unlawfully and maliciously, under pretense of official authority, arrests another, is also guilty of a misdemeanor. Secs. 854, 1846 P.L.

Arrest During Close Pursuit. Any peace officer of another state who enters New York in close pursuit of a person in order to arrest him, has the same right to arrest such person here as a local peace officer, provided that the crime in question is a crime in New York and provided further that such other state has reciprocal provisions. Sec. 860 Code. 

After Arrest. A defendant is entitled, upon arrest, to communicate with friends or relatives, free of charge. Secs. 81, 112 

NYCCCA. He must be taken before a magistrate without unnecessary delay. Sec. 165 Code. The failure to effect the same constitutes a misdemeanor (1844 P.L.) and also subjects the arrestor to liability for false imprisonment. Davis v. Carroll, 172

AD 729. If the court is not in session, the person arrested is to be placed under confinement until arraignment or fixation of bail. It is proper, however, to have the prisoner booked at the police precinct prior to arraignment.

BAIL Defined. The taking of bail is the acceptance, by a competent court or magistrate, of an undertaking for the appearance of a defendant according to the terms thereof, in default of which the bail will pay to the people of the state a specified sum. Sec. 

551 Code.

Excessive Bail.

Excessive bail is prohibited by the state constitution. Art. 1, Sec. 5, NY Const. 

While high bail is demed proper in important cases, the bail fixed in the Tweed case, arising out of an order of arrest, where fixation of bail in the sum of three million dollars was sustained, is unparalleled. Peo. v. Tweed, 63 NY 202.

Fingerprinting Prerequisite. No person charged with a felony or with any misdemeanor specified in section 552 of the Code, may be admitted to bail until his fingeprints shall have been taken to ascertain whether he has previously been convicted of crime. The police are required to ascertain his previous record forthwith. Sec. 552a Code. This requirement has been held unconstitutional by a city magistrate (Peo. v. Hevern, 127 

M 141), but the value of this decision as a precedent is doubtful. 

Jurisdiction To Admit To Bail. Generally, a magistrate has the right to admit a defendant to bail in case of a misdemeanor or a felony punishable with imprisonment for not more than five years. In more severe cases, bail may only be granted by the 

Supreme Court, County Court or Court of General Sessions (557 Code), more particularly discussed later in this chapter. 

In order to determine whether any of the lower courts have jurisdiction to admit a defendant to bail, not only is the nature of the crime important but the previous record of the defendant is likewise of paramount concern. 

A person arrested for a misdemeanor between 11 A.M. and 

8 A.M. the next morning may be admitted to bail by a police captain, lieutenant, sergeant or acting sergeant, by furnishing bail in the sum of $500. Sec. 554 Code.

In a case where a conviction would render a person liable to imprisonment for thirty days or less, the amount of the bail is two hundred dollars. In such an instance, the defendant may, however, be paroled until the next morning, in the discretion of the police official in charge. Sec. 554 Code. 

If the offense be the violation of a corporate ordinance, the amount of bail is one hundred dollars, and if a conviction calls

BAIL for a fine only, bail must be double the largest fine that could be imposed. Sec. 554 Code. 

When a child under the age of sixteen is arrested, a police official aforesaid may accept, in lieu of bail, the personal recognizance in writing of a parent, guardian or other lawful custodian of such child to produce the child in court on the following day, in which case no security is required. Failure to produce the child, however, constitutes a criminal contempt and subjects the child to immediate re-arrest. Sec. 554 Code. 

An employee of a public utility, arrested because of an accident arising out of the operation of a conveyance, must be taken immediately before a magistrate if one be available and if none be available before an official in charge of a police station, where he must be given an opportunity to furnish bail, which in misdemeanor cases may not exceed one thousand dollars. Such an employee may also be released on his own recognizance, conditioned upon his due appearance before a ma gistrate. Sec. 554a Code. 

A fugitive from justice from another state may be admitted to bail by a city magistrate as well as by judges of the higher courts. Sec. 845 Code; Sec. 72a NYCCCA. 

After a deposition and record has been transmitted to a higher court, a magistrate who held the defendant to answer may not admit him to bail. Sec. 559 Code.

Section 550 of the Code does not restrict the power of a Supreme Court justice to admit a prisoner to bail under section 

22 subdivision 8 thereof.

It is not necessary in the first instance to make application for bail before the court having jurisdiction; it is proper to sue out a writ of habeas corpus in the Supreme Court. Peo. ex rel. 

McManus v. Warden, 226 AD 364. This remedy may be used also to reduce bail. However, the fixation of bail should not be disturbed by another justice of concurrent jurisdiction. Peo. ex rel. Rothensies v. Searles, 229 AD 603.

Before Conviction. A defendant may not be admitted to bail in any of the cases hereinafter described, before or after indictment, except by a justice of the Supreme Court, a judge of the

Court of General Sessions where the charge is pending, or a judge of the County Court where the charge is pending. These cases are (552 Code): (a) A crime punishable with death. (b) The infliction of a probable fatal injury under circumstances that the crime is murder if death ensue. (c) If the defendant has been previously convicted of a felony or an attempt to commit a felony, or one of the misdemeanors described in subdivision "d" next succeeding, and is presently charged with a felony or any one of said misdemeanors. (d) If the defendant has previously been twice convicted of any crime such as illegally using, carrying or possessing a pistol or other dangerous weapon; making or possessing burglars' instruments; buying or receiving stolen property; unlawful entry; aiding escape from prison; disorderly conduct as defined in subd. 6 of sec 722 of the Penal Law; unlawfully possessing or distributing habit forming narcotic drugs; and is presently charged with a felony or any of the foregoing misdemeanors.

If the charge is for any crime other than that specified in section 552 of the Code, a defendant may be admitted to bail as a matter of right in misdemeanor cases and as a matter of discretion in all other cases. The revocation of bail thereafter is likewise discretionary. Sec. 553 Code.

After Conviction. If convicted of a crime not punishable with death or life imprisonment, a defendant who has appealed and has been granted a stay of proceedings, may be admitted to bail as a matter of right when the appeal is from a judgment imposing a fine only and as a matter of discretion in all other cases except the following (555 Code): 

(a) The defendant is a fourth offender.

BAIL (b) The defendant has been convicted of a felony committed while armed. (c) The defendant has been convicted of any of the crimes described in section 552 of the Code and is circumstanced as described in said section. 

Where a stay is granted pending the determination of an application for a certificate of reasonable doubt, bail may not be allowed until the application is granted. The stay referred to means a permanent stay pending appeal. Peo. ex rel. Hummel v. Reardon, 186 NY 164.

Notice Of Application. Two days' notice to the district at torney must be given on applications for admission to bail, unless a shorter time is fixed by the court. Sec. 560 Code. The district attorney may waive this notice and consent that the application be entertained forthwith, which courtesy is usually extended.

A statement is required setting forth whether any previous application was made in connection with any crime charged, and if so, the disposition thereof; also, information concerning prior convictions. Sec. 556a Code.

Sureties. A surety on a bail bond must be a resident of and a householder or freeholder within the state, and, unless otherwise directed by the magistrate, within the county. He must be worth the amount specified in the undertaking exclusive of property exempt from execution and over and above all liabilities.

However, the court may require or allow two sureties to justify, whose combined worth shall be equivalent to that of one sufficient surety. If real estate is offered as security, the assessed value less liens must be at least twice the amount of the under taking. Sec. 569 Code. 

Bail may be furnished by a surety or fidelity company. Sec. 

577a Code.

An attorney at law may not be bail in a civil or criminal case. 

Rule 27, Rules of Civil Practice.

the sureties, they may be examined under oath concerning their sufficiency and qualifications. Sec. 573 Code.

A surety who justifies falsely, or one who gives a fictitious undertaking, is guilty of contempt. Sec. 753 Judiciary Law; 

Dollard v. Koronsky, 67 M 90.

A surety who swears falsely that he owns property of a specified value is also guilty of perjury. Peo. v. Davis, 122 AD 569. 

Premium. The premium for giving a bail bond may not exceed 5% on the first $1,000, 4% on the second $1,000, and 3% thereafter, with a minimum fee of $10. A violation constitutes a misdemeanor, plus liability for treble damages in addition to the return of the overcharge. Sec. 554b Code. 

Deposit In Lieu Of Bail. Government bonds or cash may be deposited in lieu of bail. Bonds or cash so deposited on defendant's behalf by a third person will be refunded to said third person upon the termination of proceedings. Sec. 586 Code. 

However, money deposited in lieu of bail may be applied in pay. ment of any fine imposed upon the defendant although it was in fact furnished by a third person. Peo. ex rel. Gilbert v. Laidlaw, 102 NY 588.

Surrender Of Defendant. At any time before the forfeiture of the undertaking, a surety may surrender the defendant or the defendant may surrender himself. A certified copy of the undertaking should be submitted upon the surrender and a certificate of surrender obtained. Upon five days' notice to the district attorney, bail is thereupon exonerated. Sec. 590 Code. 

Forfeiture Of Undertaking Or Deposit. If, without sufficient cause, the defendant neglect to appear for arraignment, trial or judgment, or upon any other occasion when his presence in court may be lawfully required, the court must direct the fact to be entered upon its minutes; and the undertaking of his bail

BAIL or the money deposited in lieu of bail, as the case may be, is thereupon forfeited. Sec. 593 Code.

A formal order declaring a forfeiture is not necessary. A direction in writing by the court is sufficient. The failure of the clerk to record or enter it does not render it inoperative. Peo. v. Bennett, 137 NY 601.

Where the default of the defendant is noted in the minutes of the court, the recognizance becomes forfeited ipso facto. No formal order is necessary to fix liability of the sureties. However, it is necessary to obtain a formal order for the purpose of entering judgment.

The subsequent surrender of the principal does not of itself effect an exoneration of the sureties. Peo. v. Bennett, 136 NY 482. 

That no indictment was found is no defense. The discharge rests in the discretion of the court and the recognizance is effective until the court disposes of the pending charge. Champlain v. Peo., 2 NY 82.

It has been held to be a valid excuse for the non-appearance of the principal that he enlisted as a soldier in the interim. Peo. v. Cushney, 44 Barb. 118.

Arrest and commitment of the principal to jail elsewhere is a good defense to an action on a recognizance. Peo. v. Bartlett, 3 Hill 570. Inability of the principal to appear because of illness is also a valid excuse. Peo. v. Tubbs, 37 NY 586.

It is error arbitrarily to forfeit a recognizance in a misdemeanor case where a defendant appears by counsel. Peo. v. Miller, 63 AD 11.

A surety who has given an undertaking for the appearance of his principal to answer to an indictment at a given term of court, is responsible for his appearance, not only on the first day of the term but on any subsequent day thereafter, without notice. Peo. v. Parisi, 217 NY 24. 

Where compliance is burdensome, or non-compliance results from innocent inadvertence, mistake or ignorance, a district attorney will not usually take advantage of a default. In any event, a proper case for a remission might be presented.net

If the surety die before a bond is forfeited, a judgment on a forfeited recognizance may not be entered without an action against his personal representative. Peo. v. Bellando, 137 AD 

7.77; aff'd 199 NY 533.

A grand jury is a body of men, chosen by lot, and sworn to inquire of crimes committed or triable in a particular county.

DEMURRER THE INDICTMENT 

The indictment is a writing in which a grand jury charges a person with the commission of a crime. The grand jury is the accuser. The grand jury, as we have seen, is a body of not less than sixteen nor more than twenty-three persons. The presence of at least sixteen is necessary to constitute a quorum and the concurrence of at least twelve is required to order an indictment. Secs. 224, 254, 268 Code. 

IN GENERAL 

The purpose of an indictment is to apprise the accused of the charge in order to enable him to prepare his defense; also, to identify the charge so as to bar a subsequent prosecution for the same offense (double jeopardy). Peo. v. Helmer, 154 NY 

596; Peo. v. Williams, 243 NY 162. It serves as the agency by which the defendant is brought to the bar of justice. 

When an indictment is voted, the formal instrument itself is prepared by the district attorney. It is authenticated by the district attorney signing the same and by the grand jury foreman signing an endorsement thereon bearing the legend "a true bill." In the absence of such authentication, the indictment should be dismissed unless a quorum of the grand jury has appeared in open court and presented the indictment to the presiding judge. Secs. 268, 276 Code; Brotherton v. Peo., 75 NY 159;

Peo. v. Foster, 60 M 3. It has been held, however, that the mere absence of the district attorney's signature is not fatal, upon the

THE INDICTMENT 

63 theory that a mere clerical act is required on his part. Peo v. 

Rice, 56 NYS2d 833.

An indictment does not attain complete validity unless it is presented in open court by the foreman of the grand jury. The presentment merely consists of its delivery by the foreman to the court while it is in session, accompanied by an oral statement which in substance declares that the grand jury hands up the indictment or indictments. Sec. 272 Code.

Until the defendant has appeared or been apprehended, the indictment must not be shown to any person other than publi officer. Sec. 272 Code; Sec. 1782 P.L.. 

A defendant may be indicted under his true name, or under his various aliases, or a fictitious name may be used. Peo. v. 

Seidenshner, 210 NY 341. An appropriate amendment of the defendant's true name may be allowed at any stage of the proceed ings. Sec. 277 Code. 

Words used in an indictment are construed in their usual acceptation, except words defined by law, which are construed ac cording to their legal meaning. Sec. 282 Code. Numerous terms such as "corrupt" "malice" "negligence" etc., are defined and construed in the Penal Law (Sec. 3). 

It is presumed that an indictment was properly found and that it is based upon legal and sufficient evidence. Peo. v. Sweeney, 213 NY 37.

In New York, until the formal instrument which we call the "indictment" has been prepared and authenticated, the finding of a grand jury in favor of an indictment may be termed a "presentment." In re Osborne, 68 M 597. In some other jurisdictions the terms are interchangeable. The term "presentment” was deleted from the New York Constitution, as amended and in force January 1, 1940. Art. 1, Sec. 6. In its popular sense, the term "presentment" has reference to commendation or criticism noted by the grand jury.

An indictment is required in felony cases and may be utilized in misdemeanor cases as provided by law. In misdemeanor cases generally, tried in Special Sessions, an "information" discharges the same function as an indictment and is filed by the

district attorney without the intervention of a grand jury. 

However, the district attorney is not authorized to file an information unless the defendant has previously been held to answer by a magistrate.

No person may be held to answer for a capital or otherwise infamous crime except on indictment of a grand jury, except cases involving impeachments and military jurisdiction. Art. 1, 

Sec. 6, NY Const.

An indictment may be drawn in the usual common-law form or in the so-called "simplified" or "short-form" style. The type of form used rests in the discretion of the district attorney. 

SIMPLIFIED OR SHORT-FORM INDICTMENT 

Section 295-b of the Code provides that a simplified indictment must contain the title of the action (name of court and names of parties) and a statement of the specific crime with which the defendant is charged.

As the name implies, a simplified or short-form indictment is a bare statement of the crime charged, denuded of the factual allegations required in the common-law form. The following is the form of such indictment prescribed by Sec. 295-d of the 

Code: 

"Caption. 

The grand jury of by this indictment, accuse A. B. of the following crime: Burglary in first degree, contrary to penal law, sec. 402." 

Since an indictment is supposed to inform the accused of the nature of the charge and contain sufficient averments to enable him to prepare for trial, it is plain that the statutory form permitted is not beyond reproach. The deficiency is sought to be remedied by a provision that a bill of particulars is mandatory in such cases. Secs. 295-g, 295-h Code. It is difficult, however, to justify the short-form indictment in the light of fundamental principles of criminal jurisprudence. Moreover, the laws relating to double jeopardy are rendered more difficult, if not impossible to apply when such form is used.

THE INDICTMENT 

65 The legality of the simplified form has been tested and sustained. Peo. v. Bogdanoff, 254 NY 16; Peo. v. Farley, 252 AD 

811, aff'd 277 NY 617. We hesitate to say that the validity or propriety of this form has been established because close analysis of the Bogdanoff case leaves considerable doubt on the subject, and the Farley case (a memorandum decision in the Appellate Division) merely rested its decision on the Bogdanoff case without discussion of the problem.

In the Bogdanoff case, the defendant was convicted of murder. The short-form indictment was used, supplemented by a bill of particulars. The objection to the form of the indictment was not raised until the trial. The conviction was affirmed by a closely divided court (4-3). However, the prevailing opinion expressly stated that the decision was not to be regarded as a judicial approval of the form used. The affirmance apparently rested on the broad grounds that the defendant's guilt had been conclusively established and that the defendant was not in fact prejudiced or misled by the form used; also, that there was no doubt whatever of the nature of the crime and identity of the deceased.

In the Farley case, supra, the issue appears to have been timely and squarely presented by demurrer. In said case, it ap pears that a full bill of particulars was furnished.

On the other hand, the simplified form has been definitely condemned. Peo. v. Meehan, 142 M 605; Peo. v. Klein, 159 M 

465; the dissenting opinion in the Bogdanoff case. The reasons for the condemnation may be summarized thus: (a) It is impossible to ascertain from the indictment what ultimate facts the defendant is expected to meet, and this is particularly true where a series of transactions are involved. (b) The foregoing defect is clearly demonstrated where a single statute contains different crimes or degrees of crime. (c) If the question of double jeopardy is involved, it is im-

possible to determine the issue from an examination of the indictment. (d) In cases where the statute of limitations is involved, the deficiency is obvious. (e) It is within the power of a prosecutor to submit and rely upon factual circumstances not considered by the grand jury and wholly unrelated to the state of facts which prompted the grand jury to indict. In other words, it is possible to convict for an offense of which a defendant was not actually indicted. (f) The mandatory provision for a bill of particulars is inadequate because (1) it is furnished by the district attorney and may be inconsistent with the theory relied on by the grand jury in finding the indictment; and (3) the principles imbedded in our jurisprudence as to sufficient averments in an indictment, should not be cast aside. (g) Technical defects in common-law forms are curable by amendment. Former restrictions as to amendments have been substantially eliminated, or can be eliminated, leaving little or no opportunity today to escape punishment on technicalities which formerly barred prosecution.

It is worthy of note in passing that in a civil case a cause of action must contain sufficient allegations of facts, the absence of which justifies a dismissal of the complaint (except a few causes of action such as conversion and money had and received, which may still be pleaded in the conclusory form common-law counts). The prospective loss of life or liberty is therefore granted less consideration than the prospective loss of property.

AMENDMENT OF INDICTMENT 

The common-law rule that no amendment could be made to an indictment has been relaxed, and the power of the court considerably enlarged so that a miscarriage of justice by reason of some inconsequential defect might be averted.

Like the power residing in a trial court in a civil case, an amendment may be granted to conform the indictment to the proof, provided that the substantial rights of the defendant are not invaded. Secs. 93, 295-j Code; Peo. v. Geyer, 196 NY 364.

Illustrations: (a) While an amendment in respect of the description of the offense is proper, the identity of the crime may not be changed by amendment. Peo. v. Grout, 91 M 451. (b) Unless time is a necessary ingredient of an offense, an amendment respecting the date of the commission of the crime is proper. Peo. v. Lewis, 132 AD 256. (c) Where, however, the indictment antedates the date of the alleged commission of the crime, the error is one of substance and cannot be cured by amendment. Peo. v. Van Every, 225 NY 74. (d) It is fatal error, despite the defendant's consent, to amend

an indictment to include a count relating to an independent crime. Such amendment violates the provisions requiring an indictment by a grand jury in order to convict for a felony. Peo. v. Miles, 289 NY 360.

JOINDER OF CRIMES-COUNTS CONSOLIDATION 

It is permissible in certain instances to join different crimes in one indictment, viz.: Where the crimes arise out of the same transaction, or form a part of a common scheme or plan, or involve offenses of a similar character, thus making it exceedingly difficult for professional criminals to escape successful prosecution. If more than one indictment is found in said instances, the indictments may be consolidated. It is discretionary with the court to direct separate trials if the charges are unconnected or do not form part of a common scheme or plan. Sec. 279 Code; 

Peo. ex rel. Pincus v. Adams, 274 NY 447. It is not improper to set forth in separate counts two or more offenses of the same nature based upon the same or a continuous set of facts, either of which makes the accused guilty of the same crime. Peo. v. Adler, 140 NY 331.

Where the doing of any one of several things constitutes an indictable offense, an indictment may in a single count group them together and charge the accused with having committed them all, and a conviction may be had on proof of the commission of any one of the acts charged without proof as to the others. Bork v. Peo., 91 NY 5.

One good count in an indictment will sustain a conviction. 

Peo. v. Davis, 56 NY 95.

Nature And Kinds. There are three possible pleas to an indictment, to wit, guilty, or not guilty, or a former judgment of acquittal or conviction. The last named plea may be pleaded with or without the plea of not guilty. Sec. 332 Code.

No plea of guilty may be entered to a crime which is or may be punishable by death. Sec. 332 Code. 

The plea of guilty does not waive a jurisdictional defect. If no crime be charged, objection thereto can be raised by motion in arrest of judgment or urged for the first time on appeal.

The custom of pleading guilty to one indictment upon condition that others, specified at the time, be dismissed, is entitled to court approval. Peo. v. Siciliano, 185 M 149. 

Plea Of Not Guilty. Counsel for an individual defendant may announce a plea of not guilty in any case.

For example, the objections that the indictment was not found, indorsed and presented as prescribed in the Code and that an unauthorized person was present during the session of the grand jury, are waived unless promptly raised. Secs. 313-315 Code.

In its discretion, however, the court apparently has the right to permit the withdrawal of a plea of not guilty and to entertain a motion to dismiss an indictment on the ground that a person was unlawfully present before the grand jury, where it is shown that the application is made in good faith, without vexatious delay, and that the prosecution has lost no rights. Peo. v. Fuller, 156 M 404.

The following are instances where a motion to set aside or dismiss an indictment is justified. Said motion may be made at any time prior to or at the trial, unless otherwise indicated: (a) Where the indictment fails to allege facts sufficient to constitute the crime charged, or shows that the court does not have jurisdiction of the subject matter. These two objections are properly raised before trial only by demurrer, as hereinafter discussed, but since said objections are not waived by failure to raise same by demurer, they may be urged at the trial by way of a motion to dismiss the indictment. Sec. 331 Code. Obviously, said objections cannot be made where simplified indictments are used and their use sanctioned. (b) The two fundamental objections aforesaid, to wit, le-

gal insufficiency or lack of jurisdiction of the subject matter, may also be urged by a motion in arrest of judgment, which application is made after conviction but before or at the time of judgment. Secs. 331, 467 

Code; Peo. v. Meakim, 133 NY 214 (c) Where the indictment was found upon insufficient legal evidence (which motion is made following the granting of an inspection of the grand jury minutes and within the time prescribed by the rules of the court). (d) Where the bill of particulars shows that the facts alleged do not constitute the crime charged. (e) Where it appears that a conviction is highly improbable, or that it is clear that the offense charged constitutes no crime, the court is warranted in dismissing an indictment "in furtherance of justice." In such cases the reasons for the dismissal must be recorded. Sec. 

671 Code; Peo. v. Broderick, 146 M 566; Peo. v. Edmondson, 168 M 142. This remedy is a substitute for the nolle prosequi under the old practice. The dismissal in such an instance bars another prosecution for the same offense in a misdemeanor case but not in case of a felony. Sec. 673 Code. (f) Where there is a lack of prosecution. Technically, an indictment must be tried during or before the term following the term when the same was found unless cause to the contrary be shown, Sec. 668 Code. In practice, however, wide latitude is accorded to the prosecutor in fixing the time of trial. (g) Where the indictment was found on a resubmission without an order of the court allowing the same. Secs. 

327, 328 Code; Peo. v. Dillon, 197 NY 254. (h) Where the indictment has been superseded by another indictment for the same crime charged. Sec. 292a Code.

THE INDICTMENT 

75 (i) Where the defendant has been granted immunity by law from prosecution for the crime charged. (j) Where the defendant has been pardoned for the crime charged.

As to the defense of the statute of limitations, a defendant may not obtain a dismissal of an indictment by motion before trial on the ground that the prosecution is so barred. Said defense may be raised only upon the trial. Peo. v. Austin, 63 AD 

382, aff'd 170 NY 585; Peo. v. Bailey, 103 M 366. The reasons for this restriction are that an indictment is presumed to be timely (Peo. v. Blake, 121 AD 613) and the prosecutor may be able to establish upon the trial that the defense is not available to the defendant. Peo. v. Brown, 238 AD 155; Peo v. Reiser, 240 

AD 36; Peo. v. Steiger, 154 M 538

A motion to dismiss, made prior to trial, should be based on a notice of motion if the defect appears on the face of the indictment, or by affidavit and notice of motion or order to show cause in other instances. No special form of notice of motion is required except that it should specify the objections distinctly. 

The rules of the court where the indictment is pending should always be consulted for practice requirements.

DEMURRER TO INDICTMENT 

Prior to trial, the objections (a) that the facts alleged in an indictment do not constitute a crime charged, or (b) that the court has no jurisdiction of the subject matter, may be raised by demurrer only. Sec. 323 Code; Peo. v. Kahn, 155 AD 821. 

Since a failure to raise said objections prior to trial does not constitute a waiver, the same may be urged at the trial in the form of a motion to dismiss, or by timely motion in arrest of judgment. Sec. 331 Code; Peo. v. Grutz, 212 NY 72.

The usual practice is to require the defendant to plead to the indictment and allow time to withdraw the plea and substitute a demurrer. Matter of DeWitt v. McNamee, 232 AD 88; Peo. v. 

Dillard, 252 AD 125. The quickest method, therefore, of testing the sufficiency of an indictment is by way of demurrer filed

either at the time of pleading or within the time granted therefor at the time of pleading.

Formerly, a demurrer could not be sustained unless the entire indictment was defective. In other words, it was enough that one good count was found in the indictment. Peo. v. Rosenheimer, 209 NY 115, 118; Peo. v. Kupferman, 175 M 650. It seemed, therefore, that insufficient counts in an indictment which contained at least one good count could not be dismissed until the trial. The weakness of this former rule was the subject of a dictum advocating a change (Peo. v. Hines, 168 M 453) and criticism generally. By a recent amendment to the Code (Sec. 

323) a demurrer may now be addressed to any count of an indictment.

Other objections to an indictment which may be raised by demurrer only, are (a) that the grand jury had no legal authority to inquire into the crime charged (Peo. v. Willett, 213 NY 368); (b) that the indictment does not conform substantially to the requirements of Secs. 275 and 276 of the Code; (c) that more than one crime is charged in the indictment and that the same is not within the purview of Sec. 279 of the Code; and (d) that the indictment contains matter which is a legal bar to the pro secution. Sec. 323 Code. 

A demurrer must be in writing, signed either by the defendant or his counsel, and filed. It must distinctly specify the grounds of objection to the indictment; else it may be disregarded. Sec. 324 Code. Objections in language substantially equivalent to the provisions of Sec. 323 of the Code is proper. 

Peo. v. McCormack, 68 M 430.

The following is a form of demurrer: 

"Caption.

John Smith, the defendant abovenamed, demurs to the indictment presented herein by the grand jury, charging him with the crime of burglary in the first degree, upon the following grounds: 

1. This court has no jurisdiction of the crime charge since the same was allegedly committed in county.

THE INDICTMENT 

77 2. The facts alleged in said indictment do not constitute the crime charged. 

Wherefore, the defendant prays that the indictment be dismissed and that he be discharged. Dated, Yours, etc.,

Upon the filing of a demurrer, the objections are heard at such time as the court may appoint. Sec. 325 Code. The court must give judgment upon the demurrer, either allowing or disallowing it, and enter an order thereon. Sec. 326 Code. 

The allowance of a demurrer bars another prosecution for the same offense unless a resubmission is directed or authorized. Sec. 327 Code. The order of resubmission must, however, be timely. Peo. ex rel. Grossman v. Warden, 172 M 183. If resubmission is not ordered or authorized, the defendant must be discharged. Sec. 328 Code. If the demurrer be disallowed, the defendant must be permitted to enter a plea to the indictment. Sec. 

330 Code.

Chapter  SEPARATE TRIAL 

Defendants jointly indicted may be tried separately or jointly, in the discretion of the court. Sec. 391 Code.

The district attorney apparently always had the absolute right to try separately, defendants jointly indicted. Peo. v. 

Clark, 102 NY 735.

It will be noted that the right to a separate trial is not a matter of right but of discretion. In determining an application for a separate trial, consideration must be given to the due administration of justice and the right of the accused to a fair trial. 

Peo. v. Fisher, 249 NY 419; Peo. v. Feolo, 282 NY 276.

The fact that each defendant has made a confession is not necessarily sufficient to warrant a separate trial. Peo. v. Doran, 246 NY 409. Furthermore, that a confession would be admissible against one defendant but not against another, is not decisive. Proper instructions by the court will protect the second defendant. Peo. v. Snyder, 246 NY 491.

Statements of a defendant, inadmissible against a co-defendant, are not of themselves sufficient to entitle the latter to a separate trial. The jury can be instructed in such manner that his rights may be amply protected. Peo. v. Schemnitzer, 142 M 16.

Unless an abuse of discretion be shown, an appellate court will not disturb a ruling denying such an application. Peo. v. 

Doran, 246 NY 409; Peo. v. Bogdanoff, 254 NY 16.

CHANGE OF VENUE 

An application for a change of venue is addressed to the discretion of the court. Peo. v. Hines, 168 M 453.

When Granted. A crime prosecuted by indictment may be removed, on application of the defendant: (a) For good cause shown, from a County Court to the Supreme Court of the same county, or (b) From the Supreme Court or a County Court to the Supreme Court in another county, upon the ground that a fair and impartial trial cannot be had in the county where the indictment is pending. Sec. 344 

Code; Peo. v. McLaughlin, 150 NY 365.

To warrant a removal, it must be demonstrated that the defendant cannot receive a fair trial in the pending jurisdiction. 

Peo. v. Georger, 109 AD 111. The existence of bias, passion or religious prejudice, from which it appears that an impartial trial cannot be had, entitles a defendant to a change of venue. 

Peo. v. Ryan, 123 M 450. Constant denunciation by newspapers and extreme public feeling adverse to a defendant, present a proper case for the removal to another county. Peo. v. Jackson, 114 AD 697.

The district attorney has the right to remove a case to the Supreme Court of the same county, without notice to the defendant. This power has always resided in him and has not been curtailed by statute. Peo. v. Farini, 239 NY 411. However, removal to another county may not be had upon application of the district attorney. Murphy v. Extraordinary S. & T. Term, 294 

NY 440.

When Application Made. The application must be made before trial and before a juror is sworn. Secs. 344, 353 Code. If a new trial be granted, such application may be made before the second trial. Sec. 345 Code. 

To Whom Application Made. 

The application must be made to and returnable at the next term of the Special Term branch of the Supreme Court of the judicial district where the cause is pending, upon five days' notice to the district attorney. Sec. 346 

Code; Peo. v. Green, 201 NY 172.

Stay Pending Application. Upon good cause shown, after reasonable notice to the district attorney, a justice of the Supreme Court of the original county may stay the trial for thirty days in order to enable a defendant to make the application. Sec. 

347 Code; Peo. v. McLaughlin, 150 NY 365. An application for such stay, once denied, may not renewed before another justice. 

Sec. 349 Code. 

Removal. If the defendant be in custody, the order granting the removal must provide for the removal of the prisoner to the proper county. Sec. 352 Code.

The pleading and papers are transferred upon filing a certified copy of the order of removal with the clerk of the original court. Sec. 351 Code.

Removal To Supreme Court Generally. Since the Supreme 

Court has general jurisdiction of criminal cases, it may assume jurisdiction of most cases commenced in other courts and may refer cases to other courts. Sec. 22 Code.

Reversal Of Order Denying Application. The reversal of an order denying a motion for a change of venue entitles a defendant to a reversal of the ensuing conviction. Peo. v. McLaughlin, 150 NY 365.

BILL OF PARTICULARS 

Under Simplified Or Short-Form Indictments. Upon the arraignment of the defendant, or at any later stage of proceedings, the court must, at the request of the defendant, direct the district attorney to file a bill of particulars of the crime charged. Sec. 295-g Code.

The bill of particulars, signed by the district attorney, must contain the following (Sec. 295-h Code): 

(a) The title of the action, specifying the name of the court to which the indictment is presented, and the names of the parties. (b) A statement in ordinary language, without stating

items of evidence or necessarily setting forth all the elements of the crime, of such particulars as may be necessary to give the defendant and the court reasonable information as to the nature and character of the crime charged.

It should be noted that this compulsory rule applies only where a simplified, short-form indictment has been presented. 

Peo. v. Bogdanoff, 254 NY 16.

Under Common-Law Indictments. In cases where indictments have been found other than in the form prescribed by the Code relating to simplified indictments, a bill of particulars is appropriate where justice demands that a party should be apprised of the charges with greater particularity. Peo. v. Stedeker, 175 NY 57. The granting of the application is discretionary. 

Peo. v. Corbalis, 178 NY 516; Peo. v. Rubin, 170 M 969.

Where there is doubt as to some particular of the offense charged, the proper remedy is a motion for a bill of particulars. 

Peo. v. Schubert, 140 M 689. 

That there was no preliminary hearing before a magistrate may be sufficient ground for an order directing the service of a bill of particulars. Peo. v. Florence, 146 M 735.

A bill of particulars may be directed at any stage of the proceedings in any case. Peo. v. Rosen, 251 AD 584.

Amendment. A bill of particulars may be amended upon the trial provided that the defendant be not prejudiced thereby. 

Sec. 295-j Code.

A note of the order granting the amendment and of the amendment itself should be endorsed on the bill of particulars.

Sec. 295-k Code. 

Actual Practice. The courts in criminal cases direct bills of particulars with much less frequency and latitude than in civil cases.

DEFENSE OF ALIBI 

Upon demand of the district attorney, served and filed not less than eight days before the trial, a defendant is obliged to serve and file a bill of particulars to support his defense of an alibi, failing in which such testimony may be excluded, or, if such testimony be allowed, the court must grant an adjournment to the district attorney not to exceed three days in order to meet the issue. Sec. 295-1 Code; Peo. v. Schade, 161 M 212.

The exclusion has been held to apply only to testimony of a witness other than the defendant. Peo. v. Rakiec, 260 AD 452. 

INSPECTION OF GRAND JURY MINUTES 

The original stenographic notes and typewritten minutes of the grand jury are kept in the custody of the district attorney, except when impounded by the court. Sec. 952-t Code. 

The purpose of an inspection of grand jury minutes is to enable the defendant to move to set aside the indictment. Matter of Montgomery, 126 AD 72. The application may be made at any time before trial in the ordinary case.

The application for such inspection, made in the form of a motion, is addressed to the discretion of the court. It should not be granted unless the court is satisfied that legal grounds exist to warrant a dismissal of the indictment. Peo. v. Sweeney, 213 

NY 37.

Applications of this kind are seldom granted, and only when the court is satisfied that the defendant has shown a probability of success in dismissing the indictment were the inspection allowed. Matter of Montgomery, 126 AD 72.

The moving papers must contain more than hearsay statements and opinions in order to justify an inspection. Peo. v. Mc- 

Cann, 166 M 269.

In making the application, it is proper to direct the attention of the court to testimony given on prior occasions by the witnesses who undoubtedly appeared before the grand jury. Peo. v. 

Wood, 93 M 701. 

To show what testimony, allegedly insufficient, was probably

adduced before the grand jury, reference may be made to testimony given before a magistrate or other examining body, conversations with or statements of witnesses, and other ascertainable facts.

That there was no preliminary examination before a magistrate does not of itself entitle a defendant to an inspection. Peo. v. Guenther, 65 M 150.

Where a disclosure may tend to defeat the ends of justice, the application should be denied (Peo. v. Fullington, 154 M 375), or the inspection made by the court itself. Peo. v. Bob, 233 AD 94. 

An inspection may sometimes be allowed in order to prepare for trial (Peo. v. Kresel, 142 M 88), but such instances will be rare.

Public officials are allowed an inspection in a proper case. 

Matter of Attorney General, 160 M 533.

An inspection will not be granted unless an indictment is pending; an information is insufficient. Peo. v. May, 158 M 488.

Occasionally, where doubt exists concerning the right to an inspection, the court itself will read the grand jury minutes in order better to determine whether the inspection should be allowed.

If the application be granted, the district attorney is directed to furnish the defendant with a copy of the minutes. 

NAMES OF WITNESSES 

The former provision which required that the names of witnesses who testified before the grand jury should be indorsed on the indictment, has been repealed. However, it is still discretionary with the court to direct such disclosure in a proper case. Peo. v. Nationwide News Service, 172 M. 752; Peo. v. Dally, 174 M 830.

CONDITIONAL EXAMINATION OF WITNESSES 

When a defendant has been held to answer, he may, either before or after the indictment, make application to have witnesses examined in his behalf. Sec. 620 Code.

Such an application is proper when it appears that a material witness is about to leave the state, or is so sick or infirm as to afford reasonable grounds for apprehending that he will be unable to attend the trial. Sec. 621 Code.

The Code makes no provision for such an examination on behalf of the people. Since matters of procedure must usually be based on some specific provision of the Code, we assume that such a remedy is not available to the people. It seems that the people may not cross-examine witnesses on conditional examininations. 

Application-To Whom Made. Such an application is made ex parte to the court during the term (Sec. 623 Code) and if not made during the term, the same may be made as follows 

(Sec. 624 Code): 

(a) To a justice of the Supreme Court or to a county judge when the indictment is pending in the Supreme Court or County Court outside of New York County. (b) To a justice of the Supreme Court or judge of the Court of General Sessions when the indictment is pending in the Court of General Sessions. 

How Made. The application must be based upon an affidavit showing: (a) The nature of the crime. (b) The state proceedings. (c) The name and address of the witness and that his testimony is material to the defense. (d) That the witness is about to leave the state, or is so sick or infirm as to create reasonable apprehensior that he will not be available for the trial. Sec. 622 Code. 

Order Granting Application. If the court be satisfied that the examination is necessary to the attainment of justice, an or-

der must be made directing the examination to be held at a specified time and place, and that a copy of the affidavit and order be served upon the district attorney within a specified time before the date fixed. Sec. 526 Code; Peo. v. Guidici, 100 NY 503. 

If the order be made by the court, the examination may be directed to proceed before a judge thereof or before a designated magistrate. If the order be made by a judge it must direct the examination to proceed before him. Sec. 626 Code. 

The attendance of witnesses may be enforced by subpoena. 

Sec. 634 Code. 

Opposing Examination. If the district attorney satisfy the court by affidavit or other proof, before or at the time of the examination, that proper grounds for the order do not exist, the examination may be vacated. Sec. 628 Code. If there be no opposition, the examination must proceed upon furnishing proof of service of a copy of the order and affidavit upon the district attorney. Sec. 627 Code. 

Authentication And Filing. The testimony must be reduced to writing and authenticated in the same manner as the testimony of a witness taken in support of an information. Sec. 629 

Code. It must be filed in the office of the clerk of the court without unnecessary delay. Sec. 630 Code.

Exclusion-Suppression. If it appear that service was not made upon the district attorney, or that the examination was not properly conducted, the deposition must be excluded or sup pressed. Sec. 632 Code. 

The loss of exhibits which form part thereof, requires that the deposition be suppressed. Peo. v. Sugarman, 248 NY 255. 

Use Upon Trial. The deposition, if not suppressed, may be read in evidence upon the trial by either party upon a showing that the witness is unable to attend by reason of his death, insanity, sickness or infirmity, or his continued absence from the state. Sec. 631 Code; Peo. v. Bruno, 220 NY 702.

Objections to questions may be urged upon the trial the same

as if the witness were examined personally thereat. Sec.

633 

Code. 

EXAMINATION OF WITNESSES ON COMMISSION 

After the indictment, the defendant may, in a proper case, have a material witness residing out of the state examined in his behalf. Sec. 636 Code. 

Such an examination is held on a commission issued by the court. Sec. 638 Code. Since the Code does not prescribe the manner of selecting the commissioner, the procedure in civil cases is adopted. Sec. 301 CPA.

A commission is a process directed to a person, authorizing him to examine under oath the witnesses described therein and to make a return to the court as directed. The commissioner propounds to the witnesses the interrogatories annexed to the commission. Sec. 638 Code. 

As distinguished from conditional examinations, the people are permitted to join in a commission and also to examine other witnesses. Sec. 634 Code.

Application. An application for an examination of a nonresident must recite information containing the nature of the crime charged, the state of the proceedings, the name of the proposed witness, that the testimony of such witness is material and that the proposed witness is a non-resident. Sec. 639 Code. 

The application is made to the court, if made during the term, or, if not made during the term, it may be made to the same judges who entertain applications for conditional examinations. 

Secs. 640, 641 Code.

Notice Of Application. If the application be made to the court regularly in session, notice thereof need not be given to the district attorney unless so directed by the court. If the application be made otherwise, five days' notice to the district at torney is necessary. Sec. 642 Code. 

When Order Granted.

If the court be satisfied that the pro-

posed witness resides out of the state, and that his examination is necessary to the attainment of justice, an order must be made directing that a commission issue and that the people be permitted to join therein with the privilege also to examine thereunder witnesses in support of the indictment. Sec. 643 Code. 

It should be noted that first an order is entered directing that a commission issue, after which a commission is prepared under the seal of the court and signed by the clerk. Sec. 638 Code.

Stay Of Trial. The order granting the application must di rect that the trial be stayed for a specified time, reasonably sufficient to enable the execution and return of the deposition. Sec. 

644 Code.

Practice. After an order is entered granting an application for a commission, interrogatories are served by the respective parties and the same are settled before the court or judge upon two days' notice. Sec. 645 Code. Cross interrogatories may also be served and settled in the same manner. Sec. 646 Code. 

Pertinent questions only may be inserted in the list of interrogatories. Sec. 647 Code. Questions not pertinent to the issues are expunged upon the settlement of the interrogatories, or modified so as to conform with the rules of evidence. Sec. 648 Code. 

When the interrogatories are settled, the court or judge must indorse upon them his allowance thereof. Sec. 648 Code.

The manner of settling interrogatories may be changed by stipulation.

A copy of Sec. 650 of the Code must be annexed to the com mission. Sec. 651 Code. 

Manner Of Taking Deposition. Depositions are taken substantially in the following manner (Sec. 650 Code): (a) The commissioner administers an oath to the witness. (b) The interrogatories are propounded to the witness by the commissioner and the answers reduced to writing. (c) Exhibits are subscribed by the witness and certified by the commissioner.

(d) The witness subscribes his name to the deposition before the commissioner. (e) The commissioner subscribes his name to each sheet and also subscribes to a recital of the taking of the deposition as directed. (f) The commission, deposition, exhibits and any other documents forming part thereof, are sealed and returned by the commissioner as directed in the commis sion.

After Return. When the commission has been returned either to the judge or clerk, as provided in the commission or as stipulated by the parties, the same must be opened and filed unless otherwise directed. Secs. 654, 655 Code.

The deposition is open to inspection by the parties, and the clerk must furnish a copy of the deposition or any part thereof, on demand, on payment therefor at the rate of five cents for every hundred words. Sec. 656 Code.

Use On Trial. The deposition may be read in evidence upon the trial by either party, subject to objections which may be urged in the same manner as if the witness were being examined in open court. Sec. 657 Code.

STATUTE OF LIMITATIONS 

A prosecution for murder or kidnapping may be commenced at any time; there is no limitation of time. Secs. 141, 141a Code.

Unless otherwise provided, a prosecution for any other felony or for the crime of conspiracy to commit a felony, must be commenced within five years after commission of the offense. Sec. 

142 Code. A prosecution for a misdemeanor other than a conspiracy to commit a felony must be commenced within two years after its commission. Sec. 142 Code.

A special rule applies in cases of larcenies committed by fiduciaries. The statute of limitation in such cases does not begin to run until the facts constituting the crime have been, or in the exercise of reasonable diligence should have been discovered. In

the event of the death of the person injured or defrauded before the period within which the prosecution must be commenced, the date of such death starts the running of time. Sec. 142 Code.

A prosecution is commenced, within the meaning of the foregoing provisions, when an indictment is duly presented and filed or when an information is laid before a magistrate and a warrant of arrest is issued thereon. Sec. 144 Code. 

If any indictment be set aside or dismised, or a demurrer sustained with a direction for a resubmission, the time during which the prosecution was pending is not counted, provided that a new prosecution for the same offence is commenced within sixty days after the order is entered. Sec. 144a Code; Peo. v. 

Portman, 139 M 544.

Time is not computed while a defendant is without the state, or remains within the state under a false name, or conceals himself. Sec. 143 Code.

While it has been held that an indictment is presumed to be timely and that the defendant is obliged to show that he was available (Peo. v. Blake, 121 AD 613), respected authority also holds that it is necessary to prove that a crime was committed within the statutory period or to show why the defense is not available. Peo. v. Brown, 238 AD 155; Peo. v. Reiser, 240 AD 

36; Peo. v. Steiger, 154 M 538.

Such a defense is embraced by a plea of not guilty, as we have seen, and may be raised only upon the trial. Peo. v. Austin, 63 

AD 382, aff'd 170 NY 585; Peo. v. Bailey, 103 M 366. A motion to dismiss by reason of this defense has been definitely condemned if made in advance of trial. Peo. v. Rutman, 169 M 354.

DISMISSAL FOR LACK OF PROSECUTION 

Unless good cause to the contrary be shown, a person held to answer for a crime is entitled to a dismissal of the charge if an indictment be not found against him at the next term of the court. Sec. 667 Code. 

After indictment, if a defendant be not brought to trial at the next term, the indictment may be dismissed, unless the de-

fendant is responsible for the delay or good cause be shown for the continuation of proceedings. Sec. 668 Code. 

The court may, under the foregoing rules, dismiss proceedings and discharge the defendant (Sec. 670 Code), or discharge the defendant from custody on his own recognizance, or admit the defendant to bail and continue proceedings. Sec. 669 Code.

It is discretionary with the court to dismiss an indictment of its own motion and also upon application of the district attorney. In such a case, however, a written statement of the reasons therefor must be filed. 671 Code.

A dismissal for the reasons above noted bars another prosecu tion for the same offense if it be a misdemeanor, but it is not a bar if the offense charged be a felony. 673 Code. 

INSANITY 

A person may not be tried, sentenced or punished for a crime while he is in a state of idiocy, imbecility, lunacy or insanity which renders him incapable of understanding the proceedings. 

Sec. 1120 P.L. Upon the question of insanity as a defense upon the trial, see p. 112 infra.

INSANITY PROCEEDINGS AFTER INDICTMENT 

At any time before final judgment, if it shall appear that a defendant under indictment for a felony or misdemeanor is in such state of insanity, idiocy or imbecility that he is unable to make his defense, or is incapable of understanding the charge, indictment or proceedings, or pleads insanity, the court may, either of its own motion or on application of the defendant or prosecution, order the defendant to be examined to determine the question of his sanity. Sec. 658 Code. The inquiry is not mandatory but is discretionary with the court. Peo. v. McElvaine, 125 NY 596.

It is not uncommon, in ordering a lunacy commission, for the court to direct the commissioners to report also their findings as to the state of mind of the defendant at the time of the commission of the crime

Outside Of New York City. The examination of a defendant outside of New York City is to be made by two qualified psychiatrists selected, upon request of the court, by the superintendent or person in charge of a state hospital having adequate facilities therefor. The superintendent is qualified to act as one of the psychiatrists. If no such qualified persons are on the staff, any qualified psychiatrist in the state may be designated. Sec. 

659 Code. 

In New York City. The examination in New York City is to be made by two qualified psychiatrists designated, upon request of the court, by the head of the psychiatry division of the department of hospitals, of whom the head may be one. The corporation counsel is obliged to delegate an assistant counsel to aid the psychiatrists. Sec. 659 Code.

Place Of Examination. The examination may be made where the defendant is confined, or the defendant may be committed, upon recommendation of the director or superintendent aforesaid, to a hospital for observation and examination. In the latter event, the sheriff is required to guard the defendant if outside New York City and the department of correction is required to discharge such function in New York City. Sec. 660 Code. 

Procedure. Before entering upon their duties, the psychiatrists must take an oath such as is prescribed for referees, which oath is to be annexed to the report.

The psychiatrists may examine witnesses and such other data as may aid them in reaching a determination. They may compel the attendance of witnesses and relevant records by means of subpoenae issued and served by the clerk of the court. Sec. 661 Code.

Report. Upon completion of the inquiry, the director or superintendent aforesaid must transmit a report to the court in quadruplicate, including the findings of the psychiatrists who conducted the investigation, regarding the issues of insanity, idiocy or imbecility. If the report recommends commitment, the

name of the suggested institution is to be noted in the report.

Sec. 662 Code. 

Division Of Opinion. If there be no unanimity, the court may order the director or superintendent, as the case may be, to appoint a third psychiatrist to submit a report. Sec. 662 Code.

Not Binding On Court. The report of the psychiatrists is advisory only and the court is not concluded thereby. 

Disposition Of Reports. The reports are not admissible upon the trial. The reports are filed in the office of the clerk of the court, where they are subject to inspection only on court order. 

One copy is transmitted by the clerk to the head of the institution where the defendant is confined. Sec. 662 Code. Copies are also served upon the district attorney and defense counsel, who are granted the opportunity of controverting the reports. Sec. 

662a Code. 

Finding Of Insanity. If two psychiatrists certify to the incapacity of the defendant, and the court, after allowing an opportunity to both sides to be heard thereon, concurs in said finding, the trial is suspended. The court thereupon directs the defendant's commitment in an appropriate institution until his recovery. Upon recovery, the defendant is returned to his original place of confinement and proceedings against him are re sumed. Sec. 662b Code. 

It has been held that a finding that the defendant was insane at the time of the commission of the crime but sane at the time of the report, does not abate the indictment; that it was for the jury to determine whether the defendant was insane at the time of the commission of the crime. Peo. v. Whitman, 149 M 159. 

The commitment of a defendant to an institution effects an exoneration of his bail and likewise the refund of any deposit therefor. Sec. 662d Code.

In General. The trial of a criminal action is entitled to preference generally over civil suits. Sec. 7a Code. 

The former rule that a crime prosecuted by indictment required a trial by a jury of twelve, which right could not be waived (Cancemi v. Peo., 18 NY 128) except by a plea of guilty (Pierson v. Peo., 79 NY 424), has been altered to the extent that a defendant may now waive a jury trial except where the crime charged may be punishable by death. Such waiver is effected by the defendant signing a written instrument in open court before a justice having jurisdiction to try the case. Art. 1, Sec. 2, NY Const. 

In a felony case, a defendant must be prosecuted by indictment and it is immaterial that the charge is erroneously designated as a misdemeanor. Peo. v. Bellinger, 269 NY 265. 

A defendant is entitled to a speedy and public trial. Art. 1,

Sec. 6 NY Const.; Sec. 8 Code; Sec. 12 Civil Rights Law. After his plea to an indictment, a defendant is entitled to at least two days to prepare for trial. Sec. 357 Code.

A trial may not be held on Sunday but a verdict may be received or a jury discharged on such day. Sec. 5 Judiciary Law.

Adjournment. An application for an adjournment is addressed to the discretion of the court. Such application made upon the ground of the absence of a witness, must disclose that the witness is material, that the witness will be available upon the adjourned date, and that the applicant has not been guilty of neglect. Peo. v. Jackson, 111 NY 362.

A defendant is entitled to a reasonable adjournment for the purpose of obtaining counsel familiar with the case, despite the

fact that previous adjournments were granted, where it appears that his counsel was on trial elsewhere. Peo. v. Kerber, 172 AD 

755; Peo. v. Hull, 251 AD 40.

Where an adjournment has been granted for the purpose of procuring counsel, a defendant is not entitled to a further adjournment upon the ground of the absence of said counsel. Peo. v. Hildebrandt, 16 M 195.

Continual and unreasonable adjournments are not sanctioned; e. g., where seven adjournments had been previously granted, and the defendant had ample opportunity to secure counsel and arrange for the attendance of his witnesses, a denial of a further adjournment was sustained. Peo. v. Schindler, 124 M 73.

Public Trial. The sittings of every court are public, except that in cases involving sex crimes the court may, in its discre tion, exclude the public. Sec. 4 Judiciary Law. The trial court is justified in excluding the public generally from a trial containing revolting testimony, in the interests of public morals. Peo. v. Miller, 258 NY 54. The public should be excluded, for example, in a case involving sex perversion. Peo. v. Hall, 51 AD 57. It is also within the power of the court to conduct purely family or domestic hearings in private. 

Witnesses, especially in a capital case, ought to be excluded upon application of the accused. Peo. v. Cooke, 292 NY 185. 

Presence Of Defendant. The trial of an indictment for a misdemeanor may be had in the absence of the defendant if he appear by counsel. In a felony case, the defendant must be per sonally present. Sec. 356 Code. 

In a felony case, the accused ordinarily must be present from the time of the impanelling of the jury to the rendition of the verdict. Maurer v. Peo., 43 NY 1.

The temporary voluntary absence of a prisoner, who went into an ante-room during a trial in order to telephone, did not constitute error. Peo. v. Bragle, 88 NY 585.

In a case involving a felony not punishable by death, it was

held that the defendant could waive his right to object to the reading of testimony to the jury in his absence. Peo. v. La Barbera, 274 NY 339.

ORDER OF TRIAL 

Before or after the jury has been impaneled and sworn, a motion is in order to dismiss the indictment for insufficiency or lack of jurisdiction.

The trial of an indictment, where a jury trial has not been waived, proceeds in the following order (Sec. 388 Code): (a) The jury is impaneled and sworn. (b) The prosecuting attorney must then open. Peo. v. Romano, 279 NY 392. (c) The defendant may then open. (d) Evidence must be submitted by the people in support of the indictment. (e) Evidence may be submitted by the defendant in support of his case if, after motion made, the indictment is not dismissed or a verdict is not directed in his favor. (f) The parties may then, respectively, offer testimony in rebuttal. Before submission to the jury, the court may permit the parties to offer evidence upon the orginal case, in the exercise of discretion. At the close of the entire case, the court may order a verdict of "not guilty" to be rendered by the jury and such direction must be obeyed. The court is not empowered to direct a verdict of "guilty." (g) If the parties waive summation, the court must charge the jury and submit the case to them for deliberation and verdict. If summation be had, the defendant commences the same. The concluding arguments are presented by the people, after which the court charges the jury. (h) The rendition of the verdict.

(i) Motions with respect to the verdict. Setting date for sentence in the event of conviction, and discharge or other disposition of defendant in event of acquittal. 

With respect to the conduct of the trial, the court is authorized, in the exercise of sound discretion, to vary the order of proof, call witnesses out of natural order, or recall witnesses after the close of the case. Peo. v. Ferrone, 204 NY 551.

SELECTING THE JURY The Judiciary Law (Articles 16-18B) prescribes the manner of the selection of trial jurors. In New York City, the justices of the Appellate Divisions, acting jointly, prescribe the rules. 

Sec. 592 Judiciary Law. 

A special or struck jury, popularly called a "blue ribbon” jury, may be ordered by the court in an important or unusual case. 

Sec. 449 CPA; Peo. v. Hall, 169 NY 184; Peo. v. Raizen, 120 M 

182. Persistent efforts have been made to do away with special juries as unwholesome and undemocratic. 

The term "voir dire" denotes an examination of a juror who has first been sworn to make true answers. Peo. v. Rendigs, 123 

M 32.

An alien is not entitled to trial by a jury of aliens. Sec. 12 

Civil Rights Law. 

Challenges. Objections to a jury or jurors are called "challenges," of which there are two kinds, to wit, to the panel and to the individual juror. Sec. 359 Code.

Challenges must be taken in the following order (Sec. 386 

Code; Peo. v. McQuade, 110 NY 284): (a) To the panel. (b) To an individual juror, for a general disqualification. (c) To an individual juror, for implied bias. (d) To an individual juror, for actual bias. (e) Peremptorily. 

Challenge To The Panel. A challenge to the panel is an objection made to all the jurors returned. Sec. 361 Code. Such

challenge must be taken before a juror is sworn. It must be in writing and must specify distinctly the facts constituting the ground of challenge. Sec. 363 Code.

A challenge to the panel can be founded only on a material departure, to the prejudice of the defendant, from the provisions relating to the drawing and return of the jury. Sec. 362 

Code. What constitutes a material departure to the prejudice of the defendant has been construed to mean a substantial act of omission to the prejudice of the defendant. Peo. v. Schmidt, 168 

NY 568.

The excusal of jurors on unsworn statements (Peo. v. 

Schmidt, 168 NY 568), or the drawing of jurors earlier in date than provided by law (Peo. v. Damron, 212 NY 256), may be overlooked, but it has been held that the drawing of jurors at 

9 A. M. instead of 10 A.M. entitled a defendant to the allowance of a challenge, since he was thereby deprived of an opportunity to see that the drawing took place according to law. Peo. v. 

Damron, 212 NY 256.

When a defendant files a challenge to the panel, the district attorney may orally deny the sufficiency thereof and except thereto, which is noted upon the minutes. The court thereupon determines the sufficiency of the challenge, assuming the allegations of the challenge to be true. Sec. 364 Code. If the exception be allowed, the court may permit an amendment of the challenge. If the exception be disallowed, the court may allow the district attorney to withdraw his exception and deny the facts alleged in the challenge. Sec. 365 Code.

The denial of a challenge may be oral but must be noted in the minutes. Sec. 366 Code. Upon the trial of a challenge, the officer whose irregularity is complained of, as well as any other person, may be examined. Sec. 367 Code. If the challenge is ultimately allowed, the jury must be discharged; if disallowed, the jury is impaneled. Sec. 368 Code. 4,་་ 

Time Of Challenge To Individual Juror. Before a juror is called, the defendant must be informed that if he intends to challenge a juror, he must do so before the juror is sworn. Sec.

369 Code. The court, however, in its discretion, may excuse a challenged juror at any time before any evidence is given. Sec. 

371 Code; Peo. v. Beckwith, 103 NY 361; Peo. v. Hughes, 137 

NY 29.

Challenges to individual jurors must be taken first by the people and then by the defendant. Sec. 385 Code. This rule is a matter of substance and is mandatory. Peo. v. McQuade, 110 

NY 284.

A challenge to an individual juror is either peremptory or for cause. Sec. 370 Code.

Peremptory Challenge. A peremptory challenge is an objection to a juror for which no reason need be given, but upon which the court must exclude him. Sec. 372 Code; Peo. v. 

Hughes, 137 NY 29.

If the crime be punishable by death, thirty challenges are allowed and three additional for each alternate juror; if punishable by imprisonment for a term of ten years or more or for life, twenty are permitted and two additional for each alternate juror. Five peremptory challenges are allowed in all other cases and one additional for each alternate juror. Sec. 373 Code. 

When several defendants are tried together, they cannot sever their peremptory challenges, but must join therein. Sec. 360 

Code; Peo. v. Doran, 246 NY 409; Peo. v. Connolly, 227 AD 167.

The compulsion to join in the number of challenges has been severely criticized in instances where little or no latitude is afforded to the several defendants.

When a proceeding is collateral to the issue of guilt, such as a trial to establish a prior conviction, peremptory challenges are not permissible. Peo. v. Reese, 258 NY 89. 

Challenge For Cause. A challenge for cause is either "general" or "particular." Sec. 374 Code. 

For "General" Cause. A challenge for "general" cause is one which attacks the qualification of a juror to serve in any case, upon the ground that the juror has a felony conviction on record

against him or is not qualified under the rules prescribed by the 

Judiciary Law. Sec. 375 Code. Such an objection must be timely, however, and it is too late to urge the same upon appeal for the first time, despite the fact that it was then ascertainable for the first time. Peo. v. Cosmo, 205 NY 91. 

For "Particular" Cause. A challenge for "particular" cause is either for actual bias or for implied bias. Sec. 376 Code. It may be stated orally but must be entered in the minutes. Sec. 

380 Code.

Actual Bias. The more difficult objection to resolve is that relating to actual bias. Actual bias is the existence of a state of mind on the part of a juror which indicates that such juror cannot try the issues impartially and without prejudice to the rights of the challenging party. Secs. 376, 378 Code.

Where a prospective juror states that he has formed an opinion which would require evidence to remove, he should be disqualified. Cancemi v. Peo., 16 NY 501. By statute, however, if such juror declare under oath that his opinion or impression will not influence his verdict and that he can render an impartial verdict according to the evidence, and the court is so satisfied, a challenge for actual bias must be disallowed. Sec. 376

Code. 

The statement that a juror has formed an opinion as to the guilt of the accused prima facie disqualifies him, which incapacity is overcome, however, if the juror state under oath that he will render a verdict strictly on the evidence. Peo. v. Flaherty, 162 NY 532.

The formation of an opinion based upon what a prospective juror has read in the newspapers will not disqualify him if he state that he will discard his opinion and render an impartial verdict, uninfluenced by what he has read. Peo. ex rel. Phelps v 

Oyer & Terminer, 83 NY 436. 

Alleged error in the disallowance of a challenge for bias will not warrant a reversal unless all the peremptory challenges were exhausted. Peo. v. Wilmarth, 156 NY 566; Peo. v. Larubia, 140 

NY 87.

Implied Bias. A challenge to a juror for implied bias may be taken in the following instances (Secs. 377, 380 Code): (a) Upon the ground of his consanguinity or affinity within the ninth degree to the complainant, victim or defendant. (b) Where he bears a fiduciary relationship to, or is master or servant, landlord or tenant, or member of the family of any of the aforesaid persons, or is a client or counsel. (c) That he is a party adverse to the defendant in a civil action or has been accused by or complained against the defendant in a criminal action. (d) That he has served on the grand jury which found the indictment or on a coroner's jury which inquired into the death of the deceased. (e) That he has served on a jury which tried another person for the same crime charged in the indictment. (f) That he has served on a jury formerly sworn to try the defendant on the same charge. (g) That he has served on a civil jury against the defendant based upon the act charged. (h) In a capital case, in the event that he entertain conscientious scruples as would preclude his finding a verdict of guilty.

Determination Of Challenge. Exception and denials may be stated by the adverse party to challenges and the issues tried by the court. Secs. 381-384 Code. 

Completion Of Jury - Alternates. The first twelve persons who qualify, constitute the jury. They are then sworn and are ready to sit as triers of the facts. Sec. 387 Code.

It is error, once a juror has been accepted, to allow a peremptory challenge directed against him. Peo. v. Grieco, 266 NY 48.

Provision has been made for the selection of one or two additional jurors, in protracted cases, in the discretion of the court. 

The additional jurors, popularly called "alternate jurors," are seated with the regular jury but do not vote unless it has become necessary to substitute them for disabled jurors before final submission. Sec. 358 Code; Peo. v. Mitchell, 266 NY 15.

OPENING TO JURY 

The purpose of an opening is to apprise the court and jury of the proof that will be submitted in support of the indictment or defenses. Peo. v. Wolf, 183 NY 464. No fact should be stated in the opening unless it is material and competent to the issues. 

An opening should not be utilized for the purpose of inflaming the jury or otherwise creating prejudice. 

The latitude allowed to counsel in opening to the jury is a matter of discretion. The refusal of a trial court to interfere will not warrant a reversal of a conviction unless the discretion is abused. Peo. v. Stone, 125 AD 250.

Statements by a prosecuting attorney, in his opening, relating to heinous and revolting misdeeds allegedly committed by the defendant, foreign to the crime charged in the indictment, justify a reversal of a conviction. Peo. v. Wolf, 183 NY 464. 

An opening by a prosecutor consisting of a reading of the indictment, while proper technically, is not encouraged. Peo. v. 

Romano, 279 NY 392.

It is possible to obtain a dismissal of an indictment based upon the opening of the prosecutor. Peo. v. Cory, 124 M 532.

EVIDENCE 

An exhaustive treatment of the rules of evidence is beyond the scope of this work. The more important rules will be briefly considered.

With some exceptions, the rules of evidence in civil actions are applicable also to criminal cases. Sec. 392 Code.

Pleadings in a civil action may not be used in a criminal pro-

secution as proof of a fact admitted or alleged therein. Sec.

392a 

Code. 

No exceptions are necessary to preserve errors upon appeal where the court has been warned, by objection or otherwise, of the position of counsel with respect to a given issue or testimony. Sec. 420a Code. In this respect, therefore, the practice in criminal cases now conforms to that prevailing in civil cases, although the statutes which expressly grant the right to except have not been repealed. Secs. 419, 455 Code.

Testimony Of A Child. Testimony of a child, actually or ap parently under twelve years of age, may be received without an oath, if the court be of the opinion that the witness does not understand the nature of an oath but is possessed of sufficient intelligence to justify the reception of the evidence. However, such testimony alone is insufficient to support a conviction; corroboration is necessary. Sec. 392 Code; Peo. v. Dixon, 231 NY 111.

When the record is silent as to whether the court conducted a preliminary examination of a child under the age of twelve years, it will be presumed that the trial court performed its duty. 

Peo. v. Johnson, 185 NY 219.

Testimony Of Accomplice. An accomplice is one who, at common law, would have been either a principal or accessory before the fact. Peo. v. Sweeney, 213 NY 37. A witness can be considered an accomplice only if he could be prosecuted as a principal at common law. Peo. v. Cohen, 223 NY 406. 

The testimony of an accomplice should be scrutinized with utmost caution. An accomplice might be impelled to testify falsely in order to "purchase" his own freedom, or he might be a disreputable character generally and unlikely to be concerned over the truth.

A conviction may not be had upon the testimony of an accomplice unless he be corroborated by such other evidence as tends to connect the defendant with the commission of the crime. 

Sec. 399 Code. 

Evidence is required, independently, tending to show not only

that the crime has been committed but that the defendant was implicated. Otherwise stated, the corroboration must be from an independent source and relate to a material fact tending to show the defendant's implication. Peo. v. Dixon, 231 NY 111; 

Peo. v. Feolo, 284 NY 381; Peo. v. Kress, 284 NY 452; Peo v. 

Goldstein, 285 NY 376. Identification.

When identification of any person is in issue, a witness who has on a previous occasion identified such person may testify to such previous identification. Sec. 393b Code. 

Other Crimes. 

Evidence of other crimes is incompetent unless it tend to establish motive, intent, the absence of mistake or accident, a common scheme or plan, or the identity of the accused. Peo. v. Molineux, 168 NY 264.

Conspiracy. One or more overt acts must be alleged in the indictment and proved before a defendant may be convicted. 

Proof of additional overt acts, not alleged in the indictment, may also be proffered. Sec. 398 Code; Peo. v. Hines, 284 NY 93. 

Treason. A conviction for treason may not be had unless one or more overt acts are alleged in the indictment. Unlike a conspiracy case, evidence of an overt act not expressly charged is inadmissible. Furthermore, two witnesses are necesssary to prove a case of treason either as to the same overt act, or, if two overt acts are alleged, each may testify as to a different act. Sec. 

396 Code. 

Persons Jointly Indicted. All persons jointly indicted shall, upon the trial of any one, be competent witnesses for each other the same as if not included in the same indictment. Sec. 393a Code.

Acquittal Of Co-Defendant. The acquittal of one joint defendant may not be offered in evidence upon the trial of another. 

Peo. v. Kief, 126 NY 661. 

Character Evidence. The prosecutor may not show the bad

reputation of the defendant unless the defendant first submits evidence of his good reputation. Proof of a prior conviction may not be submitted until the defendant does so or takes the stand. 

Sec. 393c Code; Peo. v. Hinksman, 192 NY 421; Peo. v. Sharp, 107 NY 427; Peo. v. Nuzzo, 294 NY 227. An exception arises if the defendant has been adjudged a habitual criminal. Sec. 512 Code.

Character testimony must be confined solely to the knowledge of the witness concerning the defendant's reputation in respect of the trait involved. Peo. v. Gaasbeck, 189 NY 408. 

Evidence of good character may of itself create a reasonable doubt, when without it, none would exist. Remsen v. Peo., 43 

NY 6. The value of good character evidence is enhanced tremendously in a close case, where an instruction that such testiomny itself may create a reasonable doubt, is proper. In doubtful cases, therefore, evidence of good character is entitled to great weight. Peo. v. Kelly, 11 AD 495.

Fingerprint Records. The written certificate of a person charged with the custody of fingerprint records that the records in his custody show previous convictions, is presumptive evidence thereof. Sec. 482b Code.

CONFRONTATION OF WITNESSES 

In a criminal action, the defendant is entitled, save with some exceptions, to be confronted with the witnesses against him. Sec. 

8 Code; Art. 1, Sec. 6 NY Const. This right refers to living wit nesses and does not, of course, abolish the rules governing the admission of dying declarations. Peo. v. Corey, 157 NY 332. Nor does the right of confrontation bar the previous testimony of a witness who has become unavailable for the trial; e. g., the deposition of a witness who testified before a magistrate is properly received by the grand jury upon satisfactory proof that said witness could not, with due diligence, be found in the state. Peo. v. Albero, 119 M 339.

Another exception to the rule arises in a case where a witness becomes deceased prior to a re-trial, in which event the testimony of the deceased witness given upon the former trial, when

he was subject to cross examination, is admissible upon the subsequent trial. Peo. v. Hines, 284 NY 93; Peo. v. Qualey, 210 NY 202. 

It is improper to receive in evidence the certificate of a clerk that he failed to find any record of the defendant's naturalization. Peo. v. Bromwich, 200 NY 385. On the other hand, it appears that a certificate of a clerk affirmative in tenor and properly attested would not violate the rule if received in evidence. 

Peo. v. Reese, 258 NY 89.

It is error to deny a motion to postpone a trial, or strike out testimony, in a situation where a witness goes into convulsions at the close of direct examination, thus being rendered unfit for cross-examination. Peo. v. Cole, 43 NY 508. 

An accused may waive his right to be confronted with witnesses against him. A waiver may also be made the condition of the granting of an adjournment. There must, however, be a strict compliance with the waiver. Peo. v. Sugarman, 248 NY 255. 

DYING DECLARATIONS 

Dying declarations are admissible as such only in homicide cases and, by statute, also in abortion cases. Sec. 398a Code.

They may be oral, written or otherwise manifest. Their admission is an exception to the rule excluding hearsay evidence. 

The courts have felt that it is safe to permit their introduction upon the theory that the consciousness of impending death should dispel prejudices and impel truthful declarations. Peo. v. Sarzano, 212 NY 231.

The right of cross-examination is the most efficacious method of revealing the truth and its absence impairs the probative value of such declarations, which are admitted only because of necessity and in order to prevent a failure of justice. In the nature of things, such declarations must be scrutinized with great care. Peo. v. Bartelini, 285 NY 433; Peo v. Ludkowitz, 266 

NY 233; Peo. v. Kraft, 148 NY 631.

It is essential that the following elements exist before such declarations become competent:

(a) The declarant must be dead and must have been the victim of the crime. Peo. v. Becker, 215 NY 126. (b) The declaration must have been made while the declarant was in extremis. Peo. v. Del Vermo, 192 NY 470. (c) The declarant must have been conscious of impending death, without any hope of recovery. Peo. v. Sarzano, 212 NY 231; Peo. v. Falleto, 202 NY 494. (d) The declarant would be competent as a witness if alive. 

That part only of a dying declaration which is confined strictly to the factual circumtsances of the homicide, is admissible. 

Peo. v. Smith, 172 NY 210.

The admissibility of a dying declaration is a preliminary question for the court to decide. The weight and credibility thereof is for the jury to determine. Peo. v. Kraft, 148 NY 631. 

CONFESSIONS 

A confession is an acknowledgment of guilt. Peo. v. Mondon, 103 NY 211. It may be oral or written. Peo. v. Giro, 197 NY 152. 

A confession of a defendant, whether made in the course of judicial proceedings or to a private person, can be given in evidence against him, unless made under the influence of fear produced by threats, or unless made upon a stipulation of the district attorney that he shall not be prosecuted therefor; but it is not sufficient to warrant his conviction without additional proof that the crime charged has been committed. Sec. 395 Code; Peo. v. Kennedy, 159 NY 346.

The improper admission of a confession is also a violation of the constitutional provision against self incrimination. Schmidt v. District Attorney, 255 AD 353.

Preliminary Examination. Where a confession is offered in evidence, if the defendant object to its admission and offer to prove that the confession was obtained under circumstances which would render it inadmissible, it is the duty of the trial

court to consider such proof before deciding the competency of the confession. Peo. v. Rogers, 192 NY 331. This proof should be presented in the presence of the jury. Peo. v. Randazzio, 194 

NY 147.

The scope of said preliminary examination is necessarily within the sound judgment of the court. When, for example, there is no prospect of ultimately proving the objections to the admissibility of a confession, a curtailment of the examination is not unjustified. Peo. v. Kinney, 202 NY 389.

It is error to refuse an offer to prove that a confession was not voluntarily made. Peo. v. Fox, 121 NY 449. The request for a preliminary hearing must, however, be accompanied by a statement indicating in what manner admission is to be condemned. Peo. v. Nunziato, 233 NY 394.

If it appear that a confession was obtained by force, the court should exclude it as a matter of law. If the evidence fails to support the offer to show that it was involuntary, the court may hold as a matter of law that the same was given voluntarily. If an issue be raised as to the voluntary nature of the confession, the question should be left to the jury to determine. Peo. v. Doran, 246 NY 409; Peo. v. Weiner, 248 NY 118; Peo. v. Meyer, 162 NY 357; Peo. v. Pignataro, 263 NY 229.

When it appears that a defendant was cruelly beaten and his entire body was covered with bruises, a purported confession must be excluded as a matter of law. Peo. v. Barbato, 254 NY 170.

The fact that a preponderance of the evidence indicates a beating by the police, does not establish conclusively that confessions made later to the district attorney were made under the influence of fear produced by threats. Peo. v. Borowsky, 258 

NY 371. 

Threats. What constitutes a threat is primarily a question of fact. The following statements, made by police officers, have been held not to constitute threats: (a) "They had found enough to convict her, and she might as well own up." Peo. v. McCallam, 103 NY 587.

(b) "He was in a bad fix and had got caught at last." Peo. v. Wentz, 37 NY 303. (c) "Tell the truth" (suggestion). Peo. v. Randazzio, 194 

NY 147.

Promise By District Attorney. The promise by a representative of the district attorney that any statement made by the defendant would not be used against him, is binding, and a confession made under such circumstances may not be admitted. 

Peo. v. Reilly, 224 NY 90.

Since a prosecutor is not a magistrate, he is not obliged to caution a defendant as to his rights upon an examination of him. A confession is not necessarily rendered incompetent if such warning be not given. Peo. v. Randazzio, 194 NY 147.

Deception. A confession, not otherwise objectionable, is competent despite the fact that it was obtained by deception. 

Peo. v. White, 176 NY 331; Peo. v. Scott, 195 NY 224; Peo. v. 

Buffon, 214 NY 53.

Delay In Arraignment. Cases involving a delay in the arraignment of a prisoner seem to present the most difficulty. 

A bare denial of police brutality is insufficient when a prisoner has been held incommunicado for thirty-six hours. The full circumstances surrounding the delay in arraignment, and other developments, should be fairly and fully disclosed. Peo. v. Mummiani, 258 NY 394.

In a case that eventually reached the United States Supreme Court, the subject was explored with a divergence of opinion in our highest state and federal courts. Malinski v. Peo., 324 US 

401, 89 L ed 1029, 65 S Ct 781; Peo. v. Malinski, 292 NY 360, rehearing denied 293 NY 695. The defendant, upon his arrest, was brought to a hotel instead of being booked at a police station and arraigned before a magistrate, the customary procedure. He was stripped to determine whether he had suffered any wounds and then compelled to remain in his underwear for about ten hours, during which period he was interrogated. The

testimony indicated that he was not subjected to physical violence of any consequence while so held incommunicado. During said period, the defendant made an oral confession. After said confession, he was detained at the hotel for an additional three days, without any undue pressure, when he executed a written confession. He was then booked and arraigned (the fourth day after the arrest). The Court of Appeals, by a closely divided vote, affirmed the conviction, declaring that the issue whether the confession was voluntary was properly submitted to and disposed of by the jury. The dissenting opinion, in unmistakable terms, condemned the delay in arraignment. 

The Supreme Court granted certiorari and later reversed the conviction, by a closely divided vote. The principal reason for the reversal, however, seemed to turn on the question whether the verdict of the jury on the fact question presented was conclusive. The Supreme Court held flatly that the question whether a confession was involuntary was one on which it would make an independent determination on the "undisputed" facts. After reviewing the circumstances, it ruled that the oral confession of Malinski was coerced as a matter of law and constituted a violation of the due process clause of the Fourteenth Amendment. 

Suppression. An alleged confession may not be suppressed in advance of trial. It may be tested only upon the trial. Peo. v. 

Nentarz, 142 M 477.

Corroboration. The additional proof to warrant a conviction based on a confession may be direct or circumstantial. The independent evidence required need not, by itself, establish the crime. It is sufficient if it reasonably tends to prove the crime. 

Peo. v. Brasch, 193 NY 46; Peo. v. Becker, 215 NY 1264.

BLOOD GROUPING TESTS 

The results of blood grouping tests are admissible only in cases where definite exclusion is established. Sec. 684a Code; 

Sec. 67 (1-a) NYCCCA.

WHEN INSANITY ESTABLISHED AS A DEFENSE 

Insanity is established as a defense upon proof that at the time of the commission of the alleged crime, the defendant was laboring under a defect of reason which must have been such as to render him (a) ignorant of the nature and quality of the act, or (b) ignorant that the act was wrong. Sec. 1120 P.L.; Peo. v. 

Sherwood, 271 NY 427; Peo. v. Carlin, 194 NY 448; Peo. v. 

Schmidt, 216 NY 324, reargument denied 216 NY 762.

A low order of intellect or an uncontrollable temper is not a sufficient disability. Peo. v. Jones, 194 NY 83. Nor is morbid propensity to commit prohibited acts, of itself, a defense. Sec. 

34 P.L.; Peo. v. Moran, 249 NY 179.

The presumption of sanity (Sec. 815 P.L.) is rebutted by proof of insanity. The people are thereupon bound to prove the defendant's sanity by a fair preponderance of the evidence. Peo. v. Nino, 149 NY 317.

If the jury acquit the defendant upon the ground of insanity, they must so specify. If the court deem his discharge dangerous to the public, the defendant may be committed to a state asylum until sanity is restored. Sec. 454 Code. The remedy of a defendant so committed, upon restoration of sanity, is to proceed by way of a writ of habeas corpus, whereupon his sanity will be determined.

IMPEACHMENT OF WITNESS 

A party may impeach his own witness by proving a prior written inconsistent statement, or prior inconsistent statement made in the course of a hearing, inquiry or other proceeding. 

Sec. 8a Code. 

PRESUMPTION OF INNOCENCE 

A defendant in a criminal action is, of course, presumed innocent until the contrary is proved. Sec. 389 Code. Such presumption continues until the rendition of a verdict of guilty. 

Peo. ex rel. Gow v. Bingham, 57 M 66.

The refusal to charge that a defendant is presumed to be innocent until the contrary be proved, constitutes error, notwith-

standing the court's charge that the jury must be satisfied of the defendant's guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. Peo. v. Van Houter, 38 Hun 168.

REASONABLE DOUBT 

In a criminal case a conviction may not be had unless the guilt of the defendant is proved beyond a reasonable doubt. Sec. 

389 Code. In a civil case a litigant prevails if he establish his cause of action by a fair preponderance of the credible evidence. 

A reasonable doubt is a doubt that may be entertained by reasonable men after a careful and honest consideration of the evidence. Peo. v. Barker, 153 NY 111.

The rule of reasonable doubt applies not only to the case presented by the prosecution but extends also to any defense interposed. Peo. v. Riordan, 117 NY 71. The application of the rule with respect to defenses requires certain refinements of construction; e. g., the prosecution, it has been held, is required to prove sanity by a fair preponderance of evidence where the presumption of sanity has been rebutted. Peo. v. Nino, 149 NY 317. 

When it appears that a defendant has committed a crime, and there is reasonable ground of doubt in which of two or more degrees he is guilty, he may be convicted of the lowest degree only. Sec. 390 Code.

DEFENDANT 

The defendant may testify as a witness in his own behalf, but his neglect or refusal to testify does not create any presumption against him. Sec. 393 Code. This protection is waived when the defendant take the stand for any purpose. Peo. v. Trybus, 219 NY 18.

The district attorney, therefore, may not make any reference to the failure of the defendant to take the stand. Peo. v. Minkowitz, 220 NY 399. When there is a violation of this rule, it is not enough that the court merely instructs the jury to disregard the improper comment; the court should charge the jury in express terms that the defendant was not obliged to take the stand and that his failure to do so does not create any presumption

against him. Peo. v. Smith, 84 348. Full and fair instructions are required to cure the error of such improper comments by an over-zealous district attorney who transcends the proper limits of advocacy. Peo. v. Priori, 164 NY 459.

Sometimes, the failure of a defendant to take the stand is emphasized indirectly. Such conduct is equally improper and warrants a reversal unless the jury is properly instructed and the defendant's guilt reasonably certain. Peo. v. Watson, 216 NY 

565. Reversible error is also committed if a prosecutor, in his opening, expresses curiosity as to whether the defendant would take the stand. Peo. v. Doyle, 58 Hun 535.

Cross-Examination. 

A defendant who takes the stand is subject to cross-examination like other witnesses. Peo. v. Hinksman, 192 NY 421. He may be interrogated in regard to any criminal, degrading or immoral act of his life. Peo. v. Webster, 139 NY 73.

The extent to which disparaging questions, not relevant to the issues, may be propounded, is discretionary with the trial court, and its rulings will not be disturbed unless it appear that the discretion was abused.

A defendant may not be asked concerning an arrest (Peo. v 

Brown, 72 NY 571), or an indictment (Peo. v. Morrison, 194

NY 175), since they are not inconsistent with innocence. 

It is prejudicial error to permit the introduction of a conviction that was reversed, upon a subsequent trial in which the defendant has denied any prior conviction. Peo. v. Van Zile, 80 M 329.

DISTRICT ATTORNEY 

A district attorney should place himself under proper restraint. Undue appeal to arouse sympathy, prejudice or resentment may constitute reversible error. Peo. v. Fielding, 158 NY 542. 

Efforts of the prosecutor designed to produce in the minds of the jury the impression that a defendant has been guilty of various misdeeds, are improper and warrant a new trial in the

absence of credible or probative evidence to justify such inferences. Peo. v. Freeman, 203 NY 267.

Prejudicial comments by the district attorney unrelated to the crime charged, constitute reversible error. Peo. v. Becker, 210 NY 274.

Unless the defendant has been apprised of his rights, it is improper for a prosecutor to interrogate him in open court when he is not on the witness stand and is not represented by counsel. Peo. v. Schindler, 124 M 73.

THE COURT Duties-Powers. 

The trial court must decide all questions of law which arise in the course of a trial. Sec. 417 Code. Libel cases are excepted. Sec. 418 Code.

At any time before final judgment, if the trial court becomes incapacitated, another judge may be substituted. Peo. v. Rao, 271 NY 98.

A verdict of guilty may not be directed or advised by the court. Peo. v. Howell, 69 NY 607. If the testimony so warrant, however, the trial court may direct the discharge of the jury for the purpose of indicting the defendant for a crime of a more serious nature. Under such circumstances, proceedings are suspended and the defendant is held, with or without bail, depending upon the nature of the case. Sec. 400 Code.

Under the foregoing circumstances, if an indictment for the more serious offense be not found at or before the next term of court, the defendant must again be tried on the original indictment. Sec. 401 Code. It should be expected, however, that the question of double jeopardy would merit serious consideration in such event.

Where the facts charged in the indictment do not constitute a crime, or it appear that the court has no jurisdiction, the jury must be discharged. Sec. 402 Code. Whenever the proof falls short of the standard required by law, it is reversible error to submit the case to the jury over the defendant's objection. Peo. v. Smith, 84 M 348. Where the evidence is insufficient to warrant a conviction, the court should advise the jury to acquit and the jury must follow such advice. Sec. 410 Code.

No formal specification is necessary to obtain a directed verdict; any intelligible request, whether by motion to dismiss or application for acquittal or discharge, is sufficient. Peo. v. Ledwon, 153 NY 10. A stipulation by counsel in a criminal case "that a general exception should give the defendant the benefit of a particular exception to any part of the charge" is of no value. An exception is not exclusively for the benefit of a litigant. It is required, among other reasons, to direct the court's attention to alleged error and thus afford an opportunity for correction. Peo. v. Buddensieck, 103 NY 487.

Unjudicial Conduct. Unjudicial conduct, prejudicial to the defendant, constitutes reversible error. Peo. v. Becker, 210 NY 274.

The court may not arbitrarily deprive a defendant of the right to withdraw a motion to set aside a verdict. Peo. v. Mc- 

Grath, 202 NY 445.

It has been held to be error for the court to visit the scene of the crime in the defendant's absence. Peo. v. Harnett, 124 M 418.

Charging The Jury. The charge of the court to the jury is delivered at the close of the case if summation be waived, or after summation. The court is not required to discharge this function prior to the close of the trial. Peo. v. McCallam, 103 NY 587. 

The court must state all matters of law which it thinks necessary and must advise the jury to disregard the punishment. If requested, the court must inform the jury that they are the exclusive judges of all questions of fact (Sec. 420 Code); in a libel case, the court is required to advise that the jury may also decide the law. Sec. 418 Code.

In a criminal case, especially one involving murder, the court should do more than state legal definitions. Even if uninvited by the defendant, the evidence should be discussed in order to help the jury formulate an intelligent opinion. Such omission to present the case to the jury properly warrants a reversal

of a conviction unless the guilt of the defendant has been clearly established. Peo. v. Odell, 230 NY 481.

Within the limits of sound discretion, the court may discuss and comment on questions of fact, but cannot, of course, decide them. Peo. v. Walker, 198 NY 329. Judicial comment upon the evidence is proper within reasonable limits so long as the jury is given to understand that questions of fact are within their exclusive domain. Positive expressions of opinion are condemned. Peo. v. Barberi, 149 NY 256.

Further instructions to the jury constitute part of the charge and may not be given in the defendant's absence in a felony case. 

Maurer v. Peo., 43 NY 1.

If the charge as a whole conveys the correct rule of law, erroneous sentences, detached, will not warrant a reversal. Ambiguous language will be construed in favor of an affirmance unless it fairly appears that the jury were misled. Peo. v. McCallam, 103 NY 587.

Prejudicial comments and instructions warrant a new trial. 

Peo. v. Stiglin, 238 AD 407.

Requests to charge may be oral unless the court directs them to be in writing.

THE JURY VERDICT 

Questions of fact are determined by the jury. Sec. 419 Code; 

Peo. v. Marendi, 213 NY 600. The jury is bound to accept as law what is laid down as such by the court (Sec. 419 Code) except that in a libel case the jury have the right to determine the law and the facts. Sec. 418 Code; Art. 1, Sec. 8 NY Const. Intent is always an issue to be decided by the jury. Peo. v. Flack, 125 NY 

324. It is error for the court to encroach upon the province of the jury to determine the facts. Peo. v. Ohanian, 245 NY 227. The jury may be excused during argument on a question of law. However, it is never error to permit the jury to be present in such instance, Peo. v. Becker, 215 NY 126.

The court must admonish the jury, at each adjournment, not to discuss the case or to form any opinion until final submission. 

Sec. 415 Code.

If it develop during the trial or deliberation of the jury that a juror has personal knowledge respecting a fact in controversy, such knowledge must be disclosed and the juror sworn as a witness and examined in the presence of the parties. Sec. 413 Code. 

When the court deems it necessary, the jury may be directed to view the premises where the crime was allegedly committed or where any material episode occurred. Sec. 411 Code; Peo. v. 

Spickler, 255 NY 408. An attendant is thereupon sworn to permit no discussion or communication with the jury by a third person or himself. Sec. 412 Code.

Before submission, jurors may be permitted to separate or may be kept in charge of attendants. In the latter event, the officers in charge are sworn to keep the jurors together and to suffer no person, including themselves, to speak to or communicate with the jury regarding any matter connected with the trial. Sec. 414 Code. It is discretionary with the court to permit the jurors to dine separately. Peo. v. Dunbar Contracting 

Co., 215 NY 416.

The illness of a juror justifies the discharge of the jury (Sec. 

416 Code) unless alternate jurors have been chosen. 

After Submission Of The Case. After submission of the case to them, the jury may either reach a verdict in open court or retire for deliberation. Sec. 421 Code. Exhibits may be taken to the jury room but only upon consent of both sides. Sec. 425 

Code. Notes taken by themselves may be retained by the jury but they may not use notes taken by others. Sec. 426 Code.

Requests for further instructions or information are made by requiring the officer in charge to conduct the jury into court. 

After notice to counsel, and in the presence of the defendant in a felony case, the inquiry is made. Sec. 427 Code; Maurer v. Peo., 43 NY 1; Peo. v. Cassiano, 30 Hun 388. Communications between the court and jury while they are deliberating, without disclosure to the accused, are not in themselves improper, for propriety is assumed. Peo. v. Kelly, 94 NY 526. 

Until a verdict is reached, the court is deemed open for every purpose connected with the cause submitted to the jury. Sec. 431 

Code. A final adjournment of the court discharges the jury but

any term of the court may be continued for the purpose of finishing a trial or receiving a verdict. Sec. 432 Code. 

Discharge After Submission. After retiring for deliberation, a jury may be discharged before agreement in the following cases only (Sec. 428 Code): (a) Upon the occurrence of an injury or disability affecting the court, the jury or defendant, rendering it inexpedient to continue. (b) When the jury is unable to arrive at a verdict after the lapse of a reasonable time, provided the jury declares its inability to agree. (c) By consent of the court and counsel.

A discharge for any other reason constitutes an acquittal. 

Peo. v. Warden, 202 NY 138.

The reason for the discharge of the jury, when directed before a verdict, must be noted in the minutes. Sec. 429 Code. 

A jury may be discharged on Sunday. Sec. 5 Judiciary Law.

Verdict. The verdict of a jury should be free and untrammeled. It should represent the consensus of individual judgment. 

It must be unanimous in a criminal case. Peo. v. Cosmo, 205 NY 

91. Coercion is not countenanced. Peo. v. Sheldon, 156 NY 268; 

Peo. v. Mayer, 132 AD 646.

When the jury have concluded their deliberations, they are ushered into court and their names called. Sec. 433 Code. The jury must be asked by the court or the clerk of the court whether they have agreed upon a verdict, in which event the same must be declared. Sec. 435 Code. No sealed verdict is recognized in a criminal case. Peo. v. Pickert, 26 M 112. 

A verdict may be general or special. Sec. 436 Code. A general verdict is either "guilty" or "not guilty" and if the issue of former jeopardy is presented, the verdict is either "for the people" or "for the defendant." Sec. 437 Code. A verdict of guilty in a case involving a plea of double jeopardy, without a finding on such issue, is erroneous. Peo. v. Burch, 5 N. Y. Cr. 29. In actual practice, a special verdict is practically unknown in a criminal case.

7 The defendant must be present upon the rendition of a verdict in a felony case, whereas in a misdemeanor case it may be rendered in his absence. Sec. 434 Code.

A verdict of guilty establishes the truth of every material allegation contained in the count of the indictment which the jury has resolved against the defendant. A verdict silent on certain counts in the indictment is no longer equivalent to an acquittal on those counts. Sec. 443a Code; Guenther v. Peo., 24 NY 100. 

In a felony murder case, the jury may recommend life imprisonment. Sec. 1045a P.L. In any other case, a recommendation of mercy has no legal basis and a defendant is entitled to a specific charge to said effect. Peo. v. Lynch, 284 NY 239.

There is a conflict of opinion on the binding effect of a recommendation of life imprisonment in a felony murder case. It has been held that the court is bound to follow the recommendation. Peo. v. De Renna, 166 M 582. It has been held, on the contrary, that the court is not concluded thereby. Peo. v. Smith, 163 

M 469; Peo. v. Ray, 172 M 1004. It would be a rare case indeed were the court to reject the recommendation. 

A verdict may be received on Sunday. Sec. 5 Judiciary Law.

Jurors may not be questioned concerning their verdict, but they are subject to prosecution for corrupt conduct. Sec. 14 Civil

Rights Law; Sec. 373 P.L. 

Verdict Joint Defendants. On an indictment against two or more defendants, if the jury cannot agree upon a verdict as to all, they may render a verdict as to those in regard to whom they do agree, on which a judgment must be entered accordingly, and the case, as to the rest, may be tried by another jury. Verdicts may be recorded severally. Sec. 446 Code; Peo. v. Cohen, 223 NY 406. Verdict-Insanity.

The jury must so specify if they acquit the defendant on the ground of insanity. Sec. 454 Code.

Verdict-Different Degree Of Crime. Upon an indictment for a crime consisting of different degrees, the jury may find the defendant not guilty of the degree charged in the indictment

and guilty of any degree inferior thereto or of an attempt to commit the crime. Sec. 444 Code. Where, after rejecting unproved allegations, there would yet remain enough in the indictment to describe an inferior degree of the crime, a conviction for such lower degree is proper. Dedieu v. Peo., 22 NY 178. 

When it appears that a defendant has committed a crime, and there is reasonable ground of doubt in which of two or more degrees he is guilty, he can be convicted of the lowest of those degrees only. Sec. 390 Code.

It is assumed, unless otherwise stated, that the conv tion is for the highest degree charged in the indictment. Peo. v. Rugg, 98 NY 537; Hawker v. Peo., 75 NY 487. In a pertinent situation, therefore, it would appear that the appropriate remedy, at the time of the rendition of the verdict, would be to enforce the provision requiring the jury to specify the degree. Sec. 30 P.L. 

A person indicted for murder in the first degree, committed while engaged in an attempt to commit a felony, or while engaged in a felony, may not be convicted of murder in the second degree. Peo. v. Hoffman, 219 AD 334; aff'd 245 NY 588. In effect, this constitutes an exception to the rules above mentioned. 

A conviction for assault does not bar a subsequent prosecution for homicide if the victim die from the injury inflicted. On the other hand, if the act complained of, in a homicide case, be not proven the cause of death, the defendant is properly convicted of assault. Sec. 444 Code. Verdict-Mistake.

Upon a verdict of guilty, if it appear to the court that the jury have mistaken the law, the court may direct a reconsideration once only. Such reconsideration may not, however, be directed upon a verdict of acquittal. Sec. 447 

Code; Nelson v. Peo., 23 NY 293. Verdict-Defective. 

If a verdict be neither general nor special, the court may direct a reconsideration. A verdict may not be recorded until rendered in some form which clearly manifests the intent of the jury. Sec. 448 Code. If the jury persist in finding an informal verdict from which it is clear that they intend to find for the defendant, an acquittal must be recorded. No con-

viction may be recorded unless the jury expressly find against the defendant. Sec. 449 Code.

Special Verdict. A finding as to the facts only is a special verdict. When the jury are in doubt as to the legal effect of the facts proved (except in a libel case), they may find a special verdict. Care must be exercised in distinguishing between defective or informal verdicts and special verdicts. 

The special verdict must present the conclusions of fact and not the evidence. The conclusions must be so presented that conclusions of law thereon may be readily found by the court. 

Sec. 438 Code. 

A special verdict must be reduced to writing, entered upon the minutes, read to the jury and agreed to by them, before they are discharged. Sec. 439 Code. No specified form is necessary; it is sufficient if the facts found are intelligibly presented. Sec. 

440 Code. The special verdict may be brought on for argument by either party upon five days' notice. The defendant's counsel is entitled to conclude the argument. Sec. 441 Code. 

If the special verdict be insufficient and the court is unable to give judgment thereon, a new trial must be ordered. Sec. 443

Code. If sufficient, the court applies the law and renders judgment accordingly. Sec. 442 Code.

Polling Jury. The court may, on application of either party, require the polling of the jury after the verdict but before it is recorded. If there be no unanimity, further deliberation must be ordered. Sec. 450 Code. 

The poll consists merely of an inquiry of each juror whether the announced verdict is his verdict. Leighton v. Peo., 88 NY 117. 

Recording Verdict. The verdict must be recorded by the clerk upon the minutes and must be read to the jury. Inquiry must then be made whether it be their verdict. In the event of lack of unanimity, the jury must retire again. Sec. 451 Code.

DISPOSITION OF DEFENDANT 

A verdict of guilty requires that the defendant be remanded, committed, or continued on bail, as the case may be, pending judgment upon the verdict on the date set by the court. Sec. 453 

Code. 

A verdict of acquittal (not guilty) entitles the defendant to his discharge and an exoneration of bail unless other cause for his detention appear. Such discharge does not operate, however, when the acquittal is for a variance between the proof and the charge, in which event the court may detain the defendant and direct a resubmission to another grand jury. Sec. 452 Code.

Chapter 11 ARREST OF JUDGMENT - NEW TRIAL ARREST OF JUDGMENT 

Nature. A motion in arrest of judgment is an application on the part of the defendant that no judgment be rendered on a plea or verdict of guilty, or on a verdict against the defendant upon the plea of a former conviction or acquittal. Sec. 167 Code. 

When Made. The motion must be made before or at the time when the defendant is called for judgment. Sec. 469 Code;

Peo. v. Lee Foon, 275 NY 229. If made before, it must be on notice to the district attorney, or in his presence. Sec. 469 Code. 

Grounds. Either one or both of the following objections only may be urged in support of the motion (Sec. 467 Code): (a) That the court has no jurisdiction over the subject of the indictment. (b) That the facts stated in the indictment do not constitute a crime. Peo. v. Meakim, 133 NY 214. 

Judgment may also be arrested by the court of its own motion by reason of either of the defects above noted. Sec. 468 Code; 

Peo. v. Fuchs, 71 M 69.

The allegation that the grand jury had no jurisdiction of the offense because it was committed in another county, will receive no recognition on a motion in arrest of judgment, as said objection must be made before trial and also within the time allowed by the Code. Peo. v. Willett, 213 NY 368.

Effect Of Arresting Judgment. When judgment is arrested the same normally operates as an acquittal. However, if there be reasonable ground to believe the defendant guilty of any crime, he may be recommitted or admitted to bail to answer a new in-

125 dictment, in which event the former verdict is not a bar to the new indictment. Sec. 470 Code.

Repeal Of Penalty. The repeal of a penalty after conviction and before judgment, arrests the judgment. The repeal after judgment warrants nullification thereof. Hartung v. Peo., 22 

NY 95.

NEW TRIAL 

A motion for a new trial is addressed to the sound discretion of the court and a denial will not be disturbed on appeal unless the discretion is abused. Peo. v. Sprague, 217 NY 373.

The denial of a motion for a new trial is not appealable except as an intermediate order forming part of the judgment roll. Peo. v. Trezza, 128 NY 529. After an affirmance, there is no right of appeal from an order denying a motion for a new trial. 

Peo. v. Mayhew, 151 NY 607.

Application When And To Whom Made. An application for a new trial, made before the trial court, must be made before judgment, except that an application founded on newly discovered evidence must be made within one year. In a capital case, the application may be made at any time before execution. Sec. 

466 Code; Peo. ex rel. Jerome v. General Sessions, 185 NY 504; 

Peo. v. McGrath, 202 NY 445; Peo. v. Phelan, 127 M 502.

The death of the trial judge between the time of the verdict and pronouncement of sentence, does not entitle the defendant to a new trial. In such event, a motion for a new trial may be entertained by any judge of the court in which the conviction was rendered. Peo. v. Rao, 271 NY 98.

GROUNDS FOR NEW TRIAL 

A new trial will be granted if any of the following statutory grounds be established, provided, however, that the substantial rights of the defendant have been prejudiced thereby (Sec. 465 Code): (a) When the trial of an indictment for a felony occurred in the defendant's absence.

(b) When the jury has received any evidence out of court other than a view of the premises under the direction of the court. (c) When the jury have separated after retiring, without leave, or been guilty of misconduct. (d) When the verdict has been reached by lot or by any means other than a fair expression of opinion on the part of the jurors. (e) When the court has misdirected the jury in a matter of law or has refused properly to instruct the jury, provided that the defendant has duly noted his exception thereto upon the trial. (f) When the verdict is contrary to law or against the weight of the evidence. (g) Upon the ground of newly discovered evidence.

Aside from these statutory grounds, such relief will likewise be granted when justice requires a new trial (Peo. v. Cashin, 259 NY 434), or where the whole truth relating to the crime has not been fully given and the testimony is uncertain and unreliable. Peo. v. Gallo, 149 NY 106.

When justice has been perverted by practices dehors the record, or the accused has been injured by circumstances occurring on the trial not the subject of legal exception, the remedy of the aggrieved party is an application for a new trial or an appeal to the Governor for clemency. Walsh v. Peo., 88 NY 458. 

Evidence Out Of Court. Statements made to a jury out of court, if sufficiently important, constitute grounds for a new trial. Peo. v. Gallo, 149 NY 106.

The clandestine visit of a juror to the locale of the crime and a discussion thereat, warrants a new trial. Peo. v. Tyrrell, 36

Hun 642. However, the unauthorized visit of a juror to the scene of the crime does not of itself constitute reversible error. Peo. v. Kraus, 147 M 906.

Separation Of Jury—Illness. 

The trial court may properly permit the jurors to separate when dining. Peo. v. Dunbar Contracting Co., 215 NY 416.

ARREST OF JUDGMENT-NEW TRIAL 

127 The temporary illness of a juror and his separation from the jury while under medical care, does not constitute prejudicial error in the absence of proof of improper communications or other irregularity. Peo. v. Hoch, 150 NY 291.

The denial of a motion for a new trial has been held to be proper in a case where, after the jury arrived at a verdict, a juror collapsed, but later recovered and resumed his duties. Peo. v. Buchanan, 145 NY 1. 

Misconduct Of Jury. Jurors may not recite their own misconduct for the purpose of attacking or discrediting their verdict. Peo. v. Sprague, 217 NY 373.

A new trial is warranted in a case where the prejudice of a juror is not disclosed at the time of his selection and not discovered until after the rendition of the verdict. Peo. v. Leonti, 262 

NY 256.

Newly Discovered Evidence. To justify the granting of a new trial upon the ground of newly discovered evidence, the following elements must be present (Peo. v. Priori, 164 NY 459): (a) The evidence must be material and of such nature that it will probably change the result if a new trial be granted. (b) The evidence was discovered since the trial. (c) The evidence was not discoverable before trial by the exercise of due diligence. (d) The evidence is not cumulative, i. e., of the same nature as that previously adduced, and does not merely impeach or contradict the former evidence. 

It should be reasonably clear that the proffered new evidence is material, credible, and would undoubtedly produce a different result upon a new trial. Peo. v. Gambacorta, 197 NY 181. 

Recantation on the part of a witness constitutes newly discovered evidence, and may justify a new trial. Peo. v. Shilitano, 218 NY 161. Mere retraction is not enough where the testimony was corroborated. Peo. v. Mayhew, 19 M 313.

Upon an application for a new trial based upon newly discovered evidence, the court may compel the appearance of the affiants for examination and cross-examination. Sec. 465 Code.

Such examinations are designed to promote justice, and are sometimes directed by an appellate court before deciding an appeal. Peo. v. Shilitano, 215 NY 715.

EFFECT OF NEW TRIAL 

The granting of a new trial places the parties in the same position as if there had been no trial. The former verdict may not be used or referred to, either in evidence or in argument. 

Sec. 464 Code; Peo. v. Palmer, 109 NY 413. 

The reversal of a conviction and the granting of a new trial does not preclude a second trial on all counts where the verdict in the first trial was silent on certain counts. Peo. v. Palmer, 109 NY 413; Peo. v. McGrath, 202 NY 445.

Chapter 12 

JUDGMENT - SENTENCE - EXECUTION JUDGMENT OR SENTENCE 

The pronouncement by the court, after conviction, of the penalty imposed, is the judgment or sentence (usually interchangeable); it is rendered in open court and entered upon the minutes. Punishment may be inflicted only after conviction by a court having jurisdiction. Sec. 31 P.L.; Sec. 3 Code.

At least two days must elapse between a plea or verdict of guilty and the sentence, unless the court will not remain in session for said period, in which event sentence is pronounced at as remote a time as can reasonably be allowed under the circumstances. Any delay, however, may be waived by the defendant.

Sec. 472 Code; Peo. v. Spencer, 179 NY 408. Judgment may also be pronounced at a subsequent term. Peo. v. Frost, 198 NY 110. 

Prior Investigation. Before sentence, the court is obliged to examine into the defendant's criminal record and reports that may have been made concerning any physical, mental or psychiatric examination of the defendant. Sec. 482 Code. Also, into the prisoner's background. Sec. 485a Code. Provision is made for transmission of pertinent information from other officials.

Sec. 942a Code. 

Presence Of Defendant. 

If the conviction be for a felony, the defendant must be personally present when judgment is rendered or sentence is pronounced. If the conviction be for a misdemeanor, judgment may be rendered or sentence pronounced in his absence. Sec. 473 Code.

Arraignment For Sentence. When the defendant appears for judgment, he must be asked by the clerk whether he has any cause to show why judgment should not be pronounced against

him. Sec. 480 Code. Since the court may pronounce judgment in misdemeanor cases in the absence of the defendant, the foregoing applies only to felony cases. Peo. v. Kaminsky, 208 NY 389.

In answer to the inquiry above noted, the defendant may urge that he has become insane since the verdict, or that he has grounds to offer in arrest of judgment or for a new trial (Sec. 

481 Code), or that he has been pardoned. If the court deem it necessary, it may commit the defendant to determine his mental condition.

A proceeding to determine sanity at the time of judgment is in the nature of an inquest and a trial by jury is not required. 

Peo. v. White, 140 M 701.

If a pardon be pleaded, sentence may be deferred in order to investigate and determine the question. 

In the ordinary case, a plea for leniency is made in response to the inquiry above mentioned.

The failure to inquire whether the defendant has anything to say why sentence should not be pronounced against him, warrants setting aside the sentence for the purpose of proceeding upon the verdict properly. Peo. v. Nesce, 201 NY 111. Contrary recollection of the defendant's counsel is overridden by minutes showing that the inquiry was made. Peo. v. Bowser, 196 NY 296.

Form Of Clemency. 

A reprieve is a postponement of execution. A commutation is a reduction in or lessening of a sentence. 

An ordinary pardon is a release of unenforced penalties, but a pardon for innocence wipes out all charges.

A commutation is operative without the consent of the convict. A pardon must be accepted by the convict to be valid. Peo. ex rel. Patrick v. Frost, 133 AD 179.

Pardon For Innocence. In case a pardon is granted for innocence, the judgment of conviction must be set aside and the indictment, information or complaint dismissed by the court in which the conviction took place, upon motion duly made therefor. Sec. 697 Code. This recent amendment seemingly overrides the opinion of the Court of Appeals in Peo. ex rel. Prisament v. Brophy, 287 NY 132, in which it was stated flatly that the issuance of a pardon for innocence, of itself, could not blot out a conviction, since it was nothing more than the private opinion of the executive; that such private opinion could not impair the previous adjudication of guilt.

Ordinary Pardon. 

An ordinary pardon proceeds, not upon the theory of innocence, but implies guilt; it is granted as a favor. Roberts v. State, 160 NY 217. Such a pardon merely terminates punishment and elimnates future penalties arising out of the conviction itself.

The release of unenforced penalties, however, is limited to the punishment for the particular crime. The conviction is counted, for example, in determining the question whether an individual is a second offender; to this extent, therefore, Peo. ex rel. Prisament v. Brophy, 287 NY 132 is still good law.

An appeal by the people in no case stays the operation of a judgment in favor the defendant until the judgment is reversed. 

Sec. 526 Code. 

An appeal from a sentence of death automatically stays execution until the appeal is determined. Sec. 528 Code. The granting of a motion for reargument therein likewise stays execution. Sec. 528a Code. In case of any other type of conviction, execution of the judgment is stayed pending appeal by filing a "certificate of reasonable doubt" with the notice of ap peal. Sec. 527 Code. 

CERTIFICATE OF REASONABLE DOUBT 

The certificate of reasonable doubt is a statement, signed by a court, which declares that in the opinion of the court there is reasonable doubt whether the judgment should stand. It must recite briefly the particular rulings believed to be erroneous, together with any other grounds upon which it is granted. Sec. 

527 Code. Such a certificate operates as a stay of proceedings pending a determination of the appeal. Peo. v. McLaughlin, 150 

NY 365.

The test to be applied in determining whether a certificate should issue is whether there are questions of sufficient gravity to justify a review on appeal prior to execution. Peo. v. Wagner, 120 M 214. The court need not be satisfied that a conviction will be reversed; it is sufficient that important questions of law exist which justify a consideration by an appellate court. Peo. v. 

Hummel, 49 M 136; Peo. v. Kaplan, 147 M 752; Peo. v. Hines, 12 NYS2d 545.

If it appears, however, upon the entire case that there is no

reasonable doubt as to whether a conviction will be sustained, an application for a certificate will be denied. Peo. v. Rea, 100 

M 434. 

Application-To Whom Made. If the conviction was had in a court of record, or the Court of Special Session in New York City, the application for a certificate of reasonable doubt may be made in said courts or in the Supreme Court. Sec. 527 Code. 

As a practical matter, it is usually advisable to make the application in the Supreme Court.

A stay may also be obtained after the affirmance of a conviction, by filing a similar certificate with the notice of appeal to the Court of Appeals, if granted by a judge of the Court of Ap peals or a justice of the Appellate Division. Sec. 528 Code. 

Number Of Applications. No further application for a cer tificate of reasonable doubt may be made in the event of a de nial. Sec. 529 Code. 

Contents-Notice. The application for a certificate must be made on at least two days' notice to the district attorney. The moving papers must specify the particular rulings alleged to be erroneous and any other grounds urged in support of the ap plication. Sec. 529 Code. 

Temporary Stay. Pending the determination of an application for a certificate, the court may stay execution. Sec. 529

Code. Bail may not be allowed, however, until the certificate itself be granted. Peo. ex rel. Hummel v. Reardon, 186 NY 164.

Duration Of Stay. A stay obtained by a certificate of reason able doubt is operative until the appeal has been determined, provided the same is diligently prosecuted. If the appeal be not submitted within ninety days, the stay lapses unless the appellate court extends the time for submission of the appeal. Applications for extensions of time to submit appeals must be made on five days' notice to the district attorney. Sec. 529 Code. 

Effect Of Stay. In granting a certificate of reasonable doubt, the court may provide, in a proper case, for the defendant's admission to bail.

APPEAL PROCEDURE 

In General. The right to appeal is purely statutory. Peo. v. 

Zerillo, 200 NY 443. An appeal from a conviction does not lie until judgment or sentence is rendered. Hill v. Peo., 10 NY 463. 

An appeal does not lie from a mere verdict. The rules of the appellate court should always be consulted in order to insure the proper presentation of an appeal.

Only one appeal is permitted as a matter of right in a criminal case. A further appeal, if allowed, must be taken to the 

Court of Appeals. Sec. 520 Code; Peo. v. McCarthy, 250 NY 358.

An appeal is taken by the service of a notice in writing in duplicate, upon the clerk of the court where the judgment roll is filed, stating that the appellant appeals from the judgment. 

Sec. 522 Code. The party appealing is known as the appellant and the adverse party as the respondent, as in civil cases. Sec. 

516 Code. Appeals in criminal cases usually take precedence over all other appeals, under the rules of the appellate courts. 

If the appeal be taken by the defendant, a copy of the notice of appeal must be served on the district attorney. If the judgment be one of death, the district attorney, after receipt of such notice, must forthwith notify the official having custody of the defendant thereof. An appeal from a judgment of death stays execution until the determination of the appeal. Secs. 523, 528

Code. So does a reargument. Sec 528a Code. Incomplete service may be cured. Sec. 524a Code.

If the people appeal, notice thereof must be served on the defendant or his counsel. If personal service of a notice of appeal cannot be made after due diligence, the appellate court may direct service by publication. Sec. 524 Code. The court is empowered to allow omissions to be supplied where a notice of appeal is seasonably served or filed. Sec. 524a Code.

The rules of the respective courts involved should always be consulted.

Time. An appeal must be taken in the ordinary case within thirty days after the judgment was rendered or the order entered. Sec. 521 Code; Peo. v. De Laney, 244 NY 521.

Appeal By Defendant. An appeal may be taken by a defendant from a judgment of conviction and upon the appeal any intermediate order forming a part of the judgment roll may be reviewed. Sec. 517 Code. Separate appeals may not be taken from intermediate orders, which are reviewable only upon appeal from a judgment of conviction if incorporated in the notice and record on appeal.

An order denying a motion for a new trial, made after an affirmance of a judgment, is not appealable. Peo. v. Mayhew, 151 

NY 607.

An order summarily adjudging a person guilty of a criminal contempt is reviewable by certiorari and not by appeal. Sec. 

750 Judiciary Law; Matter of Douglas v. Adel, 269 NY 144; 

Art. 78 CPA. If, however, the contempt is based on misconduct in the court's absence, the review is by appeal. Wilwerth v. 

Levitt, 262 AD 112.

An appeal from a death sentence must be taken direct to the

An appeal from a sentence of death must be taken, as stated, directly to the Court of Appeals. Sec. 517 Code; Art. 6, Sec. 7

The power to review capital cases was taken away from the Appellate Division in order to prevent a second review and thus expedite a final determination; hence such appeals are first and finally presented in the Court of Appeals. Peo. v. Triola, 174 NY 324.

Questions of fact, therefore, are reviewable in death cases. 

Peo. v. Helmer, 154 NY 596. Likewise, the court is empowered, in its discretion, to consider errors not preserved below. Sec. 528 

Code; Peo. v. Gerdvine, 210 NY 184.

In reviewing the evidence on appeal in a capital case, the Court of Appeals, in dealing with questions of fact, is governed by the practice regulating appeals in the ordinary case. Unless there are circumstances indicating partiality, mistake, error or prejudice on their part, the finding of a jury on disputed questions and conflicting evidence is conclusive. Peo. v. Loppy, 128

Peo. v. Carmichael, 249 NY 189. As stated, it is essential to obtain leave to appeal in such cases.

The failure to move for a dismissal is a concession that there was a fact to be determined and bars a review except in a death case. Peo. v. Robinson, 237 NY 567.

POWERS AND DUTIES OF APPELLATE COURT 

A reversal may only be directed after argument or submission by the appellant in the event the respondent fail to appear. Sec. 

539 Code. A reversal may not be granted on default. 

After hearing the appeal, the court must give judgment without regard to technical errors, defects or exceptions which do not injuriously affect the substantial rights of the parties. Sec. 

542 Code. Unsubstantial errors should be disregarded. Peo. v. 

Dixon, 231 NY 111.

The powers and duties of appellate courts are stated generally in the Constitution (Art. 6, Sec. 8 NY Const.) and are amplified in the Code (Sec. 543). Sec. 543 of the Code provides that in cases where an erroneous judgment has been entered upon a lawful verdict, the judgment may be corrected to conform to the verdict; in all other cases, the appellate court must (a) re-

verse or affirm, or (b) reduce the sentence imposed to not less than the minimum provided by law for the crime, or (c) order a new trial, or (d) modify the judgment to provide for a conviction of a lesser crime. 

In reversing a judgment, the discharge of the defendant is ordered unless a new trial be directed. Sec. 545 Code.

Where an order of reversal of the Appellate Division specifies that the reversal is for error of law, it is presumed, in the absence of a contrary finding, that the questions of fact were not considered. Sec. 543a Code.

If satisfied that the verdict is against the weight of the evidence or against the law, or that justice requires it, a new trial may be granted although proper objections were not noted. Sec. 527 

Code. As we have seen, on appeals entertained by the Court of 

Appeals, however, said court deals exclusively with questions of law duly preserved except in death cases. 

The approach to the considerations of an appeal, as contrasted with the heat of the trial itself, has been well stated thus: "We review in the calm atmosphere of an appellate court the rulings made by another judge in the tense atmosphere of a criminal trial. No impossible standards of perfection may be applied. We recognize that however great the learning of the trial judge may be, and however calm and judicial his temperament, it is impossible for him to rule on all questions in the excitement and speed of a criminal trial as he himself would rule after opportunity for careful consideration." Peo. v. Malkin, 250 NY 185, 196.

The defendant is not given and may not claim, as a matter of right, the benefit of errors occurring on the trial to which no exceptions were taken. The failure to make proper objections deprives him of that right. The appellate court is simply vested with discretionary power to disregard such neglect, and without regard to objections, review the case on the merits. Peo. v. Driscoll, 107 NY 414.

Despite the broad powers conferred, objections are usually necessary in order fully to protect the rights of the defendant.

NY 278.

Where no exception was taken to proof that the offense was committed a year before the date mentioned in the indictment, no error was found, for the reason that the indictment might have been amended if objection were seasonably made. Peo. v. 

Formosa, 131 NY 478.

It is a cardinal principle in our jurisprudence that the jury is the ultimate tribunal for the investigation and determination of questions of fact. When the facts are capable of diverse or opposing inferences, an appellate court should not interfere. Peo. v. Cignarale, 110 NY 23. If, however, the defendant did not have a fair trial, it is the duty of the court to reverse a conviction. 

Peo. v. Creasy, 236 NY 205; Peo. v. Herman, 255 AD 314. 

Threats by a prosecutor in summation; appeals to fear; exposing the jury to ridicule in the event of an acquittal, constitute reversible error, especially when the trial court does not cure or otherwise condemn inflammatory statements. Peo. v. Mull, 167 

NY 247.

The failure of a trial court to instruct the jury properly, although no request to charge was made, may warrant a reversal. 

Peo. v. Van Zandt, 224 NY 354..

Erroneous rulings by the trial court will not justify a reversal unless they are of sufficient gravity to affect the substantial rights of the defendant. Peo. v. Patrick, 182 NY 131.

In a close case, errors which might otherwise be overlooked assume great importance and may warrant a reversal. Peo. v. 

Montesanto, 236 NY 396.

A conviction erroneously secured on one theory may not be sustained on the conjecture that it would have been rendered if the correct theory had been applied. Peo. v. Smith, 232 NY 239;

Matters subsequently brought into the case by stipulation must be disregarded. Peo. v. Dewey, 128 NY 606. However, the people are at liberty, in support of a judgment of conviction, to supply record evidence which, if offered at the trial, could not be overcome. Peo. v. Travis, 257 NY 474.

The law does not contemplate exceptions to the conduct of counsel but only to the action of the court in doing or refusing to do something. An exception noted to the address of the prosecutor raises no reviewable point; counsel should move for a mistrial. Peo. v. Pindar, 210 NY 191.

A question of constitutionality is not reviewable unless raised in the court below. Peo. ex rel. Bush v. Houghton, 182 NY 301. 

An escaped prisoner may not apply for relief. It is essential that he be in custody, either factual (confined) or constructive (bail). Peo. v. Genet, 59 NY 80.

If a new trial is ordered, the same shall proceed as if no trial had been held. Sec. 544 Code. Upon an affirmance, the original judgment must be executed as the appellate court may direct, and if the defendant be at large, a bench warrant may be issued for his arrest. If the judgment be corrected, the same must be executed as the appellate court may direct. Sec. 546 Code. 

Reargument. The rules of the appellate court involved should be consulted for the proper presentation of an application for a reargument. Generally, it must be shown, in any event, that the court misapprehended or overlooked a decisive point.

In the interests of justice, reargument has been granted later to correct obvious error. Peo. v. Meshel, 245 AD 673. The Court of Appeals has gone so far as to grant reargument made solely upon the ground of newly discovered evidence. Peo. v. Regan, 292 NY 109.

Remitting Papers. When the judgment of the appellate court is rendered, it must be entered in the judgment book and a certified copy of the entry remitted to the clerk with whom the original judgment roll is filed. Sec. 547 Code. The decision of the court and the return are remitted to the court below in the same form and manner as in civil actions. Sec. 548 Code. Thereafter, the appellate court has no further general jurisdiction in the matter. Sec. 549 Code.

DOUBLE JEOPARDY 

Constitutional And Statutory Enactments.

Jeopardy means danger of punishment. The Fifth Amendment to the Constitu tion of the United States, which forbids double jeopardy, is not binding upon the states. Peo. v. Adams, 176 NY 351. However, ample protection is afforded by the enactments of the several states.

The New York Constitution provides that no person shall be subject to be twice put in jeopardy for the same offense. Art. 1, Sec. 6.

Inhibiting provisions have also been enacted into the Penal 

Law and Code of Criminal Procedure, as follows: 

"No person can be subjected to a second prosecution for a crime for which he has once been prosecuted, and duly convicted or acquitted." Sec. 9 Code. "Where a prisoner is acquitted or convicted, upon an indictment for a crime consisting of different degrees, he cannot thereafter be indicted or tried for the same crime, in any other degree, nor for an attempt to commit the crime so charged, or any degree thereof." Sec. 32 P.L. "An act or omission which is made criminal and punishable in different ways, by different provisions of law, may be punished under any one of those provisions, but not under more than one; and a conviction, or acquittal under one bars a prosecution for the same act or omission under any other provision." Sec. 1938 P.L.

These apparently simple enactments are difficult of application in many instances. The confusion arises when it becomes necessary to determine whether the second prosecution is for the same offense and also whether the prosecution is a second prosecution.

How Raised. This defense must be pleaded to be available upon the trial. Sec. 332 Code; Peo. v. Taft, 174 M 1033. The issue is usually raised, however, by a habeas corpus proceeding (Peo. ex rel. Bullock v. Hayes, 215 NY 172), which is a more expeditious remedy.

In General. Where an act violates both federal and state laws, both jurisdictions may punish therefor. US v. Lanza, 260 

US 377. 

Under the Penal Law (Sec. 33) it must appear that the offense charged was the particular offense of which the defendant was convicted or acquitted on the merits in another state or country, and that such offense was founded upon an act charged in the New York indictment.

It appears that a conviction or acquittal is necessary before the defense may be sustained. However, proceedings in certain instances may operate as an acquittal, without a formal adjudication, as hereinafter discussed. 

In order to sustain a plea of former jeopardy, it must be shown that the charges are the same in law and in fact. Where the offenses are distinct crimes, the fact that they are part of one transaction is not a bar. Peo. ex rel. Dawkins v. Frost, 129

AD 498. However, if one offense constitutes an essential part of another offense, and both are in fact one transaction, a conviction or acquittal of one is a bar to the prosecution for the other. 

An exception arises where death results after a conviction for assault. Peo. v. Purcell, 16 NYS 199.

It seems clear, in any event, that a conviction or acquittal on the merits is not essential to sustain a defense of double jeopardy. Peo. ex rel. Stabile v. Warden, 202 NY 138; Peo. ex rel. 

Meyer v. Warden, 269 NY 426. 

Fraud. To be available as a defense, the former conviction, for example, must be valid and not based on fraud or collusion. 

Where a person, learning of a contemplated felony prosecution against him, collusively goes before a magistrate and is convicted of a misdemeanor, he may not thereafter properly plead a former conviction. Peo. v. Cuatt, 70 M 453.

Unless, for example, a trial is terminated by the disagreement of the jury, or by their discharge according to law, or by consent of the accused, or through extreme or absolute necessity, or by illness or death, a defendant has been put in jeopardy. Peo. v. Goldfarb, 152 AD 870; Sec. 428 Code. "age

A trial court has no right to discharge a jury arbitrarily or capriciously. A jury may be discharged with leave of court and consent of both parties, or discharged without the defendant's consent if, by his own act, he has brought about a condition under which the trial could not proceed with justice to all concerned, or if urgent necessity or dire emergency demand `it.

Short of same, a defendant is put in jeopardy. Peo. ex rel. 

Jimerson v. Freiberg, 137 M 314.

The arbitrary discharge of a jury immediately brings the defense into play. Such conduct is tantamount to an acquittal. In such event, a habeas corpus proceeding affords an expeditious remedy and the plea of double jeopardy in such circumstances warrants a decision that the writ be sustained. Peo. ex rel.

Chapter 16 HABEAS CORPUS AND CERTORIARI 

The history of the writ of habeas corpus is lost in antiquity. 

It was in use to some extent before the Magna Carta. This remedy is placed beyond the pale of legislative discretion, except that it may be suspended when public safety requires in either of the two emergencies named in the Constitution, viz., rebellion and invasion. Art. 1, Sec. 4 NY Const.; Peo. v. ex rel. Tweed v. 

Liscomb, 60 NY 559. Nature-Allowance.

A person imprisoned or restrained in his liberty, within the state, for any cause or upon any pretense, is entitled to a writ of habeas corpus or a writ of certiorari, for the purpose of inquiring into the cause of the restraint or imprisonment. Sec. 1230 CPA.

The right to a writ of certiorari to inquire into the cause of detention, apparently abolished under section 1283 of the Civil Practice Act, is preserved by the express provisions of section 

1230 of the Civil Practice Act. Peo. ex rel. Semenoff v. Nagle, 

118 M 176.

The only difference between the two writs is that the writ of habeas corpus brings before the court the person of the prisoner while the writ of certiorari causes the record of the commitment or detention to be produced. In every respect except the actual production of the prisoner, the proceedings are the same. Generally, a bailable offense warrants an application for a writ of habeas corpus while the writ of certiorari is utilized where the offense is unbailable. Peo. ex rel. Taylor v. Seaman, 8 M 152.

In deciding whether a writ should be sustained, the sole question to determine is the jurisdiction of the court to render the judgment or to issue the process pursuant to which a person is deprived of his liberty. Peo. ex rel. Young v. Stout, 144 NY 699.

Except in cases where the facts cannot be materially changed, qualified or explained, the determination of important issues should await a regular trial, where the facts will be fully developed. Peo. ex rel. Collins v. McLaughlin, 194 NY 556. A writ of habeas corpus may not perform the functions of an appeal. 

Peo. ex rel. Doyle v. Atwell, 232 NY 96.

Generally, the writ of habeas corpus may be utilized to challenge the sufficiency of a deposition (Peo. ex rel. Perkins v. 

Moss, 187 NY 410), a warrant (Peo. ex rel. Miller v. Foley, 28 

Barbour 630), a commitment (Peo. ex rel. Howey v. Warden, 207 NY 354), and to try the questions of double jeopardy (Peo. ex rel. Bullock v. Hayes, 215 NY 172). Also to consider the question of excessive bail. Peo. ex rel. Rothensies v. Searles, 229 AD 603.

A writ may also be issued upon the application of the attorney general or a district attorney in cases where the people are a party or in which they are interested. Sec. 1233 CPA.

Exceptions. A person is not entitled to a writ in the following instances, described in section 1231 of the Civil Practice 

Act: 

"Where he has been committed or is detained by virtue of a mandate issued by a court or judge of the United States in a case where such courts or judges have exclusive jurisdiction under the laws of the United States or have acquired exclusive jurisdiction by the commencement of legal proceedings in such a court. 

Where he has been committed or is detained by virtue of a final judgment or decree of a competent tribunal of civil or criminal jurisdiction; or the final order of such a tribunal made in a special proceeding instituted for any cause, except to punish him for a contempt; or by virtue of an execution or other process issued upon such a judgment, decree or final order."

So long as the court rendering final judgment has jurisdiction of the person of the prisoner and of the offense involved, the question of an erroneous judgment cannot, therefore, be

tested by a habeas corpus proceeding. Peo. ex rel Carr v. Martin, 286 NY 27. This restriction would not apply, however, where the prisoner has already served his maximum legal imprisonment.

The Application. An application for a writ must be made by a written petition, signed by the person detained or by someone in his behalf. Sec. 1232 CPA. It is proper for an attorney to sign such a petition.

The application must be made to either of the following courts or officers: (a) A justice of the Supreme Court or Appellate Division located in any part of the state. Sec. 1232 CPA; Peo. ex rel. Patrick v. Frost, 133 AD 179. (b) An officer located within the county of detention who is authorized to perform the duties of a justice of the Supreme Court at chambers. If such official is not available or has refused to grant a writ, application may then be made to an officer residing in an adjoining county authorized to perform those duties, in which event proof must first be submitted showing the absence or incapacity of the proper official in the county of detention. Sec. 1232 CPA.

A court or judge authorized to grant a writ must grant it without delay whenever a petition therefor is presented, unless it appears that the petitioner is prohibited by law from prosecuting the writ. Failure to allow a writ in a proper case subjects a court or judge to a forfeiture of one thousand dollars, to be recovered by an action therefor. Sec. 1235 CPA.

Contents Of Petition. The petition must be verified and must state in substance (Sec. 1234 CPA): (a) The name of the person imprisoned and that he is imprisoned or restrained in his liberty; the place where; the name of the person by whom he is imprisoned, and if such name be unknown, a description should be given.

(b) That he has not been committed in a case specified in section 1231 of the Civil Practice Act. 

(c) The cause of the imprisonment or restraint. (d) If the imprisonment be by virtue of a mandate, a copy should be annexed if obtainable; if unobtainable, the the reasons should be stated. (e) In what manner the imprisonment is illegal. (f) Whether any previous application has been made therefor, in which event the circumstances and determination should be recited, together with the additional facts. (g) Whether an appeal has been taken, in which event the circumstances and determination should be recited, together with the additional facts. (h) Whether a writ of habeas corpus or a writ of certiorari is desired. 

For failure to comply with the foregoing requirements, the application may be denied or a writ vacated without notice. Sec. 1234 CPA. 

Issuance Of Writ-Where And When Returnable. The writ must be issued under the seal of the court by which it is awarded, or if granted by a judge out of court, under the seal of the court before which it is returnable. Otherwise, the seal of the 

Supreme Court must be used. Sec. 1236 CPA.

The writ is issued in the name of the people, and if awarded upon the application of a private person, must show that it was issued upon the relation of that person, called the relator. Sec. 

1236 CPA.

The official or person having custody, against whom the writ is issued, is styled as the defendant. Sec. 1236 CPA.

A writ should be made returnable before the Supreme Court in the county of detention. Peo. ex rel. Whitman v. Woodward, 150 AD 770.

During the session of the Supreme Court in any county, no person detained therein upon a criminal charge may be removed by a writ of habeas corpus unless the same was issued by or

made returnable before such court. Sec. 25 Code. This restriction, however, has been disregarded. Peo. ex rel. Van Buren v. 

Sup't, etc., 118 M 145. 

A writ may be made returnable forthwith or on a future day certain as the case requires. Sec. 1239 CPA.

If application for the writ be made to a Supreme Court justice outside of the county of confinement, the same may, in the discretion of the court, be made returnable before any judge of the county of confinement authorized to grant it. Sec. 1239 CPA. A writ directed to the agent or warden of a state prison or to the supervising body of a state institution, must be made returnable before a justice of the Supreme Court or a county judge in the county of detention. If no judge be available, the writ should be made returnable before the nearest accessible justice of the 

Supreme Court or county judge. Sec. 1239 CPA.

When it appears in a judicial proceeding before a judge authorized to grant a writ that a person is illegally imprisoned or restrained, he must issue a writ although no application therefor has been made. Sec. 1241 CPA.

Where

the prisoner is detained for a sum of money, the undertaking must be for at least twice the sum for which the prisoner is detained; in other cases, the undertaking is for one thousand dollars. Sec. 1243 CPA.

In all other cases, the judge who grants the writ may provide that charges for the prisoner's delivery be paid, in which event service is not complete until the fees specified in the certificate granting the writ are paid. Sec. 1243 CPA.

When a writ is allowed upon the application of the attorney general or a district attorney, the fees and security above noted need not be tendered. Sec. 1243 CPA.

Obeyance to the writ without receiving the statutory fees and undertaking, constitutes a waiver thereof. Peo. ex rel. Jenkins v. Kuhné, 57 M 30.

Disobedience Of Writ. Disobedience is punishable as for a contempt of court. Peo. ex rel. Jenkins v. Kuhne, 195 NY 610. 

When a person who has been duly served with a writ fails to obey it without sufficient cause, the court before which it is returnable, upon proof of due service, must issue a warrant of attachment forthwith, commanding the agent designated therein forthwith to apprehend the delinquent official. Upon apprehension and production, an order must be made committing him until he makes return to the writ and complies with any order which may be made in relation to the person confined. Sec. 1248 CPA.

At the time of the issuance of the warrant for the apprehension of the delinquent, or afterwards, the court may issue a precept for the production of the prisoner. Sec. 1249 CPA.

Return. An official other than a sheriff, coroner, constable or marshal, upon whom a writ is served, must obey it and make a return thereto without requiring any bond or the payment of any charges except such as are specified in the certificate allowing the writ.

The return is made at the time and place specified in the writ. 

Sec. 1244 CPA. 

If the writ be returnable forthwith, at a place within twenty

miles of the place of service, the return must be made and the prisoner produced within twenty-four hours after service. A like period is allowed for each additional twenty miles. Sec. 1244 CPA.

The prisoner must be produced unless his production would endanger his life or his health. Sec. 1246 CPA.

A writ may not be disobeyed for any defect of form. Any person upon whom the writ is served is deemed to be the person to whom it is directed, although it is directed to him by an assumed appellation, or by his office, or is directed to another person. The person to be produced may also be identified otherwise than by name. Sec. 1240 CPA.

Contents Of Return. The person upon whom a writ has been duly served must state plainly and unequivocally in his return 

(Sec. 1245 CPA): 

(a) Whether the prisoner was in his custody or under his power at the time the writ was served, theretofore, or thereafter. (b) The authority and cause of the imprisonment. If the prisoner be detained by virtue of a mandate, a copy thereof should be annexed. The original must be exhibited upon the return of the writ. (c) If the prisoner has been transferred, the substance of any mandate may be given if the original is no longer available, together with particulars showing to whom, at what time, for what cause, and by what authority the transfer was made.

The return must be signed by the person making it, and unless he is a sworn public officer and makes the return in his official capacity, it must be verified. Sec. 1245 CPA. 

Proceedings On Return. If neither the attorney general nor a district attorney appear for the people, the attorney for the relator is deemed also the attorney for the people. Sec. 1247 CPA.

A prisoner produced upon the return of a writ may deny under oath any material allegation thereof, or make any allegation

to show either that his detention is unlawful or that he is entitled to his discharge. Sec. 1259 CPA. This is known as the answer or traverse to the return. Thereupon, the court must proceed in a summary way to hear the evidence and may dispose of the case.

When the return shows that the prisoner is so sick that the production of him would endanger his life or health, and the return is otherwise sufficient, the court, if satisfied of the truth thereof, must decide the writ upon the return and dispose of the matter as if a writ of certiorari had been issued. 

If no lawful cause for the imprisonment be shown, an order must be made discharging the prisoner. Sec. 1251 CPA.

If the prisoner be in custody by virtue of a mandate in a civil cause, he can be discharged only in one of the following cases 

(Sec. 1253 CPA): 

(a) Where the jurisdiction of the court which issued the mandate has been exceeded. (b) Where, although the original imprisonment was lawful, the prisoner has become entitled to his discharge. (c) Where the mandate is defective in substance. (d) Where the mandate was issued in a case not allowed by law. (e) Where the person having custody of the prisoner under the mandate is not the person empowered by law to detain him. (f) Where the mandate is not authorized by a judgment, degree or order of a court, or by a provision of law. 

If it appear that the time of his detention has not expired, the prisoner must be remanded if he is detained in the following cases (Sec 1252 CPA): 

(a) By virtue of a mandate properly issued by a federal court. (b) By virtue of a final judgment or order of a competent court, except one punishing him for contempt, or by

virtue of an execution or other process issued upon such a judgment or final order. (c) For a criminal contempt specially and plainly charged in a commitment made by a proper court. 

Pending determination of the proceedings, the prisoner may be committed or placed in such care and custody as circumstances require. Sec. 1257 CPA.

It is discretionary with the court to impanel a jury to decide the issues, although not bound by their findings. Peo. ex rel. 

Woodbury v. Hendrick, 215 NY 339.

If it appear that the prisoner is unlawfully imprisoned, the court must make a final order discharging him. If it appear that he is lawfully confined, a final order must be made dismissing the proceedings. Sec. 1262 CPA. In a proper case, a prisoner may be admitted to bail. Sec. 1255 CPA. 

A writ is either "sustained" or "dismissed." 

Notice Before Discharge. A person in custody by virtue of a mandate in a civil case may not be discharged unless eight days' personal notice of the proceedings, or less notice when prescribed by the court, is given to the person or his attorney interested in continuing the imprisonment. Sec. 1258 CPA.

When the prisoner is committed from a children's court of New York City, such notice must be given to the presiding justice thereof. If committed from a children's court of any county outside of New York City, notice must be given to the judge who made the commitment. Sec. 1258 CPA.

In all other cases, notice of the proceedings must be given to the district attorney of the county where the prisoner is detained and also to the district attorney of the county from which the prisoner was committed. Sec. 1258 CPA. 

Concealing Or Transferring Prisoner. 

Concealment of or transferring a prisoner in order to avoid a writ, constitutes a misdemeanor. Sec. 1270 CPA; Sec. 1789 P.L.

Certiorari Instead Of Habeas Corpus. Upon an application for a writ of habeas corpus, if it appear that the offense for which the person is imprisoned is not bailable, a writ of certiorari may be granted instead. Upon the return, proceedings continue as upon a return to a writ of habeas corpus. Sec. 1261 CPA.

Notwithstanding that a 

Habeas Corpus After Certiorari. writ of certiorari has been issued or returned, the court before which it is returnable may issue a writ of habeas corpus. Sec. 1263 CPA. 

Bail On Certiorari. On a writ of certiorari, if it appear that the prisoner is entitled to be bailed, the court must make a final order fixing the amount of bail.

Upon the production of a certified copy of a court order, or the original order if made by a judge out of court, together with a recognizance, acknowledged by the prisoner and supported by two sureties (householders and worth twice the sum fixed), the judge before whom the foregoing is presented must issue a certificate showing compliance, which entitles the prisoner to his freedom for any cause stated in the return to the certoriari. Secs. 

1264, 1265 CPA.

Warrant Instead Of Writ. When it appears that a person is unlawfully confined and that he will be carried out of the state, or suffer irreparable injury before relief can be afforded by a writ, the court must issue a warrant, reciting the facts, and commanding the immediate production of the prisoner. Sec. 1271 CPA. 

The warrant may also contain a direction to arrest the custodian of the prisoner if the facts warrant. Sec. 1271 CPA.

Upon the return of the warrant, proceedings must be taken as if a writ had been issued. Sec. 1272 CPA.

Appeal. An appeal may be taken from an order refusing to grant a writ and from a final order made upon the return of a writ. Where bail is fixed, the appeal by the people from an order of discharge does not stay a release on bail. A prisoner charged

with a bailable offense must be admitted to bail even on appeal, if he complies with the provisions relating thereto. Secs. 1276, 1277 CPA.

An appeal from an order sustaining a writ must be taken by and in the name of the people. Peo. ex rel. Breslin v. Lawrence, 107 NY 607.

Chapter 17 EXTRADITION FUGITIVES FROM JUSTICE 

Source. Extradition is based, not on comity but upon the strict provisions of the Constitution. Peo. ex rel. Corkran v. 

Hyatt, 172 NY 176; aff'd 188 US 691.

The Constitution of the United States provides (Art. 4, Sec. 

2, subd. 2): "A Person charged in any State with Treason, Felony, or other Crime, who shall flee from Justice, and be found in another State, shall on demand of the executive authority of the State from which he fled, be delivered up, to be removed to the State having Jurisdiction of the Crime." 

Procedural rules and regulations have been enacted by Congress in order to execute the requirements of the Constitution. 

Title 18, Sec. 662 of the United States Code Annotated (Sec. 

5278 Revised Statutes) provides: "Whenever the executive authority of any State or Territory demands any person as a fugitive from justice, of the executive authority of any State or Territory to which said person has fled, and produces a copy of an indictment found or an affidavit made before a Magistrate of any State or Territory, charging the person demanded with having committed treason, felony, or other crime, certified as authentic by the governor or chief magistrate of the State or Territory, from whence the person so charged has fled, it shall be the duty of the executive authority of the State or Territory to which such person has fled to cause him to be arrested and secured, and to cause notice of the arrest to be given to the executive authority making such demand, or to the agent of such authority appointed to re-

ceive the fugitive, and to cause the fugitive to be delivered to such agent when he shall appear. If no agent appears within six months from the time of the arrest, the prisoner may be discharged. All costs or expenses incurred in the apprehending, security and transmitting said fugitive to the State or Territory making such demand, shall be paid by such State or Territory."

In order more effectually to carry out federal mandates relating to extradiction, many states, including New York, have adopted the Uniform Criminal Extradition Act. Secs. 827-859 Code.

Duty Of Governor. While a governor is duty-bound to comply with the constitutional and legislative enactments above noted, it was discovered at an early date that the courts have no power to compel such compliance. Ex parte Kentucky v. Dennison, 65 

US 66. Performance of such duty is left to his sense of courtesy and fidelity. Peo. ex rel. Harris v. Warden, 158 M 945. 13.

Law Of Demanding State Governs. When extradition is sought on the basis of an indictment, the form of pleading prevailing in the demanding state is conclusive. However, when an affidavit is the basis of a warrant, it will be carefully scrutinized and the accused discharged if the affidavit be insufficient, or does not recite the source of the affiant's information and belief, or is too vague and indefinite. Peo. ex rel. de Martini v. 

McLaughlin, 243 NY 417; Peo. ex rel. Kassin v. Sheriff, 149 M

That the indictment in the demanding state is demurrable under the laws of this state, does not entitle a defendant to his release. If the foreign indictment, however inartificially worded, charges a crime, it is sufficient. If a crime is alleged which can be reasonably sustained under the laws of the demanding state, a rendition warrant will be sustained. Doubt is resolved in favor of the demanding state. Peo. ex rel. Hayes v. McLaughlin, 247 

NY 238.

It has otherwise been stated that if by any standard of criminal justice which can rationally be supposed to prevail in the

EXTRADITION 

175 demanding state, the facts set forth state the substance of a crime, the defendant should be surrendered. Peo. ex rel. Gellis v. Sheriff, 251 NY 33.

Procedure. It is the duty of the Governor to issue a warrant for the arrest of a fugitive from justice of another state or territory provided that a requisition has been made upon him by the executive of the demanding state, accompanied by a certified copy of the indictment or affidavit of the crime charged against the alleged fugitive. Secs. 829, 830 Code.

Before the person arrested may be delivered to the agent named in the warrant, he must be taken before a judge of a court of record, who must inform the prisoner of the nature of the process and the cause of his arrest. He must also allow a reasonable time within which to apply for a writ of habeas corpus in the event the legality of the arrest is desired to be tested. 

Notice of the time and place of such application must be given to the district attorney and to the agent of the demanding state. 

Sec. 838 Code. 

A person arrested as a fugitive from justice of another state may waive the issuance and service of a rendition warrant and may consent to return to the demanding state. Such waiver and consent must be in writing, signed in the presence of a judge of a court of record, provided that before the consent is signed the court informs the person arrested of his right to the issuance and service of a rendition warrant and of his right to a hearing thereon. Sec. 851 Code.

A magistrate is empowered to issue a warrant of arrest against a fugitive when supported by an affidavit of a credible person showing the necessary facts. Sec. 842 Code. 

Upon reasonable information that a person is charged in another state with a crime punishable by death or imprisonment for a term exceeding one year, he may be arrested, without a warrant, but must be taken immediately before a magistrate and a complaint made against him. Sec. 843 Code. If the magistrate find that the accused is the same person charged with the commission of the crime and has fled from justice, he may commit the accused for the purpose of enabling

the prosecution of extradition proceedings. Secs. 844, 846 Code.

A person so held by a magistrate may be admitted to bail by a justice of the Supreme Court or a county judge unless the offense is punishable by death or life imprisonment under the laws of the prospective demanding state. Sec. 845 Code. 

Challenging Extradition. The validity of an extradition warrant is challenged and tested by a writ of habeas corpus. 

On a writ of habeas corpus for the discharge of a person held for extradition, the questions to determine are: (a) The identity of the prisoner; (b) Whether, under the laws of the demanding state, the accused is sufficiently charged with a crime. (c) Whether the accused was in the demanding state at the time of the commission of the alleged crime. Peo. ex rel. Plumley v. 

Higgins, 109 M 328.

Whether the accused was in the demanding state when the crime was committed, is essentially a question of fact for the 

Governor to determine. Peo. ex rel. Margolis v. Warren, 248 

NY 609.

The burden of proof rests on the accused to establish that he was not in the demanding state at the time of the commission of the crime. Where the evidence is conflicting, a writ will be dismissed. Peo. ex rel. Steel v. Mulrooney, 139 M 525; Peo. ex rel Hauptmann v. Hanley, 242 AD 257. The burden resting on the accused requires evidence which is practically conclusive. Ex parte Montgomery, 244 Fed. 967.

Where it is conceded, however, that the accused was not in the demanding state at the time of the commission of the crime, a warrant by the Governor is improperly granted, and his action may be reviewed and remedied by a writ. Peo. ex rel. Corkran v. Hyatt, 172 NY 176; aff'd 188 US 691.

Exception. The presence of the accused in the demanding state need not be shown, however, where the acts for which extradition is sought would be punishable by the laws of New 

York. Sec. 834 Code. 

It has been held, in a case based upon a complaint filed by a wife in Minnesota for abandonment, that since the prisoner was

EXTRADITION 

177 never in Minnesota, always having retained his New York domicile, a writ should be sustained. Peo. ex rel. Buck v. Britt, 62 

NYS2d 479.

Trial For Other Offenses. A fugitive from justice, extra dited, may also be tried for a crime other than that charged in the warrant by virtue of which he was surrendered. Peo. ex rel. 

Post v. Cross, 135 NY 536; Sec. 856 Code. 

It is immaterial under what circumstances a prisoner came to or was brought into this state. Matter of Thaw, 167 AD 104. 

Unlawful Extradition. The unlawful extradition of a fugitive from justice constitutes a crime. Sec. 839 Code.
