THE TIME TO PRAY 

To every thing there is a season, and a time to every purpose under the sun. Ecclesiastes 9:1. 

David prayed at eventide, at noon and at dawn, the three visible points of the sun: rising, southing and setting.

Paul prayed night and day, implying that he did not pray to God directly, but through the Mediator, Jesus. He did not face the sun, or any shrine.

BOOK II

Prayer: An Interpretation PART I  

Prayer is an established fact, and has been throughout the ages. Insofar as we have any definite or traditional knowledge, we know that all people, all races, all nations have prayed. He who never prays is the exception.

This universal concept and practice indicates the powerful influence and important factor of prayer in the life and progress of humanity from its cradle until the present time.

Prayer is a human address made to a Higher Power, an invisible and omnipotent Supreme Being, and may be in the form of: 

1. A petition for wisdom, or deliverance, or guidance. 

2. A confession of sin, helplessness, lack. 

3. A humble, earnest supplication for assistance. 

4. An intercession for a friend, for community, for race, for nation. 

5. Adoration, giving glory to God. 

6. Worship. 

7. Praise. 

8. Thanksgiving. 

9. Consecration. 

10. To Bless, or to Curse. 29

These ten forms of prayer may be expressed through one of four modes; as, 1. Mental, as inaudible prayer. 

2. Private, in secret chamber. 

3. Family, at the home altar. 

4. Social, with several others, as in a meeting, or church.

Prayer, regardless of form or mode, to be effective must be accompanied by the proper mental and emotional states to give it life and vitality, so that it may rise and tap the Source of Supply which lies in the inner planes. This proper mental attitude includes constructive desire and holy aspiration. The proper emotional state must be unselfish, compassionate, altruistic, unfolding Love and Gratitude, combined with other ennobling emotions. 

Thus we find the process of prayer involves an intellectual communication between the pray-er and the Source of Supply.

This simple analysis does elucidate prayer, but does not assist us to get a concrete idea of the working principle back of prayer.

We have seen that all prayer is a form of worship, during which the mind is solemnly reverent and peaceful; the voice sings a low, slow, glad chant: the posture even depicts adoration, supplication, while the spirit and soul is refreshed, strengthened and inspired by this contact with the unseen power. 

So men and nations continue to pray. "But," you ask, "just what is prayer?" "Why must the mind and heart be in sympathetic accord?"

"Why does the position count?"

The mystery of the radio helps us to unravel the mystery of prayer. The radio has a sending Station, or Source, and an unlimited number of receiving Stations where benefits are received. In prayer, we have a Source from which the countless millions draw for benefit and mercy. And neither of these two modes of receiving and giving is conducted over visible or invisible lines or wires. 

The radio receiving set is a delicate, finely adjusted arrangement of wires, switches, batteries, tubes, dry cells, condensers, transformers, amplifiers, adapters, coils, dials, loud speakers, aerial and ground equipment, and a cabinet, etc., adapted to collect the vibrations (of a certain rate,) which are received from the air and to reproduce them instantaneously to the listener-in. The radio receiving set is thus seen to be a "tap" to get at the vibrations we cannot hear without this expert aid.

The pray-er is also finely and delicately equipped. 

He has certain definite and specific capacities, faculties and powers which include: "Reason, Self-Consciousness, Independent Choice, and 

Independent, Self-Conscious and Rational Will," * as well as a full set of appetites, passions, emotions, desires, ambitions and aspirations; a physical body with its five senses and nervous systems which is built upon a spiritual body, and then, too, he has a 

*The Great Psychological Crime, p. 328.

Soul-the God-Ray. These are the possessions which constitute the stock in trade of the Ego of the Man in the business of life. 

The separate constituents of the three-fold organization of the Ego (pray-er) must be assembled and in tune to be able to receive the messages which are borne in the stellar spaces for him. And this is accomplished through intense, active, directed desire of the Ego to commune with its Father, an invisible and omnipotent Supreme Being. 

In this light, we see the radio to be a mechanical toy, a plaything, a diminutive object imitating a larger, nobler, more replete blessing to man, and illustrating the necessity for perfection of its parts, and the harmonious regulation of these parts into a well-rounded whole, the real pray-er, Man.

And so we discover prayer is simply TO BE IN 

TUNE WITH THE INFINITE, so that we may go to our Father in adoration; ask for guidance, help, and wisdom; and give praise and return thanks for all the manifold blessings received.

PART II THE OFFICE OF PRAYER 

We have seen that prayer is communion with God, our heavenly Father. And we know that this must be so. History abounds with such records, and the lives of saintly men and women of more recent times also testify to the possibility of becoming active conductors of spirit, recreating the whole nature-the personality, mentality, and individuality alike. And their prayers are answered.

The records, as well as our personal experiences, are replete with instances that our prayers—those involving our most urgent requests, the heart's most cherished desires-are unanswered. 

From these facts we draw the following conclusions: 

1. Harmony in mind, heart and spirit is necessary to contact Harmony, God, and so is a conductor of spirit. 

2. Discord in mind, heart and spirit is antagonistic to Harmony, and so fails to make connection with the Godhead. Therefore, we know that discord is a non-conductor of spirit. 

These conclusions are corroborated directly by our own personal experiences, and indirectly by our world teachers, leaders, seers, and prophets, who realize that transgression of the law-physical, nat- 35

ural, ethical, moral, mental, spiritual or psychicalis fraught with grave danger. The Law of Compensation regulates the penalty which we must pay to the last farthing whether the Law be well-known or unknown. 1 

Socrates bids us to "Know Thyself," and Paul says: "Examine yourselves, whether ye be in the faith; prove your own selves." We are constantly admonished to mend our ways, or modernly speaking, to "get in tune" so as to adjust the possessions of our three-fold constitution into a harmonious whole which will be mutually receptive to the benefits and blessings of God. 

Solomon says, "The Lord is nigh unto all them that call upon him . . . in truth.”

Again Solomon speaks: "The Lord is far from the wicked." ³

This last statement is reiterated many times, by different persons in divers ways. It is an important fact. And from this we draw the conclusion that the penalty for increasing our iniquities is the deepened density of the spiritual body which will bear us down into the pit, away from God. The human wreckage strewn along life's path testifies to the accuracy of Solomon's wisdom.

Various failures and near successes find their way to the psychoanalyst to be relieved of the tedium of self-analysis. There the unpleasant, repulsive or disheartening experience of the past are uncovered, ironed out of the subliminal mind and annihilated. 

The experience of Christians in being "born again" of the spirit of the living Christ is a similar instance. 

Both methods recognize the sins of commission and omission that lead down and down into the

deepest depths of the Soul's experiences. In both, the person's life will be reformed as touching these things if he is sorry enough to quit them. In that case he is "forgiven."

When we are once freed in mind, heart and spirit by either method, a new and more spiritual life is sought, desired. And we are not left to ourselves to find the way. Our need is amply provided for. 

1. Moses gave us the ten commandments as a staff to lean on. 

2. Isaiah advises, "Learn to do well; seek judgment; relieve the oppressed; judge the fatherless; plead for the widow." 

3. The Master, Jesus, in his effort to help us gave us two new commandments, and a host of constructive data for living the Life. 

4. Saint John exhorts us to keep in the straight and narrow way which leads to Peace, Truth, Love, Light, God. 

5. Paul blazed a trail for us, and modern men in modern times, through psychology, are demonstrating the worth of the old-time advice.

Living the Life of righteousness refines our bodies, quickens the vibrations and surely lifts us up into a life of Faith and Knowledge.

When we have in our possessions every needed part to build the greatest radio in existence, we build it. Why do we build it? First, because it is small enough for us to examine, test and understand. 

Second, because we realize the joy and pleasure to be derived from its use.

Now, we have in our possession every part needed to build a Master Man, even to the mortar. We have a pattern perfect in every part. We also have full and complete directions for carrying on the work of building a Temple not made with hands: the 

Temple of Human Character. 

The pattern is the Master, Jesus.

The directions are his teachings. 

The mortar is prayer.

This conception of prayer brings out the particular duty, or function of prayer to be: tuning the mind-raising the vibrations—to be synchronous with those of God, our Father.

Therefore, we know that by prayer, with prayer and through prayer we are enabled to see God face to face; to give praise and return thanks; to make known our requests and to receive our full reward.

Spirit to Spirit. 36 God is a Spirit, and we worship Him in Spirit and in Truth. The spiritual counterpart of man knows

"God screens us evermore from premature ideas.

Our mind is as yet unripened, the time has not yet come when we can behold God, or even get a shadowy vision of Him. Yet God is everywhere, is the active principle in everything; His physical counterpart is well known, appropriated and appreciated; and His Ray is felt, recognized and respected. 

When it appears through the noble actions of a brother man, our knees unconsciously bend, and we hail it with joy; when it breathes through the intellect, we recognize it as genius; when it breathes through the will, we have virtue; when it flows through the affections, it is love. These are the attributes which disclose the nature of the Soul and speak of the employment of heaven.

When we break down the fences which hedge us about we find no screen or wall separating our heads from the infinite heaven; there is no distinguishing mark to show where man the effect, ceases, and God the cause, begins. VIII A WRITER'S REQUEST OF HIS MASTER 

Lord, let me never tag a moral to a story, nor tell a story without a meaning. Make me respect my material so much that I dare not slight my work. 

Help me to deal very honestly with words and with people because they are both alike. Show me that as in a river, so in writing, clearness is the best quality, and a little that is pure is worth more than much that is mixed. Teach me to see the local colour without being blind to the inner light. Give me an ideal that will stand the strain of weaving into human stuff on the loom of the real. Keep me from caring more for books than for folks, for art than for life. Steady me to do my full stint of work as well as I can: and when that is done, stop me, pay what wages Thou wilt, and help me to say, from a quiet heart, a grateful Amen. 

-Henry Van Dyke.

A CONVERTED SINNER'S PRAYER 

O Lord! our Father! who lovest thine own and givest us abundant daily proof of that dear love in constantly enlarging stream of beneficence, inspire our hearts to reciprocal love for thee, so that recognizing these, thy tokens, we may vie with each other in the effort to do what pleaseth thee; thereby enlarging our own happiness each day and hour we live!

Thus drawn closer to thee and sustained by the comforter whom thou hast sent; feeling each moment the tender pressure of thy sympathetic hand wherever we go, we rejoice in the hope that we shall, at no distant day, be one with thee, partaking of thy joy and abiding in mansions which it is declared thou hast prepared for us; walking in the way which saints departed have learned to walk before us; feeling that our earthly journey onward is but an excursion through pleasant pathways to the land of everlasting beatitudes, and that its trials and distresses are but the natural incidents which beset all lines of travel; so that we may thank thee at our journey's end that thy kind hand has guided us, and thy loving care has guarded us, and thy generous smile sustained us and made us glad. 

O, our gracious Father! in thee we trust! The hope we have had gives place to the assurance that we are indeed heirs and children of the promise,

and that our inheritance is with the blessed, through thy dear son, Jesus Christ, whom thou hast sent; with whom it is our privilege to name thee Father. 

May we look upon all mankind as brothers for whom we should pray and labor, that they too may be inspired with love for thee, and learn to walk in the way everlasting. And may we feel it a pleasure as well as our bounded duty to bear their souls' interests in mind throughout our daily walk; teaching and encouraging by our example at all times, and by injunction when opportunity favors, so that we may ourselves continue the Master's work which he began on earth, and thereby earn the great reward which is promised to those who diligently serve thee. 

O! thou omnipotent Creator of all mankind and everything existing, and whose laws the universe obeys, our times are in thy hands. To thee we commit our souls and bodies, with unwavering confidence in thy mercy. Thou has pardoned our sins which we have confessed and greatly bewail. Didst thou punish iniquity, who could stand? Be with us in the hour of death, pass us comfortably over the mystical river, and bespeak for us a welcome on the farther shore where there is fulness of joy and delectable communion with spirits of just men made perfect. 

We ask thy favor, whatever betide, in the name of the Saviour to whom, with thee, O Father, and the Holy Ghost be all honor, praise and thanksgiving, now and forever. Amen! -Author unknown.
