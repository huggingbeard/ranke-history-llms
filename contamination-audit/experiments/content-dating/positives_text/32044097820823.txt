REDISTRICTING FOR CONGRESS 

In view of the probable reduction in the number of Tennessee Representatives in Congress from 10 to 9, there is presented herewith alternate maps and related data which, it is hoped, will aid the General Assembly in its deliberations on this matter. (322)

53 Direct Americans have strong traditions which play an important role in the loyalties, behavior and general outlook of the individual. 

In a major civil matter such as the congressional redistricting of a state, some of those traditions come to the fore. One of the traditions older than the nation itself is the desire of individual Americans for reasonable representation in government. A second tradition relates to the sense of belonging--loyalties to groups and items of common interest with friends and fellow citizens of like circumstance, environment or region. 

To reasonably satisfy both of these traditions the redistricting of Tennessee most likely to be reasonably acceptable to the largest number of people probably will be something short of arithmetical perfection--the arbitrary division of the state into districts purely and precisely on the basis of population. Map I which follows is offered in the light of this general philosophy. 

In the drafting of the map several procedural items were observed:

1. 

Split no county between two districts.

2. 

Avoid separation of residence of incumbent Representative from district which elected him, insofar as possible. 

3. Respect the traditional cohesive quality of the First and 

Second districts. 

4. Divide the remainder of the state as equitably as possible according to population and within limitations of the foregoing enumerated items.

It is not contended that Map I portrays the only pattern for redistricting and it may not be the best. It is offered rather for serious consideration and as a starting point for such adjustments or alterations as may be necessary and defensible.
