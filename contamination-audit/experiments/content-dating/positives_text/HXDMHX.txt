Page three 

I lay in my valley. 

There on a pond 

Swans silently 

Rippled the water, And the pond 

Mirrored the swans, Woods around 

The shining pond, The striving peaks 

Enclosing it, And the sun

Shining on it.— 

Clouds sailed 

In quiet air; 

I watched them passing 

Over my head 

I thought and said: "O clouds, O clouds, No weighing chains 

Will keep you fettered 

To the earth!" 

The clouds would move, The sun would stay. 

The sun would go, And for one moment 

There would stray 

A golden gleam 

Of sunset shadow 

On the seam 

Of the enclosing 

Mountain ring. 

The clouds were gone, The sun had moved. 

The nerveless grey 

Of evening sky 

Now filled the vale. 

From where I lay 

I saw the swans 

Arise and circle 

Into air.

They kept their wings 

In constant beat 

Until they left 

The mountain peaks 

Far back below. 

Like point of gold 

They flew through darkness 

Of the sky, Lit by the sun

Behind the brim 

Of mountain peaks.

The darkened pond 

Was like a crystal, Quiet, still. 

And I would know 

Where, after this, The swans had gone? 

I went away

Through dense grown woods 

In shadowed air 

When all flowers 

Wrapped their faces

In their dainty 

Blossom leaves 

And all the birds 

Had stopped their songs;

When all the trees 

Had gone to sleep, There came out. of 

The falling dusk 

A swarm of thousand 

Glimmering lights. 

They rose and fell. 

Out of their midst 

Arose a white 

And silvery mist. 

Then out of this

A flashing star 

Gave me command: "Walk on this path!" 

And round the star 

I saw the swans 

In circles fly.

And with a knell This sank again 

Into the mist, Into the glimmering 

Thousand lights, Into the night.

I stood there, puzzled, On the path.

The surging tone 

Of forest trees 

Had filled my ears 

Like distant murmur

Of the sea. 

I looked around 

And saw a crow 

Who, in the moonlight

Page four 

Flying, croaked: "You see the moon 

Clothe what is dark 

With silver light. 

Look and see 

How stolen are 

The heaven's lights 

To cover all

The spreading trees With false, and untrue 

Lighted, forms." 

Now, as she croaked 

Into my ears 

This ghastly song 

I looked and saw

All which I'd thought 

As fair and fine 

Lose its false form. 

The silver light

Had left the trees 

And like a mist 

Fell through the ground.

The crow sat perched 

Upon a dead 

Or dying tree 

And with her blackness

Lighted things 

All around us.

It was a bleak 

And desolate swamp 

Which now unrolled 

Itself before 

My wandering eyes. 

I stopped and looked 

Upon the crow. "Tell me," said I, "How deep the swamp, How far this wood?" 

The crow then laughed: "The flashing star 

Has told you that 

You follow but 

Her airy flight, But you are bound 

With earthly bands. 

Tramp on!" The crow 

With flapping wings 

Into the distant 

Darkness passed. 

I shrieked: "Stay here 

You black night's crow!" 

But she heard not. "Why, God," I cried, "Was I left here 

With darkning night?” 

But all was still 

And all was dark, For, with the crow 

Sight disappeared. 

I could not see 

Through anything, I could not feel 

That I was there.

The imperturbed 

And heavy black 

Had stricken me 

Like a collapse 

Of mining shaft. 

Now, as I stood 

There quiet, still, In growing fear 

To make a false, Misleading step, There came out of 

The swampy ground 

A shimmer of 

Phosphoric lights. 

They showed me still 

This ghastly sight. 

With heavy heart 

I turned again 

Upon the path, Which wound itself 

Around the trees, Eternally Meandering. 

At once again

The crow sat on 

A tree and croaked "I am your fate. 

My mother Time 

Sent me to lead 

You from this wood." 

I said: "Away! 

You black night's crow!"

The crow was mute 

And left me there.

I further walked 

Upon the path. 

A whistling sound, Tense, and strangely 

Far away, Had reached my ear.

Page five 

This whistle grew

Into a noise 

Which seemed have size 

Increasing with Velocity, And grew into 

A noisy bawl; 

Then all sound stopped 

As if a ghost 

Had touched the air. 

It was a storm 

Without a sound. 

Rain would not fall

To fill the muddy, Marshy, holes, Or fall upon 

The dying trees 

Who died from thirst 

For cleansing rain. 

And all the trees 

Loomed like gigantic 

Ghost like arms 

Against the sky.

Like purging souls 

In agony 

Against the fiery 

Air they strove. 

I fought for them, For, when I saw

The forest's plight 

It seemed to me 

This was not right. 

But lighting's flashed 

With fiery signs 

Upon the wood: "With these against 

Us you'll not fight." 

I hurled boldly 

Challenge words 

Into their midst: "You are but lights 

Who are a small 

Part of sun's light. 

You try to fight

The trees who have 

With you the same 

Great origin!" 

They answered not, But from the storm 

A whirlwind came.

Its sharpened end 

Moved on the gound. 

It hit me. Then 

I found myself

Within its cone 

And heard there wind swept

Organ sounds: 

"Blessed are but those 

Who fearless strive!"

When this passed by 

I stood like shaken 

From the sky. 

I left the trees 

And turned again 

Upon the path. 

Now, after storm, A stillness came.

The stillness settled. 

And, at once, One of the swans

Appeared above 

My head. She circled 

All around 

My turning head. 

She swooped in front 

Of me, alighting 

On the path, And then gazed keenly 

With her clever 

Eyes at me. 

She started changing 

All her substance. 

And a light Penetrated 

All her being. 

This light grew, Like when the eye 

Had looked into 

The molten sun, And then receded. 

There she stood, A being sweet 

And fairy-like. 

Then she started 

Speaking softly; 

Touched my ear 

With gentle force. 

Not like a flute, Nor like a harp, But like a gentle 

Spring wind's breathing. 

She stretched out

Page six 

Her hands and touched 

My eyes. I looked 

And I adored her. 

Power rose 

Inside of me. 

She was my love.

We touched our palms 

And from the path 

As if on wings 

Rose high and clear 

Into the air. 

We rode the winds 

And saw the world 

As if in distance 

Far engulfed. 

But yet we saw

The world's events 

In our eyes greatly Magnified. 

And this we saw: 

When vulcan's fire 

Was belching flames 

Upon the worth 

Of many cities,

Built and raised On vulcan's walls, The people in 

Them had their day 

Of feast when prey 

Was given to 

The circus beasts. 

When prophet cried: "Displease the gods, The fire will come, You'll be destroyed!" 

They laughed with scorn, Shook fists at him: "A thousand years

Of prophecy 

Had not come true!" 

Here vulcan's fire 

Was in their streets. 

They could not flee. 

The lava trapped them. 

Ash and brimstone 

Struck them down 

Onto their knees, And vulcan's thundring, Fierce glow, laughter

Shook the scene 

Like in a dream; 

All that remained 

Was stopped expression, Frozen form. 

And then we saw

That tanks were moving Through the woods. 

There was a flash,

A dry, hoarse, crack, The tank, with bolts 

Of flame, exploded. 

Little further, Under misty, Dark, oppressing, Autumn sky, Under rock 

And into holes

The people crept. 

Again, again, They heard the thuds. 

Explosions rumbled 

Through the air. 

Then again

The planes were off. 

From their holes 

The people came. "The bomb delayed!" 

They shrieked and ran. 

And then we saw 

Fair swallows trekking 

Over the land. 

They cried: "We are 

Not for the land! 

The air! The air!" 

And more the mist 

Closed on us. 

Then, once, blue light, And back again 

Into the mist. 

I held her hand 

And we ascended, Flung ourselves 

Into the air.

It clove past us 

And from above 

Or from below

No things reached us 

But air rushed past. 

Until at once

We stopped and stood 

Upon a rock.

We climbed from there 

Down lovely hills

Page seven 

Clothed in grey mist

The hill woods murmured 

Haunting songs.

We came from there 

Upon a plain 

But all was twilight, Mist, and still.

At once through shimmering 

Mist sun's ray

Had danced its way 

Into our eyes. 

One beam, then more

They rose and tore 

The mist apart, Whisked over the grass. 

They hit the stream 

Which glistened like 

A silver band.

Sun slowly climbed 

The distant peaks 

Where eagles rose 

Into the air, Hovering there. 

Sun's flooding light 

Revealed to us 

A valley green, And fair, and fine. 

With joyfilled eyes

We wandered through 

This promised land. 

And the sun 

Hurried to 

And forth the meadow 

To awaken 

All the flowers 

And to bring them 

From their bowers 

Of green leaves. 

One, and more, The buds then faintly 

Started coming 

From their screens

Of stems and leaves, Opening them 

Unto the sun. 

Their blossom fragrance 

Filled the air. 

Here and there 

Like tufted colors 

On the plain 

Bushes of roses 

Scattered were. 

And we alighted 

By the stream 

Which clear and fine 

Moved through the plain.

In the dewy, Silent, morning 

Rose a shrill 

And clear tone note. 

A bird gave note 

And with it rose 

The merry din 

Of many more. 

All birds with wings 

And will to greet 

The sun were there. 

A merry sight 

It was to see. 

A rainbow spanned 

Her colored arch 

Across the sky. 

The sun now shone 

With dazzling light. 

I heard a sigh 

Behind. I looked. "Why love, my love?" 

I turned my look,

She was not near 

Nor anywhere. 

All nature round me 

Joy did sing. 

My love was still. 

I went to seek her 

Here and there, Crossing the valley Everywhere. 

The day came on, Its burden light 

Oppressed me dark.

I sat and wept. 

Although all birds 

Round me did sing 

A heavy load 

Was on my brain.

The midday passed, The day did end, And then a star 

Of evening hue

Appeared above 

When I raised 

My stricken head. 

My tear filled face

Page eight 

I saw her there. 

Whom I had loved. 

She called to me! 

And rapture filled 

My tired heart. 

I looked at her 

Until at dawn 

She faded light;

Then crossed the plain 

Towards high cliffs 

There to ascend. 

In front of me

The rock was hard. 

It once grew from 

A fiery, melted,

Cataract. 

Behind me was

The flowing air, Save in the distance 

Far and wide

The peaks enclosed 

The valley's sides. 

And over me 

Against the sun

The eagles cleft 

The streaming air. 

Oft I passed 

The eagles' nests, There for a moment 

I would rest.

But higher I climbed. 

The only thing 

Which eased my sight 

Was mountain flowers' Flowering. 

For mountain flowers 

Are a fair thing,

They are the mountain's Caroling. 

In every crack,

On every stone, Where mountain flowers 

Grew alone, I caught my hold. 

And if the flowers 

Would not have been there

I would not have climbed 

So steep in the air. 

I stopped to kiss 

Of flowers last 

Who clung to stone 

Of mountain mass 

And left below 

Me trees and grass. 

And now the sky 

Had grown more clear, Envolving me

Into the spheres 

Of glowing hue. 

The rock, reflecting, Threw the light 

With beaming glitter 

Into air. 

Then more and more 

The sky expanded, Leaving me 

Alone in midst 

Of growing light.

I climbed, I climbed, I reached the top, I sank, immersed, In deepest light. 

This blinded me 

And took my sight. 

And with a shriek 

Triumphant, beaten, Downward flung I, Me, the mountain, To the valley, While the winds 

Around me sang: "He has seen it. 

It is holy. 

He will see her 

Now ere soon!" 

Now I felt 

A song, a laughing 

Soft and sweet, Immersing in 

My echoing soul. 

I heard Love 

Sing her sweet song.

From the whelming Grief she called 

Me laughing into 

Light of sun, Joy gave us

A beaming Being, Clear sweet air 

Went through our veins, Here to be 

Complete and free!
