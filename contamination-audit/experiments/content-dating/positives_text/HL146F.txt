REPORT OF DRAFTING COMMITTEE 

In response to the directions in the decision filed in this matter on June 22, 1956, requiring the undersigned petitioners to draft and submit to the court on or before September 20, 1956 proposed rules for integration of the bar, the undersigned petitioners, herein referred to also as the "drafting committee”, have drafted the proposed rules and by-laws appended to this report, and hereby submit the same to the court for consideration. 

In preparation for the task assigned by the court the drafting committee, through subcommittees of its members, examined all of the various statutes and rules of court under which bar integration has been effected in twenty-four states. 

The latter statutes and court rules have been recently compiled by George E. 

Brand of the Michigan bar, in a volume published by the American Judicature 

Society, entitled "Bar Associations, Attorneys and Judges." Seven copies of Mr.

Brand's book are filed with this report, to facilitate reference by the court to these statutes and court rules.

After consideration of the precedents the committee selected therefrom certain provisions peculiar to the integrated type of bar organization which have become somewhat standardized by experience, relating to such matters as (i) classes and definitions of membership, (ii) enrollment requirements, (iii) the payment of membership dues and (iv) disciplinary procedure. Using those provisions as guides the committee formulated corresponding provisions deemed suitable for adoption in Wisconsin in relation to membership, enrollment, membership dues and discipline. Hence the proposed rules and by-laws pertaining to those matters are based largely on principles and methods, the validity and practicability of which has been established by experience in other states. 

In other respects the features and activities of the new association which the proposed rules would create conform generally to familiar patterns found in state bar associations of the voluntary type. [3] 91885

In its consideration of powers and duties which might appropriately be assigned to State Bar grievance committees, it was decided by the drafting committee to recommend no change at this time in the statutory functions of the State Bar Commissioners. Hence the authority of the district grievance committees under Rule 10 is limited to the investigation of charges of professional misconduct, and does not exceed the authority of grievance committees of existing voluntary bar associations except in one respect; viz., members of the State Bar would be required to appear before association committees and answer questions in relation to charges of professional misconduct. 

The undersigned petitioners respectfully recommend that the proposed rules and by-laws appended hereto be approved and adopted by the court and they pray for an order accordingly.

PROPOSED STATE BAR RULES RULE 1 ORGANIZATION OF STATE BAR OF WISCONSIN Section 1. Creation of association. All persons licensed to practice law in this state are hereby organized as an association to be known as the "State Bar of Wisconsin", subject to the provisions of the rules hereinafter set forth. 

These rules, which are adopted in exercise of the court's inherent authority over members of the legal profession as officers of the court, may be called "State Bar Rules". The words "State Bar" wherever used in these rules mean the State Bar of Wisconsin. The State Bar may, for the purpose of carrying out the purposes for which it is organized, sue and be sued, enter into contracts, acquire, hold, encumber and dispose of real and personal property. 

Section 2. Purposes. The purposes of the association shall be to aid the courts in carrying on and improving the administration of justice; to foster and maintain on the part of those engaged in the practice of law high ideals of integrity, learning, competence and public service and high standards of conduct; to safeguard the proper professional interests of the members of the bar; to encourage the formation and activities of local bar associations; to provide a forum for the discussion of subjects pertaining to the practice of law, the science of jurisprudence and law reform, and the relations of the bar to the public, and to publish information relating thereto; to the end that the public responsibilities of the legal profession may be more effectively discharged. 

RULE 2 MEMBERSHIP Section 1. Persons included in membership. The membership of the State 

Bar initially shall consist of all those persons who on the date when these rules take effect are licensed to practice law in this state, and after these rules take effect shall include also all those persons who become licensed from time to time to practice law in this state; subject in each case to due compliance with the conditions and requirements of membership. Residence in the state of Wisconsin shall not be a condition of eligibility to membership in the State Bar. 

Section 2. Enrollment. Each person licensed to practice law in this state shall enroll in the State Bar by registering his name with the secretary of the association within sixty days after the effective date of these rules, in accordance with the requirements of the By-laws. Every person who becomes licensed, after these rules becomes effective, to practice law in this state shall enroll in the State Bar in like manner by registering his name with the secretary of the association within ten days after admission to practice. Every change after such enrollment in any member's office address or residence address or principal office location shall be reported promptly to the secretary. [7]

Section 3. Classes of membership. The members of the State Bar shall be divided into two classes known respectively as "active" members and "inactive" members. The class of active members shall include all members of the State 

Bar except the "inactive" members. The class of inactive members shall be limited to those persons who are eligible for active membership but are not engaged in the practice of law in this state and have filed with the secretary of the association written notice requesting enrollment in the class of inactive members. Any inactive member in good standing may become an active member by filing with the secretary a written request for transfer to the class of active members and by paying the dues required of active members. No inactive member shall be entitled to practice law in this state or to hold office or vote in any election conducted by the State Bar. No person engaged in the practice of law in this state in his own behalf or as an assistant or employee of an active member of the State Bar, or occupying a position the duties of which require the giving of legal advice or service in this state shall be eligible for enrollment as an inactive member.

Section 4. Only active members may practice law. No individual other than an enrolled active member of the State Bar shall practice law in this state or in any manner hold himself out as authorized or qualified to practice law. Any court in this state may by special permission granted by it allow non-resident counsel to appear and participate in a particular action or proceeding in association with an active member of the State Bar of Wisconsin who appears and participates in such action or proceeding. 

Section 5. Membership dues. Every active member shall pay membership dues in such amount not exceeding twenty dollars per year, as may be fixed in the By-laws; and every inactive member shall pay membership dues in such amount, not exceeding ten dollars per year, as may be fixed in the By-laws. 

Judges of courts of record not engaged in the practice of law shall be exempt from the payment of membership dues. All dues shall be paid to the treasurer of the association and shall constitute a fund for the payment of the expenses of the association.

Section 6. Penalty for non-payment of dues. If the annual dues of any member remain unpaid at the expiration of ninety days from the time when such dues are due and payable, the membership of such member may be suspended by the board of governors in the manner provided in the By-laws; and no person whose membership is so suspended for non-payment of dues shall be entitled to practice law during the period of such suspension. 

Section 7. Voluntary resignation of membership. Whenever a member of the State Bar in good standing files with the secretary written notice of such member's election to surrender his license to practice law and to terminate his membership in the association, such person shall thereupon cease to be a member of the State Bar, and his name shall be removed from the membership register. 

Section 8. Avoidance of hardship. The board of governors may in any case in which to do otherwise would result in hardship or injustice, permit the retroactive enrollment of members and waive penalties prescribed for delinquency in the payment of membership dues. [8]

RULE OFFICERS Section 1. Titles. Nomination and election. The officers of the association shall be a president, a president-elect, a secretary and a treasurer, who shall be nominated and elected respectively in the manner provided in the By-laws. Only active members of the State Bar residing in Wisconsin shall be eligible to serve as officers of the association. The term of office of each officer shall be one year and until the election and qualification of his successor; except in the case of interim officers appointed by the court to hold office until the annual meeting of the association next following the effective date of these rules. The officers shall receive no compensation for their services. 

Section 2. Duties of officers. 

President: The president shall be the chief executive officer of the association, and shall preside at all meetings of the association and at all meetings of the board of governors. He shall appoint and announce as soon as possible after the annual meeting of the association in each year the membership of all committees for the ensuing year, the appointment of which shall not have been otherwise provided for. He shall be a member ex officio of every standing committee and every special committee.

President-elect: The president-elect shall perform the duties of the president during any absence or disability of the president; and at the expiration of the one-year term of office of the president the president-elect shall succeed to the office of president. In the event of the death or resignation of the president prior to expiration of his term of office, the vacancy shall not be filled and the duties of the office during the unexpired term shall be performed by the presidentelect, and at the expiration of the term the president-elect shall succeed to the office of president in the same manner as if such vacancy in the office of president had not occurred.

Secretary: The secretary shall act as secretary at all meetings of the association, of the board of governors, and of the executive committee, and shall keep a record of all of the proceedings thereof. He shall notify officers and members of the committees of their election or appointment. He shall prepare and maintain a register of all active and inactive members of the State Bar and, subject to direction of the board of governors, shall send out notices of all elections and meetings; and he shall perform all other duties assigned to him by these rules or the By-laws or by the board of governors. 

Treasurer: The treasurer shall receive, collect and safely keep, and under the direction of the board of governors disburse, all funds of the association; and render reports of receipts and disbursements as required. He shall assist the Executive Committee in preparing the annual budget. He shall furnish a surety bond at the expense of the association in such amount as may be required by the board of governors.

Section 3. Delegation of duties. Any duties imposed by these rules or by the 

By-laws of the association upon the secretary or treasurer may be performed under the supervision of such officers respectively, by assistants or employees of the association. [9]

RULE BOARD OF GOVERNORS Section 1. Composition of board. The affairs of the association shall be managed and directed by a board of governors consisting of the officers of the association, who shall be ex officio members of the board, and twenty-eight members elected from the several State Bar districts hereinafter defined. 

Ten of the elected members of the board shall be elected from State Bar District No. 4, four members shall be elected from State Bar District No. 2, and two members shall be elected from each State Bar district other than No. 2 and No. 4.

The rights and powers of the ex officio members of the board shall be the same as those of elected members. 

Section 2. State Bar districts. For the purpose of conducting elections of members of the board of governors, the state is divided into nine State Bar districts, as follows: 

State Bar District No. 1, comprising the following counties: Green, Kenosha, 

Racine, Rock and Walworth. 

State Bar District No. 2, comprising the following counties: Columbia, Dane, Dodge, Jefferson and Waukesha. 

State Bar District No. 3, comprising the following counties: Crawford, Grant, Iowa, Juneau, La Crosse, Lafayette, Monroe, Richland, Sauk and Vernon.

State Bar District No. 4, comprising the following county: Milwaukee. 

State Bar District No. 5, comprising the following counties: Calumet, Fond du Lac, Ozaukee, Sheboygan, Washington and Winnebago.

State Bar District No. 6, comprising the following counties: Adams, Green Lake, 

Langlade, Marathon, Marquette, Portage, Shawano, Waupaca, Waushara and 

Wood. 

State Bar District No. 7, comprising the following counties: Brown, Door, Florence, Forest, Kewaunee, Manitowoc, Marinette, Oconto, and Outagamie. 

State Bar District No. 8, comprising the following counties: Barron, Buffalo, Chippewa, Clark, Dunn, Eau Claire, Jackson, Pepin, Pierce, St. Croix, and Trempealeau. 

State Bar District No. 9, comprising the following counties: Ashland, Bayfield, Burnett, Douglas, Iron, Lincoln, Oneida, Polk, Rusk, Sawyer, Taylor, Vilas, and Washburn.

Section 3. Term. Qualifications. Nomination and election. The term of office of each member of the board of governors shall be four years, commencing at the annual meeting of the association next following his election, except as otherwise provided in the By-laws in respect of members elected in the year 1957. The terms shall be staggered as to expiration so that the terms of only seven members will expire in any year. No person shall be eligible for election to the board of governors from any district unless he is an active member of the association and maintains in such district his principal office for the practice of law. No person shall be eligible to election to the board of governors for two consecutive terms.

The eligibility of any person to serve as a member of the board of governors from any state bar district shall cease upon removal of such person's principal [10]

office for the practice of law from such district. Nominations and elections of members of the board of governors shall be conducted in accordance with the provisions of the By-laws. 

Section 4. Functions. The board of governors shall have general charge of the affairs and activities of the association. It shall have authority to fix the time and place of the annual meeting of members of the association; to make appropriations and authorize disbursements from the funds of the State Bar in payment of the necessary expenses of the association; to engage and define the duties of employees and fix their compensation; to receive, consider and take action on reports and recommendations submitted by committees, sections and the assembly of members of the association at any annual or special meeting; to arrange for publication of an official State Bar bulletin or journal; to conduct investigations of matters affecting the association, or the practice of law or the discipline of members of the association; to fill vacancies however arising, in the membership of the board of governors, or in any office except the office of president, and in each case the person appointed to fill such a vacancy shall hold for the unexpired term; and to adopt by-laws and regulations, not inconsistent with these rules, for the orderly administration of the association's affairs and activities.

The board of governors shall meet at least four times each year. Fifteen members present at any meeting shall constitute a quorum. 

The board of governors shall establish and maintain standing committees having respectively the functions defined in the By-laws, as follows: 

Committee on Legal Education and Bar Admissions Committee on Post-Graduate Education Committee on Administration of Justice Committee on Judicial Selection Committee on Professional Ethics Committee on Public Services Committee on Inter-Professional and Business Relations 

State Bar District Committees on Grievances 

Committee on Legislation

Committee on Legal Aid Committee on Unauthorized Practice of Law Committee on State Bar Bulletin 

The board of governors shall have authority also to create additional standing committees and such special committees as may be deemed desirable from time to time, and to define the authority and functions of such standing and special committees.

The board of governors shall establish and maintain sections for carrying on the work of the association, each within its proper field of study defined in its by-laws, as follows: 

Section of Corporation and Business Law Section of Family Law Section of House Counsel Section of Insurance, Negligence and Workmen's Compensation Law 

[11]

Section of Labor Relations Law Section of Military Law Section of Real Property, Probate and Trust Law Section of Taxation 

Each section shall consist of members who voluntarily enroll therein because of a special interest in the particular field of law to which the section is dedicated. New sections may be established and existing sections may be consolidated or discontinued by the board of governors from time to time. Each section shall be governed by by-laws not inconsistent with State Bar Rules or State Bar By-laws. Section by-laws and amendments thereto shall become effective upon approval of the board of governors. 

Whenever any committee or section of the association expresses publicly any conclusion or opinion not previously approved by the board of governors or by the members of the association, such expression shall indicate that the conclusion or opinion is that of the section or committee from which it emanates, rather than the conclusion or opinion of the State Bar. 

The members of the board of governors shall receive no compensation for services to the association, but they and also the members of committees and the officers and directors of sections may be reimbursed for necessary expenses incurred in the performance of their duties. 

RULE 5 EXECUTIVE COMMITTEE Section 1. Number of Members. Selection. There shall be an executive com mittee consisting of the president, the president-elect and five additional members selected annually by the board of governors from among their number. 

Section 2. Chairman. Minutes of meetings. The president shall preside at meetings of the executive committee and the secretary shall keep minutes of its proceedings. The minutes of each meeting of the executive committee shall be submitted to the next meeting of the board of governors. 

Section 3. Powers. Unless otherwise ordered by the board of governors, the executive committee may exercise all the powers of the board between meetings of the board; but shall not amend the By-laws, make rules or regulations governing nominations or elections, or prescribe regulations for proceedings before grievance committees. The executive committee shall prepare an annual budget for submission to the board of governors and shall perform such other duties as the board of governors may from time to time prescribe. 

Section 4. Meetings. The executive committee shall meet at the call of the president, or upon call of the secretary upon written application of three members, upon forty-eight hours' notice by mail, telegraph or telephone. Five members shall constitute a quorum. Action of a majority of the members present at a meeting shall constitute action by the committee. [12]

RULE MEETINGS OF THE ASSOCIATION 

Section 1. Annual meeting. There shall be an annual meeting of members of the association during the month of June in each year. The board of governors shall determine the time and place of the annual meeting, and shall arrange therefor a suitable program which shall include an address by the president. 

Reports of officers and reports of proceedings of the board of governors subsequent to the last previous annual meeting shall be presented to the meeting. 

Section 2. Special meetings. Special meetings of the members of the associa tion may be called by the board of governors or the president; and shall be called by the secretary whenever he receives a petition signed by not less than 500 members of the association requesting such meeting and specifying the purpose thereof, and the meeting shall be convened accordingly within fortyfive days after the secretary receives such petition. It shall be the duty of the board of governors to fix the time and place of every special meeting, to make suitable arrangements therfor, and to cause the secretary to give notice thereof to the members.

Section 3. Quorum. One hundred active members present in person at any annual or special meeting shall constitute a quorum. No member shall be entitled to be represented by proxy. 

Section 4. Notice. Notice of the time and place of every annual meeting of members shall be mailed by the secretary to each active member of the association or published in the official bulletin of the State Bar at least thirty days prior to the meeting.

Notice of the time, place and purpose of every special meeting of members shall be mailed by the secretary to each active member of the association or published in the official bulletin of the State Bar at least ten days prior to the meeting.

Section 5. Recommendations to board of governors. The members present in person at any annual or special meeting of members of the association, herein also referred to as the "assembly", may consider and adopt any proposal pertinent to the purposes of the association; provided that every such proposal which is adopted shall constitute a recommendation from the assembly to the board of governors, and shall be referred accordingly by the presiding officer to the board of governors for such action as the board of governors may deem proper, except as otherwise required in the case of a direction for a referendum under the provisions of Rule 7, or in the case of a proposal for amendment of these rules. [13]

RULE REFERENDUM PROCEDURE 

The board of governors may at any time by the affirmative vote of twothirds of its membership conduct a referendum vote upon any question of association policy. If the members of the association by resolution adopted by vote of two hundred members present at any annual or special meeting direct the board of governors to take such a referendum, the board of governors shall forthwith comply with the direction. Every such referendum shall be conducted by mail ballot in accordance with such regulations as may be prescribed by the board of governors. The result of any such referendum, as determined by a majority of the votes cast, shall control the official action of the board of governors, officers and committees of the association.

RULE 8 DISBURSEMENTS 

The board of governors shall make necessary appropriations for disbursements from the funds in the treasury to pay all necessary expenses of the association, its officers and committees. It shall be the duty of the board of governors to cause proper books of account to be kept and to procure an annual audit thereof by a certified public accountant. At each annual meeting of the association the board of governors shall present a financial statement showing the receipts and disbursements of the association; and a copy of such statement shall be filed with the Clerk of the Supreme Court and published in the official publication of the association prior to the annual meeting. 

RULE 9 RULES OF PROFESSIONAL CONDUCT 

The rules of professional conduct set forth from time to time in the Canons of Professional Ethics of the American Bar Association, as supplemented or modified by pronouncements of this court, shall be the standards governing the practice of law in this state.

RULE 10 STATE BAR DISTRICT GRIEVANCE COMMITTEES Section 1. Composition and selection. There shall be a State Bar District 

Committee on Grievances, herein called "district committee", for each of the State Bar districts defined in Rule 4, consisting of not less than three nor more than fifteen members of the association residing in such district. The State Bar 

District in which the members of a district committee reside is herein called [14]

the "district of the committee". The members of each district committee and the chairman thereof shall be appointed by the president of the association with the approval of the members of the board of governors in the district of the committee. Initially one-third of the members of the committee shall be appointed for a term of one year, one-third for a term of two years, and one-third for a term of three years; and thereafter the term of office of each successor member shall be three years.

Section 2. Duties. It shall be the duty of each district committee to receive and investigate complaints of alleged misconduct on the part of lawyers residing or having their principal offices in the district of the committee, and also misconduct committed within the district by any lawyer regardless of residence. 

The committee shall have responsibility also, to the extent herein defined, for investigating or causing to be investigated all complaints of professional misconduct on the part of members of the State Bar residing or having their principal offices outside the district of the committee, whenever a request for such an investigation is received from the board of governors, executive committee or president of the association, or from the State Bar Commissioners, or from a judge of any court in this state, or from the district attorney of any county in the state. The committee shall have responsibility also for investigating and reporting on the merits of any petition for reinstatement to the practice of law referred to the committee under the provisions of Sections 6 or 7 of this Rule. 

Section 3. Committee procedure. The work of the committee shall be carried on under the supervisory control of the board of governors. The committee shall meet on call of the chairman; a majority of the members shall constitute a quorum; and each committee member shall perform such part of the investigation in any case as may be delegated to him by the chairman. The committee may also utilize in any investigation the services of investigators and counsel provided by the board of governors. 

Whenever it appears, upon preliminary consideration of a complaint, that the facts do not support a charge of misconduct and do not warrant disciplinary action, the committee may dismiss the complaint without proceeding further, but in that event shall notify the complainant accordingly. If it appears upon such preliminary consideration that the complaint may have merit and is worthy of a further investigation, the committee shall cause the complaint to be reduced to writing, and also to be signed by the complainant if practicable; and thereupon a copy thereof shall be mailed to the person complained of, herein called the "respondent". It shall be the respondent's duty to submit to the committee within fifteen days after the date of mailing such complaint his written answer thereto, containing a full statement of the material facts in relation to the acts of misconduct alleged in the complaint; and it shall be the respondent's duty also, if required by the committee to do so, to appear in person before the committee and answer oral or written interrogatories concerning the acts of misconduct alleged in the complaint; and any deliberate failure on the part of the respondent to submit to the committee his written answer to a complaint, or to appear before the committee and answer interrogatories when requested [15]

by the committee to do so, and any willful misrepresentation or concealment of material facts in relation to the matter complained of, shall be grounds for disciplinary action. Before the investigation is concluded in any case the respondent shall be afforded an opportunity to appear before the committee and to present evidence on his behalf.

If after investigation in any case the committee finds that the facts pertaining to the matter complained of do not merit disciplinary action, it shall file a written report to that effect and shall notify the complainant, the respondent and the secretary of the State Bar that the complaint has been dismissed. 

If after investigation in any case the committee concludes that the facts warrant a complaint by the State Bar Commissioners under applicable provisions of the Wisconsin statutes, the committee shall make a report of its proceedings, including a summary of the material facts and the recommendations of the committee; and such report shall be filed with the State Bar Commissioners and a copy shall be filed with the secretary of the State Bar. 

In the case of any complaint against a respondent residing or having his principal office outside the district of the committee, which is referred to a district committee for investigation, the committee's responsibility shall be limited to investigation thereof within the district of the committee; and the information obtained from such investigation and the files pertaining thereto shall be disposed of as follows: (a) In the case of complaints referred by the board of governors or executive committee or president, the information and files shall be transmitted to the board of governors. (b) In the case of complaints referred by the State Bar Commissioners, the information and files shall be transmitted to the State Bar Commissioners. (c) In the case of complaints referred by any judge or district attorney, the information and files shall be transmitted to the chairman of the district committee for the district in which the person complained of resides or has his principal office, unless otherwise ordered by the board of governors or executive committee; and the latter district committee shall proceed with the matter by completing the investigation and making its report.

Section 4. Hearings not open to public. Files confidential. The public shall not be admitted to any hearing or investigation before the committee in relation to charges of professional misconduct; and all of the committee records and files pertaining to such charges in any case shall be treated as confidential, and shall not be open to inspection by other persons, except in accordance with such regulations as may be prescribed by the board of governors, or as may be ordered by the Supreme Court.

Section 5. Improper conduct of judicial officers. It shall be the duty of each district committee to receive complaints of improper conduct on the part of members of the judiciary within the district of the committee. Upon receipt [16]

of any such complaint the committee shall make such investigation as it deems appropriate, and shall in its discretion report to the board of governors its findings and recommendations for such action as the committee deems proper. 

Section 6. Petition for reinstatement. Investigation. Whenever any person who has been disbarred from the practice of law or suspended for professional misconduct petitions the court for reinstatement, a copy of the petition shall be filed by the petitioner with the secretary of the State Bar; and the board of governors thereupon shall cause an investigation of the petitioner's eligibility for reinstatement to be made by a district committee. It shall be the duty of the petitioner to appear before the committee in person, if requested to do so, and to answer oral or written interrogatories relative to his eligibility for reinstatement; and failure on his part to appear and answer interrogatories, or any concealment or misrepresentation in that regard shall be grounds for denying the petition. Upon completion of the investigation, the report of the committee containing its findings and recommendations with respect to the petitioner's eligibility for reinstatement, and also the recommendations of the board of governors with respect thereto shall be filed by the board of governors with the Clerk of the Court and a copy thereof shall be filed with the secretary of the State Bar; and hearing upon the petition shall be deferred until such report and recommendations shall have been filed. The petitioner shall be required to reimburse the State Bar for the reasonable expense of the investigation. 

Section 7. Petition for reinstatement after suspension for non-payment of dues. Whenever any member of the State Bar applies to the association for reinstatement after having been suspended for non-payment of membership dues, the application shall not be granted if it appears that the applicant's membership dues have been in arrears for three consecutive years, unless reinstatement is ordered by the court. If the applicant in such case petitions the court for reinstatement, the petition shall set forth a description of the petitioner's business and professional activities during the period of suspension and shall state whether or not such activities have included the practice of law and, if so, the particulars in that regard; and the petitioner shall file a copy of the petition with the secretary of the State Bar and shall pay the full amount of membership dues then in arrears, including accrued penalties; and thereupon the board of governors shall cause an investigation to be made by a district committee concerning the petitioner's business and professional activities during the period of his suspension and his eligibility for reinstatement as a member of the State Bar. It shall be the duty of the petitioner to appear before the committee in person, if requested to do so, and to answer oral or written interrogatories relative to his eligibility for reinstatement; and failure on his part to appear and answer interrogatories, or any concealment or misrepresentation in that regard, shall be grounds for denying the petition. Upon completion of such investigation the committee's report containing its findings and recommendations with respect to the petitioner's eligibility for reinstatement, and also the recommendations of the board of governors with respect thereto, shall be filed by the board of governors with the Clerk of the Court; and hearing on the petition for reinstatement shall be deferred until such report and recommendations shall [17]

have been filed. The petitioner shall be required to reimburse the State Bar for the reasonable expense of the investigation. 

Section 8. Petition for late enrollment. No person required by the provisions of Rule 2 to enroll in the State Bar who fails to enroll within one year after becoming eligible for enrollment shall be permitted to enroll thereafter unless enrollment is ordered by the court. If the applicant in such case petitions the court for an order directing enrollment, the petition shall set forth a description of the petitioner's business and professional activities during the period of default in enrollment and shall state whether or not such activities have included the practice of law and, if so, the particulars in that regard; and the petitioner shall file a copy of the petition with the secretary of the State Bar, and thereupon the board of governors shall cause an investigation to be made by a district committee concerning the petitioner's business and professional activities during the period of his default in enrollment. It shall be the duty of the petitioner to appear before the committee in person, if requested to do so, and to answer oral or written interrogatories relative to such activities, and failure on his part to appear and answer interrogatories, or any concealment or misrepresentation in that regard, shall be grounds for denying the petition. 

Upon completion of such investigation the committee's report containing its findings and recommendations with respect to the petitioner's activities during the period of default, and also the recommendations of the board of governors with respect thereto, shall be filed by the board of governors with the clerk of the court; and hearing of the petition for enrollment shall be deferred until such report and recommendations shall have been filed. The petitioner shall be required to reimburse the State Bar for the reasonable expense of the investigation.

Section 9. Committee expenses. Members of any district committee shall be entitled to reimbursement for actual travel expenses incurred in connection with their duties, and the chairman shall be entitled to employ, at the expense of the State Bar such clerical help and reportorial service in any matter before the committee as may be authorized by the board of governors or the executive committee.

RULE 11 AMENDMENT Section 1. Amendment of Rules. Proposals for amendment or abrogation of provisions of the State Bar Rules may be presented to the court by (i) petition of the board of governors, or by (ii) petition of members of the association showing that the changes proposed have been approved by vote of a majority of the members present at two consecutive meetings of the association. Hearing upon such a petition will be pursuant to notice in such manner as the court may direct. [18]

Section 2. Amendment of By-laws. The provisions of the By-laws of the 

State Bar of Wisconsin appended hereto shall be subject to amendment or abrogation by resolution adopted by vote of two-thirds of the members of the board of governors. When any change in the By-laws has been made by the board of governors the secretary shall publish notice thereof, including a copy of the amendatory resolution, in the official bulletin of the State Bar, and he shall file a certified copy thereof with the clerk of this court. A petition for review of any such change in the By-laws will be entertained by the court if signed by twenty-five or more active members of the association and filed with the clerk of the court within sixty days after publication of notice of such change. Hearing upon such a petition will be pursuant to notice in such manner as the court may direct. 

RULE 12 EFFECTIVE DATE OF RULES 

The State Bar Rules and also the provisions of Article I to VII inclusive of the By-laws of the State Bar of Wisconsin appended to these rules shall take effect on January 1, 1957. [19]

PROPOSED STATE BAR BY-LAWS ARTICLE I MEMBERSHIP Section 1. Membership register. The association shall maintain a member ship register for the enrollment of members of the State Bar, which shall contain as to each member a record showing his address, the date of his registration, the class of his original membership and each subsequent change of membership status, and such other information as may be required by the board of governors from time to time.

Every member shall enroll in the State Bar by signing and filing in the office of the association a registration card showing the following information concerning the registrant: (a) Full name. (b) Residence address. (c) Office address. Location of principal office. (d) Date of admission to practice in Wisconsin. (e) Date of admission to practice in any state or states other than Wisconsin. (f) Date and place of birth; and in the case of a naturalized person, the date and place of naturalization. (g) Particulars regarding any previous suspension or revocation of right to practice law in any state or country. 

Every change after enrollment in respect of any of the matters above specified shall be promptly reported to the secretary. Communications from the association to any member shall be sent to the latest address furnished by such member. At the time of enrollment of each member admitted to practice after these rules take effect, the association shall deliver to the new member a copy of the lawyer's oath set forth in sec. 256.29 Wisconsin Statutes, as follows: "I will support the constitution of the United States and the constitution of the state of Wisconsin; "I will maintain the respect due to courts of justice and judicial officers; "I will not counsel or maintain any suit or proceeding which shall appear to me to be unjust, or any defense, except such as I believe to be honestly debatable under the law of the land; "I will employ, for the purpose of maintaining the causes confided to me, such means only as are consistent with truth and honor, and will never seek to mislead the judge or jury by any artifice or false statement of fact or law; "I will maintain the confidence and preserve inviolate the secrets of my client and will accept no compensation in connection with his business except from him or with his knowledge and approval; "I will abstain from all offensive personality and advance no fact prejudicial to the honor or reputation of a party or witness, unless required by the justice of the cause with which I am charged; "I will never reject, from any consideration personal to myself, the cause of the defenseless or oppressed, or delay any man's cause for lucre or malice. So help me God." [22]

Section 2. Membership dues. Every active member shall pay annual mem bership dues in the sum of fifteen dollars except as otherwise hereinafter provided in respect of members under the age of thirty-five years; and every inactive member shall pay annual membership dues in the sum of eight dollars. 

Membership dues for each calendar year shall be paid to the treasurer of the association on the 2nd day of January of such year. The annual dues of any active member under the age of thirty-five years shall be eight dollars during the first five years of such person's membership in the State Bar. In the case of persons admitted to practice law in this state after the State Bar Rules become effective, membership dues for the full calendar year in which such admission occurs shall be paid at the time of enrollment in the State Bar. The board of governors may exempt any member serving in the armed forces of the United States at the beginning of any year from the payment of membership dues for such year provided satisfactory proof of exemption is submitted to the secretary prior to April 1 of such year.

Section 3. Penalty for non-payment of dues. If the membership dues of any member for any year are not paid prior to the first day of February of such year, notice of non-payment shall be mailed to the member at his business address shown in the membership register. If such dues are not paid prior to the first day of March of such year, thereupon a second notice of the arrears shall be sent to the member by registered or certified mail. On the first day of April in each year the secretary shall deliver to the board of governors a list of all members whose dues remain unpaid after notification in the manner aforesaid, accompanied by proof of mailing the required notice of arrears in each case. 

Thereupon the board of governors by resolution shall suspend the membership of each such dues-delinquent member, and shall file with the clerk of the Supreme Court a certified copy of the resolution declaring the suspension, and shall mail a copy thereof to each person whose membership has been thereby suspended. 

Whenever a member so suspended for non-payment of membership dues makes full payment of the amount owing, and in addition thereto the sum of five dollars as a penalty, he shall be re-instated as a member by the board of governors, and the fact of re-instatement shall be certified by the secretary to the Clerk of the Supreme Court. Provided however in the case of any person whose membership dues shall have been in arrears for a period of three or more consecutive years, no application for reinstatement shall be granted unless ordered by the court pursuant to the provisions of Section 7 of State Bar Rule 10. Provided further however, that no person whose membership is suspended for the non-payment of dues shall be entitled to practice law during the period of such suspension. 

ARTICLE II OFFICERS Section 1. Nominations. The president-elect, the secretary and the treasurer of the State Bar shall be elected from a list of candidates nominated in the manner herein prescribed. A committee of nine members to nominate candidates for said offices to be voted on at the next annual election shall be appointed by the president of the association with the approval of the board of governors on [23]

or before the first day of September in each year. Such nominating committee shall consist of one governor from each of the State Bar districts defined in the State Bar Rules. The report of the committee shall be filed in the office of the secretary on or before the thirty-first day of January of the year of the election. 

Such report shall name two or more candidates nominated by the committee for the office of president-elect, two or more candidates nominated for the office of secretary and two or more candidates nominated for the office of treasurer. 

Other persons may be nominated for any of said offices by petition signed in the case of each candidate by not less than fifty active members of the association, and filed in the office of the secretary on or before the second Friday of April of the year of the election, provided that before the filing of such petition a statement shall be endorsed thereon by the nominee to the effect that he consents to nomination for the office designated in the petition. 

Section 2. Voting and canvass of ballots. The provisions of Sections 4 to 8 

inclusive of Article III of these By-laws relating to the election of members of the board of governors shall be applicable also to the election of officers. 

Section 3. Commencement of term of office. Officers of the association shall take office at the conclusion of the annual meeting next following their election. 

Section 4. Other officers. The board of governors may appoint one or more assistant secretaries and one or more assistant treasurers, as may be required from time to time.

Section 5. Location of principal office. The principal office of the association shall be located in the city of Madison, Wisconsin. 

Section 6. Interim officers and governors. The officers and board of governors of the association to serve during the period from the effective date of the State Bar Rules until their successors are elected and take office will be appointed by the Court.

ARTICLE III BOARD OF GOVERNORS 

Section 1. Qualifications of electors. Each member of the board of governors shall be elected by the active members of the State Bar eligible to vote in the State Bar District in which such member of the board of governors has his principal office for the practice of law.

Section 2. Term. At the annual election in the year 1957 members of the board of governors, herein also called "governors", shall be elected in the several State Bar Districts as follows: One governor shall be elected for a term of two years and one governor shall be elected for a term of four years in each of the Bar Districts numbered as follows: No. 1, No. 3, No. 5, No. 6, No. 7, 

No. 8 and No. 9; one governor shall be elected for a term of one year, one for a term of two years, one for a term of three years and one for a term of four years in Bar District No. 2; and three governors shall be elected for a term of one year, three for a term of two years, three for a term of three years and one for a term of four years in Bar District No. 4. [24]

At the annual election in each year subsequent to 1957 the member entitled to vote in each Bar District shall elect a governor for a term of four years to succeed each governor from such District whose term of office expires in that year. 

The term of office of each governor shall commence at the annual meeting of the State Bar next following his election and shall continue until his successor is elected and takes office.

Section 3. Nomination petitions. Nominations for the office of governor shall be by petition signed in respect of each nominee by not less than ten persons entitled to vote for such candidate. Blank forms for that purpose shall be supplied by the secretary of the association on request. Nomination petitions for candidates to be voted on at the annual election in any year shall be filed in the office of the secretary not later than the second Friday of April of such year, provided that before the filing of such petition a statement shall be endorsed thereon by the nominee to the effect that he consents to nomination for the office designated in the petition. 

Section 4. Voting list. On the last Friday of April in each year the voting list shall close for the election in that year. Every active member of the association in good standing on that date shall be entitled to vote in the State Bar District in which his principal office for the practice of law is located, for officers of the State Bar and for the governor or governors for such district to be elected that year. The secretary shall prepare a voting list of all active members so entitled to vote in each district.

Section 5. Mailing of ballots. On or before the first Friday of May in each year the secretary shall prepare and mail two ballots to each active member of the State Bar entitled to vote at the annual election. One form of ballot sent to persons entitled to vote in each State Bar District shall contain the names of the nominees for the several offices of the State Bar to be filled at the annual election, and a separate form of ballot shall contain the names of the nominees for the office of governor from such district. If any such person entitled to vote in such election fails to receive his ballots, or if it appears that any such ballot has been lost or destroyed, a new ballot shall be furnished to him by the secretary. The third Friday of May in each year shall be the last day for voting in such election and no ballots received after that date shall be counted. 

Section 6. Voting of ballots. No ballot shall be counted unless returned to the office of the secretary of the association on or before the last day for voting, in an envelope furnished by the secretary marked "Ballot", which in turn shall be enclosed in a covering envelope furnished by the secretary and addressed to the secretary of the association, containing in a blank space provided therefor the member's name and address. The voter's signature shall not be required.

Section 7. Checking and custody of ballots. The secretary shall have custody of the ballots after they are voted until they are canvassed. As such ballots are received by the secretary they shall be consecutively numbered and segregated as to State Bar Districts from which received, and the names on the cover envelopes shall be checked against the voting list. If the name on the envelope [25]

corresponds with the name on the voting list, an appropriate mark shall be placed opposite the name to indicate that such member's ballot has been received. The envelope containing the ballot shall be retained by the secretary unopened until the tellers canvass the ballots. 

Section 8. Canvass of ballots. The ballots shall be canvassed by three tellers appointed by the president, with the assistance of a certified public accountant selected by the tellers. The tellers may delegate to such certified public accountant the duty of counting and tabulating the votes cast for each candidate and may accept the results of such count as certified by such accountant. The candidate receiving the highest number of votes for each office shall be declared elected. In case of a tie vote the tellers shall determine the successful candidate by lot drawn by the candidates or by the tellers if authorized by the candidates. 

Promptly upon the conclusion of the canvass the tellers shall certify the results to the secretary of the association, who shall forthwith announce the result of the election and notify each candidate as to whether or not he has been elected. 

Upon completion of the canvass the tellers shall replace all ballots in a locked receptacle where they shall thereafter remain subject to the further order of the board of governors.

Section 9. Meetings of board of governors. There shall be a regular meeting of the board of governors in each year at the time of the annual meeting of members of the State Bar, and also at the time of the annual mid winter meeting of members of the State Bar. There shall be a regular meeting also during the month of April and during the month of September in each year. The meetings in the months of September and April shall be on the second Friday of each of those months unless changed to other dates in said months by action of the board of governors. The time and place of every meeting of the board of governors shall be determined by the board except special meetings called by the president.

Special meetings of the board may be held at any time upon call of the president, and shall be called by him upon written request signed by three members of the board. Notice of the time and place of regular and special meetings of the board shall be given to each member by the secretary by mail, telegram or telephone at least five days before the meeting. At any regular or special meeting of the board any business may be transacted which is within the power of the board, whether or not specified in the call or notice of the meeting. Action of the board may be taken without a meeting by resolution signed by a two-thirds majority of the members of the board, provided that such resolution shall have been first submitted by the proponents thereof to all of the members of the board.

Section 10. Members of Judicial Council. Upon expiration of the term of office of each member of the judicial council selected by the Wisonsin Bar Association pursuant to the provisions of sec. 251.181 Wisconsin Statutes, his successor in such office shall be appointed by the board of governors from among the active members of the State Bar, and every vacancy thereafter occurring in such office shall be filled in like manner. [26]

Section 11. American Bar Association delegates. Upon expiration of the term of office of each member of the House of Delegates of the American Bar Association, selected by the Wisconsin Bar Association, his successor in such office shall be appointed by the board of governors and every vacancy thereafter occurring in such office shall be filled in like manner. 

ARTICLE IV STANDING COMMITTEES Section 1. Appointment. Number of members. Term. Each of the standing committees referred to in this article shall consist of nine members, unless a different number be fixed by the board of governors; and the members of each such committee shall be appointed by the president with the consent of the board of governors, for a term of three years, so arranged that the term of office of only one-third of the members shall expire in any year. The chairman of each committee shall be designated by the president for a term of one year. In the event of any vacancy in any committee it shall be filled by appointment by the president for the unexpired term. Members of committees shall serve until the appointment of their respective successors. Unless otherwise specified in these 

By-laws or by vote of the board of governors, a majority of the members of any committee shall constitute a quorum for the transaction of business. Each committee shall keep a record of its meetings and proceedings and shall submit an annual report to the board of governors. 

Section 2. Committee on legal education and bar admissions. This committee shall make continuing studies of the curricula and teaching methods employed in law schools, and of standards and methods employed in determining the qualifications of applicants for admission to the bar; and whenever requested by the State Bar Commissioners shall assist in the investigation of the qualifications of persons seeking admission to the bar. 

Section 3. Committee on post-graduate education. This committee shall formulate and promote programs designed to afford to the members of the State Bar suitable opportunities for acquiring additional professional knowledge, training and skill, through publications, lectures and discussions at regional meetings of association members and law institutes, and through correspondence course study.

Section 4. Committee on administration of justice. This committee shall study the organization and operation of the Wisconsin judicial system and shall recommend from time to time appropriate changes in practice and procedure for improving the efficiency thereof; and in that connection shall examine all legislative proposals for changes in the judicial system. 

Section 5. Committee on judicial selection. This committee shall study and collect information pertaining to judicial selection, tenure and compensation, including retirement pensions, and shall report from time to time to the association with respect thereto. [27]

Section 6. Committee on professional ethics. This committee shall formulate and recommend standards and methods for the effective enforcement of high standards of ethics and conduct in the practice of law; shall consider the Canons of Ethics of the legal profession and the observance thereof, and shall make recommendations for appropriate amendments thereto. The committee shall have authority to express opinions regarding proper professional conduct, upon written request of any member or officer of the State Bar. 

Section 7. Committee on public services. This committee shall prepare and present to the board of governors plans for advancing public acceptance of the objects and purposes of the association; and shall have responsibility for the execution of such plans as are approved by the board of governors. Such plans shall include arrangements for disseminating information of interest to the public in relation to the functions of the departments of government, the judicial system and the bar; and to that end the committee may operate a speakers' bureau and employ the facilities of the public press and other channels of public communications.

Section 8. Committee on inter-professional and business relations. It shall be the duty of this committee to serve as a liaison agency between the legal profession and other professions and groups with whom the bar is in contact in order to interpret to such professions and groups the proper scope of the practice of law.

Section 9. Committee on legislation. This committee shall study all proposals submitted to the Wisconsin Legislature or the Congress of the United States for changes in the statutes relating to the courts or the practice of law, and shall report thereon to the board of governors; and with the approval of the board of governors may represent the State Bar in supporting or opposing any such proposals.

Section 10. Committee on legal aid. This committee shall promote the establishment and efficient maintenance of legal aid organizations equipped to provide free legal services to those unable to pay for such service; shall study the administration of justice as it affects persons in the low income groups; and shall study and report on methods of making legal service more readily available to persons of moderate means, and shall encourage and assist local bar associations in accomplishing this purpose. 

Section 11. Committee on unauthorized practice of the law. This committee shall keep itself and the association informed with respect to the unauthorized practice of law by laymen and by agencies, and the participation of members of the bar in such activities, and concerning methods for the prevention thereof. 

The committee shall seek the elimination of such unauthorized practice and participation therein on the part of members of the bar, by such action and methods as may be appropriate for that purpose. 

Section 12. Committee on State Bar Bulletin. This committee shall assist and advise the officers of the association and the board of governors in matters [28]

pertaining to the production and publication of the Wisconsin State Bar Bulletin, the Wisbar Letter, the Supreme Court Calendar Service and such other periodical publications of the State Bar as may be authorized by the board of governors from time to time.

ARTICLE V SECTION ORGANIZATION AND ACTIVITIES Section 1. Establishment, consolidation and discontinuance of sections. Upon approval of an application for the establishment of a new section, the board of governors, by vote of a majority of its members may establish such a section dedicated to a field of law not committed to any other section or committee of the association. Every application to the board of governors for the establishment of a section shall set forth: (a) The field of law to which the proposed section is to be dedicated, which shall be within the purposes of the State Bar and outside the field of law committed to any existing section or committee of the association. (b) A statement of the need for the proposed section. (c) The proposed by-laws for the government of such section. (d) The names of the several committees, if any, of the proposed section. (e) A list of members of the association who have signified their intention of applying for membership in the proposed section. 

An application for the consolidation of existing sections shall set forth the information required in the case of an application for establishing a section. 

Such an application may be granted by the board of governors in its discretion, by vote of two-thirds of the members.

A section may be discontinued by vote of a majority of the members of the board of governors, but only after notice by mail to the members of such section.

Section 2. Membership of sections. Any member of the State Bar shall be entitled at his election to enroll in any section. 

Section 3. Section officers and council. Each section shall have a chairman and council and such other officers as the section by-laws may provide. The council of a section shall consist of the officers ex officio and such other members as may be provided in the by-laws. No change in the by-laws of any section shall be effective until approved by the board of governors. 

Section 4. Section dues. The members of any section may be required to pay section dues in such amount and for such purposes as the section, with the approval of the board of governors, may from time to time determine. 

Section 5. Section meetings. There shall be a meeting of members of each section immediately preceding or during the period of each annual meeting of the association, as the board of governors may direct. There shall be a meeting of the members of each section also immediately preceding or during the period of each annual mid winter meeting of the association, as the board of governors may direct. [29]

Section 6. Publication of proceedings of sections. The proceedings of any section meeting may be published in the discretion of the board of governors. 

Section 7. Reports. Each section shall submit to the board of governors prior to the annual meeting of the association in each year a report of the activities of the section during the year. 

Section 8. Expenses. Expenditures out of the dues of sections shall be made only by direction of the council of the section; and the treasurer of the State 

Bar shall pay out of such dues only such amounts as the chairman of the section shall certify to have been so authorized. 

ARTICLE VI EXECUTIVE DIRECTOR 

The board of governors may appoint an executive director to assist the board of governors and officers of the association in the performance of their respective duties. He shall devote his full time to the affairs of the State Bar. 

It shall be a part of his duties to assist the chairmen of committees and sections of the State Bar in performing administrative functions incident to the work of committees and sections, as directed by the board of governors or the president. His compensation shall be fixed by the board of governors; and he shall perform such duties as the board may from time to time prescribe. 

ARTICLE VII AMENDMENT 

The provisions of these By-laws shall be subject to amendment or abrogation by resolution adopted by vote of a majority of the board of governors. 

When any change in the By-laws has become effective by such action of the board of governors the secretary shall publish notice thereof, including a copy of the amendatory resolution, in the official bulletin of the State Bar, and he shall file a certified copy thereof with the clerk of the Supreme Court. A petition for review of any such change in the By-laws will be entertained by the court if signed by twenty-five or more active members of the association and filed with the clerk of the court within sixty days after publication of notice of such change. Hearing upon such a petition will be pursuant to notice in such manner as the court may direct. [30]
