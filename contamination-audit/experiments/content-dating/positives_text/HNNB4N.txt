FLARY DEMOCRACY vs. DICTATORSHIP 

LET US THINK of Democracy that makes this little country of ours the best in the Far East. Let us think of Democracy that made our people free for the last 42 years while other nations were suffering from the blunders and the horrors of war. 

You may ask me: From where did our present democracy come?

From our forefathers who, unhesitatingly, gave their lives that we might be FREE! From our beloved patriot, Dr. Jose Rizal, who wrote the rules of Democracy and Liberty for us. From Andres Bonifacio, General Antonio Luna, del Pilar and Aguinaldo who led the great struggle for freedom. From Ricarte who voluntarily is suffering the pains of an exile just for the sake of showing the Filipino people that a free country is a better and happier place to live in. And finally, don't forget Patrick Henry, who exclaimed, "Give me LIBERTY or give me DEATH!!”

It must be borne in mind that the continuous slaughtering of Chinese people by the Japanese, the Italian massacre of the Ethiopians, the Spanish Civil War, the invasion of Poland and Finland by Russia, the conquest of Austria, Czechoslovakia, the Free City of Danzig, the invasion of Norway, Denmark, Holland, Belgium and France, and the present war of Germany and Italy against Great Britain are factual demonstrations which prove that Democracies can not live in the same world with Dictatorships. For dictatorship is, by its nature, a full fledged military government designed to take orders from one man, who can read and write but does not want to, who can speak and bluff a lot but who hates to talk about peace and liberty, so that whatever problem comes must be solved by the brutality of FORCE. Even without the consent of the public, this kind of organization always prepares for war and pillage, and strikes at any time at any place without warning.

WINGS OF PHILIPPINE INDEPENDENCE 

The author, ERNESTO M. JOSE THE CALL 

My dear fellow countrymen: It gives me much pleasure to present this message of call before you. This is the urgent present day call of our nation, the call you must not fail to hear, the call you must not neglect to answer. This is a sincere appeal to every libertyloving, patriotic son of a country that is now about to be free. I beg you therefore, my Filipino brethren, my friends, my countrymen to lend me your thoughts in this significant moment.

We will soon be a free nation. Six years hence, or sooner, Uncle Sam will leave us, as we have fervently asked him to do. We will then be drifting alone, paddling our own canoe, in the midst of the world's 10

troubled seas. It will be a great risk although a daring challenge to our own ability, but it will be a clear chance to realize our coveted dreams . . . and the lost dreams of the blood that dried up in the battlefield in the name of our FREEDOM. 

FREEDOM IS DEAR 

THE WORLD has lost more lives in the cause of the fight for freedom than through any other cause. Such lamentable truth makes freedom the most valuable human possession beneath the heavens. Yes, what a wonderful thing is FREEDOM! For over four centuries our kin had found no rest in their unceasing struggles to regain their freedom, but in vain. Though countless lives had been sacrificed, and an endless flow of blood had been lavishly wasted, this most obnoxious bondage is still holding us, but, thank God, for now we can see that the true light of Freedom is shining upon us, shining brighter than ever before. Like the sun, it is rising constantly higher above the horizon of hope. At last, rejoicing is coming to you and me. A few more years and the black curtain of servitude and restraint that has, for ages, been keeping us in the dark shall completely furl to allow the full, radiant light of LIBERTY to shine upon us. Oh! how grand is FREEDOM! It makes life worth living; in fact, it is life within itself . . . in truth, it is a God-given right to every living creature, so 

-FREEDOM we all must have! (The United States 

Congress has fixed, July 4, 1946 as the date for our complete Independence.)

THE GHOST BEHIND 

BUT OUR enemies are waiting as eagerly as we are for the day of our deliverance, for the advent of our independence. They have viewed with great interest the enchantment of our beautiful land, the untouched mineral deposits, the huge volume of gold, the vast forests of high class timber, the ever growing tropical crops and many other rich resources of our beloved country. 11

Japan is attentively watching and patiently waiting for an open chance to strike. She knows very well that her mighty forces will be able to conquer the Philippines as soon as Uncle Sam steps out. Such is not only my own conception, but it is rather an established premonition of the world; it is a coming event already written in the log book of the Nipponese Program of Territorial Ex pansion, just as Adolf Hitler had marked in plain words, in his book 'Mein Kampf,' the cruel fate of 

Austria, Czechoslovakia, Danzig and Poland. And

Japan could easily realize her ambition unless the Philippines, when her independence comes, shall be fully prepared to assume the great responsibilities of defending herself against the assault of any aggressor. 

Philippines Free Press, Manila, May 4, 1940—With the United States acquisition of the Philippine Islands, Japan's old dream of getting hold of the Islands was set back. (Yes, just SET BACK; postponed for future action.) 

When Secretary of War William H. Taft stopped in Japan on his way to Manila, he and Prime Minister Taro Katsura made an agreement about the Philippines. 

His conversation was later reduced to a 'memorandum of good understanding,' which provided among other things that Japan would not harbor any aggressive designs whatever in the Philippines' as long as the United States was sovereign.

On July 4, 1946, the above agreement will be but another scrap of paper good only for the waste basket. 

Note the last nine words of the sentence above.—THEN WHAT???

WHY JAPAN WILL SEIZE THE PHILIPPINES 

JAPANESE OFFICIALS have been expressing their views with regard to our forthcoming independence. They always declare that they have only a "commercial" interest in our country. Yes, their only interest in us is our commerce. This alibi invites reflection whenever the case of China comes to mind. 

But, shall we believe them? Did they not use on 12

various occasions, the same argument in the case of China? 

I brand their statement as false, and whatever peaceful diplomatic maneuver they use it must be considered as a seemingly honest gesture with traitorous intentions.

After all these years we know the needs of Japan as fully as any person knows the daily needs of the late Dillinger and other notorious robbers and murderers. 

No wise judge will ever trust the words or promises of such dangerous criminals. "A snake is always a snake, and even if it is smiling and wearing a false tooth it is still a snake."

One must judge a man by his actions and a nation by its doing, past and present. Let us look at Hitler, Mussolini, Stalin and Hirohito. Let us compare Germany, Italy, Russia and Japan! What are their differences? Nazism, Fascism, Communism and Imperialism. No difference at all; all ISMS! Like peas in a pod; with one intention... subjugation and massacre.

Japan, from time to time, has been showing her ravenous undertakings. Like Germany, she will never be content or stop until her plan of conquering foreign soil is accomplished. We can no longer believe her politicians. They are just a big bunch of good liars, and to trust them will only bring us a long chain of extreme regret, perhaps a slow death.

There are many reasons why Japan wishes to conquer the Philippines. Here are a few: (1) Our population is barely sixteen millions, approximately 120 inhabitants to the square mile. Japan's is five times as large, about 500 persons are crowding in every square mile of land. Her population is rapidly increasing at the rate of over a million a year. And to them there is only one way left of solving this problem. 

According to the statement of one of her generals "Japan must seek, at any cost and anywhere, territories for the excess of her population." This means conquest and forceful invasion as exemplified in the present case of her fight with China. (2) Our soil is fertile and well adapted to agricul- 13

ture. At present, there are still thousands of acres that are untouched and sparsely populated. The Japanese, we should know, are as much interested in farming as they are in commerce. (3) Our forests are teeming with high class timber -famous all over the world. Japan, who imports about 

15,000,000 yen worth of wood every year will really enjoy to cut down our trees for us-not for our own benefit of course. (4) Our country is endowed with rich mineral resources. The gold output of the Philippines today is one of the greatest in the world.

Our coal reserve amounts to millions of tons. 

The greatest deposit of chromium, one of the most important metals for the manufacturing of machinery, tools and war implements, is in the Philippines. 

We also have the richest iron ore mine in the 

Far East.

These are Japan's greatest deficiencies, and like the Germans, she needs these valuable minerals as badly as any baby needs its milk. 

No wonder that a Japanese officer, who had been visiting our country lately, told me that "Japan would rather have a small Philippines than a big China!" (5) The products of Japan's thousands of factories must have sure markets, otherwise a great part of her people, dependent on these enterprises will starve to death. The Japanese find in the Philippines a warm welcome for their cheap products. (6) Japan wants to monopolize industry and commerce throughout the Far East. But the question is, how could she impose her monopolistic greed without full sovereignity over the place? She knows this only too well and here lies her far-reaching ambition of integrating the whole Orient under her domination. Unfortunately, our "Pearl of the Orient Seas" is within that scope. 14

JAPANESE ARE NATURAL MARAUDERS 

LET US START with a short study of the character of the Japanese people. I have lived and worked with them, played and went to school with them. For several years 

I have associated with them. I know them, their traditions, their ways, their likes and dislikes. 

Go anywhere in Los Angeles, San Francisco, New 

York; in Hawaii, Paris, London, Peking, Japan or

Manila; go any place where you can meet a Jap. Examine him or her from head to foot. His shoes, socks, shirt, necktie, suit and hat-98%, if not 100% are "Japanese made." Go to his house, 100% of the utensils and 

98% of his food are either imported from Japan or produced by Japanese. If a Japanese wants something that is not manufactured in Japan, for instance an automobile, tires or electric appliances, he goes to a Japanese dealer no matter how far the latter is located from his own house. If a Jap wants groceries, he will not patronize his next door grocery if not owned by a Japanese, but he will walk for ten or twenty blocks until he finds a Japanese owned market. 

I have a Japanese family as a neighbor. On the corner of the block where we live is a theater. Whenever they go out for a show they drive about seven miles from our place to a Japanese theater which is showing the same features as the theater on our corner. One day, the family bought a refrigerator and an electric range. I thought that they had purchased them from the big hardware store across the street. I finally learned that they were bought from a Japanese hardware store that is 61 miles away. . . very patriotic people, indeed! 

For the last six years of my residence in the United

States, I have eaten in many different restaurants operated by white people, yes, in hundreds of them. But, did I ever see a Jap in one of them? Please, don't ask me! 

One of my Japanese friends has his milk delivered 16

by a Japanese dairyman, eggs by a Japanese poultryman, fish by a Japanese fisherman, his vegetables and fruits by a Japanese farmer. Another Japanese friend of mine, my co-student at the airplane school, drives about 49 blocks for his gasoline, passing 11 or 12 other gas stations, but not Japanese owned.

In the United States, unlike the Filipinos, the Japs do not have any dance hall where they can enjoy themselves after the day's hard work. Why? I think they hate to spend their money. As far as I know the most expensive suit worn by a Japanese young man costs $35, while the Pinoys, who earn less money than the Japs, wear suits that cost from $50 to $120 each. Even among the wealthy Japanese, big cars such as Packard, La Salle and Buick are very rare; these gaudy limousines are very common among the Filipino boys. 

A Pinoy in the United States will not mind to spend $100 in one night, just to celebrate and be happy; a Jap. will surely grieve to do so. Once they take hold of money, it is "Good-bye Mr. Chips," they see to it that it is theirs and forever if possible. 

Of course, some will say, "Well, that's why in the 

United States, the Japanese are financially in a better position than the Filipinos." I don't deny the truthfulness of that assertion, but what's the use of the grass after the horse is dead? I am proud to say that Filipinos in the United States enjoy every kind of entertainment that rich people do. They wear clothes that give them the reputation as the best dressed men in all the Americas. Do they understand better than the Japs that life is too short? It begins at 20 and ends completely at 60, I guess so... don't you think so? But the Japs, like money. They think that money is God, and that, if they have money they will live forever on this generous earth. And, when he sees his coffin coming he dies with the image of money in his mind instead the thought of the good Creator. What is it? Tradition? 

Custom? GREED?

By branding them as marauders, I do not mean to 17

instigate ill feeling between the Filipinos and the Japanese people. I do not want to provoke trouble. I will state here only true happenings and what history has recorded, no more, no less. It is up to you to find a substitute for the word "marauder."

It is true that from time to time groups of Japanese plunder our coasts, robbing our people of their belongings, crops and domestic animals. These freebooting people do not have any respect at all for the Filipinos, not even for our law-enforcing officers. There are many instances, known to you, to the Japs and to me, to our Government as well as to their Imperial Government, when they fought our policemen and constabulary soldiers along our coasts, and sometimes, before sailing away in their powered boats to Japan they first instruct our arresting officers in the acrobatic way of plunging into the salty sea. Why they do this I cannot understand. Is it for fun? Fun with our law in the Philippines? Or, is it to show us their superiority?

Even in the United States I have observed that the 

Japanese consider the Filipinos as a very low type of human being, in spite of the fact that we have a much higher standard of living than theirs. They think that they are really superior to us just because of the vegetable plantations where the Filipino laborers work, and also most of the cheap hotels are managed by Japanese. 

Even in this land of free and civilized people the Japanese carry on their old tradition of discriminating against people not their own race. They even see to it that their girls, who prefer to associate with the more stylish and more generous Filipino boys, do not do so. 

Of course, there are now many Japanese with modern ideas and ways who ignore any unreasonable criticism and they find their way into the Filipino social circles. 

We are under the American flag and its protection, yet, the Japanese do not have any regard for us . . . can you imagine what will happen to us after Uncle Sam bids us his Last Farewell?

Frankly speaking, we know very well that Japan is 18

a starving world power. Her great army and navy are maintained efficiently not only for home defense but for the purpose of gratifying her capricious instinct, and, particularly, to accomplish her long-time ambition to integrate the whole Orient under her banner. She knows there is no other country in the Far East strong enough to resist her policy of expansion through force. 

She has announced to the world that she must be supreme in the Orient politically and militarily and that, the whites must be driven off from there as Hitler has chased the Jews out of Germany. Please understand that this is the NIPPONESE MONROE DOC- 

TRINE. 

Here are the proofs of Japan's covetous enterprise. 

The seizure of the Ryuku Archipelago in 1609. Later came the seizure of Formosa and the Pescadores, in the year 1895, 13,889 square miles of rich country. Fifteen years later, she invaded Korea, and to be sure of not losing the stolen cow she changed the old brand to Chosen. Fifteen years later they occupied Shantung or 

Kiaochow. Unopposed and exorbitantly happy over their booty they cut their waiting period to seven years, so that in 1932, they ripped off Manchuria from China, and baptizing her, with Emperor Hirohito as Godfather, named her Manchukuo. Then, after resting for a short period of one year they started again on their looting adventure, taking Jehol Province as a prey and annexed it to Manchukuo. NOW? ... Hell with the waiting period! North China, then northeast China, northwest China, north-north China and what not? Out of the continent the Yellow Peril stretches its tentacles to Pratas Reef (between Hongkong and Manila) and

Hainan Island. Then, in 1946, do I have to prophesy where the destructive typhoon shall blow? "The true responsibility of strength is to protect and not oppress the weak." 

Duchess Eleanor of Bavaria, who visited Manila, commented, "Japan is so clever that some day she will be on top of the world."

Aware of all these facts, our leaders must now quit 19

underestimating the danger before us and must not fail to prepare against it. The warning signals are clear and there is still time to get things in readiness. We can not just lean back and wait! . . . and let time shape our own destiny! Only irresponsible persons with the mentality of a child would take such a feeble-minded attitude. We should understand by now, that Japan really needs the Philippines as badly as she needs Manchukuo and other parts of China, not only for commercial reasons but for the purposes of colonization and the exploitation of our rich mineral resources. Here, my comrades, lies the very point we must seriously consider, otherwise, another Ethiopia or Czechoslovakia will be created in the Far East. With this presage in view, may 

I ask you, my countrymen: "Shall we allow Japan to repeat China's painful experiences in our own native land? Shall we let her step in just to trample upon our own rights as she has been and is still doing in the case of the Chinese people? 

Can we allow her to bury our own freedom under the dust of oblivion as Germany did to Poland?" No, my dear countrymen! A million times NO! Never shall we give up the flag again! Once we are brought under the Mikado's might-mark my words-WE ARE

"A nation that does not eliminate the cause of invasion, which lies in unpreparedness, can not escape the dreadful consequences of conquest."

AGAINST SUCH an inhuman and complete annihilation of our national existence, we must cooperate solidly in building up an adequate National Defense Force. 

We respect the rights of other nations and we expect them to respect ours. We do not covet one square foot of soil of any nation and the sublime purpose of this national defense preparation is to insure that no covetous nation shall secure a foot of ours. Therefore we must be so strong that we can face any eventualities. 

Let us not be lulled into a false sense of security by the thought that we will not need a POWERFUL NA- 

TIONAL DEFENSE on account of the United States which may protect us from foreign aggression or because of the existence of the League of Nations which 21

may do as well. This is very far from the truth for the 

United States has troubles and problems of her own. 

We cannot expect her to help our cause. The League of Nations is undependable, not even worth mentioning nowadays for it is as powerless and ineffective as when it was created. It failed in all of its major undertakings. When Germany withdrew its membership over the armament issue and Japan quit on account of the Manchurian dispute and Italy, in spite of its membership, unmolestingly plundered Ethiopia and harassed Albania . . . that was the end of the League of 

Nations. Of the world's seven great powers, four are not members, the United States, Russia, Japan and Germany. Therefore, to join the League when our independence comes, as has always been the talk of our leaders heretofore, and depend upon its insignificant capacity to protect us from belligerents, is a vain hope.

The present conflicts in China and Europe are not new. They are just a repetition of the past when the law was "might is right," and only the hardiest can expect to survive. For today, as in the old days, we can not depend any more on diplomacy alone, nor can we appeal to the "spiritual nature" of "physical minded" people. To them treaties and pacts are worthless. Munich Pact? Of the Disarmament Conferences? Of

"Any competent student of government and history must agree that the very backbone of any government is force-constitution, statutes, laws, treaties and pacts are in the long run mere scraps of papers unless there is back of them means for keeping them in force by military power if and when necessary.

Too cheap to command respect and too easy to burn or tear up and throw away. The most valuable human documents are the Bill of Rights and the United States Constitution. Yet, I regret to say, they are now be- 22

Alright, we are still subjects of the United States; we are still under its jurisdiction but, where do you think we are now? We still owe allegiance to the United

Flag and always ready and willing to fight for that flag anywhere, anytime.

Eventually, as a free people, we must depend upon our own national strength to safeguard our liberty. 

There is no other course left. As a Christian, I hate war as much as I hate the devil' because of its waste and suffering, but we must do our national duty nevertheless, especially since we are living around the sphere of a selfish neighbor who is maddened by a voracious greed for conquest. In this connection allow me to quote here what I was told one day by a young Japanese co-student of mine at the Curtiss-Wright Technical Institute of Aeronautics. "Very soon Ernie, Japan will give the United States her right medicine." I did not get the exact meaning of these words, so I asked him to explain. "Well, just watch out and you will be surprised but you will be glad because you shall work under me 23

then," he said with a tone of assurance in his voice. He was talking about Japan taking over the Philippines and simultaneously driving the Americans out of the 

Orient. 

I just laughed as if I didn't mind what he said, although I know well that it is bound to happen, unless we shall be prepared fully to defend our shores as soon as our independence comes. As a precaution therefore, I believe in maximum preparedness with a power that will be strong enough to crush any invading foe. Only then can our people feel confidently the security of their lives, happiness, peace and, above all their FREE- 

DOM. Nowhere on earth is a prepared nation in danger of any attack or conquest. With the undying words of George Washington, who said, "If we desire to avoid insult, we must be able to resist it; IF WE DE- 

SIRE PEACE AND FREEDOM... IT MUST BE KNOWN THAT WE ARE AT ALL TIMES READY FOR WAR."-(Experto Crede) 

As a man is the sole master of his own fate, so is a nation. A man can best protect his rights by preparing himself physically, mentally and morally. A country can best preserve peace and liberty by preparing a high class, powerful, dependable, concentrated DEFENSE 

FORCE. Failure to do so will surely result in a national catastrophe. I am sure that the majority of our legislators and politicians have no adequate knowledge and information about the defense problem which confronts an independent Philippines. (They may have now, but not before the outbreak of this Hitlerite War). 

As a consequence, their action along this important matter is very slow. As a matter of fact, the present military tactics of our army,which are too obsolete are still maintained. Those old military textbooks which are now in the hands of our soldiers should be torn up. 

Horses for cavalry are but a reminiscent of the past; parachute machine-gunner troops have taken their place, showing that the modern army has gone "airstreamlined." What is the idea of enlisting men just. for "town-to-town" maneuver purposes? Hiking, camp- 24

ing in the field and cooking meals is an old military dogma. The way we are now training our civilians for reserves is but a big waste of precious time, money and energy. Instead of hiking laboriously along the roads and playing hide and seek among the "talahib" and "cogon" grasses and keeping the future soldiers in barracks for morning drills, breakfast, lunch and dinner, put them to work, and I do mean real work; build warplanes, anti-aircraft guns, machine-guns, bombs and munitions. Only then can their training be of real worth to the defense of our nation.

IGNORANCE OR INDIFFERENCE WHICH? 

THE FILIPINOS in the United States, aware of the coming independence and the great necessity of their motherland for an invulnerable national defense, form aviation organizations in almost every big city throughout the continent. Their honorable purpose is to give financial aid to every qualified Filipino, in this part of the world, who is interested in aeronautical works, particularly in the training of aviators, airplane engineers and mechanics, radio operators and aerial photographers that are urgently needed nowadays in bolstering the defense of any independent nation. Because a majority of these patriotic Pinoys, although well educated, are but common laborers; their financial condition makes it quite difficult for them to bring their praiseworthy aims to a great success. Outside help must be obtained. 

The Philippine Aero Club of New York, like many other Filipino organizations in the United States, solicited financial aid from the Philippine government in 1934, backed by several petitions that were sent to 

President Manuel Ouezon, Speaker Paredes and to the

Chairman of the Philippine National Defense. A bill was then introduced in the Philippine Legislature by Representative Jose Uy, urging the Government to extend aid to aviation students in the United States. The news of such an inspiring step, brought great happiness 25

and rejoicing among the Filipino aviation enthusiasts in America, but, when it was presented to the House, to our great disappointment, THE BILL WAS 

KILLED. That ended the pitiful plight of the many patriotic Filipinos in the United States, who, in spite of the great hazards of flying aspired to serve their beloved country.

Nevertheless, it revealed the reluctancy of the administration, showing its complete indifference, or, shall I say, ignorance of most of our old 'politicos' in the building of modern national defense? For, what is there in the college courses that a student can readily apply to flying? The releasing of bombs? To operate a machine gun? The English grammar, calculus, trigonometry, physics, arts, philosophy and other college subjects can not help any man in the art of flying. Ambition, courage and physical fitness are the real things that count in this respect. The present Air Corps Regulations are valid in the United States and we adopted it. So we believe that it is also good in the Philippine Islands and insist that only the college students or graduates can be qualified to be of service in the air for the motherland, as if by natural law they are the only sons who have the nerve to do so. No, sir, you are mistaken. Only quacks who have never flown an airplane will adhere to such a senseless doctrine. Moreover, why go through so many sacrifices, spend so much time and money for a boy to go to school for thirteen or fifteen years and afterwards place him in the BLITZKRIEG'S slaughterhouse where, in a few seconds of combat, he may never return alive again? Let us apply their remarkable brains in better and less dangerous positions where they can serve their native land longer and to a greater extent; make engineers out of them, chemists, miners, expert machinists and mechanics, then "we can go real places" and accomplish something stupendous.

Their brains and college education belong to those big jobs and those jobs belong to them. Any normal person can fly an airplane as good as any degreed flyer can, for, as I have already stated, flying needs only training and practice; high education and technical minds are not at all vital. Therefore, let us not be so strict with our regulation in the qualification for our flying force.

In the United States Army Air Force it is true that educational attainment is an indispensable prerequisite because of existing conditions in that country that are 46

hazardous to flying, but not existing in the Philippines.

For example: The wide area of its continent, infested with great mountain ranges, snow, floating ice, hail, sleet, frost, thick blankets of fogs, ice storm, dust storm, tornado and cyclone. With all of these, it is but proper for pilots in the United States to learn very well the sciences of meterology, navigation, astronomy, radio, and the army and civil air regulations.

It would therefore be wise for us to modify our present Air Corps Regulation as to the qualifications of applicants for enlistment and training in the various stages of aviation. This means that we must enroll in our airforce defense: (1) Every male citizen of the Philippine Islands, whether married or not, capable of bearing arms. (2) Between the ages of 15 and 45. (3) Be of good moral character and in a healthy condition. (4) Can read and write (regardless of educational attainment or height).

From these "enlisted men" we will make the selection of personnel for the different divisions of our Airforce, such as: Pilots, airplane mechanics and machinists, parachutist-machine gunners, aeronautical engineers and chemists.
