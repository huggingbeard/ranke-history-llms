FOREWORD 

'HIS illustrated brochure contains the brief but interesting history of the famous French Shrine of Our Lady in the Grotto of Lourdes. For the most part the entire collection of pictures is a reproduction of personal camera shots and motion pictures taken at Lourdes in the summer of 1936. For some of the older pictures the author is indebted to Mr. Andrè Rebsomen, whose book, "Notre 

Dame de Lourdes," has been an inspiration for this little volume.

The aim of this little volume is to bring the message of the mercies of Our Lady to the minds and hearts of those who have heard the glories of Lourdes, but have never seen the hallowed spot in its Grotto. 

This Pictorial History of Lourdes was first published serially in the 1937 issues of the Parish Monitor of the Church of Saint John the Evangelist, Philadelphia, Penna. It was through the kindness of the pastor of Saint John's, Rt. Rev.

Monsignor Francis X. Wastl, P. R., that the author was privileged to procure many of the pictures on these pages. 

The author takes this occasion to thank all those who by their interest and aid have contributed to the preparation of this work.

May these pages bring to those who peruse them a blessing which will lead them closer-"To Jesus through 

Mary" (Ad Jesum per Mariam).

Chapter I. 

The Name The History of the Name "Lourdes" 

ORE than five hundred miles from the gay capital of France, nestling in the most celebrated valley of the Pau, beneath the shelter of the high southern Pyrenees and nurtured by the winding river Gave, reposes the historic and renowned town of Lourdes. Because of its importance and position in the country of the Normans, it is historic; because of its Shrine to Our 

Lady in the Grotto of Massabielle (Old Rock), it is renowned. From the eastern slopes of the hills of Lourdes, one can see the beautiful bright granite Basilica, as it stands in contrast to the ancient bronze stone castle-fort of the town. The history of the castle is the history of Lourdes. 

From this stately citadel it commands the pass into the 

Page Eleven

seven-fold valley of the Pau. The Romans, the Vandals, and the Visigoths occupied it successively. When the 

Saracens were defeated by Charles Martel at Tours in 732, they invaded the Pau's placid valley. They seized and occupied its acropolis and built a square dungeon within its walls. 

The history of Lourdes continues to the time of Charlemagne's march to Spain in 778, when he halted his army before the old Mirambel* to besiege it. After a few days' 

The Winding River Gave battle Charlemagne demanded the Saracen chief, Mirat, to surrender. The latter steadfastly refused, declaring that he would never submit to a living man. Then the Bishop of Le Puy, a member of the imperial escort, went to Mirat to plead his emperor's cause. "Mirat, as thou wilt not give in to the emperor," he said, "as thou wilt not recognize 'The old fortress in the valley was so pleasing a sight to the soldiers that they cried out: "She is beautiful," hence the name "Mirambel."

Page Twelve

any lord or master, at least obey the most noble of Ladies, the Mother of God, Our Lady of Puy."

By a miracle of grace Mirat promised to become a Christian on the sole condition that his country should forever belong to Our Lady. On the word of the bishop, Charlemagne acquiesced, and Mirat, true to his promise, was baptized. He was given the name Lordus, and the castle was from that time on called "Lordum" or "Lourdes." 

The Old Castle-Fort 

During the feudal days the castle was the commanding encampment of unscrupulous barons. The Treaty of 

Bretigny in 1360 gave Lourdes and its acropolis to the English who ruled it for nearly fifty years. 

During the religious wars, Lourdes, belonged successively to the Catholics and to the Protestants. The abjuration* of Henry IV in July, 1593, put an end to these bloody strifes. 

* Henry IV was converted from French Protestantism to Catholicism.

Under the Rule of Louis XV the castle became a 

A Valley of the Pau state prison. It was used as a prison also during the French 

Revolution and under Napo leon I. During the World War of 1914-1918 prisoners, who were natives of Alsace-Lorraine, were kept in the castlefort. 

So runs the history of

Lourdes and the legend of its naming. Whether this legend be fact or fable is of no great consequence. The important thing is that through the centuries, beginning with the conquest of Charlemagne in 778, from generation to generation there has been promoted in the town of Lourdes a constant devotion to Mary. 

Here from time immemorial the priest and the townspeople gathered in the parish church at the end of each day to recite the rosary. Various guilds for workmen were formed under the titles of Our Lady. Thus the Guild of Our Lady of Grace was for farm laborers, the Guild of Our Lady of Mount Carmel, for slate workers, and that of Our Lady of Montserrat was for bricklayers. These titles can still be found among the ancient records of Lourdes. On great feast days the guilds assembled and made pious pilgrimages to the chapels surrounding the town. Thus devotion to Our Lady was known and practiced in the land of Lourdes long before the famous apparitions took place in the Grotto. Small

The Family Bernadette's Parentage and Childhood 

'HE inhabitants of Lourdes were for the most part simple farmers and hard-working quarrymen. About the middle of the nineteenth century these numbered over four thousand. Among those in the Lapaca quarter lived Francois Soubirous and his wife, Louise Casterot.

Francois, a miller by trade, through his marriage to Louise became the owner and operator of the Mill of Boly (Moulin de Boly). He was inclined to be carefree and indolent, while Louise, his wife, was generous but improvident.

Chapter III. 

The Apparitions Our Lady Appears to Bernadette 

ESET by poverty, Bernadette's family practically had to beg for the meager necessities of life. One day with her sister Toinette, eleven, and a companion, Jean Abbadie, twelve, she went in search of dry wood for the fire. It was the memorable day of February 11, 1858, in the fourteenth year of Bernadette's life. The children wandered just beyond the banks of the river Gave to the base of the Massabielle Rocks. As her companions hurried across the rocky rivulet to the island beyond Massabielle, Bernadette paused to remove her shoes and stockings. Just then she heard a sound like a gust of strong wind. Looking Massabielle-the Grotto before the Apparitions about her she could not see anything unusual. Again she heard the same sound coming from the direction of the Grotto. When she lifted her head, this time, she saw a beautiful Lady standing in the crevice high above the Grotto ground. Frightened by the sight, she knelt on the ground, clasped her rosary in her hands and began to 

Page Twenty-One

pray. The beautiful Lady was dressed in a long white robe bound with a pale blue sash. Her feet were bare, and she stood on a rose entwined rock. On each foot was a golden-colored rose; from her right arm hung a gleaming white rosary strung on a golden chain. She joined in the prayer each time that Bernadette said the "Gloria." As the child finished the rosary, the vision vanished. 

Bernadette and Her Companions

Arising from her knees, Bernadette was surprised to see her two companions moving about unconcernedly, filling their aprons with the dry wood from the ground. 

Upon inquiry she discovered that they had not seen anything or anyone. She confided her secret to them, and exacted the promise from them that they would not tell anyone. However, they soon forgot their promise. That evening, moreover, as her family knelt in prayer, Bernadette began to cry. Afterwards she told her mother of the vision of the Beautiful Lady in the Grotto. The mother, thinking the story the result of childish fancy, forbade her to go again to the spot.

Page Twenty-Two

Three days later the girl begged leave of her mother to visit the Grotto once more. The permission was granted reluctantly. Fortified with a bottle of holy water, Bernadette and her two companions hurried to the rocks of Massabielle. Kneeling on the ground they commenced the rosary. Suddenly Bernadette cried, "She is there!" "She is smiling!" Rising from her knees she approached the vision. 

Sprinkling holy water, she said, "If you are a messenger from God, come." The Lady smiled again and approached 

Bernadette, who knelt motionless, pale, and in an ecstasy. 

This, the second apparition, occurred on Sunday, February 

14, 1858. 

The First Apparition 

Bernadette went again to the Grotto on Thursday, February 18, 1858. This time she brought a blessed candle as a defense against the wiles of Satan. As soon as she arrived at the rock she cried out, "She is here!" It was on this occasion the beautiful Lady asked her to return every day for a fortnight. During the two weeks subsequent to this, apparitions followed at almost regular intervals. In 

Page Twenty-Three

them the heavenly Visitant made known to Bernadette her mission and her message. Occasionally, however, the vision failed to appear. During the apparitions Our Lady spoke to Bernadette in Lourdes patois, the language used by the Pyrenees people in the Valley of the Pau. Bernadette alone could see the vision and hear Our Lady's words. During an examination by the Ecclesiastical Authorities Bernadette told them how Our Lady had taught her a prayer which she said ever after until her death but never revealed its contents. 

The Ninth Apparition-The Miracle of the Spring 

In the meantime, word of the wonders of Lourdes had gone far and wide. Believers and unbelievers, the credulous and the cynical, scientists and scoffers, the saint and the sinner, out of conviction or out of curiosity, crowded the Grotto. The civil authorities tried in vain to halt the throngs; the ecclesiastical authorities withheld authentication of the visions.

Among the many witnesses at these events were Monsieur J. B. Estrade and Doctor M. Dozous, both of whom 

Page Twenty-four

were skeptics at the time. Monsieur Estrade, the official tax-gatherer at Lourdes, later wrote the first authentic history of the apparitions. Doctor M. Dozous, a prominent physician of the town, later, too, testified to his conviction of the supernatural character of what he had witnessed.

OT long after the apparitions ceased, and at the request of Father Peyramele, Bernadette made her home at the town hospice conducted by the Sisters of Charity of Nevers. Relieved of family cares and protected from the curiosity of visitors, her health was safeguarded in the confines of the Convent.

Chapter V. 

The Commission The Message of Our Lady 

IN THE fourteenth apparition which took place on Tuesday, March 2, 1858, Our Lady had commanded Bernadette: "Go to the priests and tell them to have a chapel built here and to come here in procession." Four years later, when the Ecclesiastical Authorities officially proclaimed the apparitions true and authentic,* they began the building of a church on the heights of the Massabielle Rock. 

The Basilica in 1870

May 21, 1866, saw the crypt of the Church opened in the presence of three hundred clergy and approximately fifty thousand other pilgrims. Bernadette was present at this ceremony but not recognized, forgotten in the great crowd, just as she wished to be. "We pronounce judgment that Mary Immaculate, Mother of God, has really appeared to Bernadette Soubirous, on 11th February, 1858, and the following days, to the number of eighteen times in the Grotto of Massabielle, near the town of Lourdes; that this apparition carries with it all the marks of truth, and that the faithful have good ground for believing it certain."-Decree of the Bishop of 

Tarbes, Monsignor Laurence, published on January 18, 1862.

The main church under the title of Our Lady of Lourdes was opened for Divine Service on the feast of Our Lady's 

Assumption, August 15, 1871. In a Brief issued March 13, 1874, Pope Pius IX accorded to it the title of Minor Basilica.* 

In order to accommodate the ever increasing number of pilgrims, another church was built at the base of the rock of Massabielle. This is commonly called "The Rosary 

Chapel." The first stone of this edifice was laid by Cardinal Deprez, Archbishop of Toulouse, on July 16, 1883, the twenty-fifth anniversary of the apparitions.

The Monument of the Allies stands on the left side of the Esplanade. Begun on November 12, 1919, this monument was erected by popular subscription. Often it has been called "The Victory Monument" because it is dedicated to the memory of the French and Allied soldiers who died during the World War of 1914-1918. An exterior altar, where Mass may be said for pilgrims, is a feature of the monument. The crypt of this monument is lined with tablets commemorating the soldier-dead. 

The Calvary of the Bretons

Nearby the Victory Monument is a shelter for pilgrims. 

Its two large halls, one for men and one for women, offer a useful shelter for pilgrims during the interval between the ceremonies. The second floor contains about thirty rooms reserved for the Brancardiers. Directly behind the pilgrims' shelter is the printing office where the official journal, "Le Journal de la Grotte de Lourdes" and the "Annales de la Grotte" are published. On the same side of the roadway stands the Bernadette Museum. It contains many things pertaining to her life as a child at Lourdes. 

One may see on exhibition her rosary, her shawl and copies of her letters.

In the year of 1864 the first organized groups of pilgrims came from the village of Loubajac, a few miles north of Lourdes. This pilgrimage was made in thanksgiving for the healing of a woman from their village. Since then, on each Easter Monday, the first procession of the year to

Chapter XIV. 

The Environs The Town and its Surroundings 

INCE January 27, 1920, the old castle-fort of Lourdes has S bee been used as a Pyrenean museum. Many evidences of the primitive methods of warfare are to be seen within its weather-beaten walls. From the top of the tower there is a magnificent view of the valley of the Pau, the town of Lourdes, and the Grotto. 

The town of Lourdes has normally a population of from eight to ten thousand people. During the pilgrimage season the visitors have increased the number to over a hundred thousand. 

The Old Castle-Fort 

The parish church of the Sacred Heart was established in 1875 by the stern but kind Father Peyramele. Romanesque in style, the church was not completed until September 8, 1903. Father Peyramele, later honored with the title of Monsignor, is buried in the crypt of the church. His white marble tomb is surmounted by a statue of Our Lady of Lourdes.

One of the saddest things in the life of St. Bernadette was her last visit to the Grotto. The tears streamed down her cheeks as she kissed the rock of the Apparitions. When the good Sisters brought her back to the hospice they said
