THE COURT OF COMMON PLEAS OF ALLEGHENY COUNTY ANNUAL REPORT - 1975 This 12th Annual Report of the Court of Common Pleas of 

Allegheny County covers the calendar year of 1975 and enables the judges and the staff of the Court, as well as interested citizens, to assess the work of the entire Court by comparing caseload data with previous years.

A decade ago this Court was one of the first of the metropolitan courts of the nation to compile and publish information about its operations to inform the public as to its problems and successes. Now virtually all large courts issue similar annual reports. 

The Court, seated in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, remains among the leaders in multi-judge courts in keeping its caseload current. This has been possible not only because of the dedication of the judges, but also because of the adoption of modern techniques of case management and caseflow monitoring. 

Since 1969 when the voters of Pennsylvania approved the constitutional amendment consolidating the Courts of Allegheny County, the administration of the Court has sought to integrate the four divisions into one operation so as to utilize the full resources of the expanded Court. The consolidation process will be considerably aided during 1976 with the relocation of the 

Juvenile and Adult Sections of the Family Division in renovated space on the Sixth Floor of the City-County Building. 

This move will mean that all courtrooms will be located in the Courthouse and City-County Building. 

Although still restricted by space and funding limitations, the Court, its judges and its 700 employes are proud that its multifaceted operation is effectively serving the public with promptness and at a minimum cost to the taxpayers. 

The services of the Retired Judges Hugh C. Boyle and 

William S. Rahauser are acknowledged in subsequent pages of this report. It would be proper to add that the Court is grateful for the services of Retired Judges Loran L. Lewis and Charles D.

McCarthy and the services of numerous visiting judges who have presided in the Criminal Division of the Court. -5-

CIVIL DIVISION 

A sharp increase in the number of cases filed in virtually all categories was experienced by the Civil Division during the last two years.

Indeed, 838 more cases were placed at issue during 

1974 than in 1973, a 22.9 percent increase. The number of cases placed at issue in 1975, 4,421 cases, continued at their increased trend and represented a 21.1 percent increase over cases placed at issue in 1973.

The Division would be seriously backlogged were it not for the fact that the judges of the Civil Division managed, despite numerous handicaps and no increase in judicial staff, to dispose of 88 more cases at issue in 1975 than in 1974, an increase in the disposition rate of 2.6 percent.

The increase in the number of cases placed at issue was the largest increase since 1970, when the cases placed at issue numbered only 3,710 as compared with 4,421 in 1975. 

Despite numerous injunction actions and complex cases which required judges to spend unusual amounts of time on individual cases, the Civil Division's increase in filing rate resulted in an increase in the number of pending cases at issue at the end of the year. 

Cases at issue pending December 31, 1975 totaled 4,204, an increase of 8.6 percent over December 31, 1974.

The Commonwealth's new no fault insurance law, which went into effect July 1, 1975, obviously had only a limited effect to date in the reduction in the number of pending auto trespass cases in Court, down 5.5 percent since 1974. Many observers, agreeing that the law is too new to draw final conclusions, believe, however, that the eventual effect, if any, will be on the number of arbitration cases filed during 1976.

By the end of 1975, however, the no fault law has had no appreciable effect on the Court's Arbitration Program. Indeed, a total of 9,974 new cases were filed during 1975, an increase of 649 cases, or 6.96 percent, over 1974. The arbitration program dealt efficiently with this challenge, disposing of 5,918 cases, a total of 489 more than in the previous year, without any increase in the number of arbiters or staff. Furthermore, the average cost per case dropped from $41.59 in 1974 to $38.22 in 1975.

One of the primary tests of the effectiveness of court operations is the average age of disposed cases. During 1975, the average of all pending civil cases was 10.2 months. This is only .8 months higher than in 1974. -6-

CRIMINAL DIVISION 

The Criminal Division, faced with an ever rising volume of incoming cases, managed again during 1975 to increase its total of dispositions over the previous year. During the year, the 

Division disposed of 9,498 cases, up 1,416 or 17.5 percent over the number concluded during 1974.

The disposition rate of the judges of the Division was critical during 1975 because during that year the number of indictments returned by the grand juries increased by 28.8 percent, 1,728 more indictments than in 1974. In 1975, 9,015 cases were added to the Court's workload.

Despite the increase, the Division ended the year of 1975 with only 3,559 cases pending, 550 less than were awaiting trial at the beginning of the year.

Another measure of the Division's effectiveness is the average age of cases disposed of during the year. Excluding ARD cases and cases restored from inactive status, the average number of days for all disposed cases from filing to the first day of trial was 100 days. This compares with 137 days for all cases during 1974. 

ARD cases were disposed of in an average of 152 days from filing to 

ARD hearing during 1975.

One of the major factors in the increase in dispositions during the year was the expansion of the Accelerated Rehabilitative Disposition program (ARD). During 1975, 1,717 defendants were placed on ARD as compared with 1,043 the previous year. 

Aided by a grant from the Governor's Justice Commission, the Criminal Division established a Calendar Control Unit which assumed responsibility for case management including a careful monitoring of the 180 day rule and for preparation of the trial list, a function formerly performed by the District Attorney. The new calendaring process includes an arraignment procedure conducted by non-judicial personnel which has resulted in positive notification of trial to defendants and aided in reducing the number of cases postponed by identifying, before trial, bond forfeitures and defendants who had not obtained counsel.

Perusal of the pages of this report devoted to statistical summaries of Criminal Division activities will disclose that the statistical analysis of the Division's caseload has been expanded and improved.

For example, the Court now is capable of monitoring cases from the moment they are filed in the Clerk of Court's office through final disposition. In 1975, a total of 10,047 complaints were filed in the clerk's office, an increase of 19.3 percent over 1974. -7-

The caseload of the Adult Probation Office continued to rise during 1975, the office handling a total of 7,406 regular probation cases, 2,880 ARD cases and 96 cases referred from other jurisdictions, a total of 10,382. This represents an increase of 

10.9 percent over cases handled in 1974. During the year, the 

Adult Probation Office, aided by a Governor's Justice Commission grant, established five community offices--McKeesport, South Side, East Liberty, Oakland and North Side. These offices permit probation officers to work more closely with probationers and afford the probationers easier access to advice and support. 

ORPHANS' COURT DIVISION 

Virtually, every category of cases handled in the Orphans' Court Division increased during 1975 but the full-time assistance of two retired judges, Hugh C. Boyle and William S. Rahauser, former regular members of the Division, enabled the Court to handle the workload without accumulating a backlog. 

Hearings on petitions for adjudication of incompetence increased from 98 in 1974 to 171 in 1975. in 1975. Decrees entered in civil commitment cases also increased from 588 to 672. The sharpest caseload increase was in the number of adoption decrees entered, 556 being entered in 1975 compared to 430 the previous year, an increase of 29.3 percent. Not reflected in the statistical matter in this report is the millions of dollars involved in the distribution of estates over which the Court presided during the year. 

FAMILY DIVISION 

As in other Divisions of the Court, the number of cases handled in the Adult Section of the Family Division increased again during 1975.

Dispositions in the Adult Section (support cases) during the year totaled 10,653, an increase of 3,013 or 39.4 percent over the previous year. At the end of 1975, only 319 such cases were pending as compared with 374 pending on the same date in 1974. 

Custody and Habeas Corpus cases increased to 226. This is 

102 more cases than in the previous year. Coincidentally, and probably reflective of the inflationary spiral, the Collection and Disbursing Office collected and paid out in support payments $14,899,485.23, a total of $2,306,410 more than in 1974.

The $14,899,000 was disbursed in 199,314 checks, a mammoth task for the outmoded accounting and checkwriting equipment operated by that office. The implementation of a new computer system is – 8-

expected to take over the accounting precedures, considerably increasing the efficiency of the operation. 

The Juvenile Section of the Family Division has experienced a continual increase in its workload over the past four years. Final hearings for deprived and delinquent children increased from 5,553 in 1974 to 6,115 in 1975, an increase of 10.1 percent. In addition to information on final hearings, the Annual Report for the first time has included information on intake dispositions. Intake dispositions of deprived and delinquent children totaled 4,347 in 1975. 

The Court assigned a third judge to the section and appointed a master in an attempt to deal with the increased workload. -9-
