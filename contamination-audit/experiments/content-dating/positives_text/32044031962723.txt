MARRIAGE ESSENTIALS OF CONTRACT OF MARRIAGE, See Madden on Domestic Relations, p. 6. 1. Mutual Consent. 2. Capacity. 3. Celebration. 

Consent-consent must be to marriage-Therefore, agreement to fornicate or jest ceremony insufficient. 

But if relation assumed by man and woman is exclusive and intended to be permanent, it is not prevented from being a valid marriage by some additional or collateral provision varying the usual terms of marriage. 

In Brooke vs. Brooke, 60 Md. 524 and Wimbrough vs. Wimbrough, 125 Md. 619, the Court held that a statement by a man that altho he will marry a woman, he will never live with her, is not sufficient to render the marriage ceremony void, since the intention was that the marriage itself be permanent and exclusive.

In Samuelson vs. Samuelson, 155 Md. 639, the plaintiff alleged that the marriage, before a Christian minister, was regarded by both as merely the engagement contract, contemplated to be followed by a ceremony before a Rabbi which was to be the true marriage ceremony. The defendant denied any such agreement and the Court found that none existed altho it intimated that even if it had been proved the annulment would not have been granted.

Ceremony without consent makes marriage invalid; however from ceremony consent is implied-non-consent must be shown.

In Schaffer vs. Richardson, 125 Md. 88, the Court held that when a marriage has been consummated in accordance with the forms of law, it is presumed that there were no legal impediments to such marriage; and the fact, if shown, that either or both of the parties have been previously married, and that the other party to such previous marriage was still living at the time of the second marriage, the presumption is that the former marriage had been legally dissolved, and the burden of proving that the former marriage has not been dissolved, rests upon the party seeking to impeach the second marriage. 

In the Whitehurst case, 156 Md. 610, it was pointed out that a secret lack of marital intent would not avail that spouse if the other spouse believed marriage was contemplated.

Party to marriage must not only be competent to give his consent thereto, but such consent must be real consent; it must be given voluntarily and upon sufficient comprehension of the essential facts. 

Consent may be wanting because of (a) Fraud; (b) Error; (c) Duress. 

FRAUD-See Madden, Dom. Rel., p. 13.

In executory stage of the marriage agreement, if promise to marry is obtained by fraudulent concealment or such false representations as in law amount to fraud, the defrauded party may refuse to marry.

But if the contract has been executed, that is the parties have actually married before the fraud is discovered, special considerations arise which greatly limit the operation of the general rule applicable where the marriage has not actually been entered into. Mistake as to rank, fortune, character, etc. will not affect the validity of the marriage. 

Fraudulent representations affecting marriage in its essentials will invalidate it, because under such circumstances party committing the fraud cannot make proper husband or wife. 

Fraud affecting essentials of marriage-concealed pregnancy by another man existing at time of wedding justifies avoidance.

Concealment of syphillis in incurable form is sufficient to avoid marriage. 

In Lorenzo vs. Lorenzo, 174 N. Y. 467, the Court held that annulment should be granted where the consent of the plaintiff to marry the defendant was obtained by a fraudulent representation causing him to believe that he was the father of her child, and that but for the fraud he would not have consented to the marriage, and the plaintiff had not, with full knowledge of the facts constituting the fraud, voluntarily cohabited with the defendant. 

Whether a husband can obtain an annulment for fraud when the wife induces him to marry by falsely alleging pregnancy to have resulted from their illicit relations is an unsettled question in Maryland law, although it is doubtful that he may, in view of the rule of the high burden of proof when premarital relations have occurred. See Lurz vs. Lurz, 170 Md. 428.

Fraud Combined With Other Circumstances. 

Although the fraud does not touch the essentials of marriage, but there are other circumstances present, the combina-

tion will be sufficient for avoiding the marriage. Such other circumstances are: weakness of mind, where proper capacity to consent is present; advantages taken of youth. 

Especially is this true where the marriage has not been physically consummated, that is, the parties have not cohabited, and therefore no rights of unborn children are involved. Under these circumstances Courts should be less reluctant to interfere. 

In Brown vs. Scott, 140 Md. 258, a college girl, 18 years of age, was married to a man who represented himself to be a lieutenant in the U. S. Army and as a person of large income, whereas as a matter of fact, he was a confirmed criminal, and was at the time of the marriage being sought by the authorities for a Federal offense. The status of the parties could not be affected by the "intervention of children born or unborn.”

Under these circumstances the Court annulled the marriage. 

See Madden, Dom. Rel. p. 17. 

In Corder vs. Corder, 141 Md. 114, misrepresentations as to past personal history, among others were present. There, defendant husband, then chauffeur for the girl's father, after unsuccessfully attempting her seduction, induced her to go from her home in Pennsylvania into Maryland to be married, on the combined false representations, first, that his past life had been above reproach, and, second, that they were old enough by Maryland law to obtain a license without parental consent (she was 16). The marriage had not been consummated. The Court held this to be a sufficiently strong case of fraud and granted the annulment. 

In Oswald vs. Oswald, 146 Md. 313, the bill alleged that the woman misrepresented to the husband before the marriage, that she was a widow. The parties were married by a Priest of the Roman Catholic Church. After living together for several years, the husband ascertained that she was not a widow at all, but a divorced woman, her former husband being still alive. This she admitted. He filed suit for annulment because the husband contended that under the Roman Catholic Church of which he was a devout member, such marriage was not recognized. The Court of Appeals refused to annul the marriage, and quoting from Boehs vs. Harger, 69 N. J. Equity, page 10, said: “There is no allegation in the complaint from which it can be inferred that the defendant is not able and willing to perform all the duties of the marriage contract, and we must hold that it appears on the face of the complaint that the essentials of the marriage contract, as construed by the great weight of authority, were complied with."

6 The invalidating fraud must be participated in by the party against whom the relief is sought. A party cannot take advantage of his own wrong. 

ERROR, Consent wanting because of-See Madden, Dom.

Rel., p. 9. 

Where one party marries one person thinking he or she is marrying another. Thus if A marries B thinking it is C, intending to marry C, marriage is void. But if A marries B, intending to marry B, but thinking it is C, marriage is valid. 

DURESS, Consent wanting because of-See Madden, Dom. 

Rel., p. 10. 

Where one, due to fear of threatened death or bodily harm or to escape unlawful imprisonment marries another, such marriage will be set aside. The duress must be such as to overcome the will of the person subjected thereto, so as to render his or her consent involuntary.

Duress varies with the character of the person upon whom it is practiced. The question is not whether a person of ordinary courage would have yielded under similar circumstances. 

The imprisonment threatened must be unlawful, imprisonment that is for a crime which party to marriage did not commit. 

In Lurz vs. Lurz, 170 Md. 428, the case was heard on demurrer, but was never prosecuted to a decision on the merits. 

There the plaintiff-husband alleged both his immaturity and that the defendant threatened him with arrest for the nonsupport of the (as yet unborn) child and so fraudulently persuaded him he could be arrested and induced him to marry against his will. The Court held plaintiff's bill sufficient to require an answer, although it was indicated that the premarital relations would impose the very high burden of proof. 

The case emphasized that a threat of lawful criminal prosecution (as for bastardy) would not be an actionable duress, although a threat of an unlawful or an impossible one would be. For a full discussion of Lurz case, see Md. Law Rev., June, 1937. 

In Owings vs. Owings, 141 Md. 416, the man asked for annulment on the ground of duress. The Court did not believe that duress had been practiced, but said that even if there had been duress, he could not use it as a ground for annulment because he had physically consummated the marriage by cohabitation immediately after the ceremony. The Court said:

"A marriage contract which when entered into was voidable merely, may be ratified by the parties by cohabitation as husband and wife after the condition which made it voidable has ceased to exist. This rule has been applied in cases of duress." 

Is Marriage Resulting From Invalidating Fraud, Error or Duress, Void or Voidable? 

Before this question can be answered, one must understand the distinction between the terms Void and Voidable. 

A void marriage is one that never had any existence. It is a mere nullity and its validity may be impeached in any court whether the question arises directly on a suit to set it aside or collaterally, and whether the parties be living or dead. Being a nullity ab initio, no decree of divorce is necessary to set it aside, though expedient and proper. (Patterson vs. Gains, 6 

How. 551.) Void marriages may be questioned even in suits between third parties-such marriages cannot be ratified or confirmed.

A voidable marriage is one that is good for all purposes until a competent tribunal has pronounced a decree of nullity upon direct proceedings instituted for the purpose of setting it aside. The proceedings must be begun and concluded during the life of the parties; if not, it cannot be avoided at all. But when avoided, it is void ab initio, and the children are bastardized. (Schouler Dom. Rel. (1905), Sec. 14.)

Void and voidable marriages defined in Fensterwald vs. 

Burk, 129 Md. 138. See also dissenting opinion in Lurz case, 170 Md. 428.

Examples of absolutely void marriages: bigamous mar riages; marriages between white and black in Maryland; marriages without religious ceremony in Maryland. 

Examples of voidable marriages: Impotency; marriage be tween uncle and niece.

Now, answering the above question, whether marriages resulting from fraud, error or duress, are void or voidable, Judge Harlan, in his work on Domestic Relations, page 8, contends that they are neither strictly void nor strictly voidable. The marriage is voidable by the deceived party without the necessity of divorce or annulment actions, but it is expedient to have such decree. The marriage cannot be said to be an absolute nullity or void, for after the fraud, etc., is discovered, the marriage can be ratified by the innocent party by words, conduct or cohabitation.

In an article entitled "Void and Voidable Marriages in 

Maryland and Their Annulment" appearing in the Md. Law 

Review, April, 1938, p. 238, the author disagrees with the classification of "neither strictly void nor strictly voidable" but contends that marriages resulting from lack of marital intention, insanity without complete adjudication, intoxication, fraud, error and duress are voidable. See Madden, Dom. Rel., p. 20.

For a full discussion of void and voidable marriages, see 

L. R. A., 1916 C, 690.

CAPACITY, Essentials of Contract of Marriage. 

In England, both ecclesiastical and common law courts had jurisdiction to pass upon the validity of marriages. 

Canonical Impediments (in ecclesiastical courts-divine law): (a) pre-contract; (b) relationship by blood-consanguinity; (c) relationship by marriage-affinity; (d) physical incapacity-impotence. 

By pre-contract is meant, where a party to a marriage was under a prior agreement to marry a third person or had already married a third person but not according to the forms prescribed by the ecclesiastical law-now abolished in England and never recognized in this country. 

Marriage with canonical impediments were regarded as valid for all purposes unless sentence of separation in spiritual court was actually made during the life of both parties. 

Civil Impediments (legal-not supposed to know the divine law): (a) existing prior marriage; (b) want of age; (c) want of consent of parents or guardians; (d) want of reason. 

Impediments to Marriage Under the Maryland Law. 

(a) Relationship by blood (consanguinity), or by marriage (affinity); (b) physical incapacity (impotence); (c) an existing prior marriage; (d) want of age; (e) want of reason (mental incapacity); (f) race.

RELATIONSHIP, (a)—See Madden, Dom. Rel., p. 33. 

Code, Art. 62, Sec. 2, declares particular degrees of relation ship by consanguinity or affinity, within which marriages are prohibited. Marriages not prohibited therein are valid.

Marriages nearer than first cousins are invalid, because Sec. 

2 provides that a man shall not marry his brother's or sister's daughter (uncle and niece). See Opinion of Atty. Genl., Daily 

Record, 11/17/39.

No ruling, as yet, whether adoptive relationships come within prohibited degrees, but Atty.-Genl. has ruled that relationships of the half-blood come within the enumerated degrees (half-uncle and half-niece). 

By Art. 27, Sec. 314, "every person who shall knowingly have carnal knowledge of another person, being within the degrees of consanguinity within which marriages are prohibited by law in this state, shall be deemed guilty of felony 

(See also Art. 27, Secs. 355-359-Marrying Unlawfully.) 

* * Although Sec. 1 of Art. 62 provides that all marriages mentioned in Sec. 2 of said Article shall be void, Court held in 

Fensterwald vs. Burk, 129 Md. 138 (citing Harrison vs. State, 

22 Md. +68), that the marriage of uncle and niece is not incestuous and under the laws of Maryland is not void but voidable. 

It would appear from Art. 27, Sec. 314 (supra) that all marriages mentioned in Art. 62, Sec. 2, are incestuous, but the Court held in the case of Fensterwald vs. Burk, 129 Md. 

138, that such a marriage (between uncle and niece) was not incestuous according to the generally accepted opinion of Christiandom which relates only to persons in direct line of consanguinity and brothers and sisters.

In the Fensterwald case, the parties were married in Rhode Island and then came here to live, and the Court held that as long as their marriage was valid in Rhode Island, the place of performance, the marriage would be regarded as valid here. 

Art. 62, Sec. 14, gives jurisdiction to the county courts and to the Superior Court of Baltimore City, upon petition of either party, to determine the validity of any marriage contrary to the table mentioned in Sec. 2 of said Article. County courts and Criminal Court of Baltimore City have jurisdiction of criminal prosecutions of these violations. 

(See Opinion of Judge Leser in Darling case, Circuit Court 

No. 2, Daily Record, 12/27/37, holding that a suit to annul a bigamous marriage must be brought in the Superior Court.) 

Marriage procured by fraud, duress, etc., may be set aside by the equity courts of Maryland under their general powers to set aside contracts so affected. 

Wimbrough vs. Wimbrough, 125 Md. 621.

PHYSICAL INCAPACITY, (b)-See Madden, Dom. Rel., p. 36.

Parties must be capable, at time of marriage, of normal sexual intercourse. 

By impotency is meant, not mere barrenness or sterility, but permanent incapacity preventing complete and natural sexual intercourse. J. G. vs. H. G., 33 Md. 401.

Incapacity must be incurable and must have existed at time of marriage; must have been unknown by complainant. 

Impotency renders marriage strictly voidable. It is valid. until avoided. Impotency is cause for divorce. 

But fraudulent concealment of sterility might be held to work an actionable fraud.

EXISTING PRIOR MARRIAGE, (c)-See Madden, Dom. 

Rel., p. 39.

Person once married cannot validly marry again unless the first marriage is (1) void; (2) voidable and duly avoided; (3) dissolved by absolute divorce or death. 

Second marriage is strictly void. No decree is necessary to set it aside and its invalidity may be inquired into directly or collaterally, during the lifetime or after the death of either of the parties, and this rule applies even though the second marriage was entered into in good faith and in the reasonable belief that former spouse was dead.

By Art. 27, Sec. 23, as amended by Acts of 1937, Chap. 142, when party has been absent from domicile unheard of for seven years, and the other party to the marriage believing the absentee dead, marries again, no bigamy. Act also provides for loss of dower rights, etc., in case of bigamous marriages.

If, however, absent party was in fact alive, second marriage is void. 

INFANCY, (d) Want of Age-See Madden, Dom. Rel., p. 28.

At common law, the age of consent to make a binding marriage, was fourteen for males and twelve for females.

By the Acts of 1939, Chap. 728, it is unlawful for any female below sixteen or any male below eighteen to marry, except on the certificate of a physician which shall be presented with

the application for the marriage license, to the effect that the girl is pregnant, or for any female between the ages of sixteen and eighteen years, or for any male under twenty-one to marry unless parent or guardian assent thereto. Penalties for violations are provided.

In executory contract to marry, the infant, either male or female below twenty-one, may withdraw from contract, since capacity to contract as well as capacity to marry are necessary. 

MENTAL INCAPACITY, (e) Want of Reason—See Madden, 

Dom. Rel., p. 22. 

As to mental capacity, the test is: Did the alleged insane person possess sufficient mental power to have understood in a reasonable manner the nature and effect of the marriage contract and the rights and obligations arising therefrom? 

See Elfont case, 161 Md. 458.

Marriage does not require the same degree of mental capacity as other contracts.

Want of reason which will invalidate marriage, must exist at the time of the marriage. Subsequent insanity is no cause for divorce. 

In Elfont vs. Elfont, 161 Md. 458, suit was instituted four years after the marriage by the father of the husband, for annulment, altho the husband himself was opposed to having the marriage set aside. The evidence disclosed that the son was declared to be incompetent by three doctors of the Veterans' Administration, he being a veteran of the World War, and he was awarded compensation of $150.00 per month. 

Two years later he married the defendant. Court held that there was not sufficient evidence to show that he did not understand the nature of the marriage contract and the legal consequences deducible therefrom, and therefore upheld the mar riage. (Such a marriage was, at its worst, only voidable and could be ratified by cohabitation in a lucid interval.)

In the Elfont case, supra, the question was raised whether any court of any state other than the one where the marriage was entered into, can annul a marriage. The Court did not decide the question, however, as it was raised for the first time on appeal and therefore, too late.

But Alexander British Statutes, page 757 (15 George II, c.

30) provides that where a lunatic whose insanity has been

established by inquisition, marries before he shall be declared sane, "every such marriage shall be null and void to all intents and purposes whatsoever." This Statute is in force in Maryland.

Drunkenness: Drunkenness must be of such nature that party affected thereby is unable to comprehend what he or she is doing. 

In the case of Montgomery vs. U'Nertle, 143 Md. 200, the plaintiff, a boy of 20, married a chorus girl of 16, she being a member of the Ziegfield Follies. Immediately thereafter, he filed suit for annulment on the ground that he was intoxicated at the time of the marriage. The Court said “ * ** to constitute a valid marriage, there must be an understanding and appreciation of what the ceremony was which was being gone through with, and what were the legal consequences naturally deductible therefrom." (Cohabitation was denied by the plaintiff). Court found plaintiff was sufficiently intoxicated and annulled the marriage.

Want of sufficient mental capacity renders the marriage neither strictly void nor strictly voidable. 

See Harlan, Dom. Rel. 15. 

RACE, (f)—See Madden, Dom. Rel., p. 38. 

Difference in race was no disqualification at common law. 

But by Art. 27, Sec. 358, a minister may be fined for joining in marriage any negro with a white person, but the marriage up to the time of the passage of the Acts of 1884, Chap. 264, was good. (Jones' case, 45 Md. 144.) 

But since the passage of said law (now Art. 27, Sec. 365, as amended by the Acts of 1935, Chap. 60), white persons and 

Malayans are forbidden to intermarry and both are forbidden to marry negroes or persons of negro descent to the third generation. The statute makes such marriages void and the parties are guilty of an infamous crime. 

Jackson vs. Jackson, 82 Md. 30.

CELEBRATION-See Madden, Dom. Rel., p. 47. 

Mutual consent and capacity necessary everywhere, but formality is not required in every state. 

Common Law Marriages (contract per verba de praesenti) 

are not valid in Maryland-here there must be a religious ceremony.

In Denison vs. Denison, 35 Md. 361, the alleged wife of a decedent claimed a share in his estate, which was resisted by the administrator. There was no pretension of any solemnization of a marriage ceremony. The parties simply agreed mutually to be husband and wife and in pursuance of this agreement, they cohabited and lived together as husband and wife; they both acknowledged and recognized each other as such and were reputed among their friends as husband and wife. The Court refused to recognize such a marriage as valid. 

See also Scott vs. Ice Co., 135 Md. 343; 

Mitchell vs. Frederick, 166 Md. 42.

Civil marriages are not permitted in Maryland. There must be a religious ceremony, but no particular form is prescribed by law.

The celebrant must be a minister of some religious denomination. A minister cannot marry himself-mere presence of minister insufficient. (7 Mass. 48.)

Art. 62, Sec. 4, as amended by Acts of 1927, Chap. 380, provides as follows: 

"The following persons are authorized to solemnize marriages in this State; any minister of the Gospel, or official of a religious order or body authorized by the rules and customs of said order or body to join persons in marriage." 

In the case of Knapp vs. Knapp, 149 Md. 263, the Court held that even if the celebrant lacked appropriate authority, if the parties acted on the belief that he had it, the marriage is valid. 

In Feehley vs. Feehley, 129 Md. 565, a Catholic couple were married and then divorced. They decided to intermarry again and a priest was called to effectuate their wishes. No license was procured. The priest proceeded, as he testified, to "bless their reunion," as he considered them already married to each other by virtue of the original marriage. The question whether this ceremony made them married was presented to the Court and it was held that a valid marriage resulted. 

A marriage without a religious ceremony taking place in 

Maryland must be regarded as strictly void. 

LICENSES See Madden, Dom. Del., p. 48.

Art. 62, Sec. 4 (as amended by Act of 1927, Chap. 380), pro vides: "No person within this State shall be joined in marriage until a license shall have been obtained from the Clerk of the

Circuit Court for the County in which the marriage is to be performed, or if in Baltimore City, from the Clerk of the Court of Common Pleas, or unless the names of the parties intending to marry shall be thrice published in some church or house of public worship in the county where the woman resides on three several Sundays by some minister residing in said county." (Special provision is made for Quaker marriages, before 12 witnesses, in lieu of celebration by religious official.) 

Licenses are good until used. See Op. of Atty.-Genl. Daily 

Record 1/27/39.

By the Acts of 1937, Chap. 91, application must first be made by the parties for a marriage license, and no license shall be delivered by the Clerk until after the expiration of 48 hours from the time of application. Judge of County in which application is made, or Judge of Court of Common Pleas of Baltimore City, if application is made in City, may authorize clerk to issue license immediately. (Art. 62, Sec. 5.) This

Act was carried into effect by Governor's proclamation on 

December 8, 1938. 

As to when Clerk may issue license, see Art. 62, Sec. 7, as amended by the Acts of 1939, Chap. 728, supra. 

But while minister may be punished for marrying the parties without a license (Art. 27, Sec. 362), as well as the parties (Art. 27, Sec. 360), the marriage is valid, because the rule of construction applied to statutes regulating marriage is "when a statute prohibits marriage between persons or certain classes or prescribes certain formalities, marriages contrary to prohibition, or in disregard of the forms are valid in absence of express words of nullification, unless already void or voidable at common law." (Denison's case, 35 Md. 361.)

Question of failure to obtain license was raised in Feehley vs. Feehley, 129 Md. 565, and the Court said, "In view of important considerations of morality and legitimacy involved, it is manifestly a sound and just rule of construction that statutes providing for marriage licenses are not held to have the effect of nullifying, for non-compliance with their terms, a marriage valid at common law, unless such an intention is plainly disclosed."

How is the Fact of Celebration of a Marriage Proven ?—See 

Madden, Dom. Rel., p. 66. (1) By Direct Evidence establishing the fact (a) testimony of eye witnesses; any person present, the celebrant or generally the parties themselves, but in Maryland if one of the parties

is dead, and his or her executor or administrator is a party to a suit in which a judgment or decree might be rendered for or against said executor or administrator, "no party to the cause can be allowed to testify as to any transaction had with or statement made by the deceased" * * "unless called to testify by the opposite party." (Art. 35, Sec. 3.) (Under this section a plaintiff in a suit against administrators is incompetent to prove her marriage to intestate, either directly or indirectly; neither may survivor, by his own testimony, establish his identity as one of the parties to a marriage certificate. 

Such identity must be proved by other testimony than that of the parties themselves. Bowman vs. Little, 101 Md. 273, Redgrave's case, 38 Md. 96). But admissions and declarations of husband and wife have always been accepted in this state to prove their marriage. Brell vs. Brell, 143 Md. 448; W. B. & A. R. R. vs. State, 136 Md. 105; Hensel vs. Smith, 152 Md. 381. (b) Production of marriage register kept by the clergyman in regular discharge of his clerical duties, the clergyman having since died. Weaver vs. Leiman, 52 Md. 708. (c) Producing certified copy of record of marriage and certificate which is returned to and kept in clerk's office from which the license issued. (Art. 62, Sec. 9.) But in addition, the parties mentioned therein must be identified. 

Bowman vs. Little, 101 Md. 273; 

Hensel vs. Smith, 152 Md. 380. (2) By Indirect or Presumptive Evidence, that is by proof of such facts and circumstances from which the law will assume celebration to have been had. Where parties live together as husband and wife, demeaning themselves toward each other as such, and are received into society and treated by their friends and relatives as having been entitled to that status, the law will in favor of morality and decency presume that they have been legally married. See Jackson vs. Jackson, 80 Md. 191; Jackson vs. Jackson, 82 Md. 17. Thus a marriage may be proved by general reputation, cohabitation and acknowledgment. When these exist a religious ceremony is inferred, and this proof will not be invalidated because evidence cannot be obtained of the time, place and manner of celebration. But this evidence is only presumptive and may be overcome. 

Richardson vs. Smith, 80 Md. 93; 

Schaffer vs. Richardson, 125 Md. 88.

Even if the cohabitation of the parties is shown to have been illicit in its inception, the subsequent marriage can still be

proved by indirect or presumptive evidence, but more exactness or proof is required, and it must be shown with some degree of certainty when the illicit arrangement ceased and the legal relation of husband and wife commenced. 

Jones vs. Jones, 45 Md. 144; 

O'Leary vs. Lawrence, 138 Md. 147.

Where, however, a party asserting the validity of the marriage relies upon a particular form of ceremony performed at a definite time and place, and fails to prove that, he cannot afterwards rely on general reputation to establish it. It is thus apparent that in cases where it is felt that available evidence of marriage in fact is weak or where the absence of a marriage certificate cannot be properly accounted for, the safer course to follow is to rely first on the presumption arising from cohabitation and reputation, if such proof is available. If this fails, resort may then be had to direct evidence of marriage in fact. 

Bauder vs. Blackiston, 149 Md. 322; 

Bowman vs. Little, 101 Md. 294.

It is Necessary to Prove Marriage by Direct Evidence 

(a) in all criminal prosecutions for bigamy and adultery, and (b) in all civil actions for criminal conversation. 

Redgrave's case, 38 Md., page 97.

CONFLICT OF LAWS, 

See Madden, Dom. Rel., p. 75.

Laws of different states on question of capacity and requisite celebration vary, and when validity of marriage is to be determined, it must be settled whether (1) law of place where contest is pending-law of forum (lex fori); or (2) law of place where parties lived-law of the domicile (lex domicilii); or (3) law of place where the marriage was celebrated-law of the place of contract (lex loci contractus) is to govern. 

Sufficiency of Celebration Depends Upon the Law of Place of Contract-See Madden, Dom. Rel., p. 76.

Marriage complying with forms required by law of place where celebrated, is valid as to celebration everywhere. Differences in the requirement of formality of marriage have been held not to vitiate public policy, so that we have recognized marriages from other jurisdictions entered into by non-religious ceremonies there provided for, or by simple contract there permitted. Redgrave's case, 38 Md. 96; Whitehurst's case, 156 Md. 610.

And this is true even though parties deliberately leave their domicile in order to evade the law thereof. People not desiring religious ceremony, may be married in another state before a Justice of the Peace or other civil officer. Marriage would be absolutely valid here. 

Jackson vs. Jackson, 82 Md. 29; 

Hannon vs. State, 63 Md. 125. 

In Whitehurst vs. Whitehurst, 156 Md. 610, the Court found a simple contract marriage to have been entered into in New York by writing in a prayer book, and that such conduct created marriage under the then New York law. The marriage was, therefore, held valid here. 

Fiction of Extraterritoriality: By this fiction territory of one state is presumed to be projected into another state and residents of the former state within the latter are presumed to be still within the jurisdiction of their own domicile. (a) Ambassadors enjoy this right and may marry according to the law of the country they represent; they can certainly do so in the official residences which they occupy; (b) a merchant vessel when on the high seas, and a ship of war everywhere, is within the jurisdiction of the country to which it belongs, and laws of such country must be complied with in case of marriages on water. Parties on board vessel of commerce not upon the high seas would have to be married according to the law of the place where ship was visiting. (Dicey on Domicile, pages 206-209; Harlan Dom. Rel., p. 21).

U. S. Revised Statutes, Sec. 4082, provides that marriages in presence of any consular officer of the U. S. in a foreign country, between persons who would be authorized to marry if residing in the District of Columbia, shall be valid to all intents and purposes, and shall have the same effect as if solemnized within the U. S. 

Capacity-Controlling Law-See Madden, Dom. Rel., p. 78.

In the Jackson case, 82 Md. 29, our Court laid down the following principles (covering celebrations and capacity):

General Rule: Marriages valid where performed, valid everywhere. 

Exceptions to general rule: (a) Marriages deemed contrary to natural law as generally recognized in Christian countries, to which class belong those which involve polygamy and incest (by incestuous marriages we mean marriages between

persons in direct line of consanguinity, and brothers and sisters); (b) Marriages prohibited in terms by the local statute (marriages between persons of different races prohibited to intermarry in Maryland.) It is the declared policy of this 

State to prohibit such marriages. 

In Fensterwald vs. Burk, 129 Md. 131, where uncle and niece, forbidden to marry by Maryland law, left Maryland and were married in Rhode Island which permitted such marriages under the particular circumstances, our Court recognized the validity of the marriage. 

Parties may also deliberately leave their Maryland domicile to achieve marriage between persons related by affinity in some of the locally prohibited relations. 

Fensterwald vs. Burk, supra.

Where no foreign law is proved, law of forum is applied. 

But under the Acts of 1937, Chap. 128, in courts of equity, questions of foreign law should be decided by the Court in the same fashion as domestic law; and that the courts should have discretionary power to take judicial notice of such foreign law if it be that of a jurisdiction having the Anglo- 

American system of law.

A similar statute was passed in 1935 (Chap. 51) for Courts of law.

Both of said Acts were repealed and re-enacted by Acts of 1939, Chap. 131. 

DISSOLUTION OF MARRIAGE 

By death: Death terminates the relation and leaves the surviving party free to contract another marriage. 

As to presumption of death when party has been absent for seven years, see Art. 27, Sec. 23, as amended by Acts of 

1937, Chap. 142, supra.

DIVORCE 

See Madden, Dom. Rel., p. 256.

Federal Courts have no jurisdiction to grant divorces. Only 

State Courts have jurisdiction which of course is statutory. 

In Maryland, until 1841, only legislature had power to grant divorces. From 1841 to 1851, equity courts had concurrent jurisdiction with legislature. Since 1851, equity courts have had exclusive jurisdiction.

There are two kinds of divorces in Maryland: a vinculo matrimonii, and a mensa et thoro. 

A vinculo divorce: dissolution of marriage-parties restored to status of unmarried persons--rights in each other's property destroyed.

A mensa divorce: from bed and board-mere legal separtion of the parties, leaving marriage tie unaffected-still husband and wife but rights to cohabitation destroyed-property rights undisturbed.

Causes for Divorce A Vinculo Matrimonii. 

Art. 16, Sec. 38 (As amended by Acts of 1939, Chap. 558): 

First, the impotence of either party at the time of marriage. 

Secondly, for any cause which, by the laws of this State, render a marriage null and void ab initio. 

Thirdly, for adultery.

Fourthly, when the court shall be satisfied by competent testimony that the party complained against has abandoned the party complaining, and that such abandonment has continued uninterruptedly for at least three years, and is deliberate and final, and the separation of the parties beyond any reasonable expectation of reconciliation. 

Fifthly, when husband and wife shall have voluntarily lived separate and apart, without any cohabitation, for five consecutive years prior to the filing of the Bill, and such separation is beyond any reasonable expectation of reconciliation. (This cause was added by the Acts of 1937, Chap. 396.)

In Etheridge vs. Etheridge, 120 Md. 11, Court held that Bill of Complaint need not set out all the facts and circumstances which constitute the evidence of the causes of the divorce alleged; if the bill charges the enumerated grounds of divorce in the language of the statute, it is sufficient. 

Court also held in that case that where the bill for divorce contains the allegation that the parties were married, it will be assumed that the marriage was a lawful one, until proof to the contrary, appears.

Abandonment as Cause For Divorce See Madden, Dom. 

Rel., p. 276.

The abandonment must be the deliberate act of the defendant with intent to terminate the marriage relation. 

Following elements must be present: 

1. Ceasing to live together (but the permanent and irrevocable refusal without proper cause of the wife to have sexual intercourse, constitutes abandonment if of the duration of a period of at least three years, and this is the law, even though the parties be living together. See Fleegle vs. Fleegle, 136 Md. 

632; Owings vs. Owings, 148 Md. 124; Fries vs. Fries, 166 Md. 

604; Dotterweich vs. Dotterweich, 174 Md. 697). 

2. Cessation must be with intent that marriage relation shall no longer exist. The separation and intent must concur but need not commence at the same time. 

Muller vs. Muller, 125 Md. 72. 

3. Must be without consent or justification. 

4. Continue uninterruptedly for statutory period and without reasonable hope of reconciliation. 

Cases where abandonment not made out: see Twigg vs. 

Twigg, 107 Md. 677; Wheeler vs. Wheeler, 101 Md. 433; Schilling vs. Schilling, 167 Md. 152; Tarr vs. Tarr, 164 Md. 206 and 

Crouch vs. Crouch, 150 Md. 608. 

In Melson vs. Melson, 151 Md. 196, Court held that where articles of separation and accompanying circumstances show that, after a desertion by one spouse, but before expiration of necessary period of three years, both husband and wife agreed that they would live separate and apart from each other, and thereafter did so live apart by common consent, a statutory desertion for three years is not established.

But if the husband by his cruelty compels his wife to leave him, this constitutes abandonment by him. Same is true if wife compels husband to leave her. See Lynch vs. Lynch, 33 

Md. 330; Pattison vs. Pattison, 132 Md. 368.

If abandoning partly demands reconciliation and the other refuses, latter would be guilty of abandonment from the time of the refusal, provided offer to return is made in good faith, and is free from improper qualifications and conditions. 

Backus vs. Backus, 167 Md. 19; Kirkwood vs. Kirkwood, 165 

Md. 547; McClees vs. McClees, 162 Md. 70; Chableau vs. Chableau, 164 Md. 370. 

In Wise vs. Wise, 159 Md. 596, Court held that duty rests upon wife to accept husband's bona fide offer of reconciliation even after she has procured a decree for separate maintenance on grounds of cruelty and abandonment. 

But the spouse not at fault is not required to solicit the return of the other. Keezer, Marriage & Divorce, Sec. 341.

In the case of Simmont vs. Simmont, 160 Md. 432, court said: Desertion is a continuing offense, and ordinarily, until it has by the efflux of the statutory period ripened into a complete cause for absolute divorce, the duty rests upon each party to the marriage to accept any offer made in good faith by the other to resume suspended cohabitation, if it appears with reasonable certainty that such offer may be accepted without any reasonable sacrifice of self-respect, health, safety, or comfort. 

ADULTERY, as Cause for Divorce-See Madden, Dom. Rel., p. 264. "Because of the furtive and clandestine nature of the offense, it seldom happens that it can be shown by direct evidence, nor is it necessary that it should be thus shown, for it may be inferred from circumstances, where such an inference is the only plain, natural and logical deduction to be drawn from such circumstances when they themselves have been established. In such an inquiry, the two most important facts to be considered are:

First, whether there was a disposition to commit adultery, Second, whether there was an opportunity, for if the two concur, ordinarily it will be inferred that adultery was committed." Swoyer vs. Swoyer, 157 Md. 18. 

See also Shufeldt vs. Shufeldt, 86 Md. 519; 

Pattison vs. Pattison, 132 Md. 362.

The marriage and cohabitation of a husband with another woman before his former marriage was legally dissolved is adulterous, even though such marriage was contracted in honest belief that the conviction and incarceration of his former wife permitted him to marry without a divorce. 

Gieselman vs. Gieselman, 134 Md. 453. 

That one was under influence of liquor at the time of commission by her of adultery is no defense to a bill for divorce on that ground. Miller vs. Miller, 140 Md. 60.

A decree of divorce a mensa in favor of the wife on the ground of abandonment does not prevent an absolute divorce for the wife's subsequent adultery.

Williams vs. Williams, 156 Md. 10. 

In a bill alleging adultery as cause for divorce, it is not necessary to disclose with exactness the time and place of the offense. Darner vs. Darner, 157 Md. 98.

Adultery taking place after filing of the original bill cannot be brought into the case by supplemental bill as ground for relief. Schwab vs. Schwab, 96 Md. 592; Wagner vs. Wagner, 130 Md. 349. 

In Schwab vs. Schwab, 93 Md. 385, Court held that a supplemental bill charging adultery could not be filed in a case where the original bill sought a partial divorce on grounds not constituting cause for divorce a vinculo, and that such cause could only be asserted in a separate suit. (Cited in Williams vs. Williams, 156 Md. 12.)

The wife's premarital unchastity as cause for divorce was eliminated by the Acts of 1939, Chapter 558. 

See Hoff vs. Hoff, 162 Md. 252; Wiegand vs. Wiegand, 155 Md. 643.

Five Years Voluntary Separation as Cause for Divorce. 

In Campbell vs. Campbell, 174 Md. 229, Court held that the

Acts of 1937, Chap. 396, is applicable irrespective of whether separation began before or after the Act became operative. 

See Daily Record of August 18, 1937; also Md. Law 

Review, June, 1938, page 357. 

See also France vs. France, Ct. of App. of Md.-Daily 

Record, 3/28/39.

Causes for Divorce A Mensa Et Thoro. 

Art. 16, Sec. 39: 

First, cruelty of treatment; secondly, excessively vicious conduct; thirdly, abandonment and desertion; and the court may decree such divorces forever, or for a limited time; and in all cases where divorce a mensa et thoro is decreed, it may be revoked at any time thereafter by the court granting the same, upon the joint application of the parties to be discharged from the operation of the decree; and the court may decree a divorce a mensa et thoro in cases where a divorce a vinculo matrimoni is prayed, if the causes proved to be sufficient to entitle the party to the same. 

Causes for limited divorce must be grave and weighty. 

Sharp's case, 105 Md. 581.

Drunkenness as an independent ground does not justify divorce. Shutt vs. Shutt, 71 Md. 193; Wheeler vs. Wheeler, 101 Md. 432.

By weight of authority, abusive and unkind treatment must to some extent result in or threaten bodily harm. Mere mental suffering caused by unkind, abusive or insulting words or conduct is not enough. But mental suffering so caused resulting in ill health is bodily injury. 

Porter vs. Porter, 168 Md. 303.

Sallies of temper no ground for divorce. 

Hawkins' case, 65 Md. 104.

Single act of violence is not legal cruelty. (Hoshell's case, 51 

Md. 72; Porter vs. Porter, 168 Md. 303.) But if the act indicates an intention of doing serious bodily harm, or is of a character to cause a reasonable apprehension of serious danger in the future, it would amount to legal cruelty. 

Porter vs. Porter, supra.

Abandonment as cause for partial divorce: Divorce a mensa may be granted for abandonment without regard to its duration. Harding vs. Harding, 22 Md. 345; Buckner vs. Buckner, 118 Md. 113; Young vs. Young, 136 Md. 85.

Refusal to cohabit for less than three years is ground for partial divorce. Faulkner's case, Ct. of App. of Md., Daily 

Record, 2/15/39. 

Art. 16, Sec. 41, provides "when bill prays for divorce a vinculo, the fact that the parties have been divorced a mensa

shall not be taken to interfere with the jurisdiction of the court over the subject, and a party who has obtained a divorce a mensa on the ground of abandonment, which at the time of obtaining said divorce was not of the character and duration specified in Sec. 38 of this Article, shall not be estopped thereby from subsequently obtaining a divorce a vinculo on the ground of abandonment proved to be of the character and duration as specified in said Sec. 38."

Although the abandonment has not continued for three years, partial divorce may be decreed where a divorce a vinculo is prayed. Art. 16, Sec. 39-Downs vs. Downs, 154 Md. 434.

A divorce a mensa cannot be decreed when the only ground alleged is adultery, since adultery is not a ground for partial divorce. Stewart vs. Stewart, 105 Md. 297.

But a divorce a mensa may be granted for abandonment without reference to duration, and even though the abandonment has continued for over three years (which could be cause for absolute divorce). But in such case plaintiff cannot thereafter obtain a divorce a vinculo on the ground of abandonment. 

Miller vs. Miller, 153 Md. 218.

In McCurley vs. McCurley, 60 Md. 189, court held that a divorce suit being a personal action, the death of either party before decree abates the divorce proceeding; and this effect extends to whatever is identified with those proceedings. In 

France vs. France, Daily Record, 3/28/39, our Court of Appeals set aside a divorce decree where appellee died pending appeal.

There Must Be Corroboration of Plaintiff's Testimony in All Divorce Cases. 

* * 

Art. 35, Sec. 4, provides * "and in all suits, etc., instituted in consequence of adultery, or for the purpose of obtaining a divorce or for damages for breach of promise of marriage, no verdict shall be permitted to be recovered, nor shall any judgment or decree be entered upon the testimony of the plaintiff alone; but in all such cases testimony in corroboration of that of the plaintiff shall be necessary." 

Where There Is No Possibility of Collusion, the Corroboration 

Required by This Section in Divorce Cases Need be Slight. 

See Heinmiller vs. Heinmiller, 133 Md. 494; Rodgers vs. 

Rodgers, 142 Md. 561; Jacobs vs. Jacobs, 170 Md. 408. 

In Porter vs. Porter, 168 Md. 304, Court said: "Rule relating to corroboration must not be confounded with that concerning

the burden of proof. * * * Slight corroboration will be sufficient, especially where the nature of the case is such, and upon consideration of the whole evidence there is no appearance of collusion. The burden of proof requires that the appellee sustain her allegations by a fair preponderance of testimony. 

Corroboration, as required by the statute, does not mean evidence sufficient to satisfy the rule as to burden of proof. 

There may be corroboration but the weight of the entire testimony may be in favor of the defendant." 

In the case of Miller vs. Miller, Circuit Court of Baltimore 

City, Daily Record of January 18, 1938, husband prayed for absolute divorce on ground of wife's adultery. Husband testified, but his testimony was not corroborated. The defendant offered no evidence in her own defense. In her answer, she neither admitted nor denied the allegation of adultery, but demanded strict proof of the same (Rule 20, General Equity 

Rule of Court of Appeals, and Sec. 179 of Art. 16). Judge 

Dennis held that that equivocal answer, in the light of the Rule and Statute cited, is equivalent to a confession of adultery. Court held that under such circumstances, it would seem that the corroboration appearing from proof, coupled with implied admission of guilt, entitle plaintiff to a divorce a vinculo. 

In Wiegand vs. Wiegand, 155 Md. 648, Court held that corroboration of plaintiff's testimony is not necessary under the above statute (Art. 35, Sec. 4) in a proceeding for separate maintenance. But in Roeder vs. Roeder, 170 Md. 579, Court held that where permanent alimony without divorce is sought, the same requirements as to proof, including corroboration, are necessary as where either type of divorce is sought, but where there is no appearance of collusion, little corroborative evidence is required to satisfy the statute. 

See Md. Law Review, April, 1937, p. 266.

Defenses to Divorce Suits. 

Besides the denial of the causes for divorce alleged in the 

Bill of Complaint, five other defenses may be made: 

1. Connivance: Complainant's consent to commission of acts complained of-may be active, passive, express or implied-usually a defense to adultery-also applicable to desertion and cruelty, as where wife provokes husband's misconduct. Barclay vs. Barclay, 98 Md. 371. Madden, p. 294. 

2. Collusion: the corrupt agreeing of the parties to make up a case for the purpose of obtaining a divorce. Applicable to any cause. Madden, p. 298.

Condonation: Forgiveness of the act complained of. 

Misconduct constituting a cause for partial divorce only, is sufficient to revive a condoned offense which is a cause for absolute divorce. Bonwit vs. Bonwit, 169 Md. 189; Fisher vs. 

Fisher, 93 Md. 298; Kremis vs. Kremis, 163 Md. 223. donation is usually a defense to adultery. Con- 

In the case of Glass vs. Glass, 175 Md. 693, Court said: "The authorities generally hold that acts which in themselves do not constitute a ground for divorce may nevertheless constitute a breach of the condition of condonation" (quoting from 9 R. C. L. 385). The court further said (quoting from 

9 R. C. L. 383): "Cruelty as a ground of divorce is generally a course of conduct rather than a single act; and the rule is that sexual cohabitation after acts of cruelty, cannot be considered as condonation in the sense in which it would be after an act of adultery." The authorities generally agree that condonation after acts of cruelty depends upon circumstances and the situation of the parties, and whether there has been forgiveness and reconciliation. Madden, p. 300. 

4. Recrimination: a counter charge by the defendant of a cause for divorce against the plaintiff. When each has cause for one, neither can obtain one. But cause for limited divorce will not bar cause for absolute divorce. Pryor vs. Pryor, 146 

Md. 683; Appletoft vs. Appletoft, 147 Md. 603. Madden, p. 305. 

5. Laches: Unreasonable delay in complaining after knowledge of offense-What is unreasonable depends on the circumstances of the case. Kremelberg's case, 52 Md. 553.

In divorce cases, court may intefere sua sponte to refuse relief. It may refuse a decree to a party not entitled to it, although no defense be made. Fisher vs. Fisher, 95 Md. 319.

The Act of 1931, Chap. 220 (now Art. 16, Sec. 39A), provides that agreement made between husband and wife respecting support and property rights, etc., shall be valid, and such agreement shall not be a bar to divorce, whether cause existed at time of agreement or arose subsequent thereto and whether at the time of agreement parties were living together or apart; provided that whenever such agreement shall make provision for the care, custody, education or maintenance of infant children of the parties, the Court shall have right to modify agreement in respect to such infants as Court may deem proper.

A separation agreement will not be construed to bar property rights unless such intention is clearly expressed or neces-

sarily implied. Hewitt vs. Shipley, 169 Md. 221-See Md. Law 

Rev. for December, 1937, p. 75.

In the case of Dockman vs. Dockman, Circuit Court No. 2. 

of Baltimore City, Daily Record of October 8, 1937, Judge

Dickerson held that an agreement which provides for the future separation of husband and wife is illegal on grounds of public policy, but an agreement based on an existing or immediate separation is valid. It was further held that reconciliation and resumption of cohabitation, subsequent to an agreement of separation, will abrogate those provisions of the agreement which relate to alimony, counsel fees and custody of children, but will not affect provisions for the settlement of the property rights of the parties. Brantly, Contracts, 2nd Ed. 

232—R. C. L. Div. & Sep. Sec. 345; Mach vs. Baranowski, 152 

Md. 53, and other cases cited in said opinion. Madden, p. 331.

CONSEQUENCES AND EFFECTS OF DIVORCE A VINCULO. 

1. Either party at liberty to remarry. .

In Maryland, by Act of 1872, Chap. 272, defendant in divorce case, guilty of adultery or abandonment could be restrained from marriage during other party's life. Elliott case, 36 Md. 

357; Garner case, 56 Md. 127.

The Act of 1872, Chap. 272, was repealed by the Act of 1888, Chap. 486, and the Courts no longer have the power to so restrain either of the parties. Dimpfel vs. Wilson, 107 Md. 329. 

2. Husband no longer obligated to support wife, but alimony may be decreed, except where cause of divorce is in effect a cause of nullity. 

3. If divorce is granted for prevenient causes, making marriage void ab initio, i. e., marriages void or voidable at common law, the children are bastardized. But where divorce is granted for supervenient cause, such as adultery or abandonment, marriage relation being dissolved from that time and not ab initio, prior children born would be legitimate.

In all divorces, court has power to award custody of children. Art. 16, Sec. 39. 

4. Rights in each other's property destroyed. 

By decree of absolute divorce, tenants by the entireties become tenants in common. Reed vs. Reed, 109 Md. 691; Meyers vs. L. & S. Assn., 139 Md. 613.

CONSEQUENCES OF DIVORCE A MENSA. 

(1) Marriage status not destroyed; (2) Husband's control over wife's person and rights of cohabitation suspended but property rights remain undisturbed (Hokamp vs. Hagaman, 36 Md. 511); (3) Husband's obligations to support wife continue; and the court has power to decree her alimony. 

JURISDICTION IN DIVORCE MATTERS. 

Art. 16, Sec. 37, as amended by Acts of 1931, Chap. 451, pro vides that Courts of Equity of this State shall have jurisdiction of all applications for divorce. The bill must be filed either where the plaintiff or defendant resides, but if the defendant is a non-resident of the State of Maryland, the bill must be filed where the plaintiff resides. 

By term "residence" is actually meant domicil, which means a person's permanent home. Presumption is that the domicil of a person is where he actually lives, but this presumption may be rebutted. Intent, voting, etc., must be considered. 

Harrison vs. Harrison, 117 Md. 612; Etheridge vs. Etheridge, 120 Md. 13; Willingham vs. Willingham, 162 Md. 539; Wagner vs. Scurlock, 166 Md. 284.

In Williams vs. Williams, 156 Md. 10, Court held that the fact that a partial divorce and permanent alimony had been previously awarded in another circuit for abandonment did not affect jurisdiction to grant absolute divorce for adultery. 

Art. 16, Sec. 40, provides that where the cause for divorce occurred out of the state, then plaintiff or defendant must have resided in this State for two years next preceding the filing of the bill.

In Adams vs. Adams, 101 Md. 507, while the husband and wife were residents of another state, the husband committed adultery in the State of Maryland. Subsequently the wife became a resident of this State and within a few months filed a bill for divorce on the ground of such adultery. Held that our Courts had jurisdiction under Art. 16, Sec. 40, since in this case the cause of divorce arose in this State. 

By the Acts of 1927, Chap. 225 and Chap. 494 (now Art 16, 

Sec. 37A), all persons residing on property lying within the physical boundaries of this State but on property over which the Government has jurisdiction, may file suit for divorces in 

Maryland as residents.

Case of Lowe vs. Lowe, 150 Md. 593, in which a person living in Maryland on government property, was held not to be a resident, was decided prior to the Act of 1927, supra. 

Order of Publication. 

Who may be brought into Court by this process? 

1. Non-residents (or those whose whereabouts are unknown). Person temporarily out of state is not a non-resident. 

2. By Sec. 134 of Art. 16, residents twice successively re turned non-est, or once returned non-est and proved to be avoiding the process of the Court. 

Under Art. 16, Sec. 142, notice must be given in one or more newspapers stating the substance and object of the bill or petition and warning such party to appear on or before the day fixed in such order and show cause why relief prayed should not be granted, and such notice shall be published as the Court may direct, not less, however, than once a week for four successive weeks, previous to 15 days before the day fixed by such order for the appearance of the party. 

Order of Publication includes title of case, a short statement of the object followed by the grievances, and the order of court warning absent party to appear upon a day named. 

But while no personal summons is necessary in divorce matters, in order to award alimony, court must obtain jurisdiction over the defendant by personal summons. 

See McSherry's case, 113 Md. 395.

ALIMONY, 

See Madden, Dom. Rel., p. 319.

Alimony is an allowance which husband pays by order of the court to his divorced or separated wife. The amount of alimony depends on the circumstances of the parties. 

Art. 16, Secs. 14, 15 and 16A (16A added by Acts of 1935, 

Chap. 261), give the equity courts jurisdiction in all causes for alimony. See Wallingsford vs. Wallingsford, 6 H. & J. 485.

Where the allegations of the bill are sufficient to support either form of divorce, they are sufficient to support a bill for alimony alone. Outlaw vs. Outlaw, 118 Md. 502; Hood vs. 

Hood, 138 Md. 360; Wald vs. Wald, 161 Md. 493; Madden, 330.

While in divorce cases, suit may be instituted either in the county or city where either of the parties reside, suits for alimony alone must be filed where defendant resides. 

Woodcock vs. Woodcock, 169 Md. 40.

In Woodcock case, supra, a woman sued her husband for permanent alimony, not asking for divorce. He lived in 

Wicomico County and she came to Baltimore, and after establishing a domicil here, filed suit in this city. Summons was served on the husband in Wicomico County. 

He immediately filed a bill in Wicomico County for partial divorce on ground of abandonment. He appeared specially in his wife's case in Baltimore and denied jurisdiction of the Court here on the ground that he neither lived nor carried on his regular business in Baltimore (Art. 75, Sec. 157).

Court held that although suit for divorce may be brought either in county where husband or wife resides, suit for alimony, without divorce, may be brought where defendant resides only.

The Court further held in the Woodcock case that an alimony case may not be amended into one for divorce. But see 

Wald vs. Wald, 161 Md. 493. See also Md. Law Review, Dec., 1936, p. 81.

Property in this State of a non-resident defendant may be subjected to the payment of alimony; also applies to alimony pendente lite. Art. 16, Sec. 16. 

If husband obtains divorce, no alimony is awarded the wife.

In Hilbert vs. Hilbert, 168 Md. 374, Court held that parties cannot agree before marriage that the wife shall never have claim for alimony, because against public policy. 

But antenuptial agreement which provides as to what interest the parties shall have in the property of the other, during coverture or after death, is enforceable. Schnepfe case, 124 

Md. 337.

In order to decree alimony (which is in personam), court must have jurisdiction over the person of the defendant. (Rule also applicable to costs of divorce suit.) McSherry vs. Mc- 

Sherry, 113 Md. 395.

But once defendant is served with subpoena, subsequent orders with reference to alimony require no personal notice.

Notice on attorney of record would be sufficient. McSherry case, supra.

Remarriage of a man who has been decreed to pay alimony, does not relieve him from paying it or give him the right to have it reduced. Langrall vs. Langrall, 145 Md. 340; Marshall vs. Marshall, 164 Md. 107. 

Definition of Alimony: As defined in Spear vs. Spear, 158 

Md. 675. "Alimony is a provision by the husband for the wife's support that continues only during their joint lives, or so long as they live separate and apart, or until the wife remarries." 

As defined in Roberts vs. Roberts, 160 Md. 521: "No allow ance to the wife in actions for divorce or separation will be treated as alimony which does anything more than provide for the payment of money at stated periods to the wife for her support during the joint lives of the parties so long as they are separated."

Both of the above definitions should be considered together. 

See also Wallingsford vs. Wallingsford, 6 H. & J. 485.

Unless an agreement of the parties incorporated in the divorce decree meets the above requirements or essentials of the definition of alimony, the Court would have no power to later change the provisions of the decree, since the Court would be interfering with an agreement over which it has no jurisdiction. 

In Spear vs. Spear, 158 Md. 672, where a decree of divorce provided in accordance with the agreement of the parties, for payment by the husband to the wife of a monthly sum as “permanent alimony," to continue during the wife's life without regard to whether she remarried, and without reference to husband's life, the allowance was not alimony, and was consequently not subject to modification by the Court, upon wife's remarriage. 

In Emerson vs. Emerson, 120 Md. 584, Court held where the agreement provides for something more than alimony, as where it binds the husband to pay the wife a certain sum until her death, irrespective of whether she survives him or not, *** and such agreement is embodied in the decree of divorce, a Court of Equity should not afterwards disturb the agreement even though the divorced wife marries again.

In Roberts vs. Roberts, 160 Md. 521, Court held that a decree requiring the husband to pay charges for taxes, public dues, and assessments, ground rent, and mortgage interest, against home owned by him and wife as tenants by the entireties, cannot be supported as an award of alimony. 

In Bushman vs. Bushman, 157 Md. 166, Court held that when a decree of absolute divorce provides, not for alimony within the meaning of the Statute but for the payment of a specified sum, payable in installments, in accordance with the agreement of the parties, the defendant cannot be punished by imprisonment because of his refusal to pay the money as agreed, in view of the constitutional prohibition of imprisonment for debt. · 

In Cohen vs. Cohen, 174 Md. 61, Court held that a provision, in a divorce decree, awarding to the wife a weekly sum "as alimony for the support of herself and the infant child" was a provision for alimony, with a recognition therein of the needs of the wife in fixing the amount, and was not in part a provision for the infant child, and consequently the husband was subject to contempt proceedings for failure to comply with the award. 

The Court distinguished this case from Bushman vs. Bushman, 157 Md. 166. In that case, an agreement relating to alimony, etc., was incorporated in the divorce decree, such provisions being set forth in separate paragraphs, one paragraph providing for payment to wife as alimony, and the other for payment to the wife for the maintenance and support of the children. For this reason the Court held no confusion to exist respecting the two liabilities of the husband, further that the former husband could be attached for contempt only when he defaulted in payment of alimony set forth in the decree. (However, it was also found that the decree in that case was not for alimony within the meaning of the Maryland statute, but was for the payment of a specified sum of money in accordance with the agreement of the parties, and the defendant could not, therefore, be punished by imprisonment because of his refusal to pay the money as decreed.) 

For a full discussion of the Cohen case, see Md. Law Review, Dec. 1938, p. 93.

See also Knabe vs. Knabe, Ct. of App. of Md., Daily Record, 6/5/39.

In Staub vs. Staub, 170 Md. 202, Court held that where a divorce a vinculo is granted and there is no provision made for the payment of alimony, proceeding cannot thereafter be maintained for alimony.

But where decree provided for alimony or Court reserved jurisdiction with reference to alimony, court may later modify decree. Marshall vs. Marshall, 162 Md. 116. See also Winkel case, Ct. of App. of Md., Daily Rec., 2/11/39

For non-payment of alimony, defendant may be imprisoned for contempt of court unless he can purge himself by paying or by showing that he has neither the estate nor the ability to pay. 

Dickey vs. Dickey, 154 Md. 681. Art. 16, Sec. 188. 

Fi fa may issue against husband's property for purpose of satisfying alimony due. Art. 16, Sec. 205.

A judgment may be obtained in Maryland for accrued alimony on an award made in another State. 

Rosenberg vs. Rosenberg, 152 Md. 49.

The estate of a deceased husband is responsible for alimony accrued up to the date of the husband's death. 

Kalben vs. King, 166 Md. 640. 

In Yake vs. Yake, 170 Md. 75, it was held that alimony is not an incident to, and cannot be predicated upon, or granted in consequence of, an annulment of marriage. 

Madden, Dom. Rel., p. 325. 

Alimony Pendente Lite.

By alimony pendente lite we mean maintenance, also counsel fees, to the wife during pendency of suit-may be awarded regardless of the merits of the case. 

Taylor vs. Taylor, 108 Md. 129.

Rescinding Divorce Decrees. 

Court may set aside divorce decree if obtained by fraud. 

Taberling vs. Taberling, 157 Md. 429. 

In Harris vs. Harris, 159 Md. 629, Court held that notice of such proceeding served on the attorney of record for the plaintiff is sufficient. But Court held that when the fact of such representation is challenged by the attorney in the proceedings arising after the termination of the original suit, authority vel non becomes a question of fact to be determined by the Court. Harris vs. Harris, supra.

Where the wife moved to set aside a divorce decree which had been granted her, on the ground of the subsequent incompetency of her husband, she, not alleging, however, his incompetency at the time of the desertion or within three years thereafter, the Court held that the divorce decree should not be annulled. Noellert vs. Noellert, 169 Md. 559.

Divorce decrees may be set aside for fraud even after the death of the complainant. 19 C. J. 169.

In France vs. France, Court of Appeals of Md., Daily Rec ord, March 28, 1939, a divorce decree was reversed where the appellee (to whom the decree had been granted by the lower court) died pending the appeal to the Court of Appeals.

CUSTODY OF CHILDREN IN DIVORCE SUITS, 

See Madden, Dom. Rel., p. 377.

In awarding custody of children in divorce suits, the welfare of the children is the primary consideration of the Courts. 

The wife is given the custody of the infant children in the majority of cases, but not where the husband is granted a divorce on the ground of the wife's adultery. Since she would not be a proper person under such circumstances to raise the infant children, the Courts usually do not award her their custody in such cases. Art. 16, Sec. 80; Acts of 1931, Chap. 

220 (Art. 16, Sec. 39A); Pangle vs. Pangle, 134 Md. 166. 

CONFLICT OF LAWS, 

See Madden, Dom. Rel., p. 312.

Jurisdiction in divorce matters depends upon the domicil of the parties at the time of the commencement of the proceedings. The validity of a marriage on the other hand, depends upon the law of the place of celebration, without regard to the law of the domicil. 

Therefore, if an offense is a cause for divorce in the domicil of the parties, that is sufficient, even though it may not be a cause for divorce in the place where the marriage was celebrated or'in the state where the offense was committed. 

See Cheely vs. Clayton, 110 U. S. 701; Walker vs. 

Walker, 125 Md. 649; Garner vs. Garner, 56 Md. 127. 

In Walker vs. Walker, 125 Md., the husband went to Nevada for the purpose of obtaining a divorce. He did not resign

his position here in the Internal Revenue Department, but merely secured a leave of absence. After remaining in Nevada for six months, he obtained a divorce there, and returned to Maryland. When his wife brought suit for alimony, he offered in evidence his Nevada divorce. Our Court held that where a party goes to another state "with the view and for the sole purpose of securing a divorce, without any intention of abandoning his domicile in Maryland, or of becoming a resident of Nevada for any other purpose, and with a well defined intention to return to this State as soon as his object is attained" does not acquire a bona fide residence or domicile in

Nevada, and the Nevada court did not acquire jurisdiction to grant him a divorce. Our court, therefore, refused to recognize the divorce as a valid one. 

DUTIES HUSBAND ASSUMES, 

See Madden, Dom. Rel., p. 179.

At common law, and in Maryland prior to 1880, the husband was bound to pay his wife's antenuptial debts. Madden, p. 205. 

Now changed by Art. 45, Sec. 14, which provides "no husband shall be liable in any manner for any debts of his wife contracted or for any claims or demands of any kind against her arising prior to marriage, but she and her property shall remain liable therefor in the same manner as if the marriage had not taken place." 

And by Art. 45, Sec. 21, it is provided that the liability of the husband shall continue as at common law for the debts, contracts or engagements which the wife may incur or enter into upon the credit of her husband or as his agent or for necessaries for herself or his or their children. 

By "necessaries" we mean such articles as are essential to her health and comfort.

Corpus Juris, Vol. 30, page 602, states that "necessaries" include food, shelter, household furnishings, dresses, medical services and funeral expenses.

Husband must furnish her such support as is suitable to the proper station in life of the parties. McFerren vs. Goldsmith, 137 Md. 577; Bliss vs. Bliss, 133 Md. 61; Jones vs. Gutman, 88 Md. 355; Madden, p. 195.

Husband's Liability for Wife's Funeral Expenses-See Mad den, Dom. Rel., p. 198. 

In Stonesipher vs. Shriver, 100 Md. 24, the wife died intestate and the personal representative attempted to charge her estate with the funeral expenses, but the Court held that her husband was responsible for the same and such expense could not be charged to her sole and separate estate. 

See also Barnes vs. Starr, 144 Md. 220; Anderson vs. 

Carter, 175 Md. 540.

But by the Acts of 1933, Chap. 69, it is provided that "whenever a married woman dies, her estate if solvent shall be liable for payment of funeral expenses (not to exceed $300.00, except by special order of Court) *** and her personal representative shall collect such sum from the deceased's husband." 

The purpose of this Act is to make her estate, if solvent, a primary source for the payment of her funeral expenses, but not to release her husband therefor.

A married woman may provide, of course, by her will that the funeral expenses shall be paid out of her own estate. 

Farver vs. Pickett, 162 Md. 12.

Matter of "necessaries" may arise (1) where parties are living together, (2) where they are living apart. (1) Where parties are living together, it is presumed that husband intends that he shall be charged with wife's purchases of articles suitable to his station in life. Jones vs. Gutman, 88 Md. 355; Noel vs. O'Neil, 128 Md. 204; McFerren vs. Goldsmith, 137 Md. 579; Adkins vs. Hastings, 138 Md. 464; Madden, p. 187.

In Maryland, a wife may purchase necessaries and charge her husband with their payment, although she has an estate of her own from which she may buy such necessaries. 

McFerren vs. Goldsmith, 137 Md. 579.

But husband's liability may be rebutted by showing (1) that articles furnished wife were not necessaries, or (2) by showing that he has provided her with such necessaries, or (3) has furnished her with money with which to purchase them. 

By Art. 16, Secs. 49-51, husband may prohibit his wife from pledging his credit, by Court action, if he is supplying her with necessaries.

If the goods are given to the wife but not on her husband's credit, he is not liable. Weisker vs. Lowenthal, 31 Md. 413; 

Jones vs. Gutman, 88 Md. 368; Noel vs. O'Neil, 128 Md. 204; 

Farver vs. Pickett, 162 Md. 12.

On the other hand, where she purchases "necessaries" on his credit, she would not be liable as she is merely acting as his agent by operation of law. 

McFerren vs. Goldsmith, 137 Md. 579.

Persons furnishing "necessaries" may sue husband, notwithstanding express prohibition by husband to them to trust her. (2) Where parties are living apart, the husband's liability depends on the manner in which the separation occurred. (a) Where separation is by wife's fault, she cannot bind him. (b) Where separation is by husband's fault, he is liable for necessaries. (c) Where separation is by mutual consent, then if husband provides wife with sufficient allowance and he actually pays same to her, he is not bound for her purchases. 

See Harlan, Domestic Relations, p. 51; Madden, p. 189.

Fact that they are living apart is caution to all dealing with her, because if she wrongfully left him, creditor could not recover, and if husband is providing for her reasonably during a mutual separation, creditor could not recover. Schindel vs. 

Schindel, 12 Md. 108; Medairy vs. McAllister, 97 Md. 497; 

Frostberg vs. Hitchens, 99 Md. 629; Banking Co. vs. Neilson, 164 Md. 13; McFerren vs. Goldsmith, 137 Md. 573.

Husband's Notices in Newspapers that he will not be responsible for wife's debts is rarely of any legal effect, for if separation is by wife's fault, there is no need of notice; if separation is by husband's fault, he is liable in spite of the notice; if parties are separated by mutual consent and he reasonably provides for her, he is not liable anyway. 

In 30 C. J., page 600, it is provided as follows: "* * It would seem that the question of notice is immaterial, liability being determined in cases where notice is given, as in cases where it is not given, according to whether or not the circumstances are such as to impose on the husband the duty of supplying the wife with necessaries or the means with which to purchase them, and if so, whether he has performed the duty."

TORTS OF WIFE, 

See Madden, Dom. Rel., p. 206. 

Since 1880 (Art. 45, Sec. 14) husband is no longer responsible for torts committed by wife before marriage. 

For torts committed by a married woman prior to 1899, husband was jointly liable with her, even though she was not acting as his agent at the time of the commission of the tortious act. Property of either could be taken in execution. 

Brown vs. Kemper, 27 Md. 666.

If, however, the tort was committed by her in the husband's presence, he alone was liable, because law presumed that he coerced her, but this presumption could be rebutted, and if overcome, she too would be liable. Nolan vs. Traber, 49 Md. 466. 

Since 1899 (Art. 45, Sec. 5) married women may be sued separately for wrongs independent of contract committed by them before or during marriage as fully as if they were unmarried * and that no husband shall be liable for any tort committed separately by her out of his presence, without his participation or sanction. * * (Act of 1931, Chap. 398, abolishes presumption of coercion if in husband's presence, as to crimes). 

Under Sec. 5 of Art. 45, it was held that a married woman could not sue her husband for a personal tort, since this section provides that they (married women) "may sue for torts committed against them" but section does not specifically state that they may sue their husbands in tort. Since such right would be in derogation of common law, the statute must clearly give her such right.

Furstenburg vs. Furstenburg, 152 Md. 247. 

And the same reasons which preclude the wife from maintaining an action in tort against her husband also apply to an action in tort by a wife against a partnership of which her husband is a member. David vs. David, 161 Md. 532.

But it appears that wife may maintain suit against her husband for a tort committed against her property. Cochrane vs. 

Cochrane, 139 Md. 534. Madden, p. 221. See Md. Law Review, December, 1936, p. 65, for an excellent discussion of right of wife to sue husband.

CONTRACTUAL POWERS OF WIFE, 

See Madden, Dom. Rel., p. 137.

At common law, husband and wife were considered as one, and she could not contract with her husband or with third persons.

In Equity, however, her contracts with her husband would be enforced, and she could contract with third persons as to her sole and separate estate. 

Since 1899 (Art. 45, Sec. 5), married women are given the absolute right to contract with third parties. 

And by Art. 45, Sec. 20, she may contract with her husband and may even form a partnership with him, or with any other person * and upon all such contracts, a married woman may sue and be sued as fully as if she were a feme sole. * * Under this section and Section 5, a married woman may sue or be sued by her husband upon her contracts with him. 

Cochrane vs. Cochrane, 139 Md. 533.

MARRIED WOMEN, 

See Madden, Dom. Rel., p. 82.

In considering the validity of a married woman's deed, made at any particular time, it is necessary to consider the state of the law at that time.

By the common law, the deed of a married woman was absolutely null and void. She could only convey her estates in lands by fines or by common recovery. Those were collusive suits. Preston vs. Evans, 56 Md. 476.

Except as to her Sole and Separate estate settled upon her, with power to convey as a feme sole, she could not convey alone or jointly with her husband.

Equity followed the law, but introduced the doctrine of "wife's equity of settlement". This meant that whenever a husband had to resort to an equity court to recover or reduce to possession any portion of his wife's property, equity compelled him to make suitable provision out of such property for the maintenance of his wife and children. 

McVey vs. Boggs, 3 Md., Ch. 94.

The "Sole and Separate Estate" heretofore mentioned, is a creature of equity. It is created by contract or will. Words in common use are, "for the sole and separate use of wife." (Controlled by instrument creating it.)

To carry out the purposes of such a trust, a trustee was necessary, but failure to appoint him did not invalidate the scheme, as the Court would regard the husband, if in possession, as occupying that capacity.

The wife's power over this property was the same as if she were a feme sole. She could even dispose of it by will. 

In this separate estate on the wife's death, intestate, husband had the same rights as in her general property, his control being only suspended during coverture. But where it appeared upon the face of the instrument that the intention was to carry title to the property beyond the period of the wife's death and to exclude the husband, that intention would prevail.

By the Act of 1842, Chap. 298, a married woman was entitled to become possessed and seized of real property or slaves by direct bequest, devise, gift, purchase or distribution, in her own name and as her own property and to make will with her husband's consent, if made of privy examination sixty days before her death, and to hold to her sole and separate use as a feme sole, all property of value of $1,000.00 which she should earn by her skill, industry and personal labor. (The Act of 

1892, Chap. 265, removed the $1,000.00 limitation.)

In 1860 (Code of 1860, Art. 45, Secs. 2-11) the married women's "Statutory Estate" was created. 

The "Statutory Estate" must not be confused with her "Sole and Separate Estate." The former is, of course, statutorythe latter, a creature of equity.

The Statute of 1860 declared that real and personal property owned at marriage or acquired thereafter by purchase, gift, grant, devise, bequest or in the course of distribution, she should hold to her separate use. By the Act of 1874, Chap. 57, property acquired by descent after marriage was added to her statutory estate, and by the Act of 1892, Chap. 267, property acquired "in any other manner" was added to the Statutory 

Separate Estate. 

But she could only convey her statutory separate estate by joint deed with her husband.

The Acts of 1894, Chap. 326, limits this power to married women who have attained the age of 18 years, but whatever, her age, she might relinquish her dower by joint deed of herself or husband or by her separate deed.

The Act of 1898, Chap. 457, Sec. 4, provides that no conveyance, if made by her under the age of 18 years, shall be valid, unless her husband unites therein (Amended by Acts of 1914, Chap. 406).

By the Act of 1872, wife was given power to convey as feme sole, where her husband had been found insane upon inquisition.

The Act of 1890, Chap. 394, gave her the right to constitute an attorney to sell and convey her statutory separate estate by a power of attorney executed jointly with her husband. 

Prior to Jan. 1, 1899, there were, therefore, three ways in which a married woman could own property in Maryland; (1) property secured to her sole and separate use, (2) property held under Art. 45, Secs. 1 and 2-Statutory Separate Es tate, (3) property earned by her skill, industry and personal labor earnings.

Acts of 1898. 

Legislature in 1898 passed two Acts-Chaps. 457 and 231, the general purpose of which was to (a) put husband and wife upon a footing of equality as far as concerns their respective rights in their own and each other's property during coverture, and give to the survivor, upon its determination by death, the same share and estate in real and personal property, to the other (b) give wife power to make contracts, and to sue and be sued, etc. (c) to make it clear that husband is not liable for her contracts, etc.

Present Law. 

Art. 45, Sec. 3-"It shall not be necessary for a married woman to have a trustee to secure to her the sole and separate use of her property

This Statute specifically recognizes her "sole and separate estate" as still being in force.

Art. 45, Sec. 4—-Married women shall hold all their prop erty of every description for their separate use, as fully as if they were unmarried, and shall have all the power to dispose of by deed, mortgage, lease, will or any other instruments that husbands have to dispose of their property, and no more. 

By Art. 45, Sec. 12, married woman or married man, at any age, may relinquish dower in real estate. 

By Art. 45, Sec. 13, if married man or married woman is found to be insane upon inquisition, or when either has been absent or unheard of for seven years, either may grant or convey by separate deed, whether the same be absolute or by

Property Passing From One Spouse to Other. 

Art. 45, Sec. 1 (as amended by Acts of 1929, Chap. 398) pro vides: "no acquisition of property passing from one spouse to the other shall be valid if the same has been made or granted in prejudice of the rights of subsisting creditors, who, however, must assert their claims within three years after the acquisition of the property, or be absolutely barred, and for the purpose of asserting their rights under this section, claims of creditors not yet due and matured, shall be considered as due and matured."
