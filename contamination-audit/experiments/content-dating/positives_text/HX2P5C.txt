DEDICATION I DEDICATE THE WRITINGS EMBODIED IN THIS BOOK TO THOSE PERSONS WHO HAVE TAKEN THE TASK TO BRING INTO EXISTENCE, SOCIAL JUSTICE AND ECONOMIC STABILITY.

INTRODUCTION 

The contents of this article are written in plain and simple language so that the average person will understand its objective. Its purpose is to straighten the backbone of our country, to enlighten the common citizen so that he can familiarize himself with the functioning processes of government, to drive from our midst those persons who are the parasites of the economic system, to establish a more perfect democratic constitution and to bring about a well rounded economic system.

Reforms in monetary policies, governmental and political organizations are of a necessary importance in these pioneering days of the New Deal and upon our entrance into an era of a closer relationship between nations and a clearer understanding of social justice for the world population. 

In our economic system there are many elements composing the whole. These elements, whether large or small, must all be considered as important in establishing our well rounded economic system. 

It is necessary that the ratio of work these various elements perform be estimated according to their economic necessity to the whole system. 

If the small elements are not given the proper care we soon discover that this damages the whole system. This likewise happens when the larger elements are neglected. Who is to care for this system? There is only one answer, the government.

Then what must the government do to justly supervise each element so that it will fulfill but not exceed the importance of its existence and will not try vii

viii

to overpower other elements? These problems are all outlined in this article. 

I have tried to point out in this article that the various governments within the country are only insurance agencies. They protect the individual and promote rules and regulations that are used to regulate each element in our national life so that the whole system is economically balanced. Our national government therefore becomes the mediator for the three principal classes in our national life, the Capitalist, the Farmer, and the Laborer. 

I have always considered the importance of constructive criticism as a necessary factor to reach a more concrete objective. This criticism must be based upon honest conclusions and must be entirely without prejudice. If we could obtain all of the criticism there is in our country today it would take but a short time to remedy the existing conditions. 

However, it seems that some of the criticism offered has been of an individual nature, in that it considers self-preservation to a selfish degree. Self-preservation is necessary and most important from the individual or national point of view but the individual and the nation must learn "There's a limit to everything." When we learn where this limit is, a more concrete plan can be formed. Social justice will then be born to this new era for a better understanding among the people of our country, and between our people and the people of other countries. The radio will carry our thoughts toward this objective and the truth will then be known. Honest people will make honest government. Honesty will be practiced at home among the people and also in the relationships with other countries and their people.

PART I INSURANCE 

Government is the people's prime insurance agency, whether it be the National, State, or Local government. The National government should be that insurance agency which protects and promotes the methods of livelihood of its people. Also to insure them social justice and economic stability; it insures them against dangers foreign and domestic, and insures them as individuals against dangers public and private. These safe-guards should be amended into a constitutional system of government whereby the whole voting population of the nation shall select a system of governmental operations that shall be of them, by them, and for them, at all times. 

In order that the above suggestions could be practically carried out, the whole people of the nation should vote, on a day set by the Congress of the United States and to be known as Constitution Day; thus exercising their constitutional rights by, approving amendments that they feel would be beneficial if enacted, or eliminating some amendment

that has been enacted and is thought harmful to democratic government. This Constitution Day should be free from voting on political candidates and be broad enough in scope to include constitutional amendments of the State and Local governments. To my way of thinking, the practical functioning of a democracy in such that each person eligible to vote is a ruler equal to a king, queen, prince, or princess. The persons chosen by them to operate the governmental systems are no more than their agents who are to carry out the wishes of the majority of these individual rulers. It is also my belief that those persons who do not vote and are eligible to do so, break down the perfect functioning of the democracy and forfeit their powers as rulers to become an inconsiderate class of which the country is burdened.

It is important to begin with such insurance that protect and promote the methods of livelihood, such as the right to earn a decent annual income and the pensioning of those persons who are considered too old to work in the industrial machine. The insurance of Health, Education, Police, and Fire should be divided between the National and State governments, the National government providing for health and education. The various life insurances are needed but they should be issued by the National government. This will stop many of the abuses now existing on the part of both the insurance company and the insured. This will be entirely a free will insurance. Nursing, hospitals, and the departments of health should be open to all. I will in the following make mention of the different insurances with

regard to their importance for protection of our means of livelihood. 

Employment Insurance: We must guarantee those persons dependent upon their services to others for a living, an annual income consistent with the decent living conditions of that class. 

Pensions for the Aged and Disabled: We must care for those persons who are not able to secure employment because of their age or physical disability. These persons should receive a pension sufficient to care for them above the bare necessities of life.

Life Insurance: We must set up a sound businesslike organization for the maintenance of life insurances. There are many reasons for placing life insurance upon a national basis. A well-known fact that perfectly established itself in the depression was this, why do we have private insurance agencies that, in time of emergency, the government must loan money to keep financially stable? If an insurance company cannot secure itself in such manner so that when it is called upon to serve its purpose, it is unable to do so, then private insurance has failed. The National system would really care for the injured and the person responsible for the injury would have a difficult time fighting the government to evade paying the penalty. The courts where much of this has occurred will find it more difficult to pull the wool over the eyes of the government officials. Steps such as these will really bring about a realization of social justice for all the people. 

Health Insurance: This insurance is to be used to support the Department of Health and the public

hospitals which are under the supervision of a Federal health officer and under a single department. Each state should have its own health officer who would be a member of a national commission. 

The local health officer should receive from this Health Insurance on a per capita basis the money necessary to run the hospitals and the departments of health. The employees of the hospitals and departments of health should receive above their respective salaries a profit-sharing fee from the locality for their efforts in preventing epidemics and their work in caring for the sick in an efficient, healthful manner, thus bringing that locality to higher health standards. The money necessary for this insurance is to be taken from the federal tax rolls and to be a part of the national budget. I believe that it is the duty of the government to give its citizens decent funerals. 

In a good economic system there is enough money for everyone and if a person did not have some at the time of his death then that person was too faithful to the system. Then those of us who are sharing this wealth should not object to give this person a decent funeral.

Educational Insurance: This insurance organi zation shall be supervised by the Secretary of Education of the Presidential Cabinet, (this office is further described later on in this article). These educational institutions of the nation are to be financed by the national tax plan as part of the budget. The money necessary for the maintenance of these institutions is to be allocated to the various states, taking into consideration the number of educational institutions in the state and the number of enrolled stu-

dents. The Secretary of Education is to have an assistant in charge of national health who is also chair man of the national Health Commission. The Secre tary of Education himself is to be Chairman of the national Educational Committee. There is one member from each state on each of these committees. 

As far as I have gone, I have tried to give a general outline of these insurances according to their importance and to the method of financing them. 

The time has arrived when we are compelled to face these questions frankly and boldly and to follow truth wherever it may lead us. Upon us is the responsibility of removing the obstacles in the road to economic stability. If the conclusions that we reach run counter to our prejudices, let us not flinch, but bravely face these facts as truths. We can then hope for a more perfect nation and progress will show us the way.

State Insurance Agencies: The state govern ments should adopt insurance systems that would act as the agencies for the protection of real estate and personal property against fire or other destructive forces. Motor vehicles should be insured by the 

State before they are allowed to operate upon any public highway. This could be brought about by levying an insurance fee that would license the vehicle to operate. (This will replace the present license plate system operated by the State). This 

Motor Vehicular insurance would help support the State police department. It will then become the economic benefit of the police as well as the motorist to insist upon a practical code for the safe operation of motor vehicles upon public highways. I

believe that if our present laws were amended to set up a code-of-operation for the movement of motor vehicles upon public highways, they would be more easily enforced and comprehended. This would go farther towards causing obedience to the law and would correct many existing abuses. We should have one main statute to cover every movement of operation of motor vehicles upon any public highway. This statute is to have two parts, the first a reckless-driving act and the second an act to cover drunken driving or driving under the influence of drugs or narcotics. The reckless-driving act would set up a code of particulars about unethical or criminal operation. This law should be designed to set up a moral responsibility on the part of the driver by making that person realize at all times how the vehicle is being operated. The instant a parked car starts in motion and until it ceases motion it is governed by this law, or the instant a vehicle begins movement into any public highway it is governed by this law. This omits parked vehicles which are under supervision of local ordinances. Double parking can be considered as blocking traffic and therefore would come under the state law. The profits of this insurance agency should, above cost of maintenance and a reasonable profit go to help support the state police department. The other insurances that are taken out for the protection of property should also be used to support the police department. These members should receive, above their respective salaries, a profit-sharing fee for their efforts in keeping accidents and road hazards at a minimum. (The state police force is further considered in this article

as the consolidated police departments under the supervision of the Commissioner of Police for the State.)

Fire Insurance: This agency would levy the in surance against fire upon property both real and personal. This agency would wholly support the fire departments within the State. It would be the duty of all members of the various fire departments to see that the construction of buildings was in accordance with the fire insurance laws and that a check was kept on fire hazards. These members would share from this insurance agency a profitsharing fee for their work in keeping fires to a minimum. Many social problems now affecting the fire departments would be worked out through cooperation on the part of the members and the insurance agency. It would be necessary for these members to have some concern about who was to run the insurance agency. From every point of view of economics it can be seen that systems of insurance such as these are much better with regard to moral and social relationships.

PART II REORGANIZATION OF GOVERNMENT 

To accomplish such an aim as a practical government, that is, of the people, by the people, and for the people, my first suggestion would be to consider such changes as would make the national, state, and local governments practical functioning organizations. These governments should be so divided as to set up certain duties for each government, so the state governments would not duplicate what the federal government is doing and the local governments would not duplicate what the state governments are doing. The methods of taxation should be such as that there are no duplication of tax methods. The national government should be looked upon as that organization of which the state governments are a part as well as the local governments. The state governments should be looked upon as a part of the national government and also as a superior government to the locality. In the following paragraphs of this article I shall define what each government should do, so that all local and state governments shall comprise a national government that is one synchronized mechanism. 

This national system of governments will in a practical manner establish a system of governments for a civilized people free to rule themselves by means of a Cooperative Liberal government under a prac-

tical democratic constitution, and free from jungleindividualism.

Reorganizing the National Government: The 

United States Senate should be eliminated and a House of Congress set up to be composed wholly of representatives to Congress. The Vice-President of the United States would become the Speaker of this House of Congress. The duties of a Senator are in many respects similar to those of the President. 

A senatorial position is impractical mostly because a Senator under his constitutional duties must serve the whole people when he is elected from only a part of the people. In other words, it tries to make a statesman out of a politician. Representatives are sent to Congress to represent their respective districts from which they are elected to act as a direct voice of the people of these districts. Senators can stop them if they believe such legislation to be harmful to the whole people of the nation. The Vice- 

President can, as Speaker, give his opinion about such legislation. The Vice-President is elected by the whole people and he can then truly act as a representative of all the pepole. The President is likewise chosen by the whole people. Therefore, why is it necessary to have persons who are elected from only part of the people perform duties similar to the Presidential duties? I am sure this change in the Congress would eliminate much of the buckpassing that has been so evident in the past. 

Election of the President and Vice-President by 

Popular Vote: A question frequently heard is, do the people of the United States really elect their 

President? I find that there are two answers, one

answer being yes in a theoretical sense and the other is no in a practical sense. At the conventions where candidates are nominated for the Presidency and Vice-Presidency the delegates attending are not directly voted upon by the people to nominate any particular candidate. This makes the candidate chosen at the convention not a truly selected candidate for that party insofar as the mass of people of the country are concerned. Quite naturally the question comes up, what can we do to change this? 

I suggest that the people of the nation nominate these candidates by popular vote. There have always been several leading men or women in all the political parties who stood the better chance of being nominated but there was no conceivable method of foretelling who was to be the candidate for the Presidency or Vice-Presidency. So the practical way in which to select these candidates by popular vote would be for the different political parties to meet at a convention and select not more than ten, nor less than five, candidates to run for the nominations of President and Vice-President. These conventions should be held not later than the fifteenth of January of the Presidential election year. On the first Monday in March of this same year there should be held a nation-wide primary election to nominate by popular vote candidates for the Presidency and Vice-Presidency. The candidate of any party who shall receive the largest number of votes of any of that party shall be nominated as the Presidential candidate of that party, and the candidate of any party who shall receive the second largest number of votes shall be nominated as the Vice-

Presidential candidate of that party. On the first 

Tuesday after the first Monday in May of this same year there shall be held a nation-wide election to elect from these nominees a President and Vice- President. The candidate of any party who is running for the Presidency and who shall receive the largest number of votes cast for Presidential candidates shall be the President-Elect. The candidate who is running for the Vice-Presidency and of any party who receives the largest number of votes shall be the Vice-President Elect. It is then possible to have the President from one party and the Vice-President from another. Eliminate the

Electoral College. The Secretary of State shall cer tify the nominations of the Presidential and Vice- 

Presidential candidates after receiving the state primary returns. In the same manner he certifies the election of the President and Vice-President. 

The President shall be inaugurated on the fourth day of July of the same year. The fiscal year begins July first and for this reason I believe all public officers should begin their terms of office at this time. Congress should convene on the second 

Monday in September.

Sacred Election Laws: To govern the total ex penditures and the amount contributed by the individual or an individual group, it should be regulated according to the campaign, whether nation wide, state or local. In the local campaign the total expenditures should not exceed one-half the yearly salary of the Office that the candidate is seeking. 

The contribution by individual or individual group should not exceed five dollars. The total expendi-

tures for the State campaign should be regulated according to the popular vote cast in the previous general election in the State. A percentage of this total vote should be considered. The amount of contribution by an individual or an individual group should not exceed ten dollars. The total expenditures for a nation wide campaign should be regulated according to the previous vote cast in the previous Presidential election. A percentage of this vote should be considered. The contribution by individual or individual group should not exceed one hundred dollars. A candidate must file prior to the election a list of expenditures showing the total amount and how this money was spent. This reform has long been needed in this country. A person who shall donate money from his own personal wealth or a candidate who shall use his money to gain publicity shall be violating the spirit of these laws. A friend who is interested in the election of a friend can not use his personal wealth in such a way that he could attempt to excuse himself and the candidate by saying "That this was done and the candidate did not know therefore could not file such expenditures." This reform will give the honest person the same break as the dishonest person who is running for the same office. I believe if such laws were put to use there would not be so many self seekers in political positions. 

Revising the Presidential Cabinet and the Duties of the Cabinet Members: The Navy and War departments are to be consolidated under a Secre tary of National Defense. This Secretary would have a first assistant in charge of the Navy, a second

in charge of the Army, and a third in charge of 

Aviation. 

This vacancy in the cabinet would be filled by a 

Secretary of Education. The duties of this Secre tary would be to keep the educational facilities of the nation as uniform as possible. This Secretary is to have charge of the distribution of monies necessary to maintain efficient educational systems throughout the nation and would allot the money to the various state departments of education. This secretary also is chairman of the committtee of educational insurance.

The Secretary of the Treasury would levy and collect all national taxes. This secretary would work in cooperation with the states upon tax methods so that there will be no duplication. It is important from economic view-points that the money of a nation be controlled by the nation. I believe that a tax on the circulation and non-circulation of money to be the best method of control and this would also bring in the necessary federal revenue. A central bank should be created to stabilize the currency throughout the nation by the reports of the effect of this circulation and non-circulation tax upon the money. 

If some section of the country has a decline in tax revenue there must be something wrong, the people either haven't enough money in circulation or there are some people evading the tax. This central bank would check conditions there and would remedy them. This would give the Treasury Department a constant check upon the whole country. The present federal reserve system could become a part of the central bank, using its va-

rious districts as disbursing places. I believe more perfect control could be had over checks and drafts and all such paper transactions if the branches of the central bank would act as government clearinghouses. This would stop many rubber checks from bouncing around. (Further in this article there will be found a national tax plan and under the state governments there will be found the state tax methods.)

The Attorney General should handle the prosecution of all offenders who have violated federal laws, with the exception of those relating to the Department of National Defense, which is subject to military justice. The State Attorney General should be helped in the enforcement of state laws. It is important that the federal government should do something that will give it supervision over all law enforcement relating to criminals. I believe that we could accomplish much by the following suggestions: Have all major criminal law offenders tried in federal courts, federal judges presiding; have the offenders prosecuted by the Attorney General of the state; have the breakers of federal laws tried in these same courts; have a centrally located federal detective agency to aid in the enforcement of state laws and also to handle inter-state matters and other matters that are nationally important; have the detectives who now work for the local government deputized as federal officers and work in cooperation with the local and central office. 

Have all uniformed law-enforcing officers of the state embodied into a state police force. These officers would be governed by a superintendent for

each local body. This superintendent is to be a person who has earned this rank through merit, intelligence, and service. The entire state force is to be supervised by the Commissioner of Police. 

Most of the investigation would be handled by the local federal detectives working in conjunction with the central office. This set-up of law enforcing machinery will give the federal government the necessary supervision and will also give the states the right to make their own laws. 

The Secretary of Labor should be the spokesman for all labor and should be represented by a person who shall have the approval of Labor. This secretary is to see that the various laboring groups are receiving a just share of the profits of the product they are manufacturing. 

The Secretary of Agriculture should be the spokesman for all the farming people and should be a person who has the approval of this class. This 

Secretary would see that these farming people receive their just dues in accordance with the standards of the other laboring men by receiving a just price for their produce.

The Secretary of Commerce should, with his present duties, be also the so-called middle-man of business. This is the man who controls the distribution of commodities and food-stuffs and makes the producer and consumer pay for this folly. This change will give the government the power of distributing the necessities of life. This will bring about more economic freedom on the part of not only a few but all the people as well as to stabilize the economic system itself. These middle-men of yes-

terday who gypped the farmer, the laborer, and the respectable manufacturer and made the most profits by the least productive effort are in every way, shape, or form parasites preying upon the necessary elements of a sound economic system. The Secretary of Commerce, by becoming the middle-man, will help the various groups dependent upon the middleman for distribution of their raw or manufactured products into a position to relate production with consumption.

The Secretary of the Interior should handle the duties that are now vested in that department. The duties would be broadened so that this department would operate for the best results to the whole people with all natural resources and utilities now under control of private interests. The government should buy these interests out, offering a reasonable price with the aim in view of preventing the stockholders from losing their initial capital investment. In the oil industry there are probably more abuses through waste than in any of the other industries engaged in putting natural elements into consumptive use. The refining of petroleum into gasoline, motor oils, and the by-products should be done by the government. 

The by-products should be sold to private people and the gasoline and motor oils distributed through government stations. The operation of the gas stations would result in the right number of stations and the price of the gasoline would be much lower than it is today. Above maintenance and a reasonable profit the remainder of the money paid for the gasoline should go for the maintenance of all public highways. There can be no doubt that in our pres-

ent day method of living an automobile is a necessary convenience for every family. An attempt should be made to keep these natural necessities as uniform in price as possible throughout the states. 

After we have set up plans for a better distribution of these necessities and put them into operation, we will cut the national cost of making and distributing these things, thereby allowing more money to flow into the more productive channels, the industrial and farming channels. A genius of industry or invention will not be hampered or blamed for what these parasitical groups have done to the economic system. Fields of broader understanding will then be found and a person's intellect can be expanded into genius and the mind of the individual will be free to expose its wealth of knowledge. 

The remaining Cabinet members shall perform their duties as they do now.

Consolidation of State: When our country has been adjusted to fit itself into the scheme of modern progress, that is, the progress of the industrial, the farming and laboring, the governmental and political functions, we should then look toward a plan to keep government national, state, and local in acceleration with the progress of all these elements. 

This should be brought about by enlargening the territory of the States. Area should be the first consideration, then equality of area among the States. 

Today consideration should be given to the making of larger and fewer States. I believe that to accelerate the progress of the State government to that of the other functions, our country should be divided as equally as is possible into four States. The pres-

ent States to become Local-State governments. 

These Local-State governments should gradually consolidate into a single State as set up by the new State boundary. When the present States that will be within the new and larger States are adjusted to the new territory, these new States will function as the State government that is set forth in this book. 

Reorganizing the State and Local Governments: 

We should abolish the state senate and simultaneously reorganize the house of representatives so that the members of the new house of representatives are of the same number as the present members of the state senate and represent their districts as the present members of the senate are now doing. 

In other words, make the senatorial districts representative districts, reorganizing the present house of representatives and abolishing the state senates. 

This will make the Lieutenant Governor the Speaker of the House of Representatives which composes the legislative body, the Governor and his cabinet composing the executive. The Governor is to have a similar cabinet to that of the President of the United States. This cabinet will include a Secretary of State, an Attorney General, a Commissioner of 

Police, a Commissioner of the Interior, State Treasurer, and a Commissioner of Health and Education.

This change in the State government will make the State more uniform in the establishment of law. and order and it will in great measure replace the present divergent systems of Local government. 

The present Local governments should likewise be reorganized to set up managers in the cities and villages to govern those departments that pertain

wholly to the city or village. This will abolish the county system of government. Taxation for the upkeep of this new system will be surprisingly lower than what it now costs to maintain all of the various ox-cart systems now within systems. 

Duties of the Cabinet Members: The Secretary of State shall perform the duties now vested in that Department.

The Commissioner of Police shall supervise all law-enforcing agencies within the state under a body known as the State Police Force.

The Attorney General shall prosecute offenders of all State laws and cooperate with the Attorney 

General of the United States.

The Commissioner of the Interior shall supervise for the people of the State all the natural resources. 

This commissioner shall cooperate to the fullest extent with the federal Secretary of the Interior. This 

State commissioner shall be a member of a National commission of which the Secretary of the Interior of the Presidential Cabinet is chairman. 

The Secretary of Health and Education shall su pervise for the State the Department of Health and Hospitals and be a member of a National commission under the chairmanship of a United States 

Commissioner of Health. This commissioner shall supervise the educational systems of the State and distribute the funds allocated from the Secretary of 

Education of the Presidential Cabinet. He is a member of the National Educational Board of which the Secretary of Education is chairman. 

The Treasury of the State shall levy and collect State taxes and shall cooperate with the Secretary

of the Treasury of the United State to avoid duplicate tax methods.

This cabinet appointed by the Governor will give the chief executive of the State broader powers in carrying out his program. 

The legislature will confirm appointees of the Governor and during the absence of the Governor from the State or in case of death, the Lieutenant Governor shall be sworn in as Governor. Next in seniority is the Secretary of State, then the Commissioner of Police, the Attorney General, the Commissioner of the Interior, the State Treasurer, and the Commissioner of Health and Education. 

National Tax Methods: The present system of levying a tax upon the material things of life is the same as levying a tax upon one's existence. The national tax methods as well as the state tax methods must be the most just and least felt. A tax on the circulation and non-circulation of money is, to my way of thinking, the most just and least felt method for a national tax. This circulation and non-circulation tax is to be known as the direct or national system. The indirect system, would be a personal property tax system to check over the circulation and non-circulation tax to form the tax method for the State government, or indirect system. 

Direct System: Every time the dollar is exchanged from one person to another in business transactions and the money received from the employer, the employee is subject to this direct tax. This tax should not exceed one per cent of the amount circulated. The tax is paid each time the money circulates in the form of wages and the money is taken

from each individual pay envelope. The business places pay this tax in accordance to their daily receipts and pay it to the government in as short a time as is practicable. This tax system places the 

Secretary of the Treasury in a position to keep the money in circulation so that the government will receive its necessary revenue, thus keeping the country prosperous at all times. Money will then flow throughout the country in the same manner as blood does throughout the body and it will be the duty of the government to keep the circulation of money as healthful as possible. Single persons should be allowed a reasonable amount of exemption to provide for the necessities of life and any sum over this amount would be subject to an income tax. Married people would receive a reasonable amount of exemption and additional amounts for children. Incomes over eight thousand dollars and up should be subject to a compound income tax. This tax would compound itself in such a manner so that no one person could possess over three hundred thousand dollars in actual money. The non-circulation tax would be of a higher percentage than the circulation tax. This tax would begin with amounts over five thousand dollars.

Indirect System: A personal tax should be levied upon property whether real estate or personal. This tax should not exceed three dollars per thousand of valuation. Real Estate properties should be taxed according to the equity of the parties interested in the ownership of such property and taxed as personal property. A person buying a home will not under this plan have to pay the tax on something

that is being bought but will pay for the equity in such property; not until the time the property is wholly paid for will that person have to pay for the entire assessed valuation. This will encourage people to buy homes and will make more individual stock-holders in the government. For it is those people who own homes that are the real stockholders in our country. 

This direct and indirect system of taxation will separate the National and State tax methods and will also give to both governments certain duties in their relationship with the people, the National government to promote and protect the methods of livelihood for its people, the State government to protect and promote property rights for the people in the form of public and private ownership. This tax system will undoubtedly give the federal government more supervision than any of the alphabetical groups now existing by allowing the government to go into these places where tax payment investigations are necessary. The stock market could be supervised by the government officials checking the market for non-payment of taxes. To assure the government more taxes and a better supervision all stocks should be sold outright. This means cash deals in all stock transactions. These tax methods should limit the individual to five hundred thousand dollars. The possible amount of active cash is three hundred thousand dollars and the possible amount of property supported from this money should not exceed two hundred thousand dollars, thus bringing about a greater distribution of wealth. 

Finance: Finance, as the method by which the

people get their necessary medium by which the economic system is kept stable, has been very much abused in the past. Money has been referred to as the blood of the nation. It has likewise been said that the government should control this money so as to more evenly distribute it on a per capita basis. 

By this I mean that there should be circulation at all times enough money to keep our economic system stabilized. This is done by the circulation tax and the non-circulation tax upon the money to keep this money moving and thereby keeping the country prosperous. Today I find that the money of this nation is concentrated in the hands of a very small percentage of the total population. I believe that the compounded percentage tax on the non-circulated money that limits persons to three hundred thousand dollars will go a long way in more evenly distributing the wealth. This will place the wealthy, as the industrialist, the inventor, and the business man, in a category where each of these men are the same or nearly the same in wealth. There will be no desire to obtain more to preserve what they already have obtained because it will not be required so long as the wealth obtained was earned by good business ethics. 

Then come the other classes who are dependent upon finance as their medium of exchange, the farming and laboring groups. It is necessary that each of these groups receive for their productive efforts a like sum of money. The farming and laboring classes have already been referred to as classes that must have a voice in the national administration through their respective secretaries in the Presidential Cabinet in order to see that they are receiving

just dues for their efforts in keeping our economic system stable. I have found that credit expansion through interest charges has done much to bring on this present depression. Of course there is some truth about the war having something to do in bringing on this present depression, but the war is indirectly connected to this expansion of interest because of the huge sums of money loaned and the enormous interest charged to these loans. To make my point more clear let us consider five hundred dollars to represent the total amount of money in this country. We take this five hundred dollars and apply the average legal rate of interest or seven per cent upon the first circulation of this five hundred dollars. 

This amounts to thirty-five dollars for the first year in circulation. Now if this five hundred dollars changed hands only every year it would take a little more than seventeen years before the interest would equal. Then at this time if everyone were paid up, we would not only have the original five hundred dollars but there would also be a like amount of credit expansion. In other words we would have five hundred dollars practically and five hundred dollars theoretically and some of us would imagine we were richer in money. Now let us take the actual picture and include all the various interest charges that we have in this country. One person is borrowing for four per cent, another for seven, and the loan shark will gladly loan you money for forty-two per cent. 

Now let us again take this five hundred dollars and start it along this journey not setting any time between exchanges so that in the first year there can be any number of exchanges and most like-

ly ending with the loan shark's forty-two per cent. 

How many years do you think it would take to equal the real and imaginary money? The sub-normal exchange of money is twelve times a year. If we multiply all these different exchanges by twelve and then added them together we would find that time to be very short when the amount equaled. This imaginary money is represented in the stocks, the bonds, the contracts, the mortgages, and the numerous other papers that are of interest bearing value. I will now attempt to explain how the modern financiers got control of this money and then worked the system on paper values all to their own selfish interests. First, they acquired control of the issuing of our currency by buying government bonds and then printing money to cover these bonds. Here they only made a hundred per cent and this wasn't enough. They then took the money of the nation of which they had control and started a boom. They told us we were all soon to be rich. Naturally all valuations increased, the laborer received more wages, the farmer a better price for his produce and people began putting money into the bank. They then schemed to get this money and did so by rapidly raising their stocks in the stock exchange which they controlled. People fell for this and gave them real money for the wall-paper because it paid higher interest. By this scheme they had more money to entice others into the get-rich-quick idea. The time then arrived when the money became scarce, most of it in the country being of paper value, the people being so intoxicated by this get-rich-quick scheme that they were like a gang of saloon-keepers on a picnic

having a good time. At least they imagined they were. But none of them knew what it was all about. 

The time then came when payments of interest were scarce so it was believed that there might possibly be a few sober ones among us. It was then that the dollar was found to be large and the cost of making it too expensive. New dollars were issued and after receiving the old dollars these financiers discovered that they had sold all the paper they could and the money of their treasuries had become exhausted. 

The ratio of silver to gold could not be changed because the international bosses did not want this and prevented it. The same was true with regard to inflation which was also poison to them. We all know what happened when the depression came and now these financiers are upon us and claiming our very existence. There is nothing wrong with the people of this nation, they are humans, the majority of them, but there is something wrong with the systems under which they try to live. We cannot hope for a better nation that will bring about the final realization of the ideals that our forefathers fought for as long as we try to pattern our government with that of another. I believe that we should learn to the best of our knowledge from the experiences of the other countries and consider these experiences in building somthing better. The things which the peoples of all nationalities have inspired into this nation when it was first founded must always be remembered as the spirit that resulted in our being here today. George Washington knew that the powers of Europe would try to entangle us in their squabbles. He knew Great Britain would try to set

up a government here such as hers and then try to have an international connection with this country. 

This has happened in a modern way and the international financiers are upon us as their prey. We must stop this. We must set up a practical democracy as perfect as is possible. These international bankers who prey upon the world have no religious beliefs other than hypocritical ones; no patriotic spirit toward any country; no feeling of neighborliness. Their minds are directed entirely toward the golden calf of personal gain. They want to control the money of the world and the various mediums of exchange all to their own selfish interests. If these pirates would leave their worship of money long enough to consider an old philosophy that teaches "there is a limit to everything" they then could see that their limit has been reached. We cannot expect to convert these pirates but we can take from them the privileges they now have. 

There should be adopted by the countries working upon similar plans to ours, two mediums of exchange, gold and silver. Gold should become the international medium of exchange and silver the national medium of exchange. Under this system it will be necessary for each nation to have the required ration of gold to silver, or the required ratio of silver to gold. This part of the plan can be accomplished by the various nations exchanging gold for silver or silver for gold so that both metallic substances will be redistributed over the world. 

Gold would become the nation's bond of monetary security with relationship to the other nations. Silver with a fixed relation or ratio to gold on an inter-

national basis would become the nation's internal monetary standard.

If an exchange were necessary between a business group of this country with that of another country, it would be necessary to make this transaction in gold or the international medium of exchange. The person making this transaction would exchange his silver for gold and the government would make this transaction with the government of the other nation wherever this other business group was located. Under this system the governments all over the world could control international finance and commerce. When transactions were made in foreign drafts or investments, the government from where this money was leaving could place a tax upon such transactions or could prohibit such transactions. 

When the transaction dealt with substances or materials or manufactured products, the tariff could be adjusted upon these articles by the government where these articles were shipped. The ideas surrounding the practicalness of reciprocal trade agreement the world over would be much easier to work out to a practical solution. Under this plan the various governments could regulate international commerce so that a country would not receive too much of a certain substance or a scarcity of something else by the exchange of certain surpluses from one country for something that was scarce in that country. A reciprocal trade balance could then be maintained throughout the world.

If we were to travel to the far off land of China, a most unexpected sight would meet our eyes, that is if we ventured to go beyond the walls of the con-

cessions which are segregated in all the large cities throughout China. These concessions are called the foreign concessions. The people who live in them, live in very much the same manner as they would in the country they represent. If we were travelling we would naturally stop in the concession where we understand the habits and means of livelihood of the people. Prices are rather high in these concessions, so we are led to believe that the Chinese people who made these things get a fairly good wage for what they do. Here is where we are led falsely. We are cautioned by the police and our hotel keeper that we should not go beyond the limits of these concessions because of the vicious banditry. 

They go on to tell us of the many people who were killed by these bandits when they left the concessions and if we verified their story we would find they were telling the truth. When visitors hear this they haven't the least desire to go beyond these limits. 

However, should we let our curiosity get the better of us and get a feeling for adventure and decide to see what is going on beyond these concessions, the true story of the struggle of the Chinese people for financial freedom can then be seen. It would be necessary for us to learn something of the habits and the means of livelihood of these people before we could attempt this adventure so that we could live in the guise of a person who would not be suspected of being a spy.

As we travel over this country, and up and down its many rivers we learn that most of the country's industry is controlled by business interests from the United States and Great Britain. The laborers or

coolies who are employed by these industries get a very small wage which fluctuates from day to day in accordance with the produce of the industry. 

These wages at times are equivalent to our penny and very seldom exceeds our five cent piece for their daily toil. When we compare one of these industries with a similar one in this country we can see that the product costs but a fraction of what it would cost to manufacture it here. We can understand then why these businesses have interests there, and why the Chinese people are crying "out with the foreign devils."

A product can be manufactured for ten cents in China under this slavery that would cost us one dollar and a half to manufacture here under our mass-productionism. If a tariff of one thousand per cent were placed upon this ten cent article it would still undersell our market. To speak of a tariff of one thousand per cent is ridiculous. Then what can we do to stop these cheaply manufactured products from entering our country? We find a concrete solution to this problem in the restoration of silver upon an international basis. If silver was of the same value the world over these people of China would get better wages, the cost of the raw material would be greater and the cost of manufacturing the product would be more thereby bringing these products the world over somewhere near par. This restoration of silver on an international basis will not only protect our market from cheaply manufactured goods, but the best thing it would do would be to give the people of Asia their financial freedom. 

This would in turn drive from these countries the

selfish business interests and promote a better feeling between the people of Asia and the people of this country. Neighborliness between nations would then become a reality. 

In the earlier part of the history of this country it is recorded that the people of the New America were bonded by similar means to the powers of 

Europe. Through the Revolutionary War, we gained a freedom for the people of all nationalities that had settled here and through their untiring efforts we have today a mighty fine country and a very sensible type of people. We, of today, have forgotten the sufferings of our forefathers and are allowing individuals to leave this country and go to far-off lands and bring unrest to many peoples. Let us set up a motto for the world and be a nation that minds its own business. Let the government tell these people in China that the interests which these business groups have accumulated from them shall find no protection from this government. 

Let us consider that should a nation possess more than half of the world's medium of exchange the rest of the nations would abandon this medium and find a new. Such a change would make that nation which was richest, the poorest, and then that nation would learn that there is a limit to everything. Let us also consider that when anyone makes a million dollars there are many people who suffer a loss. 

Let us remember that money is the blood of our nation and must flow steadily throughout the nation by the power of the national government so that our economic machine will at all times be estable. We must at all times be aware of "blood suckers."

Direct Credits by the Government: The interest that is paid to the government would not accumulate credit interest because this interest when it reaches the national treasury goes into the general fund of the public and is again loaned out. The interest charged by the government should begin at one-half per cent per year on large loans made to states or city government or to private enterprises. This interest should gradually increase as the amount of the loan decreases with a fixed maximum of three per cent per year. This would set an average amount of credit interest for all the various types of loans whether large or small, thereby setting an average minimum of interest charge upon the total credit structure of the nation. 

Banking: There should be three classifications of depositors of a bank, the savings account, the speculative savings accounts, and the commercial to be regulated in the following manner. (a) Savings accounts would be guaranteed by the bank for safe-keeping and insurance of the account. These accounts would pay to the bank a certain percentage for this protection. (b) Speculative savings would be used by the bank for the purpose of loans made by the bank or any other speculative purpose. These accounts would pay on a mutual profit-sharing basis to the depositor. (c) Commercial accounts would pay the bank for the necessary work in making up checking accounts and the handling of financial work for the depositor.

These reforms in the functioning process of bank-

ing houses will not be impractical but will be constructive establishments. (It should be noted in the savings account that under the proposed tax systems in this booklet, savings accounts over the amount of $5,000.00 will be subject to a tax on non-circulated money).

After all of the catastrophes that have arisen from the collapse of banking establishments in this country during the past few years, the American people must make it their duty to see that such occurrences will not appear again. To do this we must find a more constructive road that will lead us not theoretically to prosperity but that will practically accomplish this.

The Three Principal Classes of People: 

The Wealthy 

When we speak of the limitation of wealth, there are many questions that arise. One question is, what shall happen to the establishments that are over this rating? When any individual creates a wealth up to this limitation, this does not mean that he cannot work any more or that the government will confiscate his place of business. The person who has reached the limitation can work at his previous position as long as his heart desires, but, after he reaches this limit, he then begins to work for those under his own management. The government will supervise all the profits over this limitation and the first benefits will go to the laborers of that establishment (although not to the extent that it would

cause unrest among other laborers in other establishments). If a further profit exists, it is used to expand the business. The people who are today over this limit would receive a guarantee from the government of this limited amount as long as they remain with the establishment under which this limitation was reached. These Industrialists, who have accumulated this wealth and keep on working without compensation to help improve the condition of the people of the nation or of the world, would become patriots equal to any statesman, who has helped the people to advance socially by the enactment of laws and regulations.

The legal age limit whereby minors may possess this limitation of wealth should be the legal age limits now set by law. This will prevent the handing down from generation to generation huge sums of money which these heirs never earn and which is kept from the general circulation of the public. 

These fortunes are like a cancer eating upon our monetary system.

Industry What part has industry in our plan of a wellrounded economic system? What benefits do the whole people get from industry? These questions find their solution in the limitation of wealth insofar as are concerned the manufacturing and selling of the products of industry. Nearly all of the material benefits of life come through the channels of industry with the exception of some raw foods. So, therefore, industry is quite important if we wish to maintain our methods of livelihood and to advance

further through these methods or new methods that may come into use.

The NIRA has as its objective a rigid regulation of profiteering within the capitalistic views of industry. The NIRA codes should set up a plan of profit sharing between employer and employee. 

Employees would know their cooperation would be rewarded.

These profits could be awarded through bonuses and vacations with pay. When the individualistic viewpoints of both employer and employee become a unionized strength, these classes will then share with each other the blessings of production of all material things and they will become neighbors beyond any point of doubt.

A question that has stirred up much public comment is whether or not industry is taking a backward step because of the NIRA codes. Some argue that industry cannot expand because of the grip these codes have upon it; that whatever they get in profit they must give to labor so how are they going to expand their factories to meet demand? We find a solution to this question where the industrialist has a limitation of wealth because such profits could be used to expand the enterprise, but the industrialist would not get any advantage out of this expansion insofar as wealth is concerned. For the industrialist who is not as yet within this limitation of wealth, the codes should allow him a certain profit, any profit over this amount would go to labor to a certain amount and any profit that would still exist would go to expand the business, until the time the industrialist should have this limitation of wealth.

The government should never regulate competition in industry where such competition is derived from the sale of the product, as long as the sale is done according to good business ethics. The product may be superior in quality and sold at the same price as an inferior product. Now as a matter of common sense it would be foolish to get the good product down so as to regulate competition between these products. This sort of competition is what advances us in our means of livelihood. 

The Laboring Class 

Labor should be organized so as to demand a just wage for the product it produces, but it must not demand more than a just share of the profits. The government, through the Department of Labor, must be the mediator for laboring classes and should represent them in seeing that they get their just dues. When the laboring man receives a fair wage under the shortest possible number of days per week, he will then be able to keep up his buying power and also have time to spend his earnings sanely.

Shortening the hours per day and the days per week will in time absorb all the unemployed people. 

But when this is done today, the industrialist shortens the hours per day and the days per week and at the same time maintains a certain purchasing power to the individual employee, but at the same time forces the employee to do more work per hour thus offsetting the difference so in reality he is getting the same as in the beginning. The NIRA codes

before they can accomplish the objective of employing all unemployed people and at the same time increase the purchasing power of the public to absorb the products of industry as they are increased in production, must set up an hourly rate of work that one man can do in an hour. A rate of man power per hour will maintain the objective of the NIRA codes and will also stop the industrialist from employing human slavery. If someone overloaded a horse that was hauling a wagon we would call the Humane Society and have the driver arrested, so then why not ask the Secretary of Labor to set up a Humane Society for Labor.

The required amount of pay must be determined by the Government or by a Mediator appointed by the government to work with capital and labor to see that labor gets its just dues and not any more. 

After we have built up our consuming power in the country to its highest possible peak, we then can regulate production in accordance with this consumption.

There should always be in circulation within a nation a certain amount of money on a per capita basis whereby this consumption peak can be maintained, but not too much money where it takes a lot of money to buy a certain product. Printing money is not the whole solution to this problem. It is foolish to take a bundle of money to the bakery to purchase a loaf of bread, and it is just as foolish to deprive the consuming power of the country from enough money to buy a loaf of bread after it has been made. 

In the past, mass-productionism has thrown a wrench into the works of our economic machine and

we hear from all sources "down with mass-productionism." We hear the same about labor-saving machinery. Under the plan of the limitation of wealth and the profit-sharing plan we find the solution to these questions. If discovery and invention were to be so limited as not to bring into existence any labor-saving devices, do you believe we would advance very far in the methods of livelihood and improve working conditions. So it seems that laborsaving machines should be allowed, but that the laboring classes should get the benefit of these machines by working less hours and receiving more pay so that the purchasing power to maintain the production of the product of the machine can be kept at its peak.

Mass-productionism and labor-saving machinery should not create too many luxuries, nor must it allow any product to become too plentiful so that the product becomes wasted. This would break down the morale of the people. The consumption of any product must be used to better the living conditions of the individual. Most of the waste today is found among the very rich but these wastes are not because there is an over-plentifulness in the country, it just isn't distributed among the whole people. The limitation of wealth will adjust this sort of practice in the future.

It is the duty of the Laboring Class to familiarize itself with the economic problem confronting the nation and they must, through neighborliness learn of their importance to the whole economic system and then maintain that relation. Then all of the people of all the various classes will share with

labor the fruits of the land, fruits of knowledge and wealth, of production and exchange. 

The Farming Class 

Agriculture is the most important industry and must, therefore, be the chief concern of all the people. Our national government, under the leadership of our great president and the helping hand of the Secretary of Agriculture, Henry A. Wallace, has made great progress toward a greater consideration of this chief industry.

The whole of agriculture is, today, under a single plan of one great farm where all of the produce of this farm is divided proportionately to regulate a supply for the consumption of these products and to maintain a minimum surplus of all the crops of this great farm. This is the greatest achievement ever made the world over. There are, however, other suggestions that may be helpful in reaching a concrete objective to the whole plan. These suggestions deal with the practicability of reciprocating surpluses of a certain crop for a crop that did not yield so well and created a scarcity to maintain a crop balance over the entire country. These reciprocal agreements would first be within the state, then between the states, through a committee of crop adjusters appointed by the Secretary of Agriculture. 

Anyone who has studied farming, or who has done any farming will know that the weather is the farmer's greatest gamble. They will also know that some crops do well in wet seasons while others do well in drier seasons regardless of the type of soil

these crops are planted in. For example, if a crop that requires constant rains was planted in some low soil because the planter believed this soil was damp enough to offset a dry season, this certain plant would not do well in this soil because it may be rich in too high a degree for this plant. This also could happen to a plant that does well in a dry season.

So in our plan for agriculture, we divide our various crops over the entire farming territory. Then, if a section of the country has a wet season we have a certain amount of wet crops and in the dry parts of the country we have a certain amount of dry crops. Then by prohibiting the wet crop from going into wet sections and likewise the dry crops through the crop adjusters, there will be maintained over the entire country a reciprocal crop balance. 

The interstate commerce commission should cooperate with the crop adjusters by aiding these adjusters in prohibiting crops in sections where there is a surplus of these crops. The railroads, bus lines, and also the farmers own means of transportation should be all checked to see that this crop balance is maintained.

The individual farmer would familiarize himself with the plan of the government and should learn that his success depends upon the success of the whole plan. I am sure that when neighborliness is practiced among the farming classes and also between them and the other classes there shall be cooperation among all these classes for the betterment of the nation as a whole.

PART III THE FUTURE 

To visualize the future we must look toward a more scientific scheme in reorganizing our government. Our government should at all times be advancing so that it can fit into its scheme any new discovery or any other improvement, so that the people who are really the government, can share in an equal advantage from this discovery. The person making this discovery should perfect his invention not to see how much money he can make but rather how much he can advance his countrymen and neighbors throughout the world in their methods of liveihood. 

The reality of world peace must also be obtained through neigborliness between nations. 

The younger generation should interest themselves in government and economics because they will soon be called upon to shoulder the responsibilities of government and industrial, agricultural, and laboring functions. 

It should be the concern of these young people to see that these elements composing the economic machine are all at their highest efficiency when the time comes for them to step in and run this economic machine.

It should be thought of the present operators of our economic machine to keep it adjusted at all

times so that when others take it over, it will function effectively.

The Creed of Neighborliness 

When the governments of the various nations of the world enact their prime obligation to their people, that is, give everyone within the nation decent living conditions; guarantee to those older than twenty-one and up to fifty-five years of age the right to earn a decent income, and pension the aged, the people of these nations will then feel proud of their country. Patriotism will be strengthened and will bind the whole people together for all common purposes. Simultaneously with these reforms poverty would be banished. When all of these social problems have been remedied, the world population will then learn and understand the meaning of neighborliness within the nation itself and also between the nations.

The Creed of Neighborliness must, first of all, be practiced at home among the individuals of the nation, in the smalest hamlet, the largest city, and among the rural population. Then it should be practiced between the people and the local governments within the state, and then between the people and the various state governments within the nation. 

When this has been done the people of the nation will then learn the many benefits that can be obtained through this creed.

To bring about a feeling of neighborliness among the world population, the world population must exhibit the same honesty and good will towards other

people in other countries as was practiced between the people of the nation itself. The creed of neighborliness when practiced among the population of a single nation must never allow the individual of the nation to interfere with the personal affairs of neighbors, or of that neighbor's household. This likewise applies to the individual nation, in that no nation should interfere with the internal affairs of another nation or try to run that nation's governmental household. When the world population has learned and has practiced this creed of neighborliness between nations, the fear of future wars between these nations will grow weak among the population of these nations.

To bring about a feeling of neighborliness between the races of the world, these races must redeem themselves by allowing each other their own way of thinking. The prejudices that now exist between these races are traceable mostly to the one race trying to compel the other race to do as they do. This is the most unnatural attempt in the whole history of this world. When these races become independent of each other and do as their nature has planned for them, unmolested by the other races, the people of these races will become humans beyond any point of doubt. If we in America attempted to compel all of our people to believe in one religious creed, we know how serious a problem we would create. This problem is identical with the racial problem to the world.

We shall establish throughout the world individual rights and freedom, when we shall define to the world population the real meaning of individual

rights and freedom. In the past and present individual rights and freedom have been interpreted to mean individual free-for-all. This misinterpretation has caused us many difficulties in trying to prove the benefits of democratic government. Whenever an individual group acts as an individual that is mob rule. We must enact laws in this democracy that will permit individual rights and freedom to all individuals within the country and that will prohibit individual groups from acting as individuals. It is practical under this form of government to have class distinction and also to have equality between these classes by the enactment of regulative laws that do not favor one class above any other. When this has been accomplished and these classes learn to respect each other through the Creed of Neighborliness, and to give each other due consideration, the people of all these classes will then share in a greater prosperity for all of the people. 

The people of a nation should abide by a code of self preservation from an individual and a national point of view, but the individual or the nation must not go so far as to exhibit selfishness. The nations of the world and their people must always realize the existence of the other nations and their people in a human regard. We of America should become the leaders of these principles and prove to the world the benefits of democratic government. Then our neighbor nations will follow the leadership of this country into an era of neighborliness in world relations based upon a clearer understanding of social justice to all peoples of all nations.

Preservation of World Peace 

If we should trace the many wars that were fought upon this earth far into ancient history, we would find that they were all fought for some financial gain, whether individual, national, or otherwise. 

Money can always be traced as the cause. Some of the political leaders throughout the world say that war is natural. I believe that war is not natural, even if there have been wars since the earliest time history can reveal. Wars and depressions are equally man made, in that depressions have always followed wars. These depressions can to a great degree be traced as the aftermath of war. 

Nature has wrought upon us many times her methods of natural catastrophes in which many people have been killed and many people injured. 

These natural catastrophes change the livelihood of the people throughout the world, and through these happenings and the experience gained from them civilization advances. So in the future should the world population bring about a closer relationship between the people of the world and find some assurance for world peace, nature would take care of her people.

Much could be accomplished toward the elimination of war profiteers if our people would insert into the constitution of this country, an amendment -to give the government the right to take over the wealth or anything that is of value (during time of war); to place all the citizens of the country on an equal basis; and to give to the people who are taking part in the actual warfare more than those who are

behind the war zones making supplies. The government should be the sole manufacturer of munitions and should control the sale of substances that could be manufactured into munitions. We should prohibit the shipment of munitions, or substances that can be manufactured into munitions, or any other substance of a food class or anything else into any country outside the United States that is at war with another country.

We, the people of the United States, through the representation of our government, must prohibit the protection of investments made by business syndicates of this country in some other country. We should call all of our military forces from any place where such forces are located outside of the United States or her territorial possessions. The protection of the citizens of this country who are in these places can be taken over through the consuls of this country who are stationed in these places. 

These policy reforms, regarding world relationships, will leave a clear thought in the minds of our neighbors throughout the world. I believe that this would insure the people of the world from the fear of the danger of war, where such war is fought for money.

Among the most important things that should be done to bring about world peace, the following items should be considered: I.

The Philippine Island should be given their independence immediately. The Government should remove the Army and Navy from the islands. 

2. The laws of world relations should be considered and enforced through a World Court. To-

day, we in our own country are only theoretically law abiding, we must reduce these laws to a practical enforcing point, then rigidly enforce them. 

This is the same as laws pertaining to world relations, they must first be practical, then enforced through the suggestions of a World Court.

The World Court is a necessary establishment but in the present world situation it would not prove practical. After the creed of neighborliness has been practiced or attempted, then it will be necessary to establish this court to accomplish better relations the world over.

There is a certain instrument that should do much to bring the world's population closer together and this is the Radio. It is even now doing more than any other instrument in existence today.

The truth must be told and the honest people must rule. The dishonest people must be overruled by scientific methods, that will give them the poison of life that they feed to the honest. These dishonest people who have a one-track mind directed toward the golden calf of personal gain must learn: that there is a limit to everything; that whatsoever they sow so shall they reap; and that they must do to others what they would want others to do to them.

When the world population will have accomplished these reforms the honest people will then rule. 

Right will be right and wrong will be wrong the world over and the social standard will be governed by a moral code of Live and Let Live. Civilization will then advance.
