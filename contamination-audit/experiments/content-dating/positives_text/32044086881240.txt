KENTISH FIRE : 

In praise of the aspiring human soul, seeking its own completer liberation: whether on a nineteenth century Penenden Heath, in Kent, or on the twentieth century High Plains of 

Kansas, or at now-unknown, distant times and places.

If all should end in frost, if grass and goat

On the frigid hill turn to ice, with solid blood; 

With praise, or curse, congealed within his throat,

If Man, and his uproar, glaze in a frozen flood,- Let none now foresay an epitaph to mark 

Life, everlastingly deposed by death. 

From Chaos Deity would reëmbark

This many-breathing life that had left off breath: 

From other clays shape midge and Man: devise 

New breath and bodies: cause lost life to spring

To some new form. Man's faith, Man's bones would rise, Called forth by Him who uses everything: 

God keeps no finished Vegas: He keeps one sound,

The stirring of renascence, under ground. [7]

II 

But why read Life's fate in sinister ice? Or maim 

Man's germ with mischievous ire? Does such prophet speak 

Self-wrecking Man to be God's most precious aim?

With a mind doom-stricken-yet superior-seek The lost Cretaceous Bird, blandly re-see The long-lost Reptile, only to foreordain A grounded wire for Deity whose decree- From long-lost life-evolved his prophet-brain? 

The placid Pterodactyl sensed no time: 

Time-driven, the nervous poet reaps despair

From fifty million years. More aware than slime 

Was the toothed Snake-bird. Prophet, than Bird. Aware 

Of all is Master of the evolving germ:

He writes no epitaph for egg or sperm. [8]

III 

Even granting doom, why write a dirge for Man- With much sententious craft and careful art? 

Invisible mind guides the hand, by foremade plan, To roll visible ball for the child, wheel for the mart, As Man part-mimics Deity's cosmic act. 

But to roll moon-ball around earth, as earth around sun, And sustain them in a void, is invisible fact 

To which short arms reach not, nor lead feet run. 

Much sound transcends the ear; sight, eye; speech, tongue. 

For Ape-man, blinking blinding skies of dawn 

And forefeeling a part-awe, no dirge is sung:

Man feels no need to mourn such primal spawn: 

Nor need, should his electric-frozen brain 

Return to Deity's gestative pain. [9]

IV 

Yet this dark word defends, this epitaph- Perused by whom, after Man shall have lost 

Body, ambition, conscience, dream, and laugh?

It is a rigorous part of the struggled cost 

Mind pays to grow. Forth, to that final strife,

The poet projects himself, a living ghost; 

Reports back dismal tale of foredoomed Life; 

Foretells a procreant and sinister host 

Shall slay Man, as he battles toward his glaive:

Thus, writes the epitaph. This is not done 

For a blind, insensible ocean's frozen wave:

Heard today, by him who fends for an unborn son, It warns, in time. A germ-maimed race-to-come 

May purge itself from a festering thorn in thumb. [10]

V 

Not having tasted, whence the certainty? 

How know that death is bitter, to the root?

Man loathes corpse-hunting worms. They cannot see, These slow dead-feasting worms that, arrogant, loot 

Both child and prophet; that cannot hear

Long dirge and scanted Gloria prophets blend; 

Nor know the anguish of the grief-wrung tear- 

Worm being worm, and powerless to apprehend. 

Yet prophet's vision waxes paramount,

Foreseeing all that his Creator can? 

Compassing death within a life-account? 

Weighing the sanctity of worm, and Man?

That he is more aware than worm- or slime- 

Proves little of his death-lamenting rhyme. [11]

VI Those who bewail dismemberment by death Rail both at death for never coming late 

And at the too-long purposelessness of breath:

Life, too tedious. Death, too importunate. 

The Dinosaur felt death's Triassic shroud 

Mucking the brute-power of that monstrous tail:

Now air-galleons, cruising over cloud, Pawn his liquid strength to power their sail. 

Infertile and unborn forever, lies 

The Dinosaur and his all-menacing seed: 

A sprightlier form, the Man, heads up the skies, Dreaming of godlier life, yet undecreed, Half guessing that the cryptic dualhood, Life-death, are halves of one eternal Good. [12]

VII 

Who deems his finite senses ripe to guess, Firmly concludes that body-stuff, in space, His stuff, in Chaos, would feel as forgiveless stress As if alive, and struck down in this earthly place. 

And the latest law that methods of science find 

Is always final to those prophets who,

Firm upon changing hints, straightway remind Man of his sequel, haul the ice in view. 

They make no grass or pebble, yet ungird 

Blank cold, or horrid heat? Announce, THE END? 

God has no period. Continuous, word on word,

He writes on wider skies, as glassses mend. 

And, if old systems vanish from the sky?

New ones are building in the nebulae. [13]

VIII 

Though he crush minute life, housing there unknown, Man, by his wit's infinitesimal grain,

Works as creator, when he crushes stone. 

From lives he builds his home, his eagling plane: 

By lives, he lives. He gathers his trillion wheat 

And grinds the living germ to lifeless dust

From which he kneads sustaining bread to eat. 

Eager, docile lives, in myriads, must 

Cease their breath, to source his: Unmourning, he 

Stirs egg, spice, wheat, and cane to unctuous cake: 

Spreads on his bread life-reaping sweets of bee,- 

But doubts, securely, that mere God can make,

As he with cat-gut souls a violin, Some finer essence, from his upflung chin? [14]

IX 

Convict God-with the senses' range so short?

Pass sentence-with so much of the evidence out? 

Dismiss the spurious case, and seek report

From antennae catching more of what God is about. 

To beseech an endless, deictic, hosannaed chant,

Or a flesh-and-blood body, breathing through all time, Stultifies God and makes living toxicant, And Heaven, a Promethean rock of Creator-crime. 

Change is God's hand-maid, altering the days; 

Change is Life's lover, and Time's happiest tale.

Grandly, then, through the fast-dizzying haze, Breast the Great Mystery's congealing gale. 

Change, the essence, fruits repose from toil, Hope from grief, blue crocus from blank soil. [15]

To chafe that change is endless, and to sigh

That God makes and re-makes recalcitrant stone, Come from universe-neglect by the partial eye

That, squinted selfward, values self, alone. 

Compost, rottening by the October wall, Is marigold, to make men smile, mid-June, Or corn, to make them thankful, by late fall. 

Summer leaves rot under winter moon 

As sorrow rots out laughter; but memory smiles 

At rich loam, pregnant with spring flax and maize. 

Meteor dust, showered dead, through light-year miles,

Can green the mountain grass where wild goats graze, Or, falling a rock, sink deep into the earth 

To nourish unguessed, future life toward birth. [16]

XI 

Now, with our gill of time, our spoon of 

To poison life and seek to poison death?

To murder faith and gain a barren place 

Takes myopic fury and a self-cursed breath.

Faith, both dependable and competent, Contains an essence that is lost to Chaos:

She vows by seed, rain, light, in eloquent 

Disdain of altars raised to Setebos.

She intercepts tranquility from air; 

Waved and kilocycled without cue,

Pulses a dial whose orchestration's flair Broadcasts the universal, major clue: space, Damn life's known gift, damn this good lustiness, Because of death's bad theft, we only guess? [17]

XII 

Who feels as though today were God's last loom,

Man's history on it, woven and complete, Ready to be cut out and sold to doom, Its drab yarns furnished by the farm and street,- 

Is only science-throttled. With blinking eyes, Who strains to see that last, that ice-locked gate 

He does not see, should rename the thing surmise. 

Deity's plan? His own ultimate fate? 

Though algid where the sleet-drifts interwreathe,

These may thaw out again, and warmly limn 

That final Nothingness with magnetic sheathe: 

A street's-end sunset, rosy synonym 

Of yet remembering mercy, may make long 

Requiem end in penitential song. [18]

XIII 

The blindliest-shuffling herdsman may at night

Strip down a limpid, more significant sound 

Than milk in pail. Above, at melodic height, Stars stream to him. Nearer, in minor round,

His listening roof is flailed by ringing sleet; 

Or summer winds warm, fragrant chords may blow:

The herdsman, unfrustrate in his rich retreat, May measure much that lies outside of woe.

Man's darkling roofs, town-girt or country-lone, Are articulate with the challenge of one who dares

Define a justice, inscribe the meteor-stone, Fashion a thousand beauty-guided wares, And name himself-of grief and hope the king- 

Mind, attemptant, before the great Lifespring. [19]

XIV 

To melt earth's coming glacier in the glow 

Of evening's bud and morning's unfailing bloom; 

To plant the bulb beneath November snow;

To raise the temple on the volcano's tomb; 

To blast the rock and carve its enraptured mouth; 

To bruise the root and paint its dream-seen face;

To plow for harvest in the hungry drouth While the heart abides in some faith-nourished place,- By such stance, only, has Man gained his Good. 

Building on faith, he gave the poet home 

And relied on what his senses counseled would

Induce all needful harvest. He looks to loam 

To bear his food, his healing, grave and flower, And raise new issue for the future hour. [20]

XV 

His early ledgers brought Man scanted end. 

His world held more than this half-sighted man Could reach to gather, or question to apprehend. 

The unknown sired his school. One day he can 

Exchange war, plague and storm for harmless air.

Losing his droplet he so briefly owns, The wastrel drips down darkly on despair; 

While the thrifty, surging upon stars and stones, Is party to the worlds in flow and ebb, Glimpsed through blind rock boiled to far-sighted lens: 

He computes Arcturus by a spider's web, While smarting a yet completer glass to cleanse: puny mind would penetrate all space His 

To bequeath its secrets, solved, to a fear-free race. [21]

XVI 

And when he battened only upon self, Man starved, till he exuded war's horrid stench: 

Rejecting both the Ghibelline and the Guelph,

He built prison work-towers, dug the putrid trench: 

Till the poet came, to resurrect, as new,

The old lost power of renovating time, To reattest old justice, to restamp the true 

Old litany of love within the rhyme.

Each warring age thus learned, anew, to know- 

Through poet's peace a defamatory sword:

It chose to spar and weep, or sing and sow- 

Appraising concord by harsh disaccord.

Thus, through clean suckering, the race shall run To a peace-fed genus, closer to the sun. [22]

XVII 

War's victory, defined and reasoned well, Is disclaimed by death. More victorious the dead child 

Than all the war-lords histories can tell.

Cut down in the dimpled years, in the undefiled, His was no claim to eminence, or power: 

His was a humble, world-unnoted name;

His, an unheralded, ungilded hour,- No army, battle, victory, acclaim. 

Yet his was more than all: His shortened days, Passed consummately beneath a common roof, Valored in love, in peace, in simple ways, Leaving a memoried pricelessness, in proof: 

Victory, his: replete with innate worth, His soul conceived no greed, no hate, no dearth. [23]

XVIII Grown more composite with each new-heaped mound, Our race-mind-separate from, yet part of each— 

Stores all the wisdoms that the dead have found.

It persists, an entity, whose impacts reach 

Philosophies and acts of living men. 

It adds to their environment and blood, More significantly than all of science-ken, Truths that have climbed from earliest primal mud. 

Books guard race wisdoms in the printed word,

Garner these fruits of new and ancient mind: 

Faint hope that in some vanished dreamer stirred 

Is reality, succeeding dreamers find. 

Poet, bemused, dream high your dream, today: 

Its consummation future life shall sway. [24]

XIX Who tries, fails, tries, fails, tries, yet keeps in sight Of what he struggles toward, could deflect sun rays To a completely rime-encrusted night 

And thaw its ice-floe's life-forbidding glaze.

To try—and try—could heal the procreant germ 

Of defects that its forebears' failures brought,- 

When, caught in the brambles, their wit waxed infirm,— And envigor it with all they had not wrought. 

Whence Man's unlanguaged music? His powerful knack 

Of impossible-dreaming? Of patterning his delf?

With his scrivenings, his potter's wheel, his plaque, This tryer is ambassador for himself. 

A god who would maim such germ—then, bid it fly— 

Could not make a race with its heart set on the sky. [25]

XX Mark how the dreamer builds Man thus and so, Fills him with all he dreams and fancies fine, Then looks about and sees how far below His pattern is this pen of swilling swine. 

Yet, he is one of them,-with dreams in him. 

Careless, he may unpattern his dream with self, Yet ever he clings to it and, patient, prim, Frames his race-greedy picture for his shelf. 

One day Man's greed to master, his greed to know 

Shall be accredited as saving sin, When greedy dreamers-of-heaven, listening low, Shall hear long-coveted, distant viols begin

The Maestro's fate-motif, as his raised baton Commands a super-life's first bow be drawn. [26]

XXI 

Not more impressive is that portraiture

Of galactic marvel, out of unvisioned space, Than, illumined by lens, in undreamed miniature, Is the invisible cell, near, warm, and commonplace.

The far, though far, first yielded completest sight; 

The near, though near, remained less manifest. 

Man, his least known, stands nearest. Friendly light

Granted the reachless image, made senses rest Secure in its presence. By it Man has learned to know 

And chart moon-mountains, unestrangeably:

Made disparate time and space together grow And reckoned by them, interchangeably. 

Yet he to a mightier wrestling shall leap, intent 

To win from self his full affranchisement. [27]

XXII Today mind on its telescope rides out To worlds that once were fatal sin to trace; 

Tomorrow, what? Much now unrhymed about 

Shall be cancelled by this mind that cancels space. 

Wherefore completion? Creation seems aware

That stagnate life is always most disabled. 

Though prophets, hinting devils, may declare

Arch-enemies in life's secret germ-cell stabled,- 

Mind on mind, by one intent inclined, Shall solve the close-hid secret of the cell; 

Tipping no scale for, or against, mankind, Shall free us of our mad, self-centered hell: 

Shall lose us war and wrong, and fear and fate: 

Shall atrophy the will to dread and hate. [28]

XXIII When earth was flat, cowards stood back from the edge; 

When globed, they yet preferred to stay at home, Disdained a heavens-hoist from Parnassus ledge, With crystal bowls screwed, doubling, to sky-dome.

Yet globe and high-heaven won . . . But now, dwarfed in space,

Earth recedes to a point, with heaven fled from its sky. 

But glasses, blotting earth-point and its pigmy race, Cannot thus silence forever Man's aspiring cry.

For there is a spirit that, purposed above despair, And steeled above fear, can ice, fire, space outbrave:

Can wrest a way to some up-running stair 

That leads well out of the abyssal cave:

Always, somewhere, safe-kept, a child is born That of cataclysmic end none need forewarn. [29]

XXIV Though all about be plague and death and fear, Day after creeping day the heart hopes on 

That a serener, wiser age is drawing near. 

At last appears the day of blessed dawn,

The hour, whose wrapt perfection must suffice New devastation that on it shall lean. 

Thus waiting, ages pay their sacrifice In faith, and live but by a vision seen; 

Until appears in deep, perfected strength, One soul who satisfies all hopes, all tears.

He comes, he glows, but must depart, at length, The dayspring of new hope through darkened years: 

And cycle patient cycle must displace 

Till dawns, again, a savior of the race. [30]

XXV Age-patient Man who, at last, with his faery glass, Roams the long waited realm of the swift light-year, Returns to remember points that he could not pass

In the heart's long-haunting, unknown aerosphere. 

He has always felt there were stars he has never found In this aerosphere, where multiple systems throng, Whose mystically supersonic chords resound 

And hover, at times, over his spontaneous song.

The gadget is helpless, helpless the lens, to trace 

That essence of the light and of the dark

No scale can weigh, nor logarithm place 

For it lies beyond what dullard sense may mark.

Yet its freighted ships, with swift, intangible oar, Sweep in from the ports of Time, to touch our shore. [31]

XXVI 

Elusive, yet heart-near us, Beauty mocks 

Our fears. A shadow, strange with radiance, Finger to lip, with age she sedately walks:

Finger to lip, with youth flits in the dance. 

Her secret is inviolable. She keeps

Its import,-half glimpsed by us, half guessed,- 

Yet whose dénouement not one ever reaps,

Though sensed as truest of all things, and best. 

Yet Beauty ever gives. Her generous hand 

Is filled with immortelle of earthless white 

Whose brilliancy we cannot understand:

Feel only, through it, that strange, still delight That, inexorably, constrains to grant our trust 

To Beauty. We grant, because we must. [32]

XXVII 

Should Man one day learn to extract her voice,

Catch it, and amplify by tube and wire, Will she teach that old philosophy of choice By which to shortly gain the heart's desire? 

Or, command him to that hyperborean place 

He, the adolescent Man-child, dare not own? 

Or say, in secret, how the vanished face 

May be recalled out of the lipless stone? 

Or, propelling Phoenix-ash through breathless air,

Will she give him share in the systems' wide emprise, To rise, rewinged and intimately aware, Upon some star that now unvisioned lies?

Or, will she say how slow and plodding art 

Will get him bread and bed, and a peaceful heart? [33]

XXVIII Close-netted, amplest words draw in but part 

Of the gist of what the half-schooled senses snatch:

Half-sweeps of half-sweeps of the Almighty Art, 

An evasive, shadow-finny, ghosted catch.

And the unample words-conclusion, end, and death- Long mouthed as final, disinherit all 

That calculative, subtler, bloodless breath 

Of outer realms, where Man-words sag and pall.

The once-final noun and verb of priest and school 

Begin to sway; too slight to apprehend,

They shrink in stature as mind deflates its fool And feels embryonic powers that move to amend Inadequate speech. Seraphic as Seraph's shall be 

Communion implicit in tomorrow's imponderable sea. [34]

XXIX 

No word dares trail hearts out upon those hills, Unswept by eye, untenable by feet; 

Yet, real as last year's cry of the whippoorwill 

Nightlong above the stubbled, shadowy wheat; 

Real as love of mothers, gone to dust, For children, gone to dust before the Flood,- 

Constantly, and uncorrupt of rust, A wordless call sustains faith in the blood.

In creation's mighty flux, a drop am I; 

Yet, pulsed with galactic flow, partner in power, Dim cognizant of this dim earth where I lie, Gyral, dynamic with the spinning hour,—

Upon the moving disc I straightly trace 

Not requiem, but a Gloria for my race! [35]

XXX While prodigious headlines bomb the suns, Or decimate helpless Man on his pin-point spot, Faith, unconcerned with creaking skeletons, Leaves fear to terror with the what-is-not.

While sensual mind recoils in consuming awe, Spinning tales to rouse the general fear When Deity does not follow Man-made law In realms that are not solid, warm, and near,— Deep in the germ strong militaries rest, To rise, reclaimant, as Man's need shall be: 

Deep in the germ, the firmaments attest, Lie genes that sire him by eternity:

Though he sink by fire, or bomb, or slowing sphere, He shall but rise, along some new frontier. [36]
