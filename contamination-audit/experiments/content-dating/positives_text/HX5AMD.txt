California Names THEIR HISTORY AND MEANING 

MERICAN TRUST COMPANY, through its predecessor banks, traces its history back to 1854. It is natural, therefore, that we should share with other longestablished California institutions, and with all this State's sons and daughters, a pride in its glamorous past.

This storied past still lives in many California place names, recalling romantic and historical people and incidents. This little book, containing many of these colorful place names with their pronunciation and meaning, is our modest contribution to the State's historical record. 

We first issued "California Names" in 1933. A second edition, published in 1935, contained additional names and certain revisions resulting from the helpful criticism of authorities on the State's history. 

This reprint of the second edition is proffered to those whom we were forced to disappoint when our supply was exhausted.

Agua Caliente (Sonoma County) 

Ah-gwah Cahl-yayn-tay . . . hot water. Aguas 

Calientes means hot waters. 

Ahwahnee (Madera County) 

Ah-wah-nee... Awani, signifying a deep or grassy valley, was a large Indian village at the foot of Yosemite Falls.

Alameda (County) 

Ah-lah-may-dah . . . An avenue or boulevard shaded by trees. Derived from Alamo (Ahlah-moh)... poplar or cottonwood. Alameda was first called Encinal (Ayn-see-nahl) ... oak grove.

Albion (Mendocino County) 

Al-bee-on... New Albion (New England) was the name given to California by Sir Francis Drake. For a period thereafter, cartographers designated California as "Nueva (New) Albion" on the early maps.

Alcatraz (Island) 

Ahl-cah-trahs... Pelican.

Balboa (Explorer) 

Bahl-boh-ah ... Vasco Nuñez de Balboa, Castilian nobleman, discovered the Pacific 

Ocean at Darien, Panama, in 1513.

Benicia (Solano County) 

Bay-nee-syah... Named in honor of Francisca Benicia, wife of Gen. Mariano G. Vallejo.

Berkeley (Alameda County) 

A member of the committee assigned to select a site for the University of California, on viewing San Francisco Bay and the Golden

Gate, quoted the stanza by George Berkeley, Bishop of Cloyne, beginning: "Westward the course of empire takes its way." A confrère said: "Why not name it Berkeley?”

Bodega (Sonoma County) 

Boh-day-gah... Bay named for its discoverer,

Juan de la Bodega y Quadra. 

Cabrillo (Explorer) 

Cah-bree-yoh . . . Juan Rodriguez Cabrillo,

Portuguese navigator, sailing under the flag of Spain, discovered San Diego Bay in 1542.

Cajon (San Bernardino County) 

Cah-hohn ... A big box; boxed . . . . . hence 

Cajon Pass. 

Calaveras (County) 

Cah-lah-vay-rahs . . . Skulls. Capt. Moraga, who led the first expedition up the Sacramento and San Joaquin Rivers, found quantities of skulls, relics of battles fought between 

Indian tribes for possession of salmon in the 

San Joaquin. California ...

Cah-lee-fohr-nyah . . . Etymology uncertain. 

The name "California" so far as is known, first appeared in a novel entitled, “Las Sergas de Esplandían"... "The Adventures of 

Esplandían," written by García Ordonez de

Montalvo, and published in Toledo, Spain,

between 1510 and 1521. California was the name given to a fabulous island, rich in minerals and precious stones, said to be the home of a tribe of Amazons. 

Bernal Díaz, companion of Cortez, wrote of 

California as a "bay" following an expedition to Lower California.

Calistoga (Napa County) 

Samuel Brannan, its founder, envisioning this town as the Saratoga of the West, spliced "California" and "Saratoga" to form this name.

Carquinez Strait (San Francisco Bay) 

Cahr-kee-nez... Either from "Karkin," an 

Indian village; or from "Karkin," Greek word for crab, so applied because of numbers of little crabs found there.

Cazadero (Sonoma County) 

Cah-sah-day-roh... A hunting-place. 

Chico (Butte County) 

Chee-coh... Little. Derived from Rancho del 

Arroyo Chico (Ranch of the Little Creek), purchased by Gen. John Bidwell.

Coloma (Eldorado County) 

Coh-loh-mah . . . Derived from "Koloma,” a 

Nishiman Indian tribe. James W. Marshall discovered gold here January 24, 1848. 

Colusa (County)

Co-loo-sah... A corruption of Ko-ru-si (also spelled Co-lu-si and Co-lu-se). Said to mean "scratcher," because it was the privilege of tribal brides to begin the honeymoon by scratching the faces of their braves.

Conejo (Fresno and Stanislaus Counties) 

Coh-nay-hoh... Rabbit; or place where rabbits are plentiful.

Contra Costa (County) 

Cohn-tra Cohs-tah ... Opposite coast. Originally, this name was applied to the entire shore of San Francisco opposite the Peninsula. 

Cordorniz (Orange County) 

Corruption of codorniz (koh-dohr-neez), Spanish for quail. The California valley quail is the official State bird. 

Coronado (San Diego County) 

Coh-roh-nah-doh . . . Francisco Vasquez de 

Coronado, Spanish caballero, explored the 

Southwest, 1540-42, in a vain search for the "Seven Cities of Cibola." 

Cosumne (River) 

Coh-sum-nay... From Kos-summi, Indian for salmon. Another explanation is that the ending "umne" or "amni" means people; hence Cosumne, signifying the people of the village Coso.

Coyote (Santa Clara County) 

Coy-oh-tay... From Coyotl, an Aztec word. 

The mountain coyote (Canis latrans lestis) has a wide range through the Sierra. The coyote figures prominently in the myths of the California Indians. 

Diablo (Mountain)

Dee-ah-bloh ... 

Devil. Indian tribe of 

Bolgones, fighting the Spanish soldiers from

San Francisco, had a medicine man called 

Puy or Pui... (evil spirit) who appeared from the mountain to inspire them. 

Dolores (San Francisco) Doh-loh-race... 

... Sorrows. The site of the Mis sion San Francisco de Asís, popularly known as Mission Dolores, was chosen by Juan Bautista de Anza on the feast of Nuestra Señora de los Dolores (Our Lady of Sorrows). 

El Camino Real (Highway) 

Ayl Cah-mee-noh Ray-ahl... The royal road; or the king's highway. Name of the highway connecting the California missions, extending northward from Mission San Diego de 

Alcalá to Mission San Francisco Solano de So noma, 45 miles north of San Francisco.

El Capitan (Madera County) 

Ayl Kah-pee-tahn... The Captain; an outstanding cliff of the Yosemite Valley. The

Indian name is To-to-kon Oo-lah, from Toto-kon, the sand-hill crane, a chief of the First 

People. 

El Cerrito (Contra Costa County) 

Ayl Say-ree-toh... The little hill.

El Dorado (Eldorado and Calaveras Counties) 

Ayl Doh-rah-doh... The gilded (man). The 

Indians of Peru and other South American countries were constantly pointing out to the 

Spanish conquerors and explorers, first in one direction and then another, a land of fabulous riches, said to have a king whose body was covered every morning with gold dust, whence the phrase "El Dorado" came to be

applied to regions where gold and precious metals were plentiful. Francisco Orellana, companion of Pizarro, conqueror of Peru, wrote a fictitious account of an "El Dorado" in South America, which fitted this conception.

El Portal (Mariposa County) 

Ayl Pohr-tahl ... The gate.

Entrance to the 

Yosemite Valley. 

El Tejon (Kern County) 

Ayl Tay-hohn... The badger. 

Embarcadero (Street in San Francisco) 

Aym-bar-cah-day-roh... A landing place.

Escalon (San Joaquin County) 

Ays-cah-lohn... A step; or stepping stone.

Esparto (Yolo County) 

Ays-pahr-toh... Feather or broom grass.

Farallones (Islands) 

Far-al-lones . . . (Spanish pronunciation is 

Fah-rahl-yoh-ness)... Small, rocky, pointed islands in the sea. 

Feather River (Northeastern California)

Don Luís A. Argüello, explorer, discovering quantities of wild pigeon feathers floating on its surface, named it: Río de las Plumas (Reeoh day lahs ploom-ahs) ... River of the Feathers. 

Fresno (County) 

Frez-noh (Spanish: Frayce-noh)... Ash tree.

So named on account of an abundance of mountain ash in this territory.

Gaviota (Santa Barbara County) 

Gah-vee-oh-tah . . . Sea gull. The town of 

Gaviota was so named because a sea gull was killed there by a soldier. 

Golden Gate (San Francisco Bay) "To this gate," says Gen. John C. Fremont, in his memoirs, "I gave the name of Chrysopylae, or Golden Gate; for the same reasons that the harbor of Byzantium (Constantinople afterwards) was called Chrysoceras, or 

Golden Horn."

Gualala (Mendocino County) 

Gwah-lah-lah. . . Pomo Indian, meaning where streams meet; or the point where they meet the ocean. 

Guinda (Yolo County) 

Gween-dah... Cherry; wild cherry. 

Hacienda (Alameda and Sonoma Counties)

Ah-see-ayn-dah... An agricultural estate in the country. A "rancho" is primarily devoted to cattle raising.

Hetch Hetchy (Tuolumne County) 

Hetch Hetch-chee . . . From Hatchatchie, a 

Central Miwok Indian name for a grass or plant which grows in the meadow at the lower end of this deep valley, producing edible seeds which the Indians pounded into meal in mortars. 

Honcut (Butte County) 

Hahn-kut... Derived from a tribe of Maidu

Indians, formerly dwelling near the mouth of 

Honkut Creek.

Hornitos (Mariposa County) 

Ohr-nee-toce . . . Little ovens. Indians in

Mariposa County cooked their food in little pit ovens. First Spanish settlers used hornos (ovens) of brick and adobe to bake bread out-of-doors. 

Inyo (County) 

Een-yoh... Name given by the Indians to the mountains to the Eastward. It signifies the dwelling place of a great spirit. 

Jacinto (Glenn County) 

Hah-seen-to... Hyacinth; also a given name. 

Jolon (Monterey County)

Hoh-lohn ... The valley of dead oaks, an Indian name. 

Kaweah (Tulare County) 

Kah-wee-ah . . . From Kawia or Gawia, a 

Yokuts tribal name.

Klamath (Del Norte County) 

Clam-ath... Perhaps a corruption of “Maklaks," the encamped (people). Indians dwelling on the Klamath called themselves Lutuami, but they were known to the Chinooks by the name of Klamath, also spelled Clamet and Tlametl. 

Lagunitas (Marin County) 

Lah-goo-nee-tahs . . . The little lakes; little lagoons.

La Jolla (San Diego County) La Ho-yah... Meaning disputed: (1) from hoya, a pool surrounded by hills; (2) from joya (hoh-yah) jewel; (3) of Indian derivation.

La Panza (San Luis Obispo County) 

Lah Pahn-zah ... The paunch. At this point hunters used the paunch of a beef to bait a bear-trap. 

Lassen (County)

Lass-en. 

(Pioneers pronounced it “Lawsen.") For Peter Lassen, who blazed “Lassen 

Trail" and who was one of John C. Fremont's guides.

Lobitos (San Mateo County) 

Loh-bee-toce... Little seals or sea lions. Lobitos is the diminutive of lobos (loh-boce) which means wolves, seals or sea lions. 

Lodi (San Joaquin County) 

Low-die . . . Two versions of its name history: (1) Robert Cope, early resident and admirer of Napoleon, who defeated the Austrians at Lodi, on the Adda River, Italy, suggested the name because the site of the new California community had features similar to those of the Italian city; (2) name bestowed in honor of a great horse which brought the thrill of the sport of kings to a near-by race track. 

Los Angeles (County)

Commonly pronounced "Los An-jel-es"; the 

Spanish pronunciation is "Loce Ahn-haylace"... The Angels. Its full name, originally was "El Pueblo de Nuestra Señora de la Reina de los Angeles de la Porcíuncula" ... the town of Our Lady the Queen of the 

Angels of Porcíuncula. Porcíuncula, meaning little portion, was the abode of St. Francis de Assisi.

Los Banos (Merced County) Loce Bahn-yoce . . . The baths; a bathing place. Derived from the fact that the pools of the creek were used by the pioneers for bathing. 

Los Gatos (Santa Clara County) Loce Gah-toce... The cats. Originally called 

"La Rinconada de los Gatos (lah Reen-cohnah-dah day loce Gah-toce)," the rendezvous of the wildcats, which infested this region. 

Los Molinos (Tehama County) 

Loce Moh-lee-noce . . . The mill; mill-stones.

Los Osos (San Luis Obispo County) Loce Oh-soce ... The bears. 

Loyola (Santa Clara County) 

Loi-oh-lah... Ignatius Loyola, born in the

Basque provinces of Spain, founded the 

Order of the Jesuits and was canonized by

Gregory XV in 1622. 

Madera (County) 

Mah-day-rah... Wood, timber. So named because it was the end of a 63-mile flume carrying lumber from Soquel Basin in the Sierra sugar-pine forests.

Manteca (San Joaquin County) 

Mahn-tay-cah . . . Butter, or lard, although mantequilla (mahn-tay-keel-yah) is properly the Spanish word for butter. Dairying is a leading industry here.

Manzanita (Butte and Marin Counties) 

Mahn-zahn-nee-tah . . . Little apple, dimin utive of manzana (mahn-zahn-nah). This shrub, so characteristic of California mountains, has berries from which the Indians used to make a cider.

Mare Island (Solano County) Originally named by General Vallejo, “La 

Isla de la Yegua" (lah Ees-lah day lah Yaygwah) The Island of the Mare, to commemorate the recovery of a much-prized old white mare. The mare, with other animals, principally horses, was being transported by rude ferry-boat from Martinez to Benicia, when the boat was capsized in a squall. The mare succeeded in swimming to the island.

Maricopa (Kern County) 

Mah-ree-coh-pah... An Arizona tribal name said to mean "bean people." 

Marin (County) 

Mah-rinn... Spanish pronunciation is Mah reen ... From El Marinero (Ayl Mah-ree nay-roh) the sailor. A San Francisco exploring party had a desperate fight with the Licatiut tribe in Petaluma and captured its chief. 

He was subsequently converted to Christianity and did such good service in navigating the bay waters that he was called "El Marinero." 99 

Mariposa (County) 

Mah-ree-poh-sah . . . The butterfly. Padre 

Muñoz, who accompanied the Gabriel Moraga expedition in 1806 into the Sierra, wrote in his diary that they found a great multitude of butterflies in this section.

Martinez (Contra Costa County) 

Mahr-tee-nace . . . For Ignacio Martinez, alcalde of San Francisco, who received the grant of El Pinole Rancho. 

Marysville (Yuba County) 

Name voted unanimously by pioneers at a mass meeting in honor of Mary Murphy Covillaud, wife of Charles Covillaud, because she had been very gracious to miners who were ill or in trouble in this bustling mining camp. 

Mendocino (County) ... 

Mayn-doh-see-noh . . . From the name given to Cape Mendocino by Juan Rodriguez Cabrillo in 1542 in honor of Don Antonio de 

Mendoza, first viceroy of New Spain (Mexico). 

Merced (County) 

Mer-sed... Mercy. Captain Gabriel Moraga named the river "El Rio de Nuestra Señora de la Merced": The river of our Lady of

Mercy (The Virgin Mary), because of the joy that he and the members of his exploring party felt over its refreshing waters after trekking forty miles through a waterless country. 

Mesa (Marin County) 

May-sah... Table; high, flat table-land. 

Mesa Grande (Grahn-day): big table-land. 

Milpitas (Santa Clara County)

Meel-pee-tahs ... Perhaps diminutive of Milpa (Meel-pah): corn patches, hence, little corn patches.

Modesto (Stanislaus County)

Moh-days-toh... Modest . . . Originally it was suggested that the town-site be named for 

William C. Ralston, California financier, but he declined. A Mexican in the party said: "El Señor es muy modesto" (The Señor is very modest). Mark Hopkins commented: "That is a good name, 'modesto'," and the name was adopted. 

Modoc (County) 

Moh-dok... Indian tribal name: (1) meaning "head of the (Pit) River"; (2) "People of the south"; (3) Shasta Indian term for "stranger or hostile Indians"; (4) from Modu-kus, chief of the tribe under whose leadership they seceded from the Klamath Lake Indians.

Mojave (Kern County) 

Moh-hah-vay... An Indian tribal name. 

Mokelumne (Calaveras County)

Moh-kel-um-nee . . . Corruption of “Wakalumitoh," Miwok Indian name for this river. 

The younger Miwok Indian women singed their hair short as a sign of widowhood. 

Mono (County)

Moh-noh... Probably a corruption of Monache or Monachi (Chi: tribe or division), the appellation given these Shoshonean Indians by the Miwok and Yokuts Indians. 

Monterey (County) 

Mohn-tay-ray... Named by Sebastian Vizcaíno, in honor of Gaspar de Zuñiga, Count of Monterey and Viceroy of Mexico. Formerly spelled Monterrey and Monte Rey.

Moraga (Contra Costa County)

Napa (County) 

Nah-pah... This name is all that is left of the Napa Indians, as that tribe was virtually exterminated by smallpox in 1838. Meaning: 

(1) "fish"; (2) "near home"; (3) “abundant"; Napa Valley being, according to tradition, the cradle of the Suysun (Suisun) race.

Natoma (Sacramento County) 

Nah-toh-mah... An Indian name indicating direction. Interpreting it as meaning "upstream,' one translation gives it as "the girl from the mountains." 22

Ohr-oh-vil... (Spanish pronunciation: Ohroh-veel). This name is a combination of

Pacheco (Contra Costa County) 

Pah-chay-coh... For Salvio Pacheco, a Mexican officer; not for Gov. Romualdo Pacheco.

Pajaro (Santa Cruz County) 

Pah-hah-roh... Bird. Named from an eagle which had been killed and stuffed by the Indians.

Palo Alto (Santa Clara County) 

Pah-loh Ahl-toh . . . High tree. (Palo also means stick.) Named by the Anza expedition from a tall redwood tree, rising like a great tower, on the bank of San Francisquito (Little St. Francis) Creek.

Pit River (Northeastern California)

Sacramento (County)

Both Cermeño and Drake missed the great landlocked harbor which was named San Francisco Bay in the latter part of the 18th Century.

Shah-stah... Probably for the Shas-ti-ka or 

Sustika Indians who called the mountain I-eekah. 

Sierra (County) 

See-air-rah... Saw; saw-toothed mountains. 

Sierra Madre (Mountains) 

See-air-rah Mah-dray... The mother sierra. 

Sierra Nevada (Mountains) 

See-air-rah Nay-vah-dah... Snowy sierra. It is incorrect to say "Sierra Nevadas,” or “Sierras." Say "Sierra." 

Siskiyou (County)

Siss-kee-you... Probably from a Cree Indian word, sisikiyawatim, meaning "spotted horse." In 1828, Archibald R. McLeod of 

Hudson's Bay Company, crossing the Siskiyou Mountains in a severe snow-storm, lost most of his animals, including a noted bobtailed horse, whereupon his Canadian followers named the place "Pass of the Siskiyou."

Solano (County) 

Soh-lah-noh . . . Spanish word for easterly wind. Surname of Francisco Solano, a celebrated missionary, conferred at the request of 

Gen. Vallejo on a Suisun Indian chief who had aided him in war against other Indians. 

Chief Solano's name in his own tongue was "Sem Yeto," the "Fierce one of the Brave 

Hand," or "Sum-yet-ho, the Mighty Arm."

Sonoma (County) 

Soh-noh-mah... Of doubtful origin. Indian rather than Spanish, meaning the "Valley of the Moon," or the "Valley of Many Moons." 

Also said to be derived from "Sono" (Indian for prominent nose) and "Ma," valley or land. Hence: "Nose Valley" or "Nose Land."

Sonora (Tuolumne County) 

Soh-noh-rah... Named for the Mexican state of Sonora.

Sotoyome (Sonoma County) 

Soh-toh-yoh-me... Name of a former Chumash Indian village, perhaps meaning "Valley of Flowers."

Stanislaus (County) 

Stan-is-law... Derived from “Estanislao," baptismal name given an Indian chief who later turned renegade. The Indian name for the river is "La-kish-um-na.”

Stockton (San Joaquin County) 

Stock-ton ... Named by Capt. Charles M. 

Weber, founder of this city, in honor of Commodore Robert Field Stockton, commander of the U. S. Frigate, "Congress," also of field forces while California was being wrested from Mexico.

Suisun (Solano County) 

Soo-soon... Indian, signifying "West Wind.". 

Sunol (Alameda County)

Soo-nohl... (Spanish pronunciation is Soonyohl)... For Don Antonio Suñol. 

Sutter (County) 

Sut-ter... Original pronunciation was Sooter ... For Gen. John A. Sutter, soldier of fortune, who established Sutter's Fort at Sacramento.

Tahoe (Placer County) 

Tah-hoh... An Indian name, said to mean "big water." 

Tamalpais (Marin County)

Tah-mahl-pah-eece . . From two Indian words, Ta-mal (bay) and Pi-is (mountain). 

Tambo (Yuba County) 

Tahm-boh... South American Spanish for "inn," a wayside stopping place.

Tassajara (Contra Costa and Monterey Counties) 

Tahs-sah-hah-rah... From Tasajera, a place where strings of beef or venison (jerky) are hung out in the sun to dry.

Tehachapi (Kern County) 

Tee-hatch-eh-pee . . . From an extinct Indian tribe who called themselves Ta-hi-cha-pahan-na and who were called Ta-hich by the 

Kern Indians. 

Tehama (County) 

Tee-hay-mah... From an Indian tribe, perhaps meaning "high water."

Temescal (Alameda County) 

Tem-ess-kahl... Sweat-house, an Aztec word.

Tenaya Peak (Yosemite Valley) 

Ten-ai-yah...

Ten-ei-ya was the chief of the 

Yosemite Indians. 

Tia Juana (San Diego County) 

Tee-ah Whan-ah ... Aunt Jane.

Tiburon (Marin County) 

Tee-boo-rohn... Shark.

Tolenas (Solano County) 

Toh-lay-nahs... A corruption of Yolenos (see Yolo). 

Tomales Bay (Marin County)

Toh-mah-leez... A corruption of "tamal," 

Indian for "bay."

Trampas (Contra Costa County) 

Trahm-pahs... Traps; snares.

Tres Vias (Butte County) Trayce Vee-ahs... Three roads. 

Trigo (Madera County) 

Tree-goh... Wheat. 

Trinidad (Humboldt County) 

Tree-nee-dahd... The Holy Trinity. Bay so named by Capt. Bruno Ezeta because it was discovered on Trinity Sunday (1775). 

Truckee (Nevada County)

Truck-ee... For an Indian guide who was a great favorite with John C. Fremont, the 

Pathfinder, who gave him a Bible. Truckee

was buried, with the Bible, near Como, Nevada County.

Tulare (Tulare County) 

Too-lah-ray. . . . From “Valle de los Tules," 

Valley of the Tules (rushes), name given by 

Moraga in 1813. 

Tuolumne (Tuolumne County) 

Too-ol-a-me . . . Corruption of the Indian word "talmalamne,” stone houses or caves.

Turlock (Stanislaus County) Tur-lock... Name obtained by H. W. Lan der from Harper's Weekly in 1871, which told of a lake in Ireland called Turlough, meaning "dry lake." 

Ukiah (Mendocino County) 

You-ky-ah... From the Indian Yokaia, “yo,” 

South and "Ka-ia," Valley. 

Vacaville (Solano County)

Vack-a-vil... (Spanish pronunciation: Vahkah-veel) ... From Spanish family, named 

Vaca, and of which Manuel Vaca was the founder.

Vallejo (Solano County) 

Vah-lay-ho... (Spanish: Vah-yay-ho). For 

Gen. Mariano Guadalupe Vallejo, founder of

Sonoma, and prominent in the early history of California. 

Venado (Sonoma County) 

Vay-nah-doh... Deer; venison. 

Visalia (Tulare County) 

Vi-sayl-yah... Name formed from that of

Nathaniel Vise, a bear hunter and the reputed founder of Visalia, and that of his wife, Salia Matilda.

Visitacion (San Mateo County) 

Vee-see-tah-syohn . . . Visit; refers to the visitation of the Virgin Mary to St. Elizabeth. 

Wawona (Mariposa County)

Wah-woh-nah . . . Perhaps Mokelumne Indian for "big tree." 

Yerba Buena (Island) 

Yayr-bah Bway-nah... The good herb, which the Hupa Indians wove in their hair on account of its perfume. This herb grew on this island; hence its name. Also the name of San 

Francisco in early days. 

Yolo (County) 

Yoh-loh... From Yo-loy, the name of a Patwin Indian tribe, meaning “a place thick with rushes." Also spelled "Yolones." 

Yosemite (Valley)

Yoh-sem-it-tee ... Derived from Oo-hoó-mate, O-hám-i-te, or Oo-sóo-ma-te, meaning "grizzly bear" Indians. the name of the Yosemite 

Yreka (Siskiyou County) 

Wy-ree-kah...Formerly spelled "Wai-ri-ka.” 

An Indian word, perhaps meaning "mountain of the cave," referring to Mt. Shasta. 

Yuba (County) 

Yoo-bah... Probably from a tribal name of the Maidu Indians.
