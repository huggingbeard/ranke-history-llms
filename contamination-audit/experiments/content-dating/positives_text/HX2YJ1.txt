Pete had been gassed in the war. He shook violently at times and for that reason was refused a driver's license. Before the bloody conflict he had been the chauffeur of a very lovely old lady who had trusted him implicitly. Pete was a man to be trusted. He was genuine and anyone in that category need worry little. I sent him to a friend of mine who is an eminent neurologist. Between us we convinced the lords of law and motor vehicles that Pete was capable of hackneying.

He worked very conscientiously, happy to be rid of odd-jobbing, floor-sweeping, and alm-receiving. Before long he had saved sufficient money to invest in a cab of his own. Once purchased, he cruised about the streets continuously-he slept only four hours and ate his sandwiches in his cab. He made $22.00 profit each week.
