Vivian Flowers tackled Broadway with courage and confidence. But her feelings turned to uncertainty when she discovered that "the street" was not arranging a welcome. She becomes a dress model, but the constant pursuit of men connected with her business, who present propositions rather than proposals, force her to leave. An advertisement offering "concert and cabaret work” in South America looks promising and she is engaged. She is sent to a cheap night-club in Panama and the glowing promises of the agent are not fulfilled.

Besides singing and dancing for a salary too small to live on, she is expected to be more pleasant to the men than she understood. 

Mamie, an old hand at the racket, teaches her to promise, rather than to give. Armed with the constant advice of the older woman, she manages to get by for a while, until Maston, a tractor salesman from the States, rescues the "classiest attraction of the Plaza Zone."

Two days later a steamer put off from Colon headed south. On the dock, waving it away, stood two figures. 

Both were gorgeous, one in uniform, the other in the flamboyant costume of an Amazon of Venus. As the ship hulled down in the Caribbean swell Mamie

Rooney turned back to Captain Romero. "Well, there's where the Plaza Zone loses its classiest attraction," she sighed. "So long as you are there, Señorita, there is always an attraction," he assured her gallantly.
