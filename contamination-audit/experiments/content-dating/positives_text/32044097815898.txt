Registration and regulation of power and sail boats in Massachusetts. 

On March 2, 1956 the Legislative Research Bureau received from Mr. Lachlan F. Blair, Chief of the Planning Division of the 

Phode Island Development Council, a letter requesting information relative to "regulation... enforced in the Commonwealth of Massachusetts concerning the registration of power and sail boats and ..fees charged.. 11 Because of the intimate relationship between regulation and registration, the scope of this memorandum has been enlarged to cover motor and sail boat regulation generally, in summary form. 1 

Registration and licensing. 

The Commonwealth does not have a state-wide state licensing and registration system for sail boats and power boats.

The Metropolitan District Commission, a state agency, is empowered to regulate the use of the waters of the Charles, Neponset and Mystic rivers within the Metropolitan Park District, and ponds and other waters along which it holds abutting lands for reservations (General Laws, c. 92, s. 37). 

37). Under this grant of authority, the

Commission has established regulation for the use of the waters of the Charles River Basin, from the Charles River Dam in Boston to the Watertown Dam; and power boat operators in the Basin are required to secure a power boat permit, for which no fee is charged. No such requirement has been established for the Neponset and Mystic rivers. 

The Metropolitan District Commission is authorized to grant licenses to municipalities, educational institutions, clubs and others for the construction and maintenance of boathouses and other recreational facilities in the Charles River Basin (General Laws, 3.92, s.74A).

The Department of Public Works, under General Laws, c. 91, s.10, is vested with the ...general care and supervision of the harbors and tidewaters within the Commonwealth, of the flats and lands flowed thereby, of the waters and banks of the Connecticut river and the banks and waters of the non-tidal portion of the Mer. rimack river... The Department has not moved, under this statute to regulate, or to require the registration of, power boats or sail boats operated on such waters. It has left salt water registration and regulation to the United States Coast Guard, and has deferred to the cities and towns where fresh water operation is concerned. 11

General Laws, c.102, s.15, empowers c.102, s. 15, empowers the State Commissioner of Fublic Safety to make regulations governing the safe use of boats propelled in whole or in part by internal combustion engines. The 

Commissioner's regulations, written in 1932, do not require boat registration.

Cities and Towns may require the registration of motor boats operated in their rivers and in other bodies of water lying within their jurisdiction, but may not charge any fee therefore. 1 

Boating on great ponds2 may be subject to municipal registration requirements implicitly authorized by General Laws, c.131, s.34.Ordinances, by-laws and votes establishing such requirements are subject to the approval of the State Department of Public Works. 

A municipality may license the renting of boats for use on so much of the waters of any great pond as are situated within its boundaries, and may assess penalties not exceeding $10 for violations of its licensing requirements (General Laws, c. 140, ss.194-196).

If a motor or sail boat is used commercially as a ferry, it is subject to licensing by the county commissioners of the county or counties in which ferry service is provided (General Laws, c.88, s.1).

Municipal and county licensing and registration fees are fixed by the individual local government, to be paid into its treasury. There are no uniform fee requirements.

The Special Commission on Uniform Boat Regulations, established by Chapter 73 of the Resolves of 1955 has filed a report printed in Senate No. 580 of 1956recommending a registration fee of $3 for inboard and outboard motor boats, 16 feet or less in length, which are used on coastal or inland waters of the State, and a registration fee of $3 is proposed for motor boats over 16 feet in length if used in inland waters. The latter category, if used solely in coastal waters, would be exempted from the state registration law, since they are subject to United States Coast Guard registration requirements. Senate 580 would not apply to sail boats not carrying motors. Its provisions would be administered by the State Department of Natural Resources.

2. Regulation by state and local authorities. 

Regulation of the use of sail boats and motor boats is primarily a local matter in Massachusetts, except for the following 

(a) Regulation of motor boats by the State Commissioner Public Safety pursuant to General Laws, c.102, ss.15 15A 

(b) Metropolitan District Commission regulation of boating on the Charles, Neponset and Mystic rivers, authorized by General Laws, c.92, ss.37 and 74A, 

(c) Regulation by the State Department of Public Works under General Laws, c.91, s.10, and (d) Federal regulation, by the United States Coast Guard of boating in coastal and other waters under Federal jurisdiction, pursuant to Title 46 of the United States Code. 

Apart from brief regulations made by the Department of Public Safety relative to motor boat mufflers and exhausts, and the rules made by the Metropolitan District Commission for the Charles River Basin, no effort has been made by state agencies to exercise their latent regulatory powers relative to boating. 

Cities and towns may control boating under authority of the following provisions of the General Laws, primarily cited: c.102, s.15B, c.131, s.34; and c.140, ss.194-196. Regulations made under chapters 102 and 131 are subject to the approval of the State Department of Public Works. There is no uniformity to these regulations. 

Fire safety inspections of boats may be made by local fire departments under authority given to them by General Laws, c.148, ss.4 et al.

The Special Commission on Uniform Boat Regulations, in its recent report to the General Court, 2 has proposed certain statutory standards which would be state-wide, and would transfer the function of approving municipal boating ordinances, by-laws and votes from the State Department of Public Works to the State Department of 

Natural Resources. Local governments would continue to have authority to regulate boating, but would henceforth operate within the confines

3. Federal regulation of Massachusetts small boating. 

Coast Guard regulation of small boats under 16 feet in length is currently confined to boating in coastal and other waters under Federal control, and is very limited. 

Small craft engaged in commercial activity, such as the carrying of passengers for hire, are fully controlled as to licensing and safety.

Non-commercial small craft, under 16 feet, are subject to Coast Guard inspection as to the adequacy of their equipment if they have inboard motors; but this inspection requirement does not extend to outboard motor boats under 16 feet. 4.

"All boats propelled by gasoline or naphtha, or by steam, electric or mechanical power, on ponds, lakes and rivers, when in use from one half hour after sunset until one half hour before sunrise, shall display lights as follows: (a) On the starboard side, a green light of such a character as to be visible on a dark night, with a clear atmosphere, and so constructed as to show a uniform and unbroken light

over an arc of the horizon of ten points of the compass, and so fixed as to throw the light from a point right ahead to two points abaft the beam on the starboard side. (b) On the port side, a red light of such a character as to be visible on a dark night, with a clear atmosphere, and so constructed as to show a uniform and unbroken light over an arc of the horizon of ten points of the compass, and so fixed as to throw the light from a point right ahead to two points abaft the beam on the port side. 

The red and green lights shall be fitted with inboard screens. (c) A white light at the stern plainly visible above any deck-house or other obstruction."

Department", Department of Public Safety. d ''Muffler", any device designed and used to deaden the noise of the escaping exhaust gases of an internal combustion engine.

SECTION 3. Motorboats, pleasure and commercial fishing safety requirements and registration procedure.

The latest law affecting motorboats in the matter of their equipment is contained in an act of Congress dated April 25, 1940 (46 U.S.c. 525-526t), which superseded the Motorboat Act of 1910.

This statute and the regulations issued thereunder are applicable to all motorboats and certain other vessels propelled by machinery other than by steam more than 65 feet in length, except (a) tugboats and towboats propelled by steam, (b) vessels propelled by steam more than 65 feet in length, (c) vessels having on board inflammable or combustible liquid cargo in bulk, (d) vessels which are subject to the International Convention for Safety of Life at 

Sea, 1929, carrying or certificated to carry more than 12 passengers on an international voyage by sea, as defined in said Convention, and (e) seagoing vessels of 300 gross tons and over propelled by internal combustion engines subject to inspection and certification by the Coast Guard. The act of April 25, 1940, and the regulations issued thereunder must be complied with by all vessels subject thereto operating on the navigable waters of the United States. 

3. The act of June 7, 1918, as amended, which provides for the numbering and recording of undocumented vessels, is applicable to every undocumented vessel propelled in whole or in part by machinery, owned in the United States and found on the navigable waters thereof, except public vessels and vessels not exceeding 16 feet in length measured from end to end over the deck excluding sheer, temporarily equipped with detachable motors. The words "public vessels" as used in this act include vessels owned by the United States or by any state, county, city or municipality where such vessels are used in a governmental capacity. The exemption in favor of vessels not exceeding 16 feet in length temporarily equipped with detachable motors, is construed to apply to any undocumented vessel not exceeding 16 feet in length equipped with an outboard motor. 4.

Prior to issuing regulations under the Motorboat Act of April 25, 1940, and the Numbering Act of June 7, 1918, as amended, the cooperation of yachtsmen, yacht and boatbuilders, and manufacturers of boating equipment was solicited. The regulations, therefore, have been formulated for the safety of the boating public by practical men who represent both the Government and the industry, and their comments and suggestions were followed wherever possible in drafting the regulations. The regulations embody the safety requirement felt necessary by the public and the Government. The operation

of motorboats in compliance with these regulations will increase safety of life on the navigable waters of the United States and should not be found burdensome The numbering regulations were drafted with a view to the expeditious handling of applications for certificates of award of number for undocumented vessels and owners of such vessels will find that in the great majority of cases and with little effort on their part they may immediately operate their vessels without unnecessary delay.

AN ACT ESTABLISHING A REGISTRY FOR UNIFORM BOAT REGULATIONS AND ESTABLISHING A UNIFORM BOAT REGULATIONS CODE. 

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives in General Court assembled, and by the authority of the same as follows. 

SECTION 1. Sections fifteen, fifteen A, fifteen B and sixteen of chapter one hundred and two of the General Laws are hereby repealed.

SECTION 2. The General Laws are hereby amended by inserting after chapter 21, section 6D, the following new chapter: 

CHAPTER 21A UNIFORM BOAT REGULATIONS 

Section 1. Notwithstanding the provisions of any general law or any special law to the contrary, there shall be established within the division of law enforcement of the department of natural resources a registry for uniform boat regulations. The director of said division shall be the chief legal authority for the enforcement of all rules and regulations hereinafter adopted. Said director may, by rules and regulations prescribed by him, delegate specific authority to the law enforcement officers within his division in so far as they apply to the proper enforcement of this chapter. 

Section 2. Application and Jurisdiction. The provisions of this chapter shall be applicable to all boats navigating the waters, tidal, non-tidal and inland, within the jurisdiction of this state, except where inconsistent with any laws or regulations of any higher authority, in which case such laws and regulations shall prevail. 

Section 3. City and Town Ordinances and By-Laws. Any ordinances or by-laws of any city or town in effect at the time of the adoption of this chapter shall forthwith become null and void. However, any city or town hereafter may adopt by ordinance or by-law any rules or regulations relative to boating laws, but all such ordinances or by-laws must be approved by the director of the division of law

- 

enforcement and must be consistent with the provisions of this chapter. 

Section 4. Authority of the Director of the Division of Law En forcement, and Law Enforcement Officers. - It shall be the duty of the director or his duly constituted or delegated representative to enforce the following: 

(a) Inspections. All boats shall be inspected annually and shall comply with all the standards hereinafter prescribed. It shall thereupon be the duty of said director to notify the owner, proprietor or agent in charge of any such boat to comply with such standards and provisions of this chapter prior to further use of such boat. (b) Boats for Hire. All boats used for hire shall be licensed within ninety days after the effective date of this chapter, and each year thereafter, prior to June first of each successive year, every person or firm or corporation who shall rent or offer to rent any boat, or furnish the same with leased or rented living accommodations for use upon any of the public waters of this state, shall first apply for a license so to do from the director of the division of law enforcement upon forms furnished by said director. 

No boat shall thereafter be rented or offered for rent until such license has been granted and the boat marked as hereinafter provided. (c) Inspection Markings. With the license herein provided for, there shall be issued to all boat owners a durable tag or plate, as prescribed by the director, for each boat showing the year of such inspection and such other matters as the director may deem necessary, said tag or plate must be affixed to some prominent part on the boat.

There shall also be painted or stenciled conspicuously on the after quarter of each boat the number of pounds of weight authorized to be carried therein, and no owner, his agent or lessor, shall knowingly permit more than that number of pounds of weight to occupy said boat at any time. 

(a) Safety Equipment. 

All safety equipment, such as life jackets or preservers, ring buoys or buoyant cushions, portable fire extinguishers, lights, lanterns, flashlights, carburetors, ventilators, or any such other safety equipment, shall be inspected by the director or his officers, and required on all boats and in proper proportion as the director may require or designate. 

Section 5. License and Inspection Fee. The fee for each license and original inspection shall be one dollar annually for each boat inspected and licensed under this chapter. 

Section 6. Law Enforcement Fund. - All the license and inspection fees shall be payable to the division of law enforcement and credited to the law enforcement fund, and may be expended by the director, upon the approval of the commissioner of natural resources, for items of law enforcement only.

Section 7. Cooking Fuel. - On boats carrying passengers for hire, the use of liquefied petroleum gases and certain inflammable liquids for cooking, heating or lighting is hereby prohibited.

Section 8. Muffling Devices. 

It shall be unlawful to use a boat propelled in whole or in part by gas, gasoline or naphtha unless the same is provided with a stock factory muffler, underwater exhaust or other modern devices capable of adequately muffling the sound of the exhaust of the engine, both inboard and outboard. The phrase adequately muffling" shall mean that the motor's exhaust at all times be so muffled or suppressed as not to create excessive or unusual noise. The discharge of cooling water through the exhaust of an inboard engine shall be considered an adequate muffling device. 

Section 9. Intoxication. It shall be unlawful for any person who is found to be under the influence of intoxicating liquor or narcotic drugs or barbital, or any person who is an habitual user of the same, to operate, propel or be in actual physical control of any boat. It shall also be unlawful for the owner of any boat, or any person having such in charge or in control, to authorize or knowingly permit the same to be propelled or operated by any person who is under the influence of intoxicating liquor, narcotics, drugs or barbital, or any person who is known to be an habitual user of the same.

Section 10. Reckless Driving and Speeding. 

(a) Every operator of a motorboat shall at all times navigate the same in a careful and prudent manner and at such rate of speed as not to endanger the life, limb or property of any person. (b) Reckless navigation of a motorboat shall include operating the same in a manner which unnecessarily interferes with the free and proper use of the navigable waters of the state, or unnecessarily endangers other boats therein, or the life and limb of any person. (c) No person shall operate any motorboat at a rate of speed greater than will permit him, in the exercise of reasonable care, to bring the boat to a stop within the assured clear distance ahead. (a) Nothing in the provisions of this chapter should be construed to mean that the operator of a boat actually competing in a race or regatta which is sanctioned by an appropriate government authority shall not attempt to attain high speeds on a marked racing course.

Section 11. Accidents. The operator of any boat involved in an accident resulting in injury or death to any person or in damage to property shall immediately stop such boat at the scene of such accident, and shall render to any person injured or in distress in such accident all reasonable assistance, and shall also report such accident to the nearest police station, law enforcement officer of the division, harbor master, assistant harbor master, or to any such convenient law enforcement agency or office. A full written report of such accident shall be reported in writing to the director by those involved in the accident within five days from the date of the accident.

Section 12. Dual Authority. 

The provisions of this chapter shall also be construed as to be enforceable by the police departments of all cities and towns, by all duly elected or appointed harbor masters, assistant harbor masters or any other person so appointed, in writing, by the director of law enforcement. 

Section 13. Penalty. Any person found guilty of violating any section of this chapter shall be punished by a fine not to exceed one hundred dollars.
