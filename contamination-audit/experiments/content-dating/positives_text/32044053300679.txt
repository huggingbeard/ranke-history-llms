The Commonwealth of Massachusetts REPORT OF THE COMMITTEE ON STATE AD- MINISTRATION UNDER AUTHORITY OF THE ORDER PRINTED AS SENATE, NO. 717 OF 1951. 

To the Honorable Senate and House of Representatives. 

Under authority of the order printed as Senate, No. 717 of 1951 and adopted in the Senate on September 25, 1951, and the House of Representatives on September 27, 1951, the Committee on State Administration has made an investigation and study of the Fourth and Fifth Reports of the Special Commission on the Structure of the State Government. The commission's reports and recommendations concern state agencies and functions in the fields of industrial relations (printed as House, No. 2523 of 1951) and public welfare (printed as House, No. 2591 of 1951). 

Our investigation and study has been a continuation of the consideration of those proposals which began with the public hearings held in 1951. It has included conferences with state officials concerned, representatives of interested labor, representatives of welfare, citizen groups and members of the staff of the Special Commission. 

Submitted herewith is the report and recommendations of our investigation and study together with drafts of legislation necessary to carry such recommendations into effect.

Part A. 

INDUSTRIAL RELATIONS REPORT. 

(House, No. 2523, 1951.) 

Appended hereto are four separate appendices of proposed legislation. Except for omitting the proposed advisory council and including the separate legal division, the proposed legislation in our four appendices, taken together, incorporates the proposals in House, No. 2523 of 

1951. The major features of the proposed legislation are as follows: 

Appendix A provides for (a) consolidating in one division - a new Division of Economic Research - the statistical and research work now done by three divisions in the Department; (b) consolidating in one division - a new Division of Industrial Health and Safety the protective, investigative and enforcement work now done by four divisions in the Department; (c) abolishing the three positions of Associate Commissioner and creating a new position of Deputy Commissioner to perform executive and administrative duties under the supervision of the Commissioner; and (d) creation of a separate legal division under a counsel to the Department. No change would be made in the functioning of the Board of Con ciliation and Arbitration or the Labor Relations Commis sion, and it is specifically provided that the present Associate Commissioners shall remain the members of the Board of Conciliation and Arbitration. This proposed legislation could be enacted by itself; the changes noted in (a), (b) and (c) above would give effect to the less controversial recommendations of the Special Commission.

Appendix B provides for a Division of Conciliation under the Commissioner and would create an independent tripartite Industrial Relations Board to perform the functions now performed by the Board of Conciliation and Arbitration and also the Labor Relations Commission.

LO 2. 

Appendix C calls for consolidation of the Department of Industrial Accidents with the Department of Labor and Industries, though the Industrial Accident Board would remain completely independent in discharging its func tions. A new Division of Workmen's Compensation would be established in the Department of Labor and Industries to perform the administrative work now done by the Department of Industrial Accidents. 

Appendix D incorporates verbatim the recommendation of the Special Commission that workmen's compensation insurers be assessed certain expenses of administering the workmen's compensation laws.
