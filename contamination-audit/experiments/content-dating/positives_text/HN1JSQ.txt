I. THE HEAD OF GOLD 

"This image's head was of fine gold" (verse 32), and these are Daniel's words in reference to it: "Thou, O king, art a king of kings; for the God of heaven hath given thee a kingdom, power, and strength, and glory, and wheresoever the children of men dwell, the beasts of the field and the fowls of the heaven hath he given into thine hand, and hath made thee ruler over them all. Thou art this head of gold” (verses 37, 38).

This shows that the Babylonian Kingdom was represented by the head of gold, and Nebuchadnezzar was its first king. If, therefore, the image represents the times of the Gentiles, then these times commenced when King Nebuchadnezzar had taken Jerusalem, and had carried its wicked King (Jehoiakim) to Babylon, about 606 B.C.

The head of the image was of gold. Without doubt, in this chapter, this precious metal is a symbol of the absolute autocratic rule of Nebuchadnezzar. 

He was an absolute monarch. All power was vested in him. His very word was law. He could make laws and could change them at will. No one could question his authority. How true was the symbol of this, the most precious metal of the image.

2.-BREASTS AND ARMS OF SILVER 

"His breasts and his arms of silver" (verse 32).

Daniel's interpretation gives three significant statements, "After thee shall arise another kingdom inferior to thee" (verse 39).

We know from history that the Medo-Persian Empire arose after defeating the Babylonians in battle. Daniel 5. 30, 31, gives a brief, but accurate statement of historical facts in relation to this matter. 

It reads: 

"In that night was Belshazzar the king of the Chaldeans slain, and Darius the Median took the kingdom, being 62 years old." 

Some critics proclaimed Belshazzar to be a myth, but history-given through archaeology-testifies to the fact that he was regent in Babylon during the absence of Nabonidus, his uncle, and that he was killed on the night that the Persian army entered the city. This shows the reason why Daniel was made the third ruler in the kingdom, Belshazzar himself being only second. (See Daniel 5. 29.)

At this time, about 538 B.C., the Medes and Persians had united, and had made certain unbreakable laws. One was that the King could make a law, but he himself could not break it. It was not within his power. An instance of this is given in Daniel 6. 

King Darius had been tricked by the leaders of the kingdom into making the absurd law that, with the exception of himself, no one should petition God or

man for thirty days. When the law had been signed by the king, these conspirators caught Daniel praying to God, and they told the king. We then read in verses 14, 15: "Then the king, when he heard these words, was sore displeased with himself, and set his heart on Daniel to deliver him. 

Then these men assembled unto the king, and said unto the king, Know, O king, that the law of the Medes and Persians is, that no decree nor statute which the king establisheth may be changed. 

These verses show that the king's autocracy was lessened. The less precious metal-silver-symbolizes the limited monarchy of the Medo-Persian kings, whose powers were limited by an hereditary aristocracy.

As the single head of the image represented the single power of Babylon; so the arms of the image, joined to the breast, represented the unbreakable bond which bound the Medes and Persians together; that is, two countries absolutely united under one ruler. How remarkably striking is the symbol of this part of the image. It will be noted that the symbolism, as seen in the details of the other parts, especially of the fourth kingdom, is still more wonderful.

"His body and sides of brass" (verse 32, Revised

39: "And another third kingdom of brass which shall bear rule over all the earth."

The kingdom which followed the Medo-Persian was that of Greece under Alexander, which came into power about 333 B.C. Alexander of Greece was a great conqueror, but the still inferior metal (brass) symbolizes that his power was still further limited in comparison with that of the world-kingdom which he had supplanted. The power of the monarchy, at this time, was shared with the army and the influential men of the Empire. 

4.-LEGS OF IRON 

Of this kingdom, Daniel says: "And the fourth kingdom shall be as strong as iron; forasmuch as iron breaketh in pieces and subdueth all things; and as iron that breaketh all these, shall it break in pieces and bruise" (verse 40).

Rome was called in history the iron kingdom. 

But iron, though stronger, is still inferior in value to the other metals mentioned, namely, gold, silver, and brass, showing that the powers of the monarchy were still further limited; yet the empire itself was stronger than the three which preceded it. Though the Caesars were nominally dependent upon the people, they were really chosen by the Army, which practically held the Empire under its control. This part of the image is described in the words "Legs of Iron."

How symbolic they are of the old Empire

of Rome. Is it not historically true that the Roman 

Empire became a really great power about 32 B.C. 

It ruled in Christ's time as a single power, but after many unsuccessful attempts had been made to divide the Eastern portion from the Western, it became an accomplished fact about 395 A.D. The divided legs of the image thus became wonderfully typical of divided Rome. How marvellously God prophesied of these Gentile world powers centuries before their actual fulfilment.

Here is a significant and wonderful symbolism, opening up a marvellous vista of prophetic truth. 

Let us quote Daniel's interpretation in full, and see the significance of the emphasized words which will be carefully dealt with : "And the fourth kingdom shall be strong as iron; forasmuch as iron breaketh in pieces and subdueth all things; and as iron that breaketh all these, shall it break in pieces and bruise. And whereas thou sawest the feet and toes, part of potters' clay, and part of iron, the kingdom shall be divided; but there shall be in it the strength of iron, forasmuch as thou sawest the iron mixed with the miry clay. And as the toes of the feet were part of iron, and part of clay, so the kingdom shall be partly strong and partly broken. And whereas thou sawest iron mixed with miry clay, they shall mingle themselves with the seed of men; but they shall not cleave one to another, even as iron is not mixed with clay. And in the days of these kings shall the God of heaven set up a kingdom, which shall never be destroyed; and the kingdom shall not be left to other people, but it shall break in pieces and consume all these kingdoms, and it shall stand for ever" (verses 40-44),

Some have called Daniel a forgery; others have endeavoured to prove that his prophecy was written about 150 B.C., which is really absurd. Did not

Even Gibbons admits that Daniel traces the course of the Babylonian, Medo-Persian, Grecian, and Roman Empires with the accuracy of an historian. How truly the Bible is proved to be the veritable Word of God. 

See how definite Daniel is in his interpretation : "The fourth kingdom shall be as strong as iron," "and whereas thou sawest the feet and toes, part of potter's clay and part of iron, the kingdom shall be divided" (verse 41). We know that the present advance of democracy began at the time of the French Revolution, and it has made rapid strides since those days. This is now called the day of democracy. Every country in the world, even Japan, is seeing the tremendous increase in the demands of the people. 

The year 1939 is witnessing a violent attempt by dictatorship to cruelly stifle these aspirations; but the future will reveal interesting developments.

Of the Allies: Belgium, France and Japan have had Socialistic Governments. Italy is being controlled by the Fascisti, and Spain is now ruled by the same. Greece was a Republic, but is again a 

Kingdom under a strong-willed Premier. The Turkish

Egypt with a parliament chosen by the people. Parts of Ireland with its own Government, with Scotland and Wales waiting for theirs. 

The different parts of the British Empire more and more jealous of their democratic control.

F the different parts of the image are symbolic, even in detail, such as the two arms joined to the breast representing the indissoluble union between the Medes and Persians; and the two legs representing the division of the old Roman Empire between East and West, surely the ten toes are symbolic of ten kings bound together in a League of Empire. If this is the true interpretation, it ought to be supported by two proofs. First, there should be indications that ten kings will together hold the territory of the old Roman Empire, who will form a covenant or League of Nations with an Emperor as head. Second, other Scripture passages must support this interpretation. We will take the second premise first, and show that other Scriptures do prove this contention to be true. 

The first Scripture that we will examine is Daniel chapter 7. Daniel was given a vision by God during the first year of Belshazzar, King of Babylon. His

vision was of the Great Sea, which we know in the Word of God to be the Mediterranean. As this is a very important matter, for proofs of this see the following passages, Numbers 34. 6; Joshua 1. 4; 

15. 12 ; 23. 4 ; Ezekiel 47. 10, 15.

In the Word of God the term "The Great Sea" is not used for any other place than the Mediterranean. 

Daniel saw in his vision four great beasts coming up from this sea, each one diverse from the others. 

This vision of the four beasts is but another prophecy of the progress and development of human government during the "Times of the Gentiles. 

Let me point out that though both chapters 2 and 7 give the development and progress of human government during the period known as the "Times of the 

Gentiles," yet there is a great difference between them.

In chapter 2, it was Nebuchadnezzar who had the vision, therefore it gives the human view, and shows this Gentile government during the "Times of the 

Gentile" as a human figure, grand and beautiful.

In chapter 7, it was not Nebuchadnezzar, but the prophet Daniel, who had the vision, therefore it gives the Divine view and the true character of this Gentile government and shows it forth as a terrifying wild beast. 

Let me ask why it is that up to chapter 2. 4 was written in Hebrew, chapter 2. 4 to chapter 7. 28, in

Chaldee, or Aramaic, and from chapter 7. 28 to the end in Hebrew? Modern critics have joked about it, but God has a wonderful purpose in it. 

The book of Daniel in its original language, is so divided because of its distinctive characteristics. The first section gives the story of the fall of Jerusalem and Israel's captivity; therefore it is in sacred Hebrew.

The second section deals mainly with the Gentile powers, that is, the world-kingdoms and their glory, while Israel is only incidentally referred to. Hence it is in Aramaic, or Chaldee. 

The third section. The nation chiefly referred to here is Israel, and the Gentiles being mentioned merely because of their contact with Israel and their attitude (mostly one of hostility) towards the chosen people. Hence it is in Hebrew. 

The reason is clear. After Jerusalem is destroyed, Israel is in captivity and the Gentiles take her place. 

Her very language becomes dead, but she is again to come into her own, and her language exalted before all.

Is it not true that Israel is coming into her own and her sacred language is taking the place prophesied ; for to-day Hebrew is one of the official languages of Palestine. (For further discussion, see "The Remarkable Jew.") Note that chapters 7 and 8 are not chronologically stated. They do not come after

Daniel's lions' den experience in chapter 6. Chap ters 6, 7 and 8 come in the first and third years of

Belshazzar's reign, but chapter 5 in the 17th year. 

It is there for a special purpose, which we cannot further refer to here.

THE FOUR BEASTS 

We are now considering chapter 7, and we note that four beasts come up from the Mediterranean

Sea. We have already pointed out that this chapter gives the Divine view of what we have considered as the human view in chapter 2. We know that Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece and Rome controlled that sea, and most of the land, surrounding it. In the time of the Roman Empire that sea was called a Roman lake. 

The interpretation of these beasts is given in verse 

17 and the following verses. We read "These great beasts, which are four, are four kings which shall arise out of the earth."

The first beast, Daniel says, was like a lion with eagle's wings. How wonderfully descriptive this is of Babylon. The results of archaeology have shown us that the winged lion was the national emblem of Babylon. The British Museum holds in stone the very proofs of this fact.

The second beast was like a bear. It represents 

Medo-Persia. "It raised itself on one side," for did not Media arise first, and when Cyrus came to the

throne, Persia was predominant. The three ribs in his mouth denote the three kingdoms of Lydia,

Babylon, and Egypt, which made a league against the Medo-Persians and were completely crushed. 

The third beast was like a leopard which has four wings and four heads. It represents Greece. Wings mentioned on this beast represents its swiftness in flight. The four heads refer to the fact that at the sudden death of Alexander, his four generals— 

Ptolemy, Cassander, Lysimachus and Seleucusdivided the Empire among them. Ptolemy accepted rule over Egypt, Cyrene, Coele-Syria and part of the northern portions of Asia Minor. Cassander reigned over Macedon and Greece.
