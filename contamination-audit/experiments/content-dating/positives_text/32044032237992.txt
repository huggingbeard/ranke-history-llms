STATE OF NEW YORK EXECUTIVE CHAMBER ALBANY, March 18, 1959 To the Legislature: 

The people of our State must have decent housing at costs they can reasonably afford. The problem is most acute for our "middle-income" urban residents, namely those who cannot meet the price of privately-financed new housing and who are not in the lowest income group for which there has been provided subsidized public housing.

Despite the best efforts of capable developers and builders, private enterprise has not kept up with our housing needs. It is estimated that if we are to reach our goal of decent housing for every family in the State, 125,000 dwelling units must be built each year from now to 1975. This is 35,000 more dwelling units per year than our annual average of 90,000 housing starts over the past ten years. This gap affects preponderantly the income groups which cannot afford privately-financed new housing. 

The high cost of site acquisition, construction and mortgage financing makes it difficult or impossible for private enterprise, acting alone, to fill the gap in this income range. It is incumbent upon us, therefore, to find new ways to help private enterprise to meet these vital housing needs. This was the principal problem assigned to the Task Force I designated earlier this year. 

It is the view of the Task Force that an upsurge of more dwellings within the financial reach of our middle-income urban residents can be attained only through an action program of collaboration of local and State government with private enterprise and other interested groups. I commend for your prompt consideration at this session certain proposed legislation designed to make this collaboration possible.

The program of collaboration would involve: 

1. More effective use of authorized State bond funds; 

2. Continuation of present temporary tax abatement of local real estate taxes; 

3. Use of urban renewal property to lower land acquisition costs; 

4. Organization of local sponsoring committees to develop a local middle-income housing program; and 

5. Opportunity for transferring the new housing to full private ownership after a period of 15 years.

The program which I am outlining will be of benefit not only to developers of rental housing, but also to sponsors of projects to be jointly owned by the occupants-commonly called cooperatives. Among the sponsors of successful cooperative projects have been unions, fraternal organizations and other non-profit organizations! These groups are to be commended for the impetus they [3]

have given to middle-income housing construction and should be given every possible additional encouragement. 

More Effective Use of Authorized State Bond Funds 

In the most recent election, the voters of our State approved the issuance of State housing bonds in the amount of $100,000,000. 

These bond funds were approved for the purpose of providing loans to limited-profit housing companies for the construction of new rental and cooperative housing for our middle-income groups who cannot afford to pay for privately-financed new housing. If these bond funds are administered in the way similar bond funds have been handled in the past, they can be expected to provide, at best, fewer than 7,000 new dwelling units. 

To make more effective use of the $100,000,000 State bond funds, the Task Force recommends that private groups be encouraged to participate with the State in making middle-income housing loans. Insurance companies, savings banks and savings and loan associations will be asked to make available at least $200,000,000 of their funds for this purpose. With the combined use of private and State loan funds, it is my hope that a goal of 21,000 dwelling units can be reached. This would accomplish for our middle-income residents the construction of three times the amount of new housing that could be obtained by use of the 

State's bond funds alone.

In order to encourage and enable private capital to participate in the State loan program, the Task Force recommends: (a) The establishment of a limited-profit housing mortgage corporation, the members of which will be savings banks, savings and loan associations, and insurance companies. 

It is hoped that these members will make approximately $200,000,000 available to the corporation. The corporation will then be enabled to participate jointly with the State and other institutions in extending mortgage loans to limited-profit housing companies under attractive mortgaging terms. (b) Permitting a fiduciary holding a fund over $1,000,000 for investment to participate jointly with the State, the limited-profit housing mortgage corporation, banks, insurance companies, labor unions, employers' associations, veterans' organizations and foundations in making mortgage loans to limited-profit housing companies. (c) Servicing of these mortgage loans by the State in order to reduce administrative costs.

Continuation of Temporary Tax Abatement of Local Real Estate 

Taxes

The present availability of temporary tax abatement of a portion of local real property taxes is a major attraction of the limited-profit housing program. The Task Force recommends

continuation and more effective use of the present provisions of law which permit a municipality to agree to exempt up to 50 per cent of the value of the property. Our municipalities are urged to grant the temporary tax abatement required to give impetus. to the middle-income housing program. It should be noted that, even with these tax concessions, the municipalities will receive higher taxes than at present, because the property will be improved with new and valuable housing.

Use of Urban Renewal Property to Lower Land Acquisition Costs 

Savings in land acquisition costs can greatly assist in lowering the price of new middle-income housing. Our municipalities can significantly help if they make land available on favorable terms by combining a middle-income housing program with a local urban renewal program.

Local Sponsoring Committees 

For this middle-income housing program to be effective, community leaders must assist in working out a program for each municipality involved. These leaders, by forming sponsoring committees, can bring about the organization of one or more limitedprofit housing companies and promote participation by local and State government, private enterprise and other interested groups. 

Opportunity for Transferring the New Housing to Full Private Ownership

There is little encouragement of private enterprise in building and operating middle-income housing under the Limited-Profit Housing Companies Law because of its limited return of 6 per cent on invested capital before income taxes. Other investment opportunities will produce not only an investment return substantially greater than 6 per cent but also a capital gains advantage.

A long step toward inducing private equity investment in middle-income housing would be made if it were made possible for the developer to take advantage of capital gains taxation by selling his interest after he has built up an equity in the project. 

Under existing law, this is not possible because a limited-profit housing company may not be transferred to full private ownership until 35 years after initial occupancy, payment of the State loan and payment to the municipality of all accrued taxes previously exempted. 

Under the Task Force proposal the developer would wait 15 years, rather than 35 years. At that time, he could, depending on economic conditions, refinance the mortgage, pay off the State loan and the coordinate institutional loan, and sell the housing project at a profit on which only a capital gains tax would be imposed. 

For the municipality this means a much earlier return to full real estate taxation, and for the State earlier return of its loan funds.

For private enterprise this means return to full ownership on a private basis. 

Conclusion 

Neither private enterprise nor the State acting alone can solve the housing needs of our middle-income families. We desperately need a far greater supply than our authorized loan funds can provide. Private financing must assume a far greater participation with the State in this housing field on a basis that will minimize its risks, compete equitably with other investment opportunities, and provide an increased amount of rental and cooperative housing that our middle-income residents can afford. 

If we can triple the additional middle-income housing which the $100,000,000 bond authorization would alone provide, by obtaining 21,000 dwelling units rather than 7,000, we will have made. a significant inroad on our housing deficit and a great stride forward. I urge your early enactment of the enabling legislation required for the development of this sound, cooperative program between local and State government and private enterprise and other groups interested in increasing our supply of middle-income housing. 

(Signed) NELSON A. ROCKEFELLER
