"FIFTY STATES-OR FIFTY FEDERAL DISTRICTS?" 

Ridiculous? It is happening.

Almost daily out of Washington comes some new edict, some new ruling whittling away at the powers and functions of our supposedly sovereign states. 

Schools. . . elections transportation aries. reapportionment. . . urban affairs what next? our very boundhospitals- Can anything be done about it? We in Louisiana think so. At least we're not giving up without a struggle. 

That is why the Louisiana Legislature saw fit to create the State Sovereignty Commission, to carry on this fight, this struggle to preserve the rights of the people of our state as guaranteed in simple language in the Constitution under which the states became a nation.

We believe we have carried out this legislative mandate with some considerable success. There follows a brief report on some of our efforts.

The Louisiana State Sovereignty Commission was created by the Louisiana Legislature in the Regular Session of 1960. It is composed of the Governor, the Lieutenant Governor, the 

Attorney General, the President Pro Tempore of the Senate, and the Speaker of the House of Representatives, who are exofficio members, and eight citizens appointed by the Governor, one from each of the eight Congressional Districts in the state. 

The Commission is charged with the responsi bility of exercising every legal means to preserve and protect for the State and its people those rights which are legally and traditionally theirs, and which are necessary for their well-being and safety and for the orderly conduct of governmental affairs. 

We consider the functions of this Commission to be such that its effectiveness cannot be developed to any appreciable extent without the very best in the field of public information. 

In recent months, particularly, we have attempted to broaden our operations in that area.

LONG RANGE 

We feel the value of such a program is long-range in nature, toward a clarification of some of the conflicts between state and federal authority. It is believed that our program regarding state operations and state policy on such a long-range basis will be invaluable to these United States in their efforts toward a return to more local self-government. 

This phase of our efforts has included the production and distribution of two films and a cartoon booklet, as well as many speeches, conferences and news releases for press, television and radio.

The films produced were: 

1. “A Way of Life," which showed, among other things, the great steps that have been taken to provide good educational facilities for all Louisiana children, regardless of race, as well as equality of opportunity. This film has been shown on television and to numerous civic clubs and other groups throughout the State. A limited number of showings has been obtained in other states. 100.

2. "We, the People," which depicts the great strides made by all Americans under old-fashioned constitutional government and some of the efforts being made to destroy that way of life. Wide distribution efforts on this film are underway. 

The cartoon booklet, with the same title of "We, the People," is a simplified version of the film and has been distributed to all high school juniors and seniors in the State of Louisiana, to all elected officials in the State, and to all daily newspapers throughout the United States. A considerable demand for copies of this booklet has been received from various areas of the United States due to newspaper articles which appeared in those areas. These resulting newspaper articles prompted requests for our booklet from college professors, college students, teachers who desired to use the booklet in their classrooms, and from people in all walks of life, in all areas of the U. S. 

In addition, there has been wide publicity given to the Commission's stand on such subjects as tidelands, an oppressive agricultural bill, and other matters; and as instructed specifically by the Legislature, the mailing to officials of other states copies of resolutions and acts of the Louisiana Legislature having to do with Louisiana's efforts to run its own schools. 

Much time and effort have been devoted to conferring and advising with various elected officials as well as groups and com mittees of citizens interested in the various problems connected with the formation and operation of satisfactory cooperative schools, and the most suitable solution to the problems connected therewith. The Commission produced a booklet in question and answer form dealing with the most pertinent problems connected with co-ops.

OTHER STATES 

Committees from this Commission have conferred with similar groups in the States of Virginia and Mississippi with a view to cooperative efforts in matters in which we have a mutual interest.

All these, and more, efforts were undertaken with the realization that Louisiana, alone, could not change

a situation confronting the entire country. For this reason, much of our material has been given national circulation including all daily papers, members of Congress, and legislators and other officials of all other states.

In the general discharge of its duties the Commission has found it effective to operate under three divisions: A. 

INFORMATION DIVISION 

The purpose of this Division is to keep the people of the State of Louisiana informed as to the activities of organizations or individuals within the State who are propagandizing or working toward decreasing state powers. 

The Information Division has investigated and is keeping records on many such organizations and individuals and, from time to time has submitted confidential reports to the members of the Louisiana Legislature, or fully reported the facts to the proper officials.

Many of our studies have been at the suggestion of members of the Legislature and other state officials interested in our work. Many of these proved to be of considerable value, and we welcome further such suggestions.

The Division plans to continue all activities listed above, with the feeling that its experience should enable it to perform an even better service to the Legislature and the general public. B.

REGISTRATION DIVISION 

The Commission believes that one of the best ways of preserving our state sovereignty is to have an informed, qualified electorate choose our public officials and pass judgment on such matters as constitutional amendments. For this reason, we have been interested in a system of voter registration which is fair to all, simple and practical, reasonable and capable of uniform enforcement, and one which will provide an electorate having certain minimum qualifications for decisions affecting the public interest.

We have worked toward this end in cooperation with the 

State Board of Registration, the Director of Registrars, Regis trars of Voters, other public officials and interested citizens throughout the State. The recommended procedures include a test of citizenship requiring the applicant for registration to answer three out of six simple, objective, multiple-choice questions on our Constitution and our Government, and are premised upon the provision in our Constitution that a registered voter shall be a person "attached to the principles of the Constitution of the United States and of the State of Louisiana . . .,” “and that he must understand the duties and obligations of citizenship under a republican form of government.' 

Generally speaking, the questions are of such a degree of simplicity that correctly answering them will display only an elemental knowledge of our Constitutional form of government and an attachment thereto. 

No discretion is left to the Registrar as to the correctness of the answers. The procedure is a simple one, fair to all, lending itself to uniform application, and one which we should be able to support legally. 

Changes, statutory or administrative, will be recommended as may be needed from time to time in order to carry on this program in a manner that will best serve the interests of all the people of Louisiana.

C. EDUCATION DIVISION 

Working with an advisory group composed of constitutional lawyers, political scientists, educators and elected officials, this Division has undertaken to develop a program intended to inform our people as to those features of our form of government which distinguish it from all other forms and have made our country the leader of the free world; a program which will create among our people an awareness that the United States of America can continue to exist as a free republic only if such features of government are preserved. We have specific reference to the preservation of states' powers, particularly where such preservation is consistent with the needs of the state, the best interests of the nation, and a well-developed sense of public responsibility.

PROGRAM 

This program contemplates, among other things, the following: 

1. The production of educational films, tapes and similar material. 

2. Development of a series of informative and interesting papers dealing understandably with constitutional principles for use in lectures and possible publication. 

3. Participation in training seminars on constitutional principles. 

4. Encouragement of state-wide competition in writing papers on our form of government with appropriate recognition at the state level. 

5. Establishment of contact with other states toward interesting them in state support for a similar program. 

6. Development of an appropriate textbook, possibly in cooperation with other states, on the subject of Constitutional Government, for use in grade schools and institutions of higher education. 

WE ARE CONCERNED about the impact of U. S. Court decisions upon long-established and recognized constitutional freedoms, particularly the freedoms of choice and association. 

We recognize an individual's right to equality of opportunity and equality before the law. We do not recognize that any individual or race has a legal right, directly or indirectly, to social equality; such equality can only be earned.

The Commission's interests go beyond matters that may arise out of region or race. Our goal is to do all within our power to promote a restoration of Constitutional Government. The concept of Constitutional Government we seek to preserve most assuredly demands a strong central government, vested with all necessary and proper powers to act in fields of truly national concern. But this strong central government presumes an equality of power among its three branches, and if any one branch either assumes too much power, or fails to responsibly assert its proper authority, the entire system is placed in jeopardy. That is our present problem, and the one to which we direct our attention because of the effect upon the statesparticularly where many of the powers assumed should more properly vest in the states. 

Our aim is simply that a balance be restored; that each department of our federal government assert its proper authority; and that the states be encouraged to insist upon exercising the powers reserved to them. Toward the fulfillment of these purposes we respectfully direct ourselves and solicit the support of like-minded Americans everywhere.
