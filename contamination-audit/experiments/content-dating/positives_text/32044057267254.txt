RACE, RACISM AND WORLD WAR 

(Racism-the theory of superior and inferior races) "We must know the facts of race... for racism is an issue to which every one today is exposed; for or against we must take sides. And the history of the future will differ according to the decision which we make."

Benedict (1940) 

We cannot even win this war without convincing our colored allies that we are not fighting for ourselves as continuing superior over colored peoples." 

Pearl Buck

This war is not a racial war, but racial theories and racial prejudices played their part in bringing it about, and it may yet merge i into a racial war. 

If victory is to be followed by peace, the false ideas, the old superstitions, that lie back of racial antagonisms must be replaced by sound conceptions based on modern scientific knowledge. 

Ifew Knowledge vs. 

Old Assumptions

The development of a wholly new attitude toward racial problems is not impossible, for research has made a new body of knowledge available to us. Huch the same sort of thing may happen as is said to have happened in regard to belief in witchcraft. Educated as well as 560634

ignorant people at one time acted on belief in witchcraft, but as science developed and the facts which it revealed were absorbed into general thinking, new ideas crowded out the old, and belief in witchcraft went into the discard. 

It may be said that man's Inowledge does not always determine his behavior, but certainly we cannot act in harmony with facts unless we know the facts. It is doubtful whether anyone would question the difference that it would have made had the knowledge of biologists today as to the elements that mean survival been anounced a hundred years ago, and the assumption that "survival of the fittest" meant survival of the nost powerful never been accepted. To attempt to know facts is something that we all can do and it would seem to be the least we can do. 

The important thing is for those of us who form the general public to know and to spread knowledge of what scientists have to say on this problem of race. It involves relationships not only between white men and colored men but between groups of white peoples. What follows is an attempt to summarize briefly conclusions of accepted authorities. Figures that occur in the text refer to works listed in the bibliography, in which substantiation of statements will be found and from which readers can correct the limitations of a layman's brief outline. 

Race a New Excuse For Conflict 

From the earliest times closed groups have believed themselves unique, have used the word "men" to describe themselves only, have believed that if they perished all would be lost, and

- 3 have claimed the right to destroy those who differed from them. The reason for attacks on those who differed has sometimes been economic, the difference, a difference in possessions or a way of life; at other times the difference leading to attack has been a difference in religion. It is only in fairly recent times that difference of race has been a cause of or an excuse for war.

During the middle ages the belief was that all men were descended from Adam. With the ex-. ploration of the earth the idea arose of differ eat races originating in what amounted to separ ate acts of creation. For many years there was bitter debate as to the single or multiple source of mankind. With Darwin's theories the dispute was ended in favor of man as a single species.

White Domination - 

White "Superiority"

When white men spread their domination over undeveloped continents and sought the subjection or extermination of native peoples, they needed to claim a superiority which would justify their acts. They claimed it at first on the ground of religion. Natives were enslaved as "on-believers" and were freed when converted. Since originally it was accepted as the duty of a conquering nation to Christianize the conquered, and missionaries were active, the number of slaves was steadily reduced. It therefore became necessary to base superiority on some other difference. Difference in color was the most obvious, and the theory of the superiority of the white race rapidly developed.

- 4 

Theory of "Survival of The Fittest"

Through limited understanding of Darwin's theories, there grew up a strong conviction that the elimination of the unfit was a service to progress, and that power to eliminate others was proof of fitness to survive. The white man's power to dominate was accepted as proof of his right to dominate. His superiority was said, as by LeBon, to be demonstrated by the fact that "a handful of Britishers could hold under the yoke 250 million Hindus."

In the history of the Jewish people there was the same shift from religious to racial persecution. During the Crusades, Jews and 

Turks were grouped in condemnation because of their relation to Christian beliefs and holy places. The current idea of a Jewish race is "based on a groundless myth" The Jews spring from "innumerable racial elements." (9) 

Persecution and exploitation, have been the constant factors. 

The excuses for them have differed from age to age. When segregation has accompanied them, it has developed, in any group against which it has been enforced, protective traits which have come to be considered unalterable racial inheritances. 

First Superior Classes, Then Superior Races 

In Europe theories of racial superiority were first formulated as between the ruling nobility and the peasant classes. The various ruling houses of Europe were supposed to be entitled to rule because they belonged to a

5. superior strain of humanity. The idea of race was not tied in with nationalism until national conflicts became bitter, then the idea that every group which differed was an inherently different race was taken over by nationalists. 

Following the Franco-Prussian War leaders of French thought worked out a theory which gave wholly different ancestries to the people of France and Germany. Against the earlier claim of the blond "longheads" of northern

Europe, from whom it had previously been proclaimed the ruling class was descended, they asserted the superiority of the darker "broadheads" of the south, and traced French descent from the Gallic and Celtic peoples. This alloved for racial ties with England then growing in popularity in France.

Early Race Theories 

In Germany

The claims of the French were met in Ger many by reassertion of the superiority of the early Germanic, "Teutonic, tribes and the boast that these were the ancestors only of 

Germans. Because various groups in Germany differed greatly in physical characteristics. 

German writers distinguished the ancestral group, not so much by physical traits, as by certain qualities including acceptance of leadership and loyalty to chosen leaders. these qualities were found in other nations, this was held to prove that wherever greatness was found, it was due to a mixture of German blood. Even in these early days it was stated, "Whoever reveals himself German by his acts, is a German," from which follows today's theory of the "German soul in a non-German body."

Racial Theories Shift With Politics By the time of the first World War racism was a nationalistic faith (Germany explained its defeat in that war as due to failure to maintain racial purity.) National racial doctrines have consistently followed shifts in politics, and this or that racial strain has been emphasized according to political expediency.

Before the first World War Englishmen cited ancestral ties with Germany. When France was allied with Italy, the southern European ancestry of both nations was dwelt upon. When 

Italy became the ally of Germany the "Germanness" of northern Italy was pointed out. To day Germans assert that Nordics were among the ancestors of the Japanese and that Japan shares biological guarantees of leadership. (1,2,3,4,9)

Such varying claims as to inheritance have been possible for the very reason that, due to the constant movement of peoples from the times before recorded history, all modern nations are today of mixed descent. The whole matter has been summed up in this sentence, "Political theories about race are nothing more than instruments of propaganda devised for the child minds of totalitarian populations."

What Scientists Say About Race 

What then are the facts of race from the scientific point of view? All students of race do not agree as to the relative influence of heredity and environment, but soundly based and widely accepted opinion today runs along these lines:

All Groups 

Are Hybrid "All living men spring from a common ances*** try, are essentially similar in form and function, breed freely among themselves.... so we can only belong to one species.” Because of their common and universally mixed inheritances, "The natural difference between races," so far as important characteristics are concerned, "pales into insignificance when compared with the natural differences between individuals." (5) (11) 

To quote Emerson's statement, as scientists do, "Each variety shades down inperceptibly into the next and you cannot draw a line where race begins or ends.... All our experience is of the gradation and resolution of races and strange resemblances meet us everywhere." Or as Whitman puts it in a poet's parase, "A vast similitude interlocks all."

Every civilized group of which we have record, has been a hybrid group. The more advanced a people, the greater its vitality, so much the more intermixed with others it is found to be. 

If any race is deemed pure from all mixture, it is only because we cannot disentangle its constituent elements." (10)

Inherited Characteristics 

Not Permanent 

There is not only no pure race, but "race as something permanent is an artificial notion." 

There are two reasons why racial, in the sense of inherited, character is not permanent.

In the first place, inheritances flow through family lines and the range of potential combinations results in constant change. 

In the second place, even established traits are modified by changes in environment or circumstances or education. It has more than once been demonstrated that when circumstances divide a well established group, one section may continue to carry on the old way of life, while another may come in contact with a different culture, take it over and contribute to it. In any people there are potential capacities which conditions may suppress or develop. Furthermore, "generally speaking, a trait is just as quickly modified in the case of the American Indian or the legro and other races as in the case of the white."(3) 

Students of life in Africa produce much evidence of the rapidity with which the native people learn to man the intricate tools of modern civilization. (9) 

When circumstances call forth a special ability or trait, there is within a group a uniform development which gives the appearance of a "racial" characteristic. Within historic times the effect of conditions of life upon group character has been clearly show. For one example only, the Scandinavians, once the aggressive Vikings and explorers, are now the stay-at-home creators of cooperatives. (1)

Obviously among men no inherited racial pattern debars the possibility of change. No other proof of this is needed than that changes in racial composition are too slow to account for rapid changes in social behavior. (1,2,3)

Han's Progress Due

To Learned Behavior 

As a matter of fact man's claim to superiority over all other creatures is in large part based on his capacity for "learned" behavior. 

This claim is incompatible with the theory of fixed, unchangeable racial characteristics. "Anthropologists, geneticists, biologists alike hold to the belief that races are unstable and not permanent, therefore no race is inevitably lord or servant of another." (1) (3)

Hobility of Races Contradicts 

Racial Inequality Differences anong groups of people at any given period must be thought of not in terms of race, but in terms of what one scientist calls "areas of characterization." there groups have been temporarily isolated, inbreeding and the effects of special environment have resulted in superficial differences, which have given rise to theories of unalterable racial differ ence. (1) (3) 

Cultures Not 

Racial or Unmixed The fact that there is no pure or unmixed race means also that there can be no such thing as a pure racial culture. Research has revealed the appearance of similar primitive cultures in widely separated areas, showing common human reactions to certain conditions and situations. 

To more advanced cultures, it has likewise been established by research, many so-called different races have contributed. The source of our own civilization was once traced back to Greece,

but its debt also to peoples of Egypt and Asia is now recognized. The civilization of what are today advanced groups may owe much to contributions from those who are today backward. The method of working iron ---- one of the greatest assets to the white man's way of life is said by anthropologists to have originated with the Negro people of Africa. Japan owes to Korea, which she now claims she must rule because of Korea's backwardness, much of value. European culture developed in the region of the Mediterranean, but the claim of the Nordics to superiority repudiates the creators of the culture of which they boast. (4) Go

It is through the various contributions of many groups whose life experience has developed different abilities and qualities that civilization is enriched that the full potentiality of humanity is realized. A sense of superiority by excluding all difference can only impoverish. It is this fact which we have neglected, to our own loss in culture and in friendship, in our cultural relations with Latin America. It is this fact which we ne glect when we think merely in terms of treating other peoples honestly and kindly. Unity in plies more. It implies mutual need. It is assurance of a recognized right to a voice in the adjustment of world relations that will follow this war, that India, that China, that other peoples who have heretofore been "told", desire.

President Roosevelt took account of this when he said recently that hereafter China must not only rule its own country freely, but must play its part in world affairs.

No Proof of Present Racial Inferiority 

But it may be said that the fact that no race is pure or its characteristics permanent does not exclude the possibility of superior and inferior racial quality at any given moment. 

At this point we must turn to racial psychologists. In brief, the conclusion of their studies is that no racial inferiority has been proved that therefore no individual can be condemned to inferiority because of racial inheritance. Το determine relative value of races requires careful test and measurement of capacity. Such tests and measurements have only recently been developed and applied. Early opinions were based on assumptions, not on findings of fact, which accounts for the wide divergence of earlier opinions from those of today.

In the first place, attempts have sometimes been made to arrange races in an ascending series from primitive man or his animal ancestry, but it has been found that the arrangement must be a different one according to the trait selected for comparison flat nose, thin lips, growth of hair on the body, shape of limbs. 

Black peoples have flat noses, but the full lips that are "most human." Europeans have nose forms far removed from primitive, but growths of hair on their bodies, while the bodies of the people of the Orient are free from such hairy growth. for brain size the variation within groups and overlapping among them is marked. No relation between physical characteristics regarded as racial and mentality has been proved by any test.(2) 

Even the claim that primitive men, like animals, have naturally keener senses than white

men is discredited by tests. 

Difference in acuteness is amply explained by the fact that the conditions of their lives direct their attention in ways that ours is not directed.

The results of many mental tests have been studied from the point of view of race with the generally accepted conclusion that up to this time none indicates "significant racial differences that might not be adequately explained by the effect of social experience... 

For large racial groups acceptable proof of marked mental differences due to organic, not social causes, has never been given." (1) (2)

Tests Emphasize Importance of Environment 

Tests given white and colored men who entered our army in the first World War were originally interpreted as indicating a definite mental inferiority of the colored race, but later this interpretation was discredited. 

Comparison of the results of the tests of different colored groups showed that as colored men approached, in the regions where they lived, equal opportunities with white men, they approached equal rank in the tests with white men.

Psychologists have increasingly emphasized the psychological effect of belonging to a subjected race. This in itself tends to prevent full and free development aside from the limited educational and economic opportu nities which it carries with it. As John Dewey has said, "any people held in subjection and at a great disadvantage exonomically and politically is bound to show the consequences.

It is kept back while the other people go ahead. 

Then the dominant group finds plenty of facts to quote in support of their belief in their own superiority."

In all mental tests there have been outstanding individuals, ranking with the best, in groups whose average rating was low. This is further proof that the low average cannot be charged to racial inheritance. "The results of racial tests are believed. today to show that hereditary aptitude is not distributed by races and that when environmen→→ tal conditions for different groups become similar, average achievement also becomes similar. 

Individual differences will still remain in all races, but the results indicate that any program which proposes to increase human achieve ment will attain its ends by providing adequate and continued opportunity for any individual without regard to race or color." (1) Science backs up democracy by insisting that ve think in terms of individuals.

Racial Antipathy 

Not Instinctive

Whatever the facts in regard to race, the existence of racial prejudice cannot be denied. 

It has long been held that between peoples of different colored skins, there is an instinctive antipathy. But all the evidence, produced by careful study, is to the effect that there is no instinctive antagonism: Small children as reported by Bruno Lasker in "Race Attitudes in Children," show no racial antipathy until they respond to social pressures and acquire the attitude of their elders. Even among adults

Chads

A11 there is no aversion to personal cont acts so long as they arise within the relationship of master and servant. The history of slavery and the vide-spread mixture of races also proves there is no instinctive physical aversion. white peoples, furthermore, have not insisted equally on segregation. The attitude of Spanish, Portuguese and French settlers towards native populations differed among themselves and from that of Anglo-Saxon settlers. "There is no necessity to postulate the existence of a specific and universal instinct of racial antipathy, while on the other hand there is strong positive evidence that such instinct does not exist. An adequate explanation of racial antagonisms can be found in impulses and motives that are independent of race."(1,2,7)

Economic Reasons 

For Racial Antagonisms The "impulses and motives" that have resulted in racial antagonisms and been excused on racial grounds have been predominantly economic. In times of depression race prejudices are intensified. Anti-Semitism and other an tagonisms rise when a villain of the picce is required or, as was illustrated in Germany after the first World War, a scapegoat is needed. 

The cry of racial inferiority has been raised when it was desired to take over the resources of a native people or to restrict a labor market or to prevent invasion of a field of economic opportunity. Illustra tions can be found in our own country in the resistance to the northern movement of Negroes

and the refusal of many labor unions to admit them to menbership; in the exclusion of Orientals by immigration laws; in discrimination against later immigrants as compared with the earlier comers, in the fears reflected in the false and disproved charges of wide-spread Jewish control of the press, radio, notion picture industry and banks. 

Immigrations and

Racial Prejudice Our recent restrictive immigration laws which first based quotas on the 1910 figures, and later on the earlier 1890 figures, were explained on the racial ground of the superiority of the earlier im migrants from northern Europe, but illustrated the tendency of established groups to shut out new. In this matter of old and new immigration it needs to be borne in mind that early comers have many advantages and opportunities which later ones lack, and that these, therefore, being less able to make their way successfully, appear "inferior."

The Oriental Exclusion Act in itself made little difference economically, for if Oriental peoples had been given equal treatment with other nations and included under the quota system, very few would have been admitted. The prejudices underlying the Exclusion Act went back, however, to the economic competition of early Oriental immigrants. It was accepted as an expression of racial antagonism against Japan because it affected that country particularly. At the time it was passed, H. G. Wells wrote: #It implies that Japanese and Americans are forever incompatible, are forever two peoples; that forever on this planet their destinies are to be worked out arallel or apart — or to mingle only in bitter conlict for exclusive survival. These are immensely langerous implications." The New York Times desiglated the day the act was passed as "Black Friday."

The "Superior" 

Are Warned

Our conviction of superiority was one reason for our giving no heed to such warnings as these. Today we are being warned by those who know well the thoughts of the yellow and the black peoples, of the catastrophes that lie ahead for all humanity if racial prejudices are not discarded.

The American people - even today when the inter-relation of all nations is clear to them “ fail to see the extent to which our own racial problems merge into those of the world and into those of this war. Leaders of India are today expressing alarm over the arrival of American troops in their country and talk of the possibility of American domination replacing British. 

In an article in the May Atlantic Mme. 

Chiang Kai-Shek feels it necessary to warn

Great Britain and the United States, where there is still much talk of an Anglo-Saxon dominated peace, that China will never again tolerate outside controls in her country. And this warning comes although there have been official statements that after this war we will no longer insist on the extra-territorial rights upon which we long insisted though it weakened China's position as an independent nation in Asia. 

One reason that our protestations of desire for freedom for all, are questioned in Asia is because its people are aware of the discrimination in this country against our own citizens who do not happen to be white men. 

It is safe to say that colored peoples of other

countries know the story of race relations in this country better than many of us know it ourselves. Few Americans will read without surprise the article in the April Harpers, "American Negroes in the War." A few of the facts it cites are these: 

Racial Discrimination and the War Effort 

As late as the period from January to 

March, 1941, of 8,769 skilled and semi-skilled jobs in aircraft only 13 went to non-white workers, and in the metal trades occupations only 500 out of 60,000. Not only have employ ers refused to hire colored workers, but many unions have excluded them from membership.

Referring to this situation the Social Security Board states, "in spite of the increasing difficulty in finding experienced skilled and semiskilled workers, many employers continue to rėm fuse to hire available Negroes for production work."

So serious did the situation become that last summer the President issued an executive order prohibiting all discrimination in em→→ ployment in defense industries and in government departments. A committee was established to see that this order was carried out, and to report discrimination to the President, who would have power to revoke a defense contract or dismiss an offending official. The committee has held hearings and has unearthed many cases of discrimination, more, it is said, in Federal departments than in defense plants but according to the Harper's article it has not certified any complaints to the President.

It is not reported in the Harper's article, but it is freely said in Washington that in spite of all the President's efforts and in spite of the Civil Service ruling that race shall not be indicated on an applicant's card, colored stenographers in the stenographic pools in the various departments sit idle because officials will not use them. The situation in the cafeterias which have been opened to all government employees is recognized as serious. 

To summarize again from the Harper's article: The Negroes' loyalty has never been questioned. He has fought valiantly in every conflict in which this country has been engaged since the Revolutionary War. A Negro regiment was the first of all the allied armies to reach the Rhine and never lost a foot of ground nor had a man taken prisoner. In spite of this proof of their ability as soldiers, all but 

42,000 of the 200,000 Negro troops sent to Europe were used in labor battalions. At the end of the war the four regular army Negro regiments were demobilized as fighting units and many of their members were assigned to serve as servants to white officers.

Race Discrimination Aids Axis 

With these facts in mind it is possible to understand why Negroes are saying about this war, If we win, I lose. So what?" Yet as a 

Negro leader points out in April "Common Sense," "politically, socially, educationally, culturally and spiritually, what we believe in and follow is the direct opposite of what the Nazis, the Fascists and the Japanese militarists believe in."

The important point is the one that Mrs. 

Roosevelt made when she said: "The nation cannot expect colored people to feel that the 

United States is worth defending if the Negro continues to be treated as he is now." And certainly it is true, as the Harper's article concludes, that "morally speaking, nothing that is being done in the United States today gives the Axis Powers a better opportunity to condemn democracy than the treatment of our colored citizens."

Race Prejudice War and Peace. 

The implications, both for the war and for the peace after this war, of the American white citizen's attitude to American colored citizens, are boldly emphasized by Pearl Buck in recent letters to the press and in other publications. 

Among the things she says are these; (6) "Japan is busily declaring in the Philippines, in China, in India, Malaya and even Russia that there is no basis for hope that colored peoples can expect any justice from the people who rule in the United States, namely, the white people. For specific proof the Japanese point to our treatment of our own colored people, citizens of generations in the United States. The discriminations of the American army and navy and the air forces against colored soldiers and sailors, the exclusion of colored labor in our defense industries and trade unions, all our social discriminations, are of the greatest aid today to our enemy in Asia, Japan. 'Look at America, Japan is saying to millions of listening ears. 'Will white 

Americans give you equality?'....

"The persistent refusal of Americans to see the connection between the colored American and the colored peoples abroad, the continued, and it seems even willful, ignorance which will not investigate the connection, are agony to those loyal and anxious Americans who know all too well the dangerous possibilities………. #Democracy, if it is to prevail at this solemn moment in human history, can do so only if it purges itself of that which denies democracy....#

In this connection it is essential to bear in mind the effect on the peoples of Asia of any racial unfairness toward Japanese aliens in this country. We have interned American citizens of Japanese, but not of German or Italian origin. We are treating them as a race, not as individuals, and in such treatment lies the essence of racial prejudice. 

Here again the greatest hardships suffered have been due to individual prejudice and exploitation, rather than to the administration of the government's policy. 

The Individual's Responsibility 

It is within the individual mind that race prejudice must be broken down. Our religion does not tolerate it. Our science gives it no sanction. Our Government seeks to correct it. 

Human unity is not an aspiration to be achieved. It is a fact from which to proceed. "We are at variance with the reality of life until we come into the personal and social realization of the solidarity of humanity."
