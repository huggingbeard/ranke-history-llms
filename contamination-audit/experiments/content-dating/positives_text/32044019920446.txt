FOREWORD 

In my experience as a student and teacher of philosophy I have found that the most difficult hurdle in the mastery of this field is the terminology. For this reason I have selected for definition those terms which my students find most difficult and have arranged them in a convenient sequence. 

Since this guide is designed to be used as a supplement to logic and introduction to philosophy courses and since it is the nature of philosophers to be hyper-critical, I have left a blank page opposite each page of definitions and space between definitions which should be used for examples, references to specific philosophers, and variant definitions. 

I want to emphasize the fact that this guide is designed to be used as an aid to students and not as a glossary for professors. 

Sept. 12, 1938 

John H. Melzer.

PHILOSOPHICAL TERMINOLOGY Philosophy 

Philosophy in general is a systematic interpretation of the universe. It is a systematic interpretation, because it must be based on the laws of reason or, in other words, logic. Of course there are specific philosophies such as a philosophy of education. Such a philosophy would be a systematic interpretation of all the known facts in the field of education. 

Logic 

Logic is the science or "rule book" of all reasoning. All logic or all rules of reasoning are based on what philosophers call "the law of identity." In other words "A" cannot be "A" and "not 

A" at the same time. An animal which has all the qualities of a horse is logically a horse and nothing but a horse.

PHILOSOPHICAL TERMINOLOGY Metaphysics 

Originally metaphysics was nothing more than that portion of a book or series of books which followed after a discussion of physics. Today, however, the term has a slightly different meaning. 

Metaphysics is that part of philosophy which deals with an interpretation of ultimate reality as it is distinguished from the reality we know through our senses. Since metaphysics is not a discussion of things we can see, hear, taste, feel, or smell, it is necessarily a purely logical speculation. It is speculation about the ultimate reality of the world or universe on the basis of pure logic. It is not mere imagination.

Ethics 

Ethics is the science or "rule book" of conduct in which all the values of every day life are systematically interpreted. For a long time ethics or the rules of right conduct was considered by many people to be merely a part of some religious belief. But people are gradually realizing that there is a rule of right conduct in every walk of life-from politics to business. Even the boarding-house lady wants to know what system of ethical conduct her prospective boarders have before she allows them to live in her home. -7-

PHILOSOPHICAL TERMINOLOGY Aesthetics 

Aesthetics is the systematic interpretation of the individual's reaction to the beautiful and the sublime. It is an attempt to show just why we call something beautiful and why we have a feeling of awe when we see certain things or happen to be in certain circumstances. Aesthetics embraces the entire fields of fine arts and poetry. 

Theology 

Theology is an attempt to explain the relation of God to the universe on the basis of logic. It is distinguished from religion in that it must follow reason or the rules of logic while religion is not necessarily a logical interpretation of the divine relationship. Ontology

Ontology is an attempt to determine the nature of ultimate being or the fundamental unchangeable stuff underlying the universe. There is some dis- -9-

PHILOSOPHICAL TERMINOLOGY 

agreement among thinkers on this subject. Some philosophers think that it is numerically one; others think that it is numerically more than one. Some also think that it is one spirit or personality; others think that it is one kind or piece of matter or one force like an electrical charge, and still others think that it is many kinds of matter or forces. 

Cosmology 

Cosmology is a less inclusive term than ontology. Ontology includes both the supernatural and the natural while cosmology deals only with the cosmos or nature.

Psychology 

Philosophical psychology should be distinguished from ordinary psychology. Ordinary psychology deals primarily with a statistical interpretation of mental processes, sensations, and neural reactions, while philosophical psychology deals primarily with the soul as a directing force and as an existing reality. Of course there is also much disagreement among philosophers on this point. -11-

PHILOSOPHICAL TERMINOLOGY Epistemology 

Epistemology is an attempt to find out just why we know things. In other words it answers the question, "How do we get knowledge?" Epistemology is the starting point for all intelligent and systematic treatments of educational psychology. Monism

Any system of philosophy which tries to interpret the universe or any part of the universe on the basis of one principle is a monism. 

Theological Monism 

Theological monism is a theory that the divine or the supernatural is numerically one. -13-

PHILOSOPHICAL TERMINOLOGY Ontological Monism 

Ontological monism is an attempt to show that the fundamental reality or being of all nature and of all outside of nature is numerically one thing or force or spirit.

Cosmological Monism Cosmological monism is an attempt to show that the world, exclusive of the supernatural, is made of one fundamental principle such as water or electricity. All variations in the world are variations of this one principle. 

Psychological Monism

Psychological monism is the conclusion that the soul is one real and abiding principle. -15-

PHILOSOPHICAL TERMINOLOGY Epistemological Monism 

Epistemological monism is the theory that when a person knows something he becomes one with the thing known at the time when the knowledge relation takes place (cf. mysticism).

Dualism 

Any system of philosophy which tries to interpret the universe or any part of the universe on the basis of two principles is a dualism. 

Pluralism 

An interpretation of the universe or a part of the universe on the basis of more than two principles is called a pluralism. -17-

PHILOSOPHICAL TERMINOLOGY Agnosticism 

Agnosticism is a theory that it is impossible to know anything about the fundamental nature of the universe.

Skepticism 

Skepticism differs from agnosticism in only one point, i.e. some skeptics admit the possibility of knowing fundamental reality but they are doubtful of this possibility ever being realized. 

Eclecticism 

Eclecticism is a system of philosophy in which the philosopher takes the good points from a number of systems and blends them all into one system of thought. -19-

PHILOSOPHICAL TERMINOLOGY 

Hedonism

Hedonism is a system of ethical conduct in which pleasure is the sole end of all human actions. 

Pragmatism 

Pragmatism is a system of thought by which certain thinkers arrive at what they call truth by testing a number of hypotheses. Then, when they find an hypothesis which works satisfactorily, they call it a true hypothesis and hence arrive at truth. 

Representational Pragmatism 

Representational pragmatism is the theory that an hypothesis gives us truth when it satisfactorily fulfills all the purposes of the hypothesis-when it represents all the purposes involved. An idea is true when it represents reality. It represents reality when it fulfills all the purposes of an hypothesis. -21-

PHILOSOPHICAL TERMINOLOGY Behaviorism 

Behaviorism is a system of ordinary psychology in which certain psychologists set forth the belief that all human behavior can be explained on the basis of stimulus and response. In other words all human behavior is the result of the neural response of the individual to various stimuli. Human behavior, especially mental behavior, is a purely physical reaction. Consciousness or mind as an entity is a myth.

Mysticism 

Mysticism is the theory that certain individuals can, through a proper amount of concentration, transcend the limits of human mental processes and become one with God. The mystic claims that he has a direct perception of God.

Rationalism (Intellectualism) 

Rationalism is an attempt to explain the workings of the entire universe on the basis of reason or pure logic. The rationalist believes that if a phenomenon cannot be explained rationally, it cannot be real and therefore cannot exist. Therefore that which is rational must be real. -23-

PHILOSOPHICAL TERMINOLOGY Nominalism 

In the Middle Ages there were two important schools of thought, the nominalists and the realists. 

The nominalists believed that universal terms were mere class names for groups of objects. In other words there is no such thing as an ideal perfect horse which actually exists and is the prototype of all the horses we see. Universals are mere names and nothing more.

Realism (Scholastic) 

The scholastic realism of the Middle Ages was pure rationalism or what we might call rationalistic realism. Rationalistic realism, then, was the belief that universal terms were realities which really existed. The idea of a perfect horse really existed. 

Idealism 

Philosophic idealism is very much different from literary idealism. Literary idealism embodies the idea of striving for perfection-it is what may —-25—

PHILOSOPHICAL TERMINOLOGY 

be called philosophic perfectionism. Philosophic idealism, however, is first of all a pure rationalism or intellectualism. It naturally follows that since the component parts of the process of reasoning are ideas and since the world is an orderly and hence a rational world, that the cosmos is made up of nothing more than concrete and abstract ideas. 

Absolute Idealism 

Absolute idealism is the theory that the entire universe is one all-inclusive perfect idea, God, who is gradually realizing himself in the world. All the objects of matter are concrete ideas in the mind of God and all the immaterial things in the world are abstract ideas in the mind of God. They must be in the mind of God in order to be permanent. 

Logical Idealism 

Logical idealism is the old Greek notion that since every thing in the world is imperfect, there must logically be a realm of perfect ideas. Our ideas of the things about us are accordingly imperfect copies of the logically perfect ideas (cf. scholastic realism). -27-

PHILOSOPHICAL TERMINOLOGY Psychological Idealism 

Psychological idealism is perhaps the simplest and least important form of idealism. It is small wonder then that the uninitiated in the field of philosophy think that all idealism is psychological idealism. This form of idealism teaches that all the knowledge we have of anything comes through our senses. Therefore the existence of anything, so far as our knowledge of its existence is concerned, depends directly on its perception through some one's senses. God perceives all things at all times. Therefore things exist when humans are not perceiving them.

Subjectivism 

Subjectivism is a term which is also applied to psychological idealism. It merely indicates that the entire emphasis of a system of thought is on the subject or individual instead of on the external world. -29

Solipsism

Solipsism is the logical conclusion of psychological idealism. If one follows the definition given above, he will see that since each individual has different sensations of the same phenomenon, the only real knowledge a person could have would be his own. sensations. Hence he cannot get out of his own little circle of sensations to those of others. 

Ego-Centric Predicament 

The ego-centric predicament is the same as solipsism.

Personal Idealism (Personalism) 

Personalism is based on the proposition that a personality is the only agent in the universe which has the quality of self-determination. There must be a first mover or a first cause in the universe and that cause must be self-caused. Hence God must be a personality. Since the essential qualities of any -31-

Personalism, in spite of many apparent contradictions, is the most fascinating of modern philosophies. It is readily accepted, because it agrees with the traditional form of the Christian religion. 

It is an American philosophy. 

Materialism 

Materialism is a system of philosophy in which it is thought that the entire universe is ultimately material. Even the psychical realm is considered to be entirely material.

Naturalism 

Naturalism is nothing more than a softer, warmer, and more polite name for cold materialism. -33-

PHILOSOPHICAL TERMINOLOGY 

Mechanism

Pure mechanism is a theory that the cosmos is a sort of perpetual motion machine. Deismis a modified mechanism. The deists believe that God started the universe in motion and from that time on the universe has taken care of itself. 

Realism 

Realism is the direct opposite of idealism. The realists believe that the universe is an eternal reality independent of any subjective influence. Logically realism should coincide with materialism. 

Naive Realism 

Naive realism is the philosophy of the man in the street. It is the unreflective conclusion that what we see is what we see. In other words "a tree is a tree and that's the end of it." -35-

PHILOSOPHICAL TERMINOLOGY Neo-Realism 

Neo-realism should be called absolute realism, because it is an attempt to show that the universe is an absolutely objective system of phenomena. What we usually call subjective phenomena, i.e. ideas, images, or other mental constructs, are either denied or given an objective interpretation. Consciousness as an active agent in the subject is absolutely denied. 

Critical Realism 

The Dictionary of Psychology defines critical realism as a theory that "knowledge arises only through the selective and critical elaboration of the data of consciousness." This definition may be all right for psychology (It sounds like Boring, The Physical Dimensions of the Mind), but it is not satisfactory as a philosophical definition. Critical realism is a system of philosophy in which a group of thinkers decided that the universe should be interpreted on an absolutely realistic basis. But being skeptical about the proof of their stand, they decided that the position needed no absolute proof, because people generally act on the basis of an animal faith in realism anyway. The "critical" in critical realism means "almost." Some would prefer to call critical realism "pragmatic realism." --37-

Critical Monism (Critical Monistic Realism)

Critical monism is an attempt to harmonize the good points of idealism and realism. It is a theory that there are two sets of phenomena in the universe -the subjective and the objective. These come together in a monism by means of a mystical union (intuition).

Phenomenology 

Modern phenomenology is a return to the logical idealism of Plato. It is an attempt to show that the real objects or phenomena of the external world are "intentions" of the person judging. They are absolutely external to the person who makes a judgment. "Hence objects may be perceived, but they are never experienced."

A Fortiori 

A fortiori is the term applied to an argument in which the proof of the conclusion is established by a more inclusive proposition which is known to be true. -39-

PHILOSOPHICAL TERMINOLOGY A Posteriori 

An a posteriori argument is one which proceeds from the particular to the general-induction. It is an argument which is based primarily on experience. 

A Priori 

An a priori argument is one which proceeds from the general to the particular-deduction. In geometry we have numerous instances of this form of argument.

Analogy An analogy is a comparison of two things or events which are dissimilar in kind but similar in function. An argument from analogy is worthless as far as logic is concerned but very valuable for political oratory. -41--

PHILOSOPHICAL TERMINOLOGY Apperception 

Apperception is the process of focusing attention on something. It is the becoming aware of ideas which we already have. Perception

Perception is the becoming aware of some object through our senses. Perception is simple sensation. A percept is a sensation. 

Conception 

Conception is the process of forming an idea of a percept. First we have a sensation or percept and then we have an idea of the sensation, which is a concept. -43-

PHILOSOPHICAL TFRMINOLOGY Primary Quality 

A primary quality of a thing is that quality which does not change. In other words when two or more persons look at the same thing, each person has slighty different sensations of that thing. But the outlines of the thing remain permanent, because these qualities, e. g. solidity, extension, figure, motion, rest, and change are inherent in the thing itself. They do not change with each individual's perception of the thing.

Secondary Quality 

Secondary qualities are the sense qualities. which change with each individual's perception. 

Secondary qualities are largely dependent on the observer. Some philosophers go so far as to say that they are entirely created by the observer (cf. psychological idealism).

Tertiary Quality 

Tertiary qualities are those qualities which are. entirely created by thought about an object. -45-

PHILOSOPHICAL TERMINOLOGY Categorical Imperative 

The categorical imperative is a command which is absolutely binding at all times and at all places. 

It is a universal mandate. 

Category 

A category is an ultimate classification of any thing or idea.

Causality 

Causality is the relation which ensues when one agent acts on another agent.

First Cause 

In reasoning back from effect to cause in a series of events the human mind demands a first cause or some agency which creates the beginning of events. This cause is called the first cause. -47-

PHILOSOPHICAL TERMINOLOGY Efficient Cause 

The efficient cause of an event is the sum total of all direct antecedents to the event.

Formal Cause 

The formal cause of an event is the plan of the event.

Final Cause 

The final cause of an event is the purpose which the event achieves. Cognition

Cognition is, properly speaking, the act of knowing. It is the act of becoming aware of an idea, a sensation, or an external object. -49-

PHILOSOPHICAL TERMINOLOGY Deduction 

Deduction is a process of reasoning in which a particular conclusion is drawn from a general hypothesis. In other words it is reasoning from the general to the particular.

Induction 

Induction is a process of reasoning in which a general conclusion is drawn from a number of particular instances (cf. a posteriori reasoning). Dialectic

PHILOSOPHICAL TERMINOLOGY 

Determinism

Determinism is a theory of the universe in which it is thought that all events and phenomena are the necessary results of antecedent events and phenomena. Indeterminism

Indeterminism is the theory that some events are not necessarily the results of antecedent events. 

Freedom 

Philosophic freedom includes not only the postulates of indeterminism but also the possibility of creation. In other words if a person is free, he ought to be able to make a choice without any antecedent influences. Such a choice, then, would be an act of creation. -53-

PHILOSOPHICAL TERMINOLOGY External Relations 

External relations are those which have an independent existence. An absolute externality of relations (cf. neo-realism) would imply that all possible relations exist at all times and at all places. A special relation of an individual to his environment would have existed there before the individual came into that particular relation. When several persons enter a room where there is a fire in the grate, each person has a different feeling of warmth. This doctrine would assert that each degree of warmth existed in the room prior to the entrance of the persons into the room. Hence the relations of warmth were absolutely external. The doctrine of the externality of relations eliminates consciousness as an entity or the mind as an active creative agency. 

Internal Relations 

Internal relations are those which appear and disappear with change of place or condition or mental attitude. In other words the exponents of the absolute externality of relations would maintain that the relation "north of" is an independent existence, even at the North Pole. The exponents of internal relations would say that the relation "north of" disappears when we reach the North Pole. -57-

PHILOSOPHICAL TERMINOLOGY Innate Ideas 

The doctrine of innate ideas teaches that every normal individual is born with a fund of ideas which are gradually realized through his experience. Intuition

Philosophical intuition is direct perception without any mediation of the senses (cf. mysticism). 

Mental Chemistry

The term "mental chemistry" is an attempt to explain the creativity of the mind on a materialistic basis. Monad

The term "monad" is used by some philosophers to designate an infinitely small, immaterial, and indestructible element of the universe. -59-

PHILOSOPHICAL TERMINOLOGY Objective and Subjective 

Philosophers use the terms "objective" and "subjective" to differentiate between the thing acted upon and the person or agency which does the acting. In general one might say that "subjective" refers to some person and "objective" refers to some thing.

Sensation 

A sensation is any response which occurs when any part of the nervous system is stimulated. 

Dogmatism 

Dogmatism is the name given to any system of thought which makes unfounded, illogical, or unproven statements. It is a system that asserts rather than reasons. -61-
