ERALD TOWNSEND'S grandfather, the fourth of Lord Barham's six sons, had been a throw-back to some free-thinking, free-acting ancestor, for whom England was too small in which to stretch his restless legs. Shortly after the Civil War, he emigrated to Canada and wandered amid hardships that delighted his adventurous spirit, half-way across the continent. 

Eventually, he crossed Lake Superior and settled in the Keweenaw district in Michigan. It was, no doubt, an outcome of his temperament that the fascinating uncertainties of mining drew and held him. From small and accidental beginnings, the Townsend Copper Company arose, developed and spread, like a well-nourished infant.

In 1872 he married a niece of the Governor of Michigan. Gerald Townsend the second, born the following year, was brought up and educated as a one-hundredper cent American. Ann Arbor was his alma mater and 46

Michigan his native soil. He was a son to delight his rugged father, a chip of the old block, physically and temperamentally in accord with the ex-Englishman.
