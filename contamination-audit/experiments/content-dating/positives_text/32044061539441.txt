23 The Investment Code of the Central African Republic (CAR), adopted in 1963, is designed to attract foreign private capital and to encourage private investments for the creation and expansion of industrial, agricultural, and mining enterprises. The Code offers substantial tax incentives, and provides for special import duty exemptions. Investors are guaranteed the right to transfer abroad duly recorded profits and funds derived from the sale or termination of an enterprise. Companies may also be given preference in the allocation of foreign exchange, and priority in the procurement of necessary raw materials and goods. These benefits can be included in contracts of establishment between the Government of the CAR and approved enterprises. 

The Government of the CAR has repeatedly indicated that it will respect the integrity of the private sector of the economy. It is deeply committed to the development of available resources and to the establishment of a modern industrial sector of the economy. 

Studies in the CAR by various research groups indicate that the following industries are economically feasible: dairy, small foundry, charcoal manufacturing plant, ceramics plant, cement plant, brick works, and paint factory. Existing industries (shoes, bicycle assembly, pots and pans, handicrafts, etc.) might be modernized or enlarged.

Law No. 62.355 of February 19, 1963, providing an Investment Code in the Central African Republic 

The National Assembly has deliberated on and adopted; 

The President of the Republic and President of the Goverment hereby promulgates a law reading as follows: 

Article 1. In conformity with the general principles expressly stated in the Preamble to the Constitution of the Central African Republic, industrial, agricultural, or mining activities may freely be established in the territory of the Republic, with the exception of those activities which, for reasons of public interest or public policy, are subject to authorization. 

Article 2. Private investment in the Central African Republic shall enjoy the benefits of ordinary law or preferential treatment. 

The systems of preferential treatment accorded to previously approved enterprises are defined in the Convention of November 1960 on investment regulations in the Equatorial Customs Union and consist of: 

(1) System "A", applicable to enterprises whose activity is limited to the national territory; (2) System "B", applicable to enterprises whose market extends to the territory of two or more States of the Equatorial Customs Union; (3) System "C", permitting enterprises to enjoy stabilized preferential tax treatment for a definite period. 

Moreover, contracts of establishment may be concluded between the Government and the approved enterprises.

Article 4. Under the foreign exchange regulations, the State guarantees the right to transfer capital, particularly: profits duly recorded and funds derived from the transfer or termination of an enterprise.

Arbitration 

Article 43. The settlement of disputes arising from the application of a contract of establishment and the determination of any compensation payable for failure to honor the commitments made may form the subject of an arbitration procedure whose terms and conditions shall be established for each contract.

The arbitration procedure must include the following steps: 

(a) Appointment of one arbitrator for each party; (b) In the event of a disagreement between the parties, appointment of a third arbitrator with the approval of the parties or, failing that, by a highly qualified authority who shall be designated in the contract; (c) A final decision rendered by a majority vote of the arbitrators, ruling in equity, the procedure to be left to their discretion.
